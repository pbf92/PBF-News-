TITLE: Cathie Wood Swapped Nvidia for SpaceX Over the Risk of "Losing Jensen." Now Jensen's Back
OUTLET: 24/7 Wall St.

Cathie Wood Swapped Nvidia for SpaceX Over the Risk of “Losing Jensen.” Now Jensen’s Back

Less than a week after the chairman of NVIDIA (NASDAQ:NVDA | NVDA Price Prediction) told investors the board “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,”[1] it did exactly that. The board voted unanimously on Nov. 23 to bring Jensen Huang back as president and CEO, and the stock rose 32.3% to $166.08 in Monday trading. Cathie Wood’s ARK Invest had sold NVIDIA a week earlier and moved the money into SpaceX (NASDAQ:SPCX). She cited the risk as having now gone away.
Wood Sold Over One Man Leaving
Wood explained the trade on the Money Rehab podcast with Nicole Lapin, as reported by TIKR: “Our sale was all about losing Jensen [Huang] to someone else. That company being able to take a lot of share from Nvidia.” She also said who that company might be: “Elon Musk has said he’d be delighted to invite Jensen to join his team, probably to help with TeraFab.”
SpaceX sat at the center of her thinking. Wood said ARK had added to NVIDIA weeks earlier because “SpaceX said that it was committing to Nvidia exclusively for its orbital data centers.” If Huang joined Musk, she worried he “could go somewhere else and attract a lot of the talent from Nvidia,” and SpaceX “could basically take all of their business from Nvidia.”
ARK sold about $85 million of NVIDIA on November 16, and its flagship Innovation fund bought about $46.8 million of SpaceX the same day.
Huang’s Return Erases ARK’s Stated Risk
Musk himself signed a petition asking the board to restore Huang. The board moved after what it called “input from senior levels of the United States government,” mentioning “Mr. Huang’s candor and ability to lead NVIDIA.” Huang said: “I can’t wait to get back to work.”
The business kept growing during the leadership turmoil. Fiscal third-quarter revenue reached $115.84B[2], up 103.2% year over year. Non-GAAP EPS came in at $2.68[2], ahead of the $2.47 consensus. Fourth-quarter guidance calls for $130.0B +/- 3%[2], and that guide assumes no Data Center chip revenue from China. Management also raised its fiscal 2028 revenue growth outlook to approximately 75%[1] from 70%. SpaceX is still a customer, too, with Vera Rubin deployments coming online at SpaceXSI[2].
SpaceX Delivers Growth at a Steeper Price
SpaceX gives ARK a strong business to own. Third-quarter revenue hit $14.6B[3] against a $13.02B consensus, and the company swung to GAAP net income of $4.4B from a $1.1B loss a year ago. AI segment revenue rose 830%[3] to $7.6B.
Investors pay more for that growth. SpaceX carries a market value near $1.79 trillion, while NVIDIA, worth about $4.01 trillion, earned $65.287 billion[4] in net income in a single quarter. NVIDIA trades at about 25 times trailing earnings, and SpaceX traded at a forward P/E of 48 when ARK bought. SpaceX shares sat at $136.05 Monday, 15.47% below their June 12 starting price.
Open Questions That Outlast the CEO Drama
Huang’s return leaves several issues unresolved. The board said its independent committee’s investigation into reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort “will proceed to conclusion.” Reuters reported that the SEC is looking into possible accounting issues, and NVIDIA’s quarterly report is delayed, according to TIKR. The company’s own risk list includes $390 billion[4] of supply commitments and guarantee exposure of up to $118 billion.
Some big investors object to the policy change itself. On the All-In podcast, Chamath Palihapitiya said: “You cannot reinstate somebody like that into a public company with that much valuation that has been destroyed. It just completely throws governance out the window and puts everybody at risk.”
What ARK’s Move Means for NVIDIA Holders
If you’re saving for retirement and own NVIDIA through an index fund or directly, ARK’s trade shows the risk of selling a dominant franchise over one headline scenario. Wood’s case needed Huang to stay away, sign on with Musk, and take NVIDIA’s engineers with him. Within days, he was back in the CEO chair. Despite Monday’s rally, the shares are 25.18% below their Oct. 21 level, while management keeps raising its outlook.
ARK sold to avoid a risk that has now passed. NVIDIA holders should focus on the delayed quarterly filing, the result of the independent investigation, and whether fourth-quarter revenue reaches the $130.0B[2] guide.
