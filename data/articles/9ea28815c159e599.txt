TITLE: Husband who drugged and raped wife breaks down in court telling jurors he wants to 'tell the truth'
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

Husband who drugged and raped wife breaks down in court telling jurors he wants to 'tell the truth'
The husband has been called as a prosecution witness
A husband who drugged and raped his wife for more than 20 years broke down in tears as he said he ‘wanted to tell the truth’.
The defendant, a man in his 60s from Stockport, has admitted multiple offences against his wife including rape and administering substances with intent to stupefy her so other men could engage in sexual activity with her. Nine men are currently on trial accused of sexual offences against his wife, with seven of them accused of attending their home and abusing her while she was unaware.
The husband was today (November 26) called as a prosecution witness. He told the court he felt 'guilt and shame' and accepted he'd caused his wife 'pain', adding that he initially used online chat groups 'for the sexual thrill'. Wearing a navy suit and tie, he was questioned by prosecutors about why he had initially pleaded not guilty, before changing his pleas to guilty.

He told the jury that he ‘needed’ to be at the trial. Louise Oakley KC, prosecuting, asked him: "For you why is it important that you are here in this trial?" The husband then broke down and appeared visibly emotional in the witness box, appearing to be in tears. He said 'sorry' and Judge Maurice Greene told him 'take your time'.
After composing himself, the husband said: "So I can tell the truth of what happened to [his wife]." As he began his evidence, the husband confirmed his name and age, and said he and his wife have been married for several decades.
He confirmed that following his arrest at his home in October 2025, he was taken to a police station. Jurors heard that there came a time when he was 'made aware' that the police had 'discovered' his offending against his wife.
The husband said he did not answer questions asked by police during a subsequent interview. Asked why, he told the court: "I was told I had the right to remain silent and I was advised to stay silent." Jurors heard that he was subsequently charged with a number of offences and appeared in court.
After the prosecution served evidence and he appeared at the crown court for a plea hearing, the husband told jurors that he pleaded not guilty.
He accepted there then came a time when he pleaded guilty to some offences but not others. Asked why he initially pleaded not guilty, he said: "Because I thought if I pleaded guilty to all the charges I would not be part of the trial so I wouldn't be able to tell the court what happened in my own words."
Asked why he pleaded guilty to some offences, the husband said: "Because I felt guilty. I felt I needed to let people know that I was guilty. I just needed to be here."
“In respect of all the charges you have pleaded guilty to, was [your wife] aware you committed these offences?” Ms Oakley asked. “No, she wasn’t,” the husband said.
“At the times you committed these offences, was [your wife] consenting to the offending on her?” the prosecutor asked. “No,” the husband said.
“Did she have any knowledge whatsoever?” the prosecutor asked. “None at all,” he said.
“Would she have ever consented to that type of sexual activity?” Ms Oakley asked. “No, she wouldn’t,” he said.
“With you, or others?” the prosecutor asked. “No, she wouldn’t”, the husband said. The prosecutor continued: “Have you come to court to tell the truth?”
The husband said: “I have.” “What is your motivation now?” the prosecutor asked. “So the court knows the truth what happened to my wife, so my wife knows and I’m here to tell the truth and give my account of what actually happened,” he said.
“Do you have anything to hide?” the prosecutor asked. “I don’t,” he said. The husband said he found the process of speaking with police while he was making a witness statement 'very difficult'.
Asked why, he became emotional again and paused before saying he felt 'guilt and shame'. He also accepted that he had caused his wife 'pain'. Ms Oakley asked: "Who is responsible for that pain?"
"I am," the husband said. The prosecutor asked: "Who could have stopped that pain?"
"Just me," he replied. "Who was responsible for facilitating others to attend the home?," the prosecutor asked.
He replied: "Me. I was." Ms Oakley asked: "Who was responsible for talking to other men online about your wife?"
"I was," he replied. She asked: "At any time during your offending did [the wife] consent to what happened to her?"
"Never," the husband said. The KC asked: "Did [the wife] know any of them men you had invited round to [their home]?"
"No she didn't," the husband said. He said that the men did not speak to her when they attended. Asked why he originally began using online chat groups in 2004, the husband said: "It was for the sexual thrill, I suppose."

The Manchester Evening News and other media organisations are not naming the man in order to protect his victim's anonymity. The nine men on trial deny the charges they face. Seven of the nine defendants have been described as 'contact offenders', who jurors have heard visited the couple's home expecting her to be 'wasted' and 'out of it'.
It is alleged the 'contact offenders' are taxi driver Philip Wild, 59; recruitment agency worker Mohammed Sabir, 28; building supplies employee Jordan Wallace, 31; industrial cleaning firm worker Alan Keelan, 42; retiree Robert Stewart, 70; sheet metal worker Richard Townsend, 38; and senior paramedic Jonathan Kirk, 43.
The Crown alleges 'non-contact' offenders, who allegedly 'hid behind a screen' as they conspired to administer substances to the wife, are kitchen firm sales rep David Graves, 60; and self-employed mechanic Daniel Rayner, 42. Four other men - Keith Fotheringham, Karl Lindsay, Sean Peers and Graham Brougham - pleaded guilty at earlier hearings to various offences linked to the case.
- David Graves, 59, of Milton Avenue, Derbyshire, denies one count of conspiracy to rape; one count of conspiracy to commit assault by penetration; and one count of conspiracy to administer a substance with intent
- Robert Stewart, 70, of Sandpiper Drive, Stockport, denies one count of conspiracy to rape
- Philip Wild, 58, of St Davids Road, Cheadle, denies one count of conspiracy to rape; one count of sexual assault; one count of attempted rape; and one count of assault by penetration
- Daniel Rayner, 42, of Sherwood Drive, Whitstable, denies one count of conspiracy to rape; one count of conspiracy to commit assault by penetration; and one count of conspiracy to administer a substance with intent
- Mohammed Sabir, 28, of no fixed address, denies one count of conspiracy to rape and three counts of rape
- Jordan Wallace, 31, of Hindsford Close, Wythenshawe, denies one count of rape and one count of attempted rape
- Alan Keelan, 42, of Foscarn Drive, Wythenshawe, denies two counts of attempted rape and one count of assault by penetration.
- Richard Townsend, 37, of Woodland Street, Heywood, denies one count of assault by penetration
- Jonathan Kirk, 43, of Telford Close, Macclesfield, denies one count of conspiracy to rape and two counts of rape
Four other men pleaded guilty at earlier hearings to various offences linked to the case, the jury has been told. They are Keith Fotheringham, 59, of Cleghorn Street, Dundee; Karl Lindsay, 56, of Wordsworth Drive, Taunton; Sean Peers, 38, formerly of Sandwich Road in Eccles, now of Stockport; and Graham Brougham, 74, of Birtwistle Road, Northwich, Cheshire.
