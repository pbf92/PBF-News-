TITLE: Corticosteroid Injections Provide Only Short-Term Relief for Greater Trochanteric Pain Syndrome
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Corticosteroid injections may provide short-term improvement in pain or global recovery for patients with greater trochanteric pain syndrome, but they show no sustained long-term advantage. A systematic review assessing quantitative clinical outcomes across twenty-one independent studies concluded that injections offer temporary symptomatic benefit rather than serving as a definitive standalone treatment for lateral hip pain.
Greater trochanteric pain syndrome is a common cause of lateral hip pain associated with substantial functional impairment. Although clinicians widely use corticosteroid injections in practice, their place relative to other conservative treatments and the optimal injection technique remain uncertain. This systematic review aimed to assess the effectiveness of corticosteroid injections and clarify their role within a multimodal management strategy.
The systematic review was conducted in accordance with PRISMA 2020 guidelines. Investigators initially searched PubMed and the Cochrane Library on November 13, 2023. During manuscript revision, the search was expanded to EMBASE, Scopus, and Web of Science, with all database searches updated through September 8, 2026. Eligible papers comprised full-text original studies reporting quantitative clinical outcomes following corticosteroid injections.
Risk of bias was assessed using RoB 2 for randomized trials and ROBINS-I for nonrandomized studies, while certainty of evidence was evaluated using the GRADE framework. Because of substantial clinical and methodological heterogeneity across the twenty-one included studies reported in twenty-two publications, findings were synthesized narratively rather than pooled quantitatively.
Results indicated low-certainty evidence that corticosteroid injections may provide short-term improvement in pain or global recovery compared with placebo, usual care, or a wait-and-see approach. However, no sustained long-term advantage was demonstrated. Moderate-certainty evidence suggested that exercise-based interventions and platelet-rich plasma may offer more durable benefits for selected outcomes, while low-certainty evidence suggested a possible longer-term advantage of shockwave therapy.
Adverse events were inconsistently reported across the included studies. The authors concluded that corticosteroid injections may provide short-term symptomatic benefit in greater trochanteric pain syndrome, but should be considered as one component of a multimodal management strategy rather than a definitive standalone treatment. Exercise-based rehabilitation should be integrated into management, while other treatment options may be considered according to clinical context, available resources, and patient preferences.
