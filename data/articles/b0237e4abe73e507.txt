OUTLET: The Economic Times

Digital platforms account for 98% of advertising violations in H1, Meta alone contributes 73.4%: ASCI
Synopsis
Digital platforms accounted for 98% of advertising violations identified by ASCI between April and September 2026, with Meta alone contributing 73.4% of cases. Misleading claims dominated the violations, while illegal betting, personal care, real estate and food and beverages were among the biggest problem areas, prompting hundreds of referrals to government regulators.
The report highlighted the growing role of proactive monitoring in identifying advertising violations, with 85% of cases detected through ASCI's surveillance efforts rather than consumer complaints.
Voluntary compliance among advertisers remained strong, standing at 86% during April-September 2026. Compliance levels have improved from 80% in 2023-24 to 83% in 2024-25 and 86% in 2025-26.
Public complaints also increased, rising 20% to 1,347 during April-September 2026, compared with 1,124 in the corresponding period a year earlier. Of the complaints received during the current period, 84% were filed through ASCI’s TARA platform.
Meta platforms accounted for 1,563 advertisements found to be in violation of the ASCI Code. Of these, 90% were sponsored advertisements generating revenue rather than organic brand content, while 41% belonged to categories in which advertising is prohibited. A significant share involved offshore and illegal betting advertisements, which were reported to regulators and Meta for action.
The report noted that such advertisements continued to appear despite the introduction of the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025, which prohibits online money games.
ASCI referred 633 cases involving restricted or prohibited advertising to government authorities. These included 590 cases of illegal betting and gambling, 28 cases under the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, six related to alcohol, four involving provisions of the National Medical Commission and five pertaining to other matters.
Additionally, 333 real estate advertisements were referred to the Telangana Real Estate Regulatory Authority (TGRERA) under an MoU for violations of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act. Another 136 advertisements were referred to relevant regulatory authorities for continued non-compliance.
Illegal betting advertisements were predominantly identified through proactive surveillance, accounting for 99% of the 590 cases. Sponsored advertisements made up 98% of these cases, which ASCI reported to the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) and the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting.
After illegal betting, personal care, real estate and food and beverages recorded the highest number of violations, with 456, 360 and 312 cases, respectively. Electronics and consumer durables accounted for 95 violations, healthcare 82 and education 68. Together, these seven categories accounted for 90% of advertising violations during the reporting period.
Influencer advertising also remained a major area of concern. Of the 659 advertisements processed for influencer-related violations, 95% required modification. Personal care accounted for 263 influencer violations, involving exaggerated skin and hair transformation claims, misleading safety and ingredient claims, and unsubstantiated environmental assertions.
Electronics and consumer durables violations were primarily related to mobile phone launches. The report also noted that 83.5% of influencer advertisements were not contested, with such advertisements modified within an average of six business days.
ASCI CEO and Secretary General Manisha Kapoor said the increasing speed and scale of digital advertising made it necessary for consumer protection measures to keep pace with the evolving environment.
She said misleading claims, inadequate disclosures and advertisements for products prohibited by law continued to pose risks to consumers. While rising voluntary compliance reflected greater advertiser responsiveness, the scale of violations underscored the need for sustained vigilance and accountability across the advertising ecosystem.
ASCI also reported improvements in complaint resolution. The average turnaround time for formally investigated complaints fell to 15 days during April-September 2026, compared with 30 days in 2023-24. The share of cases not contested by advertisers rose from 49% in 2023-24 to 71% during the latest reporting period, with such cases taking an average of eight days to close.
