TITLE: The hefty 175-page plan that will shape the future of Rochdale
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

The hefty 175-page plan that will shape the future of Rochdale
A document has been approved by town hall chiefs
A plan that will shape the future of Rochdale has been signed off and sent to the government for approval.
The borough’s Local Plan will mould how it will look and its priorities until 2039. Under the plans, around 11,000 new homes will be built and the borough’s railway stations will get a major revamp.
After one final round of consultation, the hefty 175-page document has been approved by town hall chiefs. Once given the green light by the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government, it is expected that formal adoption of the plan will come later in 2027 or early 2028.

The main controversy of the Local Plan had been reducing the minimum threshold of affordable housing from 20 per cent to 15 pc. This was done because it was not financially viable, a town hall meeting in July was told.
But the latest town hall meeting saw the cabinet put that threshold back up to 20 pc for ‘all developments of 10 dwellings or more’.
Housing forms a substantial part of the plan, with the land carved out for new homes already mapped out by the Places for Everyone plan. The PfE plan is a giant housing strategy adopted by nine out of 10 Greater Manchester councils.
PfE would see around 175,000 homes built across the city region over the next two decades and is expected to bring forward investment and major redevelopment in Rochdale and across the city-region.
The Rochdale council is anticipating a major boost in the coming years through the Atom Valley scheme. The vision to build 7,000 new homes, create 20,000 ‘high-quality’ jobs and carve out 17m sq ft of employment land has already taken hold.
But the Local Plan is focusing on Rochdale alone. The Rochdale Rail Corridor Strategy is at the beating heart of what the local authority really wants to achieve.
The concept would see the land around transport hubs along the Manchester to Leeds railway line reconfigured into places for people to live, work and play. These hubs would focus on the stations in Rochdale, Castleton, Mills Hill, Smithy Bridge, Littleborough and a new Slattocks station.

Combined with walking and cycling routes, park and rides, new public realms and community spaces, these hubs hope to attract more people into the area and in turn boost the local economy.
Overall, the strategic plan covered a huge array of topics that would influence how the borough would grow. These areas include climate and energy, economic growth and town centres, housing, natural environment, design and place-making, healthy communities and sustainable transport.
All these categories have been considered for each town and local centre across the area, which have been prioritised in order of importance to the borough’s growth ambitions.
