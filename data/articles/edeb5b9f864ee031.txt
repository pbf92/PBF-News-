TITLE: Amazon faces study saying 29% of 351 holiday 'deals' were no cheaper
OUTLET: PPC Land

Nearly one in three products that Amazon promoted as Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals in 2024 and 2025 cost no less during the sale than at some point in the month before it, according to a report published on November 16, 2026 by Groundwork Collaborative, a US economic think tank that also describes itself as an issue advocacy organization. Of the 351 products the group tracked, 29.1% had a sale price that was the same as or higher than their lowest price in the previous 30 days, and 9.4% cost more during the sale than they had weeks earlier.
The report, titled "Deal or Decoy? Amazon's Deceptive Discounts," is written by Breyon Williams, Elizabeth Pancotti and Lindsay Owens. The document itself carries no date. Amazon answered in a post on its corporate news site, updated on November 20, in which it called Groundwork a political advocacy group and described the findings as flawed and false. According to Amazon, 98% of the products the report cited were priced the same as or lower than over the previous 30 days.
The report landed three days before Amazon's 2026 Black Friday Week, which runs from November 19 to November 30 and is still under way.
In Short
An advocacy group called Groundwork Collaborative tracked the prices of 351 products that Amazon promoted as Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals in 2024 and 2025, and found that 29.1% cost the same or more during the sale than at some point in the month before. That matters to anyone who waits for a big sale to buy something, and to the sellers and advertisers whose holiday plans rest on a "deal" badge meaning a real price cut. Amazon says the sample was small and unrepresentative, and that 98% of the products it rechecked were priced the same or lower than in the previous 30 days. Much of the gap between the two counts comes down to one question: whether a sale price that only matches an earlier price counts as a deal. Groundwork wants US rules that make stores show the lowest price of the past 30 days next to any sale price, as the European Union already does.
What a shopper sees
Anyone who opens Amazon during one of its big sale events meets the same visual language. A product shows a sale price. Next to it sits a higher price with a line through it, which Amazon labels a "list price" or "typical price." A red badge shows the discount as a percentage, and a red label names the event.
That crossed-out figure is what the industry calls a reference price: the number a shopper is invited to compare the sale price against. The report's own example is a Weber Smokey Joe 14-inch portable grill during Black Friday 2025. The product page showed a crossed-out list price of $64.99, a deal price of $24.99, a red "-62%" badge and a "Black Friday Deal" label.
Amazon does more than badge individual products. Each sale event has a landing page with a grid of discounted products, and in recent years the company has also published curated lists of what it presents as the best offers. On its Black Friday 2025 list, Amazon told readers: "With so many deals to explore, we've handpicked some of the best ones you'll find."
The company's executives have framed these events as the moment to save. Doug Herrington, CEO of Worldwide Amazon Stores, said in November 2025 that Amazon was "providing customers with incredible Black Friday Week and Cyber Monday deals" with "deep discounts across more than 35 categories and some of our lowest prices of the year so far on top brands," according to the report, which also quotes Amazon's stated aim to "meet or beat other retailers' prices every day so customers don't have to spend time comparing prices."
Groundwork did not ask whether those crossed-out prices are honest. It asked something a shopper might ask: would waiting for the sale have saved any money at all?
How the test worked
The report sets out its method in some detail. According to Groundwork, the test ran in six steps.
- Pick the products. The authors took every product Amazon featured on its curated deals lists for four events: 185 for Black Friday 2025, 152 for Cyber Monday 2025, 53 for Black Friday 2024 and 26 for Cyber Monday 2024. The report's footnotes show that these lists were articles on AboutAmazon, the company's corporate news site, with titles such as "Top Black Friday Deals to Shop on Amazon Today" and "190+ of Amazon's Best Deals to Shop Before Black Friday Week Ends on November 28." According to the report, "After learning of our research, Amazon edited or removed these pages."
- Collect the prices. Prices came from Keepa, an outside service that records prices from Amazon product pages several times a day, and at least hourly for popular items. The main analysis used two price series Keepa tracks: the Prime-exclusive price and the Buy Box price, which is the price tied to the main Add to Cart button on a product page. That button can belong to Amazon itself or to an independent seller. Where Keepa recorded more than one price in a day, the authors kept the lowest. Products whose data Keepa refreshed less than once a day on average during the event, about 6% of those advertised, were dropped. For the products kept, Keepa refreshed prices roughly five times a day during the sales.
- Keep only products with enough data. A product stayed in the sample only if it had at least one recorded price during the sale and at least one in the 10 days before it. That left 351 unique products: 160 from Black Friday 2025 (87% of those listed), 123 from Cyber Monday 2025 (81%), 48 from Black Friday 2024 (91%) and 20 from Cyber Monday 2024 (77%). Products promoted for both events in the same year, three in 2024 and 13 in 2025, were counted once, under Black Friday. The authors say this lowers the share of deals they flag.
- Find the sale price. The sale price was the lowest price recorded at any point during the event. If an item sold for $100 on some days of Black Friday Week and $120 on others, the report treats $100 as the sale price for the whole week, which the authors say may understate how often a deal was genuine.
- Find the earlier low. The authors then took the lowest price in five windows before the sale: 10, 15, 30, 60 and 90 days. Every window ends the day before Black Friday Week began, which was November 19, 2025 and November 20, 2024. That way, Cyber Monday prices are compared with ordinary prices rather than with Black Friday prices.
- Compare the two. A product whose sale price was equal to or higher than its earlier low was counted as a "same or worse deal." Headline figures use the 30-day window, because no US rule sets a single look-back period and the European Union requires a discount to be measured against the lowest price of the prior 30 days.
The report also states plainly what it is not doing. "The findings in this report describe the products in our analysis sample unless otherwise stated," it says. The 351 products are the ones Amazon chose to showcase, not a random draw from everything on sale.
What the numbers show
At 30 days, 102 of the 351 products were no cheaper during the sale than they had been in the preceding month, and 33 cost more (PPC Land calculations from the report's percentages). The share was similar across all four events, ranging from 26.8% to 31.2%.
The further back the comparison reaches, the worse the deals look. Over 10 days, 23.5% were no cheaper. Over 90 days, the figure reaches 57.3%, and 26.8% of products cost more during the sale than at some point in the previous three months.
Two products drop out of the 10-day and 15-day windows because of how the Cyber Monday windows are cut, according to the report's notes.
Were these one-day blips? Groundwork tested that directly. It counted how many of the 30 days before the sale each "same or worse" product had been selling at or below its sale price. The typical product, the median, had been available at that price or less on eight of those 30 days. Among products that cost more during the sale, the median overpayment was 19% above the lowest price in the previous month.
Examples
The report names eight products. Four cost exactly the same during the sale as they had weeks earlier; four cost more.
The UGG boots are the starkest case. Sold as a Cyber Monday deal at $179.98, they had been $98.40 less than four weeks earlier.
By product category
Results varied widely by what was on sale. Toys and games were more than three times as likely as beauty products to be "same or worse" deals. The report only shows categories with at least 10 products, which covers 329 of the 351 (PPC Land calculation).
Small groups move easily. The sports figure rests on seven of 14 products.
The stress tests
Any study like this invites objections, and the report anticipates four of them. Each time, the authors changed one assumption and ran the numbers again.
One-day dips. If a product dropped below its sale price for a single day, perhaps through a glitch, the authors ignored it and only counted lows that lasted at least two days in a row. The 30-day share fell from 29.1% to 25.1%.
Amazon versus other sellers. The Buy Box price is not always Amazon's own. To isolate prices Amazon sets, the authors re-ran the test using only offers sold by Amazon. That covered 248 products, and the share of "same or worse" deals rose to 35.9%. For Black Friday 2025, 41.1% of Amazon-sold deals were no cheaper than a month before, and 14.3% cost more.
Lightning deals. Amazon also runs a lightning deal, a discount that lasts a few hours on a limited number of units. When any such flash price during the sale was counted as the sale price, the share fell to 26.2%.
Early deals. Amazon often cuts prices a few days before an event and holds them through it. Because the authors could not tell which products were early deals, they assumed all of them might be and ignored the seven days before each sale. One in five deals, 20.5%, still had a lower price between eight and 30 days earlier. Between eight and 90 days, the share was 51.6%.
One figure in this set does not behave as described. The report calls the lightning deal test "a generous inclusion that works in Amazon's favor," yet its chart shows the Black Friday 2024 share rising, from 27.1% in the main analysis to 29.2%, while the other events fall or hold. The report offers no explanation.
Amazon's answer
Amazon rejected the findings. In a post on its corporate news site, updated on November 20, the company called Groundwork a political advocacy group and the claims flawed and false. Its specific objections, according to Amazon, are these.
- The sample. The report covers 350 products, against more than 10 million deals offered during those events, and includes only 17 of Amazon's top 200 best-selling deals.
- A changing study. Amazon says Groundwork first shared with media outlets an analysis of nearly 1,500 products from Prime Day 2026 and the 2025 Black Friday and Cyber Monday events, then delayed publication, switched to older data from 2024 and 2025 and cut the sample to about 350 products. Amazon says Groundwork declined to share its dataset, so the company rebuilt it from what news outlets had shared.
- The pages. Amazon says the updated research appears to rest on two low-traffic corporate blog pages that listed some products as deals by mistake. Some of those items, according to Amazon, were never labeled as deals in its store. The company says it found the error in a blog post marked as subject to change and has updated its processes.
- Early deals. Amazon says the analysis did not account for all of its early event deals.
- List prices. Amazon says Groundwork wrongly used List Prices to set comparison prices for the prior 30 days.
Amazon also gave its own numbers. Across the products the report cited, 98% were priced the same as or lower than over the past 30 days, according to Amazon, and 99% for the 2025 events.
