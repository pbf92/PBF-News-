TITLE: The base PlayStation 5 will experience drops below 30 FPS in Grand Theft Auto VI.
OUTLET: GameGPU

Rockstar Games has set a performance target of 30 frames per second for Grand Theft Auto VI on all current-generation consoles, including the PlayStation 5, PlayStation 5 Pro, and Xbox Series X|S. However, the developers' target and technical analysis of pre-release builds indicate that the base PlayStation 5 may not be able to maintain a stable 30 frames per second throughout gameplay. In the most detailed locations of the state of Leonidas, as well as during dynamic scenes with explosions, chases, and dense city traffic, owners of the standard Sony console may experience frame rate drops below 30 frames per second and uneven frame generation times.
The main reason for potential performance drops on the base PlayStation 5 is the high computational load on the eight-core AMD Zen 2 CPU with a dynamic frequency of up to 3,5 GHz, which is paired with an AMD RDNA 2 GPU with a performance of 10,28 teraflops and memory with a bandwidth of 448 GB/s. Under peak GPU load, SmartShift power distribution technology reduces the actual CPU frequency of the base PlayStation 5 to 3,2 GHz, causing the system to lose power during complex scenes. By comparison, the processor in the junior Xbox Series S console operates at a fixed frequency of 3,6 GHz without multi-threading or 3,4 GHz with the technology enabled. SMT, and in the older Xbox Series X, the clock frequency of the cores is rigidly fixed at 3,8 GHz without SMT and 3,6 GHz with active multithreading, which prevents power supply drops during intensive gaming logic calculations.
Within Sony's console lineup, the top-of-the-line PlayStation 5 Pro will be able to deliver a stable 30 frames per second in Grand Theft Auto VI thanks to a range of hardware enhancements. It features a 10% boost clock speed up to 3,85 GHz, a 28% increase in GDDR6 RAM bandwidth to 576 GB/s, an additional 2 GB of DDR5 memory for background system needs, and accelerated ray tracing units for fast rendering of the BVH hierarchy. On the base PlayStation 5, simultaneous processing of global illumination, destruction physics, and behavior algorithms for hundreds of NPCs at 1440p without a hardware PSSR upscaler could exceed the frame generation time limit of 33,3 ms, which will lead to reduced smoothness in the most intensive sequences after the game's release on November 19, 2026.
