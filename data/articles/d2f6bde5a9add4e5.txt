FA release statement after Man United row and leaked Premier League panel vote
Leny Yoro was sent off against Liverpool at the weekend for denying a goalscoring opportunity, but will not have to serve a one-match ban against Brentford on Saturday
The FA have shared written reasons why Leny Yoro's red card against Liverpool on Sunday was overturned. That's despite Howard Webb and ProRef insisting that Sam Barrott's call was the right one to make.
The Frenchman was shown a straight red by the referee just eight minutes into Manchester United's trip to Anfield after bringing down Alexander Isak as the last man back on the edge of his own area. VAR John Brooks agreed with the decision.
Despite Webb backing his officials, the FA has now decided to overturn the red card, meaning Yoro will be free to play for United this weekend against Brentford at Old Trafford. Giving their reasons for the decision, a three-person panel made up of former professional footballers said that Isak's heavy touch meant that he "had in fact lost control of the ball."
Under the Premier League's rules, this means that Yoro should not have been sent off for the foul. A statement read: "The Commission members were unanimous that, taking the foul committed by Yoro out of the scenario, Isak would not have had an obvious goal-scoring opportunity.
"Despite the fact that Isak had reached the ball initially, his touch was heavy to the extent that he had in fact lost control of the ball - that control had, in effect, passed to the advancing MUFC goalkeeper who in the view of the Commission would have been able to gather or smother the ball before AI [Alexander Isak] could get to it and shoot at goal.

"To the mind of the Commission, the referee had made an 'obvious error' in deciding that AI would have had an obvious goal-scoring opportunity."
Elsewhere, the reasons for overturning the one-match suspension included that United had provided a wealth of 'evidence' highlighting why Yoro should not have been sent off. They also provided three separate angles of the incident, as well as Pascal Struijk's similar red card against Sunderland, which was later overturned.

As per the Mail, meanwhile, United turned to its in-house data and statistics gurus to help prove Yoro's innocence. Led by former Formula 1 guru Michael Sansoni, United outlined how bigwigs at Carrington calculated the probability of Isak touching the ball prior to the contact from their defender.
That data concluded that Isak would have been around 1.8metres short of reaching the ball, even if he achieved optimal speed and inclusive of directional factors. The appeals commission responded to that by notifying United that such data was unverified and had to be treated with 'caution.'
It comes as Premier League officials are thought to be furious over Yoro's red card being overturned, with the EPL's Key Match Incidents panel voting 5-0 in favour of Barrott's decision to send off the centre-half. The panel are also claimed to have sided with Brooks' decision not to intervene.
Speaking about the matter on Mid'd Up, meanwhile Webb shared his take on the sending off. He said: "That (the red card) was the referee's call, made on the field. Then the VAR checks whether the decision made on the field was clearly and obviously incorrect - and formed the opinion it wasn't.
"I think by and large, most people would see that as a correct decision, with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball. The alternative view is that Lammens might get there first, but I think it's a little bit debatable, I guess. Certainly not clearly wrong."
The Premier League and ProRef have not commented on claims that officials are angered by the FA's decision to overturn Barrott's ruling. An Old Trafford source said the interpretation of ProRef was 'totally wrong' and had been compounded by Webb's failure to recognise the error.
The MEN understands that United will raise the matter with the Premier League and ProRef.
