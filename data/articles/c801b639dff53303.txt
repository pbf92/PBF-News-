TITLE: Wall Street ends higher as battered AI stocks gain in volatile week
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

By Saeed Azhar, Shashwat Chauhan and Avinash P
NEW YORK, Nov 27 (Reuters) - Wall Street's main indexes ended higher on Friday and posted weekly gains as investors returned from the Thanksgiving holiday and flocked to hard-hit AI-linked shares.
US stocks have swung sharply this week, with Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO and Anthropic's brief fall below its initial public offering price adding to volatility across the AI trade.
Investors have alternated between bargain-hunting after a steep selloff and fretting over soaring AI costs, heavy borrowing and doubts about companies' returns on investment.
"The market is so oversold that a bounce was inevitable," said Jamie Cox, managing partner at Harris Financial Group.
Chipmakers that investors battered this month recovered ground, with Micron and Marvell gaining 7.6% and 7.3% respectively. The broader chip gauge also climbed 4.7%. Nvidia, after remaining weak for most of the session, ended 0.8% higher.
The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 591.99 points, or 1.18%, to 50,774.94, the S&P 500 gained 114.71 points, or 1.60%, to 7,297.49 and the Nasdaq Composite gained 543.70 points, or 2.31%, to 24,129.50.
AI beneficiaries, including data-storage firm Sandisk, surged almost 16%, while optical-products maker Lumentum and server maker Dell also gained sharply.
AI hyperscalers — tech companies that run large data centers — also rose, with Amazon.com, Microsoft, Meta Platforms and Oracle posting gains. Tesla surged 5%.
"There is a bounce in technology which started Monday, continued Wednesday with an intraday reversal and now continues today. So much fast money has delevered out of tech that this could have persistence next week as they are forced to chase back in," said Andrew Slimmon, senior portfolio manager at Morgan Stanley Investment Management.
WEEKLY GAINS
For the week, the S&P 500 gained 5.6%, the Nasdaq rose 6.68%, and the Dow climbed 5.46%. The S&P 500 weekly gain was the index's biggest since April 2025.
The S&P 500 bank index rose 1.6%, fueled by JPMorgan Chase's almost 2% gain.
The market's gains have done little to erase November's losses. The S&P 500 and the Nasdaq are on track for their worst November since 2008, pressured by concerns over tougher AI regulation following the Democrats' US midterm election victory and expectations that monetary policy will remain restrictive.
Traders are almost fully pricing in a Federal Reserve interest rate hike in December after data this week showed the US economy grew much faster than initially estimated in the third quarter and inflation accelerated further in October.
