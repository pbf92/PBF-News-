TITLE: Apple waives its $14.99 Apple TV fee for F1's Las Vegas Grand Prix
OUTLET: PPC Land

Apple said on November 17, 2026 that every session of the Formula 1 Heineken Las Vegas Grand Prix 2026, the last race of the season held in the United States, would stream free on Apple TV, the service that otherwise charges US viewers $14.99 a month for its Formula 1 coverage. Univision will simulcast the race in Spanish, and FOX-owned Tubi will run a free creator-hosted alternative broadcast built on Apple's feed.
In Short
Apple is letting anyone in the US watch the Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend, from Thursday's first practice to Saturday night's race, on Apple TV without paying the usual $14.99 monthly fee. The race also reaches Spanish-speaking audiences through a Univision simulcast and a free, ad-supported crowd through Tubi, which matters for advertisers because Apple's own Formula 1 rights otherwise sit behind a subscription. Apple is using the weekend to push its Sports, News, Maps, Music and Fitness+ apps alongside the stream, while its footnotes still describe a subscription requirement that the headline says does not apply.
A paid service, opened for one weekend
The post appeared on Apple Newsroom labeled "UPDATE" and dated November 17, 2026, under the headline "Formula 1 returns to the U.S. this weekend with the Las Vegas Grand Prix, streaming live on Apple TV." Its standfirst reads: "Final U.S. race of the season to stream free on Apple TV for fans nationwide."
The body is direct about scope. The event streams "for free in the U.S. on Apple TV, with every session live and on demand," according to Apple. That sentence carries a footnote, and the footnote says something else: "To watch F1 on Apple TV, viewers are required to have a U.S. Apple TV subscription." The same footnote adds that "An Apple TV subscription does not include third-party branded subscription services or content available for rental or purchase in the Apple TV app." Apple does not reconcile the two statements anywhere in the post.
Outside reporting points to the headline as the operative claim. Racer reported on November 9, 2026 that all sessions of the Las Vegas weekend would be free to watch in the US without a subscription. According to the same report, the Austrian Grand Prix in June was the first 2026 race Apple offered without a subscription, and Las Vegas is the first race in a US time zone to get the treatment. Racer did not say whether viewers need to sign in with an Apple Account.
Free weekends were part of the plan from the start. When Apple secured exclusive US Formula 1 rights in October 2025, the five-year agreement covered every practice, qualifying, Sprint and Grand Prix session from the 2026 season, with select races and all practice sessions to be made free in the Apple TV app. The same deal folded F1 TV Premium into Apple TV subscriptions in the US at no additional cost. A month later, Apple applied the bundling logic to soccer, moving every Major League Soccer match into the base Apple TV subscription from 2026 and ending the separate MLS Season Pass after the 2025 season.
The paid offer around the free weekend is spelled out in the "How to Watch" section of the post. Apple TV costs "$14.99 per month with a seven-day free trial for new subscribers," according to Apple, and "For a limited time, eligible customers who purchase and activate a new iPhone, iPad, Apple TV, or Mac can enjoy three months of Apple TV for free." That price is recent. Apple raised the monthly fee 30% from $9.99 to $12.99 in August 2025, its third increase since the service launched in 2019, and 9to5Mac reported a further rise to $14.99 on August 28, 2026. By PPC Land's calculation, the US monthly price is about 50% higher than it was before August 2025.
The weekend schedule
Apple's post gives one time: Univision's race coverage starts at 10 p.m. ET. Session times come from Formula 1's own schedule, published on November 16, which lists all times in Las Vegas local time and confirms the event is not a Sprint weekend. Formula 1 lists it as the 21st round of 2026.
The 8 p.m. local start puts lights out at 11 p.m. on the US East Coast and at 4 a.m. UTC on Sunday, November 22. Univision's coverage therefore opens about an hour before the start, consistent with the "dedicated pre- and post-race programming" Apple describes.
What the coverage includes
Apple's description of the broadcast package is longer than its description of the access terms. Coverage carries English and Spanish commentary and "access to up to 30 additional live feeds across all sessions," according to the post. Four are named.
The one product change in the post concerns Multiview. "And now Multiview is available on supported smart TVs in addition to Apple devices and streaming players," Apple wrote. The post does not list which smart TV models qualify. Sports Video Group reported in March that Apple launched its Formula 1 coverage with the same set of up to 30 extra feeds, Multiview and Dolby Vision, and that Sky Sports broadcasts were also available to viewers during the season.
How viewing works, according to Apple
Apple's post sets out the path to the stream in its "How to Watch" section and its footnotes. Described in order:
- Location. Formula 1 on Apple TV is available in the US only, according to Apple.
- Device. The Apple TV app runs on iPhone, iPad, Apple TV 4K, Apple Vision Pro, Mac, smart TVs from Samsung, LG, Sony, VIZIO, TCL and others, Roku and Amazon Fire TV devices, Chromecast with Google TV, and PlayStation and Xbox consoles. The service is also available at tv.apple.com.
- Access. For this weekend, Apple says every session streams free in the US. For other races, the footnote requires a US Apple TV subscription at $14.99 a month, with a seven-day trial for new subscribers and a limited-time offer of three months free for eligible buyers who purchase and activate a new iPhone, iPad, Apple TV or Mac.
- Commentary and feeds. Viewers pick English or Spanish commentary and choose among up to 30 additional live feeds. The Podium feed runs only during race and Sprint sessions.
- Multiview. Up to four live feeds share one screen on Apple devices, streaming players and supported smart TVs.
- Picture and sound. Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos work through the Apple TV app on supported Apple devices and compatible Dolby-enabled equipment.
- Phone and watch. The free Apple Sports app for iPhone links to the live race page on Apple TV. Its Live Activities need "compatible Apple devices running iOS 18 or later and watchOS 11 or later," according to Apple's third footnote.
Univision and Tubi carry the race to free audiences
Two outside distributors widen the reach of Apple's production. "Univision will simulcast Apple TV's broadcast of the Formula 1 Las Vegas Grand Prix, with race coverage beginning at 10 p.m. ET," the post states, with programming before and after the race aimed at Spanish-speaking audiences across the US. The arrangement covers the race. Apple does not say whether Univision carries practice or qualifying.
Hispanic viewers are already heavy streamers. Nielsen data from September 2025 put streaming at 55.8% of Hispanic TV time, against 46% for the general population. The race reaches Spanish-language viewers both ways: over broadcast through Univision, and in streaming through the Spanish commentary inside the Apple TV app.
Tubi's role is different. Its Tubi Original "The Fast Lane" returns for its "third and final live F1 altcast of the 2026 season," according to Apple, streaming free on Tubi. YouTuber Michelle Khare, known for "Challenge Accepted," and former racing champion Justin Bell, of "Drive to Wynn," host it. The altcast uses "the main Apple TV race feed and onboard footage alongside real-time driver tracking and entertaining, culture-forward commentary," the post says, and it closes the show's first season. Tom's Guide reported in February that Apple described free Tubi altcasts for select races when it announced its 2026 Formula 1 partnerships with Netflix, Tubi and IMAX.
Tubi runs on advertising, which makes this the most directly ad-supported outlet for Apple's Formula 1 production. Tubi is an AVOD service, and its growth has been steep. FOX reported that Tubi revenue rose 35% and view time 23% in the quarter to September 30, 2026. Comscore's first Score Report found that Tubi held 11.6% of streaming ad exposure time in the US in August 2026, second to YouTube's 44.4%. Tubi passed 100 million monthly active users in June 2025.
Programming built for social feeds and a video game
Apple surrounds the race with four original series. Two are framed as social-first.
Circuits in Focus, a track preview series, pairs 2016 world champion Nico Rosberg with content creator and driver Emelia Hartford. The pair run the Las Vegas Strip Circuit in the "2026 Season Pack for EA SPORTS F1 25, the official video game of the FIA Formula One World Championship," according to Apple, breaking down braking zones, overtaking spots and the effect of cool night temperatures on a low-grip street surface. The series doubles as a showcase for Electronic Arts' licensed game.
The Fashion Circuit is described as Apple TV's "social-first digital content series celebrating the intersection of fashion, culture, and Formula 1." The Las Vegas edition covers paddock looks and race-weekend fashion.
Full Throttle returns with an off-road episode led by content creator and engineer Christina Roki.
The POV, a social series that runs after the race, has former Red Bull Racing senior technician Calum Nicholas and Roki reacting to overtakes, strategy calls and turning points.
Formula 1's own commercial reach is the context for this kind of programming. The sport claimed 827 million fans worldwide when it deployed a Salesforce AI agent in March 2026, 43% of them under 35. Creator hosts, short-form reaction shows and fashion content are aimed at that younger slice rather than at long-time viewers who already watch every session.
Seven Apple services, one race
The remainder of the post reads as a cross-promotion across Apple's services, with a consumer brand attached.
Apple Sports, the free iPhone app, carries leaderboards, standings and live updates for every qualifying, Sprint and race session, with a link to the live race page on Apple TV. Live Activities push updates to the iPhone Lock Screen and Apple Watch. Game Card Sharing lets fans share results, and My Sports syncs favorite teams and drivers across Apple TV, Apple Sports and Apple News. It is a second screen owned by the same company as the first one.
Apple News offers notifications, coverage from publishers and live results, and "Users can also view the race directly within the News app through a special Las Vegas Grand Prix feature," according to Apple, with a jump to the Apple TV app.
Apple Maps returns its Las Vegas circuit map with turn numbers, the finish line and race cars, now with realistic pavement coloring, a refreshed 3D pit building and more colorful grandstands. For people attending, Maps shows entry gates, rideshare locations, water stations, walkways, overpasses, road closures and walking directions. A Race Week Spots Hyperlocal Guide, built with the Las Vegas Grand Prix organization, lists places to visit near the circuit along with fan zones and driver activations. The guide is editorial, and the post does not mention advertising in it. Apple Maps has carried paid placements since Maps ads went live in the US and Canada on August 21, 2026, so the race-week guide to nearby venues runs in an app where Apple already sells placements to local businesses.
Apple Music adds a DJ mix from Martin Garrix, a personal playlist from Cadillac driver Valtteri Bottas and a refreshed "Formula 1: Sounds of the Circuit" playlist. Apple Fitness+ has a cycling workout led by trainer Kym Perfetto, set to Bottas's "The Warm-Up" playlist.
