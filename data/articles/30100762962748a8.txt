TITLE: Arthur Hayes warns Bitcoin could test $50K if the Nasdaq keeps sliding
OUTLET: TradingView

Arthur Hayes warns Bitcoin could test $50K if the Nasdaq keeps sliding
Arthur Hayes has a message for anyone hoping Bitcoin would shrug off the latest tech rout: it didn't. The BitMEX co-founder and chief investment officer of Maelstrom said Bitcoin has slipped below its previous local low. He added that it could test $50,000 if the Nasdaq continues to fall.
That is a conditional warning, not a prophecy. Still, when one of crypto's best-known macro voices ties Bitcoin's next move to a stock index, the market tends to pay attention.
Hayes posted the warning on social media on November 21, 2026. His argument was simple. Bitcoin has lost a level that previously acted as a floor, and the path lower now runs through tech stocks.
The timing was not random. On November 20, 2026, the Nasdaq Composite fell approximately 5.3%. Heavy selling in AI stocks drove the decline.
After Hayes' post, Bitcoin was reported trading near $59,200. That put it down about 7.6% for the week.
Crypto was not alone in its discomfort. A weak debut for the Anthropic (ANT) IPO added to the sense that investor nerves extend well beyond digital assets.
Put differently, the selling did not start in crypto. It started in the parts of the stock market most associated with growth and risk, then spread to the asset that often trades like a leveraged version of that mood.
The $50,000 figure is not a fresh invention. Hayes has pointed to it before.
In an essay dated August 4, 2026, Hayes cited $50,000 as a potential low for Bitcoin. In the same piece, he predicted Bitcoin could range between $60,000 and $70,000.
Bitcoin near $59,200 sits just below the bottom of that range. The range, in other words, is currently being tested from the wrong side.
Hayes has also linked the $50,000 level to liquidity dynamics within the AI sector. The logic runs roughly like this: when money floods into a hot theme like AI, risk appetite is high across the board. When that theme cracks, the same capital retreats, and assets that depend on abundant liquidity feel it.
The broader point in Hayes' warning is about correlation. Bitcoin has often been pitched as an asset that moves independently of traditional markets. Days like November 20 suggest that, at least for now, it is moving alongside tech stocks rather than against them.
The 5.3% Nasdaq drop and the roughly 7.6% weekly decline in Bitcoin fit that pattern. Both moves point in the same direction, and both appear to be driven by the same anxiety about AI valuations and liquidity.
The Anthropic IPO adds another layer. A soft debut for a closely watched AI company signals that public market buyers are getting more selective.
The key word in Hayes' warning is "if." His $50,000 scenario depends on the Nasdaq continuing to fall. That makes tech stocks, and AI names in particular, the indicator to watch rather than any crypto-native signal.
If the Nasdaq stabilizes, the case for a slide toward $50,000 weakens. If the selling in AI stocks resumes, Hayes' framework suggests Bitcoin could face more pressure, with $50,000 acting as the level he has flagged since at least August.
