TITLE: Can You Run in the Morning and Steal Cars at Night? Dealing with GTA FOMO.
OUTLET: Outside Magazine

(Photo: Getty Images)
On my run this morning, the streets of New York seemed a little emptier. You may have noticed the same thing in your part of the world. It is not the Rapture. It’s Grand Theft Auto.
Across the country, a lot of people have come down with a sudden and very specific illness. Even the man who profits from it saw this coming. Back in April, Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick said, “I think a lot of people will be calling in sick on November 19.” One economist puts the cost in lost productivity at around $1 billion. To be fair, it’s been a long wait. GTA V came out 13 years ago. My daughter was in daycare then. (That was the only time I could sneak in some game time.) She is now a freshman in high school.
Here’s the thing, though: for a game about crime, a surprising amount of it is about nature. I mean, there’s a National Park you can explore. It’s been less than 24 hours, and my Instagram feed has already served up players feeding NPCs (the game’s random bystanders) to alligators and a Bigfoot lurking near a swamp called Cottonmouth Lake. The Bigfoot is the Skunk Ape of actual Florida folklore. It knocks you out with noxious fumes, and the last thing you see is a pair of giant hairy feet coming toward you. In its demented way, it’s a Florida nature documentary. Which is to say: you are about to lose a lot of people to the swamp.
Then there’s the time commitment. Rockstar Games says one of its studio heads needed about 80 hours to get through the story, side quests included. That’s a 16-week marathon build at five hours a week. For those of us with a training plan to execute, a ski trip on the calendar, and a whole winter we’d actually like to spend outside, the math is cruel. Like me, you may be suffering from GTA FOMO.
When GTA IV came out in 2008, it was easily the best game I’d ever played. It was set in Liberty City, a barely disguised New York City. Niko Bellic, the main character of the game, had a story that was darker and more grown-up than anything the series had tried before: a war-haunted immigrant chasing the American dream through its back alleys, with the pathos of a Michael Mann criminal. Plus it was fun to just walk the streets. That spring I interviewed Dan Houser, one of the game’s creators, for GQ. I asked him why New York was the perfect setting. “You just meet freaks everywhere,” he told me. He described a guy who walks up with a CD player and asks if you like hip-hop, “the real stuff.” Say yes, and it turns into a little scene with people laughing at you. “We were inspired by living here.”
This new GTA received the same obsessive treatment, this time in Florida. Rockstar has had a research team based in Miami for years, and its developers have described riding along with ex-cops, hitting the clubs, and logging beach time, all so that Leonida, the game’s version of Florida, would feel like a real place.
I never finished GTA V. Its release coincided with new fatherhood and a new gig, and, well, I chose to be a responsible adult. I’m told this was the right call. My character is presumably still sitting in a stolen car somewhere outside SoCal inspired Los Santos, engine running, waiting for me. To be honest, I didn’t love it the way I loved IV anyway. But VI, if all the reviews are correct, is a masterpiece. My kids now know how to operate the microwave. So I will be playing it.
Meanwhile, the real outdoors isn’t going anywhere, and neither is my list: I’ll be hiking in Mexico next week, off-roading in Texas in December, and maybe squeezing in a ski trip. But there is GTA that must be played. I believe in you. You can do both. Run in the morning, steal cars at night. Don’t neglect your children or your pets if you have them. But if you miss a few runs, fine. Maybe that spring race isn’t the one where you go for the PR, you know?
So take some comfort: as you hunker down this winter, you are still communing with nature, technically. Just watch out for the alligators.
