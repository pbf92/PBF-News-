TITLE: 'The Results Are Very, Very Impressive' - Digital Foundry Praises GTA 6 On Xbox One
OUTLET: Pure Xbox

The smartypants over at Digital Foundry are still putting in the work, this time going deep into the nitty gritty of how well GTA 6 performs when streamed through the Xbox Cloud Gaming app on Xbox One.
Following their rigorous testing on Xbox Series X and Series S, PS5 and PS5 Pro, the boffin boys have now spent a good week putting streamed performance, latency and image quality under the cosh.
Xbox Cloud Gaming is streamed from Microsoft servers which means the version of GTA 6 you'll be playing is the Xbox Series S version. Of course, this means the drawbacks present on the Series S version are also present in the Xbox Cloud streamed one.
"Let's start with nuts and bolts of the experience. As with other Xbox Cloud Gaming titles, which are streamed from Microsoft servers rather than your own console, the version of GTA 6 you're getting here is the Xbox Series S version. That means a few things just by the nature of the Series S port itself - slightly ropey performance, quality setting cutbacks across the board and a 720p base image upscaled to 1080p using FSR 4.1. However, you do at least get all of the game's major effects intact, including ray-traced lighting, ray-traced reflections and strand-based hair."
Performance begins where you'd expect on Xbox Series S, clocking around 20-30FPS. However, Digital Foundry notes that "frame pacing is significantly worse than the real deal, with quicker 16.7ms frames and slower 50.0ms frames surrounding the expected 33.3ms ones."
From their Xbox One Cloud tests, they used an ethernet connection, and were genuinely impressed with GTA 6's image quality. They mentioned the game isn't "significantly degraded by the streaming process", with little visible compression or artefacting when in motion. Stood still, it's hardly noticeable too.
Streaming GTA 6 at 1440p (via Xbox Game Pass Ultimate on One S or X) also appears to benefit from a higher bitrate than usual, with DF stating it gets a "higher bitrate than that afforded to other games". For context, GTA 6 streamed at about 37mbps, compared to the recently released Control: Resonant, which only got 15-27mbps.
Latency also looks to be the big winner in testing. Digital Foundry tested GTA 6 running natively on Xbox Series S with the streamed version on Xbox One, finding the latter only added a mere 9ms to latency. Barely noticeable.
"Latency is also exceptional - at least in context. We measured around an extra 9ms of added latency compared to running GTA 6 locally on Series S - 184.5ms vs 175.2ms -which is essentially imperceptible for a game that is running on all consoles with around 160 to 180ms of latency anyway. That makes the pace relatively sedate, even when driving or shooting, but it's something that you can get used to over time, and the cloud version doesn't change the equation here. These numbers come from a console attached via ethernet cable to a high-speed fibre connection with a wired controller; your mileage may vary based on location, internet speed, controller latency, display latency and so on."
So, GTA 6 looks decent and feels comparably responsive controller in hand, with the biggest drawback to streaming the game on Xbox One being that it pulls from an Xbox Series S version, and not the Series X version of the game.
Of course, if you can't access current-gen hardware, Digital Foundry thinks streaming GTA 6 through the Xbox Cloud app on Xbox One is a pretty good way to experience the universally acclaimed RPG. But that generally comes with Cloud Playtime charges, including if you go beyond your monthly allotted hours on Xbox Game Pass.
