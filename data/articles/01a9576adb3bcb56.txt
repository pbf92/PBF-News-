TITLE: World’s leading science journal carries researchers’ super El Niño health warning
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Leading researchers from Heriot-Watt University and University Cork College have warned that governments must now prepare for an El Niño that could bring hunger and disease outbreaks.
The researchers, writing in the globally respected scientific journal Nature, say the 2026–27 El Niño now developing in the Pacific could be among the strongest in more than a century.
The commentary, The super El Niño is more than a climate shock – it’s a health one, too, is written by Professor Eoin McLaughlin of Heriot-Watt’s Edinburgh Business School and Professor Matthias Beck of University College Cork.
Both warn that its effects will reach far beyond extreme weather. Food shortages and infectious disease are likely to hit the same vulnerable communities at the same time, putting health systems that are already stretched under even more pressure.
Eoin McLaughlin, Professor of Economics and Head of Research in the Department of Accountancy, Economics, and Finance in the Edinburgh Business School at Heriot-Watt University said: “El Niño is a naturally occurring climate phenomenon that can be monitored and forecast months in advance.
“Countries have developed ways to adjust to El Niño’s, but when a very strong El Niño occurs it can overwhelm food and health systems.
“This year’s super El Niño is particularly worrying because food systems are already under pressure due to ongoing wars in Ukraine and Iran, and shortages of fertilisers.”
El Niño is a climate pattern that comes back every few years. It warms the surface of the tropical Pacific Ocean far above normal, and the effects spread across the globe.
It can bring drought to some regions and flooding to others, along with heatwaves, failed crops and disrupted fisheries.
The strongest events can be felt worldwide for a year or more. The authors argue that the world too often responds to these effects one emergency at a time.
A drought is treated as a food crisis, a disease outbreak as a health crisis, and the two are rarely planned for together.
In reality, they say, the two are closely linked. Hunger weakens people’s resistance to infection. Flooding and heat can help diseases spread. Displacement and poverty make it harder for people to reach care when they need it.
Professor of Economics at Cork University Business School in UCC, Matthias Beck, a co-author of the study, said “Major natural disasters can have drastic adverse impacts on food supplies and human health.
“We need to move beyond responding to individual disasters and recognise how climate shocks can trigger wider humanitarian emergencies. Governments and international organisations must prepare for these interconnected risks before they escalate.”
The researchers are calling for coordinated preparedness around the world while there is still time to act. That means early warning, food security and public health planning working side by side, rather than in separate silos.
By recognising how climate disruption, food supplies and health connect, they argue, policymakers can anticipate what extreme weather will bring and better protect the communities most at risk.

About Heriot-Watt University
Heriot-Watt University is a global, research-led university with six campuses in Edinburgh, Scottish Borders, Orkney, Dubai and Malaysia, alongside a new Online campus, building on more than three decades of distance learning.
More than 26,000 students from over 182 countries are currently studying with Heriot-Watt, and the university has more than 182,000 alumni across 190 countries.
Heriot-Watt's purpose is to develop leaders who build an equitable, sustainable and enriching society.
Its ten-year strategy, Strategy 2035: Driven by purpose, measured by impact, focuses on applied learning for life and employability, with research that addresses real-world challenges.
It sets out how the University will grow its reach and deepen its impact as a global university, through its Global Research Institutes (GRIs), a flexible and industry-relevant portfolio, a signature student experience, and two growth pathways: its Online Campus and strategic partnerships across academia, industry and civic life.
The University specialises in business, design, social sciences and STEM subjects, with internationally recognised strengths in engineering, photonics and quantum technologies, robotics and AI, and energy and net zero, addressing real-world challenges.
Edinburgh Business School
Edinburgh Business School is one of the world's largest providers of postgraduate business education, with over 67,000 alumni across 186 countries. It is known for practical, industry-focused business education and research that tackles global challenges in work, sustainable finance, and transport.
