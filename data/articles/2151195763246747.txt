TITLE: GTA 6 PC Port Takes a Massive Leap Forward in Just Days
OUTLET: Polygon.com

Rockstar Games finally released Grand Theft Auto 6 this month after fans had waited 13 years. Unfortunately, PC players are still waiting as no PC port has been officially announced. But following the catastrophic leak of an apparent build of the game onto the internet, PC players have begun a race to create an unofficial version of GTA 6. That race started slowly, but progress has been exponential in only a few days.
If you followed the news in the lead-up to GTA 6's release, then you are probably familiar with the name Cyberleek. The prolific hacker raised millions of dollars in cryptocurrency by sharing unauthorized clips of Rockstar's big release ahead of launch. With the game now available to the public, it seemed like Cyberleek's time in the spotlight might be over. That proved to be untrue on Nov. 23, when Cyberleek released an apparent Xbox executable file that serves as a goldmine for programmers, hackers, and emulation enthusiasts, allowing them to start the hard work of making an unofficial PC port of GTA 6 a reality.
In the first hours after the build went public, multiple projects posted early builds that could get the game running. It was not in a playable state, however, with incomplete shaders and models and extremely low frame rates. Now, only a handful of days later, new updates are showing the game in a much better state. Progress on the various builds is constantly documented on sites like Reddit and PortWatch VI. The latest footage shows that GTA 6 is now actually playable on PC, though not completely. Videos of the game's opening missions are common now, with models fully rendered. One Reddit user showed that they had gotten the XCL port (one of the more prominent forks) running at over 30 fps, a pretty respectable, stable frame rate. The leading build on PortWatch VI, from Belobug, is currently said to be over 90% playable.
This speedy progress is largely the result of generative AI's newfound impact on the hacking scene. The process of decompiling and building a port of a game previously took months and even years to complete, but that can now be done in hours with the right tools. This is often called vibe-coding, which has produced mixed results in similar projects. Even Xbox is getting in on the technology, announcing the Xbox Labs division, which CEO Asha Sharma described as "a small group of game makers, engineers and scientists whose job is to turn emerging ideas into things our players and developers can experience."
GTA 6 multiplayer is already running on PC
With swift progress on an unofficial GTA 6 PC port, teams are starting to go beyond replicating the console experience. X user TeK posted a video showing multiplayer running on a fork of the CGBJ build. The footage shows multiple players and vehicles navigating in the game world with proximity voice chat. The build runs at a stable 25 to 30 fps. This marks the first time a PC build of the game has shown multiplayer gameplay.
Not even Rockstar has spoken about GTA 6's multiplayer functionality, whose inclusion feels like a foregone conclusion given the popularity of GTA Online. While Rockstar and Take Two have taken down at least one leading Discord server in the PC port race, the use of AI tools has created something of a hydra, in which more heads always pop up after one is cut off. GTA 6 is already impressive on consoles, but a PC release is even more anticipated as leaks reveal it is likely to have greater graphical fidelity and performance.
