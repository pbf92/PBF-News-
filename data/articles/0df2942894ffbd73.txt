Why are Alta shares soaring today
What happened?
Shares of equipment distribution company Alta Equipment Group ALTG jumped 33.4% in the afternoon session after the company entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Jackson Group Peterbilt's commercial truck and trailer dealership business.
According to a Form 8-K filing, Alta's subsidiaries agreed to buy substantially all of the assets of the dealership business for $272.5 million plus a net tangible asset adjustment, subject to a post-closing true-up. The acquired network spans Peterbilt and other original equipment manufacturer dealerships in Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nevada, Oregon, Utah, Washington and Wyoming that offer sales, leasing, rental, servicing, customization, parts distribution, maintenance and financing services.
In the company's press release, Alta described Jackson Group Peterbilt as a full-service commercial truck dealership group with a footprint in the western United States and estimated the deal's enterprise value at approximately $399 million. That valuation represents approximately 6.3 times trailing 12-month adjusted EBITDA as of September 30, 2026, or $6.30 for each $1 of adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Accounting for approximately $40 million of estimated tax benefits lowers the multiple to approximately 5.7 times.
What is the market telling us
Alta’s shares are extremely volatile and have had 53 moves greater than 5% over the last year. But moves this big are rare even for Alta and indicate this news significantly impacted the market’s perception of the business.
The previous big move we wrote about was 14 days ago when the stock dropped 16.1% on the news that the company reported third-quarter revenue and adjusted earnings per share below analysts' expectations. According to a company press release, Alta reported revenue of $417.4 million for the third quarter of 2026, a decrease of 1.2% year over year. That revenue was below Wall Street analysts' consensus estimate of $453.9 million. The company's adjusted earnings per share was negative 19 cents, which was below the consensus estimate of negative 17 cents per share.
The release also said the company recorded a net loss available to common stockholders of $12.2 million, or 37 cents per basic and diluted share. Adjusted EBITDA was $44.9 million, below analyst estimates of $46.23 million. In addition, the company projected full-year EBITDA of $167.5 million at the midpoint. That projection was below analyst estimates of $171.4 million.
Alta is up 22.1% since the beginning of the year, but at $6.12 per share, it is still trading 26.3% below its 52-week high of $8.30 from May 2026. Despite the year-to-date gain, investors who bought $1,000 worth of Alta’s shares 5 years ago would now be looking at only $383.15.
