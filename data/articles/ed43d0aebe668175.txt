TITLE: Why Robert Pattinson Likely Can’t Play Batman and James Bond Together
OUTLET: VGC / IGN

Our long international nightmare is finally over. Amazon MGM Studios has selected a new actor to play James Bond, and it’s none other than former Twilight star Robert Pattinson. Despite being an actor who tends to prioritize quirky indie roles (while throwing in the occasional blockbuster for good measure), Pattinson has signed on to star in another of the film world’s biggest franchises.
This means Pattinson will now be in the unique position of playing both Batman and Bond. But can that arrangement last long-term, or is this a sign that the actor's days in Gotham City are coming to an end? Unfortunately, we think the latter possibility is the more likely one. Here’s why Pattinson is probably trading one superhero role for another, and why The Batman: Part II is almost certainly the end of the road for that particular series.
Robert Pattinson: From Batman to Bond
Pattinson’s Bond casting is certainly an interesting and unexpected development. MGM has tended to focus on up-and-coming actors for the various Bond films, whether it was a then little-known Sean Connery in 1962’s Dr. No or Daniel Craig in 2006’s Casino Royale. Early on, it seemed Amazon was going to continue operating under this philosophy, with relatively lesser-known actors like Callum Turner and Josh O’Connor emerging as frontrunners. Apparently, the studio had a change of heart.
Compared to past Bonds, Pattinson is already a huge name with a wide resume and multiple key franchises under his belt. This is basically like if the studio hired Christian Bale to take on the Bond role after 2005’s Batman Begins. It’s a lot of cinematic responsibility for one actor to shoulder.
Is there a world where Pattinson continues to juggle the dual responsibilities of playing Batman and Bond? Perhaps. The actor certainly seems to like keeping busy as an actor. In 2026 alone, he’s starred in The Drama, The Odyssey, and Primetime, and we still have his sci-fi heel turn in Dune: Part 3 coming up in December.
Still, it seems unlikely that even an actor as prolific as Pattinson is able or willing to juggle both James Bond and Bruce Wayne at the same time. Again, both roles are massive responsibilities that each require a huge time commitment, including the massive promotional push that comes after filming has wrapped.
Look at what happened to Mahershala Ali when he signed on to do Blade for Marvel Studios. Even though that project never came to be, Ali had to turn down a lot of work because he was so focused on training for the role and developing the film. Or look at Xochitl Gomez, who basically stopped getting offers after appearing in 2022’s Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness because casting agents assumed she was too tied up in the Marvel machine.
Being James Bond and being Batman are both comparable. Each role can keep the actor tied up for months or years at a time. The odds that one franchise might interfere with the other in the years ahead seem rather high. With many years already having passed since 2021’s No Time to Die, Amazon MGM likely wants to push forward quickly with the reboot.
We highly doubt an actor like Pattinson, who clearly values his freedom to pursue interesting and unusual projects in this post-Twilight phase of his career, has any real desire to attempt to juggle both Batman and Bond simultaneously. This Bond news simply signals the idea that Pattinson feels his days as Batman are coming to an end. Currently, Steven Soderbergh has stepped in to finish production on The Batman: Part II while Matt Reeves deals with a personal matter. Once that wraps in late 2026 or early 2027, we’re guessing Pattinson will most likely hang up the cape and cowl and move on to other projects, one of which is the long-awaited James Bond reboot. He’s just trading one blockbuster franchise for another.
The Problem With Matt Reeves’ Batman Universe
Mind you, Pattinson may not be moving on from Batman out of a waning interest in the character, but simply due to the reality on the ground. There’s a lot currently working against this iteration of the Batman franchise. As mentioned, director Matt Reeves has had to step away from production due to personal problems. It’s unclear how heavy a role Reeves will have in the film’s post-production. We’re sure a third movie isn’t high on his list of priorities at the moment.
The likelihood is that if the newly merged Skydance even wants a The Batman: Part III, they’d have to push forward without Reeves as writer or director if they don’t want another long gap between sequels. Do fans actually want a Reeves-less The Batman: Part III? Would Pattinson even be interested in returning in that eventuality? Losing this series’ defining creative voice seems like too big a hurdle to cross.
And that’s assuming Skydance wants The Batman: Part III to happen at all. Right now, we have no idea what direction they’re taking with the DC line after firing James Gunn and Peter Safran. We’d argue the current DCU continuity should stay in place, but there’s no guarantee that’ll happen. All we can say for sure is that Batman, easily the most bankable DC character in existence, is going to be a major priority for the studio going forward.
It stands to reason that executives aren’t thrilled with how long it’s taken to put out a sequel to 2022’s The Batman. And they’re probably also not fond of the fact that Reeves’ The Batman movies exist in their own little universe, while the Batman of Gunn and Safran’s DCU is still an enigma after all these years. Historically, DC has always shied away from allowing multiple incarnations of characters to coexist in live-action, and that approach may continue under Skydance.
That leaves Skydance with two options - either focus on the Pattinson/Reeves universe as the sole Batman going forward, or reboot the character and pivot to something relatively more family-friendly and with more box office potential. We tend to think the company would opt for the latter. Incoming DC Studios head Jeremy Latcham seems to prefer more uplifting and family-friendly movies, and it would be in keeping with Paramount’s recent approach to the TMNT franchise.
Basically, between Reeves’ ongoing personal problems and the general direction of the wind at Skydance, there’s a good chance that there’s no future for the “Batman Epic Crime Saga” after The Batman: Part II finally arrives in 2028. We’re not holding our breath for The Penguin Season 2 either. That’s likely why Pattinson agreed to play James Bond. He sees the writing on the wall for his version of Batman, and he’s moving on to greener pastures.
It’s a shame in many ways, but we suspect that Skydance’s loss will be Amazon MGM’s gain in the years to come. Pattinson may not be the most bold or exciting choice to play Bond, but we can’t imagine he won’t do well in the role. There are far worse outcomes for this venerable franchise than to have Pattinson and director Denis Villeneuve rebooting it for a new era.
