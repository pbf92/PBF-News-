TITLE: Amazon limits daily unified reports to 120 days of its 24-month history
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon Ads has published the limits on how far back its unified reporting system can reach, according to a Support Center page titled "Data availability in unified reporting" and updated on November 17, 2026. The page sets out two separate ceilings for each time granularity - how much historical data is held, and how much of it a single report can pull - for the Reporting app, the Reporting API and Amazon Marketing Stream.
The gap between those two numbers is widest for daily data. Reports broken down by date can draw on 24 months of history in the Reporting app and the Reporting API, yet a single report pull at that granularity covers no more than 120 days. Weekly reports hold the same 24 months but allow a 15-month pull. Monthly reports reach back 72 months, six years, with a 25-month pull. Only yearly and date-range summaries can pull the full 72 months in one go.
The page goes further than lookback windows. It lists five dimension groups and four metrics that start later than those limits, from Reach on July 1, 2024 to ad variation fields on October 1, 2026, and it explains what a report shows when a date range runs past them. It also carries a one-line note that will matter to anyone running video in Sponsored Products: only two video metrics report data for that format, and "Other video metrics may display 0 as a placeholder, which isn't the actual measurement."
In Short
Amazon has published how much history its unified reporting holds for each time granularity and how much a single report can pull, along with the dates from which certain fields start to exist. It affects every advertiser and agency moving reports into the new system before the old report pages close on January 31, 2027, because a date range that reaches past those limits returns nulls or partial totals without failing. The page also says Sponsored Products video reports real data only for 5-second views and 5-second view rate, and that zeros in its other video metrics are placeholders rather than measurements.
Two ceilings for every granularity
The page is built around a table with three tabs, one for each route into unified reporting: Reporting app UI, Amazon Marketing Stream and Reporting API. For each time dimension it lists the date range presets the report builder offers, then two columns: "Historical data" and "Max report pull." Amazon's stated purpose is to help advertisers "avoid accidental partial reporting when selecting date ranges."
The Reporting app and Reporting API tabs list identical values. They differ only in the column heading.
Source: Amazon Ads Support Center, Reporting app UI and Reporting API tabs, updated November 17, 2026.
The presets follow the pull limits. Daily reports stop at Last 90 days and This quarter, both inside 120 days; "This year" appears only from the day-of-week rows down, and "Last year" only for monthly, yearly and date-range reports.
What do the two ceilings mean in practice? By PPC Land's calculation, covering the full 24 months of daily history takes at least seven separate daily reports, since each one spans 120 days at most. Weekly history fits into two pulls, and the six years of monthly data into three. A daily comparison between one November and the November before it cannot sit inside a single daily report, because the two periods are more than 120 days apart; a weekly report, with its 15-month pull, can hold both.
Amazon Marketing Stream: 95 days of hours
The Marketing Stream tab is shorter. It lists two time dimensions, not eight.
Source: Amazon Ads Support Center, Amazon Marketing Stream tab, updated November 17, 2026.
Hourly history is the clearest difference between the routes. Amazon Marketing Stream, the Amazon Ads API feed that pushes hourly campaign data into an advertiser's or tool provider's AWS account, holds 95 days of hourly data, against 14 days in the Reporting app and the Reporting API. The Max report pull column is left blank for both Stream rows. The page does not say why, though Stream delivers data by push rather than in response to a report request.
The rest of the page - the limited-history tables, notes and restrictions - is the same on all three tabs.
What the launch said, and what the page says now
Amazon has described these windows before, in less detail. When unified reporting opened in beta at unBoxed on November 11, 2025, the figures given were two weeks for hourly reporting, 15 months for daily or weekly reporting and up to six years for monthly, yearly or summary-grain reporting. The 15-month and six-year figures were repeated when unified reporting reached general availability on June 8, 2026.
The new page splits each of those single numbers in two. Hourly data matches: 14 days held, 14 days per pull. For daily data, the 15 months no longer appears at all; the page puts history at 24 months and the per-report limit at 120 days. Fifteen months survives as the pull limit for weekly, day-of-week and day-of-month reports. Six years matches the historical figure for monthly data, but a monthly report can pull only 25 months of it at once.
The page has no change log, so it does not show whether the 24-month daily history is an extension made since June or the same store of data described differently. Either way, the single figure of 15 months once given for daily or weekly reporting now matches only the weekly pull limit, not the daily one.
Dimensions and metrics that start later
Below the main table, the page lists fields whose history is shorter than the standard limits. "These limits apply regardless of the time granularity selected for the report," it states.
Source: Amazon Ads Support Center, updated November 17, 2026. The page writes the dates in month/day/year form.
Measured against the November 17 update date, six of the eight start dates fall inside the 24-month daily window, by PPC Land's calculation; only the reconciled cost metrics and Reach predate it. All eight fall inside the 72-month window for monthly, yearly and date-range reports. A monthly report covering its full 25-month pull, run in November 2026, would begin in late 2024, before every one of the five dimension dates and before off-Amazon conversions.
Several of the dates line up with products PPC Land has covered, and several do not.
Frequency group. Amazon Ads added cross-campaign reach reporting for its Frequency Groups feature on December 23, 2024, through the Amazon DSP interface and API. Unified reporting holds frequency group data only from January 16, 2026. The page adds a qualifier on reach: "Reach metrics reported at the Frequency group level are limited to the Frequency group availability date (1/16/2026), not the general Reach availability date." Reach broken down by frequency group, a set of campaigns sharing a frequency capping rule, therefore starts about 18 months later than reach reported without that dimension. Cross-account reach and frequency reporting arrived in the unified Reporting beta on April 2, 2026.
Benchmark group. Data starts on June 1, 2025, more than five months before Amazon Ads introduced benchmark report types on November 18, 2025. Benchmarks reached general availability in 18 markets on May 18, 2026. The benchmark report itself appears on Amazon's list of ten report types not yet available in unified reporting, a list that sits on the migration page, while this page gives the benchmark group dimension an availability date. Neither page explains how the two fit together.
Off-Amazon conversions. The unified history begins on June 27, 2025. Amazon DSP had begun reporting modeled off-Amazon conversions for US advertisers on August 16, 2024, about ten months earlier.
Ad variation. Four fields - ID, type, prompt text and video type - start on October 1, 2026, 47 days before the page update. None of them was among the 156 attributes in the dimensions reference Amazon updated on November 11, 2026. The data availability page does not define an ad variation. A field named "Ad variation prompt text" points to variations produced from text prompts, but the page itself does not say so.
Reconciled cost and ROAS. Total cost (reconciled) and ROAS (reconciled) begin on August 4, 2024. The page does not define what reconciliation adds to the standard cost and return figures, or how the reconciled and unreconciled versions differ.
How partial data appears
Amazon describes what happens when a report reaches back past a field's start date, and the answer differs for dimensions and metrics. According to the page, the report builder handles limits in six ways:
- Maximum range by granularity. The date range available depends on the time dimension selected, as set out in the Historical data and Max report pull columns.
- Start dates override granularity. The availability dates for limited fields apply "regardless of the time granularity selected for the report."
- Dimensions go null. "If your report date range starts before the availability date, the affected dimension attributes will appear as null for dates outside the window. All other dimensions and metrics populate normally."
- Metrics go partial. "If your report date range starts before the availability date, the affected metrics will only reflect data from within the available window. This means aggregated totals may be partial. For example, a 6-month summary will only include data from the portion of that range where the metric was available."
- Restricted combinations grey out. "The report builder may prevent certain combinations of dimensions and metrics when the results would not be interpretable or would produce misleading data. When a dimension or metric is not available, it will appear greyed out. Hover over it to see which selection is causing the restriction."
- Permissions hide fields. "Some metrics and dimensions may be unavailable based on account or user permissions. Restricted fields will appear as null in report output." The page directs anyone who needs a restricted field to their account team or Amazon Ads support.
The fourth point carries the most risk for anyone reading totals. A six-month summary that includes off-Amazon conversions and starts before June 27, 2025 does not fail or warn on the page's account; it returns a smaller total covering only the months the metric existed. Nothing in the description says the report flags which totals are partial.
Sponsored Products video: two metrics and a placeholder
The note on video takes two sentences, in a box below the metrics table, right after the line on frequency group reach. "Only 5-second views and 5-second view rate (video ad) report data for Sponsored Products video. Other video metrics may display 0 as a placeholder, which isn't the actual measurement."
Sponsored Products video is not new. Amazon made the format available in the United States on November 11, 2025, allowing one to five feature videos per product. At launch Amazon cited internal data showing that, among the 20% of shoppers who watched for more than five seconds, click-through rates were eight times higher. The five-second threshold Amazon cited at launch is, a year later, the only video measure its unified reports carry for the format.
A placeholder zero is a different problem from a null. A null is visibly empty; a zero reads as a measurement. A report or dashboard that sums other video metrics across formats would, on the page's own wording, add placeholder zeros for Sponsored Products video to real figures from other formats. The page does not say which other video metrics are affected, or when they will carry real data.
The legacy route offers no alternative for long. The Sponsored Products Video report is one of the ten report types Amazon's migration page lists as not yet available in unified reporting, and the old sponsored ads report pages become read-only on December 17, 2026 and shut on January 31, 2027.
