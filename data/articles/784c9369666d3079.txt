TITLE: Study Defines Core Attributes of the Planetary Health Diet
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A concept analysis published in BMC Nutrition clarifies the defining characteristics of the planetary health diet, a dietary reference pattern designed to address both human health and environmental sustainability. The study, authored by Shao Mengyao, Lu Min, and Wang Yanmei, was conducted to resolve uncertainty regarding the diet’s conceptual interpretation and operationalisation, particularly following the 2025 EAT-Lancet framework update. By synthesising existing literature, the researchers aimed to establish a clear framework that can inform future nursing research and practice.
The research team applied Rodgers’ evolutionary concept analysis method to review 69 articles. This systematic approach allowed the authors to identify and synthesise defining attributes, antecedents, consequences, related concepts, and empirical referents.
The analysis identified three primary defining attributes of the planetary health diet. First, it possesses a dual-goal orientation that operates within planetary boundaries, balancing health outcomes with environmental limits. Second, the diet features a predominantly plant-based dietary structure. Third, it provides quantitative guidance for the consumption of animal-source foods.
Antecedents to the adoption of the planetary health diet include the burden of diet-related non-communicable diseases and environmental pressures associated with global food systems. The literature also highlights interdisciplinary scientific and policy developments as key drivers. The study noted associations between adherence to the planetary health diet and favourable health outcomes. Additionally, it identified potential environmental benefits, although these are supported primarily by modelling studies rather than direct observational data.
The analysis also addressed justice-related implications, focusing on affordability and equitable implementation. The researchers distinguished justice as a normative consideration within the broader food-system framework, rather than an independently established defining dietary attribute. Existing dietary indices were found to provide empirical measures of adherence but do not comprehensively assess realised health, environmental, or justice-related outcomes.
The study concludes that the concept analysis clarifies the defining characteristics and boundaries of the planetary health diet, proposing a framework that may inform nursing dietary assessment and patient education. However, the authors emphasise that further research is needed to validate culturally appropriate assessment instruments, evaluate nursing applications, and examine equitable implementation.
