TITLE: FuboTV drops quarterly subscriber reporting at 5.75 million
OUTLET: PPC Land

FuboTV Inc. today reported fourth-quarter revenue of $1.503 billion and said it will no longer publish its subscriber count. That ends one of the few public measures of reach for what the company describes as the second-largest virtual pay TV operator in the United States. North American advertising revenue rose 22% to $119.1 million in the three months to September 30, 2026. According to FuboTV, the rise came from higher CPMs and fill rates, "including as a result of the integration of Fubo ad inventory on the Disney Advertising Server."
In Short
FuboTV, the Disney-controlled company behind the Fubo and Hulu + Live TV streaming services, closed its first full fiscal year after the merger with $6.1 billion in revenue and, for the first time since the merger, a quarter of positive free cash flow. Ad sales grew 22% in the latest quarter after Fubo's ads moved onto Disney's ad technology, while paying subscribers in North America fell from 6.01 million to 5.75 million in a year. From the next quarter the company will stop reporting how many subscribers it has, so advertisers and investors will have revenue and profit figures to go on, not audience counts.
The quarter in numbers
The fourth fiscal quarter was FuboTV's third full quarter as a combined company. Fubo and Disney's Hulu + Live TV business closed their combination on October 29, 2025. Because the Hulu Live business was treated as the accounting acquirer, the reported comparisons with fiscal 2025 compare different businesses. FuboTV therefore also publishes pro forma figures, which treat the deal as if it had closed at the start of the earliest period shown.
On the pro forma basis, revenue barely changed. Quarterly revenue of $1,503.1 million was $1.4 million above the pro forma $1,501.7 million of a year earlier, a rise of about 0.1% by PPC Land's calculation. Against the reported prior-year figure of $1,107.7 million, which covers only the Hulu Live business, revenue rose 35.7%.
The net loss of $51.8 million was larger than the reported $38.8 million loss a year earlier and smaller than the pro forma $96.3 million. Of that loss, $37.2 million went to the non-controlling interest, leaving $14.6 million for common shareholders. With 33.4 million weighted average shares, that works out to the $0.44 loss per share. FuboTV reported 30,213,935 Class A shares and 78,992,518 vote-only Class B shares outstanding at the end of the quarter.
According to FuboTV, the quarter's adjusted EBITDA of $12.3 million was its "highest-ever fourth quarter Adjusted EBITDA." The quarterly sequence is less flattering. Adjusted EBITDA was $41.4 million in the first fiscal quarter (pro forma), $37.7 million in the second, $19.1 million in the third and $12.3 million in the fourth. The fourth quarter was the weakest of the year. Its margin was 0.8%, against 2.5% in the December quarter.
Most of the money goes to content. Subscriber-related expenses, including $494.9 million paid to related parties, totaled $1,374.2 million in the quarter. That is 91.4% of revenue, by PPC Land's calculation, down from 99.8% a year earlier on the reported basis. Sales and marketing cost $70.3 million and technology and development $21.8 million.
Fiscal year 2026
Pro forma adjusted EBITDA of $110.6 million beat the $90 million to $100 million range that FuboTV had raised the previous quarter, as PPC Land reported in August. Chief financial officer John Janedis said on the earnings call that the upside was "led by better-than-expected advertising sales, which saw significant year-over-year increases in both CPMs and fill rates," according to a transcript published by Investing.com.
The full-year cash picture is weaker than the fourth quarter suggests. The four quarterly free cash flow figures add up to an outflow of about $390.1 million, after outflows of $204.0 million and $214.7 million in the first two quarters. FuboTV ended the year with $272.4 million in cash and restricted cash, including $266.3 million of cash and cash equivalents, compared with its target of at least $200 million. The cash flow statement shows $394.5 million in "Pre-combination net contributions from Disney," a $145.0 million related-party note and $144.8 million in convertible note repayments. Convertible notes of $177.5 million and $145.0 million of related-party notes remained on the balance sheet.
Advertising: what the Disney Ad Server changed
Advertising is a small part of FuboTV's revenue, about 7.9% in the quarter by PPC Land's calculation. It is also the line that moved most. The shareholder letter devotes one paragraph to it: "Reported advertising revenue was $119.1 million, compared to pro forma advertising revenue of $97.5 million in the comparable prior-year period, an increase of 22% year-over-year."
The trend over fiscal 2026 shows how much the quarter changed. In the March quarter, advertising revenue was flat at $101.6 million, and FuboTV described the migration to the Disney ad server as ongoing, PPC Land reported in May. In the June quarter, advertising revenue slipped to $108.9 million from a pro forma $109.4 million, even with the World Cup on the schedule. That was the first full three-month period with Fubo inventory on the Disney Ad Server, according to PPC Land's coverage of the third quarter. The fourth quarter's $119.1 million is 9.3% above the third quarter by PPC Land's calculation, after a flat second quarter and a slight decline in the third.
Janedis gave more detail on the call. "During the quarter, we did see double-digit growth in CPMs and a meaningful improvement in the fill," he said, according to the Investing.com transcript. "So in terms of conversions from a CPM perspective, we've actually largely converged already." He was answering a question about whether Fubo's ad revenue per user would reach the Hulu + Live TV level after the server move. He added that, "given Fubo's relative exposure to sports, there was a tailwind during the fourth quarter," and that in-season football inventory "is in high demand, and pricing is trending up year-over-year."
Chief executive Alisa Bowen pointed to non-sports inventory. "In the last quarter, we saw real improvements in the sale of our entertainment inventory on the Fubo platform," she said. She credited "Disney's scale as it relates to audience sales and audience tracking across different platforms, as well as Disney's ad platform targeting capabilities."
She also listed what Disney's ad platform will bring next: "new targeting capabilities, the ability to automate ad plans, the ability for advertisers to generate creative in the platform." On ad load, she said: "Ad loads is an area that we will continue to take a look at." She added that "specifically as it relates to the consumption of VOD content and other non-broadcast content, there may be an opportunity for us to iterate and extract some incremental value there." That is a hint, not a plan. More ads in on-demand viewing would add inventory for buyers and more interruptions for viewers.
For buyers, the arrangement matters because Fubo's inventory now runs on the same technology as Disney's own. Since early 2025, Disney has moved more of its supply onto shared systems. In April 2025, Disney made live content from Hulu and Disney+ available through programmatic integrations, with its Real-Time Ad Exchange supporting Google's Display & Video 360 Instant Deals. In June 2026, Disney moved its EMEA campaigns onto its global Ad Server. Fubo's own sponsorship business has grown too. In July, Fubo opened six shows from The Athletic's new video hub to sponsors, with home screen takeovers and interactive CTV ads among the formats.
The letter makes one forward-looking statement about advertising: "We foresee continued momentum within our advertising business during the ongoing fall sports and holiday programming seasons." It gives no figure.
Subscribers: the last count
The other headline sits in a section of the letter called "Measuring What Matters." FuboTV said: "Beginning with our first quarter of fiscal 2027, we will no longer report Total Subscribers on a quarterly or annual basis." The first quarter of fiscal 2027 ends on December 31, 2026.
The letter defines the metric being dropped, word for word: "Total Subscribers represent the total number of subscribers to our live TV streaming services, including Fubo and Hulu + Live TV, who have completed registration, have activated a payment method (reflecting one paying subscriber per plan), and from whom payment was collected during the month ending the relevant period. Subscribers participating in free or trial offerings are excluded from this metric."
Its reasoning is that "All subscribers do not deliver equal returns to the business." The press release adds: "As the Company's packaging and subscription pricing options have broadened, it believes Total Subscribers is now less reflective of underlying performance than it was previously." The company will keep reporting revenue, net income, adjusted EBITDA, operating cash flow and free cash flow. "From time to time, we may disclose subscriber milestones or other periodic updates, but we do not expect to do so on a regular basis," the letter says.
The final count was 5.75 million subscribers in North America, flat on the June quarter and down from 6.01 million a year earlier. That is about 260,000 fewer, or 4.3%. Over fiscal 2026 the count moved from 6.18 million at December 31, 2025, to 5.73 million at March 31, 2026, then held at 5.75 million in June and September. The Rest of World business ended at 327,000, down from 356,000 in June and 342,000 a year earlier.
Janedis presented the change on the call as following "the approach previously taken by many of our industry peers." Bowen said the company would prioritize "heavily engaged subscribers and higher value plan tiers." She described those viewers this way: "Since the start of the 2026 NFL season, half our audience on our core plans have watched nearly 50 hours a week on Fubo."
The change matters for media buyers. Subscriber totals were the plainest public signal of the audience behind a vMVPD's inventory. From January, outsiders will have to judge Fubo's reach from revenue, from what Disney's sales teams share, and from occasional milestone announcements.
Strategy: profitable growth as the "North Star"
The fourth quarter is the first full reporting period under Bowen. FuboTV named her chief executive on July 9, 2026, effective the next day. She came from Disney, where she most recently led Disney+, and succeeded co-founder David Gandler. Her letter describes a business review whose conclusion she put plainly on the call: "Our review concluded that fuboTV's strategy is sound. What it needs is sharper execution."
The letter sets out three "strategic imperatives":
- Core audiences. Keep winning with "highly-engaged sports subscribers on Fubo and general entertainment subscribers on Hulu + Live TV." The letter says that "Sports are a key driver of acquisition, but our best retaining subscribers are as engaged with our news and entertainment offerings as they are with sports."
- Beyond pay TV. Build new content and experiences "especially through mobile, that will open new vectors of growth including advertising and transaction revenue models." On the call, Bowen said mobile "accounts for half of all U.S. video viewing time today" and described the Fubo mobile app as "a companion product and a second screen."
- Efficiency. The strategic review covered North American operations. The review of international operations "is ongoing." Bowen told analysts: "We're very committed to prioritizing our resources to support the U.S. business."
