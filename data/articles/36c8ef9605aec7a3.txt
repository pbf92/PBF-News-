Poll Finds Fewer Americans Say Crime Rising
A Gallup survey conducted October 1-19 found 46% of U.S. adults say national crime increased over the past year, down sharply from prior years.
Updated on Nov. 21, 2026 in Violence

Forty-six percent of U.S. adults say crime has increased nationally over the past year, down from 77% in 2023 and 49% last year, according to a Gallup poll conducted October 1-19. The perception shift comes as federal data showed violent crime fell 9.3% in 2025 from the previous year.
Why it matters
The gap between public perception and official statistics shows how sharply sentiment about crime can diverge from measured trends. Thirty-three percent of adults say local crime increased, suggesting national fears may be more pronounced than personal experience.
The FBI reported the murder rate fell 18.5% to 4.1 killings per 100,000 people in 2025, while preliminary BJS data showed aggravated assaults down 31% and robberies down 27%. Final figures for the BJS measures have not yet been released.
The players
Gallup
Gallup is a global analytics and advisory company known for its public opinion polling on social and political issues.
Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI)
The FBI is the principal federal law enforcement agency of the United States, which compiles national crime statistics.
The details
Gallup asked U.S. adults about crime trends during fieldwork from October 1 to 19. Results show 70% worry at least occasionally about identity theft and 55% worry about being tricked by scammers into sending money or account access.
Timeline
- 2022: A record 56% of adults said local crime was rising.
- October 1-19 (2026): Gallup survey fieldwork was conducted.
- 2025: FBI and BJS data recorded declines in violent crime.
Seasonal Patterns
The 2026 poll results sit against Gallup's 2022 record of 56% saying local crime was rising, a high that has since eased. The drop in perceived crime tracks the 2025 federal declines in murder, robbery and aggravated assault.
Public concern about scams and identity theft is high, with 70% and 55% of adults worried, so readers may want to verify requests for money or account access. Because perceptions of crime have fallen, personal safety decisions may be better informed by current local data than by national headlines.
The takeaway
Public perception of crime often lags behind measured trends, so checking current statistics can offer a more accurate picture. Staying alert to scams remains worthwhile even as violent crime declines.
