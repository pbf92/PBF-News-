TITLE: Vascular Closure Devices Show Similar Efficacy in Large-Bore Endovascular Repair
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and meta-analysis evaluating vascular closure devices found no statistically significant difference in safety or efficacy between plug-based and suture-based systems used for large-bore femoral access during endovascular repair. Researchers evaluated comparative outcomes between the plug-based MANTA device and the suture-based ProGlide and ProStyle devices across published retrospective cohort studies.
The clinical investigation addressed an ongoing question in cardiovascular intervention regarding the optimal approach to securing femoral access sites following large-bore endovascular procedures.
To evaluate comparative device performance, the investigators conducted a comprehensive literature search spanning PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from each database’s inception through August 1, 2026. To synthesize comparative outcomes across the included studies, the authors employed random-effects models to calculate pooled odds ratios accompanied by 95 percent confidence intervals.
The systematic review identified six retrospective cohort studies encompassing a total of 1,691 femoral access sites. Patient numbers were documented in only three of the included studies, accounting for 445 individual patients. The remaining included investigations reported findings exclusively by access-site counts or omitted procedure-specific patient data, highlighting a key structural characteristic in the available observational literature.
Technical success, the primary efficacy endpoint of the meta-analysis, was evaluated across 1,630 access sites, consisting of 936 MANTA sites and 694 ProGlide or ProStyle sites. The analysis revealed comparable success rates between the devices: 91.9 percent, or 860 of 936 sites, for the MANTA device, compared with 90.9 percent, or 631 of 694 sites, for the ProGlide and ProStyle devices. The pooled odds ratio was 1.01 with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 0.55 to 1.88, demonstrating no statistically significant difference (p = 0.96), alongside moderate statistical heterogeneity indicated by an I-squared value of 54 percent.
Secondary safety and clinical outcomes similarly demonstrated no statistically significant differences across any assessed metric. Vascular complications, evaluated across 1,519 access sites, showed an odds ratio of 0.91 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.46 to 1.79. Post-procedural bleeding or hematoma, assessed across 1,167 access sites, yielded an odds ratio of 0.70 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.40 to 1.22. Furthermore, pseudoaneurysm formation across 1,630 access sites yielded an odds ratio of 1.07 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.47 to 2.42, while the incidence of surgical intervention across 1,630 access sites showed an odds ratio of 1.08 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.69 to 1.68. Notably, an I-squared value of 0 percent was recorded across all evaluated secondary outcomes, indicating an absence of detected statistical heterogeneity.
Despite the balanced numerical outcomes observed between the two device classes, the investigators emphasized several critical constraints inherent to the synthesized evidence base. Because all included data derived from retrospective cohort studies with limited study numbers and inconsistent patient reporting, the overall certainty of evidence was categorized as very low. Consequently, the research team concluded that these meta-analytic findings should be interpreted strictly as exploratory, highlighting that the results warrant prospective confirmation.
The systematic review was conducted by researchers from Chengdu Fifth People’s Hospital and Chengdu University of Traditional Chinese Medicine in Chengdu, China. The investigative team, led by corresponding author Zuoyi Yao alongside Qian Zhang, Ke Guo, Yuping Liu, Wei Wei, Kan Zhao, and Haijun Huang, published the findings in Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine within the journal’s cardiovascular surgery section after submission in October 2026 and acceptance in November 2026.
