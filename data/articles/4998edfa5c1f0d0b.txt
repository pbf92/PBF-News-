TITLE: Google Search asks for the wallet, after the inbox and the photo album
OUTLET: PPC Land

Google today began rolling out an option that lets people connect Google Wallet to Personal Intelligence in AI Mode, giving the conversational search product read access to their spending and to the payment cards linked through Wallet. Google Drive and the Gemini app are set to follow "in the coming weeks," according to a post by Robby Stein, vice president of product for Google Search, which also says AI Overviews and AI Mode will start producing responses tailored to a person's recent search context.
The three new sources join Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Photos, which could already be connected. Each connection is opt-in. The post, published on the News from Google site under the headline "A more tailored Google Search that's uniquely yours," runs to about 520 words. It names no countries, no age limits and no subscription tiers, and it does not mention advertising.
In Short
Google Search can now, if a person agrees, read their Google Wallet spending and card details to tailor answers in AI Mode, and Google Drive files and Gemini app chats are due to follow within weeks. This matters because Google's privacy policy, revised on October 16, says data that people let Search access may also be used to personalize ads, and AI Mode already carries ads in 11 countries. Money, documents and chatbot conversations now join email, calendars and photos as possible inputs to Google's search answers, and Google has not said whether the new data will also shape the ads shown beside them.
What Google added today
Stein's post sets out three connections, each with its own timing.
Google Wallet comes first. "Starting to roll out today, you can opt in to securely connect Google Wallet to Personal Intelligence in AI Mode," the post says. Google describes the purpose in financial terms: users can "map out your financial milestones and identify actionable ways to trim everyday spending," and "maximize credit card benefits - like optimizing cash-back categories, travel perks, and reward points - based on your cards linked via Wallet." The post states a limit in the same paragraph: "If you choose to connect your Wallet, AI Mode can only read information from your accounts; it can't move, manage, or invest your money."
Google Drive follows in the coming weeks. According to the post, information needed for a project or decision "can be scattered across your docs, sheets, or PDFs," and with Drive connected, responses "take all of that context into account." Google's example is "a personalized preparation plan ahead of tax season."
The Gemini app is the third. "In the coming weeks you can easily share context across the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search," according to the post. "So if you explore an idea in the Gemini app, AI Mode will have that foundation to build on, and vice versa." The traffic already ran one way. "While you could already opt in to connect your Search history to Personal Intelligence in the Gemini app, soon you can also opt in to securely connect your Gemini app to Personal Intelligence in AI Mode."
Taken together, the post says, the three bring "context from your spending, documents, and chats directly to Search."
A fourth change does not depend on connected apps at all. "You'll also start seeing more personally relevant responses in AI Overviews and AI Mode, tailored to your recent search context," Stein wrote. In Google's example, someone who has been researching a trip "can pick up right where you left off in a new search thread without starting over or repeating yourself."
How the connection works, according to Google
The post describes the setup and the controls in one paragraph, which sets out the following sequence.
- A user opens Search Personalization. The post links that step to google.com/search-personalization.
- The user selects Connected Apps.
- The user chooses which apps to connect. According to the post, the user decides "if and when you opt in."
- Each connection can be turned "on or off at any time."
- "Soon," according to Google, a "Personalization" icon below any tailored response will show the context AI Mode used.
- Corrections happen inside the conversation. Google's example is a reply in the thread saying "I'm no longer a vegetarian."
- Personalization based on recent search context, in both AI Overviews and AI Mode, can be adjusted or turned off "at any time" in Search Settings.
Step 5 is not entirely new ground. In February, PPC Land reported that source icons for Gmail, Google Photos, YouTube and Search had begun appearing beside AI Mode and Gemini answers that drew on Personal Intelligence, after Glenn Gabe documented them on January 28. Today's post describes the Personalization icon as a way to "view the context AI Mode uses," without saying how it relates to those earlier icons.
What the Wallet connection reads
The Wallet link reached the Gemini app first. In a post dated November 13, ten days before today's announcement, Google said it was rolling out the connection to all Gemini app users in the US on the web, Android and iOS. That post also described optional linking of credit card and bank accounts to Wallet through Plaid for more detailed insights, and set out the same read-only limit: Gemini can read account information but cannot move, manage or invest money. Today's post extends that connection from the Gemini app to AI Mode, the conversational search product.
Google's Gemini help page on Connected Apps is more specific about the data than either post. It defines Google Wallet as including Wallet and payments services, and lists the data involved as payment methods, data from Google services saved in Wallet, and financial account information linked through services such as Plaid. According to the page, Gemini does not train directly on Wallet data, but summaries and inferences drawn from transactions can be used to answer prompts and to train models. The page says "Interactions, summaries, excerpts, and inferences from your Google Wallet and payment services information" can be seen by human reviewers. It also tells users not to rely on Gemini's output for financial advice and to consult a professional. Today's Search post, by contrast, presents "actionable ways to trim everyday spending" and planning for "financial milestones" as the reason to connect.
The Search side of the documentation has not caught up. Google's Search help page on connecting content apps, checked today, lists Google Workspace "(currently Gmail and Google Calendar, with other Workspace apps coming soon)" and Google Photos. It does not mention Wallet, Drive or the Gemini app, and a banner at the top says Google is updating settings for Search services, so the page may not match what users see. That page restricts connected apps to users aged 18 and over with personal Google Accounts.
Wallet was already part of AI Mode, though in a different role. When Google introduced the Universal Commerce Protocol in January, checkout inside AI Mode and the Gemini app for eligible US retailers was built on Google Pay, with payment and shipping details saved in Google Wallet. Until today, Wallet supplied the means to pay at the end of a search. With the new connection, it can also supply the history of what was paid for, which AI Mode reads before the answer is written.
Gemini and Search, joined in both directions
The Gemini app link changes the relationship between Google's two consumer AI products. Search history has fed Personal Intelligence in the Gemini app since the beta launched on January 14, 2026, when Google connected Gmail, Google Photos, YouTube and Search for AI Pro and AI Ultra subscribers in the US. Today's post makes the flow run in both directions.
The two products differ in one respect that matters to advertisers. AI Mode carries ads. The Gemini app does not, and in January Google's vice president of global ads, Dan Taylor, told Business Insider that there were no plans for ads in the Gemini app. Once a user opts in, context built up in an ad-free product will be available to one that sells ads. Stein's post does not say whether that context can inform the ads in AI Mode, only that it informs the responses.
AI Overviews begin to vary by searcher
The least detailed part of the announcement may reach the most people. AI Overviews, the AI summaries at the top of standard results, had more than 2 billion monthly users as of January. Today's post says those summaries, along with AI Mode, will become "more personally relevant" based on "recent search context."
The post gives no mechanics. It does not say how far back "recent" reaches, whether the change applies only to signed-in users, or in which countries it is rolling out. It describes one control: personalization can be adjusted or switched off in Search Settings.
For publishers and search marketers, the consequence is a measurement one. Tracking whether a page is cited in AI Overviews already depends on sampling answers that change from query to query. If the summary also shifts with each person's recent searches, two people typing the same words may see different overviews citing different sources, and a tracking tool working from a clean, unpersonalized session sees neither of them. Google's post does not say how much an overview can change, or whether personalization affects which sources are cited.
In April, Google's search chief Nick Fox described Personal Intelligence as opt-in and said not all users enable it, an outcome he considered correct. The recent-context change reads differently. The post presents it as something users will "start seeing," with Search Settings as the place to turn it off, rather than as a connection users choose.
Where the ads fit
Google's post is silent on advertising. Its privacy policy is not.
Since October 16, 2026, the policy has contained this sentence: "For example, Search may process some of the data that you allow Search to access to personalize content, including ads." The same revision changed a promise Google had kept since July 2021. The bullet now reads: "We don't show you personalized ads based on your content from Drive, Gmail, or Google Photos, unless you choose to make data from these services available to other Google services that support personalized ads." Drive, one of the three services named in that bullet, is among the apps Google says will connect to AI Mode in the coming weeks.
Google's Search help page describes how connected data reaches ads. "Ads will not be based directly on your Gmail inbox or your Google Photos library," it says. Search services instead work from summaries, excerpts and inferences, and according to the page, "That same info may be used to show you ads, so they are even more helpful for you." The page adds that "advertisers will not have access to your Gmail inbox or Google Photos."
Those lines name Gmail and Google Photos. The policy sentence is broader: it covers "some of the data that you allow Search to access," without listing apps. Google has not said, in today's post, in the Search help page or in the Gemini help page on Wallet, whether spending records, Drive files or Gemini conversations connected to AI Mode can inform ad personalization. The Wallet help page for Gemini does not mention ads at all, which is consistent with Gemini carrying none.
The ads question is no longer hypothetical for Personal Intelligence users. In March, Google had said AI Mode with Personal Intelligence switched on carried no ads. In October it confirmed it was testing ads in AI Mode for users with Personal Intelligence, while saying that sensitive information would not be used to tailor them. Google's sensitive categories commitment in the privacy policy lists examples "such as race, religion, sexual orientation, or health." Financial circumstances are not among the examples given.
