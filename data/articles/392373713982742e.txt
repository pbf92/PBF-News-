TITLE: Pinterest sells feed and search ad space through Microsoft Advertising
OUTLET: PPC Land

Pinterest today announced an integration that lets eligible campaigns bought through Microsoft Advertising run in Pinterest's home feed, search results and related Pins, placed by what the company calls a "custom contextual bidding model" built on Pinterest search queries and its own intent signals. Advertisers do not need a new buying tool. According to Pinterest, the integration will be available in North America, Australia and New Zealand "to start."
The post, published on the Pinterest Newsroom under the headline "Pinterest expands search advertising reach through custom Microsoft Advertising integration" and tagged Ads and Partnerships, quotes one executive from each company. It gives no launch date, no pricing, no list of eligible campaign types and no campaign results. Europe, where Pinterest's revenue stopped growing in the third quarter, is absent from the market list, exactly as it was when Pinterest opened its inventory to outside buying platforms four weeks ago.
In Short
Pinterest says advertisers who buy through Microsoft Advertising will be able to extend eligible campaigns into its home feed, search results and related Pins, with a custom contextual bidding model choosing where the ads appear. It matters to search advertisers looking for more places to spend from one platform, and to Pinterest, which gains another source of bids after its average ad price fell 4% in the third quarter. The integration starts in North America, Australia and New Zealand, not Europe, and Pinterest has published no start date, prices or eligibility rules.
What Pinterest announced
The core of the announcement is a single sentence. "Microsoft Advertising customers' campaigns will have access to Pinterest's visual search environment through a custom contextual bidding model," according to Pinterest. The next sentence defines the inventory: "Eligible Microsoft Advertising campaigns can access Pinterest's open-auction inventory across home feed, search, and related Pins, expanding opportunities to engage consumers throughout their discovery journeys."
Two terms in those sentences carry most of the weight. Open auction is the programmatic term for impressions sold to any qualified bidder in real time, as opposed to reserved inventory or negotiated deals. Eligible means some Microsoft campaigns qualify and others do not, though the post never says which.
The model that decides placement is described in more detail. It is "built directly atop Pinterest search queries, contextual signals, and first party intent data" and "identifies relevant ad placements across Pinterest's visual search adjacent inventory," according to Pinterest. That mix is broader than contextual advertising in its narrow sense, which matches an ad to the content of the page in front of the user. Here, the context is joined by Pinterest's own first-party data on what users have searched for, saved and clicked.
Behind it sits Pinterest Intelligence, the label Pinterest now uses for its recommendation and modelling stack. The post describes it as "Pinterest's AI-powered understanding of people's interests, combining hundreds of billions of connections with visual-understanding technology and behavioral signals to surface personalized interests and anticipate what users may want next."
The delivery route is described only in general terms. "Utilizing programmatic infrastructure as an effective delivery mechanism, the integration provides advertisers access to Pinterest's distinct inventory without requiring new buying tools," the post says. It does not name the exchange, supply-side platform or protocol involved.
The integration at a glance
How the integration works, according to Pinterest
The post describes no setup path, menu, opt-in setting or review process. It describes the mechanism in five parts, in this order:
- Demand originates in Microsoft Advertising. The post says the integration "includes advertiser demand originating from Microsoft's first-party media ecosystem including, Bing, MSN, Xbox, Microsoft Casual Games and Microsoft Outlook."
- Eligible campaigns gain access to Pinterest inventory. Qualifying campaigns can reach Pinterest's open-auction inventory in the home feed, in search and in related Pins.
- A contextual bidding model selects placements. The model uses Pinterest search queries, contextual signals and first-party intent data to identify relevant placements across what Pinterest calls its "visual search adjacent inventory."
- Pinterest Intelligence supplies interest signals. The model draws on what Pinterest describes as "hundreds of billions of connections," visual-understanding technology and behavioural signals.
- Delivery and measurement run through existing workflows. Ads are delivered through programmatic infrastructure, and advertisers keep their current buying tools and "familiar measurement tools," according to Jason Fairchild of Pinterest.
The order matters for one reason. The bidding model is described as "custom" and built on Pinterest data, but the post does not say who operates it, whether it replaces or sits alongside Microsoft's own automated bidding, or whether advertisers can see or adjust its bids. That question is not academic. Five days ago, Microsoft began rolling out what it calls the biggest upgrade to its bidding models in a decade, and the post does not explain how Pinterest placements fit with it.
A search headline over a broader buy
Pinterest's headline frames the deal as an expansion of "search advertising reach." The body describes something wider. The inventory covers the home feed and related Pins as well as search results, and the demand sources named include MSN, Xbox, Microsoft Casual Games and Outlook, none of which is a search engine.
So what, exactly, does a search advertiser buy? Read literally, the demand sentence lists the Microsoft properties where participating advertisers already buy media, rather than describing Pinterest ads appearing on those properties. Demand runs one way: from Microsoft's advertisers to Pinterest's inventory. What the post leaves open is how a keyword-driven search campaign would map onto a Pinterest search query, or whether eligibility is limited to audience, native or Performance Max style campaigns that already buy across several surfaces.
There is a reverse precedent. Microsoft Advertising already offers a tool that imports campaigns from Pinterest Ads into its own platform, so advertisers running on Pinterest could copy their structure into Microsoft's system. Today's integration moves budget in the opposite direction.
The figures behind the pitch
Pinterest supports the announcement with four figures. All four have appeared in its earlier materials, and the post marks three of them with footnote numbers without publishing the footnote text.
The 661 million figure is current: Pinterest reported it with third-quarter results on October 29. The 80 billion searches figure is older. Pinterest cited about 80 billion monthly searches as early as its third-quarter 2025 results, In the year since, its user base grew by 61 million, by PPC Land's calculation, while the cited search figure stayed at 80 billion.
The unbranded share is the oldest. Pinterest used the same 96% when it launched Top of Search ads in September 2025, and the footnote attached to the figure in its October 26 materials, as PPC Land reported, said it was calculated from the top 1,000 searches globally in June 2023. A sample of the most common queries, more than three years old, is not the same as a share of all searches.
For search advertisers, though, the unbranded figure is the relevant one, because unbranded queries are where advertisers compete for people who have not yet chosen a brand. Pinterest's argument is that its searches sit earlier in the purchase process, "while they are still exploring ideas and shaping purchase decisions," as the post puts it. The post adds that Pinterest has "a predominantly mobile audience," without giving a share.
Two executives, one message
Both quotes in the post focus on workflow rather than performance.
"Now, Microsoft Advertising customers don't have to choose between streamlined buying and the environments they want to be in most," said Jason Fairchild, GM of Programmatic and Affiliate of Pinterest. "We're bringing premium inventory and Pinterest intent signals into the workflows advertisers already use, with the potential to extend existing campaigns, reach incremental audiences and use familiar measurement tools."
Fairchild joined Pinterest through its agreement to acquire tvScientific, the connected TV performance platform he ran, announced in December 2025. He also fronted Pinterest's Programmatic Deals launch on October 26.
"Pinterest offers advertisers access to a combination of visual search discovery, commercial intent and premium inventory," said Jason Mckay, general manager of Microsoft Advertising Global Partnerships. "Through this integration, eligible Microsoft Advertising customers can extend campaigns into Pinterest inventory using familiar buying and measurement workflows."
The word "potential" in Fairchild's quote is doing real work. The post offers no test campaign, no overlap data and no lift figures, which sets it apart from the Programmatic Deals launch four weeks earlier. That announcement came with a Moroccanoil case study reporting 15% incremental reach and a 1% audience overlap, based on Amazon internal data from June 2026. Today's claims of incremental audiences rest on description alone.
Another outside route into Pinterest inventory
Pinterest has spent three and a half years letting other companies' advertisers into its auction. Amazon became its first third-party ad demand partner in April 2023. Google followed with a partnership announced alongside fourth-quarter 2023 results in February 2024. Index Exchange and Criteo appeared in Pinterest's ads.txt file between November 2024 and January 2025.
The pace has picked up since the autumn. On October 26, Pinterest made Programmatic Deals generally available, putting selected display and video inventory inside outside demand-side platforms including Amazon Ads DVA+, Google's Display & Video 360, Yahoo DSP and DeepIntent. The Microsoft integration is a different kind of route. It reaches advertisers who buy through a search and native platform rather than a demand-side platform, and it covers open-auction inventory rather than deals.
Programmatic Deals details from PPC Land's October 26 coverage of Pinterest's announcement.
On the third-quarter call on October 29, chief executive Bill Ready said the DSP partners represented about two-thirds of the DSP market and cautioned that such demand takes time to tune and ramp, according to PPC Land's account of the call. The same quarter showed why more bidders matter to Pinterest. It served 17% more ads at an average price 4% lower than a year earlier, and revenue from European users was flat at $193 million after cross-border sellers pulled back spending.
That makes Europe's absence notable. Both new routes into Pinterest's auction exclude the region where demand is weakest, and neither announcement gives a reason or a date.
Microsoft's side of the arrangement
For Microsoft, Pinterest is supply. The company no longer runs a demand-side platform of its own: it announced in May 2025 that it would shut Microsoft Invest, the former Xandr DSP, and the shutdown took effect on February 28, 2026. Microsoft Advertising, the platform that began as Bing Ads, remains its self-serve buying platform for advertisers.
That platform has been adding places to spend. In the past fortnight alone, Microsoft introduced Xbox Video for Brands, a non-skippable video unit that plays before free cloud-gaming sessions, on November 12, and on November 19 said its new Discover content feed shows 35% fewer ads per page than the classic MSN experience, while ads in AI Overviews on Bing reached all markets except China.
