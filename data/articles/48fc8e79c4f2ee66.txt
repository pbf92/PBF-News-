TITLE: Church bells to chime every 15 minutes despite residents' noise complaints
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The bells of a church clock in a village in France will continue to chime through the night every 15 minutes, despite attempts to silence the "rural nuisance".
A vote was called in La Garnache, 25 miles southwest of Nantes, after dozens of residents signed a petition to the stop the bells of Notre Dame de l’Assomption church from ringing between 10pm and 7am, saying it was distrubing their sleep. However, three quarters of voters opted in favour of keeping the bells ringing round the clock.
Every 15 minutes, the clock rings out passages of the hymn of Saint Louis-Marie Grignion de Montfort, a Breton priest who died in 1716. The Virgin Mary is said to have appeared before him in the village.
“All of the people who are speaking out arrived after the bells were sounding,” François Petit, the former mayor of La Garnache Petit who called for the vote, told Le Figaro. “The residents could have installed triple glazing when renovating their homes."
In France, two laws have been passed in recent years to protect country traditions, including the 2021 Maurice's law (named after a rooster). It follows numerous lawsuits over "rural nuisances", including disagreements between residents and farmers over crowing poultry, smelly pigs and night-time harvesting.
The rural disputes have prompted some mayors to put up signposts at village entrances saying that any newcomers who take issue with every day, local life – like church bells and crowing roosters – should go elsewhere.
