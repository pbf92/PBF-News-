TITLE: International review sets out diagnosis and management of pancreatic SPN
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Solid pseudopapillary neoplasm (SPN) of the pancreas is a low-grade malignant tumor, accounting for 0.2% to 2.7% of all pancreatic tumors. Due to its rarity, most available evidence comes from case series and cohort studies.
SPN occurs predominantly in young females, with a median age of 27 to 31 years and a male-to-female ratio of about 1:4 to 1:8. It is often asymptomatic but may present as an abdominal mass causing abdominal pain. Computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) typically show a well-circumscribed mass with both solid and cystic components. Pathological diagnosis is the gold standard, combining histomorphology with immunohistochemistry including positive nuclear β-catenin, CD10 and progesterone receptor; CTNNB1 mutations are present in over 95% of cases, mostly in exon 3.
SPN is currently considered insensitive to chemotherapy and radiotherapy, and complete surgical resection is crucial for cure. The review, published in Volume 9, Issue 3 of the Journal of Pancreatology in September 2026, recommends radical surgery for resectable cases, aiming for negative surgical margins (R0 resection) while avoiding tumor capsule rupture using a no-touch isolation technique. Organ-preserving procedures, such as enucleation and spleen-preserving distal pancreatectomy, are viable in experienced high-volume centers. Reported lymph node metastasis rates are 0% for tumors of 0 to 2 cm, 0.5% for 2 to 4 cm and 8.9% for tumors larger than 4 cm, so routine lymph node dissection is not recommended in patients without metastatic evidence and with tumors smaller than 4 cm. Routine adjuvant therapy after R0 resection is not recommended.
The 10-year survival rate exceeds 95%, and even patients with unresectable SPN can achieve long-term survival with the tumor. Reported prognostic risk factors include tumor diameter over 5 cm, lymphovascular invasion, Ki-67 of 3% or higher and intraoperative rupture. Follow-up with contrast-enhanced CT or MRI every 6 months for the first 3 years and annually for the subsequent 2 years is recommended, with imaging every 3 months for the first 3 years in tumors larger than 5 cm.
