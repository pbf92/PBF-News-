TITLE: Yahweh / I Am Who I Am
OUTLET: Christianity Today

Long before the angels and shepherds of the first century inspired our Nativity scenes, the angel of the Lord appeared to another shepherd in the wilderness. And, like the birth of Jesus, Exodus 2 and 3 recount God drawing near to his people with a message of hope.
God heard and saw the Israelites enslaved in Egypt. Being faithful to his covenant and sympathetic to their suffering, he descended to Mount Horeb to bring good news of deliverance through an unlikely ambassador: Moses. When his message is met with brokenness and self-doubt, as it often is, God is glad to offer reminders and revelations of who he is. “‘I am the God of your father,’” (3:6) he says to Moses, “‘I Am who I Am’” (v. 14), and “‘I will be with you’” (v. 12). This reassurance of his nature and character gave Moses confidence to serve in the promise of God’s presence. It is comforting to know that when we walk in the will of God we do not walk in our own power and authority.
In saying “I am the God of your father” (3:6) and “I will be with you” (v. 12), God reveals his willingness and desire to be known for his nature, his works, his word, and the testimony of his people. God emphatically declares that he is a covenant maker and keeper, a deliverer, holy, and sufficient. The creator of the universe, all-powerful and enthroned in heaven, makes himself accessible to Moses and the Israelites.
God continued offering reminders and revelations of his nature and character hundreds of years later: He had not changed and his mission was still the same. When an angel of the Lord appeared to the shepherds in Luke 2, it came bearing “‘good news that will cause great joy’” (v. 10). It arrived accompanied by a multitude of the heavenly host praising God, for he was again drawing near to his people to provide deliverance. This time from slavery to sin, and this time through another unlikely ambassador, a baby wrapped in swaddling clothes.
Jesus’s entire ministry was a revelation of who he is. He forgave sins, he gave sight to the blind, and he even raised the dead; these all offer us confidence in the new covenant he established with his people, a covenant of grace. His life gives us reassurance of his presence, and because of his accomplishments on the cross, we have access to the indwelling presence of the Holy Spirit.
I’m thankful the great I Am is still at work. It is good news for me personally, as God’s deliverance isn’t just from the power of sin but also from its consequence, death. I have faith in the future, final, and full restoration to the presence of God when he draws near again to finally put an end to our suffering. I have reassurance from a trustworthy God that my hope in the promised resurrection, my hope for eternal, abundant life, is well-placed because he is who he is.
