TITLE: Amazon adds five metrics to count sales from Netflix ads
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon Ads has given advertisers who buy Netflix inventory through its buying platform a way to see which purchases followed those ads. According to a release note dated November 16, 2026, "Amazon DSP reports in Ads API v1 now include five metrics for conversions attributed to Netflix ad views." The five cover purchases, sales in two currency versions, visits to Amazon product pages and actions that happen away from Amazon.
The note is short: a title, a date, one sentence and a list of five field names. It sits in the Amazon Ads API release notes, the changelog Amazon keeps for developers and agencies that connect their own software to its advertising systems, between an entry on video bid adjustments and one on a Review Requests API, both carrying the same date. It names no markets, gives no attribution window and links to no documentation.
In Short
Amazon's ad-buying platform can now tell advertisers how many purchases, sales, product page visits and off-Amazon actions followed a Netflix ad, in reports pulled through Amazon's programming interface. That matters to brands and agencies buying Netflix ads through Amazon, because public material on the partnership since 2025 has been about reaching audiences, not about counting results. The names are public but the counting rules are not, so how a sale gets credited to a Netflix ad is still undocumented.
What the November 16 note says
The entry is titled "Netflix attribution metrics in Amazon DSP reports." Its single sentence of explanation reads: "Amazon DSP reports in Ads API v1 now include five metrics for conversions attributed to Netflix ad views." Under the heading "New metrics," Amazon then lists the five fields, each with a code name that software uses and a display name that people read.
Two words in the note carry most of the meaning. "Attributed" means Amazon's measurement credits the conversions to the ads; it does not mean the ads were proven to cause them. "Views" means the credit is tied to someone having seen the ad, not clicked on it. In industry terms, most of what these fields count will be a view-through conversion: an action credited to an ad that was watched but not touched, provided it happens within a set period afterwards.
The split also follows a line Amazon already draws. Four of the fields describe things that happen in Amazon's store - a purchase, the value of that purchase, a visit to a product's product detail page - while the fifth is reserved for conversions somewhere else. Amazon's reporting keeps "Amazon retail conversions" and "Off-Amazon conversions" in separate categories, and the Netflix set appears to mirror that division, although the note itself does not say so.
Where the metrics live
"Ads API v1" is the current version of Amazon's advertising programming interface, the channel through which software rather than people requests campaign data. Amazon runs a demand-side platform, the system advertisers use to buy ads across Amazon's own properties and outside sites, apps and streaming services, and its reports in v1 were rebuilt this autumn.
On September 30, 2026, Amazon declared "the new Reporting API (v1)" generally available together with Amazon Marketing Stream v2. According to the release note from that date, the Reporting API "lets you generate ad-hoc reports that combine dimensions, ad products, and accounts in a single request." Several rules in that September note decide how the Netflix fields will behave in practice.
The first rule means a purchase made on a Thursday after a Netflix ad seen on Monday is reported against Monday. The second means the period in which that purchase can be credited is chosen automatically by campaign type, and the Netflix note does not say which period applies to Netflix ads. The third has a small catch. The new field is called metric.netflixOffAmazonConversions, with "netflix" in front, so software that gathers off-Amazon results by looking for the "offAmazon" prefix would not pick it up.
Amazon's own index of v1 metrics had not caught up by November 25. The metrics library linked from the Reporting API overview listed ten categories on that date - Amazon retail conversions, off-Amazon conversions, Amazon video conversions, Amazon device conversions, engagement, delivery, costs and fees, reach, combined conversions and impression share - with no Netflix category and no definitions for the five fields. A November 6 release note on new supply path dimensions also listed the conversion families Amazon recognised at the time as "Amazon retail, Amazon device, Amazon video, off-Amazon or combined conversions." Netflix was not among them.
Two numbers for one sale
Two of the five fields record the same thing: sales. One is "reported with the budget currency dimension," the other "with the converted currency dimension." The first keeps the money in the currency the campaign was budgeted in. The second translates it into a currency selected for the report.
The pairing is not new. A release note dated November 17, 2026 added fees for the Barometer prebid service "in the budget currency" and, separately, "in the currency selected for conversion." An August 26, 2026 note added new-to-brand metrics with costs "in both native and converted currency."
Why does it matter here? The September launch of v1 introduced "cross-account reporting (a single report spanning all accounts you manage)." When Amazon and Netflix announced the partnership, they named 11 countries. An agency running Netflix campaigns for one brand in the UK, Germany and Japan would see sales in pounds, euros and yen; the converted field is what allows those to be added up. The note does not say which exchange rate Amazon applies, or on which date.
From audiences to attribution
Netflix has been buyable through Amazon for about a year. Amazon and Netflix announced on September 10, 2025 that Netflix inventory would be available in Amazon DSP from the fourth quarter of 2025, in the United States, the United Kingdom, France, Spain, Mexico, Canada, Japan, Brazil, Italy, Germany and Australia, through programmatic guaranteed and private marketplace deals. It was Netflix's fifth major programmatic partnership, after The Trade Desk, Google's Display & Video 360, Microsoft and Yahoo DSP, which was added in June 2025. Amazon's own announcement page that day spoke of measurement only in general terms, with no sentence on attribution or reporting.
The next steps were about who could be reached, not what they did afterwards. In March 2026, Netflix said Amazon's audience data would come to programmatic buys through Amazon DSP, starting in the United States in the second quarter, and launched its own conversion API. Amazon Audiences went live on Netflix in the United States on April 13 and reached the UK, Germany, France, Italy and Spain on May 18. At its upfront presentation on May 13, Netflix said its ads plan reached more than 250 million monthly active viewers and that Amazon DSP audience targeting would cover all its ad-supported countries by June 1, 2026.
Amazon's developer changelog recorded the plumbing. On July 31, 2026, Amazon described unified endpoints for proposals "including support for Netflix workflow." On August 4, 2026, it made the Proposal Management Unified APIs generally available, covering proposals "across Amazon Owned and Operated (Amazon O&O) publishers (called 'Amazon Media' in the UI), Amazon Publisher Direct (APD), Amazon Publisher Cloud (APC), and Netflix." Those tools handle the deals. Among the entries on Amazon's current release notes page, the November 16 note is the first to give Netflix its own results.
No other outside publisher has a set like it. Amazon also sells streaming inventory from partners such as Roku, whose June 2025 deal with Amazon covered an estimated 80 million US connected TV households, but neither the release notes nor the metrics library list a set of conversion metrics named after any of them.
Netflix's measurement gap
The timing lines up with a problem Netflix described in October. When it reported third-quarter results on October 20, 2026, Netflix cut its 2026 ad revenue expectation to about $2.7 billion from roughly $3 billion, blaming a slower rollout of some ad products. Co-chief executive Greg Peters spoke of delays in partner integrations for new ad products, without naming which. The shareholder letter said Netflix's measurement offering was expanding to in-DSP measurement, meaning reach, frequency and attribution reported inside the buying platform itself, but it did not say which platforms.
A Netflix post the same day was more specific. Yahoo DSP advertisers could already measure single publisher attribution on their programmatic Netflix buys, while in-DSP measurement with The Trade Desk was described as coming soon, with no date. Amazon appeared in that post only for audience targeting enabled in March. PPC Land found no Netflix announcement matching the November 16 Amazon note.
Who gets the credit
Single publisher attribution is conversion credit counted by one seller using only the ad exposures it can see. Its weakness is well known: several platforms can each claim the same sale. The Netflix fields raise a version of that question inside Amazon itself. A shopper who sees a Netflix ad and an ad on Prime Video, then buys, could in principle count towards either line or both. The note does not say how Amazon handles such overlaps.
Amazon has changed its view-based crediting before. From January 1, 2026, it shortened attribution windows for view-based campaigns, including Amazon DSP ads served in its own store, without publishing the new durations. Netflix ads run outside Amazon's store, and the November 16 note does not say whether the same rules apply to them.
The off-Amazon field depends on Amazon seeing what happens on an advertiser's website. Its route for that is the Amazon Ad Tag, a piece of code placed on the site. In a note carrying the same November 16 date, Amazon said the tag now sets a first-party cookie in North America that expires 30 days after a visitor's last page view, so that it can "measure and attribute those events more reliably as third-party cookies are phased out." Netflix, for its part, now offers its own pixel and conversion API. A brand running both could see the same website sign-up reported twice, once by each company's system.
A platform with two names
The note uses a name Amazon has officially retired. On September 29, 2026, the buying product formerly known as Amazon DSP was renamed DVA, and the wider platform became Amazon Ads Agent. The developer notes have not settled on either. The November 16 and November 17 entries both refer to "Amazon DSP reports," while a November 19 entry on agency fees speaks of "all DVA+ (ADSP) marketplaces." For developers, the field names matter more than the label, but the mix makes the documentation harder to search.
Why this matters for marketers
For Amazon, the fields extend its main argument to advertisers - that it can link an ad to a purchase because it runs the store - to inventory it does not own. Amazon's ad business is large enough for this to count: advertising services revenue was $19.64 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 11%, although Amazon does not report what share comes from streaming or from partners such as Netflix.
For Netflix buyers, the result is uneven measurement depending on where the ads are bought. Through Yahoo DSP, single publisher attribution is live. Through The Trade Desk, it is promised but undated. Through Amazon, five fields now exist in API reports, defined only by their names. An agency comparing Netflix campaigns across those platforms would be comparing numbers built on different rules.
There is also a practical limit. The note covers "Amazon DSP reports in Ads API v1." It does not mention the reports advertisers see in Amazon's own console, so teams without a direct integration may not see the fields at all.
