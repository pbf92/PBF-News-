Poll: 36% of Americans Back Force to Defend Estonia
A YouGov survey finds broader U.S. public support for defending NATO allies, with more hesitation over Estonia than Poland or Ukraine.
Updated on Nov. 25, 2026 in Military
Live Poll
Should the U.S. be willing to use military force to defend Estonia?
An Economist/YouGov poll of 1,623 U.S. citizens, conducted November 20-23, 2026, found 36% support using military force to defend Estonia, a NATO member since 2004. Support was higher for defending Poland (56%) and lower for Ukraine (43%), while 48% of respondents were unsure about Estonia.
Why it matters
The results show how public backing for NATO commitments varies by country, which bears on U.S. defense planning as the Pentagon completes a force-posture review in Europe. Nearly half of respondents being undecided on Estonia suggests the issue is not yet settled in public opinion.
The Economist/YouGov poll surveyed 1,623 U.S. citizens between November 20 and 23, 2026, with 36% supporting military force for Estonia, 56% for Poland and 43% for Ukraine. Whether these sentiments would shift under an actual Russian provocation has not been tested.
The players
Economist/YouGov
A polling partnership between The Economist and the market research firm YouGov that regularly measures U.S. public opinion on policy and current events.
Pew Research Center
A nonpartisan American think tank based in Washington, D.C., known for public opinion research on social, political and international issues.
Korber Foundation
A German-American foundation that partners on transatlantic public opinion research, including surveys of German attitudes toward U.S. relations.
NATO
The North Atlantic Treaty Organization, a transatlantic military alliance founded in 1949 whose collective defense commitment underpins the poll's questions.
The details
Respondents were asked whether the United States should be willing to use military force to defend each country, and 62% said the U.S. should come to the defense of a NATO member under attack. Opposition to defending Estonia stood at 16%, and a separate Pew Research Center and Korber Foundation survey found about 64% of Germans consider U.S. military bases important to their national security, up from 52% in 2019.
Timeline
- September 2025: Russian fighter jets violated Estonian airspace, prompting alliance consultations.
- May 2026: The Pentagon announced plans to withdraw 5,000 troops from Germany.
- November 20-23, 2026: Economist/YouGov poll fieldwork conducted.
- November 24, 2026: The poll was released.
Big Picture
The German figure of 64% continues a rise from the 52% level Pew Research Center recorded in 2019, even as the Pentagon moves to withdraw 5,000 troops from Germany. The American responses on Estonia arrive as the force-posture review examines how U.S. deployments in Europe should be arranged.
The poll reflects opinion rather than policy, so it does not change U.S. commitments to NATO allies. Shifts in the Pentagon's European force posture, including the planned 5,000-troop withdrawal from Germany, would have a more direct effect on U.S. service members and their families.
The takeaway
Public support for defending NATO allies is strongest for the alliance in general and weaker for specific frontline states like Estonia. That gap is likely to matter as defense planners weigh where U.S. forces are based.
