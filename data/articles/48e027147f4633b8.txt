TITLE: Amazon Autos to list Hyundai cars in the UK from early 2027
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon today said its car-shopping service, Amazon Autos, will open in the United Kingdom in early 2027, the first time the service has operated outside the United States. Hyundai is the first carmaker signed for the UK launch, and Vertu Motors, which describes itself as one of the UK's largest car retailers, will list used cars. The UK version stops short of a full online sale: shoppers browse, compare prices and reserve a car on Amazon.co.uk, then sign the paperwork and collect the keys at the dealership.
The announcement was published on About Amazon UK today under the headline "Amazon Autos is coming to the UK: a new way to shop for your next car," credited to Amazon staff. The Amazon Autos company page on LinkedIn carried the same news the same day. No exact launch date, no pricing for dealers and no list of locations was given.
In Short
Amazon will let people in the UK search for cars, see prices and reserve one on Amazon.co.uk from early 2027, starting with Hyundai and the dealer group Vertu Motors. It is the first time Amazon Autos has left the US, and it puts Amazon between car dealers and the shoppers they currently reach through classified sites and search ads. The deal itself still happens at the dealership, and Amazon has not said what dealers will pay to be listed.
What shoppers will see on Amazon.co.uk
According to Amazon, the UK storefront will combine new cars from manufacturers with used cars listed by dealers. The company set out six things customers will be able to do when the service opens.
Two of those terms are British car-trade vocabulary. Approved used is the UK label for second-hand cars checked and backed by the manufacturer's own programme, close to what US dealers call certified pre-owned. PCP and HP are two common forms of car finance in Britain. Under HP, fixed monthly payments end with the buyer owning the car. Under PCP, monthly payments are lower and a larger optional payment at the end decides whether the buyer keeps the car.
The wording on finance matters. Amazon says customers can "explore" PCP and HP deals "to understand monthly payments." It does not say customers can apply for credit or sign a finance agreement on Amazon. The trade-in figure is likewise described as "indicative," which suggests an estimate rather than a firm offer.
How buying works, according to Amazon
Put in order, Amazon's description of the UK process has five stages. Only the last one takes place at the dealership.
- Search the storefront on Amazon.co.uk by make, model, trim and colour.
- Compare the listed price, the indicative valuation of an existing car and the monthly cost under PCP or HP.
- Reserve the chosen car on Amazon.
- Connect directly with the chosen dealer.
- Complete the sale at the dealership. According to Amazon, "once customers find the right car, they connect directly with their chosen dealer to complete the purchase at the dealership, finalise paperwork, and drive away."
"Customers do the research on Amazon, then walk into the dealership with clarity on price, financing options, and the car they want," according to Amazon. The company said it had "built the experience specifically for how UK customers shop for cars."
Amazon did not say whether a reservation requires a deposit, how long a car stays reserved, whether a reservation can be cancelled, or whether the reserved price is binding on the dealer.
Hyundai and Vertu: the first two names
Hyundai's line-up
Hyundai was also the first partner when Amazon Autos opened in 48 US cities in December 2024. In the UK, according to Amazon, Hyundai "will bring a broad line-up to Amazon.co.uk from day one," including the IONIQ 3, IONIQ 5, KONA and i20.
The IONIQ 3, a compact all-electric hatchback, gets particular attention. Amazon says it "will be available on Amazon Autos UK from early 2027." A caption on the same page says the car is "now available in UK showrooms," so the model will reach Amazon some time after it reached dealers.

"Our partnership with Amazon Autos has changed the way many customers discover Hyundai in the United States, and 78 per cent of the Amazon-influenced sales at our dealerships have come from customers who are new to our brand," he said, according to the announcement.

Vertu Motors
Vertu Motors plc is the first dealer group named for the UK. Amazon describes it as a "major UK dealer" whose used vehicles will be listed. Forrester's statement was measured. "As one of the UK's largest automotive retailers, we're committed to meeting customers wherever they choose to begin their buying journey," he said.
"Amazon Autos has the potential to introduce our vehicles to highly engaged consumers, combining the convenience and transparency of online shopping with the confidence of completing a purchase through a trusted dealership," Forrester added. He referred to "the anticipated UK launch in early 2027" and said the partnership would help Vertu "reach new customers."
Amazon's pitch to dealers
The dealer case rests on one claim: Amazon brings buyers dealers would not otherwise see. According to Amazon, Amazon Autos UK "gives dealers a cost-effective way to reach new customers, putting their cars in front of people who already shop on Amazon." Customers arrive "having chosen their car, reviewed the pricing, and explored their financing and part-exchange options, making every appointment more productive."
The supporting figure comes from the US. According to the announcement, "in the U.S., 69% hadn't considered the dealer before finding them on Amazon." Amazon adds that "dealers set their own prices and own the customer relationship."
Matt Nuffort, Director of Amazon Autos International, framed the service around price transparency. "Buying a car is one of the biggest purchases people make, and we think it should feel as simple and transparent as shopping for anything on Amazon," he said. "We worked closely with UK dealers to design the experience, and they're excited that we're bringing them customers who are ready to buy."
Nuffort described today's step as "the expansion of our partnership with Hyundai as our first UK launch partner" and said Amazon would be "announcing more brands between now and the launch in early 2027."
Neither the 69% figure nor Hyundai's 78% figure comes with a method. The announcement does not say how many customers or sales were counted, over what period, or what makes a sale "Amazon-influenced." "Cost-effective" is not backed by any price, since Amazon has published no fee for dealers.
How far the US service has come
The UK launch follows two years of growth in the US. According to Amazon, Amazon Autos has gone "from 48 cities to more than 150," working "with thousands of dealers offering vehicles from 35 brands."
The US model went further at launch than the UK one is described as going. When Amazon Autos opened, US shoppers could finance the car inside the Amazon checkout, with credit responses within 90 seconds, or pay in full, with prices that included all taxes and fees and no negotiation. Pickup took place at the dealership. When used cars were added in August 2025, the US terms included a three-day, 300-mile return policy and a minimum 30-day, 1,000-mile limited warranty, with every car within 75 miles of the customer.
None of those terms appears in the UK announcement. The UK description ends at a reservation, with finance and paperwork handled by the dealer. Amazon did not explain the difference.
Amazon also uses its own money to push car sales in the US. From November 12 to November 30, 2026, eligible Prime members who buy or lease a new car through Amazon Autos receive a $1,500 Amazon.com gift card once the car is collected from a participating dealer. No equivalent UK offer was mentioned.
What the LinkedIn post adds
The Amazon Autos company page on LinkedIn posted the news with a line written for a British audience: "Amazon Autos is hitting the road in the UK. On the left side, naturally." The post calls the UK "our first international market."
Its wording differs from the About Amazon UK page in two places. The post says Hyundai Motor Company "will be one of the first brands to list on Amazon.co.uk," where the official page calls Hyundai the "first announced launch partner." The post also says customers will be able to browse "new, certified pre-owned, and used inventory," using the US term, while the UK page uses "approved used." In a comment under the post, the Amazon Autos page asked UK dealerships to register interest in partnering through a sign-up page at autos.amazon/uk. The About Amazon UK page itself links to a sign-up form for shoppers who want launch updates.
Why it matters for marketers
For car dealers, finding buyers is largely an advertising question. Dealers already pay classified sites and search engines to put their stock in front of shoppers. Amazon Autos UK adds another place to list cars, run by a company with a large advertising business and detailed data on what its customers buy.
Amazon reported $19.64 billion in ad revenue for the third quarter of 2026, up 10.9% as reported. Amazon operates a retail media network, meaning a retailer that sells ads using what it knows about its own shoppers. A car is usually bought in a showroom, out of a retailer's sight. A storefront where shoppers search for specific models, value their current car and reserve a vehicle gives Amazon first-party data much closer to that sale. Amazon did not say whether reservation or search data from Amazon Autos UK will feed its advertising products.
Linking ads to cars sold is a known weak point for car advertisers. In a Jamloop survey of 120 automotive advertisers, only 18% said their connected TV reports could show vehicles sold, even though 63% considered vehicle sales a key measure of success. Hyundai's "Amazon-influenced sales" figure shows that Amazon and its partners already count sales in the US; how that number is built remains unpublished.
Amazon is not alone in courting car stock. In May 2026, Google added automotive to the product feeds that power Demand Gen campaigns, one of several changes announced at Google Marketing Live. Both companies want dealers' inventory in a format they can search, rank and sell ads against.
There is also the shopping assistant question. Amazon told investors in October that over 400 million customers had used Alexa for Shopping in the previous 12 months. The UK announcement promises "familiar Amazon features" for searching cars but does not name any assistant. Whether car searches will run through the same AI shopping tools that Amazon is building for agentic commerce elsewhere on its store is not addressed.
What Amazon has not said
The announcement gives no launch date beyond "early 2027," no fee or commission for dealers, no list of regions or cities, and no detail on reservation deposits, cancellation or whether the reserved price binds the dealer. It does not name a finance provider, say whether credit applications happen on Amazon, or explain whether Prime membership changes anything. It does not say whether Amazon will offer home delivery, returns or warranties like those offered in the US. The 69% and 78% US figures have no stated method.
