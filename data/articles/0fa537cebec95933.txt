TITLE: Trump approval, summit in China, outages, measles, Grand Theft Auto, Thanksgiving, and more: November 20-23, 2026 Economist / YouGov Poll
OUTLET: YouGov

Plus polling on the political parties, the next Senate majority leader, Putin, NATO, Iran, Israel, and AI
- More Americans say Democrats would do a better job than Republicans handling the economy than say Republicans would do better (40% vs. 30%). The same is true for foreign policy (39% vs. 31%) and taxes and government spending (37% vs. 30%). More say Republicans would do a better job handling crime (38% vs. 30%)
- 59% aren't sure whether they would rather have Chuck Schumer or Chris Van Hollen as the leader of the Democrats in the U.S. Senate; 28% pick Van Hollen and 14% Schumer. Democrats are about twice as likely to prefer Van Hollen as to prefer Schumer (40% vs. 19%)
- 42% think Donald Trump's relationship with Vladimir Putin is a bad thing for the United States and 30% think it is a good thing. Views were similar in June 2020, when 43% said the relationship was mostly a bad thing and 29% mostly a good thing
- 43% think Trump generally gets along better with dictators, 7% with democratically elected leaders, and 32% say there is no difference. 71% of Democrats say dictators, compared with 18% of Republicans; 52% of Republicans say there is no difference
- 62% think the U.S. should defend a NATO member that is attacked, and 9% think it should not. Asked about three countries, 56% say the U.S. should be willing to use military force to help defend Poland if Russia attacks it, 43% Ukraine, and 36% Estonia (48% aren't sure about defending it)
- 63% think the U.S. is spending too much on the war in Iran, 15% say the spending level is about right, and 5% say it's too little. Looking back, 57% say the decision to go to war in Iran was the wrong decision and 25% say it was the right one
- A majority (69%) of Americans think artificial intelligence (AI) is advancing too quickly, 18% think it is advancing at about the right pace, and 3% think it is advancing too slowly. 60% think government regulation of AI should be increased, 17% think it should be kept the same, and 10% think it should be decreased
  - Democrats are more likely than Republicans to say AI is advancing too quickly (79% vs. 61%) and to say government regulation of AI should be increased (76% vs. 51%)
  - Opinions about AI advancement and regulation have changed little since early October
- 24% think AI is now better than professionals at conducting scientific research, 17% think it's about equal, and 34% think it's worse

Methodology: The poll was conducted among 1,623 U.S. adult citizens. Respondents were selected from YouGov's opt-in panel to be representative of U.S. adult citizens. A random sample (stratified by gender, age, race, education, geographic region, and voter registration) was selected from the 2019 American Community Survey. The sample was weighted according to gender, age, race, education, U.S. region, 2024 election turnout and presidential vote, baseline party identification, and current voter registration status. 2024 presidential vote, at time of weighting, was estimated to be 48% Harris and 50% Trump. Demographic weighting targets come from the 2019 American Community Survey. Baseline party identification is the respondent's most recent answer given around November 8, 2024, and is weighted to the estimated distribution at that time (31% Democratic, 33% Republican). The margin of error is approximately 2.9%.
