TITLE: 10 Games to Play While Waiting for GTA VI to Go on Sale
OUTLET: DualShockers

After 13 years, Rockstar’s long awaited follow-up to Grand Theft Auto V, Grand Theft Auto VI, has finally arrived on Xbox and PlayStation consoles to widespread critical and financial acclaim, becoming the biggest entertainment launch in history.
While millions of people are playing through GTA VI today, many are also holding off from playing the game until it goes on sale due its high price ranging from $80-$100 and massive file size of 212 GB, taking up a massive chunk of the base PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S internal storage.
For those gamers who are holding off on purchasing GTA VI until it goes on sale, here’s a list of 10 GTA VI-like titles they should check out in the meantime that are currently being sold at more affordable prices and balanced file sizes.

Some people just enjoy Grand Theft Auto games for their comedy and freedom to sow chaos throughout cities and for people who enjoy that type of experience they should check out the FPS High on Life from the creators of Rick and Morty.
Here, people play as a human as they wind up being teleported to the alien metropolis of Blim City to become an intergalactic bounty hunter with living guns called Gatlians guiding them to take down the G3 Cartel that intends on turning the entire human race into drugs.
High on Life is as ridiculous and hilarious as it sounds, essentially being a perfect translation of Rick and Morty’s signature comedy style to video game form, and it will certainly give players many laughs as they wait for GTA VI to go on sale.

Ever since Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas, flying all kinds of aircraft has been an integral part of the GTA experience and for those who enjoy flying more than driving in the franchise, there’s no better game to check out while waiting for GTA VI sales than Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024.
The latest entry in the long-running Microsoft Flight Simulator series, Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 is the most advanced publicly available flight simulator, allowing players to learn how to fly a wide variety of aircraft in first or third-person such as airships, gliders, hot air balloons, jets, and more.
Not only that, but the game additionally allows players to fly any aircraft across the planet’s most famous natural and urban environments such as Zion National Park, Mount Fuji, London, and Miami which is what GTA VI’s Vice City is directly based on.

Even more so than flying, driving has arguably been the defining factor of the GTA franchise with the series initially starting out as a top-down driving-focused game and if GTA fans wanted to check out the most advanced realistic racing game, then they’d have to play Forza Horizon 6.
Currently an Xbox Series X|S console exclusive, Forza Horizon 6 has players journey to the rural and urban streets of a stylised version of Tokyo as they strive to qualify for the Horizon Festival or simply drive around Japan’s wondrous landscapes using over 500 cars.
There’s far more to do in Forza Horizon 6 than just driving around Japan as players can customize a massive estate filled with garages and custom tracks or players can customize public roads to use in multiplayer races.

Some people may enjoy the GTA series for the driving, flying, or comedy but oftentimes people like those games for the freedom to cause chaos in any way they desire, with one game that offers a similar style of player freedom being Dead Rising Deluxe Remaster.
An RE Engine remake of 2006’s Dead Rising, Dead Rising Deluxe Remaster follows the photojournalist Frank West as he sneaks his way into a Willamette, Colorado mall to cover a zombie outbreak only to get trapped in the mall and attempt to investigate the outbreak’s origins.
While players can stick to completing the game’s many time limited narrative cases, players can instantly go about slaying zombies and psychopaths in any way they want by using practically anything found in the mall such as jugs of orange juice, CDs, katanas, potted plants, shopping carts, and more.

If people want to experience a game with a similar story and setting to GTA VI, there are actually a few good games to choose from with one of the more underrated options being Hangar 13’s Mafia III.
The first Mafia game to not be developed by series creator Illusion Softworks, Mafia III follows Vietnam War veteran turned crime lord Lincoln Clay in the U.S. gulf coast city of New Bordeaux as he sets out to avenge his adoptive father and brother after they were killed by the Marcano crime family.
While Mafia III isn’t set in the present day, it still features a similarly enthralling and emotional story, one that tackles social issues of late 1960s America and features multiple different endings depending on Clay’s actions and choices during his campaign to take down the local mafia.

When it comes to video games centered around crime, most people wouldn’t consider an entry from the war-focused Battlefield series to be one but in 2015, Visceral Games did just that by creating the crime and police centered Battlefield Hardline.
Visceral’s final game ever released, Battlefield Hardline’s campaign follows Miami Police Detective Nick Mendoza attempting to take down drug dealers and suppliers in the Florida based city only to discover corruption in the MPD and wind up being framed for a crime he didn’t commit.
Hardline may not have been the game traditional Battlefield fans wanted at the time of its release but Visceral’s writing and gameplay prowess managed to make it stand as not only one of the most unique Battlefield games but also one of the most intriguing and cinematic.

Ubisoft is famous for creating immersive open-world titles like the numerous entries from the Assassin’s Creed and Far Cry series but it wasn’t until 2014 that the publisher decided to make a more grounded GTA-like experience with Watch Dogs.
Set in Chicago and the same universe as the Assassin’s Creed franchise, Watch Dogs follows hacker Aiden Pearce who, after a heist goes wrong, sets out to find the people responsible for putting a hit on him resulting in the death of his niece Lena.
What ensues is an action-packed yet stealthy gaming experience as Pearce is able to hack particularly every piece of technology in the city from cellphones to trains and construction equipment to aid him in tracking his enemies down in between instances where Pearce has to steal cars or engage in gun fights.

While Mafia III and Watch Dogs are fun GTA-like titles, neither of them compare to the pure chaos and drama that can be unleashed in CD Projekt Red’s ambitious first-person, open-world, action RPG Cyberpunk 2077.
The latest brand-new game developed by the makers of The Witcher game series, Cyberpunk 2077 has players embody the cyborg mercenary V in the futuristic and crime-filled metropolis of Night City as they get caught up in a movement to take down the corrupt Arasaka Corporation.
Cyberpunk 2077’s reputation may have been marred by a buggy 2020 launch but it has since gone on to become one of the best modern RPGs thanks to beneficial updates and its Phantom Liberty DLC making the game’s core combat, dialogue, and side activities all the more fun.

GTA VI is set in the present day but its greatest gaming influence on a technical and narrative level is undoubtedly Rockstar Games’ previous modern gaming big hit, Red Dead Redemption 2.
Set roughly 12 years before Red Dead Redemption 1 at the turn of the 20th century, Red Dead Redemption 2 primarily follows Van der Linde gang member Arthur Morgan as he and the rest of the gang struggle to survive against rival gangs, nature itself, and the ever-dominating presence of industrialized civilization.
Before GTA VI, Red Dead Redemption 2 was Rockstar’s most cinematic and immersive game ever made as not only did it feature a far more serious and emotional story than any GTA game before but it allowed players to do all kinds of things in the old west such as hunting wildlife, taking care of horses, crafting materials, and more.
