TITLE: Wes Huff admits plagiarism and steps back from ministry
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Christian apologist Wes Huff has admitted plagiarising other people's work and has stepped back from ministry and church work.
In a post on X on 19 November the vice president of Apologetics Canada, who has more than a million social media followers said it was "wrong and sinful" that he had “failed to uphold the standards of integrity” that he encourages others to value.
The 35 year old who is currently studying for a PhD in theological studies at Wycliffe College, University of Toronto, rose to fame after appearing on Joe Rogan’s podcast and subsequently on Piers Morgan Uncensored.
Earlier this month he was accused of plagiarising significant portions of a paper on John's Gospel in 2016. Biblical scholar and YouTuber Dan McClellan posted a video which compared the piece written by Huff during his studies at Tyndale Seminary in Toronto with a paper by a Florida pastor, Brian J Wright which had been written six years earlier.
McClellan alleged that Huff’s paper included the same arguments and even matching citation errors. Other claims were then reported in the Christian Post and the Roys Report from people alleging Huff had copied parts of their work too.
In an earlier post on X, Huff said he took the concerns seriously and was committed to “full accountability”, but stopped short of admitting to wrongdoing.
In his latest post he explained that he had allowed himself to justify his actions and "even pushing them so far from my mind that I failed to reckon with what I had done."
He added: "Above all, I want to walk through this with genuine repentance before God, and with humility toward those I have wronged. I know that repentance is more than words, and I recognize that it will need to be demonstrated through my actions going forward. My prayer is that God would use this painful season to confront what needs to change in me and to lead me toward genuine repentance.
"My soul feels grieved by this situation. My heart is heavy. And my guilt at times has felt like it will swallow me whole. I am grateful for those who can still find it in their hearts to pray for me and my family."
Apologetics Canada said it will undertake a "full review" of the concerns.
Huff said he would submit himself fully to the review and any other disciplinary process and added that he would also be “under the care and counsel” of his church elders.
