TITLE: El Qanna / Jealous God
OUTLET: Christianity Today

My father knows El Qanna, the Jealous God. Right before my birth, El Qanna grabbed my dad by the collar and shook him awake.
My father grew up in an abusive orphanage, having been rejected by fiercely racist (and fiercely poor) Appalachian grandparents for being too brown-skinned. A mixed-race Pacific Islander born into a broken home, the second of five kids, whip-smart and naturally charming, he grew up under harsh conditions. All metrics pointed toward his generational trauma passing to his own children in a cycle of despair.
And yet, I am the firstborn son of a new story, one in which blessings abound where curses once hung heavy.
While I swam in my mother’s womb, my dad made a commitment directly to God and indirectly to his growing family. He would forsake all other ways and walk only in the light of God’s love. He bowed before El Qanna, the Jealous God, and promised fidelity and faithfulness. He had known about God already and, years before, had prayed for Jesus to save him. But Sunday School can only bring a child so far; El Qanna’s expectations are fierce, passionate, all-consuming. This divine passion is both warning and invitation. For those who are brought into covenant with El Qanna, intense expectations are held in tension by overwhelming goodness. This can go very poorly or very well.
El Qanna is both a proper name (“Jealous God”) and descriptive (God is experienced as jealous, zealous, and ardent in his love). The first usage of El Qanna in the Bible is in Exodus 20:2–6, a section of Torah containing the Ten Commandments. They begin with warnings against idol worship and include El Qanna’s demands for singular faithfulness.
I am YHWH your God.
Don’t bring some other god into this relationship.
Don’t even bring a picture or a trinket from another god into this relationship!
Don’t lay your body before some other god; don’t serve another god.
I am YHWH your God—El Qanna.
The sins of the father I will lay on his kids and grandkids and great-grandkids for those who hate me. But to the ones who love me and watch over my instructions, I will show them love and kindness, for them and for their kin for as long as you can imagine!
My dad experienced the fierce consequences of his ancestors’ failure to keep faith with the living God. He was among the generations haunted by the sins of their fathers. He also saw another side to El Qanna, an abundance of promised love and protection. By placing his life, and thereby his family’s future, under the protection of El Qanna, he broke an ancestral curse. El Qanna no longer stood over our family as a warning. Now the Jealous God was the protection around our home, the fire that warmed our affections, the love that helped our family weather many storms.
Every time I look at my own children, every time I count their blessings until I run out of breath, I feel surrounded by El Qanna, whose fierce love has held us for so long.
John Jay Alvaro is a pastor in Pasadena, CA where he leads the community of First Baptist Church. He also works with men at the intersection of masculinity, embodiment and spirituality. He shares his work at johnjayalvaro.com.
