Israeli Experts Assess Jewish Heritage Sites in Azerbaijan
Conservation concerns at historic Jewish burial sites in Azerbaijan prompted an Israeli archaeological delegation to assess preservation needs and explore technical cooperation with local authorities during a Nov. 1–5, 2026, visit. The Israel Antiquities Authority team examined eight archaeological and historical locations at the invitation of Azerbaijan’s State Tourism Agency, with support from Israel’s Foreign Ministry.
One of the main sites inspected was the ancient Jewish cemetery in Shamakhi, where specialists identified unstable tombstones and lichen covering stone surfaces and inscriptions. According to the Israel Antiquities Authority, the gravestones contain valuable historical information about the area's Jewish community.
The delegation also discussed practical conservation measures, including detailed documentation of heritage sites, the possible use of 3D modeling, and training programs for local conservation and planning teams.
Beyond Shamakhi, the experts visited Muju, a village that previously had a Jewish community, and examined its cemetery. Their itinerary also included Jewish heritage locations in the Quba region, alongside other significant Azerbaijani historical landmarks.
The delegation's wider archaeological program covered the 18th-century Sheki Khan’s Palace, the Diri Baba Mausoleum, and the Archaeological Park of Ancient Gabala.
Ami Shahar, deputy director of the Israel Antiquities Authority’s Conservation Administration, described the potential application of Israeli technological expertise to historical preservation.
"Israel is known worldwide for its innovation and high-tech. That same creative thinking we embed into heritage preservation," said Ami Shahar, deputy director of the authority’s Conservation Administration.
He also highlighted Israel's experience preserving archaeological remains and historical artifacts in a region shaped by multiple religious traditions.
"In a country that is the cradle of the culture of three religions, we have developed vast knowledge and experience, with innovative working methods and solutions for preserving ancient sites and artifacts," he added.
Israeli Heritage Minister Amichai Eliyahu emphasized the importance of protecting Jewish burial grounds and historical locations as part of preserving the history of Jewish communities in Azerbaijan.
"The preservation of cemeteries and Jewish heritage sites in Azerbaijan is part of our responsibility to preserve the story of Jewish communities and pass it on to future generations," said Eliyahu.
He also addressed the broader value of cooperation between Israeli and Azerbaijani heritage professionals, particularly in sharing conservation expertise and respecting the historical backgrounds of both countries.
"Continued cooperation in the preservation of all heritage sites allows professionals from both countries to share knowledge and experience, out of respect for the history and culture of both peoples," he added.
