TITLE: Here’s Some Video Game Conversation Starters That’s Not About GTA 6
OUTLET: Kotaku

It’s the Thanksgiving holiday weekend. Millions of people will soon be gathering with family, catching everybody up on the goings-on in their lives over the past year. A subset of those people will be gamers, who may be subject to blank stares from aunts and uncles whose conception of the multibillion-dollar industry has not progressed beyond calling every console a “Nintendo” or confused inquiries from relatives who’ve just recently been made aware that video games are a thing by all the breathless coverage of GTA 6 on network television.
Both of those people are my mom.
She has never really taken an interest in what I do, and while that stings a bit, she is at least happy that I am happy in my chosen profession and that’s good enough for me.
In one of the exceedingly rare times that she has ever asked me directly about my job, she called me the other day asking if I was “excited” for the pending release of GTA 6. She used stronger language than “excited” but I’m not gonna repeat exactly what she said to me because it’s something no child should ever hear their parent say to them in any context but it involved “cream” and “drawers” so draw your own conclusions.
I explained to her that 1) ew and 2) I’m not excited per se, but I am interested to see what happens with this game and how we talk about it. But I also told her that despite the sensational coverage of the game, its development price tag, and its status as the most anticipated game this decade…it’s not for me. I’ve never been into those games and I don’t see GTA 6 changing that stance much. We quickly moved on to other topics.
At first, this exchange reopened that unhealed wound of “I wish you took a greater interest in the thing I’ve built a life on.” If she had, she might know that I’m more of a Kingdom Hearts girlie, the game I used to play by ferrying my PS2 from its box under my bed to the 12-inch tube TV in her room when I was 16 years old. But as I think about it now, even though she’s wildly missed the mark on my tastes and preferences, I actually really appreciate that she asked at all. She saw a video game on TV and thought, “Oh hey, my daughter does video games, let me talk to her about that.” That was her attempt at connecting with me, and it’s way more than a lot of other parents do when it comes to engaging with their children’s passion professions, so thanks, mom.
You, too, might be a person who has a gamer in your life who you’ll have the chance to catch up with this holiday weekend. And while there’s a very good chance that, unlike me, they’re actually into GTA 6, here’s a quick list of topics to broach just in case they’re not.
What’s your take on Big Walk?
Ask this question and, depending on the gamer you target with this, you’ll either get an excited offer to play or a thoughtful discussion on the ethics of using generative AI in video game development.
Have you heard about BioWare?
Be careful with this one. Whether they have or they haven’t, they’re about to cry.
If it’s the ‘Final’ Fantasy, why are there 16 of them?
This is a fun one because you’re about to sit with your gamer for hours as they try to hash out exactly how many Final Fantasy games there are. Bring snacks.
I’m thinking about getting into Warhammer, do you have any suggestions on where to start?
Start with the book Horus Rising and don’t stop reading until you’ve reached the—wait, how’d this question get in here?
And finally, the best question you can ask any gamer in your life:
Hey, would you like to go play a video game together?
Yeah mom, let’s go.
