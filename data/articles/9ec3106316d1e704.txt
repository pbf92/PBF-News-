TITLE: ‘Grand Theft Auto VI’ Review: A Bloody, Boundary-Shifting Reclamation of the American Dream
OUTLET: Variety

“Whereas the beautiful is limited, the sublime is limitless.”
Philosopher Immanuel Kant published this in his seminal 1781 text “Critique of Pure Reason,” and it’s amazing he had such insight 245 years before he could throw an iguana in the face of a disorderly driver in “Grand Theft Auto VI.”
The game, now available from Rockstar, approached $4 billion in sales on its launch day and has already had a seismic impact on pop culture. It presents an absolutely enormous open and free world, making Leonida, a lightly fictionalized version of Florida, a playground for murder, chaos and carjacking.
The primary narrative is the love story of Jason and Lucia and their descent into the local crime scene. It’s compelling and emotional, with action-packed missions coming from the jump. Consider the first proper challenge, “Gone Fishing”: After boating around to pick up dropped packages, you must rescue your contact from the clutches of hillbillies who live on a “Tiger King”-esque compound. You can either stealthily move through the patchy foliage and quietly knock out your foes, or come in guns blazing, stealing vehicles and running folks down on the property. A brilliant score, courtesy of French electronic duo Justice, punctuates the most stressful moments, and only once you escape in a seaplane does your heart stop beating. This is all before you’re fully taught the function of the buttons on your controller.
It evokes a thrilling set-piece from a Michael Bay movie, and it’s one of over 90 main missions, ranging from death-defying ambulance hijackings and shark attacks to rogue excavator demolitions and ticking-clock armed robberies.
Not only is the scale vast, but the specifics are treated reverentially. Be it the swampy backwoods or the neon-drenched city lights, Leonida is unmistakably built around the lore of the “Florida Man.” Scores of non-playable characters swarm the neighborhoods, with unique body shapes and quips alike. The radio is filled with retro hits, original music that evokes “Miami Vice”-era swagger, and endless talk from a murderer’s row of comic minds like Janelle James, Sarah Sherman, Jason Sudeikis and “Weird Al” Yankovic.
Even the mild modifications from “GTA 5” add challenges that don’t disrupt the fun but rather urge the player to be a bit more clever. Not every car can be hot-wired instantly; some take time to create a spark with the wires, while newer models won’t budge without a fob. How needlessly violent you are influences the story tree and might lead to dire consequences for the lovebirds. Even a slightly tougher shooting system encourages patience and skill over blind mayhem.
Beyond the brilliantly staged missions, it cannot be overstated how the freedom to do anything on the colossal open map allows for astounding adventures and fun. Crime sprees aside, Jason and Lucia can work out, play basketball, putt at mini golf, shoot pool, race drones, go parachuting, shop, go hunting, fire at the shooting range and dozens of other activities. Your life is a movie, and you’re the auteur. Sure, AI can spit out a junky-looking scene from a prompt, but “GTA 6” truly democratizes a cinematic canvas.
Spend the day doing upkeep on your home and punctuate it with stabbing, à la Chantal Akerman’s masterpiece “Jeanne Dielman, 23 quai du Commerce, 1080 Bruxelles.” Fending off an outrageously large crab attack feels like a surreal, Southern-fried homage to Denis Villeneuve’s “Enemy.” The most destructive can engage in their own “Purge.” Hell, stage a sequel to Harmony Korine’s “Spring Breakers” well before Bella Thorne gets hers off the ground.
But what does this limitless gameplay add up to beyond a choose-your-own-adventure for rifle-toting louts? In the 13 years since “Grand Theft Auto 5” was released, we’ve had a cultural sea change wherein the “greed is good” ethos of the ’80s has come roaring back: Billionaires are deified, even as workers jockey for bathroom breaks; politicians advocate for eschewing societal norms and conduct in order to get ahead; and AI anxiety has made it unclear if a waning job market will ever recover.
Yet the world of “Grand Theft Auto VI” offers unique control. The American Dream seems increasingly distant, as you pull yourself up by your bootstraps and never prosper, but there’s a draw in living out your dreams in a pristine simulation of real life. As the scales get tilted, taking destiny into your hands — legal or not — becomes increasingly appealing.
Conservative commenters dinged the game for inspiring people to skip work and glorifying violence to kids. Yet the storytelling serves a purpose. The fantasy of being the king of your block is alive in “GTA 6.” Call it a societal pressure valve, but time will tell if the adventures of Jason and Lucia will inspire real-life attempts to take the power back.
