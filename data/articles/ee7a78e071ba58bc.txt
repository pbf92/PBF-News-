TITLE: ESPN's first Super Bowl will play live in IMAX theaters
OUTLET: PPC Land

ESPN, The Walt Disney Company, the National Football League and IMAX have agreed to put the Super Bowl on IMAX screens for the first time, according to an announcement Disney published on November 23, 2026. ESPN's telecast of Super Bowl LXI on Sunday, February 14, 2027, will be shown in what Disney describes as "hundreds of IMAX locations in the United States and select international markets, including Australia, New Zealand and Brazil." Disney calls it "the largest live event in IMAX history."
It is also ESPN's first Super Bowl. The network has never produced the game in what Disney calls "ESPN's 47-year history," and the cinema version now sits beside the main telecast on ESPN and ABC, a stream on Disney+, coverage on Univision and four alternate versions of the broadcast on other Disney and NFL channels.
What the announcement leaves out may matter to advertisers as much as what it contains. There are no ticket prices, no screen count and no word on whether the commercial breaks that brands pay millions of dollars for will appear on a cinema screen.
In Short
The Super Bowl will be shown live in hundreds of IMAX cinemas for the first time on February 14, 2027, carrying ESPN's broadcast of the game, the halftime show headlined by JAY-Z, and the coverage before and after it. It matters for brands because the most expensive broadcast on American television is gaining a new kind of audience, people who bought a ticket to watch together, and Disney has not said whether the ads will be shown to them. Tickets are expected to go on sale in January 2027, with a list of theaters and ticket details promised in the coming weeks.
What Disney announced
The release sits on Disney's corporate newsroom under the Entertainment and ESPN categories. Two summary lines open it. The first reads: "Super Bowl LXI to be presented across hundreds of IMAX theaters in the United States and select international markets - the largest live event in IMAX history." The second describes a "Four-hour presentation" that "will feature ESPN's main Super Bowl LXI game telecast, plus the Apple Music Super Bowl LXI Halftime Show headlined by 25-time GRAMMY Award winner JAY-Z, and pregame and postgame coverage."
This is a single showing, not a run. According to Disney, "The one-day only event will begin at 6 p.m. ET," and the full package amounts to "a more than four-hour comprehensive presentation of the Super Bowl experience in a premium theatrical environment." The theaters show ESPN's main telecast rather than a version produced for cinemas.
The voices in the auditorium will be familiar ones. ESPN's Monday Night Football team of Joe Buck, Troy Aikman, Lisa Salters and Laura Rutledge will call the game, according to Disney, so the play-by-play heard on an IMAX sound system is the same commentary carried on ESPN's main telecast.
Two executives are quoted. "Watching the Super Bowl with your fellow fans is one of the great shared experiences in sports, and this first-of-its-kind collaboration with the NFL and IMAX takes it to a historic new scale," said Ken Potrock, President, Major Event Integration for The Walt Disney Company. "With ESPN's first Super Bowl, we're reaching fans across an unprecedented range of platforms and meeting them wherever they want to be, with unique moments that only Disney and ESPN can deliver."
Rich Gelfond, CEO of IMAX, framed the deal around his company's ambitions beyond film. "The Super Bowl is a one-of-a-kind global cultural event imbued with spectacle, sound, drama, and heroes - all the makings of a great IMAX experience," he said. "This is a milestone for IMAX that further elevates our standing as a premier global platform for entertainment and events, and we're excited to work in partnership with Disney, ESPN and the NFL to give football fans an unforgettable night."
The details on record
How the theatrical event is set to unfold
According to Disney, the event moves through four stages between the announcement and kickoff.
- Promotion. "A promotional spot and materials supporting the theatrical event are expected to begin appearing in theaters during Thanksgiving week." Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, November 26, 2026, so the cinema campaign begins within days of the announcement.
- Locations and ticketing details. "Additional details, including a list of participating theater locations and ticketing information, will be announced in the coming weeks."
- Ticket sales. "IMAX theater tickets for Super Bowl LXI are expected to go on sale in January 2027." The release points buyers to IMAX.COM or to "local theaters where IMAX tickets are sold."
- Game day. The presentation starts at 6 p.m. ET on February 14, 2027, and runs for more than four hours, carrying the pregame coverage, the game, the halftime show and the postgame coverage.
What 6 p.m. ET means outside the United States
The release gives a single start time and does not say whether the screenings in Australia, New Zealand and Brazil will be live or shown later at a local evening hour. If they run live, the start falls at very different points in the week.
A live screening in Sydney or Auckland would be a Monday morning or midday event. Brazil is the only named international market where the game would land on a Sunday evening.
One game, many screens
Disney presents IMAX as an addition rather than a replacement. "Expanding into IMAX, in addition to ESPN, ABC, Disney+ and Univision, extends the reach of the Super Bowl across more platforms than ever, while creating an entirely new opportunity for fans to come together and experience the NFL's premier game in a communal setting," according to the release.
That list has been growing all year. A Disney announcement dated October 7, 2026, listed beside the release on the newsroom page, carried the headline "Disney+ to Stream Super Bowl LXI, Joining ESPN and ABC to Give Fans More Ways to Watch." When Disney reported its fiscal 2026 results on November 17, the company said ESPN would carry Super Bowl LXI on ESPN and ABC, with Disney+ audiences in the US and more than 55 international markets, and chief executive Josh D'Amaro listed ESPN, ABC, ESPN Deportes, the ESPN App and Disney+ as outlets on the earnings call.
The release also restates the alternate versions of the broadcast that ESPN had already announced. Each pairs the same game with different presenters or graphics.
NFL Network's place on that list is not a coincidence of scheduling. ESPN agreed in August 2025 to acquire NFL Network in exchange for a 10% stake, and the transaction closed on January 31, 2026, leaving Disney with an effective 72% of ESPN, Hearst with 18% and the NFL with 10%. The league is therefore both the owner of the game and a shareholder in the broadcaster selling it. Three of the four partners named in the IMAX release are tied together through that ownership structure; IMAX is the outside party.
Disney's phrase "communal setting" describes something the television business has long found hard to count. Co-viewing, several people watching one screen, is the gap Nielsen tried to narrow when it tested wearable meters on panelists during Super Bowl LX, a pilot that was set to run through the first half of 2026 and was not yet used as trading currency. A cinema full of ticket holders is co-viewing at its most concentrated. Whether those audiences will be counted in Super Bowl LXI's ratings, and by whom, is not addressed in the release.
What it means for advertisers
Advertising is absent from the announcement. So what happens to the ad breaks? Disney does not say whether the national commercials that run during ESPN's telecast will appear on IMAX screens, whether the breaks will be filled with other material, or whether advertisers who bought time in the game will receive anything for the theatrical audience.
The question has a price attached. Brands paid $8 million for 30-second spots during Super Bowl LX on NBC in February 2026, up from $7 million per 30 seconds during the 2025 broadcast. Nielsen counted 128.2 million viewers for the Super Bowl LX halftime show, and the game drew new kinds of buyers: Anthropic placed two spots in that broadcast to say Claude would stay free of ads. For the Disney game, Ferrero has booked two Super Bowl LXI spots, for Nutella and Kinder Bueno. Disney has not published a price for Super Bowl LXI inventory in this release.
There are two plausible outcomes, and the release rules out neither. If the commercials play in theaters, a Super Bowl spot reaches a seated audience on one of the largest screens available, in a room where nobody can change the channel. If they do not, the IMAX audience becomes a slice of the game's viewers that advertisers cannot reach at all. Either way, a buyer weighing a commitment of that size has a reason to ask.
Sports advertising is where Disney's pricing power currently sits. In fiscal 2026, Disney's Sports advertising grew 7% to $4.77 billion, with higher rates adding 7 points even as the number of impressions fell, and Disney named Super Bowl LXI as a reason to expect a strong second quarter of fiscal 2027 in the Sports segment. The Super Bowl is a textbook case of appointment viewing: an audience that shows up at a set time, live, for the broadcast itself.
The cinema side has its own numbers. US and Canadian theaters sold about 651 million tickets in 2025, up 9% from 2024. IMAX belongs to a narrower tier. It is one brand within premium large format, the category of oversized auditoriums with brighter projection and upgraded sound that are sold at a surcharge over standard tickets. A Super Bowl presentation puts a live television event inside that premium tier for the first time, on a Sunday that is also Valentine's Day. What IMAX will charge for the seats, and how the revenue is divided among the four partners, is not disclosed.
IMAX screens are not reserved for Hollywood features. The LISA music documentary Always Lalisa opened in IMAX and cinemas worldwide on October 12, 2026 for a limited engagement before moving to YouTube Premium. A live, four-hour sports broadcast is a different proposition: Gelfond's own description of IMAX as "a premier global platform for entertainment and events" signals where the company wants the business to go.
What the release does not say
The announcement gives no ticket price, no number of participating screens beyond "hundreds," no theater list and no complete list of international markets beyond the three it names. It does not say whether screenings outside the United States will be live, whether the commercial breaks will be shown, how theater audiences will be measured, or what financial terms the four partners agreed. Disney says ticketing and location details will follow "in the coming weeks."
