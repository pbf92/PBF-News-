TITLE: RABIT in a hat: AI platform augments electronic health record data to predict health and disease at a population scale
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

A new AI platform can estimate a patient’s plasma protein profile and has the potential to predict their risk of chronic diseases by analyzing and biologically “augmenting” commonly available electronic health record (EHR) data. The technique stands out from previous AI approaches due to its ability to work with EHR data and to reconstruct proteomic states in large patient groups, opening the possibility of more accurate disease prediction at both individual and population scales. EHR data offers a valuable source of clinical history, and some groups are designing AI models that attempt to scan through this mass of data to find meaningful predictors of disease. However, most disease prediction platforms lack in-depth data on a patient’s proteome, or their collection of proteins. Integrating proteomics into models helps to generate accurate predictions, but proteomic cohorts tend to be small due to financial constraints and logistical challenges with sample acquisition, storage, and quality control. To sidestep the need for labor-intensive proteomics, David Seong and colleagues developed RABIT, which builds a high-dimensional space that represents a patient’s inferred plasma proteome simply by parsing routine EHR data. They first trained RABIT to infer more than 2,900 proteins in plasma using paired EHR data and plasma samples from 53,017 patients. Then, they applied RABIT to EHR data from 449,223 individuals, finding that it improved predictions of the five-year risk of chronic kidney disease and 13 other conditions. RABIT performed well when retested in a larger group of 1,038,419 patients and captured organ-specific changes linked to aging. Finally, the model also identified an immune signature linked to cytomegalovirus in a small cohort of 985 people and successfully extended it to a larger clinical population, finding a link with brain tumors. “As health systems continue to accumulate longitudinal clinical data, biologically augmented EHR representations may offer a practical bridge between molecular insight and real-world patient care at population scale,” Seong et al. conclude.
