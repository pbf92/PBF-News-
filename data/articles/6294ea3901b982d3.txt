TITLE: Andy Burnham issues statement after Poundland goes into administration with 11,000 jobs at risk
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

Andy Burnham issues statement after Poundland goes into administration with 11,000 jobs at risk
The high street retailer, which has 600 stores, now faces an uncertain future
Prime Minister Andy Burnham has issued a statement after high street chain Poundland went into administration on We
The move has left 11,000 jobs at risk, after the retailer failed to find a buyer by Wednesday's (November 25) deadline. Corporate finance experts AlixPartners have been hired as administrators.
Speaking shortly after the news was announced on Wednesday afternoon, Mr Burnham said: "Poundland is part of the fabric of high streets and communities up and down the country.
“The news that it has gone into administration just weeks before Christmas will be deeply concerning for workers and their families.
“We want every effort made to secure a deal that protects jobs and keeps as many shops open as possible.
“While it is worrying, going into administration does give other potential buyers the opportunity to engage and we will work hard with all the relevant parties to ensure the best possible outcome.”

A Government spokesman added: “Poundland stores are a vital part of many high streets and communities.
“They are valued by families for their affordable goods and the local jobs they provide.
“News of its administration is very concerning particularly for the almost 10,000 workers and their families. We stand ready to support affected staff and want to see as many jobs and stores saved as possible.
“We understand there is still strong interest from bidders for the whole business and parts of the business. The administrators must now be given time and space to do their job.”
Poundland, which has around 600 stores, will continue to trade as the administrators continue to seek a sale of the retailer and its assets. Of those stores there are 14 in Greater Manchester.
A spokeswoman for the administrators said: “We appreciate this is a deeply concerning time for all involved – customers, suppliers and, in particular, Poundland colleagues.
“Our priority now is to work with all stakeholders, including representatives from Government and Usdaw, as we identify the best possible route forward.”
Gordon Brothers bought the business for £1 early last year before overseeing a major overhaul, which included shutting more than 100 shops.
Executives from Gordon Brothers met with Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds on Monday for an update on the process amid hopes that a sale could still be secured.

A spokesman for Gordon Brothers said: “First and foremost, our thoughts are with Poundland colleagues. We recognise how difficult the continued uncertainty is for them.
“Efforts to preserve as many jobs and stores as possible will continue, led by the administrators.
“Commercial negotiations with interested parties for both the whole of the company and with established retailers interested in acquiring substantial portions of the business will now continue, led by the administrators, and conclude through the administration process.
“Despite a reduced cost base, looking forward, the business is still vulnerable to inflationary pressures or any downturn in sales and margin.
“We recognise and we are thankful for all the hard work and effort by management and colleagues throughout.”
