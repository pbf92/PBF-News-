TITLE: 'Corrupts the core message of our faith': CofE distances itself from Tommy Robinson carols
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The Church of England has distanced itself from a ‘Unite the Kingdom’ carol concert being organised by Tommy Robinson, after online claims it was involved.
Robinson has urged supporters to “put Christ back into Christmas” at the event in London on 12 December, after around 1,000 people attended the first carols last year. Promotional materials suggest a “Church of England priest” will be one of the speakers.
Bishops on the Church’s Promoting Unity in our Nation working group have warned “such events misuse Christian symbols and corrupt the core message of our faith”. They have instead reiterated their welcome for all across the country at Christmas.
A statement said: “The Church of England invites everyone to celebrate Christmas, and thousands of carol services will take place in parish churches, cathedrals and other venues across the country, all focused on the wonderful gift of Jesus Christ.
“As the Church of England, we seek to serve everyone who lives in this country. We are patriotic in our service of the nation, and we will continue to build on our rich Christian heritage. We call on all our churches to be bold in their worship of Jesus Christ, their welcome of people from every background, and their service of all in need this Christmas.
“Contrary to recent claims, the Church of England is not joining the event being organised by Tommy Robinson and his supporters in central London.
“Individual clergy are, of course, free to act according to their conscience; but as a group of bishops we are concerned that such events misuse Christian symbols and corrupt the core message of our faith, which is the good news of God's love for all people. We remain committed to serving and welcoming all who call this country home.”
The working group is chaired by Bishop of Leicester, Rt Rev Martyn Snow. He is joined by Bishop of Kirkstall, Rt Rev Arun Arora; Acting Bishop of Southwark, Rt Rev Rosemarie Mallett; Bishop of Barking, Rt Rev Lynne Cullens; and Bishop of Willesden, Rt Rev Lusa Nsenga-Ngoy.
The far-right activist has called the carols “my farewell event” as he is due to be sentenced for harassing journalists on 16 December. He is seeking to appeal the conviction.
Robinson’s ‘Unite the Kingdom’ rally in September 2025 drew up to 150,000 attendees, according to some estimates. A number of church leaders criticised the former English Defence League leader and the march for its use of Christian symbols, believing crosses and slogans were utilised to “exclude”.
Christian group Better Story will be gathering on the steps of St Martin-in-the-Fields by Trafalgar Square on the morning of Robinson’s event, “to remember that Christmas is an act of divine solidarity with the stranger and the outcast.”
