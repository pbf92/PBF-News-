TITLE: BBC removes Motherland episode from iPlayer after David Walliams allegations
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

BBC removes Motherland episode from iPlayer after David Walliams allegations
The episode of the hit comedy saw a character make reference to Walliams
The BBC has removed an episode of the hit comedy Motherland from the iPlayer in the fallout of allegations surrounding David Walliams
The 55-year-old comedian brought legal action against Independent Television News (ITN) and Guardian News and Media (GNM), publisher of The Guardian, ahead of the two organisations reporting allegations against him, including of sexual misconduct and potential criminal activity.
The High Court in London was previously told by lawyers for the media organisations that the allegations involved Walliams’ alleged ‘abuse of power and status’ to contact young women and try to engage in ‘inappropriate and exploitative sexual activities’.

Walliams, who denies the claims, unsuccessfully sought an injunction to prevent these publications because of the risk he would take his own life after two previous suicide attempts. The anonymity that the author was granted was lifted after his injunction bid was dismissed and GNM and ITN published their investigations.
In a letter understood to have been sent several months ago by Walliams to journalists working on the ITV News and Guardian investigations, he wrote: “Looking back, I realise that at times I behaved thoughtlessly towards other people I had casual sexual relationships with."
It was confirmed last week that the The Metropolitan Police has launched an investigation after receiving 'a number of allegations of non-recent serious sexual offences' against Walliams.
On Friday (November 13), they said it had received a number of allegations of non-recent serious sexual offences involving a man now in his 50s. Those who have come forward are being supported by specialist officers.
Detective Superintendent Mike Cagney, whose team is leading the investigation, said: "We would encourage anyone who has been the victim of a sexual offence to come forward and speak to us, no matter when it happened.
“We know reporting allegations of this nature can be daunting, but specialist officers are on hand to provide support, answer questions and guide you through the process."

Walliams rose to fame working alongside Matt Lucas on the BBC sketch comedy series Little Britain, which ran from 2003 until 2006. They then created and starred in Come Fly With Me, which aired on the BBC for one season in 2010.
Both shows are currently unavailable on iPlayer, as is a 2020 episode of Who Do You Think You Are? featuring Walliams. Adaptations of his best-selling children’s books including Gangsta Granny and Billionaire Boy are also unavailable on the service.
As well as editing an episode of Gavin And Stacey which made reference to him, the broadcaster also removed the animated adaptation of Room On The Broom, in which Walliams voices a frog alongside Gillian Anderson as the witch and Simon Pegg as the narrator. His character has just three lines in the 30-minute film.
It’s now emerged that an episode of the hit comedy Motherland, which ran from 2016 to 2022 and spawned the spin-off Amandaland, has been removed from the BBC’s streaming service. The episode in question is the second episode of the second series.
Titled ‘Soft Opening’, the episode sees Julia [Anna Maxwell Martin] reluctantly agreeing to help Amanda [Lucy Punch] launch her new shop, where Kevin [Paul Ready] wants to sell his children's book.
One moment in the episode sees Kevin comparing his self-published children's book to Walliams’ work, saying: "A bit like David Walliams, but less sinister."
When contacted by The Manchester Evening News, a BBC spokesperson reiterated their recent comments about removing content associated with Walliams.
The spokesperson said: "These are serious allegations. As such, the BBC is looking carefully at all material featuring and written by David Walliams on our platforms. We are in the process of removing some programmes, including Little Britain, whilst this review is underway."
The episode remains available to stream on both Disney+ and Netflix at the time of writing.
Walliams has not yet responded to the allegations. But in a letter understood to have been sent several months ago to journalists working on the investigations, he denied being a 'sexual predator'.
In response to the investigation, he said: "Looking back, I realise that at times I behaved thoughtlessly towards other people I had casual sexual relationships with... I realise I did not always take people’s feelings properly into account. That is something I regret.
"And whilst a power imbalance is inevitable when one party is famous, abusing that power is something else entirely and it is something that I never did...
"I am not a sexual predator... I have not broken the law... I am devastated that some of the women I met ten to twenty years ago have now expressed hurt or anger towards me."
