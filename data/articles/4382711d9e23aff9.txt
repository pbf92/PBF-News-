TITLE: Anthropic Raises $81.3 Billion In Record IPO
OUTLET: CIO Africa

Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the Claude family of AI models, has raised approximately $81.3 billion through an initial public offering (IPO), marking the largest stock market debut on record and bringing the company’s financial performance under greater public scrutiny.
The company, co-founded by Dario Amodei and Daniela Amodei, offered 980 million shares at $83 each, exceeding its initial target price range of $70 to $78. Strong investor demand prompted Anthropic to increase both the offer price and the number of shares available. It also raised an additional $26 billion through a private placement.
The IPO surpassed the $75 billion raised by SpaceX in June, setting a new record for funds raised through a public listing. Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker symbol ANT. Its first trade was recorded at $111.50, approximately 34% above the IPO price. The stock subsequently reached $115.10 before closing its first trading session at $97.17.

However, the initial enthusiasm proved short-lived. On Friday, Anthropic shares fell to $83.05, just five cents above the IPO price, as a wider sell-off affected artificial intelligence and semiconductor stocks. The company’s shares recovered to close at $88.30, leaving them approximately 6% above the offering price but significantly below their first-day peak.
The broader technology sell-off also affected major semiconductor companies, with Nvidia and Broadcom each recording declines of approximately 12% during Friday’s session. The market reaction highlighted the volatility surrounding AI-related stocks, even as investors continue to commit substantial capital to companies developing advanced AI systems.
For Anthropic, however, the implications of the listing extend beyond its share price. The transition from private ownership to public markets means the company must provide regular financial disclosures, giving customers, investors and competitors greater visibility into its business operations.

Enterprise AI demand drives Anthropic’s growth
Anthropic’s IPO prospectus provides insight into the scale of its commercial expansion and the growing role of enterprise customers in its business. According to figures from the filing reported by Fortune, Anthropic generated $16.28 billion in revenue during the first half of 2026, representing growth of approximately 1,300%. Enterprise customers accounted for roughly 80% of that revenue, contributing approximately $13.1 billion through payments for AI services.
The concentration of revenue among a relatively small number of customers is another important consideration. Anthropic’s five largest customers accounted for approximately a quarter of its revenue during the first nine months of the year.
For businesses deploying Claude across customer service, software development and internal operations, these figures offer a clearer picture of the commercial scale behind the AI tools they are increasingly incorporating into their operations. However, the prospectus also highlights potential uncertainties around customer retention.
A significant proportion of Anthropic’s revenue comes from contracts that allow customers to reduce their spending or terminate their arrangements with limited notice. This creates uncertainty around how much of the company’s reported revenue will translate into predictable future income.
To address this, Anthropic is encouraging enterprise customers to move towards multiyear agreements that include minimum spending commitments in exchange for discounted pricing.
As of June 2026, approximately $10.8 billion had been committed under these arrangements.
The shift towards longer-term contracts could provide Anthropic with more predictable revenue while giving enterprise customers greater certainty over their access to Claude services.
Cloud commitments raise questions about AI economics
Anthropic’s financial disclosures also reveal the scale of infrastructure investment required to sustain its AI business.
The company has committed to at least $475 billion in future cloud computing purchases, reflecting the substantial computing capacity needed to train and operate increasingly sophisticated AI models.
Of this amount, at least $389.9 billion is pledged to major technology and infrastructure partners, including Amazon, Alphabet, SpaceXAI and Microsoft.
The commitments illustrate the relationship between AI developers and the cloud infrastructure providers supporting their operations. While Anthropic generates revenue from enterprise subscriptions and API usage, delivering these services requires significant ongoing expenditure on computing resources.
Despite these obligations, the company reported $974 million in operating income for the second quarter of 2026. It also disclosed cash holdings of $112.4 billion as of late June.
Anthropic says it generates more than $2 in revenue for every $1 spent on computing power, offering investors an indication of how it measures the economics of operating its AI infrastructure.
The involvement of cloud providers extends beyond commercial supply agreements. Amazon and Alphabet are also investors in Anthropic and are expected to hold approximately 28% of the company following the IPO.
This creates a close relationship between Anthropic’s growth and the infrastructure companies providing the computing capacity required to support it.
Public listing puts AI governance under scrutiny
The IPO also places Anthropic’s corporate governance and stated AI safety commitments under greater public attention.
Anthropic operates as a public benefit corporation, a legal structure that requires it to consider its stated public mission alongside its financial obligations to shareholders.
During the Nasdaq opening ceremony, Chief Executive Officer Dario Amodei reiterated that the company would not compromise its principles in response to business or political pressure.
He also addressed the company’s approach to developing and releasing AI systems, emphasising the need to balance technological progress with safety considerations.
The listing comes amid growing debate over the governance of advanced AI systems and the responsibilities of companies developing them. Earlier in November, a group of AI safety non-profit organisations filed a lawsuit against Anthropic, Google and other technology companies in an effort to slow the development of increasingly powerful AI systems.
For enterprises using Claude, the public listing introduces a different level of visibility into the company supplying their AI capabilities.
Quarterly financial disclosures will provide information on revenue growth, operating performance, customer concentration and infrastructure commitments. These are factors that technology leaders can consider when assessing the long-term sustainability of their AI vendors.
Anthropic’s planned inclusion in the Nasdaq 100 index on December 21 will further increase its visibility among institutional investors.
While its first two trading sessions demonstrated how quickly investor sentiment can shift, the longer-term significance of the IPO may lie in the accountability that comes with operating as a publicly traded AI company.
For businesses building critical operations around Claude, the change means their AI supplier will no longer be assessed solely through product performance and commercial engagements. Its financial health, spending commitments and ability to sustain its infrastructure will also become matters of public record.
