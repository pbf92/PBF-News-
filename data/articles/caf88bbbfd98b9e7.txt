TITLE: Michael Owen exclusive: “Mauricio Pochettino alone won’t fix Manchester United”
OUTLET: Press Box PR

Former Manchester United striker Michael Owen has spoken exclusively to London-based sports PR agency Press Box PR about the current state of the club he lifted the title with in 2010/11. Owen, who has followed Manchester United very closely as a Premier League broadcaster, believes the problems run deeper than the dugout: “There’s more change that needs to be made at that club.” Read his full thoughts below. 
 Key takeaways for fans
- Managerial Warning: He explains why Mauricio Pochettino alone will not be enough to fix the problems at Manchester United
- Title Drought: He discusses the ongoing Premier League title drought at Manchester United while looking at recent similarities for Liverpool and Arsenal 
- Ballon d’Or celebration: He talks Harry Kane’s Ballon d’Or success and explains what he said to the newest member of the club after the ceremony
- Reality TV plans: He talks about his reality series ‘Meet the Owens’ and whether fans can expect a second series
- Horse Racing Ownership: He describes his success in horse ownership and work with current Premier League players
On Manchester United and the arrival of Mauricio Pochettino
Q: What are your thoughts on what is happening at Manchester United?
Michael Owen: “It's not really changed for several years, has it? It's been this type of story for 15 years now since Sir Alex Ferguson left. I obviously do all the TV on the Premier League and beam it out to the rest of the world, so yes, I've been following it.
“It's the big conundrum: what is the right thing to do, and what is the right answer?
“There's been turmoil for a long period of time now and it just remains to be seen whether changing the manager is the answer.
“I can’t imagine that will be the answer. It is far more to do with the fundamental structures there.
“They have sacked the manager because a board member is not going to sack himself, is he?
“You can’t sack the players either. There's only one person you can sack and that's the manager, so he normally gets the brunt of it. 
“We will see, but I think there's more change that needs to be made at that club.”
Q: Thoughts on Mauricio Pochettino at Man Utd?
Michael Owen: “As I say, we've been here before. We've had Mourinho, van Gaal, Moyes, Solskjaer, they have had ex-players, serial winners, and new shiny toys like Amorim.
“They have gone for every single type of manager, and nothing really has worked in 15 years has it?
“I don’t want to put too much of a downer on it. Mauricio Pochettino has a good pedigree and we shall see. But I think there has to be far more meaningful change than just the manager.”
Q: It’s a really sad state of affairs for such a great club, isn't it?
Michael Owen: “It is, but these clubs have cycles. It's always happened. After Arsene Wenger, Arsenal didn't win anything for a long period of time and now they're back.
“When I was getting into the Liverpool team, Liverpool didn't win much at all in the 90s after all that great success in the 70s.
“It just goes in cycles. There's a lot of competition up at the top. And the problem is, you know, there's only one or two teams that can be successful every year. And there's probably six or seven that are expected to be successful.
“You create a drama at the other four or five every season. Chelsea for the last few years, spending all that money and what's happened?
“Even Liverpool, you know, winning the league so clearly, and then all of a sudden, you know, things go really bad. Then it's a change of manager and then it's spending all that money. Then it didn’t work.
“It is not an easy game as we know.”
On Harry Kane’s Ballon d’Or glory
Q: Harry Kane Ballon d'Or recently won the Ballon D’Or, something you achieved at the age of 21. Harry is a remarkable servant for England, isn’t he?
Michael Owen: “Incredible. An absolutely great player. I had just presented the prize to the women’s Ballon D’Or winner. So I was sort of backstage and as soon as he walked off I think I was the first person he saw so it's a nice little moment for us to, the only two living Englishmen to win it.
“It's a special honour, special title, it's probably the rarest trophy that you can win in the game really. I know the World Cups every four years but there's 26 people or 27 people or something that win it. There's only one Ballon d'Or winner every year and a lot of the time who’ve won it multiple times, so there's just not many that go around. It's a special award to win.”
On Manor House Stables and horse ownership
You’ve made Manor House Stables a huge success and introduced some big names from the sporting and football world to horse ownership. What’s the secret?
Michael Owen: “That's one of my main roles at Manor House, obviously: to get the owners in, get people enjoying the game and piece it all together to sell the dream and all the rest of it.
“You need some help from people that are willing to put their hands in their pockets and have that enthusiasm themselves.
“It all started really with the Brighton guys, with James Milner and he was at Liverpool and he had a lot of success with a couple of horses that we trained on his behalf and then he obviously left Liverpool and went to Brighton and spread the word for us and subsequently Brighton lads have had, I don't know, six or seven horses with us but obviously Seagulls are lovely and the best of them.
“Spreading the appeal of the sport to everybody is a huge part of business and at the end of the day racing benefits from what I'm doing, so it is win- win.
“We train for all kinds of people; whether it's local businessmen or women, whether it's syndicates, whether it's partnerships, whether it's individuals, whether it's sporting stars, like Liverpool players and Brighton players.
“We’re also training for the World Snooker champion, we're training for the World Darts champion, we're training for a whole host of people. So, as I say, that's sort of my role and what I feel as if I'm quite good at, that enthusiasm and getting people into the sport.
“The business is going particularly well. We're selling plenty. We went brave again this year and bought a load of yearlings and they're selling really well, so I'm pleased.”
Q: Season one of ‘Meet the Owens’ has just finished on Amazon Prime. How pleased were you with it?
Michael Owen: “It went amazingly. I am really, really, really pleased with it; the family were pleased with it, people in general, and the feedback has just been incredible. The number of viewers has just been mind-blowing. I didn't, in my wildest dreams, expect it to be so popular.
“Hopefully it's helped lots of other people as well because obviously there's a bit for everyone, you know with football or racing or fashion or whatever it is, with my daughter and just general family life.
“So, I'd say that no matter who you are, you can probably watch it and enjoy it. We enjoyed doing it, and we're pleased with the outcome.”
Q: What was the rationale for initially? Was it just to highlight racing initially and then it pivoted a little bit towards more family life.
Michael Owen: “At the start, I wanted to show off my racing style.
“No one outside racing really knew I was involved in horses. The odd person asked me, ‘Do you like horses? Do you own one?’
“Well, actually, we have about 140 in training. So, I just thought, you know what, if I want to grow this business, I've got the easiest opportunity to show it to the world. And I'm very proud of Manor House and everybody that works there and what we've created. So why not do a documentary?
“It did pivot a little bit for a variety of reasons really. I don't think racing backed it as they should. There are a lot of different factions in racing so, it pivoted a little bit more to family life and other things.”
Q: Will there be a Season 2?
Michael Owen: “We've just started speaking about it.  I am keen to, but it is a big old commitment, and obviously you're exposing your family, and you know, for the sake of trying to get your racing stables in the limelight a little bit, you are sort of compromising certain things.
“At the start, I thought to myself, well, you know, it'd be great for the racing stable, it'd be great for racing in general, it'd be great for a lot of things. But then halfway through, I started doubting whether I should have done it because I was just not getting backed at all by the sport.
“So, as I say, it ended up being more of a family documentary with a theme of racing running through it.
“We'll see what the future brings. But I enjoyed it, but as I say, it's a pretty big commitment on family and things like that and obviously the racing stables.”
Q: What was the filming period?
Michael Owen: “It took about 15-16 months for the first one. If we are to do another one, I think we're looking at a region of about 13 or 14 months.
“I thought it could do for racing what Clarkson’s Farm did for farming. That's why I was disappointed with racing.”
Q: In what way did the sport not back you?
Michael Owen: “Barely in any way, shape or form. There was a little bit of access. We were allowed to take our cameras into the Royal Ascot Parade Ring. Certain tracks were really helpful. Chester, our local track, was really helpful.
“But others, and I won't name names, didn’t co-operate.
“Hopefully they get behind us next time, but then again, I'm a little bit stubborn, so I think, well, you didn't want us the first time; now the first time we get millions and millions of viewers and you can see how good it was for racing, well, you can shove it.”
Q: Looking back at the Michael Owen of 21 and 22, and you're now 46, you must be very proud of what you've achieved in both football and afterwards?
Michael Owen: “It's not my style to sit there and ponder what's been great and what hasn't. I just feel really lucky that I've been able to have two genuine passions in my life.
“I wanted to be a footballer all my life. I managed to do that, and then that's, I suppose, given me a real leg up in terms of the next stage.
“You know what I've done at Manor House isn't so special. If you've got a massive passion for it and you're willing to risk and invest and whatever, then I think lots of people can do it.
“But I do count my lucky stars that I've had, as I say, two huge passions in my life and every single day I wake up excited to do what I'm about to do that day. So that's a lovely thing to have, and obviously, you know, I'm never going to kick a ball again. So, the only thing I can do now is try to grow Manor House.”
