TITLE: Cochrane Public Library launches artwork sales
OUTLET: Cochrane Eagle

Local artists displaying their work at the Cochrane Public Library will now have an opportunity to sell their pieces, as the library expands its monthly art gallery program.
The library is introducing artwork sales with its November exhibition, adding another way to support Cochrane and area artists while making their work accessible to the community.
“You go to a lot of art galleries and you can purchase art right off the walls, and we wanted to do the same,” said Carmen Erison, the library’s program and engagement manager.
The gallery initiative launched in August with support from a Bow Rivers Edge Campground Society grant. Following additional work by library staff and the art gallery committee, the sales component is now in place.
“That was always our intention, to be able to provide additional opportunities for artists,” Erison said. “We’re pretty excited that it’s finally going.”
Twenty per cent of artwork sales will go to the library to help cover costs associated with operating the gallery, including advertising, while the remaining proceeds go to the artists.
“Our full intention is not to make profit, but to provide the artists a viable, beautiful space to present and sell their art,” Erison said. “The library is a very natural space to be able to display artwork and we just saw this as another value to our patrons.”
The first artist featured through the new sales opportunity is Cochrane-based artist and fine-art photographer Elina Browerman.
Her exhibition, Recalibration of Memory, brings together everyday objects sourced from thrift shops, estate sales and other overlooked places with classical artistic traditions and contemporary photographic techniques. The work explores the intersection of personal histories and shared experiences.
“We're delighted to feature Elina's work in our gallery space,” Erison said. “From what I’ve heard from all our artists lined up, they are all very excited to have this opportunity.”
Browerman will also introduce another feature to the gallery program with a special artist talk at the library on Nov. 22, offering visitors an opportunity to learn more about her work.
The talk will run from 1 to 2 p.m. and, like the gallery exhibition, is free to attend.
As the program continues to develop, Erison said feedback has been positive, with the library's exhibition calendar nearly full through September of next year.
Those interested in purchasing artwork can visit the library and speak with a staff member about a piece they would like to buy.
Artists interested in participating in the gallery program can find more information at cochranepubliclibrary.ca.
