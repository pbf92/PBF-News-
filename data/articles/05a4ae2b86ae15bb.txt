How Many Endings Does GTA 6 Have? Everything We Know So Far
Contains spoilers for "GTA 6"
The map of "Grand Theft Auto VI" is huge and full of secrets, and Leonida offers amazing side activities that double as massive time sinks. Still, nothing lasts forever, and even the Bonnie and Clyde adventures of Lucia Caminos and Jason Duval will eventually reach their ending. But which ending will it be?
Many great video games have multiple endings, and "GTA VI" is no exception. In fact, players have already found at least six different endings, and it's entirely possible that others will turn up as people dig deeper into the hidden depths of the game.
The way the "GTA VI" endgame unfolds depends on important storyline decisions throughout the game, as well as your own actions during the finale. The amount of money on your BuckMe savings account, the strength of Lucia and Jason's relationship, and the status of the pair's criminal profiles are all contributing factors, too.
The end of the road can be reached in many ways
Lucia and Jason's story comes to an end in the aptly titled mission "The End of the Road," which involves a massive attack at Giorgio Gallo's penthouse and a mad dash to safety along a police-infested highway. It's all very risky, and understandably, all of the endings lead to at least one protagonist dying or being incarcerated. Before we get to the truly depressing stuff, though, let's take a look at the nicer endings where both of Tomás' parents make it out of the game's main story alive.
All of the good endings require you to make it to a bridge with a police roadblock, where you must choose to "swim for it" (head toward the water with the intention of jumping in) instead of the "no surrender" option (charge at the roadblock). From that point on, it's all down to your past actions in the game.
The best ending in "GTA VI" requires low criminal profiles, at least $2.5 million on the BuckMe savings account, and a strong Hot Together relationship. Lucia and Jason also must kill Captain Aldo Méndez before the events of "The End of the Road." This way, Jason will take the fall for the pair's crimes. Lucia takes a deal with the government and remains free, and two years later, she's able to use the BuckMe money to bail Jason out. Finally together and free of trouble, Lucia and Jason get their happy ending and start building a new life with Tomás. If your criminal profile is low but you don't meet the other conditions, Jason still takes the fall — but remains in prison while Lucia and Tomás are seen visiting him.
When things end badly in GTA VI, they end really badly
Lucia and Jason's story arc holds strong hints of impending tragedy throughout the game and many of the endings reflect that. The earliest one actually prevents you from playing the final mission at all: Should you spare Méndez and take his deal in the penultimate story mission "Protective Custody," Lucia and Jason are handcuffed and taken to a swamp, where we later see their bullet-riddled skulls next to a very happy-looking alligator.
Another early Méndez-themed exit happens if you take the deal but try to escape anyway. In this case, Jason will be shot by the police, and Lucia faces two bad choices. She can either die with the love of her life, or survive and end the game the same way she started it: A despairing petty criminal with no means of taking care of her son.
Choosing the "no surrender" option at the roadblock offers arguably the most cinematic "GTA VI" ending. It shows Lucia and Jason charging toward the roadblock and into the gunfire, still holding hands as they die. Even the "swim for it" option can lead to death — If your criminal profile is high enough, Jason will make the mistake of exiting the car with a gun in hand, at which point the cops will immediately open fire at the protagonists.
As mentioned, it's entirely possible that there are more "GTA VI" endings waiting to be discovered. Still, these six endings already give players plenty of reasons to replay those final hours. Not to mention, good cause to keep a few extra saves on hand.
