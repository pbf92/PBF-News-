TITLE: Stock Market Midday, Nov. 16: Nasdaq Tumbles as Wall Street Braces for Nvidia Earnings and Bitcoin Slides
OUTLET: The Motley Fool

Wall Street extended its recent losses on Monday, November 16, as mounting selling pressure across technology and cryptocurrency markets weighed on investor sentiment ahead of Nvidia's highly anticipated earnings report. As of 11:31 a.m. ET, the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -2.49%) led major indexes lower, falling 1.34% to 24,799, while the S&P 500 (^GSPC -1.92%) declined 0.99% to 7,487. The Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI -1.41%) slipped 0.60% to 52,032.
Selling pressure extended beyond equities, with gold declining 0.61% to $3,862.60 and Bitcoin (BTC +0.10%) falling to its lowest level since July. Meanwhile, the benchmark 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.15%, placing additional pressure on equity valuations. Sector performance remained mixed, with real estate leading gains and communication services suffering the steepest losses.
Today's biggest moves
Tyson Foods (TSN +8.72%) bucked the broader market sell-off, jumping 7.01% after the meat producer's encouraging fiscal 2027 guidance overshadowed a fourth-quarter earnings miss.
Meanwhile, Strategy (MSTR -7.70%), formerly MicroStrategy, plunged 6.78% as Bitcoin extended its recent losses. Investors are also weighing the potential selling pressure associated with the company's removal from MSCI's equity indexes, which could reduce passive investment demand for the stock.
Intel (INTC -7.02%) fell 5.82% as selling pressure intensified across the semiconductor industry. With Nvidia scheduled to report earnings tomorrow, investors are becoming increasingly cautious about elevated chipmaker valuations and the sustainability of AI infrastructure spending.
What this means for investors
This week's corporate earnings could prove pivotal in determining whether the recent sell-off represents a temporary correction or the beginning of a more sustained downturn. Nvidia's earnings on November 17 will provide critical insight into the strength of AI infrastructure demand, particularly as investors reassess elevated semiconductor valuations, escalating capital expenditures, and restrictions on chip exports to China. With Bitcoin also falling to its lowest level since July, weakness across speculative assets suggests investors are becoming increasingly selective about where they allocate capital.
Beyond technology, Walmart's November 19 earnings report will offer an important assessment of consumer spending habits heading into the holiday shopping season. Persistent inflation and elevated borrowing costs could increasingly strain household budgets, threatening corporate revenue growth and profitability. With the 10-year Treasury yield now above 5%, businesses face a more challenging financing environment while investors have increasingly attractive alternatives to equities. For a sustainable year-end recovery to materialize, markets will likely need evidence that AI investment remains productive, consumer spending remains resilient, and inflationary pressures are beginning to ease.
