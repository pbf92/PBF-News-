TITLE: Etsy plans to enforce its unedited-photo rule from early 2027
OUTLET: PPC Land

Etsy has loosened the definition of the one unedited photo it requires in most listings and, for the first time, put a date on enforcement. In a policy update dated November 5, 2026 and clarifications posted on November 6, the marketplace said sellers may now crop, resize, watermark or change the file format of that photo, as long as no AI-powered tool does the work. Etsy also said it plans to begin proactively enforcing its Listing Image Requirements in early 2027, with advance notice beforehand. Refund liability in buyer disputes already applies.
The clarifications were posted by James Ossman, Etsy's vice president of Customer Operations, in a pinned comment on the company's community announcement "Important updates to AI on Etsy," whose title now begins "UPDATED NOV 6." The policy page itself, part of Etsy's House Rules, carries the line "Last updated on 05 Nov, 2026." Together with an undated Etsy Help Center article, the documents replace the stricter wording Etsy used when it first set out the rule on October 22, when the Seller Handbook defined the required photo as one where "the photo hasn't been edited or changed in any way."
In Short
Etsy still requires at least one real, unedited photo of the item in most listings, but since early November that photo may be cropped, resized, watermarked or saved in another file format, as long as no AI tool is used. The rule covers most sellers, from handmade goods to craft patterns, with separate exceptions for print-on-demand products, some digital files and grid-based patterns such as cross-stitch. Refunds can already be charged to sellers whose listings break the rule, and Etsy says automatic enforcement is planned for early 2027 after advance notice.
What changed between October 22 and November 6
When Etsy announced the requirement on October 22, the wording left no room for any adjustment. The October 22 Seller Handbook article said the photo must be one that "hasn't been edited or changed in any way." Read literally, that ruled out even trimming the edges of a picture or stamping a shop name on it.
Two weeks of seller questions followed. Ossman's November 6 comment opened: "Thanks for sharing your questions and feedback about Etsy's updated Listing Image Requirements. We've heard several questions about how the unedited-photo requirement applies to different types of listings, and we want to clarify a few of the most common ones." He then listed three points in bold:
- "Your first listing image does not need to be the unedited photo."
- "We've updated the unedited-photo requirement to allow cropping. You can also add watermarks, change the file format, or resize your unedited photo."
- "You only need one unedited photo per listing, even if your listing has multiple variations."
The second point is the only one Etsy itself describes as an update. The first and third are presented as clarifications. The October 22 handbook article did not require the unedited photo to come first; it listed a clear, realistic first photo as a best practice. Etsy's documents therefore support a narrower reading than "the photo no longer has to be first": the position was never a written requirement, and Etsy has now said so explicitly.
The comment attached a condition to the newly allowed edits: "If you make any of the allowed changes listed above to your unedited photo, make sure you're not using AI-powered tools. Otherwise, our systems may flag your photo as AI-edited." The same warning now sits on the policy page.
Timing changed too. The October 22 handbook article spoke only of more proactive enforcement over time, and Etsy's news post that day said removals of violating listings would expand in the coming months. On November 6, Ossman wrote: "Separately, we plan to begin proactively enforcing these requirements in early 2027. We'll provide advance notice before enforcement begins."
One more change appears in the policy text itself. On October 22, the handbook said craft-pattern listings must show "an actual result made using your instructions" and ruled out simulated results. The November 5 policy page carries an exception for grid-based patterns that allows a "stitch-simulation render" in place of a photo of a finished piece.
The rule as it now reads
The November 5 policy page opens with a statement of purpose: "The Etsy marketplace is built on trust. Your listing images should help buyers understand exactly what they will receive, including how an item looks, its condition, materials, or how your design will appear on a finished product."
Under "What is Required," it sets two conditions. The first is "Accurate Representation: All images must accurately reflect the item's true appearance, including colour, condition, scale, and materials." The second is the photo rule: "Unedited Photo: For most products, you must include at least one unedited photo of the actual item against a real background. This photo does not need to be the first image in your listing. Videos can complement your photos, but they don't replace the required unedited photo."
A separate line covers the permitted edits: "While you're allowed to crop, add a watermark, resize, or change the file format of your unedited photo, make sure you're not using AI-powered tools to do so. Otherwise, our systems may flag your photo as AI-edited."
Under "What is Prohibited," Etsy lists two items. "Misleading Editing: You must not hide flaws, conceal damage, or edit an item's appearance to make it look different from what the buyer will receive." And "Deceptive Content: You must not use images that create false expectations about what the buyer will receive."
The Help Center article fills in what "real background" means. "An unedited photo should show the actual item against a real background (including a light box or white backdrop) and must not be edited or changed," it says. A light box, the small lit tent many sellers use for product shots, therefore counts as real. A background swapped in by software does not.
The page also restates a rights condition that applies to every image: "By uploading images, you confirm that you have all necessary rights to those photos and are complying with our Intellectual Property Policy."
How the requirement works, according to Etsy's documents
Across the policy page, the Help Center article and the November 6 comment, Etsy describes the requirement in nine steps:
- One unedited photo. Most listings must include at least one photo of the actual item against a real background, which can include a light box or white backdrop. One photo covers the whole listing, including all its variations.
- Limited changes to that photo. Cropping, resizing, adding a watermark and changing the file format are allowed. Lighting, colour, retouching and background changes are not.
- No AI tools for those changes. The permitted changes must be made without AI-powered tools, or Etsy's systems may flag the photo as AI-edited.
- Any position. The unedited photo can appear anywhere in the listing. According to the Help Center, the first image should still be "the clearest, most realistic representation of the item buyers will receive."
- Videos do not count. A video can sit alongside the photos but cannot replace the unedited photo.
- Other photos may be edited. Remaining images may be edited with ordinary software or AI tools, within Etsy's table of allowed and prohibited edits.
- Product-type exceptions. Print-on-demand items, digital downloads, customised items, craft patterns, recipes, seeds and grid-based patterns follow their own rules.
- Checks and reports. If AI-generated images are detected while a listing is being created, Etsy shows tips. Buyers and other members can report listings they believe use AI imagery in a misleading way; at first, those reports do not trigger enforcement.
- Consequences. If a buyer opens a case over an item that is materially different from the listing, Etsy may refund the buyer and charge the seller. Misleading listings can be removed, and repeated violations can lead to shop removal. Proactive enforcement is planned for early 2027, after advance notice.
What sellers may still do with the other photos
The policy does not ban AI from listing images. "You may use photo editing software or AI-powered tools on your other listing images, provided the final result remains an accurate representation of the physical product," the page says. The Help Center article puts the same point in one sentence: "Don't use AI to hide damage or change an item's appearance in a way that could mislead buyers."
Etsy's own examples draw the boundary visually. Allowed: "Listings that contain a real, unedited photo of the item for sale, as well as an AI-edited image that accurately represents the item." Prohibited: "An AI-generated approximation of the item that does not accurately reflect the colour, texture, quality, or other characteristics of the product." A second prohibited example covers "An AI-generated image that does not accurately reflect what the end result of the craft pattern or kit will look like."
Two smaller points in the Help Center article are likely to matter to artists. Scans can qualify: "A scan can count as your unedited photo as long as you haven't edited it. Don't adjust it, such as with color correction or retouching." And image-protection tools are permitted. "Using tools like Glaze or Nightshade to protect your images from unauthorized AI training doesn't violate our policy, as long as they don't edit or alter the image," the article says. "You can use them on any listing photo, including your unedited photo." Glaze and Nightshade are free tools designed to make published images harder for AI systems to learn from.
Exceptions by product type
Etsy's "Rules by Product Type" section, together with the Help Center article, sets out where the single-photo rule bends.
The grid-pattern carve-out rests on a specific argument. According to the policy, "these patterns serve as a 1:1 visual blueprint of the finished piece." A cross-stitch chart maps every stitch to a coloured square, so a rendered preview can show exactly what the finished piece will look like. A knitting or crochet pattern offers no such one-to-one map, and the finished-result photo rule still applies to it.
The October 22 restrictions on AI-generated listings remain. Ossman's original announcement, still at the top of the same community thread, refers to "prohibiting bot-generated listings and AI-generated functional guides, such as patterns, recipes, and instructions." AI-generated art is still allowed under conditions. According to the Help Center, Etsy allows such art "when sellers play a clear creative role in what they create and are transparent about their process," and "Sellers must still disclose when an item is created using AI and cannot misrepresent AI-generated work as handmade or entirely their own creation."
Why lighting fixes are still off limits
Brightening a dim photo is among the most common requests from sellers, and Etsy kept it off the list of permitted changes. The November 6 comment gave the reason, citing buyer feedback.
"We know lighting can make a big difference in how you showcase your items," Ossman wrote. "But we've also heard from buyers that it's becoming harder to tell which listings on Etsy use AI-generated content, and that uncertainty has become a top-three barrier to shopping more often on Etsy. Some photo editing tools also use AI, and it may not always be obvious when AI is involved. Keeping at least one photo completely unedited helps provide a more accurate view of your item for buyers and helps us identify potentially misleading AI-generated or AI-edited images."
