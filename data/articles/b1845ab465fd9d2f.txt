TITLE: ‘GTA 6’ Hacker Pleads Guilty After Using Hotel TV to Pull Off $5M Rockstar Breach
OUTLET: Complex

Key Takeaways
- Arion Kurtaj, 21, pleaded guilty in London to hacking Rockstar Games and other cybercrimes after doctors found him fit to stand trial following secure psychiatric care.
- Kurtaj used a hotel TV, Amazon Fire Stick and Bluetooth accessories to breach Rockstar in 2022 while on bail, then leaked 90 clips of unfinished GTA 6 gameplay.
- Rockstar estimated that the breach cost $5 million and thousands of employee work hours, while prosecutors described Kurtaj as a “leading light” in the hacking group Lapsus$.
The hacker behind one of the biggest GTA 6 leaks in history has pleaded guilty to breaching Rockstar Games, years after pulling off a multimillion-dollar cyberattack using little more than a hotel television and an Amazon Fire Stick. Arion Kurtaj, 21, admitted to multiple hacking offenses on Monday, November 16, at Woolwich Crown Court in London after being released from a secure psychiatric hospital earlier this year.
According to the BBC, Kurtaj was transferred from the hospital to a regular prison in January after doctors found him fit to stand trial. He had previously been ordered to remain in secure psychiatric care indefinitely following a 2023 court proceeding.
His latest guilty pleas cover the Rockstar breach and several other cybercrimes he committed at 16 and 17.
The circumstances surrounding the original GTA 6 hack were unusual. In 2022, Kurtaj was staying at a Travelodge hotel in Bicester, England, while on bail for other hacking offenses. Authorities had placed him there for his protection after rival hackers reportedly threatened him over stolen Bitcoin.
Despite having his computer confiscated, Kurtaj accessed Rockstar's systems using an Amazon Fire Stick connected to the hotel television, along with a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse.
Once inside, Kurtaj obtained internal company information, including 90 clips containing nearly an hour of unfinished GTA 6 gameplay. He also accessed Rockstar's internal Slack messaging system and issued an ultimatum: "If Rockstar does not contact me on Telegram within 24 hours, I will start releasing the source code."
Rockstar refused to comply.
Kurtaj subsequently published the stolen material online under the username TeaPotUberHacker, exposing gameplay footage and revealing that the upcoming title would feature a female playable protagonist.
Rockstar later estimated that the breach cost approximately $5 million to address, in addition to thousands of employee work hours.
Prosecutors described Kurtaj as a "leading light" in Lapsus$, a hacking group responsible for attacks against major companies including Uber, Revolut and telecommunications provider BT/EE.
The group's activities reportedly caused approximately £10 million in damage, while prosecutors alleged its members stole at least $300,000 from cryptocurrency investors.
Kurtaj also pleaded guilty to assaulting workers while in custody, including multiple biting incidents. Authorities previously discovered he had obtained a mobile phone in prison after he sent a taunting message to police and posted on Snapchat.
The guilty pleas mark another chapter in Rockstar's long-running battle with unauthorized GTA 6 disclosures. The company has faced additional leaks and threats from hackers as it prepares to release the game, currently scheduled for November 19.
Kurtaj's former Lapsus$ associate, Thalha Jubair, was separately sentenced to five years and six months in prison after continuing his cybercriminal activities with another group.
