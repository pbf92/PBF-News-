TITLE: Temu owner PDD cuts marketing spend 17% as revenue stalls
OUTLET: PPC Land

PDD Holdings, the group behind the Pinduoduo and Temu shopping platforms, cut its sales and marketing expenses to RMB25.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026 from RMB30.3 billion a year earlier, while total revenues stood still at RMB108.3 billion (US$16.2 billion). Inside that unchanged top line, revenues from online marketing services and others rose 12% to RMB59.8 billion and transaction services revenues fell 12% to RMB48.5 billion, according to unaudited results for the three months ended September 30, 2026, which the Dublin and Shanghai-based company released on November 19, 2026, and furnished to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on Form 6-K.
In Short
PDD Holdings, which owns Temu and Pinduoduo, brought in almost exactly the same revenue between July and September as a year before, but spent about RMB5 billion less on sales and marketing, and its operating profit rose by roughly a quarter. For brands and sellers, the striking change is inside the revenue: money from online marketing services grew 12% and now outweighs transaction services, which fell 12%, and the company has not explained why. PDD says it is moving spending toward logistics, overseas warehousing and merchant support, and its finance head told analysts that a single quarter's expense ratios should not be read as a trend.
The quarter in numbers
All figures come from PDD Holdings' earnings release, published as Exhibit 99.1 to the Form 6-K, unless marked with an asterisk as a PPC Land calculation. Percentage changes without an asterisk are the company's own. PPC Land's calculations use the release's figures in millions of renminbi. The release converts at RMB6.7045 to US$1.00, the Federal Reserve H.10 noon buying rate for September 30, 2026.
The non-GAAP measures exclude share-based compensation only. Each American depositary share represents four ordinary shares.
One figure in the release is wrong. The highlights section states total revenues as "RMB108.3 billion (US$116.2 billion)." The detailed results section gives US$16.2 billion, and the income statement gives US$16,159 million, which matches the stated exchange rate. The same kind of slip appeared in JD.com's release two days earlier, where PPC Land found net revenues printed as US$146.2 billion in the highlights of JD.com's third quarter results.
Marketing services overtakes transaction services
A year ago, transaction services was the larger of PDD's two revenue lines, at RMB54.9 billion against RMB53.3 billion for online marketing services and others. The positions have now reversed. Online marketing services brought in RMB59.8 billion (US$8.9 billion) in the quarter, RMB11.3 billion more than transaction services, according to PPC Land's calculation.
The two moves almost cancel out. Online marketing services added RMB6.47 billion year on year, while transaction services lost RMB6.41 billion, leaving total revenues RMB64 million higher than in the third quarter of 2025. That is how a 12% rise and a 12% fall produce a flat quarter.
What makes the third quarter unusual is how sharply it breaks from the first half of the year. Subtracting the third quarter from the nine-month totals in the release gives the first-half figures below.
For the first nine months of 2026, the release puts total revenues at RMB326.9 billion, up 6.2% by PPC Land's calculation from RMB307.9 billion. Online marketing services rose 6.1% over the nine months to RMB167.4 billion and transaction services 6.2% to RMB159.5 billion, both PPC Land calculations. In other words, transaction services grew more than five times as fast as online marketing in the first half, then went into reverse.
Neither the release nor the call explains the swing. The release names the two lines "online marketing services and others" and "transaction services" without defining them, and it does not say how much of either comes from Pinduoduo in China or Temu abroad. It does not report gross merchandise volume (GMV), buyer numbers or order counts, so the share of sales PDD keeps as revenue, its take rate, cannot be derived from the documents. On the call, Jun Liu, the company's VP of Finance, read out the two revenue figures and moved on to costs.
For advertisers, the online marketing line is the closest PDD comes to disclosing a retail media network: money merchants spend to be seen by shoppers inside a marketplace. At RMB59.8 billion, it was 2.1 times the RMB28.1 billion JD.com reported for marketplace and marketing revenues in the same quarter, according to PPC Land's calculation, though JD.com's line also carries commissions and the two companies define their lines differently.
Spending less on sales and marketing
The release attributes the 12% fall in total operating expenses, to RMB31.9 billion, "primarily" to lower sales and marketing expenses. That line dropped by RMB5.0 billion. On a non-GAAP basis, according to Liu, sales and marketing expenses were RMB24.9 billion against RMB29.8 billion, or 23% of revenues against 28% a year earlier. Total non-GAAP operating expenses fell to 28% of revenues from 32%.
The cut is concentrated in the third quarter. Over nine months, sales and marketing expenses were RMB88.7 billion, down 2.4% from RMB90.9 billion, which implies a first half of RMB63.4 billion, up 4.7% on the first half of 2025, by PPC Land's calculation. PDD spent more on sales and marketing than a year earlier in the first six months, then spent markedly less in the next three.
The release does not break the line down. It does not say how much went on media bought from search engines, social networks or other platforms, how much on promotions and subsidies, and how much on staff. Share-based compensation inside sales and marketing fell to RMB370 million from RMB526 million.
Joyce Ju of Bank of America put the question directly. Noting "solid expense ratio discipline this quarter," Ju asked: "Can investors expect these efficiency gains to continue? On the flip side, could lower spending pressure consumer mindshare and market share over the longer term?"
Liu's answer declined to treat the quarter as a template. "As previously communicated, the company's financial performance and expense ratios in any given quarter fluctuate with the pace of our business rollouts and their return cycles. Single-quarter metrics should not be extrapolated as a reliable indicator of future trends," Liu said. "We remain in the strategic investment phase of our 100 billion support program."
On the marketing question itself, Liu gave a position that will interest any platform that sells ads to e-commerce companies. "Regarding consumer monetary mindshare, we believe that a consumer's genuine appreciation comes from the price they pay and the services they receive with each purchase, rather than short-term marketing expenditure," Liu said. "Consistently building these fundamental e-commerce capabilities, such as supply chain capabilities, is the foundation of our long-term competitiveness."
The release carried a similar line from Liu: "We remain in a strategic investment phase, focused on strengthening supply chain capabilities and supporting our merchants. We will maintain investment discipline while funding initiatives that support a healthy merchant ecosystem and strengthen our platform's long-term competitiveness."
Where the money is going instead
Research and development is the one expense line that grew. It rose to RMB5.1 billion from RMB4.3 billion on a GAAP basis, an increase of 16.8% by PPC Land's calculation, and to RMB4.6 billion on a non-GAAP basis, up 24%, according to Liu. Over nine months, research and development spending reached RMB14.0 billion, up 22.1% from RMB11.5 billion.
The balance sheet points the same way. Property, equipment and software, net, rose to RMB6.4 billion at September 30, 2026, from RMB1.3 billion at December 31, 2025, nearly five times the earlier figure by PPC Land's calculation. The release does not say what was bought or where. Net cash used in investing activities rose to RMB37.8 billion in the quarter from RMB11.6 billion a year earlier, but that line also includes purchases of short-term investments and deposits, and the release gives no separate capital expenditure figure.
Management described the spending in terms of programs rather than amounts. Co-Chairman and Co-Chief Executive Officer Jiazhen Zhao said the company had extended its 100 billion support program "beyond platform operations to include more stages in the supply chain, such as R&D, production, and fulfillment," and that the third quarter "marked a full year toward our vision for a new decade of high-quality development." Zhao, and Chen during the questions, spoke in Chinese; the English renderings quoted in this article are the interpretation given on the call, which the company said is "for reference only." In the release, Zhao put it more briefly: "During the past quarter, we continued to deepen our support for the merchant ecosystem, helping merchants develop better products and reach new consumers. We see significant potential to build on this progress and will continue investing in the tools and services that help our merchants grow."
Zhao also pointed to the company's first-party brand model, under which PDD has "established partnerships with merchants across industrial belts at scale," as part of a "three-year strategic goal of building another PDD." Lei Chen, the other Co-Chairman and Co-Chief Executive Officer, gave the reasoning. "Our role as a platform is to bring our understanding of consumer trends into the R&D and production process, helping our industry partners translate their manufacturing strengths into more competitive products. This is also a key rationale behind developing first-party brand business," Chen said. During the quarter, according to Chen, the team "worked with them to produce and launch high-quality first-party products at scale." No sales figure, product count or category list was given for the first-party business.
A marketplace that defines, quality-checks and launches its own branded products sits in a different position from one that only sells visibility to independent merchants. How far PDD intends to take this is not stated in the documents.
Competition at home: "no platform is pulling back"
Alicia Yap of Citigroup opened the questions by noting that competition among e-commerce platforms in quick commerce had moderated over the past two quarters. "So at this juncture, how does management view the competitive landscape in the domestic e-commerce? Has it reached a new equilibrium? And how is company approaching the pace of investment going forward?"
Zhao did not describe an equilibrium. "From what we are seeing across the broader e-commerce market, competition in China's e-commerce remains intense. This is driven by inherent characteristics of the industry. Consumers compare prices, quality, and services all over again each time they shop online, and it is easy for them to switch from one platform to another. As a result, no platform is pulling back. We see everyone is building on their strength to improve their capabilities on all fronts," Zhao said.
On pace, Zhao said investment "will vary from project to project." A fulfillment service that has proved effective in some regions will be assessed for whether it can run "in more regions reliably and at a reasonable cost" before the company decides how quickly to scale it. "These investments may take a long time to deliver results," Zhao said, "but we will remain firmly committed to investing in the areas that create long-term value for our merchant ecosystem and also for the platform's capabilities."
Rural logistics was the example offered. In remote villages, according to Zhao, parcels often went only as far as nearby towns, "leaving consumers to travel more than 10 km in some cases to collect them."
