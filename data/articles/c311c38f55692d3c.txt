TITLE: 'Thank God for a life well lived': Ann Widdecombe remembered by former PMs at Cathedral service
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Former prime ministers and hundreds of politicians have attended a service of thanksgiving for the life of Ann Widdecombe at Westminster Cathedral.
Andy Burnham and predecessors Rishi Sunak, Liz Truss, Boris Johnson and David Cameron were in attendance on Thursday; joined by Reform UK leader Nigel Farage, home secretary Shabana Mahmood, House of Commons speaker Sir Lindsay Hoyle and Cherie Blair.
Bishop James Curry, the Auxiliary Bishop of Westminster, led the service after Archbishop Richard Moth was taken to hospital unwell.
Widdecombe has been remembered for her “fearless” Christian faith. Raised in an Anglican family, she was received into the Roman Catholic Church as an adult and was vocal about her faith in politics.
The former Conservative minister later became a spokeswoman for Reform UK. She was found dead on 9 July at her home on Dartmoor, Devon, aged 78.
Rev Roger Widdecombe, her nephew, said the service would “celebrate and thank God for a life well lived.”
In his eulogy, he thanked well-wishers for their cards and messages in response to the “tragedy” which was “impossible to understand”.
Lord Alton, a crossbench peer and Christian, used his eulogy to remember their friendship despite their political differences, him being her “favourite bleeding-heart liberal”.
He told the service: “She had courage and tenacity… following her conscience, passionate about free speech, putting her beliefs and her country before other interests… Those who knew her understood what made Ann Widdecombe tick: the twin pillars of faith and patriotism, insisting we must use our lives to the full.
“Her faith was in her DNA. It manifested itself through support for charities, including for the persecuted, vulnerable, poor; the work of Right to Life and Leprosy Mission, Aid to the Church in Need, the St Vincent de Paul Society, the Grace Charity for ME,” the peer added.
Joshua Kerry, 28 from Rotherham in South Yorkshire, has been charged with her murder. Trial is scheduled for 8 June, with a plea and case management hearing on 29 January.
The public memorial comes after a private Requiem Mass held in August.
