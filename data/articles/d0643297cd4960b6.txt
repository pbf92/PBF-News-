TITLE: Unemployment rate rises to 4.9%
OUTLET: Australian Bureau of Statistics

Unemployment rate rises to 4.9%
The seasonally adjusted unemployment rate rose to 4.9 per cent in October, according to the latest data from the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS). This is the highest unemployment rate since October 2021.
Sean Crick, ABS head of labour statistics, said: “In October, there were 44,000 less employed people and 18,000 more unemployed people.
“Similar to last month, we had a higher proportion of people remaining unemployed than we would usually see in October in the post-Covid era.
“The youth unemployment rate – that is, for people aged between 15 and 24 – increased to 11.3% in October from 11.1% in September. This is the highest it’s been since October 2021. In the post-Covid period (from May 2022 onwards), the youth unemployment rate has averaged 9.2%.
“Employment remained steady in trend terms, meaning there was very little underlying growth in the number of employed people.
“The employment-to-population ratio fell 0.3 percentage points to 63.3 per cent, and the participation rate fell 0.2 percentage points to 66.6 per cent.”
The number of hours worked fell 0.2 per cent in October.
The underemployment rate fell 0.1 percentage points to 6.2 per cent.
Trend unemployment rate rose to 4.8 per cent
Trend employment remained steady, while hours worked grew by 0.1 per cent in trend terms in October.
The number of unemployed persons grew 1.3 per cent in trend terms from last month and 13.3 per cent over the last 12 months.
Both the trend underemployment and underutilisation rate remained steady, at 6.3 per cent and 11.0 per cent respectively.
We thank everyone who participated in and supported this survey.
