Mauricio Pochettino: Man Utd boss fumes at VAR in Liverpool loss over Leny Yoro and Dominik Szoboszlai calls
Man Utd to contact ProRef and the Premier League over 'totally wrong' interpretation of Leny Yoro's red card in loss at Liverpool after call was overturned; Man Utd boss Mauricio Pochettino: "What were VAR doing?"; Poch also believes Dominik Szoboszlai’s opener should not have stood
Tuesday 24 November 2026 22:05, UK
Mauricio Pochettino has heavily criticised VAR in Manchester United's loss at Liverpool after Leny Yoro's red card was overturned, while the Argentine believes Dominik Szoboszlai’s opener should not have stood.
Yoro's one-match suspension for the red card he was shown in the 4-0 defeat at Anfield has been removed, despite referees' chief Howard Webb supporting the decision to send him off.
Man Utd will contact ProRef and the Premier League directly over what they believe to be a 'totally wrong' interpretation of Yoro's red card against Liverpool.

It is the second time this season VAR has wrongly gone against Man Utd after ProRef admitted an error in awarding Manchester City's winner in their 1-0 victory at Old Trafford in September.
United received an apology over the incorrect VAR decision to award Erling Haaland's winner, which was initially ruled offside before VAR overturned the decision.
Pochettino mentioned the Haaland call in a passionate outburst over VAR as he questioned why the VAR did not intervene to overturn the on-field decision.
Speaking at a press conference ahead of his team's Champions League match against Sporting in Lisbon on Wednesday, Pochettino said: "Very disappointed because I think this type of thing never can happen.
"My question was always the same - we can accept the referee can make a mistake because of the emotional relationship with the game.
"But if we have VAR, what were they doing? What were they doing? No, because now we are going to repeat the game? We're going to have the possibility to play with the similar tools, 11 against 11?
"The problem is that when we were there, I think they need to take their time to review the situation.
"We have an unbelievable car but we need to be in the level to drive, we need to drive in the best way.
"We love and accept the technology, but what are they doing? But it's not only this mistake or the mistake for Haaland against Manchester City, it's how after it affects everything."
Poch explains why Szoboszlai goal shouldn't have stood
Pochettino then insisted that Liverpool's opening goal should not have stood because Bradley Barcola was touching the Manchester United wall as Szoboszlai struck in.
Pochettino referenced a similar call last season, which saw Eberechi Eze's free-kick goal for Crystal Palace at Chelsea disallowed after ex-Palace captain Marc Guehi pushed Moises Caicedo to create a gap for the ball to fly through.
The IFAB Laws of the Game state: "If, when a free-kick is taken, an attacking team player is less than 1 m (1 yd) from a 'wall' formed by three or more defending team players, an indirect free-kick is awarded."
Pochettino believes that the law applied to Szoboszlai's free-kick opener due to Barcola's involvement.
He said: "No one talks about that the goal wasn't legal, it should be disallowed because (Bradley) Barcola was touching our wall. Maybe in the last two years, they've maybe changed the rule because I've been in America, but last season when (Eberechi) Eze scored for Palace (at Chelsea) you can see [it was disallowed].
"But VAR didn't check [Dominik Szoboszlai's goal]; that must be disallowed.
"But this type of situation, I need to defend my players and my club, and that is to denounce and to say we were right and the decision wasn't good, and now the three points, where are they?
"I think it's a thing we need to think a little bit more deeper and it's not for tomorrow's game.
"It's about to be tough in the way we use the technology. We need to move on. I'm so proud of the players in the way they manage these unfair situations that happen on the field.
"Our focus now is to improve, to start to win games and tomorrow we have a great opportunity here."
What did Webb say about Yoro decision?
In the latest edition of Match Officials Mic'd Up, Webb, chief refereeing officer at ProRef, described the on-field decision from referee Samuel Barrott as "decisive" and said the point of contention was around whether Isak would beat United goalkeeper Senne Lammens to the ball.
Audio from the officials reveals that the VAR, John Brooks, who backed Barrott's decision, determined Isak was "likely" to get on the end of the ball before Lammens. His assistant VAR, Paul Howard, agreed.
Webb was non-committal in his verdict but backed the process of the officials by saying "most people would see that as a correct decision".
"There's a question mark, I guess, around whether or not he would get onto that ball before Lammens," said Webb. "Now, in the moment, the referee looked at it and felt he would. That was the referee's call made on the field.
"The VAR checks that to see whether that decision made on the field was clearly and obviously incorrect and formed the opinion that it wasn't. If there was any doubt, then it was doubt.
"It wasn't going to be something that was clearly and obviously wrong and therefore allowed that decisive on-field decision to stand. And I think by and large most people would see that as a correct decision with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball.
"The alternative view is that Lammens might get there first, but I think it's a little bit debatable, I guess. Certainly not clearly wrong."
