TITLE: Sleeve Lobectomy Linked to Better Survival Than Pneumonectomy in Lung Cancer
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

In selected patients with centrally located non-small cell lung cancer, sleeve lobectomy is associated with better long-term survival and lower early mortality than pneumonectomy, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in the Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery. The investigation synthesizes comparative evidence on both surgical procedures, focusing specifically on long-term survival trajectories and perioperative outcomes.
To update comparative evidence comparing these two thoracic approaches, researchers analyzed surgical cohorts to examine how preserving lung tissue influences overall survival, disease recurrence, and postoperative complications.
The research team performed systematic literature searches across PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library through June 10, 2025, identifying studies directly comparing sleeve lobectomy against pneumonectomy. In total, twenty-seven studies met the inclusion criteria for the systematic review, with twenty-five of those studies contributing quantitative data to the meta-analysis.
To evaluate survival, individual observations were reconstructed from published Kaplan–Meier curves with study identifiers retained. Study-specific hazard ratios were pooled using restricted maximum likelihood with modified Hartung–Knapp confidence intervals. The researchers also fitted a study-stratified Cox model as a complementary analysis and pooled perioperative outcomes using the same random-effects approach.
Across nineteen studies comprising 5,491 patients, sleeve lobectomy was associated with significantly better overall survival than pneumonectomy, yielding a pooled hazard ratio of 0.62 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.55 to 0.70. This association persisted among matched cohorts across studies, showing an identical hazard ratio of 0.62 and a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.52 to 0.75. However, pooled source-reported multivariable estimates proved less precise, showing a hazard ratio of 0.70 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.48 to 1.03.
Sleeve lobectomy was similarly associated with better disease-free survival across seven studies encompassing 3,675 patients. For this oncologic outcome, the meta-analysis demonstrated a pooled hazard ratio of 0.70, with a 95 percent confidence interval extending from 0.61 to 0.81 in favor of the parenchyma-preserving approach.
Perioperative safety assessments revealed marked differences in early postoperative survival. Sleeve lobectomy was associated with significantly lower 30-day mortality, showing an odds ratio of 0.54 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.31 to 0.92, as well as lower 90-day mortality, showing an odds ratio of 0.36 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.17 to 0.77. In contrast, rates of bronchopleural fistula, reoperation, and duration of hospital stay did not differ significantly between sleeve lobectomy and pneumonectomy.
Because the analysis relied on observational cohorts, the authors noted that residual confounding by indication precludes drawing definitive causal inferences from the data. Nevertheless, they concluded that the accumulated evidence supports parenchyma-preserving sleeve resection over pneumonectomy whenever an R0 resection is technically and oncologically feasible.
