TITLE: Anthropic leader: AI is transforming engineer promotion processes
OUTLET: FlipItMoney

Lesse explained that the company, which became public recently, is seeing marked shifts in how it measures software engineering success. "The amount of time it takes to complete a project is a less useful measure of readiness for promotion," Lesse said, highlighting that AI has compressed typical delivery windows. Instead, her team is putting more emphasis on outcomes - such as reduced latency and the avoidance of critical incidents - rather than purely on speed of execution. "Ultimately, the fallback is always just impact," Lesse remarked to Gergely Orosz, the host of The Pragmatic Engineer podcast.
With AI agents like Anthropic’s own Claude Code tool now accelerating the engineering process for hundreds of thousands of users worldwide, including Anthropic’s own employees, the company finds itself at the forefront of a broader workplace transformation. According to Anthropic’s IPO filing, as of September 30 the firm had over 11,900 customers each spending at least $100,000 annually on the Claude platform.
For senior engineers, the new landscape means that traditional measures, such as the time spent on a project, are less telling than ever. Lesse noted that top performers are increasingly distinguished not by their ability to write code quickly, but by their proficiency in orchestrating complex systems and aligning both human colleagues and AI agents to deliver high-quality releases. "You really, really, really shrink the implementation part," she said, emphasizing that impact now often depends more on coordination than on hands-on coding.
The hiring process at Anthropic is also evolving to keep pace with AI. Lesse described new interview formats that test candidates’ ability to utilize AI code agents, including challenges like reviewing code generated by an AI or building a system in collaboration with an agent. The shift comes after the realization that some technically strong candidates stumbled when faced with hand-coding rounds. Lesse described it as "a big change," adding, "We're basically trying to find what is the best way to really truly get signal on whether someone can use these tools really well to get work done."
As Anthropic and other technology companies adjust their approaches in response to AI’s accelerating capabilities, managers across the industry may have to rethink longstanding assumptions about employee expertise, performance, and readiness for senior roles. For now, at Anthropic, the era of AI is redefining what counts as excellence in software engineering.
