TITLE: 1,563 Meta ads violated ASCI code in six months, digital ads account for 98% of violations
OUTLET: Storyboard18

In the period between April and September 2026, The Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) investigated 2,308 advertisements for the violation of ASCI's code and reported that 94% needed to be modified and that 98% of the detected violations were on digital platforms.
The self-regulatory organisation investigated 3,951 complaints in the six-month period, out of which 71% were non-contested by advertisers. Out of the total number of advertisements monitored, 72% misleadingly led consumers with the help of false claims whereas 30% contained products that were harmful or dangerous to consumers.
Out of the total number of 2,173 advertisements that were detected, 97.93% were posted on digital media, 1.47% on television, 0.32% on print media and 0.46% on other media platforms. The same advertisement may have appeared on different platforms.
Out of the total number of advertisements on digital media, 73.4% advertisements were posted on Meta, 14.3% on websites, 6.5% on property portals, 4.3% on Google, 1.3% on e-commerce, 0.1% on OTT and 0.05% on mobile applications.
Meta ads, betting promotions under scrutiny
In the period between April and September, 1,563 advertisements on Meta were found to be violating the ASCI Code. Among these advertisements, 90% were revenue-generating sponsored advertisements as opposed to brand handles' advertisements.
Approximately 41% of the sponsored advertisements had content of the prohibited categories, which include offshore and illegal betting. According to ASCI, such advertisements have been reported to the concerned regulator and flagged with Meta to take further action.
It can be seen that the advertisements related to offshore and illegal betting are still accessible by Indian consumers via digital media platforms, even after the enactment of the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025.
According to ASCI, the presence of such advertisements is indicating the requirement of digital platforms taking stricter steps towards identifying, restricting, and preventing the circulation of such advertisements.
In total, 590 advertisements relating to betting and gambling were referred to the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) and the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting (MIB).
A total of 1,102 advertisements were referred to regulatory authorities. Out of which 633 advertisements were referred to the government agencies for the categories wherein advertising is explicitly prohibited by law. These referrals comprised 126 advertisements to the MIB, 126 to CCPA, 12 to the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India, eight to MahaRERA and one to the Ministry of AYUSH.
Additionally, 136 advertisements in violation of the ASCI Code of Advertising Practice and which remained non-compliant were referred to respective regulatory authorities for investigation according to provisions in place. Some cases had been referred to more than one regulatory authority.
Some of the other referrals included 28 advertisements for Ayurvedic products to the Ministry of AYUSH for violating the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, six alcohol advertisements to state excise departments, four hospital and clinic advertisements to the National Medical Council for violating guidelines, and five advertisements under other categories.
Thirty-three advertisements were referred to TGRERA through a memorandum of understanding signed between the ASCI and the Telangana Real Estate Regulatory Authority. In total, 360 realty advertisements contravened the provisions of TGRERA and/or ASCI Code of Advertising Practice. While 333 advertisements were identified for non-compliance of mandatory disclosures, such as registration numbers of the projects, URL, and consent of competent authorities, another 27 were non-compliant with the ASCI Code provisions.
Influencer advertising lapses found in personal care, food ads
The maximum number of violations related to influencer advertising was observed in the personal care category, and the maximum number of uncontested cases were reported in it as well. Seventy-five percent of the advertisements in this category were not challenged by advertisers; instead, these were immediately changed or withdrawn upon ASCI's notice. Of these, 58% had issues related to influencer disclosure.
Unsubstantiated claims regarding transformation of the skin and hair due to the effects of these products on skin diseases, like acne and pigmentation and hairfall and dandruff were common issues. Lapse in representations through exaggerated before and after visuals of dramatic skin tone transformation were some of the key concerns. Environmental claims and product composition claims were also reviewed, and ASCI said laboratory findings contradicted some sustainability assertions and product claims, highlighting gaps in advertiser accountability
Out of the 312 advertisements checked in food and beverages, 79% of them were not challenged and were withdrawn or modified after ASCI's notification. Out of them, 58% were found to violate influencer disclosure guidelines.
Out of the advertisements that came under this category, 74% were nutraceuticals and out of them, 82% did not contest the complaint. Many advertisers make therapeutic claims regarding the disease risk reduction or cure, improved health, quick weight loss, and superior products without any scientific substantiation.
Claim violations related to health, wellness and nutritional superiority continued to be a major concern, ASCI said.
In the case of electronics and consumer durables, 79% of the 95 advertisements examined had influencer disclosure violations, which were mostly due to promotional campaigns associated with the launch of new products, especially mobile phones.
Influencer compliance improves, but prohibited products remain
Out of 659 advertisements that were analyzed for influencer violation, 95% needed modifications, signifying the level of violation of ASCI's guidelines for influencer advertisement at the time of publication.
The organization stated that 83.5% of the influencers accepted their mistakes and modified their posts without challenging the complaints, and this was done within six working days.
The compliance rate among influencers was 95% up until November 17, 2026, as compared to 88% the previous year. Nonetheless, 3.6% of the advertisements were found involving the promotion of illegal products.
There were 12 cases of influencer violation where inadequate disclosures of material connection and misleading claims were found together.
The ASCI data for influencer violation categorized wise includes personal care with 263 advertisements, food and beverages with 181, electronics and consumer durables with 75, fashion and lifestyle with 28, and offshore or illegal betting with 23.
According to the body, 95% of 659 advertisements were modified following the processing of complaints related to influencer misconduct. The body also said 83.5% of influencers changed their posts without objection.
What ASCI says
Manisha Kapoor, CEO & Secretary General of ASCI said the body was still improving its capacity to monitor in the digital space and report back on it.
“Self-regulation provides a strong frontline consumer protection mechanism, with speedy and robust case resolutions, at no cost to the tax-payer,” Kapoor said.
She added that newer advertising categories and formats require an agile resolution mechanism, while technology investments had helped reduce turnaround time to 15 days.
Kapoor said ASCI was also working to help advertisers improve compliance through training and dialogue, with the aim of building consumer trust.
