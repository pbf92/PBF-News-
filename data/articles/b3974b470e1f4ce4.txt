TITLE: Rockstar Owns 5 of the Top 25 Games, GTA VI at No. 1
OUTLET: shattered.io

Rockstar Games now holds five of the 25 highest-rated games ever listed on OpenCritic, according to Game Rant’s report on the ranking. Grand Theft Auto VI sits at No. 1 with an overall rating of 99. Grand Theft Auto IV follows at No. 5 with 98, Grand Theft Auto V at No. 7 with 97, Red Dead Redemption 2 at No. 11 with 97, and Grand Theft Auto III at No. 13 with 97.
That is one fifth of the list from a single publisher label, and four of the five slots belong to one series. For anyone who tracks the Rockstar best games of all time debate, the new numbers settle a lot of barroom arguments. They also raise sharper questions about what a score of 99 means, how durable the old entries are, and what the list does not tell us.
This analysis sticks to the confirmed figures, lays out the math, adds historical context, and flags what remains unverified. For the wider picture of how the launch landed, see our earlier look at the 99 Metascore against the 5.6 user score on Metacritic.
Rockstar’s Five Entries in the Top 25
The confirmed data is short and clean. Five Rockstar titles appear among the cited ranking’s top 25 highest-rated games of all time. Each one carries an overall rating between 97 and 99, and each sits in the upper half of the list. Four are Grand Theft Auto games. One is a Red Dead Redemption game.
Here is the full set, with the release year added for context. The ratings and ranks come from the ranking coverage, while the years are the well-known original launch years of each game.
Read the table from top to bottom and a pattern jumps out. The release years span a quarter century, from 2001 to 2026. Rockstar did not land one lucky hit. It landed five separate times across four console generations, which is the part of this story most rival studios would envy.
GTA VI at No. 1 With a 99
The headline number is GTA VI at 99 and No. 1. A score that high leaves almost no room above it. On a 100-point scale, the game needs a nearly unanimous run of top marks from critics to land there, and the ranking coverage puts it at the summit of the entire list.
Reporting around the launch describes a game released on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, with critics praising its scale, its characters and its technical presentation. We covered the commercial side in our piece on the record day-one revenue and how it ranks against other games, so this article stays on the critical side of the ledger.
One point deserves emphasis. A No. 1 ranking on a critic aggregator is a statement about reviewers, not about every player. Critics score a game over days, usually before most buyers touch it. That timing matters, and we return to it below when we discuss what scores do and do not measure.
Why GTA IV at No. 5 Still Matters
Grand Theft Auto IV carries a 98, one point below the new leader. It sits at No. 5, which means only three non-Rockstar titles stand above it besides GTA VI. For a game that first shipped in 2008, that is a remarkable shelf life.
GTA IV was the series’ pivot toward a heavier, more grounded tone. It traded the playful sandbox feel of its predecessors for a tighter crime story set in a dense city. Critics at the time rewarded that ambition, and the ranking suggests the score has held up as new reviewers and later re-evaluations feed into the number.
The one-point gap between No. 1 and No. 5
The gap between GTA VI and GTA IV is a single rating point, yet four positions separate them. That tells you how tightly packed the top of the list is. A point of rating at this altitude moves a game several slots, so small shifts in the underlying review pool can reshuffle the order.
The 97 Cluster: GTA V, Red Dead Redemption 2 and GTA III
Three Rockstar games share a 97 but hold different ranks. GTA V sits at No. 7, Red Dead Redemption 2 at No. 11, and GTA III at No. 13. Seven ranks run from No. 7 to No. 13, and Rockstar holds three of them.
The same rating at three different ranks means either other games share 97 too or the list applies a tiebreaker. The available material does not explain which. We flag that gap rather than guess, since the ranking methodology is one of the unconfirmed details in this story.
Even so, the cluster says something about consistency. A studio can post one great game by accident. Placing three more at 97 and one at 98 shows a repeatable process. Rockstar’s long development cycles draw criticism, but the output they produce keeps landing at the top of aggregator lists.
The Math Behind Rockstar Best Games of All Time
The headline claim is five of 25, which equals 20 percent of the list. That is simple arithmetic from the confirmed ranks. We can push the math a little further with the same data, and the results are telling.
These are our own calculations from the confirmed ranks, not figures published by the ranking itself. The standout number is the concentration at the very top. Rockstar holds roughly 38.5 percent of the first 13 positions, which is nearly double its share of the full 25. The studio’s work clusters near the peak instead of trailing off toward the bottom of the list.
The two-point spread matters as well. All five games land inside a band from 97 to 99. Few catalogs anywhere in entertainment show that tight a range across 25 years.
Historical Context: 25 Years From GTA III to GTA VI
Grand Theft Auto III arrived in 2001 and rewrote what an open city could feel like on a console. Its appearance at No. 13 on the list, more than two decades later, shows how deeply it shaped the genre. Games still borrow its structure of a living city, a stream of side activities and a lead character who answers to nobody.
GTA IV followed in 2008 and pushed the formula toward realism. GTA V arrived in 2013 and widened the scope with three playable leads and a sprawling map. Red Dead Redemption 2, released in 2018, carried the same studio craft into the Old West with an unusually deliberate pace. Each game raised the bar for the next, and each one now sits on the same list.
The long gaps between releases are part of the story. Fans waited years between major titles, and the community sometimes grumbled. The ranking offers one answer to that frustration. Rockstar spends more time per game than most rivals, and the critical return shows up in the numbers. We looked at how the wait shaped expectations in our piece on what Rockstar has and has not said after launch.
A franchise that outlives its generations
Four console generations span the oldest and newest entries on the list. Hardware has changed from the PlayStation 2 era to today’s machines, yet the critical standing of the older titles has not collapsed. That durability is rare. Most games age out of top-ranked lists as expectations rise.
What Critic Scores Do and Do Not Measure
A critic rating compresses many opinions into one number. It reflects the views of professional reviewers who play under deadline, usually with review code supplied before launch. It does not capture months of player sentiment, technical drift after patches, or the way a game feels after 200 hours.
The ranking source for this story is itself not fully pinned down in the available results. We do not know whether the list sorts by critic average, by a blended score, or by another metric. We also do not know the exact publication date of the snapshot. Readers should treat the ranks as accurate to the cited coverage, while the underlying method stays unconfirmed.
Other aggregators tell parallel stories. Metacritic ranks every Rockstar release by Metascore, which gives a second angle on the same catalog. Comparing both lists is the best way to see which results hold across different review pools and which depend on one aggregator’s approach.
How to Read an All-Time List Without Overreading It
All-time lists invite big claims. A No. 1 slot gets turned into the best game ever made, and a spot at No. 13 gets read as an insult to a classic. Neither reading holds up well. A ranking reflects a snapshot of review scores, and snapshots change as more critics file their verdicts.
Three habits help. First, check the rating before the rank, because a one-point gap can cover several slots. Second, ask how many reviews sit behind each score, since a game with fewer reviews can post a higher average more easily. Third, remember that older games had fewer outlets reviewing them than a modern blockbuster does. The sources behind this story do not give review counts per game, so we cannot adjust for any of that here.
Those caveats do not erase the result. Five entries from one studio, spread over 25 years and between 97 and 99, is hard to explain away as an artifact. Even a skeptic has to concede that the catalog is unusually consistent.
What It Means for Players Choosing a Game
For a player deciding what to buy or replay, the ranking works as a filter, not a verdict. Anyone who has only played the newest release now has four older games rated 97 or higher to explore. That is a deep bench for a weekend or for a whole winter.
A few practical points follow from the data. GTA III is the oldest entry and shows its age in controls and presentation, even if the score holds. GTA IV leans into a serious tone that suits players who want story first. GTA V is the broadest in scope. Red Dead Redemption 2 trades speed for atmosphere. We keep these as general impressions, not measured claims, since the ranking does not break the games down by feature.
Budget matters too. Launch coverage reported the standard edition of GTA VI at $79.99, which is a real commitment next to older catalog titles. That price gap is one reason the older entries stay relevant as entry points to the studio’s work.
Market Impact for Take-Two and the Industry
Rockstar’s parent company is Take-Two Interactive, and a top-of-the-list result helps its pitch to investors, retailers and platform partners. A game at 99 is easy to market, easy to feature on storefronts, and hard for competitors to schedule around. Release calendars across the industry have bent around GTA for years.
We have not confirmed any share-price reaction to the ranking story, and none of our sources report one. Stock moves depend on far more than a review list, so it would be a mistake to draw a straight line from the score to the ticker. What the ranking does is reinforce a narrative that was already strong.
For the wider industry, the signal is about attention. When one release dominates both sales conversation and critical rankings, competitors face a harder fight for player hours and media space. Coverage of Call of Duty HQ usage after the GTA VI launch showed how a single game can shift habits on rival platforms.
Competitive Comparison: How Rockstar Stacks Up Against Other Studios
The confirmed data tells us Rockstar owns five of 25 slots. By definition, the other 20 belong to other studios and publishers, and the available sources do not name them. We will not guess at their identities or their counts. The sources do not say whether any other publisher matches that count, so we make no claim either way.
That puts the comparison on a different footing. Many studios have one or two landmark titles. Few have five across multiple franchises and multiple hardware eras. The result owes much to the way Rockstar runs two very different series, one in modern cities and one in the Old West, and still lands both near the peak.
Challengers are watching. CD Projekt Red’s chief executive has argued that the next Witcher game could make a leap on the scale of the jump from GTA V to GTA VI, as we reported in our story on the Witcher 4 leap and the GTA yardstick. Whether any rival can match five list entries is a different question. It takes decades of repeat success, not one breakout.
