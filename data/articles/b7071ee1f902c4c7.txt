Will bonds work in a tech crash? Citi weighs in
Whether government bonds SHY IEF TLT ZROZ will function as an effective portfolio hedge during an equity market SPY QQQ DIA selloff depends heavily on country selection, according to Citi Research, which warns that mounting AI AIQ AIEQ ecosystem debt and extreme factor rotations threaten stock valuations while high U.S. interest rates limit Treasury protection.
Citi strategist Dirk Willer pointed to a sharp double-digit selloff in copper CPER over 10 trading days as a key business-cycle warning. Historically, Willer wrote in a Friday note, similar copper drops precede major bond rallies while equity markets remain subdued. Because copper typically peaks roughly two months after manufacturing PMIs, the signal suggests the July ISM high is already in.
However, because the Federal Reserve remains too hawkish for comfort, Citi cautioned against relying on U.S. Treasuries, preferring duration in Australia EWA (adding a five-year AUD receiver at 4.81%) and Canada EWC (reiterating a two-year CAD receiver at 3.20%) to capture fixed-income upside during an equity drawdown.
The urgency for bond protection stems from intensifying strain around AI infrastructure. Despite solid operational results, Nvidia NVDA failed to reassure markets about its expanding financing commitments. With the semiconductor giant disclosing $32B of neocloud compute backstops and accounting for 27.2% of global venture capital deal value in 2026 year-to-date (up from 7.1% in 2025), concerns are growing over ecosystem debt sustainability and revenue dependence on China MCHI, which represents about 10% of revenue via customer headquarters.
At the same time, Citi's quantitative framework is flashing rare signals of a potential market peak. Factor price action matches conditions seen at only three previous market tops—February 2000, September 2021, and May 2026—when quality QUAL and value IVE decoupled from momentum MTUM and growth SPYG factors. Furthermore, the firm's proprietary "Generals" indicator, designed to detect bubble leadership breakdowns, is on day four of triggering, with four of the top seven S&P 500 SPY VOO constituents by market cap trading below their 200-day moving averages.
Wall Street’s major averages were markedly lower on Friday, as an extended tech-led selloff is putting the main indices on track for their steepest weekly declines in over a year. The S&P 500, in particular, entered correction territory, declining just over 10% from its October all-time highs. U.S. Treasury yields tumbled across the curve, with the benchmark 10-year tenor back to below 5%, as equities came further under pressure.
Geopolitical risks further complicate multi-asset hedging. Oil USO BNO markets have priced out Iran escalation risk—Brent is down nearly 20% since its pre-midterms peak—even as hostilities have escalated, including Iranian strikes on Bahrain and U.S. retaliation on Qeshm Island.
