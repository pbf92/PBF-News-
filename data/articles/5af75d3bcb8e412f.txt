TITLE: Nvidia and Micron Slide a Day After Their Big Rally
OUTLET: Stock Titan

On this page
The short answer. Tech stocks are giving back some of Monday's surge before the market opens, led lower by chipmakers such as Micron and Nvidia. The give-back is uneven: Micron has lost about two-fifths of Monday's jump, and HPE, which reported results, about four-fifths. The main company news is Sanofi's deal to buy Insmed, a maker of lung disease drugs, for about $41 billion in cash. Several big retailers also reported results this morning.

Chip stocks give back part of Monday's rally
Chip stocks jumped on Monday, the day Nvidia brought back its chief executive. Before Tuesday's open, they are falling back. Some have given back far more of that jump than others.
On Monday, Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, and QQQ closed up 7.00%, as StockTitan's Monday live page shows. A day later, the same chip stocks are leading the market lower before the open.
The pullback is not even. Nvidia, down 4.70% at 8:01 a.m. ET, has given back about a fifth of Monday's gain. Micron has given back about two-fifths of its jump. Broadcom and AMD were also lower.
HPE stands out. It jumped on Monday, then reported after the close that fourth-quarter revenue rose 50% to $14.5 billion, and it raised its revenue outlook for fiscal 2027 (HPE release). Its adjusted gross margin, the share of sales left after the cost of the goods it sold, fell 2.2 points from the quarter before.
By 8:01 a.m. ET, HPE had given back about four-fifths of Monday's gain, falling 11.88%, though it still traded above Friday's close.
Mark Haefele, chief investment officer at UBS Global Wealth Management, wrote on Tuesday that his team does not see the swings in tech stocks as "a signal that the structural AI investment story has broken," CNBC reported. He said he expects "greater differentiation" between established platforms that visibly make money and have strong balance sheets, and companies that depend on debt, scarce capacity or "continued speculative growth."

Sanofi Insmed deal: what Insmed holders get
Sanofi, the French drugmaker, agreed to buy Insmed for $175 a share in cash. Before the open, Insmed's stock traded a little below that price. The deal still needs a shareholder vote and regulators' approval.
Sanofi put the deal's total value at about $41.0 billion (Sanofi release). It said the price is about 46% above Insmed's close on November 18, the day before media reports of a possible deal. Both boards approved it. It still needs a vote of Insmed shareholders and approvals from regulators in the US and other countries, and Sanofi expects it to close in the first half of 2027.
Insmed sells two lung disease drugs. The main one, Brinsupri, is the first approved treatment for non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis, a lung disease in which the airways are permanently widened. It brought in $1.09 billion of revenue from its US launch in August 2025 through September 30, 2026, the release says. Sanofi said it would pay with cash on hand and new debt. It said the deal should add to its business earnings per share by the end of 2028.
At 8:01 a.m. ET, Insmed traded $4.10 below the $175 offer, up 14.63% from Monday's close. CNBC reported that Insmed shares rallied in pre-market trading after Sanofi announced the deal. Sanofi's US shares were up 6.46% at 8:00 a.m. ET.
If the deal closes, each Insmed share would be exchanged for $175 in cash. Until then, the stock keeps trading, and its price can move with news on the vote and the approvals.
