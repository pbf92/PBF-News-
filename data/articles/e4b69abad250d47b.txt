TITLE: Christians part of 'unholy alliance' blocking Sunday trading changes, says ex-chancellor Osborne
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Conservative Christians were part of an “unholy alliance” which prevented the relaxation of Sunday trading laws, according to former chancellor George Osborne.
Speaking on his Political Currency podcast he said “it’s absolutely nuts that shops can’t open on a Sunday” as he argued extending opening hours would help “save the high street”.
The Sundays Trading Act 1994 banned large shops in England and Wales from opening for more than six hours on a Sunday, as part of a compromise replacing existing rules which banned trading outright. Smaller, local shops could stay open.
Osborne was defeated in his attempt to scrap the rules permanently in 2016, after the Conservatives became a majority government.
He told former Labour treasury minister and co-host Ed Balls: “After the 2015 election, we were looking for things to do to give the economy a big shot in the arm. Relaxing the Sunday trading rules was a straightforward and obvious case.
“We were defeated by an unholy alliance. I use the word unholy, because this is all wrapped in a Christian fig leaf. It was an unholy alliance of Christian conservatives and Usdaw, the shop workers’ union… Then the trinity was one or two supermarket chains, who I assumed would be on side”, Osborne added.
MPs voted by 317 to 286, a majority of 31, against the government’s plans a decade ago. A backbench Tory rebellion was led by David Burrowes, now director of the Conservative Christian Fellowship, who raised concerns about the impact it would have on shopworkers’ family life.
