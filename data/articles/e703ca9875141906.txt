TITLE: Rockstar promotes GTA 6 in China despite game lacking official approval
OUTLET: Notebookcheck

Rockstar Games is promoting Grand Theft Auto VI to hundreds of thousands of followers in mainland China, despite the game lacking the regulatory approval required for official sales there.
According to Reuters, Rockstar posted a promotional video on its verified Douyin account on 12 November, encouraging users to preload the game ahead of its global console release on 19 November.
The account, registered under Rockstar International Limited and displaying a Shanghai IP location, had attracted approximately 480,000 followers and more than 300,000 likes by 18 November. Chinese gaming outlet 3DM had also reported the account's arrival on Douyin.
Promotion without permission to sell
China requires imported video games to receive government approval before they can be officially sold in mainland China. Titles are scrutinised for content including violence, sexual material and politically sensitive themes.
The Grand Theft Auto series has never received that approval, according to Reuters, and GTA VI does not appear on the country's published lists of approved imported games.
Yet the absence of official sales approval has not prevented Chinese players from seeking overseas editions. Reuters describes purchases through foreign console accounts and imported boxed versions containing download codes. One Douyin user who said he was in Beijing reported obtaining a Hong Kong edition for approximately 500 yuan.
The commercial stakes are considerable. Rockstar's parent company, Take-Two, reported that Grand Theft Auto products accounted for 12.6% of its net revenue in the six months to September 2026, while GTA V has shipped more than 230 million units worldwide. The figures underline the franchise's global scale, even as its official sales remain restricted in mainland China.
A market Rockstar can reach, but not officially sell to
Rockstar can reach Chinese audiences through social media, while the game's official commercial distribution remains subject to a separate regulatory process.
Reuters reports that China's regulator approved 1,708 domestic games and 58 imported online games between January and October 2026. Neither Rockstar nor the regulator had responded to its requests for comment when the report was published.
Rockstar is promoting one of the year's biggest game releases to Chinese players, even though it cannot officially sell the game in mainland China without regulatory approval. Players may still obtain overseas editions, leaving a striking gap between the audience Rockstar can reach and the market it is authorised to serve.
