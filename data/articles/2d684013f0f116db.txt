Claude maker Anthropic pops in Nasdaq debut
Plus: Wall Street’s Anthropic sugar hit wears off; Canva hands users an AI off switch; Super funds set to dish out advice.
Good morning. Here’s what happened overnight and what you need to know today.
1.
Claude nine: Anthropic shares soared as much as 39% in their Nasdaq debut after the Claude maker’s record USD81 billion ($121.5 billion) IPO, before giving back much of the gain as Wall Street slid. The stock opened at USD111.50, 34% above its USD83 IPO price, and peaked at USD115.10, valuing Anthropic at more than USD2 trillion on a fully diluted basis. It had pared its gain to about 14% by 6:19am AEDT. “The Anthropic IPO has been a major success. However, the IPO does little to address the market’s bigger problems,” said Matt Maley of Miller Tabak. More than half of institutional bidders got no shares, according to reports. The raise, which tops USD107 billion including a private placement, will help fund more than USD900 billion in computing deals. “We will not bend to either business or political pressure,” chief executive Dario Amodei said before ringing the opening bell. Staff can sell up to 10% of their shares now but no more until full-year results early next year, when The Information noted a talent exodus is a risk.(Bloomberg)(FT)(Reuters)(WSJ)(The Information)
2.
Losing streak: Wall Street’s hopes Anthropic’s debut would revive the AI trade lasted barely a morning, as a short seller’s bet against chipmaker AMD helped drag stocks towards their longest losing streak in more than two years. The S&P500 benchmark was down 1.6% and the tech-heavy Nasdaq 100 1.7% in late afternoon trading, after both opened higher. Chipmaker AMD was over 7% lower after Hunterbrook Capital, whose media affiliate published a report on AMD’s exposure to China, said it was betting against the stock. AMD said the claims “misrepresent AMD’s business practices and relationships”. Meanwhile, a vendor cyberattack that exposed some JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard customer data also weighed. In deals, cybersecurity group Palo Alto Networks agreed to buy AI startup Applied Compute for USD4.5 billion in stock. Elsewhere, Walmart lifted its full-year forecasts. And oil rose 0.5% and gold fell 1.3%. (Bloomberg)(WSJ)(Hunterbrook)
