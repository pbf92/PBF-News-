TITLE: Will gold prices fall or rise? Watch these eight dates
OUTLET: The Financial Express

When gold prices rise, many people delay their purchases and wait for a fall. When prices drop, others wait to see if they will fall further. But how can buyers understand which way the market might move?
US economic reports can offer some clues. Inflation, employment, economic growth and interest-rate decisions can all influence international gold prices. These reports follow a schedule. Buyers and investors can use release dates to prepare for potential market moves.
However, a report's release date does not tell us whether gold prices will rise or fall. The key is to compare the actual figures with market expectations.
Why should you follow US economic reports?
Gold is traded internationally in US dollars. Its price is therefore linked to the strength of the dollar, US interest rates and government bond yields.
When US interest rates rise, bonds and other interest-bearing investments become more attractive. Gold does not pay interest. Some investors may therefore sell gold and move their money into these assets. This can push gold prices down.
The opposite can happen when interest rates fall, or investors expect rate cuts. A weaker dollar and lower bond yields can also support gold prices.
Recent market movements offer an example. In October, US consumer inflation rose 0.4 per cent, above the market forecast of 0.3 per cent, according to the US Bureau of Labour Statistics (BLS). Core inflation also rose 0.3 per cent, above the expected 0.2 per cent. The figures strengthened the dollar and pushed Treasury yields higher. Spot gold fell 2.7 per cent to $4,028.39 per ounce on November 10, Reuters reported.
Eight important dates to watch in the coming weeks
Several US agencies publish economic reports every month. Here are eight releases scheduled between late November and mid-December 2026 that could influence the gold market.
November 24: US Consumer Confidence
The Conference Board publishes the Consumer Confidence Index every month. It measures how consumers feel about the economy, job opportunities and their financial situation.
A higher-than-expected reading could suggest that the US economy remains strong. This may reduce expectations of interest-rate cuts and put pressure on gold prices.
November 25: US PCE inflation
The US Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) publishes Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) inflation data in its Personal Income and Outlays report.
Investors pay close attention to Core PCE. It excludes food and energy prices because they tend to fluctuate more.
For example, if the expected monthly core inflation figure is 0.2 per cent but the actual figure reaches 0.4 per cent, investors may expect fewer Fed rate cuts. The dollar and bond yields could rise, putting pressure on gold.
Lower-than-expected inflation could have the opposite effect. It may increase expectations of rate cuts and support gold prices.
November 25: US GDP growth
The BEA is also scheduled to publish its second estimate of US GDP growth for the third quarter of 2026.
Gross Domestic Product (GDP) measures the total value of goods and services produced in an economy. It helps investors assess economic growth.
Stronger-than-expected growth could reduce expectations of interest-rate cuts. This may put downward pressure on gold. Weaker growth could increase those expectations and support gold prices.
December 1: US JOLTS job openings
The BLS publishes the Job Openings and Labour Turnover Survey (JOLTS). It provides data on job vacancies, hiring, and workers leaving their jobs.
A higher-than-expected number of job openings could point to a strong labour market. This may reduce expectations of rate cuts and put pressure on gold.
December 4: US nonfarm payrolls (NFP)
The monthly Employment Situation report is one of the most closely watched US economic releases. The BLS publishes data on nonfarm payrolls, unemployment and average hourly earnings.
If job creation exceeds expectations, investors may see it as a sign of a strong labour market. This could give the Federal Reserve more reason to keep interest rates high.
For example, suppose investors expect the US economy to create 100,000 jobs in a month. If the actual figure reaches 200,000, the labour market would appear stronger than expected. This could strengthen the dollar and put pressure on gold.
But if investors expect 200,000 new jobs and the actual figure is only 50,000, the market may see signs of economic weakness. This could increase expectations of rate cuts and support gold prices.
Investors also watch wage growth. Faster wage increases can create additional inflationary pressure.
December 9: Federal Reserve interest-rate decision
The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) is scheduled to meet on December 8 and 9. Its policy decision is due on December 9.
A rate increase or a signal that rates will remain high could put pressure on gold. A rate cut or signals of further cuts could support the metal.
Investors will also examine the Fed's statement and the chairman's press conference. These may offer clues about future monetary policy.
December 10: US Consumer Price Index (CPI)
The BLS is scheduled to publish the November Consumer Price Index on December 10. CPI measures changes in the prices consumers pay for goods and services.
Higher-than-expected inflation could increase expectations that the Fed will keep interest rates high for longer. If the dollar and bond yields rise, gold prices could fall.
December 15: US Producer Price Index (PPI)
The Producer Price Index measures changes in the prices producers receive for their goods and services. It can offer clues about inflation at the production level.
Higher-than-expected PPI could signal rising business costs. This may raise concerns about future inflation and increase expectations of higher interest rates. This could put pressure on gold.
Lower-than-expected PPI could suggest easing inflationary pressure. This may support gold prices.

Other indicators that gold buyers should monitor
Economic reports are not the only factors that influence gold prices. Investors also follow several market indicators.
US Dollar Index
The index measures the dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies. A stronger dollar generally pressures gold. A weaker dollar can support its price.
US Treasury yields
The 10-year US Treasury yield is another key indicator. Higher yields make interest-bearing investments more attractive compared with gold. Lower yields can make gold more appealing to investors.
Crude oil prices
Oil prices can influence gold indirectly. Rising oil prices may increase inflation concerns. But if investors expect higher interest rates in response, gold could come under pressure.
Will these developments affect gold prices in Bangladesh?
Changes in international gold prices do not translate directly into the same changes in Bangladesh. The Bangladesh Jewellers Association (BAJUS) also considers the taka-dollar exchange rate and domestic supply conditions when setting local prices.
Buyers should therefore monitor both international and local prices. They should also follow the dollar, bond yields and the market's response to economic announcements.
Anyone planning to buy gold for a wedding or another occasion can use these reports to understand what may influence prices. But no single report can guarantee a price rise or fall.
No reliable way exists to predict exactly when gold prices will change. Following these reports can, however, help buyers understand the market and prepare for periods when prices may move.
