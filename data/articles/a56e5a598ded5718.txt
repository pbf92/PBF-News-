TITLE: Come to GTA VI for the crime spree. Stay for the manatee.
OUTLET: Vox

There are plenty of reasons to be excited for the release of Grand Theft Auto VI, which came out today. There are the heists, the incredibly realistic depictions of Florida, the surprisingly deep romance, and, you know, the car theft. It’s been 13 years since the last installment of the incredibly popular game series — basically an eternity in 2026 terms.
But personally, I’m interested in the pelicans in flight at sunset and the green scales of an iguana in the street, as seen in the game’s second trailer. There are more than 170 animals in the game, like alligators, manatees, and frogs. There are also 62 species of birds, and players can pull out binoculars and record them all in a logbook.
Now I’m not a video game writer. I write about biodiversity. So this stuff is basically catnip for me, and I’m not the only one. There are Reddit threads full of excited birdwatchers who are saying that crime sprees will come second to birding in the new game, or that they want to get one of the game’s protagonists, Lucia, some cute birding outfits. The top comment in the New York Times’s review of the game says, “my roommate gets to play the actual game. I get to find all 67 [sic] species of birds. Truly a game for everyone.”
While most of the game takes place in Vice City, which is developer Rockstar’s take on Miami, players start in a fictional version of the Florida Keys and can visit places like the Everglades and other national parks. In real life, this is one of the richest ecosystems in the country; Florida is home to more than 700 terrestrial animals, thousands of fish, and over 500 species of migratory and year-round birds.
And with GTA VI, Rockstar is going even more immersive than it did in another one of its games, Red Dead Redemption 2, by adding features like bioluminescence, feather types, and accurately depicting how hair and fur are rendered for its wide variety of animals.
“Florida has a diverse animal ecosystem that we wanted to faithfully include across all the different biomes in Leonida — urban and rural environs, aquatic life both inland and ocean, and the wetland habitats like the swamps and Everglades,” Rockstar’s vice president of dynamic art, Michael Kane, told Game Informer back in September (I reached out to Rockstar for comment but didn’t hear back by publication time). I wouldn’t be surprised if some people spend way longer than strictly necessary wandering around in the opening areas, looking for one last bird to strike off their list.
Honestly, even though I’d call myself a gamer, I wasn’t entirely sure I’d play GTA VI. There’s just so much hype, and I was expecting it to mostly be another super-violent game about committing crimes. And of course that is part of it, but I love how it also seems to give people the option to take a moment and appreciate the natural world. To be fair, the game will also feature hunting of some of these animals, but I personally will be steering away from the Teddy Roosevelt school of conservation.
It’s not the kind of thing you’d expect from a triple-A blockbuster, and maybe it’ll even get players to care more about biodiversity. Who knows, maybe afterward they’ll be motivated to, you know, touch grass.
“We’ve been painting birds on the walls of caves and writing songs about them for millennia, and it makes total sense to me that this sort of wonder that we get from birds would find an expression in the very contemporary form of art that is a video game,” said Ethan Linck, an assistant professor in the Department of Ecology at Montana State University who stressed that he is not a gamer himself. “I could totally see someone who might not have any background in natural history or birdwatching get into this fun diversion in the game and then discover that they can basically do the exact same thing in real life using apps like eBird and iNaturalist. It’s no longer an old-person hobby.”
Speaking of which, I have to go explore the Everglades myself. For, uh, work.
