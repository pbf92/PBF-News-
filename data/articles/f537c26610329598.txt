TITLE: Pochettino Says ‘I Am Not Nice' After Two Winless Man Utd Games
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

- Mauricio Pochettino told his Friday press conference that he is not nice but is trying to be good, after a 4-0 defeat at Liverpool and a 2-2 draw at Sporting.
- Jamie Redknapp said on The Overlap Daily earlier this week that he thought the Manchester United boss had gone too nice.
- Manchester United host Brentford at Old Trafford on Saturday afternoon in Pochettino's first home game, with captain Bruno Fernandes a doubt after a virus.
The Manchester United Boss Draws a Line Between Nice and Good
Mauricio Pochettino has two games behind him at Manchester United and no win. On Friday he sent his players a warning. He is not nice, he said. He is trying to be good, and he wants everyone to know those two words mean different things.
The answer came at his press conference before Saturday's visit of Brentford. Earlier in the week Jamie Redknapp had said he thought the United boss had gone too nice. Pochettino was asked about a hectic start to his time in Manchester, and his answer turned to that word.
Redknapp Pointed to the High Fives and the Bracelets
Redknapp, the former Liverpool and England midfielder, gave his verdict on The Overlap Daily. The discussion pointed to Pochettino high-fiving reporter Patrick Davidson after the 4-0 defeat at Liverpool on Sunday as evidence the former Tottenham, Paris St-Germain and Chelsea coach had lost his edge.
"When I saw Pochettino at Southampton and I saw him at Tottenham, he looked like he had that Argentinian dog in him," said Redknapp.
"I look at him now and he's high-fiving everyone, he's got the bracelets on, he's got the barnet. I think he's gone too nice."
The question put to Pochettino on Friday covered a "crazy" first week of red cards, refereeing errors, disallowed goals and a drone filming training. After the Champions League draw in Lisbon on Wednesday night, the squad flew home on Thursday evening, leaving one day at Carrington to prepare for Brentford.
Pochettino Refuses to Start Crying Over Referees
He began with the officials.
"Always happens. I think the problem is that we forgot that things happened in the past, no? We always live in the present and the future, no? But we are always in the future because the present is only a moment that always disappears quickly."
"Yes, it always happens. When this type of situation happens, the circumstance always feels like, oh, things always happen against us, and it's like, no, but it's not true. That is not true."
"That is a little bit the rhetoric of always. No, this type of thing happens. We had a big mistake from the referee against Liverpool, something happened. Not only did it happen now with us, it can happen in the future, it happen in the past. And the mistake also in Wednesday was the same."
He then turned to the two results.
"But we need to accept that. What are we going to do? Start to cry? No. Being strong, believe, keep going. I think too many positive things in the two games."
"Maybe we didn't win, okay, we didn't win, but for sure I think we are building, you know, our journey and for sure in the way that now that we are, only we can talk from the last seven, eight days, we found a group of players, a group of staff, and everyone that want to help and want to be there, you know, to help the team to perform and win games."
He would not judge what came before his first days in charge.
"That is why I cannot change my vision, I cannot say something that is not true. I cannot judge the past because who I am to judge the past? Only when I was in Cincinnati or I was in Atlanta, I say, no, we should change that or we should do this or do that. Because I was outside."
"I need to shut my mouth and say nothing and start from now when I accept the invitation to come to Manchester United and start to work in this circumstance, shut my mouth and work 12 hours every day, you know, and try to recover the feelings, the emotions, and build our mentality again and really believe in what we are going to do and this group of players deserve that and pushing. And not misunderstand my words."
Then came the line about nice and good.
"Because sometime we are so confusing two words that means sometime everyone says, oh, maybe it's a similitude or similar, you know. Nice and good. I am not nice. I am not nice."
"We have to be good. There is a difference. I am not nice. I am trying to be good. Then be careful, because we know when we need to push, when we need to be strong, and when we need to build something, you know, to improve the atmosphere and relationship and everything."
Pochettino Says It Is Only a Matter of Time
Pochettino was then asked about his man management and the importance of not being nice.
"I don't know. It's maybe a lot of expertise that have, you know, different things," he said.
"We have our experience and we are going to try to give everything to put in track again that team, that club, and those players. And we really believe. We are, we really believe that we can do it. It's only a matter of time."
Fernandes Doubt Before Brentford
Bruno Fernandes scored both goals in Lisbon but missed training on Friday with a virus, and Pochettino will decide on his fitness on Saturday morning.
"We don't know, he been affected by a virus from Portugal," Pochettino said when asked if Fernandes was available. "He finished the game with a little bit of a problem and today he cannot be a part of the training session."
"We'll see. We're going to assess during the day and tomorrow. He'll wake up and see. We're not going to take any risks because we know that when these types of situations happen you don't feel your best. We're going to pay attention but still we haven't decided."
United triggered the one-year option on the 32-year-old's deal the same day, taking it to 2028 after his stalled contract talks.
Carlos Baleba, the £70m summer signing from Brighton, is expected to be out until the last week in January with the hamstring injury he picked up against Roma on 3 November. Marcus Rashford, out with a hip problem, last played in the 2-0 defeat by Atletico Madrid on 13 October. He was back on the training pitch on Friday.
"Marcus today he did an individual session on the field," Pochettino said. "I think he's starting to feel much better. We'll see day by day with working with the feeling that he's going to provide us after every single training session, increasing the progression."
Asked if he was excited to work with Rashford once fit, Pochettino said: "I'm looking forward to working with all the players. I hope they recover as soon as possible. I think we can have the full squad fit and available, that is the most important thing for the training staff."
Brentford arrive at Old Trafford on Saturday afternoon for Pochettino's first home game, with United still waiting for a first win under him. Fernandes' fitness will be settled that morning, and Baleba and Rashford remain out. His players now know where their head coach stands on nice and good.
