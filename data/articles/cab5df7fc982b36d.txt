TITLE: Stock Market Today: Nasdaq-100 Sinks 1% as Nvidia Faces Its First Earnings Without Jensen Huang
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

Quick Read
- Nvidia's CEO exit sends chip stocks lower, dragging the Nasdaq-100 ETF QQQ down about 1% before Tuesday's earnings, its first report since Jensen Huang resigned.
- Tech weakness leaves the S&P 500's SPY down about 1% premarket while the Dow's DIA holds flat, showing how sector balance cushions portfolios.
- The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.15% and Fed officials signal more work on inflation, pressuring tech valuations that depend on distant earnings.

Chip stocks were pulling Wall Street lower before the opening bell, and the Nasdaq-100 was taking most of the hit. The Invesco QQQ Trust (NASDAQ:QQQ), which tracks the Nasdaq-100, was down 1% in premarket trading. The Dow's ETF proxy was about flat. Investors are adjusting positions ahead of NVIDIA (NASDAQ:NVDA) earnings on Tuesday. It is the company's first report since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive last Thursday.
Where the Major Indices Stand Before the Open
The table below uses premarket trading in the major index ETFs: the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (NYSEARCA:SPY), QQQ, the SPDR Dow Jones Industrial Average ETF Trust (NYSEARCA:DIA) and the iShares Russell 2000 ETF (NYSEARCA:IWM). QQQ follows the Nasdaq-100, a smaller group than the Nasdaq Composite.
The VIX sits in its normal range of 15 to 20, after hitting 28 on Nov. 5 and then falling 34% over the following week.

Nvidia's Leadership Vacuum Meets a Hawkish Fed
Huang stepped down as CEO and left the board Thursday. Nvidia named CFO Colette Kress interim chief executive, according to CNBC. Chip stocks fell again Friday, and that weakness spread overnight to South Korea, Shanghai and Japan, The Wall Street Journal reported.
