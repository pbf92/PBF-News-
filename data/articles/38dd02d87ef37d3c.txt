TITLE: Salford man handed ten-year restraining order after campaign of controlling behaviour
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

Salford man handed ten-year restraining order after campaign of controlling behaviour
Jonathan Getliffe took his victim's phone, turned up at her place of work and 'constantly' accused her of cheating
A Salford man has been handed a suspended sentence and ten-year restraining order after a campaign of controlling behaviour in which he seized his victim's phone and turned up at her work unannounced.
Jonathan Getliffe, of Hill Top Road in Walkden, tracked his victim's movements and constantly accused her of cheating, his sentencing hearing at Manchester Crown Court was told earlier this month.
The 37-year-old previously pleaded guilty to one count of engaging in coercive and controlling behaviour, and one count of assault by beating, as well as driving whilst disqualified and driving without insurance.

Greater Manchester Police are encouraging any victims of domestic abuse to come forward, as they promise to 'listen, support and act'.
In a moving impact statement read out in court on November 3, Getliffe's victim spoke of 'feeling like a hostage in [her] own life', 'walking on eggshells' and 'living under surveillance'.
"The consequences of trying to leave often felt more frightening than remaining in the relationship," she said in the statement. "At times it felt easier to stay than to leave.
"Whenever I attempted to leave, I was subjected to repeated harassment, threats, and intimidation. He also turned up unexpectedly at my workplace and demanded that I speak to him in front of my colleagues."
She added that Jonathan 'often' broke her possessions, including her mobile. "He often took my phone and would go through it for hours on end, without my knowledge.
"He would turn up unexpectedly to check what I was doing and tracked my movements. Despite this, I was continually accused of cheating.
"I became frightened of saying the wrong thing, even to my own friends, and struggled to sleep because I was constantly worried about being accused of something, even being concerned about what I might say in my sleep. I never felt able to simply be myself."

The victim said she continues to feel fear and anxiety, attending domestic abuse support groups and undergoing treatment for PTSD.
She added that the abuse has had a 'lasting' impact on her confidence and 'ability to trust people'. "I feel that a part of me has changed and I worry that these effects will remain with me for the rest of my life," she said. "I hope that, with time, the nightmares and flashbacks will lessen and I will be able to lead a normal life again.
"Despite installing additional security measures at my home, I still do not feel safe. The sense of security that most people take for granted has been taken from me.
"I have had to learn how to live without constantly being monitored or afraid of someone's reaction. I am still learning that I am allowed to make my own choices, speak freely, go where I want and live my life without having to justify myself.
"I can hold my head high knowing that I was strong enough to stand up to the abuse and not be controlled into withdrawing my statement that has happened previously. I hope that having spoken out will give others the strength to do the same.
"All I want now is to feel safe, to feel like myself again and to live my life without fear."
Jonathan Getliffe, 37, of Hill Top Road in Walkden, was handed a 26 month sentence, suspended for 30 months, and a ten-year restraining order against his victim.
He was ordered to complete 250 hours unpaid work, 26 days of the Building Choices programme, and a 90-day alcohol monitoring requirement. He will also pay a £228 victim surcharge and £1,000 in court costs.
Greater Manchester Police have previously supported the ‘#IsThisOk?’ campaign, which aims to raise awareness around the signs of coercive and controlling behaviour.
The Greater Manchester domestic abuse helpline can be contacted Monday to Friday, 10am to 4pm, except Bank Holidays. This is for anyone experiencing domestic abuse, and includes a language service and advice for the LGBT community.
The End the Fear campaign also includes information on local help all over Greater Manchester.
"We encourage any victim of domestic abuse to come forward to us by calling 101, reporting on our website, or using our Live Chat service," the force said.
"We are committed to safeguarding vulnerable people, reducing harm and bringing offenders to justice. We will listen, support and act. Whether you choose to report to police, or not, please know that support is out there."
