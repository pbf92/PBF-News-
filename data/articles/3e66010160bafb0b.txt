TITLE: Shopify, Cohere leaders join call for Jensen Huang to be reinstated as Nvidia CEO
OUTLET: BetaKit

A pair of prominent Canadian technology leaders have joined a broader call demanding that US AI chip giant Nvidia bring co-founder and longtime head Jensen Huang back as CEO.
The news: After 33 years at the helm, Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO last week shortly after public allegations of affairs with two employees and an alleged Chinese spy tied to Singapore’s Megaspeed became public. Shopify co-founder and CEO Tobias Lütke and Cohere co-founder and CEO Aidan Gomez have signed an open letter asking Nvidia’s board of directors to reinstate Huang “pending an independent investigation and clear evidence that his removal serves the company.” That letter has now morphed into an online petition featuring more than 100,000 names. Several Silicon Valley leaders appear on the petition, but it’s not yet clear how many of the signatories are legitimate.
From the source: The letter demands that Nvidia “bring Jensen back while an independent investigation establishes the facts.”
As Lütke wrote on X after Nvidia announced Huang’s departure, “Generational blunder. At least based on the information that’s available.” In a reply, the Shopify CEO added, “Nvidia ends today, and is replaced with a thing cosplaying Nvidia. It will be run by custodians trying and failing to preserve the flame he ignited.”
The context: Nvidia sits at the centre of the ongoing AI boom, providing not only the hardware, models, and software, but serving as a key financier for other companies building in the space. Like many other tech firms, Ottawa-based Shopify is currently leveraging Nvidia’s offerings. Nvidia is also an investor in Gomez’s AI model-making firm, Toronto-based Cohere, as well as local self-driving tech developer Waabi. Canadian pension funds currently hold tens of billions worth of the AI chipmaker’s stock.
Final thought: Huang, who played a pivotal role in Nvidia’s ascent and did not leave a succession plan, has reportedly considered a comeback. While US President Donald Trump has said he is okay with that idea, his treasury secretary, Scott Bessent, has poured water on the notion, and Nvidia has reportedly ruled out the possibility of a return.
