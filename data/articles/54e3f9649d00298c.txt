TITLE: England football punished for anti-Catholic chants during Spain UEFA Nations League fixture
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

England's football team have been given a suspended away supporters ban and a five-figure fine for anti-Catholic and anti-Irish chants during their UEFA Nations League fixture against Spain.
The governing body of European football, UEFA, have handed the Football Association (FA) a £12,600 (€15,000) fine and a suspended one-match ban on selling away tickets to fans.
BBC Sport has reported that the chants are of a racist or discriminatory nature. If a further offence is commited in the next two years, the ban blocking away fans from being in the stadium would come into effect.
"We will not tolerate this unacceptable and deeply offensive chanting, and will work with the police, who attend all England home and away matches, to identify the perpetrators and take the necessary action," an FA spokesperson said.
A group representing England fans who travel to away international fixtures has also voiced their the chants.
"There is no place for offensive chants aimed at religious figures. The time for these chants is over," read a statement from Free Lions, the Football Supporters' Association's travel embassy.
"The behaviour and reputation of England supporters abroad has improved significantly over the years but these chants undermine that progress.
"If the chants continue then thousands of travelling fans will miss out on future away games and players will miss that backing in the ground, putting our own team at a disadvantage, at one of the best times to be an England fan."
England picked up a 3-2 win at Riyadh Air Metropolitano Stadium on 15 November to book their place in the quarter-finals of the Nations League. The Three Lions will next face Norway home-and-away for a place in the final four.
The UK Football Policing Unit (UKFPU) also joined in condemnation of the chants, telling Sky Sports News: "The UK Football Policing Unit has consistently made clear that we will not tolerate this type of unacceptable and deeply offensive chanting.
"These incidents have taken place in Spain and fall under the jurisdiction of the Spanish authorities and Spanish law, the UK Football Policing Unit will not be leading any investigation.
"However, we will work closely with the relevant Spanish authorities and provide any assistance necessary to support their enquiries and ensure those responsible are held accountable where appropriate."
