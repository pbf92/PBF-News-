TITLE: Fourth Graders Find Bar Kokhba Revolt-Era Coin
OUTLET: IFCJ

Very few schools around the world discuss with their students what to do when students come across archaeological artifacts. However, in Israel, this is not uncommon, as students have frequently stumbled upon pieces of the Holy Land’s ancient and biblical history. The latest discovery occurred on Sunday in the Jerusalem Hills.
The Jerusalem Post reports that three fourth graders were playing with an air blower when they discovered an ancient coin estimated to be about 1,900 years old, dating back to the period of Roman rule over the land of Israel.
The trip was led by Amit Feinstein, a tour guide and student at Bar-Ilan University’s Department of Land of Israel Studies and Archaeology. According to Feinstein, the three students were playing with an air blower on one of the terraces when they suddenly spotted the coin.
They reported their discovery to their homeroom teacher, who then informed Feinstein.
“When they showed me the coin, I suspected it was from the Roman period,” he told Walla. Since he is not a numismatics (the study of coins) expert, he photographed the coin and sent the pictures to his university lecturers.
“Their initial assessment was that it was a coin from the Roman or Byzantine period,” he said.
The Israel Antiquities Authority (IAA) has yet to fully examine the coin. Interestingly, preliminary assessments suggest that the coin may not only date back to the Roman period but may also have been minted during the reign of Emperor Hadrian (117–138 C.E.), whose rule coincided with the Bar Kokhba Revolt. In a related story, an ancient Byzantine church with underground tunnels used by Jewish rebels during the revolt has opened to the public near Beit Shemesh.
The discovery also provided an important lesson in archaeological preservation for the three 10-year-old students. One of them had to be persuaded of the coin’s historical significance before agreeing to hand it over. The school principal also organized an assembly for the fourth-grade students to discuss the importance of preserving archaeological finds.
Discoveries like this are not uncommon in the Holy Land, where a school field trip or family vacation can suddenly become an unexpected encounter with history. Each new discovery offers another glimpse into the Jewish people’s ancient past and their enduring connection to the land of Israel.
