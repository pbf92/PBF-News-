TITLE: A Rockstar Game Could Be Coming to Hollywood, and It's Not GTA 6
OUTLET: Polygon.com

Dozens of video game franchises have made it to Hollywood and turned into successful movies or hit TV shows. Rockstar Games, which owns some of the most popular video games in history, hasn't taken that leap yet. Hollywood has wanted to get its hands on Grand Theft Auto for years, but The Ankler reports that Rockstar still isn't interested. On the other hand, it seems that Rockstar may be open to discussing a deal for another one of its IPs, Red Dead Redemption.
Rockstar took GTA and added a western flair, creating the wonderful world of Red Dead Redemption, which features some of the developer's most interesting characters to date. According to The Ankler, various sources claim that "the company is more open to bringing Red Dead Redemption, its wildly popular Western action-adventure franchise, to film or television."
Rockstar reportedly wants over $100 million for Red Dead Redemption rights
The adventures of the Van der Linde gang are one of a kind, and would surely appeal to fans of modern Westerns, such as Yellowstone and Deadwood. The report indicates that Rockstar may be asking for more than $100 million for Red Dead Redemption licensing rights alone. That, on top of the production budget, would make for quite an expensive show. A television writer told The Ankler as follows:
                        “The challenge for the gaming companies is that, as you’ll see with Grand Theft Auto, the amount of money they can make on these games far dwarfs anything that Hollywood could make them by adapting these games. We always thought Hollywood was the main player, and if they deigned to call you and up your game, you were really excited; but now the gaming companies more and more are the heavyweights.”
GTA is pretty much a money-printing machine, as we saw from Grand Theft Auto 6's unprecedented release. While Red Dead Redemption 2 was able to reach over 80 million sales during its lifetime, it's still nowhere near GTA 5's astonishing 230 million copies, which its successor is expected to pass.
It's been eight years since Red Dead Redemption 2 came out, so it wouldn't be a bad time to bring back the franchise. Even when a game is widely popular at release, sales tend to dwindle over time. Making movie or TV adaptations of a video game is a great way to boost sales because viewers who discover the IP through the adaptation may become interested in playing it for themselves. In 2024, Fallout games saw an influx of players following the release of Fallout on Prime Video, as veterans got the itch to come back and new fans purchased games in the series for the first time.
For the time being, half of the world is busy playing Grand Theft Auto 6 and won't have much time for anything else. It's one of the reasons why many developers avoided a November release date. Between its story and intricate open world (and, we hope, an online mode eventually), there are hundreds of hours of content ready to be explored. Once the hype dies down, perhaps Rockstar will truly consider signing a deal to make a Red Dead Redemption show; it'd be a great way to build interest in a third game and potentially pave the way for another major release for the company.
