TITLE: 'GTA 6' is here, and these are the top 3 TVs I'd play it on
OUTLET: Business Insider

Like many gamers, I've waited years to play "Grand Theft Auto VI." And now that the game is finally available on PS5 and Xbox consoles, I'm spending most of my free time driving around Vice City. "GTA 6" is one of the most impressive video games ever made, with gorgeous graphics and an insane level of detail. But to really enjoy "GTA 6" in all its glory, you're going to want a display that can do it justice. The best TVs for "GTA 6" can really showcase the game's incredible visuals, with top-notch contrast, rich colors, and low input lag.
For a truly high-end experience, I recommend the LG G6 OLED. This premium set is LG's most advanced TV, with one of the brightest pictures I've seen on an OLED. But if you want to save some cash, the midrange Samsung S90H OLED is also a fantastic TV. The S90H's panel offers the same key benefits as the G6's, but it can't get as bright, so the game's HDR highlights won't have quite the same impact. And on the budget side, the entry-level TCL QM6K QLED delivers great gaming performance for the money, though it's dimmer than pricier options and its contrast isn't as high as my OLED picks.
I've kept things simple here with my top three TV recommendations for "GTA 6," since these sets span different budgets while offering the best combination of features I've found to really get the most out of the game (and other titles). But that doesn't mean that these models are the only ones worth getting for "Grand Theft Auto." There are other OLED, Mini LED, and RGB LED sets worth considering, so if you're looking for more recommendations, head over to my full guide to the best TVs for details on all the models my team and I have tested.
Best high-end: LG G6
What we love: Its high-end OLED screen delivers an infinite contrast ratio, impressive brightness, wide color gamut, low input lag, and excellent off-axis viewing. It also has an elegant design that looks beautiful on a wall.
Drawbacks: It's expensive, doesn't include a stand, and its glossy screen could cause reflections from lamps and windows.
The LG G6 is one of the most impressive TVs on the market, offering an amazing image for video games, movies, and streaming. Like all OLEDs, it delivers infinite contrast, but the G6 gets bonus points for using one of the brightest OLED panels available (tied with Samsung's S95H). This is the TV I'm currently using to play "GTA 6" on, and the experience has been genuinely stunning.
The OLED panel's pixel-level contrast gives Vice City's neon-soaked cityscape a gorgeous sheen at night with bold highlights that shine with pinpoint precision against deep, inky black levels. Daytime exploration is just as impressive, with sunrises and sunsets that sparkle from the screen. Detail is rich, and I can sit off to the side of the couch without sacrificing color or contrast.
OLED TVs like the G6 are also known for offering a near-instant response time, which is typically a good thing for video games since it reduces blur. However, since "GTA 6" runs at 30 fps (frames per second), this lack of blur has the potential to exaggerate the choppiness of its lower frame rate. But thankfully, I haven't noticed any display-related stutter or judder while playing "GTA 6" on the G6, so I don't consider this an issue. Though "GTA 6" doesn't take full advantage of the G6's high refresh rate (120Hz on consoles and up to 165Hz on PC), the game still looks smooth, and the panel's low input lag is great for action.
I also find LG's Game Optimizer menu to be one of the most user-friendly and comprehensive gaming menus across TV brands. It allows you to pull up an overlay with details about the current gaming signal and easy access to adjust key settings.
The G6's only real downsides are its high price (though it's often on sale) and the lack of an included stand (it's meant to be wall-mounted). It also uses a glossy screen finish, which could be a pro or con depending on your preferences. Glossy screens preserve black levels better with the lights on, but they can be prone to mirror-like reflections. However, the G6 does a rather impressive job of minimizing glare for a glossy screen. It's nowhere near as effective as a matte-style TV, like the Samsung S90H, but it's better than any other glossy OLED I've tested.
Best midrange: Samsung S90H
What we love: Its OLED panel delivers pixel-level contrast with wide viewing angles and low input lag, it has a highly effective anti-glare screen, and it's available in many sizes.
Drawbacks: It isn't as bright as higher-end OLEDs, like the LG G6, and its matte-like screen causes slightly lifted black levels in bright rooms.
If the G6 is too pricey or you're worried about reflections in your room, I highly recommend the Samsung S90H as a midrange alternative. The S90H is one of the most versatile TVs around, offering excellent performance for games, movies, and sports in a variety of spaces.
Like the LG G6, this TV uses an OLED panel with all the benefits OLEDs are known for: pixel-level contrast control, wide viewing angles, and deep, inky black levels in a dark room. These picture-quality elements will ensure an excellent image while playing "GTA 6." However, the S90H is not as bright as the G6, coming in at around 1,000 nits dimmer. To be clear, the S90H is no slouch in brightness, but HDR highlights in "GTA 6" won't have quite the same impact here as they would on a brighter TV.
The S90H stands out from a lot of other OLEDs with its matte-like anti-glare screen. This could be a good or bad thing depending on your priorities. On the one hand, this screen does an incredible job of reducing reflections, making it easier to game in rooms with lots of lamps or windows. On the other hand, the way the screen scatters light can make black levels look a tad elevated when the lights are on. Some people find this side effect more subtle than others, but if you struggle with reflections in your room, the pros far outweigh the cons.
Another potential positive in the S90H's favor is its availability in a lot of screen sizes, from 42 to 83 inches. Just note that the 77-inch size uses a more advanced OLED panel, so it can get noticeably brighter than other sizes, making it an even better buy if your space and budget allow.

Best entry-level: TCL QM6K
What we love: It's very affordable, available in lots of sizes, and delivers solid contrast for a budget Mini LED TV.
Drawbacks: It's dimmer than pricier TVs, lacks the pixel-level contrast control of an OLED, and has narrow viewing angles.
For those on a budget, the TCL QM6K is a great entry-level TV for "GTA 6." This affordable QLED stands out from most rivals in this price range by offering key image-quality features, such as a Mini LED backlight with local dimming, that many budget TVs lack. This helps it deliver solid image quality with games like "GTA 6."
The QM6K isn't an OLED, so it can't match the infinite contrast ratio and true black levels offered by the LG G6 or Samsung S90H. But it's much more affordable, and its picture quality is very good for this class. It's an especially great option if you want a massive screen for a surprisingly reasonable price, as the 98-inch model is sometimes on sale for as low as $1,698.
With its Mini LED backlight and local dimming, the QM6K delivers solid contrast for the money. When reviewing the TV, I only noticed minor blooming (halos around bright objects) in some tricky scenes. However, the TV's black levels never quite disappear into a dark room, so if you play "GTA 6" with the lights off, you may notice nighttime environments looking a little grayer than they would on an OLED.
Likewise, the TV's brightness is limited compared to pricier displays, with around half the peak brightness of the Samsung S90H. This will lessen the impact of bright highlights in the game, but again, what you get here is solid for this price range. Viewing angles are also mediocre, so this TV is best for gamers who play directly in front of their screen.
Since this is a QLED TV with an LCD panel, the QM6K's response time is slower than that of an OLED. Traditionally, this would be a con for gaming, but with "GTA 6" it could actually be a benefit. Since "GTA 6" only runs at 30 fps, a slower response time could help blur some of the choppiness that comes with lower frame rates. However, as I mentioned earlier, I haven't encountered any display-related stutter while playing "GTA 6" on an OLED display, so I wouldn't choose the QM6K solely for this reason.

FAQs
Is an OLED or Mini LED better for 'GTA 6'?
OLED and Mini LED (or RGB LED) TVs can both be good choices for "GTA 6," as each has different strengths and weaknesses.
Generally, OLED is the premium pick for playing in a dark or moderately lit room because it delivers perfect black levels and excellent contrast. OLED also offers near-instant pixel response times, which is typically considered a good thing for video games. But with "GTA 6," that's not necessarily the case. The reason? Fast response times have the potential to create a stuttering effect when playing lower-frame-rate titles. However, I haven't noticed any display-related stutter while playing hours of "GTA 6" on LG's G6 OLED, so I wouldn't avoid an OLED TV for that reason.
Meanwhile, Mini LED TVs can be a better fit for especially bright rooms, since top models can reach higher brightness levels than OLED TVs. They also tend to be more affordable, especially in larger screen sizes. However, Mini LED TVs rely on backlights and local dimming to control their brightness and contrast, which can introduce halos, blooming, and elevated black levels. They also have worse viewing angles, so color and contrast degrade and distort when you sit off to the side of the screen.
Should you use HDR when playing 'GTA 6'?
In general, yes, I highly recommend using the HDR (high dynamic range) feature while playing "GTA 6," assuming your TV supports HDR. When enabled and properly set up, HDR enhances contrast and color, giving sunny outdoor scenes, neon-lit nighttime cityscapes, and other bright highlights a greater, more lifelike impact.
The Samsung S90H and LG G6 both offer exceptional HDR performance, while the QM6K is no slouch for its lower price tag. However, to get the most out of HDR while playing "GTA 6," you'll need to calibrate the game's HDR output by following the instructions in the game's Settings menu.
You should also ensure that HGiG is toggled on in your TV's picture settings menu. (On a Samsung TV, this is accomplished by selecting Basic in the TV's Game HDR field.) This will enable the game's test patterns to work properly.
However, even with HGiG toggled, the "GTA 6" HDR calibration menu is a bit trickier than it needs to be. If you're having trouble getting a good image, I recommend checking out these videos from HDTVTest and GamingTech, both of which offer strategies for optimizing the game's HDR output.
Does 'GTA 6' support 120fps on PS5 or Xbox Series X?
No, GTA 6 targets 30fps on current-generation consoles, including PS5, PS5 Pro, and Xbox Series X|S. A 120Hz TV is still a good idea if you're playing other games that support higher frame rates and features like VRR (variable refresh rate), but it won't enable "GTA 6" to run any better.
Should you play 'GTA 6' with Game Mode enabled on your TV?
Many TVs, including my picks in this guide, support specific Game Modes to optimize gaming performance, and these settings can positively impact how "GTA 6" looks and responds on your TV. Most notably, Game Mode reduces input lag, so there's less delay between pressing a button and seeing the action happen on screen. This can make driving, aiming, and reacting during hectic missions feel more responsive.
