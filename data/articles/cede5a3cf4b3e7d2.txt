TITLE: Half of young men have played Grand Theft Auto VI or plan to
OUTLET: YouGov

One in four Americans say they have either played the newly released Grand Theft Auto VI (7%) or plan to (18%), according to the latest Economist / YouGov Poll. Interest in the game is even higher among younger adults: Half of men under 45 say they've played (16%) or plan to play (36%) Grand Theft Auto VI, while one-third of women under 45 say they've played it (9%) or plan to (24%). 88% of Americans 45 and older say they haven't played Grand Theft Auto VI and have no plans to.
While the Grand Theft Auto series has long involved players committing crimes in simulated open-world cities, the sixth installment has attracted discussion for a new feature: chores. Among the game's optional missions for players are having its main character, Jason Duval, fix up his house to impress his girlfriend Lucia Caminos, the game's other main character.
Among Americans who've played Grand Theft Auto VI, 66% say the new chore features make the game better and only 9% say they make the game worse. Those who haven't played yet but plan to play it have similar views: 63% say the chores make the game better and 9% say they make it worse.
There's little difference between the views of men and women about Grand Theft Auto VI's chores. Younger and more liberal Americans are more likely to say the chores make the game better, while older and more conservative Americans are more likely to say they make the game worse.
The range of opinions about Grand Theft Auto VI reflects video games' status as a hugely popular pastime — but one concentrated among younger people. 38% of Americans play video games a few times a week or more, including 59% of 18- to 29-year-olds but only 17% of those 65 and older. Only 14% of adults under 30 say they never play video games, while 71% of those 65 and older never play.
Video games are especially popular among young men: 71% of men under 30 play at least a few times a week. But almost half (48%) of women under 30 also play video games a few times a week or more.
The Economist and YouGov asked Americans who play video games what their favorite games are, in an open-ended question. The responses included hundreds of different titles, based on coding of the responses by an AI model; some respondents named more than one favorite. 8% mentioned the Grand Theft Auto series, making it the second most-named game or game series.
Some gamers were so excited about Grand Theft Auto VI that they took off work to play. That experience should resonate with more than one-quarter of gamers: 28% of people who ever play video games (and 17% of all adults) say they've ever played video games when they were supposed to be working, including 39% of those who play video games daily or almost every day.
