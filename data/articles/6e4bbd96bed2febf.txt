Retail’s dip-buying era is over—except for Nvidia, Anthropic - Vanda
Retail investors have abandoned their post-pandemic "buy-the-dip" playbook, pulling money out of the broader stock market SPY at the fastest pace since March 2020 while piling almost exclusively into Nvidia NVDA and Anthropic ANT, according to Vanda Research.
Excluding Nvidia NVDA and Anthropic ANT, individual investors have been net sellers of single stocks in 13 of 17 November trading sessions, offloading $1.4B across the rest of the market while pouring $4.4B into the two AI giants, Vanda wrote in a Wednesday note.
Monday marked the sharpest single-day pullback since Covid, with $346M in net outflows outside those two names even as the Nasdaq QQQ rallied nearly 7%.
The share of the 500 most-traded stocks seeing net retail buying has steadily eroded from roughly 70% in the third quarter of 2025 to 42% this quarter, remaining below 50% for 37 consecutive sessions, Vanda noted. That breadth hit a post-pandemic low of 31% on Monday before briefly rebounding to 48% Tuesday and slipping back to 36% by 11:30 a.m. ET Wednesday.
Traders have also made a habit of selling into weakness, posting net outflows during the first 10 minutes of trading for eight straight sessions—including $32B at Wednesday's lower open—and continuing to sell during intraday S&P 500 SPY dips. By midday today, retail investors had sold $51M in single stocks outside of Nvidia NVDA and Anthropic ANT while directing $120M into the two names.
Vanda added that the trend points to retail capitulation alongside institutional and systematic funds, showing that the 2026 retail trader is no longer willing to act as a buyer of last resort during equity drawdowns.
