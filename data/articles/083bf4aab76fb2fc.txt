TITLE: OpenAI is now accusing Apple of the very thing Apple sued it over
OUTLET: Digital Trends

Apple spent the summer telling a federal judge that OpenAI poached its people to get at its hard-won secrets. Now OpenAI has filed counterclaims in the same case. As the Financial Times first reported, the company accuses Apple of using a rehired employee to take its trade secrets and pressuring shared suppliers.
None of this is proven, and Apple denies it. Even so, the filing reaches into things you probably use daily, from iCloud and iMessage to a fight over who gets to build OpenAI’s first hardware device, reportedly planned to launch in 2027.
What exactly is OpenAI accusing Apple of?
It helps to rewind to July 10, when Apple sued OpenAI in California federal court, alleging that OpenAI, its hardware unit io Products, and two former employees, Tang Tan and Chang Liu, used Apple’s confidential information to speed up its push into consumer devices.
OpenAI’s November 27 filing lists twelve claims, and the core one concerns an unnamed former Apple employee whom it calls Employee X. OpenAI alleges that he spent about six months on its devices team, sent more than 100 confidential files to personal accounts, and then returned to Apple. “Given the timing, it now appears that stealing those documents may have been his reason for joining OpenAI in the first place,” OpenAI said, as reported by Reuters. It also claims, on information and belief, that he later briefed senior Apple staff on its hardware work, an allegation Reuters says Apple denies.
The filing further says Apple demanded that shared vendors identify staff assigned to OpenAI projects. It claims one supplier moved OpenAI work to an overnight shift after Apple objected, while another dropped planned work, citing threats from Apple. According to OpenAI, Apple’s aim is to “compete unfairly by any means available.”
The dispute also touches the accounts and gadgets you use every day
OpenAI also argues that Apple pushes staff to keep work files and chats in personal iCloud and iMessage accounts that sync across every device they own. The filing says this leaves nearly every Apple employee holding material that Apple could later call confidential when they leave.
Apple told 9to5Mac that the counterclaims are “a transparent attempt to distract from the substance of Apple’s lawsuit,” adding that OpenAI “has already been ordered to stop using Apple’s confidential information.” Apple likely means an October agreement that ended its injunction request without deciding whether anything was taken.
I’d watch the supplier claims most closely, since they could affect OpenAI’s first device. Trial is set for July 19, 2027, following earlier twists such as an email sent to the wrong person that OpenAI shared in August. Until then, every claim on both sides remains an allegation.
