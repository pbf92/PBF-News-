TITLE: InMobi lets seven AI agents run small firms' Google, Meta and ChatGPT ads
OUTLET: PPC Land

On November 18, 2026, InMobi launched InMobi Accelerate, a platform that uses seven AI agents to plan, build, launch and measure advertising campaigns for small and medium-sized businesses and the agencies that serve them, across Google Ads, Meta Ads, Microsoft Advertising, TikTok Ads and ChatGPT Ads from a single dashboard. It is available at accelerate.inmobi.com and, according to InMobi, is free to start.
The announcement, issued from San Mateo, California at 7:00 AM Eastern, frames the product as a substitute for the specialist staff a growing business would otherwise have to hire. It also puts a new number in front of advertisers. Accelerate counts conversions with InMobi's own tracking, removes the duplicates that arise when several platforms claim the same sale, and reports one cross-channel return figure under a name of its own. That figure then feeds Mobius, the decisioning system InMobi unveiled six weeks ago.
In Short
InMobi on November 18 launched Accelerate, an online tool in which seven AI agents plan, create and launch ad campaigns for small businesses and agencies on Google, Meta, Microsoft, TikTok and ChatGPT from one place. It matters to advertisers who juggle several platforms that each report results their own way, because Accelerate counts sales once with InMobi's own tracking and gives a single return figure. Nothing goes live without approval, and continuous automatic changes only run if the advertiser switches them on and sets limits, though the announcement publishes no prices beyond "free to start."
What InMobi launched
InMobi describes Accelerate as a self-serve agentic AI advertising platform that "plans, builds, launches and measures campaigns across major advertising platforms from one dashboard." The intended users are spelled out. "Accelerate is built for small and medium-sized businesses that want to grow without standing up a large in-house marketing function, and for the agencies that serve them," according to InMobi.
The work is divided among seven agents. InMobi names them as a business analyst, an audience strategist, a media planner, a copywriter, a creative designer, a launch engineer and an optimizer. Each, the company says, takes "on a distinct responsibility," though the announcement does not describe the duties of any single agent beyond its title.
Rohit Dosi, VP and GM of InMobi Accelerate, set out the commercial argument. "AI agents change the economics of expertise," he said. "A growing business should be able to run a disciplined advertising program without hiring seven specialists first, and an agency should be able to take on more clients without adding repetitive work at the same rate. Accelerate puts that capability in the hands of the people running the campaigns."
The platform was introduced at Glance Forward in New York, which InMobi describes as a gathering of technology, commerce and advertising leaders "examining how AI is changing the way consumers find and buy products."
How Accelerate works, according to InMobi
The announcement describes the path from sign-up to optimization in sequence. According to InMobi, it runs as follows:
- A business begins with its website and a campaign objective.
- The system analyzes the company.
- It develops audiences and a media plan.
- It creates campaign assets.
- It assembles budgets for review.
- Every plan, creative asset and budget is presented for approval before anything goes live.
- Once live, campaigns can be adjusted through plain-English instructions, so a marketer can shift budget between channels or revise creative without rebuilding the campaign in separate platform interfaces.
- Continuous optimization runs only if the advertiser authorizes it, within parameters the advertiser sets.
- Conversions are counted through InMobi's first-party tracking pixel and universal conversion tracking, then put through deduplication across connected platforms and reported as a single cross-channel view.
- Those results feed Mobius, which uses campaign history and performance signals to recommend or execute changes within the advertiser's parameters, and each campaign informs the planning of the next.
The onboarding sequence on the product page
The product page at accelerate.inmobi.com describes a shorter, four-stage sequence for new accounts. Advertisers connect their existing ad accounts once; import past campaigns along with their results; review those campaigns, including which platforms are counting the same conversions twice; and then receive a first set of proposed changes from the platform's assistant, choosing which of them go live. The same page states that connected accounts remain the advertiser's own, that experiments do not spend the advertiser's ad budget and that the service can be cancelled in one click. It carries no pricing section.
The rules on automation
On control, InMobi's wording is specific. "Advertisers decide how much of this runs on its own. Every plan, creative asset and budget is presented for approval before anything goes live," the announcement states. On ongoing changes: "Advertisers who want continuous optimization can authorize it within parameters they set, while rules, approvals and role-based access keep every change inside those limits."
The default, in other words, is a person signing off. Automation that acts without a fresh approval each time is an opt-in, bounded by limits the advertiser defines. The announcement does not list which parameters can be set, whether budget caps are among them, or whether changes are logged.
One number across five platforms
The measurement claim is the part of the launch with the widest consequences. Each ad platform measures what happens inside its own environment, a structure PPC Land has described as single-publisher attribution. When a customer sees an ad on Meta, clicks one on Google and buys, both platforms can count the sale. InMobi acknowledges this directly. "Each advertising platform reports on the activity inside its own environment, which is what it is built to do," according to the company. "Assembling those reports into a single picture falls to the advertiser or their agency, and it is slow work that is difficult to get right."
Accelerate's answer is to stand outside those reports. The company says it "uses InMobi's first-party pixel and universal conversion tracking to deduplicate conversion events across connected platforms and produce one cross-channel view." InMobi calls the resulting measure True ROAS, short for return on ad spend. The announcement does not explain how a sale is credited when more than one platform touched it, which lookback windows apply, or whether view-through conversions count.
That gap matters because of who computes the number. Accelerate plans the campaigns, writes the ads, proposes the budgets, can optimize the spend once authorized, and then grades the outcome with a measure InMobi defines. Platform-reported ROAS draws the same objection: the party that sells or allocates the media also scores it. The announcement names no independent auditor or measurement partner for True ROAS.
The figure also flows into Mobius. When InMobi unveiled Mobius on October 5, it presented the system as a decisioning layer across its exchange, its demand-side platform and ads on Glance, and said it had reached 8.6 million Grocery Outlet shoppers over a 12-day campaign. The November 18 announcement repeats the scale claims. "Mobius processes more than 25 trillion predictions and evaluates more than 200 billion advertising opportunities on an average day," according to InMobi, which describes the system as one that "forecasts advertising options against the outcome an advertiser wants."
The survey behind the pitch
InMobi supports the launch with research it commissioned. NewtonX surveyed 800 US small and medium-sized business advertisers and agencies for InMobi in May 2026.
The two InMobi documents do not describe the multi-platform finding in the same way. The announcement says more than 70% run campaigns on three or more platforms; the product page puts the figure at 70% or more for two or more platforms. They also frame the 93% figure differently, as an inability to measure across platforms in one and as the biggest problem cited in the other. Neither document publishes question wording, a margin of error or the split between business owners and agencies.
The direction of the findings is consistent with other research. A NewtonX survey of 885 senior marketers for StackAdapt, published on October 19, found that only 7% run fully unified measurement, and 42% said silos waste more than a fifth of their programmatic budget. That sample covered brands and agencies with at least 100 employees across 13 countries, a different population from InMobi's US small-business respondents.
ChatGPT Ads in the launch list
The inclusion of ChatGPT Ads among the launch platforms reflects how quickly OpenAI's ad business has moved. OpenAI opened ChatGPT Ads Manager to all US businesses on May 5, with cost-per-click bidding, and by October 21 ChatGPT ads were sold in 89 countries, with OpenAI saying more than 100,000 advertisers had run campaigns since launch. The InMobi announcement does not say how its connection to ChatGPT Ads is made or which campaign settings it can control there.
Glance is the other name in the platform list, as a planned integration. InMobi calls Glance "the AI-powered personal shopping platform backed by InMobi." The ownership is set out at the foot of the announcement: Glance "is owned and operated by Glance InMobi Pte. Ltd., an associate of InMobi Technology Services Pte. Ltd." On June 3, InMobi opened premium connected TV and mobile supply, including Glance, to AI buying agents and said Glance's 300 million active devices could be reached through one protocol connection. Once the planned integration arrives, a small advertiser's budget allocated by InMobi's agents could flow to inventory from a company within the InMobi group. The announcement gives no date for that integration and does not address how Glance would be treated in budget recommendations.
A crowded field of campaign agents
Accelerate arrives in a year in which software that writes to live ad accounts has moved from experiment to product. The approaches differ mainly on who presses the button.

Two differences stand out. Most of the earlier tools are built around a chat interface and assume a marketer who already knows what to ask for. Accelerate is pitched at businesses that have no such person, and it starts from a website rather than a brief. It also bundles its own conversion measurement with execution.
For InMobi, the product extends a run of launches built around agents. Buyer Hub, which debuted for curators in June 2025, opened to agencies with a conversational agent in August. Mobius followed in October. Those products centered on InMobi's own exchange and supply. Accelerate points the same agent approach at small advertisers buying on Google, Meta, Microsoft, TikTok and OpenAI.
Why this matters for marketers
The small-business advertiser has become a contested customer. Each large platform already offers its own automated campaign types, which optimize toward conversions the platform itself counts. A third-party layer that plans across all of them, and reports results on its own scale, changes where decisions about budget split are made. If Accelerate's True ROAS shows a channel performing worse than that channel's own dashboard claims, the agents can move money away from it, subject to the approval rules the advertiser has set.
For agencies, Dosi's quote points to a different calculation: taking on more clients "without adding repetitive work at the same rate." That pressure is visible across the market.
