TITLE: Lung Cancer Review Evaluates Pneumonitis Risks Across Diverse Immunotherapy Regimens
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive systematic review and frequentist random-effects network meta-analysis published in Frontiers in Pharmacology has evaluated the comparative risk of immune checkpoint inhibitor-associated pneumonitis across sixteen treatment regimens in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer. Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally, accounting for an estimated 2.5 million new diagnoses and 1.8 million deaths in 2022, with non-small cell lung cancer representing roughly 85 percent of all diagnosed cases. Because a substantial proportion of patients receive a diagnosis only after the malignancy has progressed to advanced or metastatic stages, long-term clinical prognosis has historically been unfavorable. In recent years, immune checkpoint inhibitors administered either as monotherapy or combined with cytotoxic chemotherapy have reshaped the therapeutic landscape and established standard treatment pathways for individuals lacking targetable oncogenic driver alterations. However, while these therapeutic agents enhance antitumor immune responses, they can also disrupt normal immune tolerance and trigger immune-related adverse events across multiple organ systems. Among these toxicities, lung inflammation designated as immune checkpoint inhibitor-associated pneumonitis poses severe clinical concerns because it impairs respiratory function, prompts treatment interruption or permanent discontinuation, and carries reported historical case-fatality rates ranging between 10 percent and 17 percent.
To resolve critical knowledge gaps left by prior meta-analyses—which largely pooled broad drug classes, compared general therapeutic categories like monotherapy versus chemoimmunotherapy, or evaluated composite toxicity profiles rather than isolating pulmonary complications—investigators conducted an exhaustive multi-database search across PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science from 1 January 2010 to 20 October 2026. The search protocol was prospectively registered in PROSPERO under registration identifier CRD420251186476 and adhered closely to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses incorporating Network Meta-Analyses statement. Eligible investigations were phase II or phase III randomized controlled trials enrolling adults with pathologically confirmed advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer receiving checkpoint inhibitor agents recommended by prominent clinical guidelines such as those from the National Comprehensive Cancer Network, the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology, and the American Society of Clinical Oncology. Eligible regimens were evaluated as monotherapies or in exclusive combination with cytotoxic chemotherapy, comparing them against chemotherapy or alternative eligible immunotherapies. Studies were strictly required to provide extractable arm-level event counts and corresponding safety-population denominators for all-grade (grade 1 to 5) or severe (grade 3 to 5) pneumonitis.
The study selection process identified 11,629 initial records, ultimately incorporating 34 randomized controlled trials comprising 18,065 patients whose treatment arms contributed to at least one pneumonitis synthesis. The systematic assessment excluded trials with fewer than 30 randomized participants per eligible arm, duplicate cohorts, non-original studies, perioperative investigations, trials combining radiotherapy within the experimental strategy, and regimens containing anti-vascular endothelial growth factor agents or targeted bispecific antibodies. Furthermore, trials exclusively or predominantly enrolling patients with oncogenic driver mutations or impaired performance status were excluded. Methodological quality evaluation using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool indicated that seven included trials demonstrated an overall low risk of bias, 24 trials presented some concerns, and three trials exhibited a high risk of bias. Across individual domains, all 34 trials exhibited low risk regarding the randomization process, missing outcome data, and selective reporting. The identified methodological concerns arose primarily within the domains evaluating deviations from intended interventions, where 25 trials had some concerns and two were at high risk, and outcome measurement, where 19 trials had some concerns and one trial was deemed high risk.
Statistical modeling synthesized direct and indirect clinical evidence within a frequentist network meta-analysis framework executed in Stata version 18.0 using restricted maximum likelihood estimation to determine between-study variance across comparisons. Treatment effects were quantified as odds ratios accompanied by 95 percent confidence intervals. In the global overall network, cytotoxic chemotherapy arms were combined into a common comparator node to provide broad cross-setting evaluation, whereas the first-line network specified platinum-based chemotherapy as the reference node and the subsequent-line network designated docetaxel as the benchmark. For primary odds ratio calculations, double-zero event comparisons were excluded independently per severity outcome, and single-arm zero events received a standard 0.5 continuity correction across all four cells of the two-by-two table. Absolute pneumonitis proportions were calculated by pooling nodes supported by two or more trials via maximum-likelihood binomial-logit mixed models with study-level random intercepts, retaining zero-event arms without continuity corrections, while single-trial nodes were characterized using observed proportions and exact binomial 95 percent confidence intervals.
In the overall network, which encompassed 33 trials with 17,691 patients across 16 treatment nodes for grade 1 to 5 pneumonitis and 32 trials with 17,630 patients across 16 treatment nodes for grade 3 to 5 pneumonitis, absolute proportion estimates among checkpoint inhibitor regimens ranged from 1.88 percent to 12.50 percent for all-grade pneumonitis and from 0.45 percent to 4.13 percent for severe pneumonitis. When compared directly with cytotoxic chemotherapy, significantly higher estimated odds of all-grade pneumonitis were documented for monotherapies with atezolizumab, pembrolizumab, nivolumab, and tislelizumab, as well as for chemotherapy combined with atezolizumab, nivolumab, pembrolizumab, tislelizumab, or toripalimab. Specifically, pembrolizumab monotherapy exhibited an odds ratio of 6.34 with a 95 percent confidence interval from 3.02 to 13.30 relative to chemotherapy. For severe grade 3 to 5 pneumonitis, elevated odds compared to chemotherapy were observed for atezolizumab, pembrolizumab, nivolumab, and tislelizumab monotherapies, as well as tislelizumab plus chemotherapy. Pembrolizumab monotherapy demonstrated an odds ratio of 6.27 (95 percent confidence interval: 2.49 to 15.80) and tislelizumab plus chemotherapy yielded an odds ratio of 6.20 (95 percent confidence interval: 1.16 to 33.20).
Direct and indirect comparative evaluations among the checkpoint inhibitor-containing regimens in the overall network revealed that no statistically significant differences emerged for all-grade pneumonitis. However, distinct relative differences materialized for severe grade 3 to 5 pneumonitis. Atezolizumab combined with chemotherapy showed significantly lower estimated odds of severe pneumonitis when compared against pembrolizumab monotherapy, yielding an odds ratio of 0.27 with a 95 percent confidence interval between 0.08 and 0.94. Similarly, atezolizumab plus chemotherapy demonstrated lower estimated odds when compared against tislelizumab monotherapy, producing an odds ratio of 0.13 with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning 0.02 to 0.83. The remaining pairwise comparisons among active immunotherapy regimens in the overall network did not attain statistical significance. Absolute pooled proportions in the overall analysis demonstrated an all-grade pneumonitis frequency of 2.90 percent (95 percent confidence interval: 1.70 to 4.92 percent) for atezolizumab monotherapy, 7.60 percent (95 percent confidence interval: 5.91 to 9.73 percent) for pembrolizumab monotherapy, 6.92 percent for atezolizumab plus chemotherapy, 8.47 percent for pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, and an observed 8.99 percent for tislelizumab monotherapy.
Analysis of the first-line treatment network, which comprised 22 trials involving 12,315 patients across 14 nodes for all-grade pneumonitis and 12,332 patients across 15 nodes for severe pneumonitis, underscored notable differences across initial therapeutic choices relative to platinum-based chemotherapy. Elevated odds of grade 1 to 5 pneumonitis versus platinum chemotherapy were observed for atezolizumab, pembrolizumab, nivolumab, and cemiplimab monotherapies, as well as platinum chemotherapy combinations with atezolizumab, durvalumab, pembrolizumab, tislelizumab, or toripalimab. Pembrolizumab monotherapy demonstrated an odds ratio of 17.23 (95 percent confidence interval: 6.24 to 47.53), while tislelizumab plus platinum chemotherapy showed an odds ratio of 15.92 (95 percent confidence interval: 3.85 to 65.81). Severe pneumonitis odds versus platinum chemotherapy were significantly elevated for atezolizumab, pembrolizumab, and tislelizumab monotherapies, as well as tislelizumab plus platinum chemotherapy, with pembrolizumab monotherapy showing an odds ratio of 11.07 (95 percent confidence interval: 2.55 to 48.14) and tislelizumab monotherapy reaching an odds ratio of 18.43 (95 percent confidence interval: 1.93 to 176.32).
Crucially, first-line indirect comparisons among active immunotherapies showed that several combination regimens and single agents had lower estimated odds of all-grade pneumonitis than pembrolizumab monotherapy. These included durvalumab monotherapy with an odds ratio of 0.13 (95 percent confidence interval: 0.03 to 0.56), atezolizumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy with an odds ratio of 0.25 (95 percent confidence interval: 0.08 to 0.80), pembrolizumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy with an odds ratio of 0.16 (95 percent confidence interval: 0.05 to 0.54), and toripalimab plus platinum-based chemotherapy with an odds ratio of 0.22 (95 percent confidence interval: 0.05 to 0.88). In contrast, tislelizumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy exhibited higher estimated odds of all-grade pneumonitis compared to durvalumab monotherapy (odds ratio: 7.05, 95 percent confidence interval: 1.21 to 41.16) and compared to pembrolizumab plus platinum chemotherapy (odds ratio: 5.62, 95 percent confidence interval: 1.19 to 26.61). For first-line severe pneumonitis, atezolizumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy maintained lower estimated odds than pembrolizumab monotherapy, with an odds ratio of 0.15 (95 percent confidence interval: 0.03 to 0.83).
In the subsequent-line treatment setting, which synthesized eleven trials with 5,376 patients across seven nodes for all-grade toxicity and ten trials with 5,298 patients across six nodes for severe complications, docetaxel alone served as the reference comparator. Relative to docetaxel, higher estimated odds of grade 1 to 5 pneumonitis were documented for pembrolizumab monotherapy (odds ratio: 3.48, 95 percent confidence interval: 1.62 to 7.49), nivolumab monotherapy (odds ratio: 9.78, 95 percent confidence interval: 2.59 to 36.89), tislelizumab monotherapy (odds ratio: 51.54, 95 percent confidence interval: 3.10 to 857.33), nivolumab plus docetaxel (odds ratio: 8.69, 95 percent confidence interval: 1.57 to 48.09), and pembrolizumab plus docetaxel (odds ratio: 5.23, 95 percent confidence interval: 1.01 to 27.01). Furthermore, indirect comparisons among active regimens indicated that tislelizumab monotherapy carried markedly higher estimated odds of all-grade pneumonitis than atezolizumab monotherapy (odds ratio: 22.29, 95 percent confidence interval: 1.04 to 478.00).
