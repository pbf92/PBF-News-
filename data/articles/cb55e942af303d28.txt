TITLE: News takes 32% of Latin American podcast listening, Triton finds
OUTLET: PPC Land

Triton Digital today released its 2026 Latin America Audio Insights Report, and its headline number is one the company has printed before. News accounts for 32% of podcast downloads across the Latin American networks Triton measures, roughly double the share of fiction (16%) and comedy (15%), and it supplies 42% of all new episodes those networks publish.
The report is a two-page document built on data from January to August 2026. It combines podcast download measurement, streaming radio measurement and an AI analysis of Spanish-language episodes, and it ranks sales networks, shows, debuts and streaming publishers. Prisa Radio sits at or near the top of nearly every table. The 32% share for news is the same figure news held in Triton's first regional report, released on August 1, 2024, which covered June 2023 to May 2024.
In Short
Triton Digital's 2026 report says news makes up 32% of podcast downloads and 42% of new episodes on the Latin American networks it measures, and Prisa Radio leads both the podcast and the streaming radio rankings. That matters to anyone buying or selling Spanish- and Portuguese-language audio, because the measured inventory is concentrated in a handful of publishers and in short news shows that Triton says are mostly heard as soon as they come out. The data covers only networks that use Triton's measurement, so it describes that part of the market, not the whole region.
What the report measures
According to the release, the report draws on three Triton products. The first is Podcast Metrics, which Triton describes as "IAB Tech Lab-certified" and which "measures server log data from Omny Studio and other leading hosting platforms." Omny Studio is Triton's own hosting service, sold alongside its measurement and ad platform in deals such as the Media Prima agreement covering five Malaysian radio brands in September 2026. The second is Triton Webcast Metrics, which measures live audio streams. The third is Sounder, which the release calls "Triton's AI audio-intelligence platform for Spanish-language podcasts." Triton acquired Sounder in March 2024.
Each chart carries a footnote that sets out its basis. The podcast charts state: "Analysis is based on networks measured by Triton Podcast Metrics for downloads in the LATAM region from January - August 2026." The ranking tables are "Based on the Triton LATAM Podcast Rankers from January - August 2026." The streaming charts are "based on total listening hours (TLH) or average active sessions (AAS) from networks measured by Triton Webcast Metrics from January - August 2026."
That wording defines the sample. The figures describe networks measured by Triton, not every podcast published in Latin America. The closing panel of the report makes the point itself, inviting publishers in regions "where Triton produces rankers" to apply, with the line "You may qualify to join." The document gives no total download volume, no count of measured networks or shows, and no split by country. One network's figures are partial: a note under two of the tables states that "Sonoro Media's totals are based on 6 months of reporting (from January - June 2026)." No reason is given.
News sets the agenda and the publishing calendar
Two charts describe genre. One measures each genre's share of downloads, which the report and the release call listening. The other measures each genre's share of new episodes released. Set side by side, they show where supply and demand diverge.
News and sports publish more than they are downloaded, in relative terms. News takes 42% of new episodes against 32% of downloads; sports takes 16% of new episodes against 10% of downloads. Comedy runs the other way: 15% of downloads from 6% of new episodes, which by PPC Land's calculation is 2.5 times its share of releases. Fiction is the sharpest case. It draws 16% of downloads yet does not appear among the 14 named genres in the release chart, the smallest of which hold 2%. The report does not comment on the gap.
A download share is not a share of listening time, and the report's own duration figures show why that distinction matters here. According to Sounder's analysis, the average comedy episode runs 58 minutes, the average sports episode 35 minutes and the average news episode 11 minutes. A download of an 11-minute bulletin and a download of an hour of comedy count the same in the genre chart. Measured by minutes, comedy's weight would be larger and news's smaller, though the report publishes no listening-time figures for podcasts, and the duration data covers Spanish-language shows only.
A panel headed "Listening Spotlight" carries the line "Fresh Episodes. Instant Interest." and states: "News and Comedy content is mainly consumed upon release." No figure accompanies it, such as the share of downloads in the first day or week after an episode is published.
Daryl Battaglia, Senior Vice President of Measurement at Triton Digital, tied the pattern to last year's edition. "Considering last year's insights, Latin America remains eager for news, which continues to be the top genre for both consumers and publishers," he said in the release. "That alignment tells you the market is built around immediacy, and audiences are ready to listen as soon as episodes are released."
Spotify leads, unnamed Android apps close behind
The player chart covers new-episode downloads delivered through each podcast's RSS feed, split by the app or device that requested them.
The 2024 figures come from PPC Land's coverage of the first report. The two periods differ in length and season, and the set of measured networks may have changed, so the comparison is indicative rather than like for like. On that basis, Spotify's share slipped two points while the "Android Unspecified App" category grew from 18% to 29%. The report does not define that label or say which software sits behind it.
Google Chrome, a browser, accounts for 8% of downloads. Amazon's two entries, Alexa and Amazon Music, add up to 5% by PPC Land's count. iHeartRadio, the app of Triton's parent company iHeartMedia, holds 2%. YouTube does not appear in the chart, which counts RSS downloads only; the report does not address video podcasts or listening on platforms that do not request episodes from a host's feed.
Prisa Radio's year

The table is steep. By PPC Land's calculation, the top two networks together average 4.45 million weekly downloads, about twice the 2.21 million of the eight networks below them combined. Prisa Radio's average is roughly 25% above Sonoro Media's and about three times Grupo Globo's. Sonoro Media reached second place on six months of reporting.
Show rankings follow a similar pattern. Sonoro Media's "Relatos de la Noche" was the most downloaded podcast of the period, and Prisa Radio placed two shows in the top 10. The report gives no download figures for individual shows.
Prisa Radio also produced the single most downloaded episode, "La Corneta COMPLETA 6 de Marzo del 2026" from La Corneta, and all three of the top debuts: "6AM W" (News), "Prenderse Fuego: Las Voces de Pedro Lemebel" (Society & Culture) and "SITIO DEL SUCESO" (True Crime). Debuts are ranked "Based on average weekly downloads from the month of ranker debut." For the 2024 edition, PPC Land reported 460,000 downloads for the top episode; the 2026 edition gives no figure for its top episode.

What Sounder found in Spanish-language audio
Sounder's section of the report describes the platform as turning Spanish-language podcasts "into structured, searchable data" with "brand-safe signals". Its job in advertising is classification: deciding what an episode is about so that buyers can apply brand suitability rules and buy on content rather than on listener identity, the model known as contextual advertising. Sounder's segments went live inside The Trade Desk as pre-bid podcast targeting on May 20, 2026, and in July Sounder added four sentiment labels - positive, negative, neutral and mixed - to its content categories. Comscore covers similar ground: in July 2026 it extended transcript-level classification to Spotify, SiriusXM, Triton Digital, Acast and Libsyn.
The report presents Sounder's themes as "based on episode content, not creator-assigned genres." Sports leads at 50%, followed by entertainment at 31%, politics at 24%, family and relationships at 19% and music at 15%. Those five figures add up to 139%, so episodes evidently carry more than one theme; the report does not say whether the percentages refer to episodes, shows or listening. The contrast with the genre charts is still notable. Sports-labelled shows draw 10% of downloads, yet by Sounder's reading sports is a theme in half the content it analysed.
Each theme comes with an example show: "Espacio Deportivo de la Tarde" (Grupo Acir) for sports, "La Corneta" (Prisa Radio) for entertainment, "En Portada, historias que se escuchan" (Multimedios) for politics, "Rocío Córdova" (Grupo Acir) for family and relationships, and "YORDI EN EXA" (MVS Radio) for music.

Streaming: radio on the phone, and on the speaker
The streaming section moves from podcasts to live audio, mostly radio stations streamed online. Mobile devices account for 49% of streaming, desktops and laptops for 24%, smart speakers for 23% and other devices for 4%. The release calls streaming "mobile-first" and says smart speakers are "now nearly even with desktop/laptop listening." The report gives no earlier figure, so the direction of travel behind "now" cannot be checked from the document. Mobile is the largest device family but not a majority: the other three groups together hold 51%.
The footnote says the device figures are based on "total listening hours (TLH) or average active sessions (AAS)" without specifying which. Smart speakers' 23% of streaming contrasts with Alexa's 3% of podcast downloads, which suggests the devices are used far more for live radio than for on-demand podcasts in this data.
The publisher ranking is limited to weekdays, "Monday to Friday, 6 a.m. to 7 p.m." The report defines its main measure in one line: "AAS is calculated by dividing the total listening hours by the number of hours in the specified reporting period." Rank changes are "compared with January - August 2025."
Prisa Radio's average active sessions are 1.8 times those of second-placed Grupo Radio Centro, by PPC Land's calculation. The release describes Prisa's figure as "more than 39 million average session starts per month"; the report's table does not state the time unit for session starts.
