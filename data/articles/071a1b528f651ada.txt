TITLE: GTA 6 PC Port on GitHub Shut Down Following Take-Two DMCA Complaint
OUTLET: PCMag

Unsurprisingly, Rockstar Games’ parent, Take-Two Interactive, is trying to stop unauthorized efforts to port Grand Theft Auto 6 to PCs.
One of the most visible community projects to port the game, dubbed Leonida, abruptly shut down on Monday on the software code-hosting platform GitHub, and it turns out Take-Two played a role. GitHub published a Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) notice from Take-Two, which sought the removal of Leonida. GitHub says it “processed the takedown notice against the entire network of 3,007 repositories” that hosted “forked” copies of Leonida’s code.
The DMCA notice came from Take-Two’s “IP Enforcement Team” and flagged Leonida for copyright infringement. The project was among the growing number of community efforts harnessing the leaked Xbox build of GTA 6 from the CyberLeek hacking gang. On Monday, the group dumped a crucial executable for the Xbox build, sparking a scramble among modders to make GTA 6 runnable on PC.
The notable part is how Take-Two justifies the infringement. The developers of Leonida tried to avoid being held responsible for any piracy by not supplying the leaked Xbox files. Take-Two even noted, “The instruction to ‘bring your own files’ attempts to shift the responsibility for supplying files to the user and to make it seem as if this is a general-purpose project.”
However, the company concluded: “This is intentionally misleading — the timing of the project and its explicit focus on Grand Theft Auto establish that the ‘files’ the user must provide are the unauthorized GTA VI files (Take-Two’s IP) in open distribution, and that the developers of the project are aware of this fact. We adamantly request that GitHub disable the repositories and all associated forks from the platform.”
Take-Two also appears to have filed a DMCA takedown notice against Leonida's Discord chat server, according to the project's admin. “Rockstar taking my server down was only part of the reason. Like I’ve said before, I’m a 15-year-old kid. Managing school and sports is hard enough without having middle-aged men yell and complain to you on Discord. Goodbye,” the admin wrote on why he called it quits.
The DMCA filings also suggest Take-Two could go after the growing number of community efforts to port GTA 6 to PC, which are circulating through other GitHub pages and Discord servers. Some of those efforts have been successful at running the opening scenes of the game, although with degraded graphics and low frame rates.
Take-Two didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment. But there's been growing speculation about whether the company might rush an official PC release of the game, which has been rumored for Q1. In the meantime, we’ve seen chatter from modders suggesting they’ll keep their efforts secret to avoid facing legal penalties.
