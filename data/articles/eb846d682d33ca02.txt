TITLE: 'Not able to celebrate Mass': Catholic priests in Missouri file lawsuit after being denied access to ICE detainees
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Priests in Missouri who have filed a federal lawsuit over hearing confessions from and distributing Communion to U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) detainees have said that they sought accommodations to allow prayer.
At the U.S. Conference of Catholic Bishops’ fall plenary assembly in Baltimore, Most Rev Mitchell T. Rozanski, Archbishop of St. Louis, stated that the Archdiocese of St. Louis made attempts to make provisions after a dozen priests wanted to minister to detained individuals.
The Archdiocese was told that inmates could only be spoken to on the phone, and a lawsuit has subsequently been filed in the Eastern District of Missouri.
The suit claims that Ste. Genevieve County Detention Center has refused to allow sacraments to be presented due to a strict no-contact policy.
“They were not able to hear confessions,” Archbishop Mitchell told OSV News. “They were not able to give them communion. Not able to celebrate Mass. Nothing of the sacraments.
“We’re just asking for a space, as we are given in other incarceration institutions in Missouri prisons. We’re given a space to have Mass. We’re given a space to meet for reconciliation. I myself have been in other prisons where I have been able to hear the sacrament of reconciliation to celebrate Mass, and we’ve had no problems. But it’s just in this facility.
“There was a suggestion made to the priest that they could pass holy Communion in an envelope, and somebody would give it to them on the other side,” Archbishop Mitchell said. “That’s not the way we receive Communion."
Local outlet St. Louis Magazine has reported that a window in a steel door to receive communion has since been installed.
So-called "ICE raids" have been a symbol of President Trump's second term, where the force was often seen wearing masks searching for illegal aliens.
There was concern about the nature of the raids, including incidents when people died when authorities attempted to apprehend individuals.
The family of Renee Good, a US citizen and woman who was shot and killed by ICE agents in Minnesota, has filed wrongful death lawsuits against the US Government and names in the Trump administration.
Witnesses dispute that she attacked officers, which is what the authorities have said. The Associated Press say that at least 10 people have been killed during encounters with immigration agents in Trump's second term.
A spokesperson for the Department of Homeland Security, the US equivalent of the Home Office which oversees ICE, categorically rejected the Archdiocese's claims in a statement to OSV News: “These allegations are FALSE. This is a non-contact county jail, not an ICE facility.
“Detainees are given the opportunity to practice their religions. All religious items are permitted as soon as detainees make the request.
“DHS ensures detainees are accommodated with religious services, including access to Catholic priests, the Sacrament of the Eucharist, and the Sacrament of Penance as requested.
“Let’s be clear: county restrictions on physical contact do not mean religious services are denied. DHS has done its due diligence. The media should do theirs before turning a facility security protocol into another misleading headline.”
