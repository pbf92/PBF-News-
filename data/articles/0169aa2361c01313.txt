TITLE: Anthropic lets enterprises keep Claude logs in their own cloud as prompt theft hits rival hosts
OUTLET: Startup Fortune

Anthropic hasn't stopped storing prompts for its top models. It has moved where they sit. If you sell AI features to banks or insurers, that shift changes how you answer a security questionnaire.
Bloomberg reported on Monday, November 23, that Anthropic is rolling out an early version of what it calls Enterprise Frontier Safeguards. Enterprise customers using its most capable Claude models still have to keep prompts and responses for 30 days, but they can now hold that data in their own cloud accounts instead of Anthropic's. The headline you may have seen, a blanket zero data retention policy, overstates it.
Anthropic attached the 30-day window to its most capable models so the company could spot misuse that stretches across several sessions or accounts. Large customers pushed back, because regulated firms often can't let a vendor keep their prompts at all. Safeguards is Anthropic's answer. According to Anthropic's announcement and coverage from Help Net Security, the monitoring data can sit in a customer's own Amazon S3, Azure Blob or Google Cloud Storage account, under the customer's encryption keys, access rules and audit logs. Anthropic says it built the system with more than 100 enterprise customers and doesn't charge extra for it. Wells Fargo has publicly backed it, saying its logs stay in an environment it manages with keys it controls.
The rollout landed just as Dragos, the industrial-security firm, published results of a test that should make every buyer uncomfortable. According to Dragos's findings as reported by Dataconomy and others, it sent about 2 million bait prompts containing fake credentials through AI inference providers between July and November, then watched where those credentials turned up. Attackers had stolen prompts and credentials at at least three providers, and none of them knew until Dragos told them. Dragos didn't name the three, though Together AI has said an unauthorized party downloaded stored prompt samples after a credential was committed to a public GitHub repository, and that its review is continuing.
Some of the stolen requests had been flagged zero data retention. The flag didn't protect them. OpenRouter, the routing service many startups use, said it removed three providers and pulled 125 endpoints from its zero-retention eligibility list, while saying its own systems weren't breached.

What you should change in your answers
Anthropic's change helps only if your customer is on the enterprise tier and opts in. Customer-owned storage, customer-managed keys and automated review are each switched on separately, and non-enterprise users of the top models still sit under the 30-day rule. Anthropic is phasing the rollout in, so ask your account rep for the written terms before you repeat any of this to a customer.
Frankly, the safest thing a founder can do this week is stop saying 'zero retention' in sales decks unless you can name every hop. If you call Claude directly, say so. If you go through a router or a cheaper host, list it, say what it logs, and say who holds the keys. Regulated buyers will ask after Dragos, and a vague answer now reads worse than a narrow, true one.
Two practical steps follow. Check which model tier your product actually calls, because the 30-day rule attaches to the most capable models and not the older, cheaper ones. And rotate any API keys that ever passed through a third-party host, since Dragos's canary credentials were later used against its test systems after being stolen.
