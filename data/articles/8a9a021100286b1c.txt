TITLE: Schwarzenegger plays Santa in Amazon's Prime ad and its December 2 film
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon today released "Santa Schwarzenegger," the 2026 holiday ad for its Prime membership, in which Arnold Schwarzenegger orders a Santa suit on Amazon, has it "delivered fast with Prime," and goes undercover as Santa Claus on the streets of New York City. The actor plays Santa again in The Man with the Bag, a Prime Video holiday film that premieres globally on December 2, and Amazon's announcement sends readers from the commercial straight to the film. The spot arrives three days before Amazon's Black Friday Week opens on November 19, and 11 days after reports of a separate Amazon holiday film, Joy Delivered.
In Short
Amazon has a new Prime holiday commercial in which Arnold Schwarzenegger buys a Santa suit on Amazon, gets it through Prime delivery and spends the day in New York disguised as Santa. It matters because the same actor plays Santa in Amazon's own film, The Man with the Bag, which reaches Prime Video on December 2, so one spot advertises the membership, its delivery promise and a streaming release at once. It also sits beside Joy Delivered, a separate holiday film Amazon released earlier in November, and Amazon has not said where the Schwarzenegger ad will air, for how long or at what cost.
What the spot shows
The announcement is short. Amazon published it in the Retail section of its Amazon News site under the headline "Watch Arnold Schwarzenegger in Prime's new holiday ad," with a byline from Jacquelyn Smith, a one-minute reading time and three tags: Holiday, Shopping and Entertainment. The video is embedded from YouTube, where it carries the title "Prime | Santa Schwarzenegger" and sits on Amazon's own channel, youtube.com/@amazon.
The page's subheading frames the casting in film terms first: "'The Man with the Bag' star goes undercover as Santa Claus in New York City in Prime's 2026 holiday ad, 'Santa Schwarzenegger.'" Its single descriptive paragraph sets out the plot. "The spot follows Schwarzenegger as he orders a Santa suit on Amazon, delivered fast with Prime," according to Amazon, before he heads into the city, "blending into the holiday crowds, catching a game at a local bar, helping families celebrate, and rediscovering the simple joys of the season."
Schwarzenegger supplies the only quote. "When the Prime team shared this idea, it was the fastest 'yes,'" he said. "I've played a lot of iconic characters, but Santa might be the most iconic."
The words "delivered fast with Prime" are a hyperlink in the post, pointing to Amazon's page on Prime membership benefits. That is the commercial claim inside the story. The spot is not built around a product discount or a sale date; the purchase in it is a costume, and the benefit on show is the speed of delivery that comes with the membership.
The spot in sequence, according to Amazon
Amazon's post describes the ad in six steps, listed here in the order the post gives them:
- Schwarzenegger orders a Santa suit on Amazon.
- The suit is "delivered fast with Prime."
- He goes undercover on the streets of New York City.
- He blends into the holiday crowds and catches a game at a local bar.
- He helps families celebrate.
- He rediscovers "the simple joys of the season."
The post does not say which game is on screen in the bar, how long the ad runs or whether shorter cuts exist.
One actor, two releases
The post closes with a pointer to the film. "You can also watch Schwarzenegger as Santa in Prime Video's upcoming holiday film The Man with the Bag, starring alongside Alan Ritchson and Awkwafina, premiering globally on December 2," it says. Nothing in the text calls the commercial a promotion for the film. The sequence of the page does that work on its own: a Prime ad with a Santa, then a Santa film on a service that comes with Prime.
The film has been on Amazon's calendar for months. At Prime Video's upfront presentation in May, The Man with the Bag was dated for December 2 and described as a holiday family comedy with Schwarzenegger as a version of Santa whose magic bag is stolen. When Amazon published its November programming guide on October 30, the December 2 date still stood as the first December release on Amazon MGM Studios' slate.
Amazon's own page for the film, first published on September 30 and updated on November 2 with the trailer, adds the production details the ad post leaves out. According to Amazon, Adam Shankman directed the film from a script by Allan Rice. The cast list runs to Schwarzenegger, Alan Ritchson, Berkeley James, Michael Cyril Creighton, Liza Koshy, Kyle Mooney, Jane Krakowski, Ken Jeong and Awkwafina. The plot, as Amazon tells it, begins when Santa's red bag is stolen by a rogue elf with a grudge, played by Awkwafina, and Santa recruits a thief from his Naughty List, played by Ritchson, to pull off a heist. Amazon's page also notes that the film arrives 30 years after Schwarzenegger's Jingle All the Way, the 1996 Christmas comedy.
Using one actor in one costume for both a membership ad and a film release is a form of cross-promotion that few advertisers can arrange internally, because few advertisers own a film studio. Disney did something comparable a week ago. On November 9 it launched "Make the Holidays Magic," a single holiday campaign covering its parks, streaming services, networks and consumer products, opened by a short film directed by Dougal Wilson. Both companies are using their seasonal advertising to carry their own entertainment, with the difference that Amazon's spot also sells a shipping promise.
Two holiday films in one month
Santa Schwarzenegger is not Amazon's only holiday film this year. On November 5, the marketing news site DesignRush reported the release of Joy Delivered, a film in which a woman named Joy, played by Kate Laidler, has her Christmas Eve flight home cancelled by severe weather and hands out the Amazon packages meant for her family to the strangers who help her on the way. A ContentGrip report from November 9 adds that the film was developed by Amazon's in-house creative team, directed by Mark Molloy through the production company Smuggler, shot in New Zealand and set to a bespoke rendition of The Beatles' Golden Slumbers. According to ContentGrip, Amazon is distributing 30-, 60- and 90-second versions across television, video on demand, cinema, online video and social channels through December.
DesignRush describes Joy Delivered as Amazon's first new holiday hero film since 2024. Its account of the earlier years is set out below. Amazon's post does not refer to any of them.
The two 2026 films run on different engines. Joy Delivered relies on a stranded traveller played by Kate Laidler, a long journey home and a reworked Beatles song. Santa Schwarzenegger relies on a celebrity whose name is in the title, a New York setting and a membership benefit spelled out in the copy. Amazon's post calls the Schwarzenegger spot "Amazon's 2026 holiday ad" in its first sentence and "Prime's 2026 holiday ad" in its subheading, and it does not mention Joy Delivered. Whether the company treats the two as one campaign, or as a brand film and a separate Prime film, is not stated.
Neither film appears among the 12 ads in System1's early UK Christmas ranking, which PPC Land reported on November 12. That list was led by Morrisons and Sainsbury's at 5.9 Stars, with supermarkets holding seven places, and Disney's The Invisible Horse ranked sixth. The Schwarzenegger spot had not yet been released when the ranking was compiled, and System1 did not say whether Joy Delivered had been tested.
The commercial calendar around the ad
The post places the ad inside Amazon's sales schedule. Between the description and the quote, it embeds a module headed "Black Friday and Cyber Monday 2026: a first look at Amazon's best deals," which promises "Millions of deals across electronics, beauty, fashion, toys, and more" from November 19 through November 30, "with new daily themed deals and double drops on Black Friday and Cyber Monday." Its final line sends readers to "Amazon's 2026 Black Friday Week and Cyber Monday deal events."
Those dates were set a week ago. On November 10, Amazon announced that Black Friday Week runs from November 19 to November 30 with 12 days of deal drops, and that deals would drop twice, at midnight and at 8 a.m. Pacific time, on both Black Friday and Cyber Monday. Prime Video will carry four live games on Black Friday within a November schedule of 23 live events.
Amazon is both the advertiser here and one of the largest sellers of advertising in the market. Its retail media network and streaming inventory produced advertising revenue of $19.64 billion in the third quarter, up 11%, and the company told investors it expects growth "in the high teens" in the fourth quarter, the quarter that contains this ad and Black Friday Week. On Prime Video, Amazon reported in February an average ad-supported audience of 315 million viewers globally, up from 200 million disclosed in April 2024. Brands that buy that connected TV inventory during the holidays now share the season with a company promoting its own membership, its own film and its own sale.
The post's "Trending news and stories" module rounds out the picture. It lists four Amazon stories: Ring Robotics, a Black Friday FAQ, the partnership that gives Prime members $0 Uber Eats delivery on orders from $25, and the Amazon editors' best books of 2026. Each is a Prime benefit or a store promotion. The ad page, in other words, is itself a small piece of holiday merchandising.
Why this matters for marketers
The arrangement is efficient for Amazon in a way most brands cannot copy. A single shoot produces a membership ad that also works as a reminder for a December release on a streaming service bundled with that membership. The post does not say whether the ad was produced alongside the film, who paid for it or how its cost was split.
For agencies and brands, the more practical question is placement. Amazon has not said whether the Schwarzenegger spot will run in Prime Video ad breaks, on Thursday Night Football or NBA broadcasts, on linear television or only online. If it does run on Prime Video, Amazon would be filling some of its own premium holiday inventory with its own promotion during the quarter it expects ad growth to accelerate, but that is a question the post does not answer. Is the ad a paid media buy, or a piece of owned content that lives mainly on YouTube and Amazon's news site? Readers cannot tell from the release.
There is also the contrast between the two films. Amazon reran Joy Ride in 2025, according to DesignRush, then produced a new hero film for 2026 and, within a fortnight, added a celebrity spot for Prime. Two films in one month, one built on a journey story and one on a star, give Amazon two different routes to the same season. How each is measured, and which one Amazon credits for holiday sign-ups or sales, is not addressed in anything Amazon has published.
What the post does not say
The post gives no running time, agency, director or production company for Santa Schwarzenegger, no list of markets or channels, no air dates beyond publication, and no budget. It does not identify the game shown in the bar, mention Alexa for Shopping or Prime's price, or explain how the spot relates to Joy Delivered. The details about Joy Delivered come from two trade publications, not from Amazon.
