TITLE: First images from Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope to premiere at 2026 AGU Annual Meeting in San Francisco
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

WASHINGTON — NASA will release the first images from the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope on Monday, 7 December at 12:30 a.m. – 1:15 p.m. PST at the AGU 2026 Annual Meeting in San Francisco, California, during an extended plenary featuring a fireside conversation with NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman.
Moderated by science journalist Alex Witze, this special conversation will explore the powerful connections between Earth and space science, the discoveries shaping our understanding of the universe, and the role NASA science plays in advancing what's possible. The event will be streamed live to the public on the meeting webpage and NASA platforms starting at 11:45 a.m. PST.
Mission scientists will take questions from in-person and remote reporters at a 9:00 a.m. PST press briefing. The press briefing and images will be embargoed until the public release during the plenary session, at 12:30 p.m. PST. The briefing will support participation by registered remote reporters via a limited livestream. Additional information for press will be released in the week prior to the meeting.
The new telescope, which launched on 30 August 2026, aims to settle essential questions in the areas of dark energy, exoplanets, and astrophysics. Roman's massive field of view and sharp infrared vision will conduct sweeping surveys of the universe, revealing tens of thousands of new exoplanets and billions of galaxies.
More than 24,000 scientists are expected to attend the 2026 Annual Meeting, presenting on the latest research news from Earth’s core to distant planets, Earth’s climate past and future, oceans, Arctic change, wildfires, wild weather, earthquakes and much more. News briefings
In addition to the exciting news from NASA, AGU is thrilled to once again host the release of the 2026 Arctic Report Card. Authors and editors will present highlights from the report at a news briefing on Tuesday, 8 December. Other briefing topics include the impacts of the intense 2026 El Niño and implications for future events in a warming world; the latest results from the Curiosity and Perseverance rovers and upcoming Mars exploration missions; and the human health costs of greenhouse gas emissions.
The full press event schedule will be available on the conference Press Center on 1 December.

Eligible press should complete registration through the online portal before arrival; walk-in press registration will not be available on site in San Francisco.
Press officers and institutional writers covering the meeting are also eligible for press registration. Additional information for press can be found in the conference Press Center. Learn about our eligibility requirements.
Media registrants receive, at no charge, access to all scientific sessions and an on-site press room, along with press events. Some events and activities, including but not limited to invitation-only events and communications workshops, are not open to press registrants.
Media access to the meeting is issued at the discretion of the AGU Media Relations Office and is non-transferable. All registrants must provide credentials regardless of whether they have attended in the past.

AGU (www.agu.org) is a global community supporting more than half a million professionals and advocates in Earth and space sciences. Through broad and inclusive partnerships, AGU aims to advance discovery and solution science that accelerate knowledge and create solutions that are ethical, unbiased and respectful of communities and their values. Our programs include serving as a scholarly publisher, convening virtual and in-person events and providing career support. We live our values in everything we do, such as our net zero energy renovated building in Washington, D.C. and our Ethics and Equity Center, which fosters a diverse and inclusive geoscience community to ensure responsible conduct.
