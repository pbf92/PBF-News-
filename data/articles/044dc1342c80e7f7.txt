TITLE: Grand Theft Auto VI Was Worth the Wait
OUTLET: Vulture

It’s been over a decade since Rockstar Games released the last Grand Theft Auto game. Prior to that, fans never had to wait more four years for a new game to play, so the release comes with enormous, generational amounts of hype. Is it even possible for a new game to live up to that level of expectations? It turns out: Yes. Reviews for the new game are almost unbelievably euphoric, highlighting the level of detail, how the play style can either be incredibly mundane or outlandish, and how the love story between criminals Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos is, per The Washington Post, an “astonishing American epic.”
“This game is about Jason and Lucia, the fugitive couple through whom you experience all that Leonida has to offer. Jason is muscular, handsome, ex-military, steady and unflappable, sincere but spare with words. Lucia has been boosting cars since the age of 14, has a wicked sense of humour and takes zero bullshit. (Lucia is the scarier one; I found myself turning much more quickly to violence when playing as her.)… It took me 70 hours to finish the game and I could have taken longer.” — Keza MacDonald, The Guardian
“I’ve now completely lost track of all the things I’ve seen and done within GTA 6’s astounding open world – and I see no real limit to the amount of time I could spend here, endlessly exploring and interacting with Vice City and the massive area surrounding it. While the well-paced story effectively nudged me around the map, there are still vast sections of it that I’m yet to even visit — despite hitting the credits after almost 120 hours of hijinks. Frankly, I’ve never played anything that’s hit quite like GTA 6 — and right now I have zero plans to stop playing it.” — Luke Reilly, IGN
“Several hours into Grand Theft Auto VI, I watched a sedan get smashed from behind by a truck. I had been driving around Vice City, a fictionalized re-creation of Miami with such lifelike graphical fidelity that it can be hard to distinguish the video game from real-life footage, and I pulled to a stop when I saw the two vehicles collide. The truck driver scampered off, leaving the victim trapped in his seat as his engine began to catch fire. I ran over and helped him out of the car. He thanked me, reflecting in astonishment that he’d almost died. Then he noticed that the culprit was a delivery truck for Sahara, the Grand Theft Auto parody of Amazon. This was his big break, he declared. He could sue Sahara for millions.” — Jason Schreier, Bloomberg
“In All About Love, bell hooks argues that ‘we would all love better if we used it as a verb’ instead of a noun. In our loving relationships, she writes, the ‘choice must be made to nurture growth.’ This idea finds shape in the romantic interactivity of Grand Theft Auto VI, where players must actively choose closeness and intimacy.” — Yussef Cole, The New York Times
“I treated Vice City like any other exciting new place I’ve visited, and my wanderlust was similarly rewarded. I could fill pages and pages just by recounting all the little moments that emerged whenever I decided to head off in a random direction with only a vague plan. By default, the minimal UI isn’t littered with distracting icons. Instead, you can choose when to reveal any potential points of interest nearby. The story could wait; my own curiosity gave me all the motivation I needed to simply keep putting myself in new scenarios.” — Jordan Minor, PC Mag
“What differentiates each GTA game from one another and prevents a feeling of staleness in the series is the world around you in each one, how you interact with it, and how it reacts to you. What makes GTA 6’s Leonida distinct is the feeling of independence it instills. The appeal of GTA, and the fantasy it facilitates, has traditionally been in creating chaos to agitate a world that reacts to your every move–be it through police trying to apprehend you with escalating levels of force, or random people fighting or fleeing in response to your behavior. It’s an experience that is centered around your character and you as the player, so, naturally, all the systems the series’ cities have operated on have had their focus trained on you.” — Tamoor Hussein, Gamespot
