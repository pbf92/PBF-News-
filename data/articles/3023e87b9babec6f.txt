TITLE: Combined and Mixed Plyometric Training Boost Vertical Jump in Volleyball Players
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Conducted by researchers Yazhen Zhang, Yaochuan Chang, and Ruibin Deng, the investigation pooled evidence from randomized controlled trials to assess the relative efficacy of varied jump-training protocols. The analysis demonstrated that combined plyometric training, mixed plyometric regimens, and loaded plyometric protocols yielded statistically significant improvements in vertical jump performance when compared against control conditions.
The authors emphasized that vertical jump performance serves as a vital physical attribute in volleyball, where core match activities such as spiking, blocking, and jump serving depend heavily on repeated, high-force take-off actions. The objective was to systematically review randomized evidence and compare the effects of different plyometric training modalities on vertical jump performance in volleyball players using a network meta-analysis.
To compile the relevant randomized evidence, the researchers executed comprehensive systematic literature searches across four major electronic academic databases: PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and the Cochrane Library. The search window spanned from database inception through June 2026, without restricting the initial trial publication years. Controlled trials were judged eligible for final inclusion only if they enrolled volleyball players, evaluated either a standalone plyometric training protocol or a plyometric-based combined program, and explicitly measured and reported post-intervention vertical jump outcomes.
Within the systematic review framework, the evaluated interventions were grouped as Control, Mixed plyometric training, loaded plyometric training, Aquatic plyometric training, or combined plyometric training. For each qualifying study, the authors extracted post-intervention means, standard deviations, and arm-specific sample sizes. Trials that only featured within-node comparisons between identical training types or lacked usable post-intervention arm-level data were retained for narrative evaluation within the broader systematic review but were formally excluded from the quantitative network meta-analysis.
In total, thirty-two randomized controlled trials encompassing 1,147 volleyball players were included in the systematic review, which was registered in PROSPERO under registration code CRD420261423606. From this overarching synthesis, twenty-eight trials provided sufficiently complete and usable between-node post-intervention arm-level datasets to enter the primary network meta-analysis. This primary quantitative network synthesis comprised twenty-nine separate study strata and integrated data from 1,066 individual volleyball participants pooled through a random-effects model calculating standardized mean differences.
The quantitative synthesis revealed that mixed plyometric training yielded a substantial improvement over control conditions, generating a pooled standardized mean difference of 1.83 with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning from 0.94 to 2.71.
In contrast to land-based modalities, aquatic plyometric training demonstrated an overall positive but statistically imprecise effect relative to control conditions, exhibiting a standardized mean difference of 1.16 with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from -0.86 to 3.17. When the researchers evaluated relative intervention probabilities using the surface under the cumulative ranking curve method, combined plyometric training achieved the highest numerical SUCRA ranking across all analyzed training modalities. However, the study authors emphasized that this top cumulative ranking does not demonstrate statistically significant superiority over other active training strategies.
Ultimately, the researchers concluded that while mixed, loaded, and combined plyometrics reliably improve jumping metrics compared to control settings, practical interpretations regarding comparative superiority must remain measured. Direct head-to-head comparisons between active modalities were relatively sparse across the published sports science literature, necessitating that athletic practitioners interpret the relative hierarchy of these training approaches with appropriate caution. The research was accepted for publication following peer review in late October 2026, contributing structured comparative evidence to the Elite Sports and Performance Enhancement section.
