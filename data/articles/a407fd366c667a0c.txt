SA Asks: What's the best gold play right now for investors?
What's the best gold play right now for investors?
Seeking Alpha analysts Andrew Hecht, Brett Aschcroft Green, and Eugenio Catone weigh in.
Andrew Hecht: Gold is ugly, along with other markets that appear to be undergoing a risk-off phase. Picking bottoms is dangerous, as prices can fall to unreasonable, illogical, and irrational levels before finding bottoms.
All the analysts who were forecasting prices at $6,000 earlier this year again have egg on their face. Remember, they were the same analysts who never forecast gold reaching the level it rose to in late January 2026.
While I still believe gold will find a higher low, the next long-term support is the psychological $3,000 level. However, technical support for the bull market that began in 1999 is at the December 2023 high of $2,130.20 per ounce, more than $1,385 below last Friday's settlement price on Nov. 20. Central banks will continue to add to reserves at lower prices. That said, sentiment has taken a significant hit as gold has fallen by more than $2,000 per ounce from the Jan. 29, 2026, record high.
As a long-term investor, I would add to long positions by buying physical gold or SPDR Gold Shares ETF GLD, the most liquid gold ETF that holds physical gold bullion. In late November 2026, gold and all precious metals remain falling knives, so be careful.
(Andrew Hecht leads the SA investing group The Hecht Commodity Report.)
Brett Aschcroft Green: The recent sell-off in gold could create an opportunity, as the metal may eventually represent a flight-to-safety asset and could be boosted by rate cuts if inflation subsides. I recently covered a few metals funds, which included a physical gold ETF, the SPDR Gold Shares ETF GLD; a large-cap miner fund, the VanEck Gold Miners ETF GDX; and a junior miner fund, the VanEck Junior Gold Miners ETF GDXJ.
In the event of a sell-off, I’d be more inclined to go with the actual metals rather than the mining stocks.
Eugenio Catone: As I expressed in a recent article, the AI sell-off has led investors to sell liquid assets like gold to face margin calls. Since leverage is still high in the financial system, I expect a further drop in gold prices, at least until $3,400 per ounce, which is where the next strong volumetric support lies.
So, in my opinion, the best play here is to just wait until the dust settles ($3,400 per ounce) and buy a liquid ETC on gold for the long term. In this period, I would avoid miners as they are subject to operating leverage, something that becomes a major headwind when gold is in a sharp downward trend.
