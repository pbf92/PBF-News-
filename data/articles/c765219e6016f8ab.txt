TITLE: Rockstar explains GTA 6 driving mechanics as Jason's car leaves gamers in literal circles
OUTLET: GAMINGbible

GTA 6 has drastically changed the driving mechanics for its new open-world and players like myself are finding these alterations incredibly jarring.
Rockstar Games made these changes for a reason though, and the studio’s co-lead Rob Nelson explained to GAMINGbible as we discussed the new vehicle physics after playing GTA 6 for an initial 70 hours.
For those yet to play the highest-rated game of the year, cars, bikes, lorries, boats and everything else now have actual weight to them and respond like real vehicles.
Jason’s Chapter 1 truck for example threw everyone off with its dodgy handling, and combined with realistic crumple physics you’ll have probably needed to repair it several times in your first 10 hours with the game.

Vehicles also become unusable much faster, and even need refuelling at gas stations. Luckily when personal vehicles break down you can repair and summon them with the Scooter Bros app from Chapter 2 onwards. For a fee of course.
All of this is brand-new for a single-player Grand Theft Auto game and when asked about the changes Rob Nelson said it was “nerve-wracking” to implement, adding: “Some people love the idea, some people are going to hate it.”
However, Nelson made it clear that these changes haven’t been introduced “to annoy people or just because we're freaks”, they’re there because you asked for them.

Nelson explained: “People want a denser world, part of that means narrower roads because the roads being so wide in GTA V makes the world feel less believable, so when we made the decision to make [GTA VI’s] roads a slightly more believable size, all of a sudden the world feels better, it’s denser.”
He added that players also “want more density of traffic, so as you get in the city suddenly there's a traffic jam. Then you want better deformation on the cars..."
Most of the changes to driving and car physics in GTA VI come directly from the community’s demands and discussions over the years, but with a lot of fine-tuning under the hood. Nelson said in earlier builds the vehicle damage would total your car in one or two hits depending on the speed, so balances needed to be made.
The core idea at the centre of all these alterations however was choice. Nelson told me that the end goal isn’t 'decision-making fatigue' but just making you more aware of your choices and actions in the game.
Driving recklessly can be good fun, but if it’s a vehicle you’re keen on you’ll be more likely to repair it at gas stations or with the Scooter Bros app. Repairing damage costs money though; money you could be saving in your BuckMe account instead.
This will either encourage you to become a better driver or manage your fuel usage, or see you throw caution to wind and tear though Vice City like you own the place.
GTA VI is more realistic than any previous Rockstar game, but as explained in the GAMINGbible GTA 6 review the realism isn’t there for the sake of it. Everything is there for a reason, and it all leads back to the main theme of the game - you reap what you sow.
