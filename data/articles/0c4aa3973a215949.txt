TITLE: Primary care clinicians offering a broader range of services deliver better care
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Primary Care Clinicians Offering a Broader Range of Services Deliver Better Care
Meeting most of a patient's health needs is a core feature of primary care. However, no established quality measure captures it. Researchers built a clinician-level digital measure and applied it to 2018-2019 health record data on 2,117 clinicians in more than 1,000 practices.
Researchers found:
- Primary care clinicians’ average comprehensiveness score was 0.51 on a scale from zero to one, with values closer to one signaling more comprehensive care.
- Higher comprehensiveness scores were linked with better performance on several quality measures. A 10-point increase in the comprehensiveness score was associated with 4.2% to 5.2% higher cancer screening rates, a 14.3% higher depression screening rate, and an 11.8% lower percentage of diabetic patients with uncontrolled diabetes.
Why It Matters: Having a digital quality measure of comprehensiveness that is calculated from existing health record data could reduce the need for clinicians to report many separate disease-specific measures.
Development of a Clinician-Level Measure of Comprehensiveness and Its Associations With Primary Care Quality
