TITLE: Anthropic IPO Nears: Claude Maker to Debut on Nasdaq Nov. 19
OUTLET: KuCoin

TradingKey - Anthropic is expected to price its initial public offering on November 18 and begin trading on Nasdaq on November 19 under the ticker symbol "ANT". Based on an offering range of $70 to $78 per share, the company's fully diluted valuation could reach approximately $1.3 trillion to $1.4 trillion.
This IPO is drawing attention not just for its massive offering size. Anthropic is one of the few companies globally capable of continuously developing frontier large models and is the most direct competitor to OpenAI. Its debut performance will, for the first time, allow the public market to value a pure-play frontier AI lab, and could influence subsequent fundraising for OpenAI and other AI companies.
Unlike the previous AI rally underpinned by chips and data centers, Anthropic offers investors model capabilities, enterprise software revenue, and future AI platform value. However, its rapid growth is likewise built upon massive computing power investments. Whether ANT stock will gain market recognition ultimately depends on whether investors believe the revenue generated by Claude is sufficient to cover long-term infrastructure costs in the future.
Anthropic IPO Pricing and Fundraising Size
According to Anthropic's latest amended Form S-1 filed on November 10, the company plans to offer a total of 825 million shares of Class A common stock, with 801.35 million shares newly issued by Anthropic and an additional 23.649 million shares sold by existing shareholders. The offering price is tentatively set at $70 to $78 per share, and the shares will trade on Nasdaq under the ticker symbol "ANT".
Based on an estimated 13.09 billion shares outstanding following the completion of the IPO, Anthropic's implied market capitalization would be approximately $917 billion to $1.02 trillion. When further factoring in employee stock options, restricted stock, convertible instruments, charitable donation matching shares, and other potential dilutive items, its fully diluted valuation could rise to approximately $1.3 trillion to $1.4 trillion.
At the upper limit of the offering price range of $78, the total size of this public offering would reach approximately $64.35 billion, positioning it to rank among the largest IPOs in history. Notably, not all proceeds from the public offering will go to the company; funds raised from newly issued shares sold by Anthropic will belong to the company, while the proceeds from shares sold by existing shareholders will be received by those selling shareholders.
In addition, Anthropic has arranged a $26 billion private placement separate from the public offering. Participants include Nvidia (NVDA), Google (GOOGL), AMD (AMD), Broadcom (AVGO), Micron (MU), Microsoft (MSFT), and TSMC (TSM).
Among them, Google and Nvidia will each subscribe for approximately $10 billion, AMD for $2.5 billion, Broadcom, Micron, and Microsoft for $1 billion each, and TSMC for $500 million. These funds will enter Anthropic through a separate class of Class N shares rather than being included in the Class A shares offered to the public. Based on the offering price midpoint of $74, Anthropic expects to raise approximately $84.6 billion in net proceeds through the newly issued shares in the public offering and the concurrent private placement; if the underwriters exercise their over-allotment option in full, estimated net proceeds could increase to approximately $93.7 billion.
The market has shown strong interest in Anthropic's IPO, with subscription orders received by Anthropic reaching approximately two times the shares offered by the end of the first day of its roadshow, and rising further to about six times by the fourth day; by comparison, SpaceX's IPO only reached about two times subscribed on the second day of its roadshow.
Anthropic’s Growth Quality
The core rationale supporting Anthropic's high valuation is the rapid acceleration in the commercialization of its Claude products.
According to the latest prospectus, for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, Anthropic generated $33.242 billion in revenue, compared with just $2.545 billion in the same period last year, up approximately 1,206% year-over-year. Of this total, usage-based revenue accounted for about 79%, while subscription revenue made up roughly 21%. As of the end of September, the number of customers generating more than $100,000 in trailing 12-month revenue had increased from over 950 a year earlier to approximately 11,900, reflecting Claude's expanding penetration from an AI developer tool into the broader enterprise market.
This indicates that Anthropic's growth is no longer solely reliant on model releases and tech hype, but has begun to materialize through increased enterprise customer usage and expanded commercial contracts. Particularly across scenarios such as coding, knowledge work, and enterprise software, Claude has become part of an increasing number of corporate workflows. As customer scale broadens, the usage-based pricing model also has the potential to further drive revenue growth.
However, rapid growth has not entirely eliminated profitability challenges. In the first nine months of 2026, Anthropic's GAAP operating loss was approximately $1.26 billion, significantly lower than the $5.224 billion loss in the prior-year period. Meanwhile, the company reported adjusted operating profit of $4.1 billion, achieving a marked improvement from an adjusted operating loss of $3.1 billion a year earlier.
Notably, operating performance experienced an interim fluctuation in the third quarter, with Anthropic posting an operating loss of about $567 million for the period, compared with an operating profit of $974 million in the second quarter.
The company explained that this change was primarily related to increased short-term compute procurement costs and non-cash expenses. In the third quarter, it incurred approximately $5 billion in new short-term compute spending, which carried significantly higher costs than its previously signed multi-year contracts; in addition, the company recognized roughly $2 billion in non-cash expenses related to a donation-matching program.
From an adjusted perspective, Anthropic's third-quarter adjusted operating profit still rose to $2.5 billion from $2.2 billion in the second quarter, but its adjusted operating margin fell from 19% to 15%. This indicates that while the company's revenue scale and adjusted profitability continue to improve, compute procurement prices and the pacing of infrastructure investments affect quarterly profit performance.
Focus on Anthropic's Massive Compute Commitment
Anthropic requires substantial capital because the development of frontier models relies heavily on chips and data centers. The company's signed commitments for computing resources, infrastructure, and leases total approximately $900.9 billion, of which about $451.6 billion is expected to occur before the end of 2030, with roughly $70.7 billion due over the next 12 months.
Locking in computing power in advance helps prevent Anthropic from having its product development constrained again by shortages of chip and data center capacity. The company currently has access to AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, Nvidia GPUs, and AMD GPUs, and is also attempting to reduce its reliance on any single vendor through multiple chip architectures.
However, these contracts also saddle Anthropic with massive long-term fixed costs, with some agreements requiring the company to pay regardless of whether the resources are fully utilized. If enterprise adoption of Claude is slower than expected, or if more efficient models reduce computing power requirements, reserved capacity may fail to translate into corresponding revenue.
Price competition is also a risk that cannot be ignored. OpenAI, Google, Meta, and other model developers are continuously driving down inference costs, while China and the open-source community continue to introduce lower-priced models. As the gap in model capabilities narrows, customers may use multiple vendors simultaneously and flexibly shift workloads based on price.
This means Anthropic must prove not only that Claude delivers advanced performance, but also that enterprise customers are willing to pay sustainably, and that every dollar invested in compute generates sufficient revenue. A rise in short-term computing costs in the third quarter has already driven its adjusted operating margin down from 19% to 15%, demonstrating that profitability remains highly sensitive to compute resource pricing.
The market environment for ANT's listing is also more cautious than before. Investors have begun focusing on AI data center spending, debt financing, energy consumption, and commercial returns, while retail enthusiasm for purchasing tech stocks has also waned. Anthropic plans to allocate up to 20% of its IPO shares to individual investors, hoping to stabilize demand through broader participation, but whether its appeal can match the level of SpaceX's listing remains to be seen.
