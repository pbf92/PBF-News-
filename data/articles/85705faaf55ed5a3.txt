TITLE: Network Meta-Analysis Evaluates Interventions for Reversing Cancer Lean Body Mass Loss
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and network meta-analysis evaluated the comparative effectiveness of multiple clinical approaches aimed at countering the severe loss of lean body mass in patients diagnosed with cancer. Severe depletion of lean body mass is recognized as an indicator of poor prognosis among cancer cohorts, occurring notably when the rate of systemic protein breakdown exceeds that of protein synthesis. Although a spectrum of nutritional, pharmacological, and physical therapies has been tested across clinical settings, relative efficacy among these different strategies has remained unclear.
To assess the evidence base, investigators conducted a systematic search across PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library from the inception of each database through December 19, 2024. Eligible English-language investigations were restricted to randomized controlled trials examining interventions designed to reverse lean tissue depletion among cancer patients, requiring a minimum cohort size of 40 participants. The study protocol was registered prospectively in the PROSPERO international database under registration number CRD42024518277, and methodological quality was evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool.
The synthesis ultimately incorporated 26 randomized controlled trials comprising a cumulative total of 4,487 cancer patients, including 11 trials drawn from an earlier systematic review. Across the pooled trial database, researchers classified six distinct intervention categories: nutrition alone in 13 studies, drug treatments alone in 12 trials, exercise interventions across 7 studies, combined drug and nutrition regimens in 5 trials, diet alone in 2 trials, and combined exercise and nutrition therapy evaluated in a single trial.
Standard pairwise meta-analytic comparisons demonstrated that interventions combining exercise with nutrition, as well as regimens integrating pharmacotherapy with nutritional support, produced greater efficacy than control conditions in reversing lean body mass decline. When evaluating changes in overall body weight, pairwise analyses showed measurable effectiveness across multiple intervention formats, including diet alone, nutritional therapy, pharmacological interventions, and combined exercise and nutrition protocols.
Network meta-analytic modeling identified distinct probability rankings depending upon the assessment methodology used to monitor body composition. When lean body mass loss was tracked via dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA), combined drug and nutrition interventions achieved the highest probability of being the most effective therapy, displaying a surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) value of 100. Conversely, when body composition was tracked using bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA), single-drug interventions demonstrated the top ranking, with a SUCRA value of 87.3.
Regarding overall body mass management, network modeling indicated that exercise alone yielded the highest probability of increasing body weight among cancer patients, reaching a SUCRA value of 94.7. In addition to primary ranking metrics, investigators assessed potential dissemination distortion across all trial endpoints using funnel plot inspection alongside Egger’s test, which confirmed the absence of publication bias.
The authors concluded that while combined drug and nutrition strategies appear advantageous under DEXA evaluation and single-agent drugs lead under BIA testing, these findings represent exploratory observations shaped by underlying methodological heterogeneity across the included trials. The research team emphasized that the comparative rankings should not be interpreted as definitive clinical recommendations, noting also that body weight possesses inherent limitations and should serve only as a secondary reference indicator when monitoring lean mass recovery in oncology populations.
