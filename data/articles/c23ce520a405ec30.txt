OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

DWP change could stop PIP payments for nearly 500,000 people
A new government-commissioned review on youth unemployment has recommended sweeping changes
Nearly half a million people in England and Wales could lose personal independence payments (PIP) if a proposed review of benefits gets the go-ahead.
Former Labour minister Alan Milburn has proposed that 16 to 24-year-olds should stop receiving PIP and the health element of Universal Credit (UC), as part of his much-anticipated government-commissioned review on youth unemployment.
The report, published on Tuesday, November 17, has instead called for a new Youth Allowance for under-25s.
PIP, which is paid out by the Department of Work and Pensions, is the main benefit paid to someone who has a long-term physical or mental health condition or disability.
The money is intended to help with everyday tasks and extra living costs.

The benefit is not a single flat rate but is instead divided into two parts based on how a claimant’s condition affects their independence.
The daily living component covers the extra costs of managing everyday tasks. The standard rate is set at £76.55 per week, while the enhanced rate provides £114.80.
The mobility component addresses the challenges of getting around. The base rate for this component is £30.20, with an increased level of £80.10.
By qualifying for the enhanced daily living rate and a portion of the mobility support, eligible claimants can see their weekly total reach the £187 mark - totalling £748 per month.
According to the latest DWP figures, a total of 453,495 16 to 24-year-olds in England and Wales were entitled to receive PIP as of July 31 2026. It means that if Mr Milburn's recommendations are adopted, they would lose out on PIP payments.
Four in five of the 453,495 claimants who were under-25 in July 2026 had psychiatric disorders listed as their main disability (362,377 or 80%).
The number of young claimants is more than double the number when current data began in January 2019, which was 195,899.
The health element of UC is an additional payment on top of the standard UC allowance and is for people who have limited capability for work and work-related activity.
A total of 257,987 16 to 24-year-olds in Britain were claiming UC health payments as of June 2026, DWP figures show.
Many of these people are also likely to be receiving PIP, as both benefits can be claimed at the same time.
Mr Milburn's report stated that the new single Youth Allowance would be set at the standard rate of Universal Credit for claimants under the age of 25, with a higher rate for those living independently. The changes would see the health top up and the work capability assessment for Universal Credit abolished.
The Youth Allowance would also provide guaranteed support and opportunities to participate in learning or earning, the report said.
To replace PIP for young people, a new Additional Cost Allowance would be introduced. This would provide "targeted financial support where disability-related extra costs are well established, significant, and ongoing", the report stated. Awards would be made through a national, healthcare-professional-led assessment, the report added.

At a press conference on Tuesday, Mr Milburn said: "A lost generation is not a fact of life. It is a choice. And I believe it is the wrong one.
“Not just because it is a fiscal crisis or an economic crisis. But because it is a moral one.
“We are failing as a nation to gift the next generation the opportunities they need to succeed. It is time for a new settlement that makes work not welfare the future for the next generation.
“Demographic change makes getting young people into work a necessity. Britain’s future relies on us backing young people.”
Prime Minister Andy Burnham has already said the “status quo clearly isn’t working” and that his Government is “ready to to grasp the nettle here and make a better system for young people”.
