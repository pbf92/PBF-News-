TITLE: AdTonos drops its name after 10 years to chase in-car ads as Turms
OUTLET: PPC Land

AdTonos, a London-based programmatic audio advertising technology company, today changed its name to Turms and set out a strategy it calls Movement Media, which is meant to carry its technology from streaming audio into connected cars, digital billboards, retail media and connected TV. The announcement, datelined London and dated November 17, 2026, marks the company's tenth anniversary.
At the centre of the plan sits Movement Media AI, or MMAI, which Turms describes as "agentic media-buying technology in development". According to the company, MMAI is designed to weigh campaign objectives, permitted contextual signals, available inventory and performance feedback to decide whether an ad should appear at all and, if so, when, where and on which device. Connected vehicles are an early priority. Turms says it wants to create new revenue opportunities for car manufacturers "throughout the life of their vehicles, including those already on the road".
The release names six people in automotive, sales, product, supply and marketing roles, among them a former Ford executive. It gives no revenue, client count, funding, launch date, price or automaker partner.
In Short
AdTonos, a company that has sold ads in streaming audio for ten years, has renamed itself Turms and wants to place ads in cars, on digital screens outdoors, in shops and on connected TVs as well. The plan matters to carmakers, publishers and advertisers because Turms says its AI buying system will decide from the context of the moment, not from personal profiles, whether an ad should run and where. Nothing under the new strategy is on sale yet: the AI system is still in development, and no carmaker, market or launch date has been named.
A new name on a ten-year-old audio business
The rename does not close the old business. According to the release, "Audio remains a foundational part of Turms' business, technology, and expertise," and as the company "enters its second decade, the company will continue supporting its existing audio advertising business and partner relationships while developing the capabilities and partnerships needed to bring its broader Movement Media vision to market."
The Turms website, live at turms.com, introduces the company as previously known as AdTonos and states in its footer that Turms is a brand of Radio Net Media Ltd. The site styles the name in lower case; the release capitalises it. A post on the rebrand on the website carries the date November 16, 2026, one day before the date on the release. The old AdTonos site was still online today, without any mention of the new name. Its metadata shows a last modification on June 15, 2026, and it describes AdTonos as a digital audio advertising marketplace with 112.2 million unique listeners a week and 3.9 billion available playouts a month. Those are the company's own figures. The rebrand release does not repeat them, and it offers no other measure of the business's size.
Michael Marcinik, Founder and CEO of Turms, framed the change as a response to how audiences now consume media. "For ten years, we've shaped the evolution of programmatic audio advertising. But the way people experience media has changed dramatically, and the technology connecting brands with audiences needs to evolve alongside it," he said, according to the release. "Turms represents our ambition to build on what we've established in audio and create something broader: an intelligent infrastructure that understands context as people move through their day."
Movement Media, setting by setting
Turms defines Movement Media as its approach to "connecting advertising across the different channels people encounter throughout their day, using changing context to guide when, where and how a brand engages." The release maps it against a day in a person's life.
Each of those channels already has its own buying systems, measurement standards and dominant sellers. Digital out-of-home screens are usually sold through specialist platforms. Retail media networks are run by retailers on their own data. CTV inventory is spread across device makers, streaming services and supply platforms. The release does not say which of these Turms can already buy or sell, which it plans to connect through partners, or in what order beyond the car.
What separates the pitch from a plain multichannel offer is the claim that the system may choose not to advertise. According to the release, "Rather than treating every available touchpoint as an opportunity to serve an ad, Movement Media is designed to evaluate permitted contextual signals, campaign objectives and available inventory to determine whether an interaction is appropriate and, when it is, which channel, format and moment best fit the context."
The privacy argument rests on the same idea. The approach, Turms says, "is designed to respect consumer privacy by basing advertising decisions on the context of the moment rather than on profiling individuals." That places Movement Media in the tradition of contextual advertising, which targets the setting of an ad rather than the person seeing it. The release does not define "permitted" signals, or say who grants the permission: the user, the device maker, the publisher or the law. The Turms website, meanwhile, lists a product called Audiences, described as "contextual and audience targeting", so the company's offer is not limited to context alone.
How Movement Media AI is described to work
According to the release, MMAI "is designed to use AI agents to evaluate campaign objectives, permitted contextual signals, available advertising inventory, and performance feedback to guide decisions about when, where, and on which device or channel an ad should appear." It is a form of agentic AI, software that takes a sequence of decisions toward a goal rather than following a single fixed instruction. The release describes the process in six stages:
- Objectives. The advertiser's campaign objectives set the goal the agents work toward.
- Signals. The agents read "permitted contextual signals" about the moment, rather than a profile of the individual.
- Inventory. They check what advertising inventory is available across devices and channels.
- Appropriateness. They decide "whether an interaction is appropriate" at all. Declining to serve is presented as a valid outcome.
- Placement. When an interaction is judged appropriate, they choose "which channel, format and moment best fit the context."
- Feedback. Performance data feeds back into later decisions. According to the release, "Rather than relying solely on predefined rules, the aim is to adapt media decisions as audience context changes and campaigns generate results, helping coordinate advertising across the consumer journey."
The release describes MMAI as media-buying technology, but it sets objectives for both sides of the trade: to "help advertisers engage audiences in more relevant moments, enable publishers and media partners to better monetize their inventory, and support advertising experiences that fit the context in which they appear." It does not say whether MMAI will act for buyers, for sellers or for both, which AI models it uses, whether it will connect to other companies' agents through an industry protocol, or how advertisers would see why the system passed over an available placement.
The website lists MMAI alongside five other products, none of which is named in the release.
Cars as the early priority
Of all the settings in the plan, the car gets the most attention. "Connected vehicles and in-car advertising represent an early strategic area of development within the company's broader Movement Media strategy," according to the release. The offer to manufacturers is specific in ambition, if not in mechanics: "For vehicle manufacturers, Turms aims to create new revenue opportunities throughout the life of their vehicles, including those already on the road, without changing the driver experience."
That last clause carries a lot of weight. The release does not say whether in-car ads would be audio, on a dashboard screen, or both; whether they would reach drivers or passengers; how cars already on the road would receive the capability; or how adding advertising leaves the driver experience unchanged. Nor does it name a single manufacturer in talks.
The dashboard has become a contested space. PPC Land reported in October 2025 that 60% of vehicle owners in a Gracenote survey called infotainment a key factor when buying or leasing a car. In August 2026, Autio placed an embedded audio app in five Ford and Lincoln models, with no advertising details disclosed. On October 21, 2026, Spotify put a native app into five Audi models, free tier included, and at CES in January 2026 Amazon showed Alexa+ running in BMW's iX3. The Autio, Spotify and Amazon arrangements each put a consumer platform inside the car. Turms is pitching something different: a revenue line for the manufacturer itself.
The company's main automotive appointment points the same way. Scott Lyons joins as Automotive Board Advisor. According to the release, he spent 13 years in senior leadership at Ford Motor Company, where he co-founded the MakeItDriveable startup program, and earlier worked at Motorola, MTV and Digit London, part of WPP. He "supports Turms' in-car advertising strategy and automotive partnerships."
Audio's place in the mix
Marcinik used the announcement to make a case about where audio belongs in a media plan. "As early as 2019, our first attribution studies using Audiopixel highlighted audio's ability to influence people's decisions," he said. "But unlocking that potential requires a deliberate role in the media mix, with the right timing and coordination across channels, not simply as an add-on to extend reach. We believe the future of advertising isn't about reaching people more often. It's about understanding when an interaction actually makes sense and this is exactly what Movement Media AI is for."
The release does not publish results from those 2019 Audiopixel studies, nor describe how Audiopixel works.
The argument that audio is underused is an old one in the sector. In July 2025, PPC Land reported that AdsWizz's State of Audio Adtech Report 2025 put audio at 31% of media time but 9% of ad budgets. By December 2025, Triton Digital was predicting that programmatic audio would shift toward curation, identity and video in 2026. Agentic buying has since reached audio directly: on October 28, 2026, Triton Digital's Seller Agent booked a Talpa Media audio campaign for the brand Otrium, with Draft Digital as agency. Earlier, on May 27, 2026, Broadsign and Draft Digital ran what they called the first fully agentic out-of-home campaign in the Netherlands. Turms enters that field with technology it describes as still in development and no disclosed campaign.
Six names behind the plan
To support the new direction, the release says, Turms "has strengthened its team across automotive strategy, commercial development, product innovation, programmatic supply, and global marketing." It does not say when each person joined or whether all six are new to the company.
According to the Turms website, the company has its headquarters in London, an R&D centre in Gdynia, Poland, an EU office in Berlin and a US office in New York. The site names iHeartMedia, Katz Digital, Magnite, Microsoft and ivee among its partners. The release names no partners.
A season of new names
Turms follows several advertising technology companies that have changed names since September. On September 1, Stagwell renamed The People Platform as Numetrix with no product change. On September 16, 33Across took the corporate name WealthStage to sell an ad platform to banks and trading apps. A week later, on September 23, Scope3 became Apostra, a company of about 40 people focused on AI agents that trade ads.
