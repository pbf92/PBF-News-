TITLE: Advertiser account name
OUTLET: PPC Land

Advertiser account name is a dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that shows the human-readable label of the advertiser account a row of data belongs to. It sits beside Advertiser account ID, the numeric key, and exists for the opposite reason. An ID is built for machines: fixed, unique and meaningless to a reader. A name is built for people scanning a report that may cover dozens of brands, countries and clients at once, where it tells an analyst which rows belong to the pet food client and which to the power tools client.
The field sits in Amazon's unified reporting dimensions reference, which Amazon updated on November 11, 2026. PPC Land's field-by-field reading of that reference found that Advertiser account ID, Advertiser account name and Manager accounts apply to all five products the reference covers: DVA+, Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display and Sponsored TV. The account fields fall within the "level of detail" category, which PPC Land described as carrying most of the account hierarchy. Unlike Advertiser account ID, which was called "Advertiser ID" in former DVA+ reports, the name field has no former name listed in that summary.
What an advertiser account is
The name only makes sense against the object it labels. Amazon accounts follow a hierarchy. A manager account sits on top, optionally, and links many accounts for one agency, company or business unit. Beneath it sit advertiser accounts, which hold campaigns. Campaigns hold ad groups, and ad groups hold ads and targets.
Before 2025, an advertiser typically held one sponsored ads account and one DSP account per country, according to Amazon's announcement of the closed beta of its upgraded advertiser account on September 30, 2025. A brand selling in Germany, France and the United Kingdom could therefore carry six or more separate accounts, each with its own label. The sponsored ads API represented each of these as a profile, which python-amazon-ad-api, an open-source client library, documents as representing "an advertiser and their account's marketplace", with no more than one profile per marketplace.
Amazon then merged the pieces. The upgraded account entered open beta for new registrations in October 2025, when Pratham Sarin of Amazon announced it on LinkedIn with the line "One account to access all Amazon Ads products - no more separate registrations." On July 30, 2026, Amazon moved existing DSP advertisers to a single global account across 34 countries. Region is now chosen at account level and country at campaign level.
For the name field, that means consolidation: several labels for one brand become one.
Where the name comes from
The value is not typed into a report. It is pulled from the account's own record, set when the account is created and editable afterwards.
Amazon's Support Center page "Create a new advertiser account", updated on November 16, 2026, describes three routes into setup: the Amazon Ads homepage registration, the Advertisers tab in Campaign Manager (which requires a manager account with access to DVA+), and the Accounts area of account access and settings. The Advertiser Info step asks for three fields. Business name is defined as "the company headquarters that is shown on official documents and tax forms". The phone number is the company's primary contact, and the website is the address of the business being advertised. Partners get a specific instruction: "If you're a partner, make sure that you enter your advertiser's details here." PPC Land's reading of the page was that the client's legal entity, not the agency's, belongs on new accounts.
Amazon's guide to the advertiser account, last published on October 24, 2026, says account holders can "view and edit account details (business details, account name, etc.)" in the console, or programmatically through the UpdateAdvertiserAccount API. The September 2025 beta announcement listed that update endpoint as POST /adsApi/v1/update/advertiserAccounts, alongside create and query equivalents. Amazon's public pages do not state whether the account name and the legal business name are one field or two, or which of them fills the reporting column.
The older APIs show how the label used to travel. Third-party documentation of Amazon's version 2 profiles endpoint, last updated on February 25, 2026, gives accountInfo.name the description "Account name", and python-amazon-ad-api notes that accountInfo is read only there. Coverage was patchy: a Stack Overflow answer, in a thread with comments from December 2019, said profile names were returned only for vendor and agency profiles, with a later edit adding sellers. The DSP advertiser endpoint carried its own name field, described as "The advertiser name".
How it behaves in a report
Advertiser account name is a grouping and filtering attribute. Adding it breaks spend, impressions and sales out by account, including in manager-level reports covering many accounts in one query. Three behaviours matter.
Names are labels, not keys. Two accounts can carry similar or identical names, particularly when an agency creates accounts for the same client in different setups. Joining data on the name rather than on Advertiser account ID risks merging accounts that are separate.
Names change. Because the name is editable, a report run today may show a different label from one run last quarter for the same account. Exports stored in a warehouse can therefore hold several names for one ID.
The column is always filled. Every row belongs to an account, so unlike product-specific fields, which Amazon says appear "as null" where they do not apply, the name field should populate across all five ad products.
A worked example
Consider an agency managing a kitchenware maker from its manager account. Before 2025, that client might have appeared under separate sponsored ads profiles for Germany, France and Italy and a DSP advertiser for Europe, each labelled by whoever set it up.
In a unified report grouped by Advertiser account name, the migrated client appears under one label covering its Sponsored Products campaigns in three countries and its DVA+ streaming TV campaign. Adding Campaign ID breaks that label into campaigns. Had the agency named the account "Agency X - Kitchen", the rows would still group correctly, but the label would not match the legal business name Amazon now asks partners to enter.
Origin and evolution
Amazon's sponsored ads and its programmatic platform, launched as Amazon Advertising Platform and renamed Amazon DSP in September 2018, long kept separate account records. On October 1, 2020, Amazon rolled out Manager Account, letting advertisers and agencies link seller and vendor accounts in North America and Europe and see performance across them in one dashboard. Nested manager accounts followed in April 2024. Unified reporting opened in beta on November 11, 2025 and reached general availability on June 8, 2026, putting every product's account label into one column.
Why it matters
For agencies, the name is the first thing a client sees in a shared report and the easiest way to confirm that billing, access and spend line up with the right business. The November 2026 setup guidance ties naming to identity: the business name is meant to be the client's legal entity, and Amazon's billing options separate a manager account, for "a reseller, agency, or partner acting in your own capacity", from an advertiser account, for "an advertiser or agency acting on behalf of the advertiser".
The migration adds pressure. Under the timeline Amazon published on November 12, 2026, creation and editing of legacy reports stop on December 17, 2026, and unmigrated reports are deleted on January 31, 2027. Dashboards that matched legacy DSP advertiser names or sponsored ads profile names to clients need remapping to the unified account names.
Limitations and disputes
The field is undefined in public. PPC Land's summary of the dimensions reference quoted no definition for it, and Amazon's own help pages describe the account's editable details without saying which value reports display.
Identifiers are contested around it. Amazon's July 30, 2026 announcement said users "will receive a new advertiser account ID that replaces legacy Amazon DSP advertiser account IDs". Amazon's guide says "your existing Amazon DSP advertiser account ID is now your advertiser account ID". The API section of the announcement says existing identifiers "continue to work without modification". A name that stays the same while the ID beneath it may or may not change makes the name tempting as a join key, which is exactly the use it is least suited to.
The legal-name instruction also has gaps. PPC Land noted that Amazon's setup page gives no reason for it, names no countries and sets no deadline for existing accounts. Sponsored TV coverage is uncertain too: the reference marks it on many fields, yet states "We do not support Streaming TV Smart mode/ Sponsored TV yet".
Advertiser account name is not...
Advertiser account ID. The fixed numeric key for the same account. The ID never changes when the account is renamed; the name can.
Manager accounts. A separate dimension identifying the manager account or accounts above the advertiser account. One manager can sit above many advertiser accounts.
Entity ID. A dimension that, according to PPC Land, ties an advertiser account to an entity. It describes a link, not a label.
Brand name or Benchmark brand name. Brands are products sold under an account. One account can advertise several brands, and benchmark reports label brands, not accounts.
Recent developments
On November 16, 2026, Amazon updated its guidance for creating advertiser accounts, and PPC Land reported the next day that the business name field expects the client's legal name, with partners told to enter their advertiser's details. That followed the November 11 dimensions reference listing the field across all five ad products. DVA+ remains in US closed beta, with broader availability planned for 2027.
