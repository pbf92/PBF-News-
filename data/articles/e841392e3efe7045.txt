TITLE: IG Group to Close Its Offices in France, Spain and Italy: Report
OUTLET: Finance Magnates

IG Group (LON: IGG) is closing its offices in France, Spain and Italy and is cutting dozens of jobs at those locations, Bloomberg reported, citing “people familiar with the matter.” It might affect roughly 50 IG employees.
IG Is Cutting Jobs, Again
The London-headquartered broker has already informed its staff in those offices of its closure plans, the report added. It opened its offices in Spain and France in 2007 and the one in Italy in 2008.
Headquartered in London, IG has offices across five continents, covering Europe, Asia-Pacific, North America, the Middle East and Africa.
Finance Magnates approached IG to confirm the planned office closures but did not receive any response as of press time.
“We have refreshed our organisational model to ensure IG is best placed to serve customers,” an IG spokesperson told Bloomberg. “This includes consolidating our European presence into a smaller number of larger hubs.”
Interestingly, another report in September revealed that IG was preparing to cut hundreds of jobs as it merges its regional consumer operations into a single division. The exact reduction has not been disclosed, but the plans affect a workforce that numbered about 2,300 at the end of June.
In 2023, IG Group reduced its global workforce by 10 per cent.

IG Is Restructuring, While Shares Are Falling
Meanwhile, IG is moving its holding company to a new Jersey-based unit. It has already received court approval for the move. The company expects £30 million in restructuring costs in 2026, including £16.4 million reported in H1.
IG shares, however, are struggling, falling 38 per cent in the past six months, driven primarily by an unexpected cut to its full-year revenue outlook and a steep drop in third-quarter revenue due to weaker over-the-counter (OTC) trading conditions.
The broker retained just 70 per cent of client trading income during Q3 in OTC markets, a sharp drop from its historical average of 80 per cent. As a consequence, IG now expects Q3 revenue to fall 14 per cent year-on-year to £240 million.
eToro is another broker which is reportedly planning to cut dozens of jobs in another round of layoffs. The Nasdaq-listed broker laid off 7 per cent of its workforce, or about 105 employees, earlier in January.
