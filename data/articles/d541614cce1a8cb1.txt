TITLE: Placement size
OUTLET: PPC Land

What the field is
Placement size is a dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that labels each row of programmatic delivery data with the width and height of the slot where the ad ran. A medium rectangle on a news site reports as 300x250, a mobile banner in an app as 320x50, a billboard across the top of a desktop page as 970x250. The field exists because the same campaign, budget and audience can perform very differently depending on the shape of the space it buys. Reporting by slot size shows where the money went and what each format returned.
The field belongs to DVA+, the campaign type Amazon announced on September 29, 2026 that merges buying previously done through Amazon DSP, Sponsored Display and Sponsored TV. In PPC Land's count of the 156 dimensions in Amazon's unified reporting reference, published November 12, 2026, Placement name and Placement size are marked for DVA+ only. They sit in the Supply category, one of 35 attributes describing where an impression was bought. For any ad product a dimension does not cover, Amazon's rule is that "its value appears as null", which lets sponsored ads and DVA+ rows share one report.
How it works
A programmatic display impression begins with a request from a publisher's page or app. Under the OpenRTB protocol maintained by the IAB Tech Lab, the request describes each slot in a Banner object. That object can carry exact width and height in device independent pixels (DIPS) through the w and h attributes, or an array of Format objects listing every size the slot accepts. OpenRTB 2.5 added ratio-based fields, wratio, hratio and wmin, so a flexible slot can be described by its proportions and a minimum width rather than a single pixel count. The demand-side platform (DSP) reads those dimensions, filters out creatives that do not fit, and bids with one that does.
Once the impression serves, the slot's size is logged and unified reporting exposes it as Placement size. An advertiser building a report picks dimensions, adds Placement size, and selects metrics such as impressions, viewable impressions, clicks, total cost, cost per thousand impressions (CPM) and purchases. Each line of the campaign's results then splits into one row per slot size.
The values follow Amazon's creative specifications. Amazon's ad specs for desktop and mobile web static display list five desktop sizes: medium rectangle 300x250, leaderboard 728x90, wide skyscraper 160x600, large rectangle 300x600 and billboard 970x250, with an 800x250 variant in Germany only. File weight limits are tight: 40 KB for a static 300x250 image (50 KB in France, Italy, Spain and Japan) and 200 KB for HTML5. On mobile web Amazon adds a 320x50 mobile leaderboard and a custom 414x125 unit that, in Amazon's words, "runs on Amazon mobile shopping and mobile web only." Mobile app banners add 600x90 and 1024x50 for third-party rich media.
As a hypothetical example, a DVA+ campaign spending US$20,000 in a month might show 300x250 taking most impressions at a low CPM, 970x250 billboards fewer at a higher CPM, and the 414x125 unit on Amazon's shopping pages delivering most attributed purchases. Without the field, all three appear as one line.
Who operates it
On the sell side, publishers, apps and Amazon itself define slot sizes. Amazon-owned properties, including its store pages, are among the supply DVA+ reaches alongside third-party exchanges. Publishers declare sizes in their ad servers.
On the buy side, advertisers, agencies and the data connectors that pull Amazon reports into other tools read the field. Connector documentation shows it predates unified reporting: Funnel lists Placement Name and Placement Size among the fields of the old Amazon DSP inventory report, next to Site, Deal ID and Deal Type, while Adverity's Amazon DSP connector exposes a placementSizeDimension field. Amazon itself is the operator of the reporting and the DSP logic that decides which creative fits which slot.
Origin and evolution
Standard slot sizes are older than programmatic buying. The IAB issued voluntary ad unit guidelines from the late 1990s, and its 2011 Rising Stars portfolio added larger units such as the 970x250 billboard, the 970x90 pushdown and the 300x600 "filmstrip". According to MarTech's coverage, it was the first time the IAB had introduced a new standard portfolio since 2003. Randall Rothenberg, then president and chief executive of the IAB, said the units allowed creatives "to tell bigger, bolder brand stories."
In July 2017 the IAB Tech Lab replaced that approach. Its New Ad Portfolio moved, in the words of its FAQ, "from fixed pixel sizes to aspect-ratio based flexible ads", citing the spread of touch-screen phones, and folded in the LEAN principles (light, encrypted, AdChoices supported, non-invasive). The FAQ mapped legacy units to ratios, 300x250 to roughly 1:1 and 728x90 to roughly 8:1, allowed publishers to accept both "during a transition period", and gave Rising Stars formats six months before retirement. In practice fixed sizes never left. Google's Authorized Buyers guide still ranks 300x250, 336x280, 728x90 and 160x600 among its top-performing sizes and calls 300x600 "one of the fastest growing sizes based on impressions."
Amazon's own milestones came through creative management. On April 23, 2024, Amazon added an "Add missing creative sizes" recommendation to the Amazon DSP interface that, according to the announcement, flags "underpacing orders that are missing common creative sizes". On May 15, 2024, Amazon began reporting delivery and performance for each size of a responsive eCommerce creative (REC), a format that renders automatically at several dimensions; before that, advertisers could not see which size of a single REC asset performed best.
Unified reporting then took over. It entered beta on November 11, 2025, reached general availability on June 8, 2026, and its dimensions reference, updated November 11, 2026, lists Placement size as a DVA+ attribute. Amazon DSP was renamed DVA on September 29, 2026, and the DVA+ campaign type launched the same day in a US closed beta, with general availability in 35 markets targeted for the first quarter of 2027.
Why it matters for marketers
Slot size is one of the few supply attributes an advertiser controls directly, by choosing which creative sizes to upload. Amazon's 2024 pacing recommendation shows the cost of gaps, and the field measures that exposure after the fact.
It also matters because DVA+ hands more decisions to automation. When one campaign buys display, video and audio from a single budget, the share of spend on each slot shape is set by Amazon's models, not by separate line items. Size and viewability are linked too. From January 1, 2027, display impressions on Amazon Store placements will count only once on screen, and Amazon expects about 63% fewer impressions on average. Below-the-fold sizes are likely to lose more than top-of-page units, so CPM comparisons across sizes will shift.
The field is also part of a migration deadline: legacy reports stop accepting new reports on December 17, 2026 and unmigrated reports will be deleted on January 31, 2027, so placement size tracking in the old inventory report has to be rebuilt.
Limitations and disputes
The field describes geometry, not position or quality. A 300x250 at the top of a premium site and a 300x250 at the bottom of an unmoderated page report identically. Placement name, Site or app and viewability metrics are needed to tell them apart.
Responsive formats blur the value. A REC asset or a flexible slot defined by aspect ratio can render at a size that differs from what the publisher declared. Amazon has not published a definition stating whether Placement size records the requested slot or the rendered creative; Funnel's separate Creative Size and Placement Size fields in the old DSP reports suggest Amazon treated them as distinct.
Coverage is narrow. Placement size applies only to DVA+, so Sponsored Display and Sponsored Products data return null, even though Sponsored Display also serves in display slots. Video is reported separately through Video player size, which Amazon groups from small to large plus unknown rather than in pixels. Amazon has not published a public value list for the field.
Standards are contested as well. The IAB's 2017 shift to ratios was never fully adopted, and the industry still trades on fixed sizes alongside custom units such as Amazon's 414x125, which exists on no IAB list.
How it differs from similar fields
Ad size describes the creative that served, not the slot. For a standard banner the two match; for responsive creatives they can diverge. Placement name identifies the specific placement an impression ran on, such as a named slot on a page, while Placement size only gives its dimensions. Placement classification applies to sponsored ads and groups results by location type, such as top of search or product pages; it carries no pixel information. Video player size buckets the dimensions of a video player rather than a display slot.
Recent developments
PPC Land's November 12, 2026 analysis set out which of the 156 unified reporting dimensions apply to which product, confirming Placement size as one of 59 DVA+-only attributes. Third-party guidance on rebuilding old DSP inventory reports in unified reporting, such as Adbrew's October 2026 walkthrough, lists Supply source, Site or app, Deal and Placement name, but not Placement size, a sign the field is easy to miss in migration. On the standards side, the IAB Tech Lab published six standardized CTV ad formats for public comment on December 11, 2025, built around 1920x1080 fullscreen creative. As DVA+ moves toward general availability and on-screen impression counting starts on January 1, 2027, size-level reporting is likely to be read alongside viewability rather than alone.
