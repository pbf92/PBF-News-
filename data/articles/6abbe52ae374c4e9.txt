What happened?
A number of stocks jumped in the morning session after CrowdStrike announced, in a press release, a definitive all-stock transaction valued at approximately $43.9 billion to acquire Zscaler, uniting endpoint protection and zero-trust network security.
The mega-deal marks one of the largest consolidation moves in software history, directly influencing cybersecurity valuations across public markets. By integrating Zscaler into its Falcon platform, CrowdStrike aims to offer an all-in-one security architecture that protects both devices and cloud network traffic. Zero-trust security operates on the principle of never trusting and always verifying any user or device attempting to access private networks.
For investors, this massive transaction underscores escalating enterprise demand for comprehensive cybersecurity suites rather than fragmented point solutions, driving heightened interest and trading activity in both companies.

Zooming in on Shopify
Shopify’s shares are extremely volatile and have had 46 moves greater than 5% over the last year. In that context, today’s move indicates the market considers this news meaningful but not something that would fundamentally change its perception of the business.
The previous big move we wrote about was 20 days ago when the stock dropped 9.4% on the news that the company reported third-quarter financial results with gross merchandise volume falling below consensus forecasts. According to a company press release, Shopify generated third-quarter revenue of $3.74 billion, up 31.6% year on year, roughly in line with Wall Street’s estimates. Gross merchandise volume reached $119.1 billion, below expectations, as analysts had projected $120.1 billion in gross merchandise volume.
Looking ahead, management guided fourth-quarter revenue growth in the low-thirties percentage range. The release highlighted that Merchant Solutions revenue climbed 35% to $2.91 billion, helping lift operating income 66% to $568 million. Free cash flow rose to $816 million, delivering a 22% margin compared with 18% a year earlier. Gross merchandise volume measures the total dollar value of orders facilitated across the platform. The shortfall in this key performance indicator appeared to overshadow the headline top-line expansion, weighing heavily on investor sentiment.
Shopify is down 14.3% since the beginning of the year, and at $134.76 per share, it is trading 24.8% below its 52-week high of $179.22 from October 2026. Investors who bought $1,000 worth of Shopify’s shares 5 years ago would now be looking at only $856.68.
