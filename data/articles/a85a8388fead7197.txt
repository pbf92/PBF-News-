TITLE: Missouri Voters Keep Their Artificial Trees Up until January | Newswise
OUTLET: Newswise

ST. LOUIS - A majority of likely Missouri voters decorate an artificial Christmas tree and keep it up until January, according to the SLU/YouGov Poll.
The August 2026 SLU/YouGov Poll surveyed 900 likely Missouri voters about their holiday habits, including when they start shopping, what kind of Christmas tree they purchase and how long decorations stay up.
When it comes to trees, there is no doubt where Missourians stand. Sixty-eight percent of those polled reported planning to only put up an artificial tree this year, while 11% said they would only get a live Christmas tree. One percent of respondents said they would put up both an artificial and real tree (MoE ±4.09%).
“Artificial trees were by far more popular among all Missouri voters,” said Steven Rogers, Ph.D., director of the SLU/YouGov Poll. “But voters with children at home were about twice as likely to say they put up a real tree, 19% compared with 9% of voters without children under 18 at home.”
The real vs. fake tree debate is a bipartisan issue in Missouri, with majorities of Democrats (65%), Republicans (72%) and Independents (65%) planning to put up only an artificial tree. Fifteen percent of Democrats said they plan to put up a real tree this year, compared with 12% of Independents and 9% of Republicans.
Once the halls are decked, most poll respondents report keeping those decorations up through the new year. Only 14% of those polled reported taking down their holiday decorations before the end of the year.
Forty-two percent of Missourians polled will take down their decorations during the first week of January, with another 21% keeping their lights and trees up until mid-January. Five percent of respondents reported keeping their holiday decorations up after February 1 (MoE ±5.67%).
“Most Missouri voters who put up a Christmas tree said they would keep their holiday decorations up into January, whichever kind they use,” said Rogers. “That includes 85% of voters who put up an artificial tree and 80% of those who put up a real one.”
Holiday Shopping
Fifty percent of likely Missouri voters polled reported starting their holiday shopping before Thanksgiving. Twenty-one percent of likely voters reported starting to shop before Halloween. Twenty-eight percent began once the calendar turned to November.
Twenty-two percent of those polled wait until Black Friday to start their shopping and another 11 percent report starting their shopping in early to mid-December (MoE ±5.67%).
“About a third of Missouri voters with children under 18 years old at home start holiday shopping before Halloween, about twice the share among voters without such children” said Rogers. “We also found that 63% of voters under 45 start shopping before Thanksgiving, compared with 42% of voters 45 and older.”
Twelve percent of those polled report avoiding holiday shopping altogether. Twenty-three percent of men reported starting their shopping in December, but only 11% of women started this late. Men and women were equally likely to skip holiday shopping (12% each).
“More than half of Democratic men and Republican women told us they start their holiday shopping before Thanksgiving, compared to 44% of Democratic women and 36% of Republican men,” said Rogers.
Methodology and Funding
YouGov interviewed 900 likely Missouri voters between August 13 and August 24, 2026. The YouGov panel is a proprietary opt-in survey panel of United States residents who have agreed to participate in YouGov web surveys. Using their gender, age, race, and education, YouGov weighted the set of survey respondents to known characteristics of Missouri voters from the American Community Survey (ACS) public use microdata file, public voter file records.
The August 2026 SLU/YouGov Poll was funded by SLU’s College of Arts & Sciences. Additional grant funding from the U.S. Department of Energy supports the poll. For fuller details about funding for the SLU/YouGov Poll, visit slu.edu/poll.

About YouGov
Saint Louis University has partnered with YouGov to conduct its biannual survey of Missouri voters. YouGov conducts surveys for multiple academic institutions and is the primary, trusted survey firm for media organizations, including CBS News and The Economist. FiveThirtyEight (538) ranked YouGov as one of the top pollsters in the country, with respect to its strong commitment to accuracy and transparency in polling.
