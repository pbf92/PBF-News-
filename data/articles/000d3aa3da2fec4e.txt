Jennifer Alvarez was at work inside the home arena of the Miami Heat when she unexpectedly came across a video while scrolling.
The trailer for "Grand Theft Auto VI" — inspired by Miami — had popped up on her screen. It featured an image depicting the exact arena where she was sitting at that moment, along with several other notable city landmarks.
That spark led to an idea, and Alvarez picked up her phone.
More than two years, endless meetings, and thousands of hours of preparation later, the Heat — having established a partnership with Rockstar Games, the creator and publisher of "GTA VI" — are positioned directly at the epicenter of what may be the most anticipated video game release in history. On Wednesday, the Heat will officially represent Vice City, facing off against the Milwaukee Bucks on a unique, one-time-only court in custom, one-time-only uniforms.
"What we’re doing is creating a crossover experience of gaming and sport in a way that no one has actually interacted with before," said Alvarez, who is senior vice president for brand and chief growth officer for the Heat. "For everyone who comes on Wednesday night, they’re taking a step inside Vice City. For that night, they live in Leonida. They don’t live in Miami, Florida. And that experience begins before they even walk in the door."
Vice City serves as the game’s fictional setting, loosely modeled after Miami. Leonida is the fictional state, modeled after Florida. Meanwhile, the Heat have featured their popular "Vice" line of uniforms and merchandise since introducing the vibrant gear during the 2017-18 season.
It was a natural fit: Vice meeting Vice City.
"We thought: ‘Wait a minute. This is us. How do we figure out a way to be a part of this?’" Alvarez said. "It's the return of Vice City, which is a highly anticipated title — 13 years people waited for this game. And we’ve been synonymous with the word Vice since 2017 when we first introduced the original Vice campaign and knew we needed to work. We needed to reach out. There’s just too much here, too many kind of stars aligning, too much kismet happening for us to ignore it."
Every single detail for Wednesday — occurring the day prior to the official worldwide debut of "GTA VI" — was carefully arranged.
Miami-Dade County agreed to a $975,000 deal several months ago to place "Vice City" on the arena's roof, functioning as a billboard for airplane passengers flying into and out of Miami International Airport. Media notes provided by the Heat prior to games list the team's origin as Vice City. Arena doors were scheduled to open earlier than usual, with fans hoping to buy merchandise expected to queue up hours before the game. Vehicles from the game were arranged for display, graphics inside the venue featured "GTA" elements, a special halftime show was arranged, and the organization promised further surprises. The jersey logos match what players will see in "GTA."
"We're all just running on pure adrenaline," Alvarez said.
Heat coach Erik Spoelstra would generally prefer to remain out of the spotlight, but he realized long ago that staying low-profile is impossible in Miami — and when an initiative like Vice City arrives, he welcomes the event.
"We’re all about business and preparing for games, but this organization is larger than life," Spoelstra said. "That’s why we are who we are. And something like this is totally unpredictable, just to bring a whole lot more fanfare to all of it. I embrace it. I've loved Vice for this organization. I think this Vice Night is going to be really cool."
Features from "GTA" are appearing in other gaming franchises as well. Later this month, "NBA2K27" will release a special Vice City Edition with Heat captain Bam Adebayo featured on the cover.
"The culture of Miami is special and getting to see everyone experience that through the game is what’s so exciting," Adebayo said.
As a gesture of gratitude from Rockstar, Heat players received customized "GTA VI" consoles, controllers, and copies of the physical game on Tuesday. The jerseys worn for Wednesday’s game arrived at the arena earlier in the week, while the design of the court was kept confidential until Tuesday.
Milwaukee guard Tyler Herro, who played his first seven professional seasons in Miami and practiced Wednesday morning on the special court, has already downloaded the game.
"I know what the Vice City means," Herro said. "I know the meaning behind it and obviously, ‘GTA’ is one of the most popular games. My kids obviously won’t be playing it — keep them away from that — but it’s a big opportunity for the city to welcome ‘GTA VI.’ Obviously, I’ll be playing the game. I gotta see what that’s about."
The Heat introduced their Vice City merchandise collection on Oct. 2, with 30% of total orders arriving from international buyers across 60 countries, Alvarez noted. In-game fast-food establishments like Burger Shot and Cluckin' Bell have even been recreated at Heat home games in recent weeks. During stadium tours on non-game days, visitors regularly ask whether Burger Shot is open for business.
"And we’re like, ‘We’re sorry, it’s only open for games and events,’" Alvarez said. "This is global appeal. My hope is they come to a Heat game because we’re a way for them to experience Vice City in a way they haven’t been able to. So, it’s my job to make their game experience unforgettable."
