Fed minutes show almost all officials judged more rate rises would likely be appropriate, and the Dow falls further
Almost all Fed officials judged that further rate rises would likely be appropriate, the minutes of their October meeting show. The Federal Reserve released the minutes of its October 27 and 28 meeting on Wednesday afternoon. The rate they discussed is the federal funds rate, the Fed's key interest rate, which guides borrowing costs (Fed minutes). A couple of officials said a rise of more than a quarter point could be considered at the December meeting if inflation data were unfavorable.
The Fed held its rate range at 3.75% to 4% in October. Two voting members, Lisa D. Cook and Beth M. Hammack, preferred a quarter-point rise. Almost all officials also discussed the risk that returns on AI investment could disappoint investors (Fed minutes).
The Dow fell further in the afternoon. The Dow Jones Industrial Average was down 609 points, or 1.2%, CNBC said in its live coverage, last updated at 2:56 p.m. ET. Health insurers fell further too. UnitedHealth was down 10.84% and Humana 12.05%, both at 3:02 p.m. ET.

1:19 p.m. ET
Health insurers fall for a second day, in a week when Medicare proposed wider use of audit results
UnitedHealth and Humana fell for a second day. UnitedHealth was down 8.99% and Humana 10.67%, both at 1:18 p.m. ET. On Tuesday, UnitedHealth had closed down 5.42% and Humana 3.88%.
Other health insurers were lower too. Elevance Health was down 5.83% at 1:17 p.m. ET. Centene was down 5.54%, Molina Healthcare 4.97% and CVS Health 3.36%, all at 1:18 p.m. ET.
On Monday, the agency that runs Medicare proposed a rule. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, or CMS, wants to recover more of what it calls improper payments to Medicare Advantage plans (HHS release). These are private plans that people on Medicare can pick instead of Original Medicare. Its audits check whether the diagnoses a plan reports for a sample of members are backed by medical records. Under the proposal, the error found in that sample would be used to estimate the payment error for the whole plan contract. This method is called extrapolation, and it would apply starting with 2018 payments (CMS proposed rule).
CMS says a federal court in Texas set aside similar provisions of a 2023 rule for procedural reasons, and it proposes to reissue them. It estimates the audits would recover $90.2 billion from 2027 through 2036 (CMS proposed rule).
The rule is still a proposal. It is scheduled to appear in the Federal Register on Thursday (CMS proposed rule). HHS says public comments are due by January 19, 2027 (HHS release).
