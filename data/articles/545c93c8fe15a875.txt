Power Demand Surges 11.7%, Coal Stock Buffer Stays Tight: ICRA
India's power demand remains elevated as thermal plants operate with coal stocks well below the prescribed inventory norm
India's power sector is entering a period of elevated demand with limited coal inventory at thermal generating stations, as electricity consumption rose 11.7 per cent during April-October while plant-level coal stocks covered only about seven days of generation as of 11 November, according to rating agency Icra.
The available stock is substantially below the prescribed 19-20 days, leaving generators with a relatively narrow fuel cushion if demand accelerates or coal movement faces disruption. The situation persists despite coal supplies to the power sector rising 5.1 per cent year-on-year during the first six months of FY2027.
Domestic coal output has not kept pace with the requirement. Production fell 1.1 per cent during the April-September period, with lower output from Coal India Limited contributing to the decline, according to Icra.
Demand Pressure Persists
The pressure on the generation system has carried into November. Electricity demand increased 12.9 per cent year-on-year during the first 11 days of the month, data from Grid Controller of India Limited showed.
Weather has added another variable to the demand outlook. Above-normal temperatures in several parts of the country have sustained cooling requirements even as the winter season has begun. Industrial and commercial activity, agricultural consumption and festive demand have also supported electricity usage.
Icra expects electricity demand to expand by around 8.5 per cent in FY2027. Weather conditions, however, remain a significant variable for the forecast.
Fuel Buffer Faces Strain
For thermal generators, the seven-day inventory position leaves limited room to absorb a sudden increase in generation requirements. Any simultaneous weakness in domestic coal output or disruption in supplies could further compress the available buffer.
Icra has indicated that a sustained improvement in coal production and deliveries will be required to restore stocks to more adequate levels. The issue is therefore not only the current inventory position but the ability of the supply chain to replenish stocks while electricity consumption remains elevated.
Spot Prices Moderate
Power market prices have moved lower from October levels despite the demand pressure. The average spot tariff on the Indian Energy Exchange stood at Rs 4 per unit on 10 November, compared with Rs 6.7 per unit in October.
The November figure remains above the Rs 3.1 per unit average recorded during the corresponding period last year. Icra expects electricity prices through FY2027 to remain sensitive to the balance between demand and available supply, as well as the pace of new generation capacity additions.
