I keep adding to my Anthropic (NASDAQ:ANT) position, and the post-IPO slide has only made me add faster. Shares closed at $97.17 on November 19 and settled at $82.79 on November 25, a 14.8% drop that leaves the stock near its $83.00 IPO price. CNBC called the stock a dud since its debut (CNBC). To me the pullback leaves the largest IPO in history, which raised $81.34 billion, at roughly the offering price.
My thesis: Claude works its way into the workflows businesses run on every day, and customers who start using it keep spending more on it. I want to own that compounding for decades.
Run-Rate Growth Most Investors Are Missing
Anthropic’s annualized revenue run rate rose from $47 billion in May 2026 to $65 billion in July and $81 billion by October 31, adding $10 billion in October alone. Reported revenue reached $11.6 billion for the three months ended June 30, 2026, up from $787 million a year earlier. More than 11,900 customers now spend at least $100,000 a year, up from 950+, and net revenue retention stood at approximately 900% as of September 30.
Margins and a Balance Sheet That Earn Patience
Gross margins on customer workloads run near 85%, which looks like software economics. Revenue grew 1,368% year over year in the June quarter while compute and infrastructure expenses grew 300%. Revenue per employee exceeded $6.6 million, up from $1.4 million. The company also held $112.4 billion in cash and $19.3 billion in short-term investments as of June 30.
Why My Money Skips Microsoft and Alphabet
The software giant (NASDAQ:MSFT | MSFT Price Prediction) is a strong business, but it grew revenue 18.8% last quarter at a P/E of 27. Microsoft even booked a $3.20 billion gain from its Anthropic stake in fiscal Q4 2026. Owning the source of that gain directly seems like the better move.
The search giant (NASDAQ:GOOGL) trades at a P/E of 12, yet its Q3 capital expenditures of $51.36 billion pushed free cash flow to negative $9.07 billion, and it has sold shares through an at-the-market program. That dilution erodes the cheap headline multiple for me.
Risk That Could Break My Thesis
Anthropic carries roughly $900 billion in multi-year infrastructure commitments. Its own S-1 warns that run-rate revenue “is not a forecast of future revenue”, that consumption-based revenue can swing, and that run-rate growth will decline over time. Income investors also get no dividend here. If demand stalls, those commitments become a heavy anchor.
My buying continues because revenue keeps growing faster than compute costs, the cash pile funds ongoing investment, and compute access spans multiple chipmakers’ systems (company landscape), which reduces single-supplier dependence. What Keeps My Conviction Intact
Partners keep wiring Claude deeper into enterprises. Enterprise software and cybersecurity partners announced expanded Anthropic partnerships in November 2026. Prediction-market traders price Q4 revenue above $21.5 billion at a probability of 0.495. With a market cap near $639.98 billion, I am paying close to the IPO price for a business whose customers multiply their spending year after year. I will keep collecting shares while sentiment stays cautious.
