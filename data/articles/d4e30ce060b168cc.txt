TITLE: Walmart's $35 meal for 10 gets 14 store-brand extras, all unpriced
OUTLET: PPC Land

Walmart today published a guide to building on its $35 Holiday Meal Basket, assembled from the suggestions of four of its chefs and a sensory scientist. Every product the post names, 14 in all, carries one of Walmart's own labels, Marketside or Great Value, and not one of them comes with a price. The footnotes, meanwhile, show what the $35 rests on: a Butterball turkey of an average 13.5 lb, valued at $0.97 a pound.
In Short
Walmart today put out a list of extras for its $35 Thanksgiving basket for 10 people, chosen by its own chefs, from Marketside cheese trays and ham to a store-brand cheesecake. It matters to food brands and retail advertisers because every product named is a Walmart private label, and the post prices none of them. The $35 still covers only the basket's 26 items, so the add-ons, any substitutions and pickup or delivery fees of up to $19.95 come on top.
A follow-up with a shopping list
The post, titled "How to Make Walmart's Holiday Meal Basket Your Own", appeared on Walmart's corporate newsroom under its Holidays 2026 heading with a stated reading time of five minutes. It is a sequel rather than a launch. The basket itself was announced on October 28, when Walmart published its 2026 Holiday Trend Report and priced a 26-item meal for 10 at $35, saying the same items cost 12% less than a year earlier.
What is new is a menu built on top of that basket. "Walmart's Holiday Meal Basket offers a convenient, affordable place to start," according to the post, which presents the additions as a way to tailor the meal "to your gathering and your budget".
Five Walmart employees supply the ideas. Four are chefs: Hayden Bradberry, Kayleen Echegoyen, Chris Ghozali and Sean Pera. The fifth is Angela Hebert, Walmart's Senior Sensory & Consumer Science Manager. Their job titles explain a good deal about the list that follows. Ghozali is a Certified Research Chef and Walmart Brands product developer for meat and seafood. Bradberry is described as a Walmart Culinary Development Chef who leads product development for candy and impulse. Echegoyen is senior product development manager for shelf stable produce & cheese, pickles & olives, and Pera is Walmart's pastry chef and senior product development manager for bakery. All four chefs work in product development, two of them explicitly for Walmart Brands.
What the $35 covers, according to the footnotes
The post describes the basket in a single sentence: a turkey and "ingredients for favorites like stuffing, green bean casserole and pumpkin pie". It does not list the 26 items. The terms sit in four footnotes at the bottom of the page.
The weight-based items carry most of the cost. On Walmart's own figures, the turkey accounts for about $13.10 and the sweet potatoes about $2.82, together $15.91, or roughly 45% of the $35. That leaves about $19.09 for the rest of the basket. The arithmetic is PPC Land's, not Walmart's. A heavier bird, or a store out of stock, changes the bill: "Substitutions may affect total price," the footnote states. At the average weight, each of the 10 guests is allotted 1.35 lb of turkey.
The turkey line has not moved in a year. Axios reported in October 2025 that Walmart was pricing Butterball turkeys at $0.97 per pound, its lowest since 2019, alongside a previous basket that cost less than $40 for 10 people, and FOX LA reported a 13.5 lb bird in that basket. On those reports, the turkey contributed nothing to the 12% saving Walmart claims for 2026.
The basket itself is not a store-brand affair. FOX 13, summarizing Walmart's October release on October 30, listed Butterball turkey, Stove Top stuffing, Campbell's Cream of Mushroom Soup, Jet-Puffed mini marshmallows and Jiffy corn muffin mix among the contents. National brands are in the box. They are absent from the list of things to add to it.
Delivery is tied to the price. Walmart+ members "can have the basket delivered from their local store with no delivery fee on eligible orders", according to the post, and the footnote sets eligibility at a $35 minimum on scheduled orders. The basket, in other words, sits exactly on the free shipping threshold, mirroring Amazon, whose $25 Thanksgiving meal matches its own $25 line for free Same-Day Delivery. The post gives no delivery terms for shoppers without Walmart+. FOX 13 also reported free Express Delivery on the basket through November 30; the November 17 post does not mention that offer.
How the post builds on the basket
According to Walmart, the additions fall into seven steps, each presented under its own heading.
- Start with something to share. Hebert recommends a simple charcuterie board of cheeses, crackers, fruit and "savory favorites", arranged on a platter or cutting board the host already owns. The post then lists eight ready-to-serve products: seven from Marketside and one from Great Value.
- Add another main to the table. Ghozali proposes a fully cooked Marketside ham. "A fully cooked ham makes holiday hosting easy - just warm it in the oven, covered, low and slow and add the tasty glaze for a flavorful finish," he says. "Leftover ham is so versatile, turning into sandwiches, breakfast scrambles or hearty soups that help stretch your holiday food budget." For seafood he names Marketside Maryland Style Crab Cakes and Marketside Frozen Wild Caught Cold Water Lobster Tails with Butter. Two vegetarian mains, a Stuffed Butternut Squash Bake and a Cheesy Mushroom Pasta Bake, appear without a named contributor.
- Round out the menu with more sides. Bradberry lists Golden Crispy Smashed Potatoes, Wild Mushroom & Gruyère Bread Pudding and Macaroni and Cheese as sides that "feel special but remain affordable".
- Bring something fresh to the table. Bradberry adds a Roasted Acorn & Butternut Squash Salad and a Winter Citrus Salad with Honey-Citrus Vinaigrette. "A little acidity can brighten the flavors across the plate," he says. "A squeeze of lemon, a splash of apple cider vinegar or a quick pickled onion can bring a fresh note to your favorite dishes."
- Small addition with a big impact. Here Bradberry suggests ingredients that work in more than one dish, such as a package of fresh herbs for the turkey, the stuffing and a vegetable platter. "Finishing touches can make a meaningful difference," he says. "Even celery leaves can add freshness and brightness to stuffing. A simple garnish can bring the dish together." The post lists four pairings: fresh herbs and butter, for a brown butter and sage sauce; garlic, sour cream and chives, for mashed potatoes; orange and cinnamon, for cranberry sauce or sweet potatoes; and mushrooms and onions, folded into stuffing.
- Offer another dessert. The basket already contains ingredients for a pumpkin pie with whipped topping. Pera proposes a ready-made Marketside Variety Cheesecake as a second option. "For a finishing touch, arrange the slices on a platter and add a dollop of whipped topping garnished with crushed Great Value Gingersnap Cookies or chopped Great Value Honey Roasted Pecans for additional holiday flair," he says.
- Make the most of the leftovers. Echegoyen points to two recipes on Walmart's Recipe Hub, turkey and ham Monte Cristo sandwiches and Leftover Turkey Pozole. "Thanksgiving leftovers are an invitation to create something new," she says. "Whether it's turning turkey and ham into a golden Monte Cristo or mixing up a comforting bowl of pozole, there are many delicious ways to keep the holiday celebration going."
The post closes by pointing readers to Walmart.com/HolidayFood, promising "everyday low prices on the extra dishes and ingredients your guests love". What do those prices add up to? The post does not say.
The 14 named products
That makes 11 Marketside products and three from Great Value. Seven further dishes, the two vegetarian bakes, the three sides and the two salads, carry no label in the post, which does not say whether they are recipes or ready-made products. The two leftover dishes are recipes on Walmart's Recipe Hub. The only national brand anywhere in the post is Butterball, and it appears in a price footnote.
The survey figures behind the advice
Two statistics frame the suggestions. Both come from Walmart's own shopper panel, which makes them first-party data collected and published by the retailer that sells the products. The footnote reads: "Walmart First-Party Data, Walmart Customer Spark Community powered by Scintilla (September 2026)."
The first opens the appetizer section: "Walmart's Holiday Trend Report found that 28% of shoppers felt ready-to-serve meals would make holiday hosting easier or more enjoyable." In the October report, that figure was 28.1%, and it trailed three other answers to the same question: keeping it affordable at 40.7%, easy cleanup at 40.3% and potlucks at 39.4%. The post cites the answer that matches ready-to-serve Marketside trays, not the more popular ones.
The second sits under the "small addition" heading: "Walmart's 2026 Holiday Trend Report found that 1 in 3 customers plan to spend more on holiday meals." The October report put that share at 33.1%. PPC Land noted at the time that the report published no sample size, exact field dates or margin of error, and that its headline finding was that 43.8% of panel members named affording the holidays as their top source of stress. That figure does not appear in the November 17 post.
Scintilla is the Walmart Data Ventures business that sells shopper insight to suppliers. In April, Walmart opened Scintilla data to media partners through an API, with early partners including Pacvue, Flywheel and mPhasize.
Walmart and Amazon, side by side
Amazon moved first on price. On November 2, Amazon put a $25 price on a Thanksgiving meal built around an 8 lb Butterball frozen turkey, available until November 26. The two offers are structured differently, and neither retailer publishes a per-item price.
Walmart's bird is about 69% heavier, at 13.5 lb against 8 lb. Amazon's promotional frozen-turkey price of $0.79 a pound is 18 cents below the $0.97 rate in Walmart's footnote. Walmart's offer also runs almost a month longer, through Christmas Eve.
Why this matters for marketers
The basket is a price point; the post is about what goes around it. A fixed $35 for a recognizable meal gives shoppers a reason to open the app or visit a store, and the additions are where the order grows. Walmart's results point the same way: in its fiscal second quarter, Sparky users spent 40% more per order, a lift in average order value that Walmart attributes to its shopping assistant. Sparky's own holiday update in late October added recipe suggestions with ingredients, including a Friendsgiving for 15 on a $125 budget. The November 17 post follows the same pattern in editorial form.
The choice of products is the second point. Every add-on named is a Walmart label. For national food brands, that is a holiday merchandising slot on Walmart's own newsroom in which none of them appears, while their products sit inside the basket at the fixed price. Walmart does not say whether Butterball, Kraft Heinz's Stove Top or Campbell's contribute to the basket's price, a form of trade promotion common in grocery. The tilt towards store brands is not new. In January, PPC Land reported that Kraft Mac & Cheese's US category share had fallen to 39% from 45% in 2022, with buyers moving to cheaper store brands such as Walmart's Great Value.
Advertising is the third. Grocery brings frequent visits, and frequent visits are what a retail media network sells. Walmart's global advertising business grew 38% in that quarter, and Walmart Connect in the US grew 43% excluding VIZIO. Walmart Connect said in January that it had tested ad formats inside Sparky in the fall of 2025. The post itself carries no sponsored label and no advertising; it is Walmart's own content promoting Walmart's own brands.
