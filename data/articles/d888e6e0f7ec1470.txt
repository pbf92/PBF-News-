TITLE: Franklin Graham says Christmas shoeboxes have shared Gospel with millions of children
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

US evangelist Franklin Graham says more than 244 million Christmas shoebox gifts have helped share the Christian message with children around the world over the past 33 years.
The president of Samaritan’s Purse, which runs Operation Christmas Child, said the charity was grateful to the volunteers and donors who had helped deliver the gifts.
“We thank God for the opportunities that 244 million Operation Christmas Child shoeboxes have given us to share the Gospel with children around the world over the last 33 years,” he said.
The scheme began in 1993, when Graham was asked to provide Christmas presents for children affected by the Bosnian War. Around 11,000 boxes were collected that first year.
This year, the charity hopes to collect another 12.8 million shoeboxes, filled with toys, stationery and other small gifts for children in more than 175 countries and territories.
The UK is among 11 countries involved in collecting boxes, alongside the United States, Australia, Canada and Germany.
Graham said: "Christmas is about giving. It’s about God giving us the greatest gift—His Son, Jesus Christ. That’s why we tell the children who receive these shoeboxes the Good News of Jesus Christ and give these gifts in His Name.”
The charity says 52 million children have also taken part in its 12-lesson Christian discipleship course, The Greatest Journey, since 2009. More than 27 million have made decisions for Christ, according to the organisation.
Among the stories shared by the charity is that of nine-year-old Tommy, from the remote Fijian island of Kadavu. His family could not afford the shoes he wanted, but he found the same pair inside his shoebox. He later told his father that Jesus had given him the shoes and invited him to church.
In the Amazon, the charity has also helped translate a Christian storybook into the language of the Chapra people, so local believers can share the Gospel with children in their own language.
