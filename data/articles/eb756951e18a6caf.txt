Michael Burry Halves SMH, Nvidia Puts but Keeps Nasdaq 100 Bet
Michael Burry is scaling back his bearish semiconductor bets while retaining Nasdaq 100 puts, according to Michael Burry Stock Tracker post on X on Friday. SMH was down over 9% in regular trading Friday.
The investor known for predicting the 2008 financial crisis sold 50% of his put options on the VanEck Semiconductor ETF SMH and Nvidia Corp. NVDA, while holding on to his Nasdaq 100 puts expiring in April 2027, according to the tracker’s Friday post. Separately, Burry said Thursday that he had "tried to warn" investors about Nvidia and other AI-related stocks.
The distinction matters for ETF investors. The reported trades reduce Burry’s semiconductor-specific bearish positions while retaining a position that could benefit from declines in the broader technology-heavy Nasdaq 100.
Smaller Semiconductor Bets, Not an All-Clear
SMH offers concentrated exposure to the companies driving the artificial intelligence investment boom, including Nvidia, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. TSM, Broadcom Inc. AVGO, Advanced Micro Devices Inc. AMD and Micron Technology Inc. MU.
Nvidia is also one of SMH’s largest holdings, making Burry’s simultaneous reduction in both bearish positions particularly notable.
The reported sales leave part of each put position in place. The tracker’s text does not explain Burry’s reasons or establish whether the trades were profitable.
What the Nasdaq 100 Puts Mean for QQQ Investors
The tracker also reported that Burry still holds his Nasdaq 100 puts expiring in April 2027.
Unlike SMH, which concentrates on semiconductor companies, the Invesco QQQ Trust, Series 1 QQQ tracks a broader basket of large-cap non-financial Nasdaq-listed companies, including Apple Inc. AAPL, Microsoft Corp. MSFT, Amazon.com Inc. AMZN and Nvidia.
The tracker also reported that Burry increased his MercadoLibre MELI shares by 20%, while selling 30% of his Zoetis ZTS calls.
The update does not establish that proceeds from the put sales funded the MercadoLibre purchases.
