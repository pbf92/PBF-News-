TITLE: Node-RADS Score of Two Balances Breast Cancer Lymph Node Metastasis Detection
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and meta-analysis assessing the diagnostic accuracy of the Node Reporting and Data System has established that a score threshold of two delivers the most balanced diagnostic performance when evaluating lymph node metastasis in breast cancer. Staging regional lymph nodes preoperatively remains an indispensable clinical component in establishing tumor stage, determining surgical trajectories such as axillary lymph node dissection, and tailoring systemic adjuvant treatments. The Node Reporting and Data System, designated as Node-RADS and originally proposed in 2021 by Elsholtz and colleagues, standardizes the risk stratification of lymph node malignancy on computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging by systematically scoring both size and non-size anatomical characteristics, including internal border definition, configuration, and nodal texture.
Published by researchers Xinran Zhang, Yinbin Zhu, Xianxin Yan, and Min Ma, the comprehensive evaluation synthesized evidence across four principal biomedical databases—PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science—spanning from their inception up to October 15, 2026. The investigators adhered strictly to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines and prospectively registered the research protocol with PROSPERO under registration identifier CRD420261278918. Breast cancer and thyroid cancer were examined alongside one another because both disease processes are frequently co-managed within unified breast and thyroid surgical specialties in select healthcare environments; nevertheless, the analytical synthesis evaluated each cancer type independently, and data derived from thyroid cancer cohorts were not combined with pooled breast cancer metrics.
Rigorous pre-established criteria directed study eligibility, mandating that enrolled clinical investigations assess pathologically confirmed breast or thyroid cancer patients using Node-RADS on computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging against surgical pathological reference standards. Diagnostic test designs and comparative evaluations were required to document empirical sensitivity, specificity, and receiver operating characteristic curves, or to furnish full two-by-two contingency tables encompassing true positives, false positives, true negatives, and false negatives. Of 521 initial bibliographical records retrieved across the electronic searches, 133 duplicates were removed and 359 citations were excluded during title and abstract screening. Following the assessment of 29 full-text articles, 18 publications were excluded due to incompatible document types, lack of independent diagnostic parameters, or ultrasound use, ultimately leaving 11 published diagnostic studies for analytical appraisal.
Quality assessment executed with the QUADAS-2 instrument demonstrated that the methodological rigor across the 11 included studies published between 2024 and 2026 was generally solid, showing no overall high risk of bias or severe applicability concerns. Across all studies, the index test domain was judged as having an unclear risk of bias because original publications lacked explicit descriptions of blinded reading procedures. In the applicability assessment, ten of eleven investigations exhibited low risk across patient selection and index test domains, whereas one cohort study by Kim published in 2025 presented incomplete data concerning patient selection and reference standard domains. Patient populations in the included studies spanned ages from 22 to 96 years across eight breast cancer cohorts and three thyroid cancer cohorts.
To quantitatively synthesize diagnostic performance parameters across varying scoring cutoffs, the researchers implemented a bivariate random-effects model using Stata version 15.0 to calculate pooled sensitivity, specificity, and the area under the summary receiver operating characteristic curve. In breast cancer cohorts, the diagnostic performance of Node-RADS exhibited marked threshold dependence as scoring cutoffs moved along the five-point ordinal scale. When a threshold of one was applied, the system produced its highest sensitivity at 0.83 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.66 to 0.92, accompanied by a specificity of 0.77 and an area under the curve of 0.85. Sensitivity analyses that sequentially omitted influential outlier studies, notably a 2025 cohort by Liao, adjusted the pooled sensitivity to 0.86 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.70 to 0.94, while pooled specificity shifted to 0.74 and the area under the curve was 0.84.
Adopting a Node-RADS cutoff of two achieved the optimal diagnostic balance in breast cancer evaluation, yielding an initial pooled sensitivity of 0.65 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.51 to 0.76, a specificity of 0.93 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.89 to 0.96, and an area under the curve of 0.91. Following the exclusion of the Liao study in sensitivity analyses, point-estimate stability improved, resulting in a refined pooled sensitivity of 0.67 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.53 to 0.79, an unchanged pooled specificity of 0.93 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.89 to 0.96, and an area under the curve reaching 0.92 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.89 to 0.94. The investigators emphasized that this threshold maintains elevated specificity while preserving clinically serviceable sensitivity.
Advancing the threshold criteria to higher categorical tiers caused diagnostic sensitivity to contract substantially in exchange for near-total diagnostic specificity. At a cutoff of three, pooled sensitivity declined to 0.43 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.33 to 0.54, while specificity rose to 0.97 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.94 to 0.99, and the summary area under the curve registered at 0.89. Sequential removal of three influential investigations—specifically cohorts by Jia, Liao, and the invasive ductal carcinoma subgroup of Kim—yielded a sensitivity of 0.43 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.27 to 0.60, a specificity of 0.98 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.92 to 1.00, and an area under the curve of 0.86. At a cutoff of four, baseline sensitivity dropped sharply to 0.20 while specificity reached 0.99; however, leave-one-out sensitivity testing produced extreme fluctuations in the area under the curve between 0.73 and 0.79, and statistical models frequently failed to converge, prompting the authors to conclude that meta-analytic pooling at cutoff four lacked mathematical stability and should only be described qualitatively.
The meta-analysis uncovered significant associations between radiologist reading experience and diagnostic sensitivity when evaluating scans under the optimal cutoff of two. Subgroup evaluations classified interpreting radiologists into three operational experience categories: less than five years, five to nine years, and ten or more years of practice. Radiologists in the least experienced category achieved a pooled sensitivity of 0.53 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.31 to 0.74, a specificity of 0.93, and an area under the curve of 0.91. Interpreters with five to nine years of experience demonstrated a comparable pooled sensitivity of 0.55 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.31 to 0.77, a specificity of 0.93, and an area under the curve of 0.92. In marked contrast, readers possessing ten or more years of professional interpretation experience demonstrated a significantly higher pooled sensitivity of 0.71 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.54 to 0.84, a specificity of 0.95 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.91 to 0.98, and an area under the curve of 0.95 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.92 to 0.96.
Data synthesized for thyroid cancer remained strictly qualitative and descriptive due to the identification of only three eligible clinical cohorts. In a 2025 study by Xiang evaluating central lymph nodes, a cutoff of two yielded a sensitivity of 64.5 percent and a specificity of 84.8 percent, whereas a 2026 study by Tang reported a sensitivity of 80.7 percent and a specificity of 74.5 percent at the same cutoff. At a cutoff of three, Tang documented a sensitivity of 51.4 percent and a specificity of 89.4 percent, while an automated computed tomography deep-learning evaluation by Yu in 2024 reported a sensitivity of 84.8 percent and a specificity of 76.0 percent. When threshold criteria were elevated to four, Xiang reported an lateral lymph node specificity of 93.3 percent and a sensitivity of 55.0 percent, while Tang documented a specificity of 95.7 percent and a sensitivity of 21.2 percent. Because these cohorts exhibited divergent cutoff criteria, evaluated disparate cervical compartments, and incorporated automated artificial intelligence modeling rather than uniform manual scoring by radiologists, the authors refrained from calculating pooled summary estimates.
The authors concluded that Node-RADS provides an effective, standardized framework that converts complex morphologic nodal features into reproducible diagnostic scores, highlighting a cutoff of two as the most balanced threshold for clinical decision-making in breast cancer. However, because the pooled sensitivity of 0.67 at this threshold leaves approximately one-third of malignant lymph nodes undetected, negative scoring determinations cannot be relied upon in isolation and require integrated correlative evaluation with ultrasound, magnetic resonance imaging, and histopathology. Substantial residual heterogeneity persisted throughout the meta-analysis due to variations in retrospective study designs, breast cancer tumor histology, prior neoadjuvant chemotherapy exposure, and radiologist training, underscoring the urgent need for large-sample prospective trials to refine scoring criteria across breast and thyroid malignancies.
