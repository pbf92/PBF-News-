TITLE: Smoking in, vaping out: The social trend worrying health experts
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Smoking is becoming more socially acceptable while disapproval of vaping is growing, new international research on social trends has found.
The University of Queensland-led research studied smoking and vaping trends among almost 20,000 adults who smoked daily in Australia, Canada, England and the United States, finding vaping has become less socially accepted than smoking.
Dr Giang Vu from UQ’s National Centre for Youth Substance Use Research said researchers examined trends between 2016 and 2022, finding similar results across the 4 countries despite different vaping regulations.
“These trends were broadly similar regardless of smoking or vaping policies” Dr Vu said.
“Opinions around the 2 products have moved in opposite directions – anti-smoking norms weakened while anti-vaping norms strengthened.
“This pattern is concerning from a health perspective.
“Social norms are linked to whether people quit smoking or take up vaping, so these shifts matter for public health.
"Vaping, though not harmless, poses fewer health risks than smoking.
“A weakening of disapproval for smoking may reduce the motivation to quit among people at highest risk.
“A growing disapproval of vaping may discourage people who smoke from switching to a less harmful alternative.”
Dr Vu said national tobacco and vaping policies operated within a “global information environment’’ and attitudes could be shaped by media coverage and events like the COVID-19 pandemic and a 2019 lung injury outbreak in the United States linked to e-cigarette or vaping product use.
“The findings point to a need for public health communication that conveys the risks of both smoking and vaping transparently and accurately,” she said.
“This would allow people who smoke to make informed choices, while continuing to protect young people who have never smoked.”
The research examined data from the International Tobacco Control Four Country Smoking and Vaping Surveys.
Dr Vu said it was important to note the study described changes in attitudes and perceptions over time and did not measure quitting or switching of products or establish cause and effect.
