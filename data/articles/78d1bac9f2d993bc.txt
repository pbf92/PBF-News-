TITLE: CLL-1 CAR T-Cell Therapy Shows Early Marrow Responses in Acute Myeloid Leukemia
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Early-phase clinical evidence indicates that chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy directed against CLL-1, also designated CLEC12A, can induce bone marrow responses in patients suffering from relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia. According to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in BMC Cancer, pooled quantitative evaluations show measurable anti-leukemic activity across independent clinical cohorts, alongside notable rates of treatment-related toxicities.
CLL-1 represents a compelling therapeutic target in acute myeloid leukemia because the surface antigen is expressed on leukemic blasts and leukemic stem-cell populations while relatively sparing healthy normal hematopoietic stem cells. Despite this biological rationale, prior meta-analyses evaluating chimeric antigen receptor cellular therapies in acute myeloid leukemia were not target-specific, leaving true clinical efficacy and safety uncertain across a fragmented and partly overlapping published literature.
To establish a focused evidence base, investigators conducted a systematic literature search across PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library through August 31, 2026. The search strategy was supplemented by targeted screening of Google Scholar and conference repositories from the American Society of Hematology and the European Hematology Association. Study selection, data extraction, quality appraisal, and adjudication of overlapping patient cohorts were performed independently by two reviewers under a prospectively registered PROSPERO protocol.
The systematic review identified eleven clinical studies representing a total of 130 unique treated patients. From this evidence base, seven independent cohorts comprising 82 patients receiving CLL-1-directed cellular infusions contributed data to quantitative synthesis using random-effects meta-analytic models.
Across the quantitative cohorts, the pooled strict remission rate, defined as complete remission or complete remission with incomplete hematologic recovery, reached 26.9 percent, with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning 4.6 to 73.6 percent. When investigators calculated a broader composite response metric that incorporated morphologic leukemia-free state, the pooled proportion rose to 51.2 percent, with a confidence interval between 17.8 and 83.6 percent. In addition, among five cohorts reporting measurable residual disease evaluations, the pooled negative response rate was 45.8 percent, with a confidence interval of 28.5 to 64.3 percent.
Treatment-related adverse events occurred frequently across the analyzed patient groups. Cytokine release syndrome of any grade developed in 86.6 percent of recipients, with a confidence interval spanning 70.8 to 94.5 percent, while severe cytokine release syndrome of grade 3 or higher occurred in 26.6 percent of patients. Severe immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome of grade 3 or higher was documented in 8.2 percent of participants, with a confidence interval of 2.4 to 24.5 percent.
Following immunotherapy, a pooled proportion of 36.0 percent of patients, with a confidence interval ranging from 12.9 to 68.1 percent, subsequently received allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation. The study authors noted that proceeding to subsequent transplantation reflected protocol- and practice-dependent treatment pathways rather than serving as a direct metric of cellular therapeutic efficacy. Furthermore, the review observed that higher protocol-defined cell infusion doses were not consistently associated with superior clinical response rates.
The review concluded that while early-phase investigations demonstrate that CLL-1-directed cellular therapy can induce marrow remissions in relapsed or refractory disease, available studies remain limited by small, uncontrolled cohorts, substantial heterogeneity, and statistical imprecision. The authors emphasized that these constraints preclude definitive conclusions regarding clinical efficacy or comparative safety, highlighting the necessity for larger prospective studies to assess response durability, long-term outcomes, and transplantation integration.
