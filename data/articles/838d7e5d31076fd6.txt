TITLE: Church of England bans vicar for life over abuse and sexual misconduct
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The Church of England has banned a vicar from ministry after being found to have coercively controlled his former wife and had a sexual relationship with a vulnerable parishioner.
Complaints against Rev Dr Johnny Douglas were first raised in 2024, leading to his suspension as vicar of St Paul's Church in Swanley, Kent, and a formal clergy disciplinary investigation.
An independent tribunal found the 51-year-old had subjected his former wife to emotional and financial abuse over a period of more than two years.
It also found he had taken advantage of a parishioner, identified in the judgment as XY, who had approached him for pastoral support as her marriage was breaking down.
The tribunal said Douglas initially told the woman she had misunderstood his kindness, but later began a sexual relationship with her.
In its 55-page judgment, the panel said the relationship was not genuine on his part, describing how he had been "blowing hot and cold according to his whim".
It also criticised his involvement in other aspects of the woman's life, including plans to buy and redevelop a house, saying he had blurred appropriate boundaries.
The tribunal concluded: "She was clearly vulnerable and he took advantage of that."
In a witness statement, the woman said the impact of his behaviour had been "significant". She said she had left the church and the local community and lost several friendships.
She also described feeling isolated because Douglas had insisted their relationship remain secret, leaving her without anyone to turn to for support.
The tribunal ordered his immediate removal from office and prohibited him from ministry for life. The decision remains subject to an application for permission to appeal.
Douglas was ordained as a deacon in 2011 and as a priest the following year. He became vicar of St Paul's in January 2018, overseeing two churches, including St Peter's in nearby Hextable.
Due to the ongoing legal process, the Diocese of Rochester is unable to comment further at this time
