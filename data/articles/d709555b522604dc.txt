TITLE: Unstable Cliff Blasted at Gador Beach in Hadera
OUTLET: JFeed

The Israel Nature and Parks Authority on Thursday morning, November 26, completed the first stage of a special engineering operation at the Gador Beach Nature Reserve in Hadera, deliberately bringing down part of the kurkar sandstone cliff at Tel Gador in a controlled explosion.
The authority said the operation was meant to address a real risk that a large rock mass at the top of the cliff could collapse without any warning, endangering hikers and visitors on the strip of beach below.
The upper section of the cliff was brought down under the close supervision of the Israel Antiquities Authority and in coordination with the Hadera Municipality. The area was fenced off during the work, warning signs were posted, and the public was barred from entering the site. Further work to stabilize the remaining cliff is planned, along with a new accessible hiking trail at the top of Tel Gador.
"It is important to remember that a similar danger exists along many stretches of coastline between Herzliya and Hadera, and people should keep away from both the edge of the cliff and its base," said Guy Levian, deputy director of the authority's marine unit.
The Gador Beach Nature Reserve is one of the most striking stretches of coastline in the Sharon region and draws hikers, families and many other visitors. The sandstone cliffs that tower over the beach pose a significant hazard, however. Blocks of rock and sand, sometimes weighing hundreds of kilograms, can break away and fall all at once with no prior signs.
The danger is especially acute on narrow sections of beach that run directly beneath the cliff, where visitors have no way to move quickly out of the collapse zone. In March, the authority put up additional warning signs along a roughly 300-meter stretch of the reserve between Mifratz HaSinim ("Chinese Bay") and Mifratz 70 ("Bay 70"), warning the public about the risk of collapse and banning passage through the dangerous section.
The authority also produced an awareness video and circulated it on social media, highlighting the dangers of spending time beneath the sandstone cliffs. Despite the warnings, the authority said, visitors have been seen over the years lingering under the cliffs and ignoring safety signs, behavior it warned could end in disaster.
Cliff collapses are a familiar phenomenon along Israel's coastline. They have become more frequent in recent years, mainly in the Sharon region, Netanya and Tel Aviv. The collapses are caused by several factors, including waves striking the base of the cliffs, ongoing erosion, unstable slopes, and surface runoff. On February 21, a cliff collapsed at Sidna Ali Beach in Herzliya, once again showing how real the danger is.
The Israel Nature and Parks Authority stressed that the public should not approach the cliffs, neither at the top nor at the base, and should follow posted signs and on-site safety instructions. Visitors are asked not to enter beach sections marked with no-entry signs and to use only marked, designated trails.
