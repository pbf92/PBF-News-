TITLE: Microsoft cuts ads per page 35% in its new Discover feed
OUTLET: PPC Land

Microsoft Advertising today said ads in AI Overviews on Bing are available in every market except China, served through the campaigns advertisers already run, and that its new Discover content feed on Edge and Windows shows 35% fewer ads per page than the classic MSN experience it replaces. The two changes open a November product roundup that also adds URL inclusions to AI Max, makes labels for AI-generated images and video generally available and widens what advertisers can ask of the company's MCP server.
The roundup appeared today on the Microsoft Advertising blog under the title "Put AI to work engaging customers in more places: What's new in Microsoft Advertising this November." It carries no byline. The post is split into two parts. "Show up in more places" covers new inventory, and "Put AI to Work" covers controls and tools. A final item recaps the bidding model upgrade Microsoft announced a day earlier. The opening section promises "4 new ways" to reach holiday shoppers, "mainly through campaigns you already run," but lists three: ads in AI Overviews on Bing, Discover and Xbox Video for Brands.
In Short
Microsoft is now placing search ads next to the AI-written answers at the top of Bing results almost everywhere, and has launched a new news and video feed in Edge and Windows that it says carries about a third fewer ads per page. This matters to anyone buying search or audience ads from Microsoft, because the AI Overview ads run through existing campaigns with no separate setup, and the feed figures, while favourable, come only from Microsoft's own data. Advertisers also get a way to point AI Max at chosen pages and a system for labelling AI-made images and video, but Microsoft is clear that deciding whether a label is legally required stays with the advertiser.
What Microsoft changed
The post bundles six items, four of them new and two of them recaps of announcements made earlier in November.
Ads beside Bing's AI answers
Microsoft frames the change around a shift in how search works. "Search is changing from a list of links to a faster path to an answer," the post states. "AI Overviews on Bing pull information from across the web into a clear summary." It argues that the summaries expose demand that a keyword alone does not: "Those answers often reveal needs beyond the exact words someone typed. Each one is a new chance to offer a useful product, service, or next step."
The post places the format in a sequence. "Ads in AI Overviews on Bing are the latest step in bringing ads to AI experiences, building on ads in Copilot," it reads. Its example is a traveller asking Bing about a trip to Snowdonia National Park, where the AI Overview "might show a sample itinerary and travel tips." A hotel advertiser's ad, according to Microsoft, "can appear right next to that answer."
The operational detail is short. "There's no separate campaign set-up required," the post says. "Ads in AI Overviews run through the campaigns you already use, including Performance Max and AI Max, which are the most effective way to show up in AI experiences in more relevant ways." That last clause is Microsoft's own assessment of its automated campaign types, not a measured result. The post then states the scope: "Ads in AI Overviews are now available in all markets except China."
What the post does not include is a launch date. Search Engine Roundtable reported on October 30, 2026, that a user had posted a screenshot of sponsored results inside Bing's AI Overviews, which the site said Bing had adopted as the new name for its Copilot Search box a few months earlier. Today's post describes the format to advertisers but does not say when serving began or whether it was tested in a subset of markets first.
The 21% figure
The post supports the format with one number. "Microsoft data shows a 21% increase in purchases after a Copilot interaction," it says. The footnote on that sentence reads: "Microsoft internal data, global Edge users, December 21-31, 2024." The figure therefore comes from an 11-day holiday window nearly two years before the post, describes Copilot interactions rather than ads in AI Overviews, and compares purchases against an unstated baseline. It does not show that the interaction caused the purchases.
Microsoft has published stronger-sounding Copilot numbers before. In August 2025, Microsoft research found ads in Copilot drew 73% higher click-through rates and 16% stronger conversion rates than traditional search ads, based on data from November 2024 to May 2025. Neither study addresses AI Overviews on Bing specifically.
How the scope compares with Google
The geographic reach stands out against Google's pace. Google's ads in AI Overviews began in the United States, and in December 2025 Google expanded AI Overview ads to 11 more countries: Australia, Canada, India, Indonesia, Kenya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines and Singapore. Microsoft's "all markets except China" is broader on paper, though the post lists no markets and gives no figures for how many Bing queries now return an AI Overview. Reach on a map is not the same as reach in impressions.
Discover: fewer ads, by Microsoft's count
The second item is a new home for Microsoft's content feed. The post calls Discover "Microsoft's new, AI-powered content feed on the Microsoft Edge desktop new tab page and Windows." It describes a redesign that "clears the clutter and puts discovery first," organising content "into three distinct zones: catch up on what's happening, explore editor-picked stories, and dive into your interests - with AI making the mix more personal." Behind it, according to Microsoft, are "trusted publishers and a network of more than 3,500 creators."
Discover is not new to readers. Microsoft's Edge team introduced it in a blog post on November 5, 2026, which put the publisher count at 1,500 premium publishers alongside the 3,500-plus creators, said the feed opens on each new tab in Edge and from the Discover dashboard on the Windows taskbar, and said mobile and MSN.com versions would follow in the coming months, with the Bing.com homepage among later surfaces. That consumer-facing post did not mention advertising. Today's post is the advertiser pitch.
Microsoft attaches three figures to the feed, covering ad density, viewability and click-through rates.
"For your brand, less clutter means more room to stand out," the post reads. "Compared with the classic MSN experience, Discover shows 35% fewer ads per page. Early results are promising. Ads in Discover see 35% higher viewability and 8-12% higher click-through rates."
The three figures need reading together. Fewer ads on a page usually means each remaining ad is more likely to be seen and clicked, so higher viewability and click-through rates are in line with what a lighter page would be expected to produce. The post does not say which viewability standard it applies, whether a third party measured it, or how many impressions the October comparison covered. It also does not say what happened to total ad impressions. A page with 35% fewer slots produces fewer impressions per page view unless people open more pages, and Microsoft gives no page view figures.
Nor does the post explain how Discover inventory is bought. Its only instruction is to "Talk to your Microsoft Advertising account team to learn more." It does not say whether Audience Ads or Performance Max campaigns already serve into the feed, or whether prices change. Microsoft has sold native placements on MSN since 2018, when Bing Ads moved into display with native ads on MSN and Outlook through what became the Microsoft Audience Network, with Bing search ads filling native slots when no other advertiser did.
Xbox Video for Brands, in brief
The third placement is a recap. "Earlier this month, we introduced XBOX Video for Brands," the post says, describing "Full-screen, non-skippable video ads" that "play right before a cloud-gaming session and help eligible players unlock access, while leaving active gameplay uninterrupted." When the format was introduced on November 12, the terms meant that Xbox cloud players face 3 minutes of ads for each free 30-minute session. Today's post pitches it to buyers of premium video and connected TV as a way "to reach the XBOX gaming audience with video creative you already have." It adds no prices, markets or audience sizes to the November 12 announcement.
URL inclusions for AI Max
The "Put AI to Work" section opens with a control for AI Max, Microsoft's automated layer for Search campaigns. The post concedes a condition before it describes the feature. "Automation only works when you can steer it and see how it's working," it says, adding that Microsoft has "been committed to bringing you the controls and transparency you need to guide AI Max for Search campaigns right from the start."
The new control extends final URL expansion, the AI Max function that sends a click to whichever page on an advertiser's site the system judges the best match, rather than the landing page set in the ad. "This month, we're adding URL inclusions to final URL expansion," the post reads. "URL inclusions help you specify the parts of your website that you want AI Max to use - both as a traffic destination as well as a resource to draw from for text customization and search term matching."
The second use matters as much as the first. AI Max does not only pick landing pages; it also writes ad text and matches search terms from site content, so the pages an advertiser includes become source material for the copy as well as destinations. Microsoft's example is a home goods store that wants holiday shoppers on its gift guides: "Add your gift guide pages as URL inclusions to your holiday campaigns. AI Max can send relevant traffic there and pull ad text from those pages." URL inclusions "are rolling out now," according to the post.
The post does not say whether inclusions restrict final URL expansion to the listed pages or add those pages to the ones the system already finds, nor whether they are set at campaign or ad group level. Its example refers to campaigns. Google's equivalent, by comparison, sits at ad group level: when Google said it would sunset Dynamic Search Ads in favour of AI Max in May 2026, its Ads Liaison said URL exclusions were managed at campaign level and URL inclusions at ad group level.
The feature fills a gap in Microsoft's own rollout. When Microsoft Advertising rolled AI Max out across all accounts globally on August 19, 2026, final URL expansion came with a control to exclude specific URLs but no way to include them. Less than three weeks before that, Microsoft had said Predictive Matching would give way to AI Max, without an effective date. The post also points back to October, when Microsoft let advertisers set up to 40 messaging guidelines for AI-written ads in an open pilot for Performance Max and Search campaigns with AI Max. "Last month, we added messaging guidelines," today's post says. "They let you use plain language instructions to keep AI-generated ad copy on-brand and on-message."
AI content labels become generally available
The second control concerns disclosure. "As AI helps you show in more places, it's also critical to maintain trust wherever you appear and be open about when assets are AI-generated," the post says. It gives a legal reason as well as a reputational one: "Governments are also passing laws that may require labels on some AI-generated content." According to Microsoft, "AI content transparency controls are now generally available in Microsoft Advertising. They help you declare AI-generated image and video content and manage visible labels across supported ad formats."
