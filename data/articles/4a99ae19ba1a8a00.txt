TITLE: Pediatric Elbow Surgery Lowers Nonunion Risk but Increases Secondary Operations
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Surgical fixation substantially lowers the incidence of bone nonunion in pediatric medial epicondyle fractures compared to non-surgical treatment, but it markedly increases the probability of patients needing additional surgery and leads to worse cosmetic outcomes, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in Langenbeck’s Archives of Surgery. The study synthesized comparative evidence to clarify the therapeutic balance between operative stabilization and conservative care in children, addressing ongoing debates surrounding these elbow injuries.
The optimal management protocol for pediatric medial epicondyle fractures has long presented a controversial dilemma, particularly in cases involving displaced fractures without joint incarceration. Historically, non-surgical approaches have served as the conventional standard of care. However, interest in operative fixation has grown in recent years due to emerging evidence suggesting favorable outcomes from surgical treatment. To address this persistent controversy and determine optimal therapy for young patients, the investigators conducted a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis comparing surgical versus non-surgical management in pediatric populations.
To capture all relevant literature, the investigators carried out an extensive systematic search across PubMed, the Cochrane Library, Google Scholar, and the ClinicalTrials.gov registry from inception through April 2026. The search adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines under a protocol registered with PROSPERO (CRD420261393871). The eligibility criteria admitted randomized controlled trials and observational comparative studies evaluating pediatric cohorts undergoing surgical fixation or non-surgical management for medial epicondyle fractures.
The final screening identified six comparative investigations meeting all criteria, comprising a combined cohort of 564 pediatric patients. Among these included studies, two were randomized controlled trials and four were cohort designs. To evaluate the pooled data, the authors performed statistical analyses using a random-effects model implemented in RevMan 5.4. Methodological quality and potential risk of bias were systematically appraised using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool for randomized trials and the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale for observational cohort studies, while the overall certainty of evidence was evaluated using the GRADE approach.
The meta-analytic findings revealed clear trade-offs between the two treatment strategies regarding union rates and subsequent operations. Surgical fixation demonstrated a pronounced advantage in bony healing, significantly reducing the relative risk of fracture nonunion to 0.09 (95% confidence interval: 0.02 to 0.39; p = 0.001) compared with non-surgical care. However, this structural benefit was accompanied by a major increase in secondary interventions. Pediatric patients receiving surgical fixation experienced a significantly higher likelihood of requiring additional surgery, yielding a risk ratio of 13.37 (95% confidence interval: 5.20 to 34.35; p < 0.00001).
Patient-reported visual satisfaction also differed considerably, distinctly favoring non-operative management. Cosmetic evaluation using visual analogue scale scores demonstrated that surgical treatment resulted in significantly worse cosmetic scores, with a mean difference of -8.56 points (95% confidence interval: -12.66 to -4.46; p < 0.0001). In contrast, functional recovery metrics failed to show any demonstrable superiority for either pathway. Comparative assessments showed no statistically significant differences between groups across Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder and Hand scores, pediatric quality of life measured by PedsQL scores, or deficits in elbow flexion and elbow extension. Objective assessments of valgus instability likewise revealed no significant differences.
The authors noted that the effect of surgical fixation on functional recovery remains uncertain compared with non-surgical treatment. Consequently, they emphasized that clinical decisions should avoid a uniform approach and instead be individualized based on clinical presentation and patient-specific factors.
