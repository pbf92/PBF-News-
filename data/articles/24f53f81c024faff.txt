TITLE: Referees' chief backs Leny Yoro red card as Manchester United win appeal
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

Referees' chief Howard Webb maintains the Leny Yoro sending-off was "certainly not wrong" - despite Manchester United successfully appealing the red card.
United defender Yoro was dismissed at Anfield on Sunday after fouling Liverpool striker Alexander Isak, with referee Sam Barrott showing a red card as he judged it to be the denial of an obvious goalscoring opportunity.
United manager Mauricio Pochettino was deeply frustrated with the call, and the club lodged an appeal - with the Football Association confirming on Tuesday that both the suspension and red card would be rescinded.
However, Webb, Chief Refereeing Officer of Pro Ref, stated on the Mic'D Up show that he didn't believe it was a clear error and commended the official for his decisiveness.
Webb said, via The Mirror: "Firstly, I want to say, I think this is a really decisive decision by the referee, Sam Barrott. We know it's clearly a foul. I don't think there's any dispute about that. And we also know that it's outside the penalty area. We're thinking about the sanction, aren't we? That's why we're talking about this.
"Is it a denial of an obvious goal-scoring opportunity? Four considerations officials have to think about. Direction of play: It's definitely going towards the goal. It's not going out wide. Proximity from goal: Well, it's just outside the penalty area. The location of other defenders: Well, there's only Yoro there. The keeper is moving back towards his own goal.
"He has to put the brakes on and possibly come back out. The reason we're talking about it is the fourth consideration: That's control, or likelihood of keeping on or gaining control of the ball. Isak's got control of the ball, he's got possession, he's got the touch, he knocks it past Yoro. He's moving at pace at that moment, and a foul comes in and knocks him down.

"And there's a question mark, I guess, around whether or not he would get onto that ball before Senne Lammens. Now, in the moment, the referee looked at it and felt he would.
"That was the referee's call made on the field, and then the VAR checks that to see whether that decision made on the field was clearly and obviously incorrect, and formed the opinion that it wasn't, that if there was any doubt, then it was doubt.
"It wasn't going to be something that was clearly and obviously wrong, and therefore allowed that decisive on-field decision to stand. And I think by and large, most people would see that as a correct decision, with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball. The alternative view is that Lammens might get there first, but I think it's a little bit debatable, I guess. Certainly not clearly wrong."
