TITLE: X wins UK judgment against two users it says took £207,384 in creator pay
OUTLET: PPC Land

A London court has entered judgment for X against two people the platform accused of running a network of accounts to collect money from its creator payment programme. Master Teverson of the High Court entered the judgment on November 20, 2026, after neither Vivek Kumar Sen nor Zamyang Sherpa responded to the claim X filed in September. James Burnham, whom Decrypt and The Block identify as X's general counsel, made the ruling public in a post on X on November 24. How much the two must pay has not been decided.
In Short
A London court has ruled for X against two people it accused of using six linked accounts to collect about £207,384 from its creator pay programme between 2023 and 2026. The pair never answered the lawsuit, so the accusations were not tested at a trial, and the amount they owe will be set at a later hearing. For creators, brands and agencies, the case shows X treating faked engagement as fraud it will chase through the courts, not only as a reason to shut accounts down.
What the court decided
The judgment is a default judgment. That is the label English courts use when a defendant does not take part in a case: the claimant asks the court to rule without a trial, and the court does so because nobody has disputed the claim, not because a judge has weighed the evidence. Masters are High Court judges who handle many of these procedural decisions.
The opening of the order appeared as an image in Burnham's November 24 post. It names the court as the High Court of Justice, Business and Property Division, Business List, Rolls Building, London. It names the judge as "MASTER TEVERSON (SITTING IN RETIREMENT)" and carries the date "20 NOVEMBER 2026". The parties listed are X Internet Unlimited Company and X Corp. as claimants, and Mr Vivek Kumar Sen, Ms Zamyang Sherpa and "PERSONS UNKNOWN" as defendants.
The remaining terms of the order are known from news reports of the full document, which X published on its transparency site. According to crypto.news, X sent the claim papers to both named defendants by post and email on September 21, the court treated them as legally delivered on September 23, and X filed certificates confirming delivery on September 28. Neither defendant filed an acknowledgment of service or a defence, and X applied for default judgment on October 21, according to crypto.news and The Block.
Under the court rules of England and Wales, a defendant normally has 14 days from delivery of the detailed claim to file either a defence or an acknowledgment of service, the second of which buys a further 14 days to prepare the defence. Counting 14 days from September 23 gives October 7, a PPC Land calculation. X's application came two weeks after that.
The order does not set a sum. According to crypto.news, it enters judgment against both individuals with the amount of damages and interest left for the court to decide. That will happen at a separate disposal hearing, a short hearing held only to put a figure on what is owed. Crypto.news reported that about one hour has been set aside for it, that no date or time has been fixed, and that the court reserved the question of legal costs until then. X must serve witness statements supporting its claims for compensation and interest by 4 p.m. 14 days before the hearing, and the defendants have until 4 p.m. seven days before it to respond, according to the same report.
Either side can apply to vary or set aside the order within seven days of being served with it, according to The Block and Decrypt. The Block reported that the order was sealed, meaning formally stamped by the court, on November 23.
The judgment covers only the two named individuals. Crypto.news reported that it applies specifically to Sen and Sherpa, which leaves the "persons unknown" listed in the claim outside it for now.

How the programme worked, according to the claim
The Creator Revenue Sharing Programme began on or around July 28, 2023, according to the particulars of claim. It paid creators a share of X's revenue based on how much engagement their posts generated, and it was replaced by a new scheme, the Original Content Rewards programme, on or around September 7, 2026. The claim lists six conditions a participant had to meet:
- Hold a subscription to X Premium, Premium Business or Premium Organisations.
- Have posts that generated more than five million organic impressions in total over the previous three months.
- Have more than 500 verified followers.
- Keep the account compliant with the X User Agreement, including the Terms of Service and X's Rules and Policies.
- Agree to the Creator Revenue Sharing Terms, the Creator Monetization Standards and the Content Monetization Policies.
- Link the X account to Stripe in order to receive payments.
The terms changed several times while the six accounts were enrolled. X relies on versions dated July 27, 2023 and May 22, 2024, when the document was called the "Ads Revenue Sharing Terms", and on later versions dated November 8, 2024, March 15, 2025 and August 1, 2025.
What X says the network did
X's central allegation is that the six accounts were not six independent creators. The claim says they were run "as a single coordinated network for the purpose of manipulating engagement metrics to obtain payouts from the Programme". Posting the same material across several accounts, and having those accounts like and repost each other, would push up the engagement numbers on which payouts were calculated. Spreading activity across many accounts also meant that, if one account were suspended or restricted, "the remaining accounts in the network would continue to generate payments", according to the claim.
The particulars give dated examples, with screenshots:
- On October 10, 2025, @TrendingBitcoin and @saylordocs posted the same falling bitcoin price chart with the line "Like, if you are not selling #Bitcoin" two minutes apart. The screenshots show 1,446 likes on the first and 1,386 on the second.
- On November 3, 2025, @TrendingBitcoin, @Kalshibacktest, @Bitcoin_Teddy and @saylordocs posted the same phrase and image within seven minutes, between 11:10 and 11:17 by the timestamps shown.
- On April 20, 2026, @saylordocs and @PolyBackTest posted identical content within minutes of each other.
- On July 23, July 26 and August 3, 2026, @TrendingBitcoin, @Vivek4real_ and @Bitcoin_Teddy posted the same content within minutes of one another.
- On August 5, 2026, @Vivek4real_ and @TrendingBitcoin posted substantively similar content within 11 seconds.
- On August 13, 2026, @Vivek4real_, @saylordocs and @Bitcoin_Teddy replied to the same post within 31 seconds, with "Enjoyed listening to it!", "This is a must watch!" and "Great insights!".
Most of the accounts posted about bitcoin, and several crypto news sites described the case as a bot farm. The particulars do not use that phrase. They describe accounts run in coordination by people, and quote X's rules against both manual and automated manipulation.
X also alleges that the money trail was arranged to hide who was behind the accounts. The Stripe account attached to @Bitcoin_Teddy was registered in another person's name, but the bank account receiving its payments was in Sen's name and the email address on it was linked to Sen, according to the claim. The Stripe and bank accounts for @PolyBackTest were in Sherpa's name, with an email address linked to Sen. The claim says both defendants live in Preston, Lancashire, while X Premium subscriptions for @saylordocs and @TrendingBitcoin were paid with debit cards carrying billing addresses in Newark and Bear, Delaware.
The technical links X cites go beyond payments. According to the claim, the accounts were connected through "the same devices, software clients and other identifying information, including the same universally unique identifiers, device tokens, and cookies".
Two further allegations concern Sen directly. The claim says Sen used @Vivek4real_ to sell paid engagement services to third parties and to try to buy high-follower accounts. On December 18, 2025, while trying to buy another user's account, Sen wrote, according to the claim: "Can we continue on another channel, please as you haven't enabled encrypted chat and I don't want us to get in trouble for something X doesn't allow. If you can understand what I mean."
X suspended the accounts on August 18, 2026, "for coordinated revenue sharing fraud and platform manipulation", according to the claim. Nikita Bier, X's head of product, replied to Burnham's November post: "Incredible work James: From a user flagging it to a judgement in 3 months."
The rules X relies on
The claim quotes X's own rules at length. The most direct is a clause in the Creator Revenue Sharing Terms:
"If you are found to artificially inflate the number of views on your posts through promoted post, manual, automated, or other means, or manipulate the platform to alter the views on your posts, you will forfeit any amount due to you and you will be terminated from the Program."
The same terms let X "withhold, suspend, recoup or set-off revenue share payments" if it believes an account is in breach. Under the Authenticity Policy, which the claim says now combines what used to be three separate policies (Platform Manipulation and Spam, Misleading and Deceptive Identities, and Financial Scam), the list of conduct that is "Not Allowed" includes:
- "Operating multiple accounts that post substantially similar or identical content to one another"
- "coordinating to exchange engagement in any X features, such as Likes, Polls, Replies, Reposts, Lists, Views, or Follows"
- "Trading, buying, selling (either through monetary or virtual compensation) or soliciting access of X accounts"
The Content Monetization Policies add that "Engagement bait" and "Unoriginal or recycled content" are not eligible for payment.
What X is asking the court for
The particulars of claim rest on six legal grounds. In plain terms:
X asks for the payments to be handed back, for damages, for compensation for the trust claims, for restitution, for interest under section 35A of the Senior Courts Act 1981, and for costs. The claim was signed by the law firm Lewis Silkin LLP on September 17, 2026, and its statements of truth were signed for each X company by senior legal directors Diego de Lima Gualda and Adam Mehes.
