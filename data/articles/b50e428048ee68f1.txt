TITLE: 2 arrested in connection with sexual abuse in investigation into Presbyterian Church in Ireland safeguarding failures
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Two people from the Presbyterian Church in Ireland were arrested in connection with claims of sexual communication with a child and rape, as Police Service Northern Ireland (PSNI) investigate safeguarding failures.
PSNI has said that 168 people have directly contacted the force to report concerns, 51 more people than were reported to have approached PSNI at the start of September.
The police service has received a "significant volume" of documents which are under review, having started their investigation in November 2025.
Ten files have been submitted to the Crown Prosecution Service (CPS), for the CPS to decide whether they can charge individuals.
PSNI Assistant Chief Constable Davy Beck wants "anyone who has experienced harm, witnessed concerning behaviour or has information relating to safeguarding concerns" to approach PSNI.
Commissioner for Victims of Crime Northern Ireland, Geraldine Hanna, has also reiterated that victims still have the opportunity to come forward.
Fomer Moderator of the Presbyterian Church in Ireland, Rt Rev Dr Richard Murray, extended a public apology to victims in November 2025 speaking at an emergency General Assembly.
The Church has acknowledged serious safeguarding failures between 2009 to 2022.
He said last year: "It's necessary that we gather together in this special General Assembly to grieve and lament for those who have been hurt and harmed - and for their families who have to pick up the pieces.
"There will be things these good people have to live with for the rest of their lives. And to them we say sorry, that you have been hurt or harmed, or both, by our failings."
The former head of the child online safety group Child Exploitation and Online Protection (CEOP) Centre, Jim Gamble, was appointed to lead an independent external review of the Presbyterian Church in Ireland's safeguarding and governance framework in March.
Working alongside safeguarding group INEQE, Dr Murray said the review would be "victim focused and trauma informed".
