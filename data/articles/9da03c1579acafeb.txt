TITLE: Meta uses Muse AI agent to drive Instagram shopping conversions
OUTLET: Indian Television Dot Com

MUMBAI: Instagram’s saved posts are getting a second life, and Meta is hoping they will come with a shopping cart attached. The company’s AI agent Muse is helping users rediscover products they have bookmarked, nudging them from browsing to buying and giving Instagram advertisers another way to turn interest into sales.
Although Muse currently carries no advertisements of its own, the shopping features are already contributing to Meta’s advertising business by helping brands secure more conversions from campaigns running on Instagram, CNBC reported.
The strategy taps into a familiar Instagram habit: saving posts for later. Around 20 per cent of the platform’s daily active users save at least one post every day, according to a person familiar with the matter who spoke to CNBC on condition of anonymity. A significant proportion of these saved posts are advertisements and products available for purchase.
Muse is designed to make those saved items easier to revisit and act upon. Instead of leaving a product buried among dozens of bookmarked posts, users can use the AI agent to organise and rediscover things they have previously shown interest in. That gives shoppers a simpler route back to products they may have considered buying but never got around to purchasing.
For advertisers, the benefit lies in shortening the distance between product discovery and checkout. A senior Meta executive told CNBC that Muse is helping advertisers generate more conversions by guiding users back to products they have already viewed. The tool can therefore improve the returns on existing advertising expenditure without necessarily requiring brands to increase their campaign budgets.
In effect, Meta is using an existing consumer behaviour to strengthen the commercial value of its advertising ecosystem. Users save products because they want to return to them; Muse makes that return journey easier, while advertisers get another opportunity to convert an earlier expression of interest into a transaction.
The shopping opportunity is also reflected in how people are using the agent. Meta chief executive Mark Zuckerberg disclosed during the company’s latest earnings call that shopping accounts for 25 per cent of Muse usage, making it the platform’s most popular activity.
Zuckerberg also said the average purchase made through Muse exceeds $200, suggesting that the agent is facilitating transactions involving more than just low-value impulse buys.
Meta is looking to make money from these purchases directly as well. Zuckerberg indicated that the company intends to collect a small fee on transactions completed through Muse, potentially creating an additional revenue stream alongside its established advertising business.
The shopping interactions could also provide Meta with a clearer picture of consumer interests and purchasing preferences. Information about the products users explore and eventually buy could help the company sharpen advertising targeting on Instagram. Purchases completed through Muse would offer a further opportunity to earn transaction-based revenue.
This creates a potentially useful commercial loop for Meta: Instagram advertising introduces consumers to products, Muse helps them return to those products, and completed purchases can generate both transaction revenue and insights that may strengthen future advertising campaigns.
Muse is also gaining ground in the emerging market for AI-assisted shopping, where digital agents increasingly help consumers discover products and complete purchases.
Data from e-commerce analytics and cybersecurity company Forter Intelligence showed that Meta’s agent accounted for 51 per cent of agentic orders during the week ended 22 November.
SpaceX’s Grok followed with a 24 per cent share, while OpenAI’s ChatGPT accounted for 22 per cent. Google and start-up Instinct together made up the remaining 3 per cent.
The figures give Meta a sizeable early lead in this segment, although the market is still developing and competition is building among technology companies looking to make AI agents part of the shopping journey.
For Meta, the advantage is its existing ecosystem. Instagram already hosts product discovery, influencer recommendations, brand promotions and shopping-related interactions. Muse can build on these established behaviours rather than asking consumers to begin their shopping journeys on an entirely new platform.
For now, Meta is keeping advertising out of Muse itself. However, that may not remain the case indefinitely.
People familiar with the company’s plans told CNBC that Meta could explore introducing advertisements within the agent in the future. There is no immediate financial pressure to do so, with the company generally prioritising audience growth before monetising a new product.
One possible approach could involve placing advertisements in Muse’s feed, which functions more like a conventional social media newsfeed than an interface that responds exclusively to individual user queries. Such a format could give Meta room to experiment with advertising without limiting the experience to sponsored responses.
The company has not announced an advertising launch or a timeline for such a move.
For now, Muse’s contribution to Instagram’s advertising business is coming from the shopping activity it encourages rather than advertisements displayed within the agent. That distinction is important: Meta is attempting to make existing ad campaigns work harder by helping consumers return to products they have already discovered.
The bigger opportunity is to turn AI from a tool that simply recommends products into one that helps complete the purchase. If Muse can consistently convert saved posts into sales, Meta may have found another way to make Instagram’s advertising engine work harder, even without putting a single ad inside the agent itself.
