TITLE: England captain Maro Itoje rejects win-at-all-costs mentality due to Christian faith
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

England rugby union captain Maro Itoje said on the eve of his country's historic victory over the All Blacks that he would never compromise his Christian faith in pursuit of winning.
Itoje made the comments after a week when sporting integrity came under the spotlight. South Africa rugby union coach Rassie Erasmus was banned for six matches after accusing officials of match-fixing, while "sandpapergate" – the Australian ball tampering controversy dating back to 2018 – hit the headlines again following fresh allegations.
“There’s a line,” Itoje said. “I don’t believe in the metric of win at all costs because, depending on what that cost is, it can be extremely damaging. Integrity is really important. I’m Christian as well, so there are certain things which I wouldn’t do, or [positions] I wouldn’t put myself in.
"There’s a passage in the Bible that says something along the lines of 'What does it gain for a man to gain the world but lose his soul?' (Mark 8:36)
“I want to win... But if that power is taking performance-enhancing drugs or betting on the game – I can list a number of other examples – if that is the cost of winning, if I did those things and then win, what does the win really mean? I don’t believe in that.”
Itoje has frequently talked about his faith and revealed recently that teammates "teased" him for not swearing.
On Saturday, England thrashed New Zealand 37-12 – the biggest ever margin of victory over the All Blacks by a northern hemisphere team.
