NQ: Nasdaq Futures Jump 1% as Wall Street Returns from Thanksgiving
1 min read
Key points:
- Nasdaq futures lead the rebound
- Nvidia faces fresh export pressure
- Higher yields test holiday optimism
Global stocks found some footing after a turbulent week. Recovery is still fragile.
📈 Futures rise after a wild reversal
- Nasdaq futures gained 1% in early Friday trading, while S&P 500 futures advanced 0.8%. Wall Street returns from Thanksgiving with buyers apparently being thankful for these gains after much trouble earlier in the week.
- Wednesday’s rebound extended beyond technology. The Dow recovered more than 1,260 points from its low to finish higher, while small caps also gained.
- Today’s session is shortened, with US stock trading ending at 1 p.m. ET. Black Friday also puts retailers and consumer demand in focus.
- Reduced holiday participation can magnify price swings, making the strength and breadth of buying more informative than an isolated jump in the headline index.
🖥️ Nvidia adds another complication
- Nvidia instructed manufacturing partners to halt shipments of servers containing its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. The development puts export access back in focus.
- Server manufacturers also face scrutiny. Dell and Supermicro reportedly told affected customers that Nvidia would not permit their orders to ship.
- Earnings continue to separate individual stocks. HP surged 11.5% after-hours Wednesday after beating expectations, while Amazon fell 3.1%. HP’s results benefited from tariff refunds, however, underscoring why a strong earnings reaction deserves a closer look at the recurring profits behind it.
🌍 Global relief meets higher yields
- Asian equities advanced, with Japan’s Nikkei up about 0.7% and South Korea gaining around 2%. The US 10-year Treasury yield nevertheless rose toward 4.89%, while the dollar index hovered near 103.8.
- Tokyo core inflation reached a 16-month high, reinforcing expectations of a Bank of Japan hike; USDJPY traded around ¥159.30. EURUSD held near $1.0980.
- With a December Fed increase also almost fully priced, traders face a recovery unfolding against continued monetary tightening rather than imminent policy relief.
- Gold rebounded 1.6% toward $3,620, while Bitcoin traded around $61,700. Brent crude hovered near $96, heading for its first weekly gain in four amid supply concerns.
