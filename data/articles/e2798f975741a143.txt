EUR/USD Technical Analysis: Three Scenario Vectors Ahead of the Fed and ECB
EUR/USD traded around 1.0965 to 1.0975 in the European morning of Thursday 19 November 2026. On Wednesday the pair touched 1.0953, its lowest level since April 2025, before closing at 1.0975 (Pound Sterling Live). The trend points lower on every time frame, from the weekly chart down to the 4-hour chart. Yet the daily RSI sits at 29 and the weekly RSI at 27, so the decline looks stretched (FXStreet via TMGM, 18 November).
The key levels this week sit close together. Support lies at 1.0950 and then at the 61.8% Fibonacci retracement near 1.0904. Resistance starts at 1.1000 and then at 1.1072. US PCE inflation (25 November) and eurozone flash HICP (1 December) come first. The Fed decides on 9 December and the ECB on 17 December.

Multi-Timeframe Trend Analysis
A multi timeframe analysis starts with the slowest chart and works down. The weekly chart sets the direction, and the shorter charts help with timing.
Weekly Chart: Unwinding the 2025 Rally
EUR/USD peaked at 1.2081 on 27 January 2026, its highest level since June 2021 (Pound Sterling Live, 2021 history). Since then, it has given back a large part of the 2025 advance from 1.0176. ActionForex says the fall could be reversing the rise from the 2025 low, and possibly the rise from the 2022 low of 0.9534 (ActionForex, 19 November 2026).
On the weekly chart the pair trades below its 10-period EMA at 1.1190. The weekly RSI reading of 27 is firmly oversold, which suggests the downside momentum is stretched (FXStreet via TMGM, 18 November 2026).
Daily Chart: Below Every Major Moving Average
The current leg lower began at 1.1711 on 21 August. From early October to 10 November, the pair traded in a range of roughly 1.1105 to 1.1285. A daily close at 1.1090 on 11 November broke the floor of that range (Pound Sterling Live).
The pair has not closed above 1.1100 since then. The daily MACD is negative, and the ADX near 33 shows a trend that is still present, though fading (FXStreet, 16 November 2026).
Short-Term Chart: Lower Highs, Slower Momentum
Daily prices show a lower high and a lower low in every session from 11 to 18 November (Pound Sterling Live). Over that run, the daily high fell from 1.1170 to 1.0999. The 16 November session opened at its high of 1.1033, and the pair then slipped to 1.0953 on 18 November.
Momentum has slowed, since daily ranges narrowed this week. Traders often watch the 4-hour chart for a bullish RSI divergence near support, which can come before a pause or a bounce.

The Rate Differential Behind the Chart
US rates are higher than eurozone rates, and the gap has widened this autumn. That has supported the dollar even when eurozone data surprised to the upside.
Fed: Back in Hiking Mode
The Fed raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on 16 September 2026. It was the first increase since July 2023 (Euronews). On 28 October the Fed held rates in a 10 to 2 vote. Beth Hammack and Lisa Cook dissented in favour of a 25 basis point hike (Federal Reserve).
The FOMC minutes released on 18 November leaned hawkish. Almost all members agreed that further hikes would likely be needed in the months ahead. Two policymakers said a hike of more than 25 basis points could be warranted in December if inflation data disappointed (FXStreet via TMGM). The Admirals blog reviews the FOMC minutes of November 2026 in more detail.
US data support that stance. October CPI rose 0.4% on the month, and core CPI rose 0.3% (BLS). The PCE price index rose 3.6% from a year earlier in September, with the core measure at 3.1% (BEA).
