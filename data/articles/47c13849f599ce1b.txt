TITLE: Tyson Fury reveals diagnosis of schizophrenia and bipolar disorder
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Warning: this article discusses themes of mental health
Former world champion boxer Tyson Fury has revealed he was diagnosed with mild schizophrenia and bipolar disorder in a new documentary.
Fury, who last month said that he has been blessed "billions and billions of times" by God, disclosed in a new Netflix documentary that he was diagnosed with the condition after becoming world heavyweight champion in 2015.
Feature-length documentary TYSON FURY will be released on 27 November on the streaming platform in the UK, and will shed light on the born-again Christian's personal life and struggles before he takes on Anthony Joshua in a British superfight at Millenium Stadium, Cardiff on 11 December.
The heavyweight puncher compared his experience during acute mental illness to a hellish experience that left the once fearless fighter scared.
"It was like a living version of the pits of hell. I thought I would end up in a padded room with the door locked, and that scared me so much," Fury said.
"The anxiety was crippling me. I just could not overcome it and that's when I went and saw the psychiatrist down in London. She asked me a gazillion questions, and I ticked all the boxes. Depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder."
"The Gypsy King", born in Manchester and from Styal, Cheshire, became the unified world heavyweight champion in November 2015 after defeating Wladimir Klitschko. He was due to rematch the Ukrainian in October 2016 but was declared unfit to fight by doctors.
He struggled against drug and alcohol addiction after winning the world title and gained weight. In June 2016, he was admitted into hospital with a suspected heart attack and heard voices in his head during this hospital stay.
The NHS website describes schizophrenia as a form of psychosis when a person may not be able to distinguish their own thoughts and ideas from reality. Delusions, hallucinations and wanting to avoid people and interests are symptoms. The NHS also says that the main symptoms of bipolar disorder are rapid mood changes.
The two-time heavyweight champion, who returned to the ring after recovery and won another world title in 2020, revealed how he felt during this hospital stay where he says he believed the radio "started talking to him" and heard voices threatening to kill him and his family.
"It got that bad I was hearing voices in my mind," he said in the documentary. "I wouldn't be able to sit and have a conversation because I'd be hearing stuff in my mind.
"I didn't know where to turn and no-one knew what to do. I really needed help, or check into rehab or something. I never got a break from it for years. I wanted to be what I thought was normal again."
Fury has been married to his wife Paris since 2008, and she recounted her emotions during the period of serious uncertainty for her family.
"I was so scared, scared for him. I could see he was gone, he'd flipped the switch," Paris Fury said.
