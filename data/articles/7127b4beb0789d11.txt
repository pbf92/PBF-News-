Six arrested for damaging ancient water pit at archaeological site in apparent search for treasure
The Israel Antiquities Authority (IAA) estimated that the group hoped to find treasure inside the well. Officials called the damage a loss to Israel’s heritage and history
Six suspects were caught digging at the ancient Horvat Adash site in the Judean Hills last weekend, while others sat beside a burning bonfire in an apparent attempt to pass the scene off as a picnic. IAA antiquities inspectors caught the group in the act during an operational activity near the village of Mavo Beit She’an, the authority announced.
The suspects had been digging in a nine-meter-deep ancient water pit, according to the IAA. Inside the well, the walls and floor were damaged, and layers of ancient plaster that had survived at the site were broken through. Extensive signs of destruction were found around it.
The IAA estimated that the suspects hoped to find treasure inside the well. The authority called the damage irreversible.
Dr. Amir Gonen, head of the antiquities prevention unit at the IAA, condemned the destruction. “Unfortunately, the damage caused to the antiquities sites is damage to our heritage and history,” he stated.
Horvat Adash is a declared ancient site. The remains of a settlement there date from the Second Temple period to the early Muslim period. Earlier finds include a carved mikvah with a double opening, building remains, water systems, industrial facilities and a public building that served as a mosque in the early Muslim period.
All six were arrested on suspicion of damaging an antiquities site, an offense that carries up to five years in prison. They were taken to a police station and questioned. When the investigation ends, the case will go to prosecutors, who will consider filing an indictment.
