TITLE: A Secret Portal in GTA VI Transports You Back to the PS2 Era
OUTLET: Nerdist

Hi everyone. Obviously we’re all playing Grand Theft Auto VI. Are we remembering to break for meals? Get at least four hours of sleep? Still attend our jobs? Get flowers for our actual girlfriends? Hopefully the answer is ‘yes’ to all of those questions, but I’m gonna tell you about cool sh*t you can find in GTA VI, which is certainly not going to motivate you to keep up on your non-game responsibilities. But what am I if not the devil on the internet’s shoulder? Come with me to a land of nostalgia, in the distant pass of 1986. Or 2002, depending on how you look at it.
Popular YouTuber Memeio has uncovered a big secret over the course of his exploration of GTA VI. Following a series of subtle Grand Theft Auto: Vice City references, Memeio was able to put together the twelve substation puzzles, in the correct order and with no fast travel, and figured out that you need a 24-year-old cheat-code from Vice City to put it all together. And, boy, was his labor rewarded! At the final substation on Turtlehead Beach, if you’ve done everything correctly, you’ll find a trans-dimensional portal waiting for you. If Jason/Lucia steps in, it will transport them back to 1986 Vice City. Or, to us, a 2002 Vice City.
My first GTA game was Vice City, so to reappear back in the Malibu Club was a huge treat. I remember how it blew my mind when I was a kid. It may not seem like much at all, but an interior space like that packed with unique, interactable moving parts wasn’t always an industry standard. It’s a good reminder that Vice City was a trailblazer for the series.
Once you’ve teleported to Vice City, Jason/Lucia get an HD-less makeover, as does their surroundings. He’s dressed in Tommy Vercetti’s signature outfit, and you can wander around and absorb the atmosphere. You can also go upstairs and visit the familiar face of British music producer Kent Paul, who starred in both Grand Theft Auto: Vice City and Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas. When Jason/Lucia teleports back to modern Vice City, Kent Paul comes with, though not to your same location. He takes being thrown involuntarily into another world pretty well. I guess KP was always good at rolling with the punches.
I know this is just the tip of the iceberg so far as Grand Theft Auto VI is concerned, but this is a lovely little nod to a game that had indescribable influence on both the people that played it and the gaming industry as a hole. It course-corrected Grand Theft Auto 3‘s biggest issues, and good God, those Vice Beach sunsets still hit over twenty years late.
