TITLE: Manchester council facing call for 'full transparency'
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

Manchester council faces call for 'full transparency' after claim Man City funded event with 'unlimited bar' for councillors
The Reform UK group in Manchester has submitted a council motion about the matter.
Reform UK in Manchester is demanding ‘full transparency’ after claiming Manchester City funded an event for councillors in May with an unlimited free bar, ‘executive’ catering, and live music.
The Reform UK group has submitted a public motion about the matter which will be debated by councillors in a meeting next Wednesday (November 25).
According to the Reform motion, the event was held at Depot Mayfield on May 25 with Manchester’s Lord Mayor Shaukat Ali inviting councillors to a civic reception to celebrate the football club’s achievements at the end of the season.

That year saw Manchester City FC men's team win the FA Cup and League Cup double, the women's team win the League and FA Cup double and the youth team win the FA Youth Cup. It also marked the final season of Pep Guardiola's time as manager of the men's team.
But Manchester Reform claim it was not made clear that the football club had funded the event.
They said in their motion that the original invitation ‘did not make reference to the fact that the civic reception was wholly funded by a private corporate body.’
Reform claimed in the motion that councillors attending the event ‘were accepting private hospitality from MCFC [Manchester City Football Club], but were not aware of this fact They may have made the choice not to attend in light of the information.’
They added in the motion: “At the time of the civic reception, MCFC were under investigation for financial wrong doing and have subsequently been found guilty of 114 / 115 charges, which they are appealing, moreover there has been considerable press coverage of potential ADUG [Abu Dhabi United Group] issues.”
The Reform group also said that councillors were ‘unaware of whether they should have declared this event’ in their public register of interests, which displays gifts and hospitality worth over £100.
Reform said in its motion that a previous civic reception for Manchester City in 2024 at the National Football Museum was funded by the council at a cost of £11,458. Reform added in its motion: ‘Members were not under obligation to declare [the 2024 event], as this event was paid for by Manchester City Council.’
The Reform UK group council motion - which will be put to a vote among all Manchester councillors next week - asks Manchester City to share ‘details of the full cost of the civic reception’, and for the council to be ‘fully transparent in the future with regard to the funding of civic reception and hospitality’, among other demands.
Manchester City has been approached for comment.
It is understood that Bev Craig and Andy Burnham did not attend the event in May.
A Manchester City Council spokesperson said: “Civic receptions are held to recognise outstanding achievements by organisations or individuals based in the city.
“This event was hosted by the Lord Mayor of Manchester with invitations issued by the council to mark the success of the club’s men’s, women’s and youth teams in the 2025/26 season.
“While such events are usually funded through our civic events budget, we will always endeavour to minimise costs where possible.
“Whether an event is classed as civic is not determined solely by who covers or contributes to its costs. It is based on a range of factors including its purpose, how it is organised and its relevance to the city.”
Garry Bridges, leader of Manchester council, said he found Reform UK’s motion ‘very surprising’ given all Reform councillors were invited to the event with some attending, including Manchester Reform group leader Sian Astley.
Councillor Bridges added: “We regularly host civic events to celebrate success in our city, but we are also mindful of cost and always seek to minimise any burden on the public purse wherever possible.
“We are grateful that the club stepped forward to meet the cost of the event, meaning it could be held without any expense to local taxpayers. I would imagine that most of the Manchester public would agree with that.
“It is strange that Reform councillors attended the event and had no problem with it for months until they discovered that the council didn't pay for it. I find it a surprising and disingenuous position for them to take.
“It seems that the Reform position then is that it was right to have the event and for them to attend it, but they would rather it was publicly funded. Something I would disagree with.”
Councillor Sian Astley, leader of Manchester Reform, said: “As new councillors, the civic reception for Manchester City Football Club was the first one we’d been invited to and three of our seven attended.
“It was a glamorous event, lovely food, free drinks with all the teams, players and management enjoying their day.
“It was undoubtedly special - mixing with famous footballers up close and seeing the trophies. It struck us as surprisingly extravagant but we had no point of comparison.
“Subsequent civic events have been far less so and I was curious as to the cost to the public purse, so decided to ask.
“We also wondered, because there’d been a lot of publicity about the close relationship between MCFC, their owners and Manchester council, who had settled the bill.
“I was gobsmacked when the figures came back detailing the leap for taxpayer funded MCFC civic receptions from £823.17 in 2016 to £11,458.25 in 2024, to entertain councillors.
“So actually it wasn’t surprising that MCFC were now paying for the plush reception, but we should have been told that we were accepting corporate hospitality. There should have been full transparency.
“The event was worth well over £100 per head, so all councillors should have been declaring it on the Members Interests register.”
The Manchester Reform motion also stated that Manchester MPs Jeff Smith and Lucy Powell had attended the event in May.
Neither MP has made a declaration to the register for MPs' interests about the event.
Jeff Smith MP said: “I was invited to the civic reception by the Lord Mayor’s office as normal, and was pleased to attend. The event didn’t qualify as a declarable interest in the MPs register.”
A spokesperson at Lucy Powell’s office said: “This was a civic reception organised by the council and Lucy was invited by the Lord Mayor in the usual way for civic receptions.
“The invitation came from the council for a council organised event and therefore did not require declaration to the register for MPs interests.”
What the Reform UK council motion claimed
Manchester Reform UK said in its council motion: “On 21 May 2026, the Lord Mayor of the City of Manchester, Councillor Shaukat Ali invited Councillors to “a Civic Reception to celebrate the achievements of Manchester City’s men’s, women’s and academy teams during the 2025/26 season” on the 25 May 2026.
“It took place at the Depot Mayfield Rooftop and was attended by Manchester City Councillors, Council Officers, Senior Executives, Jeff Smith MP for Manchester Withington and Lucy Powell MP for Manchester Central, and others. The event was fully staffed and featured an open and unlimited free bar, executive catering and live music.
“The winning season trophies were on display for guests to approach and have photos with.
“The Manchester City Premier league men’s first team and women’s team were there, meeting, greeting and mingling with guests and having photos taken, along with the then manager Pep Guardiola.
“The invitation referred to a later event at the Co-op Live and a ticket to that was available on request, expressly noted to be funded by Manchester City Football Club as a complimentary general admission ticket. Councillors could apply for that separately.
“On 12 October, the Reform Group wrote to the Civic and Ceremonial Team to ask whether the May reception had been funded by MCC [Manchester City Council] or MCFC [Manchester City Football Club], and also about previous Civic Receptions.
“On 26 October 2026, the Civic and Ceremonial Team revealed that the May reception had been paid for by MCFC with the full cost stated as “Not known”.
“The original invitation did not make reference to the fact that the Civic Reception was wholly funded by a private corporate body.
“If a corporate body funds a Civic Reception then this is external hospitality. Members are required to declare any hospitality over the value of £100 within 28 days of the event.”
