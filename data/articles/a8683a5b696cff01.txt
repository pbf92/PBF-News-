TITLE: Union Vote Fails In Landslide At GTA 6 Studio Rockstar Games As 75 Percent Of Devs Vote No
OUTLET: Kotaku

Rockstar Games will not be forced to formally recognize a union at the Grand Theft Auto 6 studio after its developers based in the UK voted overwhelmingly against the initiative. The weeks-long voting process played out as Rockstar launched the open-world hit to over 30 million sales in its first day.
Voting began on November 12 and concluded today on November 26. Workers at Rockstar Games voted 75 percent against the recognition agreement with the IWGB Game Workers Union. The initiate would have formalized collective bargaining efforts around things like overtime, bonuses, layoffs, and AI. Developers in favor of unionizing can still work with the IGWB, but there will be no regular meetings with management to hash out new policies.
“We are extraordinarily proud of our team, the culture we’ve built together and the launch of Grand Theft Auto 6,” Rockstar head of publishing Jennifer Kolbe said in a statement shared with Kotaku. “This result reflects our team’s strong support for continuing the direct relationships, collaboration and open dialogue that have helped make Rockstar Games so innovative and strong. As we look ahead, we’re excited to build on all we’ve accomplished together.”
The IWGB Game Workers Union pushing the vote released the following statement:
 For anyone at Rockstar who believes in a fairer workplace, a fairer sector and a fairer society – this isn’t a sign to retreat, it’s a sign to double down on what we stand for. Hundreds of our coworkers voted in favor of our union at Rockstar, yet sadly we recognize we have more work to do to build understanding and win the trust of more of our colleagues. We’re ready to put in the effort to make that happen.
Whilst we haven’t emerged with a recognition deal, this ballot has put our union on the radar of every worker, and opened the door for new kinds of conversation to start happening. People entirely unfamiliar with unions were for the first time debating employment conditions, collective bargaining rights, and our contracts. We heard from many more colleagues about what mattered to them, and we hope people will continue to engage so that we can see where these new discussions can lead us.
A recognition agreement would have marked a path towards greater workplace democracy. But it’s one route of many. At Rockstar we have already won, and will continue to win, tangible changes that improve our everyday lives. We’ll keep fighting for a say. For pay transparency, contractual overtime pay, and protections against AI and layoffs.
The power to shape our working lives has always been, and will always be, in our hands.
The vote comes roughly a year after Rockstar fired dozens of developers it accused of gross misconduct and breaking confidentiality rules around the development of GTA 6. The fired workers accused the studio of union busting, which it has denied, and a legal fight has been ongoing over the last 12 months at a labor tribunal. A final judgement in that case is still pending. As recently as this month, members of both sides of the dispute were testifying on the record for the case.
