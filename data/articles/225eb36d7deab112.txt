TITLE: Salvation Army and Uber offer free rides to help jobseekers
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The Salvation Army and Uber have joined forces to help people overcome transport barriers to employment, providing free journeys to job interviews and funding driving lessons.
The partnership has already provided more than 500 free trips, while six jobseekers have started learning to drive.
Tracy, from Dagenham, is among those receiving driving lessons. The single mother of twin girls said having a car would give her more flexibility to find work around her family commitments.
She said: “Being able to drive will change my life, my children’s lives; it will open doors for me and give me the opportunities of finding part-time work.”
Rebecca Keating, director of the Salvation Army’s Employment Plus service, said: “For many of our clients the prospect of attending a job interview can be daunting. This could be because they’ve been out of the labour market for a long time and lack confidence, have health issues or disabilities, caring responsibilities or homelessness.”
Andrew Brem, Uber’s UK general manager, said: "No one should miss out on a job because they can’t get to it. We’re delighted to be working with The Salvation Army to change this. From offering free rides to interviews to funding driving lessons for jobseekers, we’re really hopeful that our work together will make a meaningful difference to people who are looking for their next career opportunity."
The Salvation Army says its Employment Plus service supported more than 5,200 people across the UK and Ireland last year, with 1,137 securing jobs.
