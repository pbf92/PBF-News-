TITLE: UK Christians warn European Parliament over conversion practices bans
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

British Christians have urged European politicians to reject proposed bans on conversion practices, warning they could restrict freedom of speech, religion and pastoral support.
The appeal was made at an event in Brussels this week, led by Maltese Christian Matthew Grech, who was acquitted in March after spending three years facing criminal proceedings over an interview about his faith.
Grech had spoken on Maltese television in 2022 about his decision to leave behind a homosexual lifestyle after becoming a Christian, and the availability of pastoral and professional support.
Prosecutors argued the interview amounted to the criminal advertising of conversion practices. He faced a possible five-month prison sentence and a fine of up to €5,000. The two journalists who interviewed him were also acquitted.
The event comes as the European Commission prepares to recommend in 2027 that all 27 EU member states ban conversion practices. Malta introduced its own ban in 2016.
Campaigners warned that broadly worded laws could affect conversations between parents and children, counsellors and clients, and pastors and members of their congregations.
Concerns have also been raised in the UK. Baroness Hilary Cass, who led the independent review into NHS gender identity services for children, has withdrawn her support for proposed legislation in its current form, warning it is not sufficiently tightly drafted.
Speaking in Brussels, Grech said: “I spent three years in court for a conversation about my own life. Malta’s law was meant to be a model for Europe, but my case shows where that model leads: to the police, prosecutors and courts deciding which life stories are allowed to be told.
“I came to Brussels to ask MEPs to ensure that what happened to me in Malta does not become the law for every family in Europe.
“People must remain free to share their stories, practise their faith and seek the support they choose, without fearing criminal investigation or prosecution.”
Andrea Williams, chief executive of the Christian Legal Centre, which supported Grech, said: “Matthew’s acquittal is welcome, but he should never have been prosecuted. His ordeal is a stark warning to Europe that vague ‘conversion practices’ laws can criminalise personal testimony, Christian ministry and the freedom to seek help. No one should face three years in court simply for telling the truth about their own life.”
