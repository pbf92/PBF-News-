TITLE: Israeli delegation visits Azerbaijan amid efforts to conserve heritage sites, strengthen relations
OUTLET: The Jerusalem Post

An Israeli delegation visited Azerbaijan earlier this month as part of the ongoing effort between the two countries to conserve national heritage sites, the Israel Antiquities Authority (IAA) announced on Tuesday.
The delegation’s visit, at the invitation of Azerbaijan’s State Tourism Agency and in coordination with the Foreign Ministry, also comes as Israel and Azerbaijan work to strengthen relations.
The delegation included IAA Deputy Conservation Administration Director Attorney Ami Shahar, IAA Director of Heritage Conservation Avi Mashiach, and IAA Head of Specialty Conservations Mark Avrahami.
“Israel is known worldwide for its innovation and hi-tech. That same creative thinking we embed into heritage preservation,” Shahar said. “In a country that is the cradle of the culture of three religions, we have developed vast knowledge and experience, with innovative working methods and solutions for preserving ancient sites and artifacts.”
“The cooperation with Azerbaijan, particularly right now in today’s complex global reality, is an important opportunity which allows us to strengthen friendly relations through joint professional work,” they added.
Delegation visits Jewish heritage sites
Among the sites visited by the delegation were an ancient caravanserai, ruins of the medieval city of Shabaran, which was destroyed in 1723, and Besh Barmag Mountain, a sacred pilgrimage site, both in northeastern Azerbaijan.
Shabaran, which sits on the banks of the Shabran River along the historic Silk Road, was unearthed during archaeological excavations in the late 1980s. It was home to Muslim, Jewish, and Christian inhabitants.
According to the Azerbaijan State News Agency (AZERTAC), archaeologists previously discovered a ninth-century stone sewer system, clay furnaces, and coins in the city.
The delegation also visited several Jewish heritage sites in the country, including the ancient cemetery in Shamakhi, the village of Muju, and several others in the Quba region.
"Preserving Jewish cultural heritage assets around the world, alongside strengthening international collaborations and the exchange of professional knowledge, is a central element of the Conservation Administration's vision and goals,” Shahar said. “In the Jewish cemetery in Shamakhi, where the tombstones hold important information about the life of that Jewish community, we identified tombstones whose stability had been compromised, as well as lichen covering the stone surface and inscriptions.”
Shahar noted that the delegation and Azerbaijani representatives discussed ways to document the sites, including the use of 3D models, as well as training local conservationists.
Another site toured by the delegation was the 18th-century Palace of Shaki Khans in Sheki.
The palace, which was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019, is famous for its colorful murals and shebeke windows, delicate wooden frames, and latticework inlaid with colored glass.
During the visit, Israeli experts studied the condition of the palace’s murals and the climate of the different rooms, discussing the conservation efforts of preserving the site’s ceiling and paint layers, maintaining a steady climate, and adjusting visitor numbers as needed.
The delegation also examined the preservation of the Diri Baba Mausoleum in Gobustan, a burial structure dated back to 1402 and associated with the Sufi tradition of Islam, and the Archaeological Park of Ancient Gabala, which served as Caucasian Albania’s capital for about 900 years.
Deepening ties between Israel, Azerbaijan
“The preservation of cemeteries and Jewish heritage sites in Azerbaijan is part of our responsibility to preserve the story of Jewish communities and pass it on to future generations,” said Heritage Minister Amichai Eliyahu. “Continued cooperation in the preservation of all heritage sites allows professionals from both countries to share knowledge and experience, out of respect for the history and culture of both peoples.”
“Especially these days, it is important to deepen friendly relations with Azerbaijan through joint efforts. I congratulate our partners on the invitation and the opportunity to expand ties between our countries.”
