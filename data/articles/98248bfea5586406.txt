TITLE: Americans at politically mixed Thanksgivings are the most likely to expect political arguments
OUTLET: YouGov

Americans who expect a politically mixed Thanksgiving celebration are more likely than people celebrating mostly with liberals or mostly with conservatives to expect arguments about politics, according to the latest Economist / YouGov Poll.
Among the 84% of Americans who plan to celebrate Thanksgiving, 25% say the people attending their celebration will be more liberal than conservative, 28% say more conservative than liberal, and 24% say about equally liberal and conservative.
Most liberals and conservatives who plan to celebrate expect to do so mostly with people who share their politics. 61% of liberals say the people attending will be more liberal than conservative, and 56% of conservatives say they will be more conservative than liberal. Only 12% of liberals expect a celebration with more conservatives than liberals, and 4% of conservatives expect one with more liberals than conservatives. Moderates are about twice as likely as liberals and conservatives to expect a celebration that is about equally liberal and conservative (38% of moderates vs. 18% of liberals and 22% of conservatives).
Only 10% of Americans who plan to celebrate Thanksgiving expect arguments about politics at their celebration this year; 85% don't. 17% of people who say their celebration will be about equally liberal and conservative expect arguments, compared with 10% of people expecting a celebration that is more conservative than liberal and 7% of people expecting one that is more liberal than conservative. Liberals who plan to celebrate are more likely than conservatives to expect arguments (13% vs. 7%). Younger Americans are more likely to expect political arguments at Thanksgiving: Adults under 30 who plan to celebrate are more likely than Americans 65 and older to expect arguments (20% vs. 4%).
Fewer Americans expect arguments at this year's Thanksgiving celebrations than did in previous years. Only 10% expect arguments about politics this year, compared with 18% expecting arguments at Thanksgiving in 2024.
Meanwhile, fewer Americans report that their Thanksgiving plans were negatively affected by price increases than in previous years. This year, 34% of Americans who plan on celebrating say that their Thanksgiving plans were affected by price increases, while 59% say their plans were unaffected and 7% weren't sure. A smaller share of those celebrating Thanksgiving expects effects from price increases than the 46% in 2024 and 44% in 2023 and 43% in 2022.
This year, Democrats are about twice as likely as Republicans to say they'd be affected by price increases: 44% of Democrats who plan on celebrating Thanksgiving say their plans will be affected by price increases, compared with only 23% of Republicans who plan on celebrating. Women are more likely than men to expect negative effects on their Thanksgiving plans from price increases this year (38% vs. 28%).
The poll also asked Americans to tell us in their own words what they are most thankful for this year. With the help of an AI tool to analyze responses, and counting more than one for respondents who are thankful for multiple things, we find that:
- Far more Americans are thankful for family (45%) or health (36%) than for anything else
- Health comes up more often among older Americans (47% of those 65 and older vs. 25% of adults under 30). Adults under 30 mention good fortune (19% vs. 5%), friends (14% vs. 7%), and personal growth (11% vs. less than 1%) more often than Americans 65 and older do
- Women mention family more often than men do (55% vs. 35%)
- Republicans mention faith in God more often than Democrats do (10% vs. 4%)
- 6% of Democrats mention their party's election wins, compared with no Republicans
- 2% of Republicans mention President Trump, compared with less than 1% of Democrats
