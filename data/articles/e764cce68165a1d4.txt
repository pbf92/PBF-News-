TITLE: With Grand Theft Auto 6 , The Series Has Outgrown Some Of Its Joke Brand Names
OUTLET: Kotaku

One thing that keeps striking me about Grand Theft Auto 6 is how good the writing is, even compared to that in previous games in the series. I enjoyed those earlier games, sure, but the dialogue and storytelling in GTA 6 are a cut above the rest in terms of both the game’s compelling characters and its worldbuilding. The fact that GTA is set in an analogue of the real world helps the worldbuilding bridge gaps that most fictional universes couldn’t, but I’m increasingly finding that the standout writing is fighting a war within the game itself against some of the other jokes that have been building up in the series for over a quarter century.
Grand Theft Auto, for obvious reasons, can’t use the existing brand names that we see every day in real life. In this sense it’s the opposite of the Like a Dragon series, which proudly wears all its licensed brand deals on its chest and even had to write a narrative around losing the license to a key landmark in the seventh game in the series. Talking Budweiser into lending their brand name to GTA 4 would have been an uphill battle and maybe also raised some uncomfortable questions about the game’s proximity to real-world places and events. Better to just call your in-game equivalent Pisswasser and move on.
But we’re in 2026 now. The writing in the GTA games has elevated and, to Rockstar’s credit, so have the fake brand names in general. Taco Bomb is a pretty good one as a stand-in for Taco Bell. ZIP for GAP is also good. Weazel News for Fox News makes sense on a couple of different levels, Snapmatic for Instagram is something that you recognize immediately upon hearing it. But there are a handful of brand names I think the series needs to ditch, as they’re taking away from the more interesting narratives Rockstar wants to tell.
Pisswasser is not a bad joke on its own, but it wears its welcome out fast. Marbles, the McDonald’s stand-in in GTA, sells a burger called Big Balls. I get it, Big Mac, Big Balls, sure. But come on, man. We’re scraping the bottom of the barrel here. Zombix isn’t even a good pun. It’s like someone filed that one in the ten minutes before the deadline. We need to start thinking intelligently about which of these benefit worldbuilding and which detract from it.
I’m not saying all of these need to go or even a majority of them. There are plenty of good joke brand names, but there are also ones that are very, very 2000s, and it would be better for the games if they disappear. Grand Theft Auto has grown with its audience, and it’s now delivering narratives that genuinely keep up with well-written modern movies, so why are we limiting aspects of it to jokes that would appear in an Epic Movie parody?
Rockstar can and should start taking a look at these and see what makes sense for their games every so often. Audit the world, don’t just assume that because it existed in previous games, it needs to exist forever.
It’s not like brands don’t just disappear from the real world all the time. Joann’s Fabrics isn’t around anymore, but your world keeps spinning, just like it would if the Obey car line simply did not appear in a new game. You planning to take a Spirit Airlines flight this holiday? You’re sure as hell not, nor would you be a sobbing mess on the floor if a Big Balls sandwich billboard wasn’t overlooking a highway chase.
There are already so many good jokes in these games that the lame ones can get cut out without losing anything. No one is wrong for liking them, but maybe the games themselves can be better for examining what works and what doesn’t. No series has ever escaped a need for refreshing itself and iterating over the years and, in almost every way, GTA 6 nails it. There’s just one more thing I want to see them look at before they build their next GTA world, asking themselves if each jokey brand name still fits their vision. If it does, great! If they decide it would be better to snip, that would be fine, too.
