TITLE: Perioperative Music Therapy Reduces Distress and Pain in Breast Cancer Surgery
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Perioperative music therapy significantly improves postoperative anxiety, depression, and pain while reducing systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, and heart rate among patients undergoing breast cancer surgery, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in Frontiers in Oncology. The investigation evaluated randomized controlled clinical trials to determine how music-based supportive care influences both psychological states and physiological stress parameters during the surgical treatment period.
The research was conducted by Ruohan Hu and Yan Yang from the School of Nursing at Chengdu Medical College, alongside correspondence author Xiuying Lu from the Sichuan Cancer Hospital and Institute in Chengdu, China. Working within the journal’s breast cancer section, the investigative team established an objective to systematically examine and quantitatively synthesize the therapeutic contributions of perioperative music therapy across clinical surgical populations experiencing breast cancer procedures.
To identify relevant clinical trials, the investigators conducted a comprehensive literature search across multiple biomedical and scientific research databases from their inception up through October 2025. The searched databases included CNKI, Wanfang, VIP, PubMed, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library. The search specifically targeted published randomized controlled trials evaluating perioperative music therapy in patients undergoing surgical treatment for breast cancer.
Methodological rigor was maintained through a formal dual-evaluator screening and appraisal workflow. Two independent reviewers screened all identified study records, extracted relevant experimental data, and systematically evaluated methodological quality using the established Cochrane risk-of-bias assessment tool, version 1.0. Following data extraction and bias assessments, the researchers performed statistical meta-analysis using RevMan 5.4 software. Effect sizes across the synthesized outcomes were expressed either as mean differences or standardized mean differences alongside corresponding 95 percent confidence intervals.
The comprehensive database review successfully identified and included a total of 18 randomized controlled trials representing a cumulative sample of 1,728 surgical patients. Postoperative anxiety demonstrated marked improvement with a standardized mean difference of 0.99 (95 percent confidence interval: 0.48 to 1.49, P = 0.0001). Postoperative depression was likewise significantly diminished among patients receiving music therapy, showing a standardized mean difference of 0.66 (95 percent confidence interval: 0.44 to 0.88, P < 0.00001). In addition, postoperative pain scores decreased significantly compared with control conditions, yielding a standardized mean difference of 0.64 (95 percent confidence interval: 0.31 to 0.97, P = 0.0001).
In addition to psychological relief and pain alleviation, the quantitative synthesis documented clear, statistically significant reductions across multiple physiological stress indicators. Systolic blood pressure decreased substantially among patients who received music therapy interventions, showing a pooled mean difference of 14.17 (95 percent confidence interval: 5.66 to 22.67, P = 0.001). Diastolic blood pressure exhibited a statistically significant decrease as well, with a mean difference of 7.14 (95 percent confidence interval: 0.48 to 13.79, P = 0.04). Patient heart rate was also significantly reduced following perioperative music exposure, demonstrating a mean difference of 4.75 (95 percent confidence interval: 1.02 to 8.48, P = 0.01).
Subgroup analyses conducted by the research team provided critical insights into how implementation factors modify the efficacy of the intervention. The comparative subgroup assessments revealed that the specific timing of the intervention and the music playback mode significantly altered psychological and physiological outcomes. Specifically, music therapy interventions administered preoperatively yielded more pronounced therapeutic benefits than interventions provided at alternative surgical timepoints.
In their concluding synthesis, the authors reported that perioperative music therapy represents an effective, safe, and low-cost non-pharmacological intervention capable of relieving psychological distress, easing pain, and stabilizing key physiological stress indicators in patients undergoing breast cancer surgery. However, the researchers emphasized that existing evidence highlights the need for further high-quality randomized controlled trials.
