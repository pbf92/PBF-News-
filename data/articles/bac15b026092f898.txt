TITLE: 'Take it': Mauricio Pochettino opens up on why he's leaving the USMNT and why his successor should want the job
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

CINCINNATI — If a high-end coach such as Pep Guardiola asked him whether to accept an offer to replace him as U.S. coach, Mauricio Pochettino is emphatic and unequivocal.
"Take it," he said. "If someone asked, like Pep … take it."
Why?
"It's a perfect moment to arrive at the federation," Pochettino said in a roundtable session with several U.S. reporters Monday. "Everything is [going] in the right direction. Of course, take it."
Guardiola, who has spent extended time in the U.S. since stepping away from Manchester City in May, was reportedly on the U.S. Soccer Federation's wish list. However, he reportedly is lukewarm about returning to work right away.
"Now the vision [of the U.S.] from Europe, I promise, is completely different after the World Cup," Pochettino said. "Being head coach in the USA now is prestigious. … Manchester United is coming for the coach of the USA."
He suggested the U.S. job should appeal to seasoned coaches seeking a new challenge, such as Antonio Conte, who left Napoli in May. And, he noted, opportunities will continue to flow for such figures in the future.
"It's a great opportunity, because it's not only the value to be the coach here," Pochettino said. "If Pochettino from Chelsea come here, [then goes to] Manchester United, the vision is completely different."
Potential European candidates include Conte; Roberto Martinez, who is out of work after coaching Portugal at the World Cup; and former Bayern Munich and German World Cup coach Julian Nagelsmann. Pellegrino Matarazzo is an intriguing figure, but the New Jersey native seems in a good place at Spanish club Real Sociedad.
Pochettino's decision for leaving was two-fold: While he enjoyed his two-plus years coaching the U.S., he said he missed the daily rhythms of club soccer. Also, Manchester United was the one calling.
"It's part of what I miss a little bit — miss every day doing something here," he said.
He recounted a conversation with Guardiola two months ago at Yankee Stadium, where they attended the Major League Soccer derby between New York City FC and Red Bull New York.
Guardiola asked him, "You don't want to come back to the Premier League?"
Pochettino answered, "No. I am happy here."
Guardiola responded, "But you don't miss every day?"
"A little bit, yes," Pochettino said.
Pochettino told reporters Monday, "We have a camp in December with MLS players, but after, you need to wait until March [for the next full international window]. You need to watch games, you need to travel, you need to know administrative things. That is nice, it is good. But every day to be involved — press conference, training, everything."
At United — his dream job for a dozen years — that's possible again.
"I want that," he said.
Late last week Pochettino reached agreement with United to take over a side off to its worst start in 40 years. He will fly on a United-arranged charter from Cincinnati to England after the second leg of the CONCACAF Nations League quarterfinals Tuesday against Haiti. He'll make his Red Devils' debut Sunday at Liverpool.
Asked by Yahoo Sports whether he liked the different schedule and pace of overseeing a national team for the first time in his almost 18-year coaching career, Pochettino said he had mixed feelings.
"I like and I don't like," he said. "I like, because it provides you the possibility to manage your time — to be busy, but at the same time, be free and to have some time with your family. Now I have a grandchild, and two [more] are coming. You can share life with your people."
Going back to the Premier League grind, that life is "Ciao!" he said. Having a flexible schedule, "Forget it. It's impossible to spend time with [his] people."
The international schedule pales in comparison to club demands, which stretch year-round. His U.S. tenure will include 37 matches over just 11 camps, including the 2025 CONCACAF Gold Cup and 2026 World Cup.
In other words, after two years on the irregular international schedule, Pochettino needed that 24/7 rush of club soccer. His past gigs included Southampton, Tottenham Hotspur, Paris Saint-Germain and Chelsea.
He said he will return overseas proud of what he accomplished in resetting the U.S. culture, introducing young players and capturing the public's heart during the World Cup. The U.S. won its group and got through the first knockout round before crashing out against Belgium in the Round of 16 — the Americans' trap door for several recent World Cups.
Upon his arrival in 2024, Pochettino said "the situation was worse than people believe." He was referring to "the identity of the team, the identity of the culture, the mindset of the structure, everything."
He didn't go into detail, but the low point was clearly the 2025 Nations League final four, where the U.S. team lost both matches and didn't seem anywhere close to being ready for the World Cup. A few months later, he was upset with veterans such as Christian Pulisic requesting time off instead of reporting to the Gold Cup.
Pochettino's decision to begin calling in young players was not just a shot across the bow of European-based veterans, but showed confidence in MLS prospects.
"People measure the player through the level of the leagues, and that is wrong," he said. "You need to measure the level of the player playing."
Among the revelations in 2025 were defender Alex Freeman and midfielder Sebastian Berhalter, who at the time were playing for Orlando and Vancouver, respectively. Now they are with Villarreal in Spain and Middlesbrough in England.
This fall he introduced teenagers such as Philadelphia forward Cavan Sullivan and Red Bull New York forward Julian Hall and midfielder Adri Mehmeti.
With U.S. work wrapping up, Pochettino's attention will soon turn back to the daily intensity of club soccer and the massive pressure of turning around one of the biggest names in global sports.
"I am ready," he said. "I am ready."
