TITLE: Anthropic Posts Claude-Authored VP ≠ VNP Proof With Lean Formalization, Not Yet Human-Verified
OUTLET: CASRAI

Anthropic has published on GitHub a paper credited to Claude as sole author that claims a proof of Valiant’s VP ≠ VNP conjecture over every field, together with a Lean 4 formalisation, in a repository directory released around 23 November 2026 (Anthropic’s vp-vnp README). The README itself states that no human mathematician has checked the proof, which makes the posting a test case for journals, funders and research offices.
What happened
The vp-vnp directory in Anthropic’s formal-math repository is titled “VP ≠ VNP over every field” and credits the paper “by Claude (Anthropic)”. Interesting Engineering, in a report dated 23 November 2026, described a 70-page Claude-authored paper and a Lean 4 proof. AI Weekly, publishing at 00:12 UTC on 24 November, described a Lean formalisation of 324 files totalling roughly 73,000 lines; those file and line counts are AI Weekly’s figures rather than Anthropic’s.
Anthropic’s README states the scope in its own words: circuits for the Hamiltonian cycle polynomial, and for the permanent outside characteristic 2, need more than m^(c (log m)^(1/3)) gates for all large m, and the same holds for approximating circuits. It adds that the result “does not settle P vs NP”. On authorship and review, it says AI models wrote both the paper and the formal proof: “Both were written by AI models, and no human mathematician has checked the proof.”
Key details of the claim
The bound grows faster than any fixed polynomial in m, because the exponent c (log m)^(1/3) increases without limit as m grows. That is the sense in which it is a superpolynomial lower bound.
The README is explicit about what the machine check does not cover: “Lean checks the proofs, but no machine checks that this file says what is claimed.” In practice, a formal proof establishes only the statement it was written against, so whether that statement matches the standard definitions of VP and VNP is a separate question that people have to answer. AI Weekly adds that the fit between the Lean statement and the informal paper was judged only by AI readers.
The build instructions in the README (lake exe cache get, lake build, and a script that prints the axioms the proof relies on) allow anyone with a Lean installation to rerun the formal check.
Background and context
As the Wikipedia article on arithmetic circuit complexity defines them, VP is the class of polynomials of polynomial degree that have polynomial-size circuits over a fixed field, and VNP is the class of polynomials of polynomial degree whose coefficients can be determined efficiently, given a monomial, with a polynomial-size circuit. The article notes that Valiant showed the permanent to be complete for VNP and that his seminal work proposed the VP versus VNP question as an algebraic analogue of P versus NP. It cites Valiant’s 1979 paper “Completeness classes in algebra” (STOC ’79, pages 249–261). Because the permanent is VNP-complete, circuit lower bounds for it bear directly on whether the two classes differ. The same article points out that Ryser’s formula gives depth-3 circuits for the permanent of size roughly 2^n, which is far above polynomial.
The posting follows other AI-authored mathematics claims. On 27 October 2026, OfficeChai reported that Anthropic had released a paper titled “A 2^o(n) Time Algorithm for 3-SAT”, listing “Claude, Anthropic” as author and claiming to refute the Exponential Time Hypothesis, on the same day OpenAI announced a similar claim. According to that report, Anthropic said the Lean development of 1,050 modules on Mathlib was written by AI models and reviewed by other instances of the same models, that no person was known to have checked it when the paper was released, and that it passed the Lean kernel and the independently built checker nanoda. CASRAI has also covered Anthropic’s Lean archive claiming a Riemann Hypothesis result and the Lean verification of OpenAI’s Yang-Mills proof.
Reaction
AI Weekly reports that Thomas Steinke and Shubhendu Trivedi each posted the repository link on Bluesky under the title “VP != VNP over every field”, and that Carlos Scheidegger amplified Steinke’s post. The posts it quotes consist of the title and the link. Interesting Engineering’s report carries the same caveat as Anthropic’s README, that no human mathematician has reviewed the proof so far.
What it means for researchers, universities and research offices
For mathematicians and theoretical computer scientists, the practical task is the one the README sets out: someone has to read the formal statement and confirm that it encodes the standard definitions of VP, VNP, the permanent and the Hamiltonian cycle polynomial, and someone has to read the 70-page argument. Until that happens, the accurate description of the result is a claim posted by Anthropic, which is how the README describes it.
For research offices and institutional policy teams, the posting is a concrete case to test guidance on AI-credited results. The paper names Claude as sole author, and the question of crediting AI as an author is addressed in the CASRAI entry on the ICMJE position on AI co-authorship. A related question is where such a claim sits relative to peer review: a GitHub directory marked “not accepting contributions” is neither a journal submission nor a conventional preprint with a comment channel, so any scrutiny has to happen elsewhere.
For universities that cite or promote AI-generated results, the README’s split between what Lean verified and what no machine verified is a useful template for disclosure: state which parts were checked by software, which by people, and which by neither. Three claims of this kind from one lab within roughly a month, covering the Exponential Time Hypothesis, the Riemann Hypothesis and now VP versus VNP, make a standing disclosure practice more useful than a case-by-case response.
What happens next
The next step is outside Anthropic’s control: independent examination of the Lean statement and the paper by specialists in algebraic complexity. Because the repository is not accepting contributions, that review would have to be public commentary, a preprint or a journal submission, and the reproducible Lean build gives reviewers a fixed artefact to check against.
