TITLE: FDJ United Reports 226 Unlicensed Casino Ads on Meta Platforms
OUTLET: iGaming.org

French gambling group FDJ United has filed legal proceedings against Meta over advertisements allegedly using its brands to promote unlicensed online casinos. The company wants the adverts removed from Facebook and Instagram after previous discussions failed to resolve the issue.
Meta already faces a separate legal challenge in the Netherlands, where gambling trade association VNLOK accused the company of failing to remove advertisements promoting unlicensed operators.
According to VNLOK, more than 90% of gambling advertisements on Facebook came from operators without a Dutch licence. Despite repeated reports, Meta removed just 11% of flagged advertisements in May and 15% in June.
The association eventually took the matter to court after months of warnings. As we previously reported, Dutch gambling companies have questioned whether regulator Kansspelautoriteit (KSA) has done enough to address the problem.
Justin Franssen, partner at Franssen Tolboom Attorneys at Law, criticised the preference for discussions with Meta rather than stronger enforcement.
“It was really astonishing to read the gambling authority state in a national newspaper that it would rather talk and cooperate with Meta because that would be more effective in the long term,” he commented.
“We’ve seen the last five years; it doesn’t add up.”
In France, FDJ United claims advertisers created promotions resembling its lottery retail outlets and newspaper front pages to direct users toward unlicensed gambling websites.
The company told us that discussions with Meta had failed to deliver adequate technical safeguards or assurances. Between May 18 and 21 alone, FDJ United identified 226 new pieces of prohibited content, many resembling advertisements already reported.
French law prohibits online casino games, although licensed operators can offer certain other gambling products, including sports betting, poker and lottery games. The French gambling regulator, Autorité Nationale des Jeux (ANJ), can also order the blocking of unlicensed gambling websites.
The legal action seeks urgent removal of the advertisements rather than waiting for ordinary proceedings.
FDJ United chairwoman and CEO Stéphane Pallez confirmed the complaint in an opinion article published by Les Echos, describing the problem as the “impersonation and fraudulent use of its brand through rogue social media campaigns promoting illegal gambling”.
Pallez estimates that more than 80 million Europeans encounter unlicensed online gambling offers annually. She also cited estimated tax losses exceeding €20 billion across Europe in 2025.
For comparison, FDJ United contributed €5.1 billion to French public finances in 2025 and supported 57,200 jobs.
Beyond lost tax revenue, Pallez warned that unlicensed gambling platforms can expose consumers to unpaid winnings, stolen banking information and personal data theft. Social media advertising also allows operators to reach minors without the protections required from licensed companies.
She described the consequences as “social and security related”.
FDJ United wants gambling operators, technology companies, banks, payment providers, sports organisations and youth protection groups to work together against unlicensed gambling.
The group has also introduced a public awareness campaign “built around a simple message, playing legally means playing safely”.
