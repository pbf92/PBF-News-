TITLE: Bitcoin rebounds 2% after falling to lowest level since September 2024
OUTLET: TradingView

Bitcoin rebounds 2% after falling to lowest level since September 2024
Bitcoin, the world's largest cryptocurrency by market value, was up around 2.14% at $60,192.07 at 10:16 a.m. ET (1516 GMT) on Saturday, Reuters reported, as it recovered some ground after falling to its lowest level in more than two years. On Friday, the cryptocurrency broke below $57,742, its lowest level since September 2024.
Bitcoin's latest recovery comes after a sharp selloff that has erased more than half its value since it peaked above $126,000 in October 2025. The cryptocurrency has been caught in a broader retreat from speculative assets, with investors pulling money from Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) amid a reversal in the artificial intelligence (AI) trade.
Bitcoin has faced several pressures in recent weeks. A hack of Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, in late October rattled digital assets, while sweeping Democratic victories in the US midterm elections fuelled concerns that the industry's push for friendlier regulations had lost momentum.
Bitcoin ETFs have seen year-to-date outflows of more than $3 billion. A stronger dollar and rising expectations of another Federal Reserve rate increase have added to the pressure.
The selloff has also triggered significant liquidations of leveraged positions. Around $2 billion in long crypto bets were liquidated over 24 hours on Friday, with Bitcoin positions accounting for the majority, according to Coinglass data.
Despite securing a more sympathetic White House, friendlier regulators, institutional acceptance and a legislative framework under US President Donald Trump, the cryptocurrency has struggled to attract fresh demand. The latest downturn has also raised questions about whether institutional investors will continue adding to their holdings at the pace previously expected.
