TITLE: Google Maps' AI now calls US salons, groomers and garages to find open slots
OUTLET: PPC Land

Google today gave Ask Maps, the conversational assistant inside Google Maps, the ability to look for beauty, pet care, auto and home repair appointments online and to phone local businesses on a user's behalf, according to a post on Google's blog by Maz Khorasani, Product Director at Google Maps. The same post adds day-by-day trip itineraries with numbered map pins, route notes on parking, charging, weather and tolls, and a search across saved lists that checks live availability. The features start rolling out today on Android and iOS. Appointment finding and the route notes launch first in the US, while itineraries and list search are available everywhere Ask Maps is offered.
In Short
Google Maps' AI assistant can now plan multi-day road trips, search the places people have saved, and, in the US, hunt for salon, pet, car and home repair appointments by checking online and phoning businesses itself. That matters most to local businesses in those four categories, which may start receiving calls from Google's software rather than from customers, and to marketers whose listings now feed a trip planner as well as search results. Google's post does not say whether businesses can opt out of the calls, whether Ask Maps completes the booking, or whether ads will appear anywhere in these features.
What Google announced
The post, titled "New Ask Maps features to help with holiday travel and prep," was published on November 16, 2026, in the Maps section of Google's blog and tagged Maps, Travel and AI. It frames the release around the holiday season. "To help you stay one step ahead this season, we're launching new Ask Maps features that make it easy to create itineraries, find available appointments, and search your lists in a snap," Khorasani wrote.
There are four pieces. Itineraries turn a trip request into a day-by-day plan on the map. "Know before you go" tips attach route information to directions. Appointment finding sends Ask Maps to look for slots at nearby businesses, including by phone. List search queries a user's saved places against live availability.
Timing is not incidental. US Thanksgiving falls on November 26, ten days after the rollout began, and Black Friday follows the next day. The post itself opens with the season: "The holidays are a busy time between shopping for gifts, planning travel, and celebrating with loved ones."
The post closes with a separate item: Ask Maps, it says, "starts expanding to 30 countries across Europe - including Spain, Germany, and the U.K. - bringing Ask Maps to more than 200 countries and territories globally." Because itineraries and list search work wherever Ask Maps is offered, that expansion also defines where those two features can reach.
Itineraries inside Ask Maps
The itinerary feature handles requests ranging from a single afternoon to several days. Google's example spans both ends: "a day trip for holiday shopping or something more complex - like a three-day road trip with stops for coastal hikes and oceanfront dining."
What comes back is structured. According to the post, the result is "a day-by-day schedule with insider tips and numbered map pins for each stop." Once finished, an itinerary can be shared with friends and family or exported to Google Docs on desktop. The post does not say where the "insider tips" come from - reviews, business listings, or Gemini's own summary of web content.
The more consequential element is what happens on the day of travel. An itinerary can be reopened through Ask Maps or found through an "itinerary" badge in the chat history. At that point, according to Google, the plan shows "real-time details like live traffic conditions, whether a place is open, and even what the vibe is at your next stop." Users can change the plan mid-trip by asking Ask Maps to add, in Google's words, "a scenic viewpoint or lunch spot along the way," and then tap to get directions to the next stop directly from the itinerary.
That turns a static plan into something closer to a running document tied to navigation. It also places the itinerary inside Maps itself, on phones. Google's earlier itinerary tool in Search, Canvas in AI Mode, was desktop only and limited to US users who had opted into the AI Mode experiment when PPC Land described it in January. Maps' version is available in every Ask Maps market from launch.
"Open" status comes from business data that owners partly control. Opening hours, attributes and photos sit in each business's Google Business Profile, and PPC Land reported in April that those profiles had become a data layer feeding AI, Maps and Search, with Ask Maps analyzing data from 300 million places. "Vibe" is a softer measure, and Google does not define it.
"Know before you go" route tips
The second addition applies to ordinary directions, not only to itineraries. "Whether you're taking a multi-day road trip or running errands around town, Maps keeps you a step ahead," the post reads. Pulling up directions now shows tips "about your route, including where to park or charge your car, and real-time conditions like weather, potential delays, and tolls."
Parking and charging locations are, again, place data. A car park or charging network that appears in these tips is a business with a listing. The post does not say how Maps picks which parking or charging options to show, whether operators can influence the selection, or whether the tips appear during navigation or only before a route starts.
This feature launches in the US first. Google says "more regions coming soon," without dates. There is a wrinkle in how the company presents it, though. Google's separate post on the European expansion, published the same day, lists trip itineraries with Know Before You Go tips, weather and parking information among the things Ask Maps can do, without repeating the US-first limit. Read together, the two posts leave it unclear whether European users get the route tips inside itineraries from launch or only the itinerary itself.
Appointments: the agentic part
The appointment feature is the one that changes who does the work. "Holiday prep comes with a long list of to-dos," Khorasani wrote. "Now, with new agentic capabilities, you can choose to have Ask Maps find beauty, pet care, auto and home repair appointments online and call local businesses for you."
That is a form of agentic AI: software that carries out steps on a person's behalf rather than returning a list of links. Two channels are named - online availability and phone calls - and one example prompt is given: "Find me a 4 p.m. nail appointment near me." The output, according to Google, is that "Ask Maps will show you open slots across nearby businesses so it's easy to decide what to book."
The wording matters. Ask Maps finds and displays slots. The post does not say that Ask Maps confirms the appointment, pays a deposit or completes a booking with a third-party scheduling system. The user "can choose" to have Ask Maps act, which suggests the calling is opt-in for the person asking. Nothing in the post describes what the business on the other end of the call hears, whether the call identifies itself as automated, or whether a business can decline to receive such calls.

Compared with the May list for AI Mode, today's Maps release adds one category: auto. The July 2025 Search version had a detail that today's post does not repeat. PPC Land reported at the time that businesses were opted in by default and could opt out through their Business Profile settings, and that the feature excluded five US states: Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana and Nebraska. Whether the same settings and exclusions govern Ask Maps calls is not addressed.
Speed was part of the earlier pitch. In an interview recorded on October 30, 2025, Google's Robby Stein described a demonstration in which the system contacted three pet groomers within 10 minutes and returned prices from $74 to $105. In April, Google's Nick Fox said in a separate interview that the final booking step remains a single tap by the user. Today's post is consistent with that: Ask Maps presents slots, and the person decides.
Searching saved lists
The fourth feature reads a user's own data. "If you have friends visiting and want to show them around, now you can use Ask Maps to find saved places on your lists in seconds," the post says. The example query is specific: "Which spots saved in my lists have availability for five people in the next 30 minutes?" According to Google, "Ask Maps looks across your lists and checks live availability so you can pick a place and go."
Saved lists are personal; availability for a party of five within half an hour is real-time business data. The post does not say which sources supply that availability - reservation platforms, wait-time estimates or calls - or which types of places it covers. In earlier UK and US releases of restaurant booking in AI Mode, Google named partner platforms such as OpenTable. No partners are named for Ask Maps today.
List search is available wherever Ask Maps is offered.
How it works, according to Google
According to the post, the itinerary flow has six steps:
- A user asks Ask Maps to plan a trip, from a day of holiday shopping to a multi-day road trip with specified kinds of stops.
- Ask Maps returns a day-by-day schedule with "insider tips" and a numbered map pin for each stop.
- The itinerary can be shared with friends and family, or exported to Google Docs on desktop.
- Before departure, the itinerary is reopened by asking Ask Maps or through the "itinerary" badge in the chat history, where it shows live traffic, whether each place is open, and "what the vibe is" at the next stop.
- Stops such as a viewpoint or lunch spot can be added by asking Ask Maps during the trip.
- A tap from the itinerary opens directions to the next stop, and directions in the US show "Know before you go" tips on parking, charging, weather, potential delays and tolls.
The appointment flow, as the post describes it, has four:
- The user chooses to have Ask Maps look for an appointment, with a prompt such as "Find me a 4 p.m. nail appointment near me."
- Ask Maps searches for beauty, pet care, auto or home repair appointments online and calls local businesses.
- Ask Maps shows open slots across nearby businesses.
- The user decides what to book.
Where the features are available
All five roll out starting November 16, 2026, on Android and iOS, according to Google.
Ask Maps so far
Ask Maps, which Google introduced in March 2026, has gained functions in steps. By April it was drawing on data from 300 million places. On August 6, Google added food ordering for pickup through Square and Toast, with Uber Eats listed as coming soon, connected Gmail through Personal Intelligence, and extended Ask Maps to Australia, Brazil, Canada, Indonesia, Japan, Mexico and more than 150 countries in English. Ordering, hotel and event discovery were US only at that stage.
The direction is consistent. Each release moves Ask Maps from answering questions about places toward completing tasks at them: ordering, then scheduling, now planning a whole trip. Maps had already lost one older community feature along the way. In December 2025, Google confirmed that the Q&A section on business listings was being replaced by an AI "Ask" button, with answers drawn from business-provided information and reviews.
Data quality underpins all of it. In April, Google said Maps was using Gemini to block policy-violating place edits before they go live, after blocking or removing more than 292 million policy-violating reviews and 79 million inaccurate or unverified edits in 2025.
