TITLE: Circana purchase data will shape Trade Desk audiences, then grade them
OUTLET: PPC Land

Circana, the consumer research company formed from IRI and The NPD Group, today became a lead strategic partner for Open Measurement, the sales measurement framework The Trade Desk plans to launch before the end of the year. The announcement arrives two days after The Trade Desk named Attain the inaugural data partner for the same product, and it goes further than Attain's did on one point: Circana's data is meant to feed the targeting side of the platform as well as the measurement side.
According to the joint release, datelined Chicago and New York, November 19, 2026, two Circana capabilities, Intelligence Stream and Sales Lift, "will integrate with The Trade Desk's Audience Unlimited and Open Measurement initiatives, creating a continuous feedback loop in which campaign outcomes inform future audience and optimization decisions." The companies gave no price, no launch date beyond the fourth quarter, no list of markets and no description of the method that will link ad exposure to purchases.
In Short
The Trade Desk, one of the largest independent ad-buying platforms, will use Circana's records of what shoppers buy both to help choose who sees ads and to measure whether those ads produced sales. That matters to consumer goods and retail brands that buy open-web and streaming ads through The Trade Desk, and to publishers who argue that last-click reports undervalue their inventory. Open Measurement is due by December 31, but neither company has published the method, the price, the markets covered or how Circana's results will sit alongside Attain's, so the claims cannot yet be checked.
What Circana and The Trade Desk announced
The release carries the headline "Circana and The Trade Desk Expand Partnership to Connect Audience Activation and Open Measurement to Consumer Purchases." Its subheadline states that "Circana's purchase intelligence will power The Trade Desk's Audience Unlimited and Open Measurement initiatives, bringing real-world sales outcomes into every stage of the campaign."
The central sentence on Circana's role is short. "Circana is a lead strategic partner for Open Measurement, bringing its data, insights, and AI to a framework The Trade Desk will launch during the fourth quarter of 2026," the release states. The indefinite article matters. Circana is "a" lead partner, not "the" lead partner, which leaves room for others. On November 17, Attain was described as the inaugural, foundational and early design partner for the same framework. Neither announcement ranks the two companies or says how many more partners are planned.
The purpose of Open Measurement is set out in words that closely follow Attain's announcement. "Open Measurement gives advertisers an objective view of what drives business growth and gives publishers full credit for the value their media creates," according to the release. The fourth quarter ends on December 31, which leaves 42 days from today for the launch, by PPC Land's count.
The release frames the problem as one of disconnected tools. "Audience targeting, optimization, and measurement have long operated as separate workflows," it states. For advertisers, it lists three outcomes:
- "Smarter audience targeting based on actual purchase behavior"
- "Stronger optimization driven by purchase-based performance signals"
- "Measurement tied to sales outcomes, not just impressions and clicks"
None of the three comes with a figure, a case study or a test result.
How the arrangement is meant to work
The release describes a loop with four stages. Read in order, it says:
- Circana supplies "purchase intelligence," which the release says "is built on an expansive, high-quality data set and six decades of domain expertise in measuring what consumers buy across consumer packaged goods and retail."
- Two Circana capabilities, Intelligence Stream and Sales Lift, integrate with two Trade Desk products, Audience Unlimited and Open Measurement. The release names all four together and does not say which Circana capability connects to which Trade Desk product.
- Campaigns run with audiences built on "actual purchase behavior" and optimisation "driven by purchase-based performance signals."
- Sales outcomes are measured, and those outcomes "inform future audience and optimization decisions," closing what the release calls "a continuous feedback loop."
The pairing that looks most likely is Intelligence Stream with Audience Unlimited, for targeting, and Sales Lift with Open Measurement, for measurement. That reading is PPC Land's inference from the product names. The release does not confirm it.
The four components
Two partners in two days
The two announcements describe the same product, issued 48 hours apart, by companies whose data comes from very different places.
The Attain column paraphrases the November 17 announcement that named Attain the inaugural data partner. The Circana column quotes today's release.
The contrast in data sources is sharp. Attain's announcement presented its data as independent of retailer data feeds, panel projections and survey recall. Circana's business rests largely on the first two: retailer point-of-sale data and household panels. Along with NIQ, it is one of the two main suppliers of syndicated research on retail sales and household panels, and the two companies jointly run the National Consumer Panel. Circana's website describes its data as combining retail, point-of-sale, market and consumer panel sources. The Trade Desk has now named both kinds of supplier for one product without saying how their results will be combined, weighted or reconciled when they disagree.
Kristi Argyilan, The Trade Desk's chief commercial officer, is quoted in both releases. She joined the company in July after leading Uber's advertising business. Her closing line today drops the reference to "self-graded homework" that appeared in Attain's release and credits Circana instead.
What the two executives said
Argyilan opened with the case against proxies. "Brands have been asked to prove that great media works using clicks and proxies, when the question they actually care about is whether people bought," she said. "Circana knows what consumers actually buy, and bringing that purchase data into Open Measurement lets advertisers see how each touchpoint contributed to sales and gives publishers credit for the demand their media helps create. Open Measurement is built on real transactions, and Circana is a big reason why."
Cara Pratt, president of global retail and media for Circana, spoke about the whole campaign rather than its final step. "Advertisers are under pressure to prove what their media drives, and that takes a view that spans the full campaign, not just the last click or impression," she said. "By bringing our purchase intelligence into The Trade Desk's ecosystem, we're connecting audience activation, optimization, and measurement in one workflow. Advertisers can see which audiences and investments drive incremental sales and invest with confidence."
The two statements use different measurement language. Argyilan describes seeing "how each touchpoint contributed to sales," which is the vocabulary of attribution: dividing credit for a sale among the ads a buyer encountered. Pratt speaks of "incremental sales," which is the vocabulary of incrementality: estimating the sales that would not have happened without the advertising, usually by comparing exposed people with a similar group who were not exposed. The two methods answer different questions and often give different answers. The release does not say which one Open Measurement uses, or whether it uses both.
Circana's measurement business
Circana's lineage runs through two research companies. According to a Trade Desk release from November 2023, it was "previously known as IRI and The NPD Group." It has since moved deeper into advertising measurement. In August 2024 it agreed to buy NCSolutions and Nielsen's marketing mix modelling business, and at that time it described its client base as nearly 7,000 brands and retailers. NCSolutions was one of the established suppliers of sales lift studies, which compare what people exposed to an ad bought with what a comparable control group bought over the same period. Circana completed that purchase on June 2, 2025.
Circana also measures media for the companies that sell it. On October 7, it published a marketing mix study with Best Buy Ads which claimed that nearly 16% of the sales associated with Best Buy's retail media occurred at rival retailers, and that Best Buy Ads' return on ad spend was 79% higher than all other media on Best Buy's own US sales. That release named no brands, period, spend levels or sample size. Its work now spans brands, retailers that sell advertising and an ad-buying platform.
The relationship with The Trade Desk predates today. On November 3, 2023, the two companies announced a partnership in Australia that The Trade Desk described as the first time a demand-side platform had worked directly with Circana in that country. It covered store-level measurement across large grocery, pharmacy, petrol and convenience retailers, comparing in-store sales at locations where ads ran with similar locations where they did not, and audience targeting that included modelled propensity to purchase from Circana's consumer panel. Today's release describes an "expanded partnership" but does not say what the existing partnership covered, whether the Australian arrangement continues, or whether there was a prior US agreement.
The same data on both sides of the campaign
The loop the release describes is a familiar one. A closed loop exists when the party serving an ad can also see the resulting sale, so outcomes feed back into reporting and bidding. Retail media networks have offered it for years, because the retailer both sells the ad and records the purchase. The Trade Desk's version would put a third-party data company in the retailer's position.
That design has a known weakness, and it is the same one The Trade Desk has used against its rivals. When one dataset chooses who sees an ad and then measures whether they bought, the result can credit the campaign for reaching people who were already likely to buy. On the November 9 earnings call, chief executive Jeff Green said the walled gardens of Amazon and Google grade their own homework, and that The Trade Desk does not own the media it is trying to sell. That is accurate: The Trade Desk buys media on behalf of advertisers rather than selling its own inventory. It does, however, earn its revenue as a share of the spending that runs through its platform, and Circana would now supply the purchase data used both to target campaigns and to measure them. Whether control groups are drawn so that the targeting data cannot flatter the measurement is a technical question the release does not address.
The release also leaves open how ad exposure is matched to purchases. In the 2023 Australian arrangement, the comparison was between stores near which ads ran and similar stores without them. A household-level match would require identifiers linking people who saw ads to people in Circana's panels or loyalty data, and some form of clean room or privacy-preserving match. Today's release mentions neither approach.
Audience Unlimited and the data bill
Audience Unlimited is The Trade Desk's answer to the cost of third-party data. When it was announced on September 29, 2025, the company said advertisers using third-party data typically spent close to 20% of their media costs on it, and offered an AI-scored data marketplace in two modes: Control Mode at tiered rates of 3.3% and 4.4% of impression costs, and Performance Mode, in which the product is included at no additional cost. It works as an audience marketplace, in which segments from many providers are bundled and scored for relevance to each campaign rather than bought one by one.
