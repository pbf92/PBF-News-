TITLE: Study Reveals Conjunctival Autografting Reduces Pterygium Recurrence Compared With Mitomycin C
OUTLET: Eyewire+

Key Takeaways
- Conjunctival autografting demonstrated the lowest 12-month recurrence rate among the 3 surgical approaches evaluated
- Intraoperative mitomycin C was more effective than amniotic membrane transplantation and may offer advantages in cost and conjunctival tissue preservation
- No intervention-related serious adverse events were reported across the 3 treatment groups
Conjunctival autografting (CAG) was more effective in preventing postoperative recurrence following primary pterygium excision than intraoperative mitomycin C (MMC) or amniotic membrane transplantation (AMT), according to findings from a randomized clinical trial published in JAMA Ophthalmology.1
The multicenter, open-label trial, conducted by Wen-yan Peng, MD, PhD, and colleagues at 5 tertiary hospitals in China, enrolled 750 adults aged 18 to 80 years with primary pterygium. Investigators randomly assigned participants to undergo standardized pterygium excision combined with AMT (n = 249), MMC (n = 248), or CAG (n = 253).
The primary endpoint was postoperative recurrence at 12 months. Secondary end points included best-corrected visual acuity, epithelial healing time, and conjunctival congestion. Overall, 681 participants (90.8%) completed the follow-up period.
In the intention-to-treat analysis, 12-month recurrence rates were 5.0% with conjunctival autografting, 13.7% with intraoperative mitomycin C, and 22.9% with amniotic membrane transplantation.
CAG reduced the absolute risk of recurrence by 8.7 percentage points compared with MMC (P = .001) and 17.9 percentage points compared with AMT (P<.001). MMC also demonstrated greater efficacy than AMT (P = .01).
Adverse events were reported in 6.3% of patients receiving CAG, 7.2% receiving AMT, and 11.3% receiving MMC. No treatment-related serious adverse events were reported.
The investigators noted that although CAG demonstrated the greatest efficacy in preventing recurrence, MMC may remain a practical alternative because of its potential cost advantages and preservation of conjunctival tissue.
The findings support consideration of recurrence risk alongside other clinical and surgical factors when individualizing treatment for patients undergoing primary pterygium excision.
