TITLE: VentureBeat bug-fix test finds Anthropic’s top Claude model costs triple for similar results
OUTLET: Crypto Briefing

Claude Fable 5.2 and Opus 5.5 finished about the same amount of work, but one bill came in roughly three times higher
Anthropic’s flagship model, Claude Fable 5.2, fixed 10 of 15 bugs in a VentureBeat pilot test. The total bill came to about $89.
Its cheaper sibling, Opus 5.5, fixed 9 of 15 for about $29. That is roughly a third of the price for one fewer fix.
VentureBeat’s own read is that a single-pass gap is too small to call. In practical terms, the two models did about the same amount of work. One of them simply charged a lot more for it.
What the test actually measured
The pilot was narrow by design. Each model got 15 attempts at fixing real bugs, and VentureBeat tracked two things: how many fixes passed, and how much the whole run cost.
The scoreboard reads 10 passes for Fable 5.2 and 9 for Opus 5.5. The cost gap is far harder to wave away. About $89 versus about $29 is not a rounding error.
AI coding agents rarely run once. Companies deploying them point them at large backlogs of tickets, again and again. A model that costs about three times as much per batch of work turns a modest pilot gap into a meaningful budget line at scale.
The harness problem underneath the headline numbers
VentureBeat published a study on October 23, 2026, looking at how coding “harnesses” shape both performance and cost.
A harness is the software wrapper around a model. It decides how the AI reads files, runs tests, retries failed attempts, and talks to itself between steps.

The study compared setups including Claude Code, mini-SWE-agent, and OpenCode. Its central finding: running the same model through different harnesses changed the cost per completed task by as much as threefold.
Accuracy, meanwhile, barely moved. Pass rates on SWE-bench Verified, a standard benchmark for software engineering tasks, stayed statistically unchanged across those setups.
One example from the study used the GLM-5.3-Flash model. Paired with Claude Code, it was nearly twice as expensive as with mini-SWE-agent. Against OpenCode, it cost more than three times as much. None of that extra spending bought better accuracy.
The research also pointed to a case running the other direction. Claude Opus 5, using newer extended-thinking features, solved 34 of 45 tasks at 44% of the estimated API cost of an earlier harness configuration. Per trial, that worked out to $0.73 instead of roughly $1.65.
Background: the “HarnessTax” idea
VentureBeat’s findings build on earlier work from UC Berkeley’s Sky Computing Lab. That group described the extra cost from harness choices as a “HarnessTax.”
VentureBeat’s study adds hard numbers to the concept, showing how much that hidden overhead can swing total cost of ownership.
Anthropic’s lineup was central to the testing and the surrounding coverage. Opus 5.5 and the Fable 5.x series featured prominently in 2026 discussions of pricing and performance for agentic coding.
What this means for teams buying AI coding tools
The clearest lesson is that the top-tier model is not automatically the best value. In this pilot, Fable 5.2’s edge over Opus 5.5 was one pass out of 15, and VentureBeat would not call it meaningful.
The harness research suggests that model selection is only half the decision. Two companies running the identical model could see costs differ by as much as threefold purely because of the tooling around it.
Benchmark leaderboards typically report pass rates, which this study found can stay flat while costs swing wildly. A model that looks marginally better on accuracy could look meaningfully worse once the invoice is attached.
