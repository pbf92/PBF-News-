Why Did NewMarket Stock Fall After Earnings?
The answer lies in volumes. NewMarket's profit rose year on year in the third quarter. Shipments of petroleum additives, its core business, fell again. The market focused on the second point.
NewMarket stock earnings came out after the close on 28 October. The next day the shares lost 8.04% to $803.07 on volume of 227,039. The selling then ran for another five sessions, according to Stock Analysis.

Higher operating profit and a lower effective tax rate drove the rise in net income, the company said in its Q3 release. Over the first nine months, net income reached $375.9 million, or $40.64 per share.
Shipments Fell for a Third Quarter
The volume trend is the main concern. The Q3 release came from Chairman and CEO Thomas E. Gottwald. It linked the drop to "broader market softness in lubricant additives that resulted in an 8% decline in shipments", per the company's results release.
Source: NewMarket Q1 2026 earnings call transcript, The Motley Fool and Q3 2026 results release, Business Wire via Finviz. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results.
Weaker lubricant additives demand matters because petroleum additives earn most of the group's profit. The segment is run through Afton Chemical. NewMarket also owns Ethyl.
Surcharges Held Margins Up
Pricing did the heavy lifting. The company said it "successfully implemented surcharges to offset higher costs incurred due to the supply chain disruptions in the Middle East", according to the Q3 release. Those surcharges remain in place.
The open question is how long this lasts. If input costs ease, surcharges could be reduced. Margins would then depend more on volume, and volume has been falling.
Specialty Materials Add Growth
The specialty materials segment includes AMPAC. It makes ammonium perchlorate for solid rocket propulsion and high-purity hydrazine. It also includes Calca Solutions, bought on 1 October 2025. Capacity expansion for both products is expected to be completed by the end of 2026, per the Q3 release.

TIC Flows: What the September Data Showed
The US Treasury released its September data on 18 November at 4:00 PM ET (21:00 UK). Net long-term TIC flows came in at $155.3 billion, according to Trading Economics. That was down from a record $285.0 billion in August and slightly below the $160.7 billion consensus.
What Are TIC Flows?
TIC stands for Treasury International Capital. The monthly report tracks how much foreign investors buy and sell of US securities. Long-term flows cover bonds and equities with maturities over one year. Government and corporate debt are both included. The data offer a read on foreign demand for US assets.
The report comes with a lag of about six weeks. The August data, for example, came out in October, as shown in the Treasury International Capital Data for August 2026 release.

Why the Headline Drop Needs Context
The long-term figure fell by about 45.5% from August. Total net flows, though, rose to about $261 billion. Foreign capital kept coming into the US in September. The fall in the long-term figure mostly reflects a return from a record month.
Still, the trend matters for sentiment. A slower pace of foreign buying of long-dated US securities can add to pressure on bond prices when rates are rising. Higher yields raise the discount rate applied to company profits. That can weigh on equity valuations, including for steady earners like NEU.

How Do Rate Hikes Affect Stocks Like NEU?
Rate hikes tend to hit stocks through three channels. Each one works differently.
- Higher yields lower the present value of future profits, which tends to compress price to earnings multiples.
- A stronger dollar reduces the value of sales earned abroad once they are converted back.
- Slower growth can reduce industrial demand for lubricant additives.
NEU has some cushions. Its P/E of about 14.3 is modest, and its debt load is moderate. That may limit the valuation effect. The volume channel may be the larger risk, given three quarters of falling shipments.
