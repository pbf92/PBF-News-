TITLE: Topical Insulin Shows Greater Diabetic Ulcer Reduction, but Evidence Is Very Uncertain
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and meta-analysis evaluating topical dressings for diabetic foot ulcers found that topical insulin achieved a greater percentage reduction in ulcer size than topical phenytoin, though the direct comparative evidence supporting this difference remains very uncertain. Across all four clinical wound-healing outcomes examined in the meta-analysis, the certainty of the available evidence was formally rated as very low under the GRADE framework.
Diabetic foot ulcers are a common and serious complication of diabetes mellitus. Both topical insulin and topical phenytoin have been proposed as low-cost therapeutic agents to accelerate diabetic foot ulcer healing. However, direct comparative evidence evaluating these two treatments had not previously been synthesized, and the certainty of the comparative evidence had not been graded.
To address this gap, researchers conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, with the protocol registered in PROSPERO under registration number CRD420261434269. The authors conducted comprehensive searches across PubMed, Embase, Scopus, the Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar from database inception up to 1 April 2026. The search methodology also incorporated grey-literature searches and citation searching to locate all eligible comparative studies.
The review included randomized comparative studies that directly compared topical insulin dressings with topical phenytoin dressings in diabetic foot ulcers. Two reviewers independently screened the identified studies, extracted relevant data, and assessed the risk of bias using the RoB 2 tool. Risk ratios and mean differences were pooled across trials using random-effects models, while the certainty of evidence for each outcome was rated using the GRADE approach.
The systematic review included five randomized comparative studies, all conducted in India, comprising a total of 304 participants. These participants were evenly distributed between the two study arms, with 152 individuals assigned to receive topical insulin dressings and 152 individuals assigned to receive topical phenytoin dressings.
When evaluating ulcer size reduction, topical insulin was associated with a greater percentage reduction in ulcer size across two studies, with a pooled mean difference of 15.79 (95% CI 9.27 to 22.31; p < 0.0001). However, healthy granulation tissue did not differ significantly between the two dressing groups across four studies, showing a pooled risk ratio of 1.12 (95% CI 0.98 to 1.27; p = 0.0896).
Similarly, the other evaluated outcomes showed no statistically significant differences between the two topical agents. Time to healing was shorter with topical insulin across two studies, but the difference was not statistically significant, yielding a mean difference of − 5.29 days (95% CI − 10.83 to 0.24; p = 0.0608). In addition, ulcer size measured after follow-up did not differ between groups across two studies, demonstrating a pooled mean difference of 1.62 (95% CI − 4.01 to 7.25; p = 0.5734).
The certainty of evidence was rated as very low for all four evaluated outcomes, with the downgrade driven by risk of bias, inconsistency, and imprecision. While direct evidence suggests a possible advantage of topical insulin for some wound-healing outcomes, the researchers emphasized that the overall evidence remains very uncertain. Furthermore, because the only statistically significant finding rests on just two studies, the authors stated that it should be interpreted cautiously.
Based on these findings, the study authors concluded that adequately powered, preregistered, multicenter randomized trials with standardized preparations and standardized outcomes are needed before either topical insulin or topical phenytoin can be recommended over the other.
