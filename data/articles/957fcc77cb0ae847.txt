TITLE: She had a lover behind bars, and her messages landed her there as well
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

A woman from Salford has been convicted and sentenced after an investigation into a prison drug-smuggling conspiracy. Seven people have now been brought to justice after the police probe, including a prison officer.
The conspiracy at HMP Lowdham Grange, near Nottingham, came to light in 2023 when a sniffer dog indicated prison officer Leah Robinson was carrying concealed drugs inside. Police said the discovery sparked a wider investigation under which an 'organised network' was uncovered.
Robinson, 25, had entered into 'an inappropriate relationship' with prisoner Patryk Dziewiatkowski, 32, police said. She was found attempting to smuggle drugs into the prison on her body using latex gloves.

The investigation, said police, also revealed the involvement of several prisoners and former prisoners.
A court heard Robinson was a courier, receiving drugs before breaking them down into smaller quantities and transporting them into the prison. Cannabis and handwritten love letters were found in a search of her home.
Robinson's phone was seized. Police found messages between her and Casileigh Jones, the sister of serving prisoner Jordan Jones, said police.
Messages between Robinson and Bilal Khan, an associate of serving prisoner Oscar Ingram, were also discovered, police added.
A spokesperson for the East Midlands Special Operations Unit said: "Robinson later met Casileigh Jones and Khan on six occasions, collecting drugs that she subsequently smuggled into the prison.
"Later, in July 2023, Dziewiatkowski was deported to Poland. Despite this, detectives found he continued to organise the operation from abroad, directing Robinson to bring drugs into the prison and using his mother's bank account to move money. More than £17,000 was seized from Khan's address as part of the investigation."
A financial investigation later identified Dziewiatkowski's mother, Malgorzata Szubrych, as providing funds to Robinson and 'facilitating communication between her son and Robinson during the conspiracy'.
All seven were charged in relation to the plot to bring cocaine and cannabis into the prison. Robinson and Dziewiatkowski were also charged with misconduct in public office, which they both admitted.
Casileigh Jones, 34, of Gloucester Place, Salford, was charged with conspiracy to supply a class B drug and pleaded guilty. At Nottingham Crown Court on November 20, all seven defendants were sentenced.
Robinson, 25, of Waterfords, Cherry Willingham, admitted misconduct in public office and was jailed for two years and four months. Patryk Dziewiatkowski, 32, formally of HMP Nottingham, was jailed for four years and seven months. Jordan Jones, 32, of HMP Thorn Cross, got two years and one month.
Casileigh Jones received a five-month sentence, suspended for 18 months, with the sentences of others also being suspended.
Detective Inspector Charlie Nicholls, of the Regional Prisons Intelligence Unit, said: "This was an extensive and complex investigation that brought down a major drugs enterprise.
"Narcotics in our prison system not only undermine rehabilitation efforts, but can also cause significant harm to prisoners."
