TITLE: A Guide to The Best Way To Play GTA 6, According To Rockstar
OUTLET: Kotaku

Tens of millions of people are playing Grand Theft Auto 6 right now. What’s the best way to experience Rockstar’s latest open-world epic? According to one of the developers themselves, you should play it with as little anxiety around checklists and online noise as possible.
“There is so much to do in Leonida, and so much to discover, that we hope players get to the point where they stop thinking in tasks, completion percentages, online guides and rather let the game unfold in front of them,” VP of design Stephen Donkin said in a packet of studio development notes shared with press. “When you play the game and take your time with it, playing at the pace it asks you to, it can really unfold in front of you in a way that can feel almost infinite at a macro level, but each individual encounter still feels hand-crafted and with a human behind it.
Donkin’s first Rockstar credit was as a QA tester on GTA 5, and his advice for GTA 6 players echoed the feelings of some of the studio’s other developers when it comes to taking in the immersive beauty and emergent nuance of the new entry’s complex world. “There are clearly tons of high-stakes, high-energy, fast-paced moments and thrills and laughs along the way, but there are also quiet, meditative experiences and adventures to be had that offer a fully different feeling,” said senior design director Ghyan Koehne. “If I had to be held to a single feeling, I’d say ‘wonder.’ We want players to be constantly presented with a feeling of wonder—we hope everyone feels a mixture of emotions.”
It’s a stark contrast to how GTA lives in the popular imagination of most people, which is as a chaos sim full of drugs, sex, and violent crime. GTA 6 has those things, but they are far less domineering parts of the experience this time around. It’s also completely divorced from how some people often experience modern video games, which is heavily augmented by a second-screen experience full of achievement guides, TikTok memes, and social media rage-bait.
People should play GTA 6 however they want, but I’d encourage them to take at least a few hours to experience the game in the dark with headphones on and as little knowledge as possible of what awaits them and how the whole thing works, their phones and laptops safely tucked away in a different room. Former Kotaku editor Kirk Hamilton was famous for telling people to turn off parts of the onscreen UI to make the experience more immersive and make it feel less like a chore list. Every big new open-world game that came out there would be a post about turning off the minimap.
I’ve never been able to play games that way. I’m too fidgety and squirrely for that kind of thing. But I always try to spend at least a couple hours in that headspace, treating what’s in front of me more as something worth experiencing for its own sake than just as a maze to speedrun through as efficiently as possible in exchange for in-game rewards, entertaining setpieces, and satisfying emotional climaxes. That’s an increasingly alien way of approaching anything in our algorithmically optimized world, but GTA 6 offers as good a reason as any to try it.
“If players can let go and submit to becoming immersed in the world of Leonida, and just follow their nose as they play, they will feel the Leonida I have felt,” Donkin said, “And that’s all we could ask for.”
