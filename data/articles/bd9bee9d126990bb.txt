TITLE: Google adds task checklists to Antigravity and Carbon for Pro
OUTLET: TestingCatalog AI News

Google is rolling out a wave of Antigravity updates, announced on X by Varun Mohan, who builds Google DeepMind's agent-first coding platform. Gemini quotas have been reset for everyone on Antigravity as Google upgrades models on every tier. Paid Google AI Pro users now get access to Gemini 4 Carbon, and Gemini 4 Hydrogen is opening up to all users, as Google had promised. Varun said the upgrade is still rolling out, so some users may see the reset before Carbon shows up in their model list.
As part of the same change, Google is dropping the separate Argon quota bucket for paid Pro users. That capacity moves into the general Gemini bucket, so Pro subscribers can spend it on Argon, Carbon, or Gemini 4 Neon, the model Varun keeps pointing users to for agent swarms.
Varun also said banked resets are in the works, while Google figures out how to structure them and still predict its capacity requirements.
Two workflow updates land alongside the quota changes. A new Orchestrator Settings panel lets users choose the planner and the executor separately, each with its own effort level. The orchestrator model plans the work, makes design decisions, and reviews the result, while the executor carries out searches, edits, builds, and tests; the orchestrator can also be picked per conversation from the model menu.
Varun runs Gemini 4 Neon High as orchestrator with Gemini 4 Hydrogen High as executor, and Google says a larger orchestrator paired with a faster, cheaper executor gives high quality for fewer tokens.
Antigravity also now shows checklists with live status for long-running tasks, listing each step, marking parallel steps as running, and counting completed ones. Varun called the missing visibility one of the biggest issues with the experience and one of the most requested additions, useful on hard tasks where users do not want side chats. Antigravity team member Rohan Phadte said checklists should be live for all users on version 2.34.0.
Antigravity is Google DeepMind's agent-first software development platform, and these changes follow the arrival of Gemini 4 Neon and Orchestrator (Neon) on eligible paid consumer plans, with Neon planning the work and Hydrogen subagents implementing it. Resetting quotas, merging buckets, and widening model access is a clear bet on getting more developers to run multi-agent /swarm workflows, which head of product engineering Kevin Hou is also promoting. Mohan hinted that more is coming.
