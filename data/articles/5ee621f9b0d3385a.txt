TITLE: Wall Street's brutal week brought a string of ugly stock-market milestones. Here's the damage.
OUTLET: Business Insider

I'm not going to sugarcoat it: Last week was an unmitigated disaster in the stock market.
To be fair, between Nvidia earnings and the Anthropic IPO, we knew things could get dicey. But the sell-off was deeper, broader, and more relentless than most investors anticipated.
Stock futures point to a Monday rebound, but one green open won't erase last week's damage. To show just how treacherous it's been, here's a list of the stock market's most unceremonious achievements last week, in no particular order:
Major US indexes (S&P 500, Dow, Nasdaq Composite) all posted their worst weeks in years.
The Nasdaq lost 10% over the five days, while the Dow shed 8%. Both marked their worst weeks since March 2020. The S&P 500, meanwhile, slid 9%, its biggest weekly loss since April 2025.
All 3 indexes closed in correction territory, down 10%-plus from recent highs
While the Nasdaq was already down 13% YTD through Thursday, the Dow and S&P 500 clinched their corrections on Friday.
The S&P 500's 7-day losing streak was the longest since 2020
If the benchmark index finishes today lower — for an eighth straight session — that will be the lengthiest losing streak since 2016.
The Nasdaq Composite erased its year-to-date gains
The S&P 500 (+1% YTD) and Dow (+0.2%) are hanging on by a thread after last week's selling storm. The Nasdaq, meanwhile, is now down 2.7% for 2026.
Nvidia's 7th straight weekly decline was its longest since 2008
The stock has fallen in nine of the last 10 trading sessions since scandal erupted around former CEO Jensen Huang. Shares have plummeted 33% over the period, capped off by a 14% loss on Friday alone. Nvidia has now lost $1 trillion in market value since Huang resigned.
The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index is down 43% from record highs
Among the most high-profile losers last week were Micron (-24%), Marvell Technology (-22%), AMD (-21%), TSMC (-20%), and — of course — Nvidia (-23%).
Neoclouds are getting wrecked too
The Roundhill Neocloud ETF plummeted 25% last week, with major names Iren (-35%), Nebius (-30%), and CoreWeave (-26%) paving the way.
Surely you get the point now.
Well, unfortunately, I have more bad news to pile on. Looking ahead at the calendar, there's not a ton to get excited about. And many of the negative forces afflicting markets remain unresolved, chief among them:
- Nvidia's two-week plunge at a time when AI sentiment was already tenuous
- Anthropic's blockbuster debut losing steam on day two, denting AI optimism
- A near-unanimous expectation that the Fed will raise interest rates at their next meeting on Dec. 9
A catalyst desert
When it comes to potential upside catalysts, the cupboard is pretty bare. Earnings season is basically over, with Nvidia's report last week tying a bow on a thoroughly underwhelming quarter. And the next major economic release — the November jobs report — won't arrive until after Thanksgiving.
Could oil be a saving grace?
While investors have been pulling their hair out over stocks, the price of Brent crude has fallen more than 20% in November, including 8% last week. Investors certainly fretted about oil on the way up, but so far the AI narrative has overshadowed its decline. If traders turn their attention toward the relief, a bounce could materialize.
The future of the AI trade
For the first time in a while it feels like the AI trade is under an all-out assault. Until now, its biggest names have repeatedly absorbed shocks and bounced back. But how many more hits can it absorb, especially in the absence of any truly exciting catalyst?
We're finding out the answer in real time.
