TITLE: Manager accounts
OUTLET: PPC Land

Manager accounts is a dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that labels each row of a report with the manager account sitting above the advertiser account that ran the ads. A manager account holds no campaigns. It is the layer agencies, holding companies and multi-brand advertisers use to link many advertiser accounts, give staff access and pay invoices from one place. The reporting field exists because unified reports can now draw on several manager accounts at once, and without a column naming the parent, rows from two agency divisions or two brand groups would sit in the same table with nothing to separate them.
The general concept, as Google, Microsoft and Amazon use it, is covered in PPC Land's explainer on the manager account. This article deals with the Amazon field and the Amazon structure behind it.
Where the field sits
Amazon's unified reporting dimensions reference, updated on November 11, 2026, lists 156 dimensions in 11 categories. The account-level group includes Advertiser account ID, Advertiser account name, Manager accounts, Entity ID and Ad product. PPC Land's count of the 156 dimensions noted that advertiser account ID, advertiser account name and manager accounts "apply to all five products": DVA+, Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display and Sponsored TV.
That universality sets the field apart. Many unified dimensions are product-specific, and Amazon's rule for them is that "when a dimension does not apply to an ad product, its value appears as null." Manager accounts should not hit that case, because every Amazon ad product runs inside an advertiser account, and a report scoped by manager account only pulls in advertiser accounts linked to one. An advertiser account reported on directly, with no manager link, is the obvious source of an empty cell.
The field name is plural. Amazon's published material reviewed for this article does not spell out how a row is labelled when its advertiser account is linked to more than one manager account, for example to a brand's own manager and to its agency's. The plural suggests the column can hold more than one value, a reading not confirmed with Amazon.
How Amazon manager accounts work
An Amazon manager account is created separately from any advertiser account. Linking is a request-and-approve process. According to Amazon's Manager account user guide, an undated nine-page document, the manager's administrator goes to "Link accounts", chooses "Request access" and adds the Entity ID of the advertiser account in question. The tool then generates a link that is sent "via email to an admin user on the account for your approval." The advertiser approves under "Access and settings" and then "Manager account". Either side can end it: "Manager account admins can unlink accounts at any time," the guide says.
The guide describes two roles. An admin can "view an overview of performance metrics, link accounts, invite users, manage user access, run ads, and edit campaigns for all linked accounts." The second role is contributor, with narrower rights than an admin. One user invitation at manager level "enables Amazon Advertising API access to all accounts within the manager account."
The guide also states a ceiling: "at current, you can add a maximum of 500 advertiser entities (e.g. Entity IDs) to your manager account." The document is undated, so the figure may have changed.
The Amazon Ads API exposes the same object. Documentation for the open-source python-amazon-ad-api client lists four operations: list, create, associate and disassociate manager accounts. Each manager is addressed by a managerAccountId, and a managerAccountType field takes the value "Advertiser" or "Agency". Accounts can be associated by account ID or by DSP advertiser ID, with roles named ENTITY_OWNER, ENTITY_USER, ENTITY_VIEWER and SELLER_USER. A single call can pass "a maximum of 20 accounts/advertisers using any mix of identifiers," according to that documentation. It is a third-party mirror of Amazon's reference, not Amazon's own page.
What the column does in a report
In the unified report builder, the manager account is first a scope choice. Amazon's general availability notice of June 8, 2026 describes "one report that includes campaigns across multiple manager or advertiser accounts, ad products, countries, metrics, and dimensions." Once a report spans more than one manager account, the Manager accounts dimension becomes the way to group or filter the results by that parent.
A worked example shows the effect. Suppose an agency runs two manager accounts, one for its consumer electronics clients and one for its grocery clients, with 40 and 25 advertiser accounts beneath them. A single unified report scoped to both returns rows for all 65 advertiser accounts and every product they use. Grouping by Advertiser account name gives 65 lines. Grouping by Manager accounts collapses them to two, which is the view a finance team needs when the two divisions carry separate fee arrangements or profit and loss statements.
The same structure underpins frequency groups. Since April 2, 2026, Amazon has measured shared frequency caps at manager and cross-manager account level, not only inside one advertiser account. The separate Frequency group type dimension records "the level at which the frequency cap is created, such as advertiser or manager account."
Origin and evolution
Amazon rolled out the manager account on October 1, 2020, for seller and vendor accounts in the United States, Canada, Mexico, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy and Spain. It offered cross-account insights and alerts, metric export, a consolidated billing list and access management.
Seven weeks later, on November 19, 2020, Amazon extended the tool across both of its buying products. According to its announcement, advertisers "now have a unified view of both sponsored ads and Amazon DSP advertising in a single dashboard," with a structure that "reflects the entirety of your business with Amazon."
On June 15, 2022, at its unBoxed event, Amazon made manager accounts global. Accounts from any of 18 listed marketplaces could be linked, and according to the announcement, "all new manager accounts will be global by default."
The next change added depth. On April 15, 2024, Amazon introduced nested manager accounts, so that a parent manager could hold child managers organised by operating company, region or business unit. Amazon's note said users could "create a multi-level or hierarchical group of accounts." It gave no maximum depth.
From October 2025 the unified advertiser account began merging product registrations into one advertiser account, with invoices sent either to "the manager account or advertiser account directly." Reporting followed: unified reporting opened in beta on November 11, 2025 with filters by manager account, advertiser account, campaign, country and ad product, and reached general availability on June 8, 2026.
Why it matters
The manager account has become the place where Amazon puts commercial controls, and the reporting field is how those controls are audited. On November 11, 2026, Amazon updated its documentation so that agencies set fees for Display, Video and Audio campaigns from the manager account, with the legacy fee setting available until January 31, 2027. A fee rule covering "all accounts" under a manager needs a report that totals spend at that level.
Account consolidation pushes the same way. When DSP advertisers moved to a single global account across 34 countries on July 30, 2026, PPC Land asked whether the agency was the next thing Amazon would remove. Fewer advertiser accounts per brand leaves the manager account as the main boundary between agency and client.
Limitations and open questions
Three problems stand out. The first is definition. PPC Land's review of the reference did not quote a definition for Manager accounts or a former name, while it did record that Advertiser account ID was previously called "Advertiser ID" in DVA+. How multiple linked managers are rendered remains unconfirmed.
The second is stability over time. Links can be removed by either side, and nested managers can be rearranged. Amazon's material does not say whether historical rows keep the manager label in force when the ads ran or are relabelled with the current structure. An agency that loses a client, or reorganises its divisions mid-year, could see past totals shift.
The third is ownership. Manager accounts serve agencies, but control sits with the advertiser who approves or revokes the link. Amazon's new advertiser account rules, reported on November 17, 2026, require the client's legal name rather than the agency's on new accounts, reinforcing that the advertiser account belongs to the client while the manager account belongs to whoever operates it.
Manager accounts and similar terms
Manager account (the concept). The account type itself, common to Google Ads, Microsoft Advertising and Amazon Ads. The reporting field is only Amazon's label for it in unified reports.
Advertiser account ID and Advertiser account name. These identify the account that owns the campaigns, one level below the manager. A manager groups many advertiser accounts; each advertiser account row has exactly one ID.
Entity ID. The identifier Amazon uses to link an advertiser account to a manager, per the user guide. It points to the advertiser entity, not to the manager.
Frequency group type. A Targeting dimension recording whether a shared frequency cap was created at advertiser or manager level. It describes a cap, not the account hierarchy of the row.
Recent developments
On September 29, 2026, Amazon DSP was rebranded as part of Display, Video and Audio, with DVA+ the programmatic campaign type. Amazon's Support Center page on creating advertiser accounts, updated on November 16, 2026, tells users in Campaign Manager to "choose a Manager account that has access to DVA+" and offers billing to the manager account or the advertiser account, as PPC Land reported. With fees, billing and DVA+ access routed through it, the manager account is increasingly the unit an agency reconciles.
