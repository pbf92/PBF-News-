TITLE: 'Grand Theft Auto VI': First Impressions
OUTLET: Yahoo Tech

Happy Grand Theft Auto VI Day to everyone who celebrates (and took off work to immerse themselves in the GTA V sequel). I'm only a few hours into GTA VI, but I'm already planning on packing my real bags and moving to the sunny, fictional state of Leonida. Before that, we gotta hit you with our first impressions, right?
The story's still picking up, and the tutorials are retreading GTA basics that anyone who's played one of its games for years already knows (with a few tweaks), so I'm going to largely avoid getting into what's happened so far and what I expect. The real star of the game so far is Leonida—it's vast, with blue portals to a maritime oasis, and its people, who feel more Floridian than actual, real Floridians. [Ed. Note: Some of us couldn't wait to see Rockstar Games breathe life into the "Florida Man" trend.] It's more than I expected, by far, and I'm excited to keep peeling back the game's layers.
It really does feel like Red Dead Redemption II

Let's start with some of the basics. GTA VI's presentation is unlike anything you've ever seen, with the closest comparison being Rockstar's Red Dead Redemption II. Shelves are stacked realistically with properly proportioned items. When prison guards take phone calls, they tap their fingers on clipboards as they wait for the person on the other end of the line to finish a long sentence. There's so much detail oozing out of every facet of this game that I'm constantly floored—and I'm still in the tutorial stage, for God's sake.
That presentation already extends to the little bit of story I've seen so far, told through splendidly animated cutscenes that don't just feel like static characters standing around talking. You could say the same for GTA V, but in GTA VI, it feels like you're genuinely watching a real movie unfold. Players spew realistic dialogue, featuring the verbal tics, accents, and mannerisms of real people you've met before in your life. It's utterly magical.
Jason and Lucia
Contrary to the complaints I've seen about his voice acting, I think Jason (one of the main characters besides Lucia) sounds great. People have criticized his accent and how it makes the dialogue hard to understand or feel flat, but I'm from Virginia. He's just got a Southern drawl I've heard plenty of times before. He's also, perhaps, the chillest GTA protagonist so far, but I don't think he's boring. I'm having a great time painting walls, fixing sinks, and even reading books. He's like a blank canvas you can project yourself onto. I think that's by design. Past GTA protagonists have been loud, over-the-top, and downright annoying (I'm looking at you, Trevor). It's a nice change of pace to be the cool guy in the room.
Lucia, on the other hand, is a way stronger character—but that's because she's given a bit more to chew on in the early hours. Where Jason is pretty stone-faced and just focused on money, Lucia is motivated by something I don't want to spoil. It makes her outbursts and anger feel warranted, and I'm excited to see how her story continues to shape.
Controls and handling
If you've played RDR2, you know about the weightier controls that make moving around the game slower, but more methodical. That continues here, for better or worse. It works in RDR2 because that game is a bit slower-paced, but I've found that in quick, chaotic moments in GTA VI, the same approach is just a tad clunkier. In one particular situation, I was surrounded by goons, each emptying clips into me as I tried to fire back to save my ass. Instead of hitting them in their heads, or bodies even, I'm firing bullets into the sand, nearby shacks, and beached boats. After several tense moments of sprinting to different pieces of cover and emptying the surprisingly generous ammo reserve, I beat the encounter, but I'm a bit nervous about how these controls will hold up as the game inevitably gets crazier.
I've been cruising around Leonida, taking in the sights from my retro vehicle. Enjoying the sway of the palm trees, stopping at stoplights, then flooring the engine when no one's around. Driving is much improved from GTA V, even if it's still pretty loose. There were times I was fighting Jason's car not to spin out—and I'd barely tapped the gas. Different cars control differently, and I've driven a few other vehicles that were a bit tighter, so maybe the looseness is by design, at least for that vehicle.
I Love Leonida
But Leonida is the star of the show for me so far. It feels so alive. I moved to Florida for a few months earlier this year, and I kept that in mind as I drove around the map. It feels lifted directly from real life in ways that gave me chills, from the eclectic personalities of its citizens to the lush plant life overflowing the city streets. I've already lost hours exploring its nooks and crannies, and I know I'll have to get back to the main story eventually. But this is the first game where the map is calling me to explore it, even without the promise of collectibles or cosmetic items. I know that stuff's there, but I'm not beelining to it on the map, or hoping a new scene pops up around every corner. I'm simply cruising, enjoying the sights, and hoping not to be interrupted while I take it all in.
There's a lot to love about GTA VI, and it's introducing me to its complex systems. My slight concerns about controls stem from comparisons to GTA V and RDR2, but I believe that as time goes on and I get used to how they work, I'll love this towering achievement of a game even more. Rockstar's finally created a truly living, breathing, and enviable world to get lost in. GTA VI makes a great first impression. It's time to see if it ultimately sticks the landing.
