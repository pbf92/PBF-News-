TITLE: Scoping Review Evaluates Ultrasound-Guided PDRN Injections for Diverse Musculoskeletal Disorders
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive scoping review published in Innovations in Acupuncture and Medicine has analyzed the clinical administration, procedural protocols, and therapeutic efficacy of polydeoxyribonucleotide pharmacopuncture across diverse human musculoskeletal disorders. Conducted by Taegwang Nam, Jeonghoon Lyu, Yujeong Kang, Seung-Yun Oh, and Tae Han Yook, the investigation systematically examined seventeen peer-reviewed clinical studies encompassing a collective cohort of one hundred and eighty-one patients. Polydeoxyribonucleotide, commonly abbreviated as PDRN, represents a bioactive polymer composed of specific deoxyribonucleic acid fragments with molecular weights spanning fifty to fifteen hundred kilodaltons. Extracted primarily from salmon testes, this biological agent has attracted expanding clinical interest within traditional Korean medicine and modern regenerative practice. The scoping review synthesized evidence evaluating how targeted needle injections of this marine compound influence structural healing, subjective discomfort, and objective mobility in patients presenting with cervical nerve injuries, complex regional pain syndromes, upper extremity tendinopathies, lumbosacral radiculopathies, knee osteoarthritis, ligament disruptions, and chronic plantar fasciitis.
The underlying pharmacological actions of polydeoxyribonucleotide are predominantly mediated through selective activation of cell-surface adenosine A2A receptors alongside metabolic stimulation of intracellular DNA salvage pathways. Laboratory and preclinical investigations highlighted within the review demonstrate that binding to the adenosine A2A receptor stimulates vascular endothelial growth factor expression, fostering neovascularization, cell differentiation, fibroblast proliferation, and collagen synthesis. Furthermore, the molecular compound exerts anti-inflammatory properties by diminishing inflammatory cascades and suppressing matrix metalloproteinase-13 gene expression, an enzyme responsible for articular cartilage collagen breakdown, while simultaneously inhibiting proteoglycan degradation within damaged connective matrices. Historically developed into the commercial formulation Placentex in Italy during nineteen ninety-four as a therapeutic agent for wound healing and ischemic disorders, clinical PDRN is manufactured from testicular tissues of chum salmon, Oncorhynchus keta, or rainbow trout, Oncorhynchus mykiss. Rigorous manufacturing involving purification, enzymatic digestion, and terminal sterilization eliminates immunogenic proteins to achieve purity exceeding ninety-five percent, while experimental extraction from botanical sources like Chrysanthemum coronarium and Brassica oleracea offers future plant-derived alternatives.
To evaluate the clinical landscape systematically, the investigative team executed a literature retrieval protocol designed in strict adherence to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines for scoping reviews. The authors searched five major electronic databases: PubMed, the Cochrane Library, ScienceON, the Korean Studies Information Service System, and the Oriental Medicine Advanced Searching Integrated System. The standardized search strategy identified four hundred and seven primary records, comprising three hundred and thirty-seven citations from PubMed, forty from the Cochrane Library, eighteen from ScienceON, twelve from KISS, and zero records from the OASIS platform. Following screening of titles and abstracts against predefined criteria requiring peer-reviewed human clinical trials and randomized controlled trials focused on musculoskeletal interventions, thirty-two articles underwent full-text assessment. Ultimately, fifteen studies were excluded due to irrelevance, leaving seventeen published studies that met all inclusion criteria for qualitative synthesis.
High-resolution ultrasound guidance served as the primary interventional technique in thirteen of the seventeen analyzed studies, reflecting an expanding clinical emphasis on precise needle tip visualization and accurate perineural or intrabursal deposition. Fluoroscopic C-arm guidance was documented in two clinical studies addressing complex spinal architectures, while conventional planar X-ray guidance directed needle placement in two additional spinal investigations, and a single study adhered to a standardized anatomical intra-articular knee protocol. The scoping review identified twenty-three distinct clinical assessment instruments implemented across the gathered literature to evaluate patient recovery. The Numeric Rating Scale represented the most frequently utilized pain measurement instrument, appearing in eight distinct studies, followed by the Visual Analog Scale across four investigations, while diverse functional questionnaires, manual muscle strength testing, goniometric range-of-motion assessments, and cross-sectional imaging tracked physical rehabilitation.
Anatomical categorization of therapeutic sites within the review encompassed two studies centered on the cervical spine, five investigations focused on the upper extremities, two studies examining the lumbosacral region, and eight studies addressing the lower extremities. In cervical spine applications, interventions targeted traumatic C5 radiculopathy and neuropathic pain arising from Complex Regional Pain Syndrome Type II. Specifically, a clinical report by Park DH documented an ultrasound-guided in-plane lateral approach delivering three milliliters of polydeoxyribonucleotide directly toward the injured left C5 nerve root across two weekly sessions. In a study conducted by Jang KS, three milliliters of PDRN was administered around the left L5 transverse process under C-arm visualization, demonstrating substantial relief of sensory hyperalgesia and autonomic temperature dysregulation.
Upper extremity interventions targeted complex neuromuscular and tendinous conditions, including postherpetic brachial plexopathy, rotator cuff tendinopathy, lateral epicondylitis of the elbow, and median nerve entrapment within the carpal tunnel. For postherpetic brachial plexopathy, Kim JY delivered three milliliters of PDRN mixed with seven milliliters of one percent lidocaine around the brachial plexus weekly for four weeks. In patients presenting with painful rotator cuff tendinopathy, Ryu KH injected three milliliters of PDRN combined with one milliliter of one percent lidocaine into the subacromial bursa, the supraspinatus tendon periphery, and partial tear crevices over three to five weeks. Following thirty-two injection sessions, Visual Analog Scale scores declined from five point three to one point eight at one week, maintaining one point seven at three months, while Shoulder Pain and Disability Index scores rose from forty-six point six to eighty-five point seven. For carpal tunnel syndrome, Park JS administered three milliliters at the volar wrist crease twice across one-week intervals, dropping pain scores from seven to two at six months and reducing median nerve cross-sectional area by nearly two millimeters.
Elbow tendinopathy investigations highlighted distinctive comparative efficacy profiles when polydeoxyribonucleotide was matched against conventional non-surgical modalities and physical rehabilitation. Lee GJ injected three milliliters of PDRN into the common extensor tendon origin using a twenty-seven-gauge needle for lateral epicondylitis, demonstrating post-treatment reductions in tendon hypervascularity under sonographic imaging despite unchanged structural tendinosis contours. In a randomized controlled trial, Shim BJ compared extensor muscle strengthening exercises alone against exercise combined with twenty-one-gauge PDRN injections or exercise paired with extracorporeal shockwave therapy. The PDRN combination cohort achieved statistically significant superiority over exercise alone across all primary measurement metrics, including Visual Analog Scale ratings, Mayo Elbow Performance Scores, and sonographic common extensor tendon defect dimensions. Furthermore, PDRN combined with rehabilitation exhibited statistically significant gains in Mayo Elbow scores and structural defect reduction compared to shockwave therapy paired with exercise, although functional grip endurance metrics demonstrated comparable values between the two multimodal cohorts.
In the lumbosacral region, clinical investigations targeted intractable radicular pain, deep pelvic nerve entrapments, and ischiofemoral impingement syndrome. Kang KN performed transforaminal epidural injections using fluoroscopic X-ray guidance and a twenty-two-gauge needle, delivering three milliliters of PDRN blended with five milliliters of zero-point-three percent lidocaine into left L4 and L5 neural foramina across three weekly sessions. The patient experienced a profound drop in Numeric Rating Scale pain scores from eight before treatment to five at one week, three at two weeks, and complete symptom resolution with a score of zero at six months. Kim WJ addressed ischiofemoral impingement by injecting three milliliters of PDRN and one milliliter of mepivacaine under ultrasound guidance into the quadratus femoris every two weeks for ten weeks, reducing Visual Analog Scale ratings from nine to one at seven months. Additionally, Lee WY resolved radiating sciatic pain originating from an intraforaminal cystic lesion by injecting PDRN with bupivacaine around the piriformis muscle over eight weeks, achieving complete relief.
Lower extremity investigations constituted the largest subcategory, addressing knee osteoarthritis, anterior cruciate ligament tears, pes anserine bursitis, posterior tibial tendinopathy, and recalcitrant plantar fasciitis. Mun JU treated pes anserine bursitis with an ultrasound-guided injection of three milliliters of PDRN and lidocaine using a twenty-six-gauge needle, reducing pain scores from seven to zero at two weeks and sustaining complete comfort through six months. In degenerative knee osteoarthritis, Yoon SH demonstrated that combining intra-articular PDRN with hyaluronic acid yielded significantly superior pain reduction on the Visual Analog Scale of negative thirty-three point one four compared to negative twenty-seven point eight six with hyaluronic acid monotherapy, alongside superior functional gains on the Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index. In ligament pathology, Kim YN documented structural recovery in near-complete anterior cruciate ligament and lateral collateral ligament disruptions, where biweekly ultrasound-guided injections achieved ninety degrees of range-of-motion improvement and verified magnetic resonance evidence of ligamentous regeneration, while clinical trials by Kim JK and Lee DO established prolonged relief and reduced fascial thickening in plantar fasciitis.
Within Korean medicine clinical practice, polydeoxyribonucleotide pharmacopuncture formulations—known under proprietary denominations such as Miso Yakchim, Yeon-A Yakchim, and Yeonjeong Yakchim—represent an integrative evolution blending classical meridian stimulation with targeted cellular regenerative biotechnology. Unlike traditional herbal pharmacopunctures that primarily suppress acute inflammation and modulate pain perception, PDRN directly promotes tissue repair at the cellular level by invigorating nucleotide synthesis and stimulating neovascularization. The review authors noted, however, that substantial methodological limitations persist across the available evidence base. The majority of included trials evaluated clinical progress over short monitoring durations between six and twelve months, while patient cohorts remained relatively small and direct comparative trials against established herbal pharmacopuncture agents were virtually non-existent.
