TITLE: Cochrane Review Finds Turmeric Supplements Offer Little Relief for Knee Osteoarthritis
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A new Cochrane systematic review has found that turmeric supplements may provide little or no clinically important pain relief for people with knee osteoarthritis, challenging conclusions from earlier research that had suggested therapeutic benefit. When compared to both placebos and established medications, turmeric was not shown to be more effective in managing symptoms. The international review team concluded that the current evidence does not demonstrate a clear clinical benefit and remains uncertain due to limitations in the underlying clinical trials.
Osteoarthritis is a complex joint disease and represents the most common type of arthritis, for which there is currently no cure despite available management options like exercise and medication. Turmeric, a common culinary spice, contains active compounds known as curcuminoids, including curcumin, which possess anti-inflammatory properties.
To rigorously assess treatment efficacy, researchers examined nine clinical studies comprising a total of 1,245 participants diagnosed with knee osteoarthritis. The included trials were conducted across Australia, Belgium, India, and Thailand. Participants were aged between 52 and 62 years on average, and approximately two-thirds of the study subjects were women. All analyzed trials were relatively short in duration, monitoring outcomes over periods ranging from four weeks to three months.
The reviewed studies investigated a broad spectrum of commercial turmeric extracts and curcuminoid-based formulations. These products differed substantially in their exact chemical composition, curcuminoid concentration, and daily dosage. Across the nine trials, researchers compared the performance of turmeric preparations against placebos, standard paracetamol, and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen and diclofenac.
Lead author Dr. Ivan Shirinsky, a practicing rheumatologist, placed the findings within the broader context of joint disease management. “As a clinician and researcher, I am not surprised by these findings,” Dr. Shirinsky stated. “Osteoarthritis is a complex and still incompletely understood disease. Despite intensive research, effective pharmacological treatment options remain limited, and many promising approaches have yielded limited or inconclusive evidence of clinical benefit. A better understanding of the mechanisms underlying osteoarthritis may help us develop more effective treatments in the future.”
A critical feature of the new review was its formal assessment of trial trustworthiness, which provided an essential safeguard when evaluating the medical literature. Notably, 10 trials raised methodological concerns during initial trustworthiness screening and were placed in an awaiting classification category pending clarification from their authors. Because these concerns remained unresolved, the studies were entirely excluded from the quantitative analyses. The authors utilized the Trustworthiness Screening Tool developed by the Cochrane Pregnancy and Childbirth group, highlighting that comparable tools like INSPECT-SR are increasingly vital to help researchers identify problematic studies that distort evidence syntheses.
Earlier systematic reviews had generally suggested that turmeric might improve pain and physical function, but those findings were often based on studies whose trustworthiness had not been formally evaluated. Previous reviews also included broader sets of trials, encompassing non-oral turmeric preparations, multi-ingredient curcumin formulations, or diagnoses based solely on radiography. Asked whether he would recommend turmeric supplements to patients with knee osteoarthritis, Dr. Shirinsky explained: “I would not routinely recommend turmeric supplements to my patients with knee osteoarthritis. However, if a patient asked me about them, I would explain what we know and what remains uncertain about their benefits and potential harms, and help them make an informed decision.”
