TITLE: Sam Altman’s OpenAI Foundation joins Rockefeller Foundation to launch $50 million AI initiative for citie
OUTLET: The Times of India

Artificial intelligence is beginning to find a place in the everyday work of local governments, from tracking disease outbreaks to warning neighbourhoods about floods. Yet many cities lack the money, technical support and expertise needed to put such systems into practice. A new $50 million partnership between the OpenAI Foundation and the Rockefeller Foundation aims to address some of these gaps. Announced on October 14, 2026, the initiative will fund organisations working with city governments to introduce AI-based solutions to public services. Individual grants could reach $2 million, with projects expected to address problems affecting residents across different parts of the world.
OpenAI and Rockefeller offer up to $2 million for city AI projects
Called Cities Deliver: AI Solutions for Public Services, the programme will provide grants to eligible nonprofit and civil society organisations that collaborate with municipal authorities. The funding is intended for projects that use advanced AI systems to improve how cities deliver services, respond to local problems and reach residents.
Grants will range from $500,000 to $2 million. The initiative aims to reach as many as 100 million city residents through the projects it supports. 
Applications opened on October 14 and are scheduled to close on November 29, 2026. Organisations that meet the initial requirements can submit proposals through a global open call, with those shortlisted moving to the next stage of selection.
The programme is not restricted to organisations working in a particular country. Its focus is on finding solutions that can address practical public-service challenges and potentially be adapted by other cities.
Four key areas targeted by the $50 million AI grants for cities
Reportedly, the funding call identifies four broad areas for projects: public health, economic mobility, environmental resilience and civic engagement. Each covers a different set of responsibilities that local governments handle.
In public health, proposals could involve disease monitoring, frontline healthcare, disability support, nutrition and improving access to food. Economic mobility projects may focus on helping unemployed or underemployed people find work, access benefits, develop skills or connect with employers. The intention is to use AI to make existing support systems easier for residents to navigate.
Environmental resilience is another area of interest. Projects could help local authorities prepare for extreme weather, improve emergency responses, monitor energy infrastructure or manage land in vulnerable communities. Under civic engagement, the programme will consider systems that make it easier for residents to communicate with government departments, access court services, use digital platforms and participate in local decision-making. Projects connected to voting and elections are excluded.
Earlier AI projects in Cali, Rio and Cape Town offer a blueprint for new grants
As per The Rockefeller Foundation, the new funding initiative follows several years of work involving the two foundations and city leaders. Their collaboration can be traced to a 2023 gathering that brought together more than 40 civic leaders from six continents, alongside representatives from academia, philanthropy and nonprofit organisations. Discussions centred on the practical difficulties of bringing AI into local government, including the need for trained people, suitable technology and financial backing.
The Rockefeller Foundation subsequently supported projects in Cali in Colombia, Cape Town in South Africa and Rio de Janeiro in Brazil. These initiatives have explored how AI could be used to address problems that local authorities were already dealing with.
In Cali, an outbreak forecasting system known as Dengue.AI has been developed to help authorities anticipate dengue activity. According to the city's mayor, Alejandro Eder, the technology can provide warnings up to three weeks ahead, giving public health teams more time to prepare preventive measures. The work has been reported as benefiting a population of more than 2.2 million people. The Rockefeller Foundation now plans to support an expansion of the system from Cali to Cartagena.
Rio de Janeiro has been working on an AI-assisted flood warning system, developed with Columbia University and supported by the Rockefeller Foundation. The project uses WhatsApp to communicate alerts and has focused on communities exposed to flooding, including Acari and Jardim Maravilha. The proposed expansion would extend the system to more residents and cover a wider range of extreme weather events.
Cape Town has taken a different approach, developing a platform for communication between residents and municipal authorities. The system is designed to let people interact with the city in their own language, using voice messages or text through a platform they already use. The Rockefeller Foundation intends to help extend its reach within Cape Town and potentially beyond the city.
What the two foundations want to achieve
Rockefeller Foundation president Dr Rajiv J. Shah said, “Over the past three years, we’ve seen what happens when city leaders, researchers, and local innovators build with AI around real problems. Cities Deliver takes what’s working in Cali, Cape Town, Rio, and elsewhere and opens it up to institutions everywhere, so more cities can benefit from the same kind of partnership.”
Anna Makanju, head of civil society and philanthropy at the OpenAI Foundation, said, “Cities are on the front lines of some of the biggest challenges facing communities, and the organizations working alongside them understand what it takes to deliver solutions that work.”
The initiative also comes against a backdrop of growing urban populations. United Nations estimates published in 2025 put the world's population at approximately 8.2 billion, with 45% living in cities. Local authorities are responsible for services that influence access to healthcare, employment, safety and public participation. However, the cost of implementation and concerns about protecting sensitive data can make the adoption of AI difficult for governments with limited resources.
