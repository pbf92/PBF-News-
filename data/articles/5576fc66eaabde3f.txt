Netflix Stock Hits Multi-Year Low: What's Happening?
Netflix Inc. NFLX shares are sliding Thursday to a two-year low as the stock continues its recent downtrend. Here’s what you should know.
- Netflix stock is among today’s weakest performers. Why are NFLX shares down?
Shares Slip Under Their 52-Week Floor
Thursday’s trading confirms a two-year low of $58.46, with Netflix at its lowest level since late summer 2024. The level drops below the bottom of the stock’s 52-week range of $59.41 to $115.25. Netflix had set the prior low of $59.41 on Nov. 17.
The decline continues a downtrend that has run for months. Netflix reached a high of $90.37 on May 21 and fell to $65.08 by July 17, and then climbed back to $83.60 on Sept. 3, but the shares have since surrendered that entire gain.
Netflix now trades below its 20-day, 50-day and 200-day moving averages. Its 50-day average also sits under the 200-day average, a setup that may keep pressure on the shares. A close above $59.41 would reverse the breakdown.
The 20-day average at $62.75 would be the next level to watch if the stock recovers.
CNBC: Netflix Expected to Bid for 2030 World Cup
Separately from the stock’s chart, CNBC reported Thursday, citing people familiar with the matter, that Netflix, Amazon, YouTube and Fox plan to submit bids for U.S. rights to the 2030 men’s World Cup before the deadline.
Disney, Skydance and Apple will not bid. Earlier CNBC reporting said media executives have discussed a winning price around $1.5 billion, possibly higher. Investors may be weighing what a win could cost Netflix.
FIFA wants one buyer for both the English and Spanish rights. It would consider a joint bid that pairs a broadcaster with a streamer, though it will not force a subscription service to share games. FIFA executives plan to meet bidders in the U.S. next week and expect to pick a winner before year-end.
NFLX Shares Are Dropping
