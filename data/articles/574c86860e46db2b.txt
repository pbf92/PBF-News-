TITLE: Character.ai asks court to keep its $10m buyer's backer secret
OUTLET: PPC Land

Character Technologies, the bankrupt company behind Character.ai, has asked a Delaware bankruptcy judge to let it keep secret the name of the investor behind Smirks and Pangs Acquisition, the newly formed company lined up to pay $10 million for the Character.ai app, its AI models and its users' chats. The motion, filed on November 19, 2026, as Docket No. 203 in the US Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, describes the investor only as "a New York venture capital fund that invests in technology and software companies," and proposes that its identity be shared with the court, the US Trustee, the creditors' committee and the attorneys general of 38 states and the District of Columbia, and no one else unless the court orders it. A hearing is proposed for Monday, November 23, at 2 p.m. Eastern. If the judge declines, the company asks in the alternative that the sentence in the court's own sale rules requiring the name to be published be deleted.
In Short
Character.ai wants the court to keep confidential which New York venture capital fund is financing Smirks and Pangs, the company set up to buy its app, AI models and users' chats for $10 million. The court's sale rules required that name to be published when the buyer was announced on November 14, and the US Trustee objected to the buyer's designation on the same afternoon the secrecy motion was filed. If the judge agrees on November 23, regulators, the creditors' committee and the court would know who stands behind the buyer of millions of users' conversations, while users, advertisers and rival bidders would not.
What the company is asking for
The motion runs to 10 pages, with a two-page notice and a three-page proposed order attached. It is brought by the company as debtor in possession under sections 105(a) and 107(b) of the Bankruptcy Code, Bankruptcy Rule 9018 and Local Rule 9018-1(d). The docket labels it a "Motion to File Under Seal."
What it seeks is narrower than sealing a document. According to the motion, the company wants authority "to maintain the confidentiality of the identity of the financial sponsor of the stalking horse purchaser" identified in its November 14 designation notice, Docket No. 164, "and other public filings in this case." The information would "only be provided on a confidential basis to the Court, the official committee of unsecured creditors, the NAAG Client States, and the Office of the United States Trustee."
The proposed order, Exhibit A, has six numbered paragraphs, and the first four carry the substance. "The Motion is GRANTED." The company "is authorized to maintain the confidentiality of the Confidential Information in the Additional Stalking Horse Supplement and other public filings in this case that would otherwise disclose the Confidential Information." It "shall disclose the Confidential Information to the Court and any other parties in interest as may be ordered by the Court, the Committee, the U.S. Trustee and the NAAG Client States." And the order would be "immediately effective and enforceable upon its entry."
The second request is the more unusual one. "In the alternative, the Debtor respectfully requests relief to amend paragraph 4 of the Bidding Procedures Order under Local Rule 9013-1(k)," the motion says. "Specifically, the Debtor seeks authority to remove the provision in the Bidding Procedures Order providing that the Additional Stalking Horse Supplement shall identify the Additional Stalking Horse Purchaser's parent company or sponsor." The company calls that change "non-material." The proposed order attached to the motion covers only the confidentiality request; no form of order for the amendment was filed with it.
Notice went to the US Trustee, proposed counsel for the committee, counsel to Smirks and Pangs and every party that has asked for notice under Bankruptcy Rule 2002. Objections "may be made at or prior to the Hearing," according to the notice, which sets the hearing before Judge Brendan L. Shannon in Courtroom No. 1 on the sixth floor of 824 North Market Street in Wilmington.
The sentence the company wants removed
The disclosure rule comes from the bidding procedures order Judge Shannon entered on November 4, Docket No. 106, after saying at an October 30 hearing that he would overrule the states' objections to the sale rules. That order let Character.ai name additional stalking horse bidders for any assets outside Disney's license, on or before November 14, by filing a supplement. PPC Land reported the requirement when the judge signalled he would overrule 40 states' objections to Character.ai's Disney sale rules.

The motion does not dispute that the second item applied. It says the buyer's "identity was disclosed in the Additional Stalking Horse Supplement, but, upon request of the Additional Stalking Horse Purchaser related to the commercial sensitivity of the financial sponsor, its sponsor's identity was not disclosed." The request for permission comes five days after the omission, and one day after the 9 p.m. November 18 deadline for objections to the designation set by the same order.
What the motion says about the fund
One sentence describes, for the first time in the filings PPC Land has reviewed, the money behind Smirks and Pangs: "The financial sponsor of Smirks and Pangs Acquisition, LLC is a New York venture capital fund that invests in technology and software companies."
The company has already shared the name privately. According to the motion, it "already has disclosed the Confidential Information to the U.S. Trustee, proposed counsel for the Committee, and counsel to the NAAG Client States, each on a confidential basis," and will disclose it to the court and others "as may be ordered by the Court."
The reasons given are commercial. "The Confidential Information is commercially sensitive and confidential in nature because public disclosure of the Confidential Information could reveal the sponsor's investment strategy, materially affecting the entity's ability to compete in the AI sector," the motion says. "These concerns are even more heightened given the highly-publicized nature of this chapter 11 case." The company "also believes that disclosure of the Confidential Information may deter the Additional Stalking Horse Purchaser's willingness to participate in this process thereby chilling the sale process and the Debtor's efforts."
It presents the request as restrained. "Recognizing that both transparency and public scrutiny are key elements of the bankruptcy process, the Debtor analyzed the Confidential Information to withhold only such information it believes is necessary to protect the parties from harm. Moreover, the relief sought herein is narrow."
Earlier filings had added a few details about the buyer without naming its owners. The signed purchase agreement, filed on November 16, gives a notice address on Broadway in Albany, New York, sends copies of the buyer's legal notices to Cooley LLP, carries the signature of Kai McPheeters as "Authorized Signatory," and refers to a confidentiality agreement signed by "an affiliate" of the buyer on November 4, nine days before the buyer was formed, as PPC Land reported when Character.ai's $10m sale was shown to hinge on moving chats without further consent. The motion does not say whether the New York fund is that affiliate, or how the signatory relates to it. PPC Land had asked the company's counsel on November 15 who owns, controls and funds the buyer.
A name taken from the chatbot's own habits
Footnote 5 of the motion explains where the buyer's name comes from, which no earlier filing reviewed by PPC Land had done:
"The entity name is a nod to the platform's user community. It refers to the words 'smirk' and 'pang,' which both appeared frequently in responses generated by the Debtor's AI language models, a familiar pattern with AI language models, and are widely recognized among the platform's users. The name was chosen to acknowledge that community."
The footnote is the only passage in the filings so far that speaks to how the buyer regards the platform's users, whose accounts, characters and chat content are among the assets it would acquire. It also suggests familiarity with the way the models write, a detail that sits beside one of the states' deposition topics: whether the models going to Smirks and Pangs "can reproduce or reveal users' personal information," as PPC Land reported when Character.ai faced 35 states' questions under oath on users' data.
The legal test the motion relies on
Bankruptcy filings are public records by default. Section 107(b) of the Bankruptcy Code makes an exception, and the motion quotes it: "On the request of a party in interest, the bankruptcy court shall, and on the bankruptcy court's own motion, the bankruptcy court may - (1) protect an entity with respect to a trade secret or confidential research, development, or commercial information." Bankruptcy Rule 9018, also quoted, lets the court "make any order which justice requires (1) to protect the estate or any entity in respect of a trade secret or other confidential research, development, or commercial information."
The motion's argument is that a sponsor's identity is "commercial information," and that once information falls in that category, protection is mandatory. It cites six decisions.
The three Third Circuit decisions, binding in Delaware, are cited for the existence and limits of the public's right of access. The mandatory language, "no discretion to deny," comes from the Second Circuit's Orion Pictures decision and two bankruptcy courts in New York. Under paragraph 23, counsel states that "to the best of the knowledge, information, and belief of the undersigned counsel to the Debtor, the Confidential Information is confidential, as contemplated by Local Rule 9018-1(d)(iii)."
The Trustee's objection, filed the same afternoon
The motion does not mention it, but the docket entry immediately before it is an objection to the buyer itself. Docket No. 202, filed on November 19 by the US Trustee, Andrew R. Vara, is titled "United States Trustee's Objection to the Notice of Designation of Additional Stalking Horse Purchaser and Stalking Horse Supplement for Assets Other Than the Disney Target Assets," and is linked on the docket to the November 14 notice. It argues that the bidding procedures order required the supplement to disclose the parent or sponsor of a newly formed purchaser, that Smirks and Pangs was formed on November 13 and that no parent or sponsor was named, and it asks the court to deny the designation and the bid protections. The Trustee's filing lists its own objection deadline as 9 p.m. on November 19 and a hearing on November 23 at 2 p.m., the same slot the company proposes for its motion.
The two filings pull in opposite directions on the same omission. According to the motion, the Trustee already has the sponsor's name on a confidential basis. The objection concerns its absence from the public supplement. Under the bidding procedures order, a designation that draws no timely objection is deemed approved without a hearing; one that draws an objection requires the company to ask the court for an emergency hearing, as PPC Land reported on November 4.
Who would know, and who would not
Under the proposed order, four recipients are named: the court, the creditors' committee, the US Trustee and the NAAG Client States. Others would need a court order.
