TITLE: Flight ID
OUTLET: PPC Land

Flight ID is a dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that holds the system identifier of a flight: a period with its own start date, end date and budget inside a programmatic campaign. It exists because a campaign's budget is rarely spent as one lump: a three-month campaign might carry three monthly flights, each with a different amount, and a report needs a fixed key to show what was planned and what was delivered in each window.
The field belongs to the five-strong group of flight attributes in Amazon's unified reporting dimensions reference, which the company updated on November 11, 2026. The others are Flight name, Flight start date, Flight end date and Flight budget. In PPC Land's field-by-field reading of the reference, all five sit in the "Level of detail" category, and Flight ID is described as a way to "track and manage campaign delivery". Unlike Campaign ID, which was "Order ID" in old DSP reports, none of the flight fields lists a former name. The flight kept its name when the order around it became a campaign.
Where a flight sits
Amazon's programmatic product, called Amazon DSP until it was renamed DVA on September 29, 2026, uses a four-level structure in its current vocabulary. An advertiser account holds campaigns. Campaigns hold ad groups, the unit DSP traders used to call line items. Ad groups hold ads.
Flights hang off the campaign, not the ad group. A campaign has an overall budget and an overall date range. Its flights divide that range into consecutive segments, each with a budget that the system paces against. Ad groups then compete for the money available in whichever flight is live. Teikametrics, an Amazon ads software company, described flights in its June 2025 help documentation as "specific time segments or intervals within an advertising campaign", each with "its own budget, start and end dates".
That makes the flight the level at which pacing is judged. Amazon's own launch notice for two order-level metrics, published on September 28, 2023, defines delivery rate as the pace at which an order spends relative to its budget and its progress "in the current flight", capped at 100%. Budget at risk is "the amount of budget that may remain unspent if the current spending pattern continues through the end of the flight". Both metrics appear from the second day of a flight, according to Amazon, and neither is available for orders bought on impression goals rather than spend.
How the ID is created
Amazon assigns the identifier when a flight is created, whether in the console or through the Amazon Ads API. In the version 1 DSP API, flights are managed as a sub-resource of a campaign. According to API documentation republished by the integration company One (last updated February 25, 2026), a POST request to /dsp/v1/campaigns/{campaignId}/flights takes an array of flight objects. Each requires a budgetAmount (a number with a minimum of 1, in the currency set at advertiser level), a startDateTime and an endDateTime, both in RFC 3339 timestamp format. A name is optional, as is an impressionBudgetAmount for campaigns bought on impressions.
The response returns a flightId for each new flight, in a format beginning "flg_", along with its position in the list. "Newly created flights are appended to the campaign's existing list of flights," the documentation states. A companion endpoint, /dsp/v1/campaigns/{campaignId}/flights/delete, removes flights by passing an array of flightIds. Both require the campaign_edit permission.
The ID encodes nothing about the campaign, its dates or its budget. It only guarantees that the same period can be found again after a rename, a budget change or a date extension. In a report, Flight ID is therefore the column used to join delivery data back to the planning record, while Flight name is the column people read.
A worked example
Consider a kitchenware brand running a DVA+ campaign from October 1 to December 31 with a total budget of $90,000. Its buyer splits the quarter into three flights: $20,000 for October, $25,000 for November and $45,000 for December, weighted towards the holiday peak. Each flight receives its own ID when it is saved.
A unified report with Campaign ID, Flight ID, Flight budget and spend as columns then shows three rows for the one campaign. If November spent $21,000 of its $25,000, the gap is attributed to that flight rather than spread across the quarter. Adding Ad group ID splits each flight row by ad group.
The same structure makes rolling, open-ended campaigns possible on a system that requires end dates. Intentwise, another Amazon ads software vendor, wrote in November 2025 that many advertisers set flights monthly and recreate them by hand every period. Its tool copies the previous flight forward in bulk and rejects new flights whose dates collide with existing ones, returning the message "Flight overlaps with an existing one." Whether Amazon itself enforces that rule is not stated in the public documentation reviewed for this article.
Origin and evolution
The flight is an older idea than the identifier. In media planning, a flight is the period during which a campaign runs, and flighting is the practice of concentrating spend into bursts separated by gaps, as covered in PPC Land's explainer on flights. Programmatic buying platforms carried the concept into software as a dated budget segment on the order or insertion order.
Amazon's programmatic platform launched as Amazon Advertising Platform and was renamed Amazon DSP in September 2018. Its orders carried flights from early on, though Amazon has not published a launch date for the feature. Data tools treated the flight as a first-class object. Funnel, a marketing data company, lists Flight ID, Budget Amount, Flight Start Date Time and Flight End Date Time alongside campaign fields in its Amazon DSP budget dataset, refreshed every four hours.
Several Amazon launches built on flights before the ID reached unified reports. Amazon introduced short-flight recommendations on November 20, 2024, offering real-time optimisation suggestions for DSP flights "running seven days or less", aimed at events such as Prime Day and Black Friday. Its campaign and creative management APIs reached general availability on January 9, 2025, after a beta announced in October 2024.
Unified reporting then moved the flight into a shared schema. Amazon launched the reporting system in beta on November 11, 2025, took it to general availability on June 8, 2026, and published the 156-dimension reference in November 2026. In that reference, orders became campaigns, line items became ad groups and flights stayed flights.
Why it matters
For buyers, Flight ID is the key that ties delivery to intent. Pacing complaints, underspend and budget reallocations are usually discussed per period, and the flight is the only object that carries a period's budget.
For data teams, the field matters because of the migration deadline. Amazon's November 12, 2026 timeline makes legacy reports read-only on December 17, 2026, and deletes unmigrated reports and their history on January 31, 2027. Any dashboard keyed to legacy DSP flight exports has to be remapped to the unified column before then.
Flights also shape how automation behaves. Amazon's DVA+ guidance states that flights of 14 days or longer give the optimiser time to learn, which puts short bursts at a disadvantage. Budget flighting is listed among the advanced settings rather than the default tier, so campaigns built on Amazon's defaults may carry a single flight covering their whole run.
Limitations and disputes
Flight ID applies only where flights exist. Amazon's sponsored ads products, Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display, are not built from flights. Their campaigns use daily or campaign-level budgets. Amazon's documentation says a dimension that does not fit an ad product "appears as null", so in a mixed report most sponsored ads rows will show no flight. Treating a null as zero budget is a common error.
The public record is thin. Amazon's own help pages describing flight behaviour, including limits on the number of flights per campaign, rules on overlapping dates and the treatment of unspent budget at the end of a flight, were not available to this review. Vendor documentation fills part of the gap, but describes vendors' own validation rules. The API details above come from a republished copy rather than Amazon's developer portal.
Flight ID versus adjacent terms
Flight name is the human-readable label attached to the same period. It can be edited and need not be unique, while Flight ID is fixed. Campaign ID identifies the container a flight belongs to. One campaign can hold many flights, but each flight belongs to one campaign. Flight budget is a value, the money assigned to the period, rather than an identifier. Budget segments in Google's Display & Video 360 (DV360) play a similar role on insertion orders, dividing an insertion order's budget into dated parts. The term "flight" also appears in DV360 pacing options, one of which, Flight ASAP, was removed in November 2024.
Recent developments
The flight fields gained wider visibility with the November 2026 reference update, which put them in the same table as sponsored ads data for the first time. Amazon's move of agency fees to account, campaign or ad group level after January 31, 2027 does not include flights.
Amazon's direction points towards fewer manual levers. DVA+, the AI-led campaign type that merges DSP, Sponsored Display and Sponsored TV buying, entered US closed beta in autumn 2026, with general availability planned in 35 markets in the first quarter of 2027, according to Amazon. As of November 2026, budget pacing in DVA+ can be daily, weekly, monthly or lifetime. How Flight ID will be populated for campaigns that rely on those pacing modes rather than explicit flights has not been documented.
