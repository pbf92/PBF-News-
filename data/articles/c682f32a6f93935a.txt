TITLE: Swansea in fight to keep Matos amid Strasbourg interest
OUTLET: BBC Sport

- Published
Swansea City look to be facing a fight to hold on to Vitor Matos amid serious interest in their head coach from French top-flight club Strasbourg.
Matos is under contract at Swansea until 2030, meaning the Championship side would be due significant compensation should he be lured away.
But that has not stopped Strasbourg targeting the 38-year-old Portuguese, who is due to celebrate a year in charge at Swansea later this month.
Matos' fellow countryman Hugo Oliveira is the current Strasbourg boss, but is under pressure after a run of five defeats in six matches.
Oliveira has managed only three wins in 10 league games since being named successor to Gary O'Neil - who left to become Ipswich Town boss – at Strasbourg in July.
O'Neil led Strasbourg to an eighth-place finish last season, but they are currently 13th in Ligue 1, only two points above the relegation zone.
Matos has turned Swansea from potential relegation contenders to promotion hopefuls since taking charge, with the club sitting 20th in the Championship last November.
The Swans finished 11th last season, and have made further strides in the first third of this campaign.
Matos' team have been among the early-season pacesetters in the second tier, though their form has dipped in recent weeks.
Swansea have suffered four defeats in their past five matches, though they remain only three points off top spot going into Saturday's game against West Bromwich Albion.
Matos was touted as a contender to take charge at Crystal Palace earlier this season - the Eagles have since appointed Thomas Frank - while he has also been linked with a return to Portugal on the back of his impact with the Swans.
When asked in October about the Palace links, Matos said: "I have nothing to say. I'm Swansea manager.
"I love being here, love my players, love the staff, so my focus is always on the next game and what we can do better to play against Queens Park Rangers."
In an interview last week, Swansea chief executive Tom Gorringe acknowledged that Matos' impact will not have gone unnoticed at other clubs, though he played down the idea of the former Liverpool coach having his head turned.
"The reality is there'll always be interest in anyone performing well in any role on and off the pitch," Gorringe said.
"That's one of the outcomes of being successful - we're aware of that.
"[But] we're all quite happy. We've all agreed to go on this journey together and we hope that Vitor is here a long time."
