TITLE: Explained: Why GTA 6 doesn’t reference 2002's Vice City and Rockstar’s strict parallel universe rule
OUTLET: GAMINGbible

Grand Theft Auto VI makes little to no reference to the events or characters of Grand Theft Auto: Vice City, and if you’re wondering why, we can tell you.
Despite sharing the same location, both games are entirely standalone, and fans will have no doubt noticed the massive glow-up Leonida has received for its open-world. Some landmarks remain the same, but some like Tommy Vercetti’s estate are nowhere to be seen.
This is because Rockstar Games doesn’t treat the Grand Theft Auto series as one continuous timeline but rather a collection of universes distinguished by console platform and graphical fidelity.
There are currently three known universes: 2D, 3D and HD.

The original top-down games from the OG PlayStation represent the 2D universe, including spin-offs like Grand Theft Auto: Liberty City Stories.
The 3D universe is represented by what Rockstar considers the GTA Trilogy. This being Grand Theft Auto III, Grand Theft Auto: Vice City and Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas.
Finally, the HD universe consists of Grand Theft Auto IV, Grand Theft Auto V and now Grand Theft Auto VI.
We know these universes are separate because of the references made in them, or sometimes lack thereof.
In GTA: San Andreas, for example, a GTA: Vice City character called Ken Rosenberg makes several appearances in the game’s story and mentions his good friend Tommy Vercetti by name.
GTA V, however, makes no mention of CJ’s explosive gang war, despite being set in the same location, but it does allude to Niko Bellic’s bank robberies in GTA IV.
Rockstar Games does this to keep the timeline from getting too messy, and it also helps explain why the maps are so wildly different between the games. GTA V, for instance, gets rid of Las Venturas and San Fierro entirely in favour of just Los Santos. Fans would, and have, inevitably wonder where they went.

GTA 6 has an enormous GTA: Vice City Easter egg you likely missed
Despite keeping the universes separate, Rockstar Games understands the value of nostalgia and regularly includes some cheeky homages to its previous games.
GTA VI has some fantastic Easter eggs and references, and while it’s GTA IV and GTA V that get the most fan-service, one mammoth-sized GTA: Vice City Easter egg is blowing us all away.
To do this, head to the Badger R&D radio tower in Key Lento, near Jason’s house in Chapter 1. You can’t miss it as it appears on the game’s map. Climb to the top, activate the equipment and get ready for a long, long drive...

Your goal now is to hit 12 LPL substations throughout the map, but you must hit them in order and without fast travel. No RydeMe allowed.
The location order is: Vice City's Downtown, Balsero, Key Lento, Washington Beach, Crosstown, Port Gellhorn, Hamlet, Gloriana Key, Hamlet again but on the western side, Port Gellhorn by the race track, Hamlet once more but South, and finally North Mariana County.
At each substation, you press a button with a pink and blue label attached to it, but there'll be more than one option. The correct order is as follows: D, D, Dot, A, Dot, A, A, A, Dot, W, D, W.
Once you’ve done all that, pay a visit to the T.H. Power Station between 7pm and 6am (in-game) and you’ll find a portal to the GTA: Vice City universe.
While it’s a fun, if complicated, Easter egg, it also reiterates that GTA VI and GTA: Vice City do not co-exist in the same timeline, but it does imply that there could be some crossovers later down the line…
For your trouble, you’ll get a Hawaiian shirt based on Tommy Vercetti’s iconic look and a cool motorcycle.
The Grand Theft Auto multiverse is more complex than you’d probably expect, and don’t even get us started on where Red Dead Redemption fits into all of this…
