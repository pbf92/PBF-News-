TITLE: Viatorr Stents Improve Shunt Patency Over Bare Stents in TIPS Review
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

In a comprehensive network meta-analysis evaluating stent selection for transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt procedures, researchers found that the Viatorr stent was associated with improved shunt patency compared with bare stents. The systematic review evaluated clinical outcomes across multiple stent configurations in patients receiving the shunt intervention for portal hypertension complications. The investigators assessed comparative effectiveness and safety across available randomized trials and observational cohorts, offering comparative evidence for a clinical domain where simultaneous evaluations of multiple stent strategies have historically remained limited.
Transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt, commonly known as TIPS, serves as an established therapeutic option for selected individuals presenting with severe complications of portal hypertension, including recurrent or refractory ascites as well as variceal bleeding. Clinical implementation requires appropriate prior patient evaluation because the intervention is only suitable for carefully evaluated candidates. Whether the specific stent type and stent diameter meaningfully influence long-term post-procedural outcomes has remained an unresolved controversy in gastroenterology, largely because earlier clinical investigations predominantly relied on direct head-to-head comparisons between two isolated stent types.
To address this evidence gap, the investigators searched PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science from database inception through November 2026. The search protocol identified both randomized controlled trials and observational cohort studies that evaluated and compared different stents utilized during the shunt procedure. The research team conducted a Bayesian network meta-analysis using the gemtc software package, synthesizing comparative trial data while calculating relative risk estimates alongside 95 percent credible intervals as their primary effect measures.
Methodological quality and methodological rigor were formally assessed using established assessment instruments across the included literature. Investigators applied version 2 of the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool, known as ROB 2, to evaluate the randomized controlled trials, while employing the Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies of Interventions tool, designated as ROBINS-I, for cohort investigations. Primary clinical outcomes targeted across the network synthesis encompassed shunt patency, shunt obstruction, hepatic encephalopathy, all-cause mortality, and postoperative gastrointestinal bleeding following the intervention.
The systematic synthesis ultimately incorporated 22 eligible studies comprising five randomized controlled trials and 17 cohort studies, representing an overall population of 2,868 patients. Within the Bayesian network calculations, the Viatorr stent demonstrated a statistically credible association with enhanced shunt patency when compared directly against bare stents, yielding a relative risk of 1.47 with a 95 percent credible interval spanning from 1.12 to 1.95. Under surface under the cumulative ranking curve analysis, the Viatorr stent achieved a SUCRA value of 76.48 percent for patency.
By contrast, the network meta-analysis detected no significant differences across the analyzed stents with respect to the incidence of shunt obstruction, overall patient mortality, or the emergence of hepatic encephalopathy. When assessing postoperative gastrointestinal bleeding, the analysis indicated that a stent combination strategy was associated with a reduced risk relative to alternatives, demonstrating a relative risk of 0.12 with a 95 percent credible interval from 0.01 to 0.68 and a corresponding SUCRA ranking of 14.22 percent.
The authors emphasized that surface under the cumulative ranking curve metrics remain strictly for reference and cautioned against drawing definitive clinical conclusions from these findings. Because observational cohort studies predominated across the evidence base, the resulting evidence network remained sparse, and direct head-to-head comparisons evaluating differing stent diameters were insufficient. The stent combination findings also relied on scarce data with wide credible intervals, leading the researchers to conclude that rigorous validation through large head-to-head randomized trials with extended follow-up remains necessary.
