TITLE: Gemini's $19.99 task mode goes free in 6 countries
OUTLET: PPC Land

Google today made the task mode in its Gemini app free for what it described as "hundreds of millions of people" in the US, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia and India, five weeks after introducing the feature in a post that named only paying Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. The free rollout runs on Gemini 4 Hydrogen. A second set of features, starting in the US, lets Gemini place phone calls to businesses, pay with tokenized cards through the Agent Payments Protocol, take instructions by text message and act on forwarded emails.
In Short
Google's Gemini app can now carry out errands such as booking a car service by phone, digging through old utility bills or buying a charger, and Google today opened that task mode to free users in six countries. The change puts an AI agent that phones businesses, signs into websites with stored passwords and pays with tokenized cards in front of an app with more than 1 billion monthly users, so local businesses, retailers and website operators may increasingly deal with Gemini rather than a person. The calling, texting, email and payment features start in the US, calling is unavailable in five states, and Google says the agent still makes mistakes and sometimes hands the final click back to the user.
What Google announced
The announcement came as a first-person post on Google's Keyword blog, published at 17:00 UTC and filed under Gemini App, AI and Google Labs. Its author is Josh Woodward, Vice President of Labs, the Gemini app and AI Studio. The headline reads "Put Gemini to work for you"; the browser title is "How to use Tasks in Gemini for personal assistance." Google lists it as a six-minute read. The subheading sets out the ambition plainly: "Gemini can now make the calls, pay the bills, and finish your tasks."
Two sentences carry the commercial news. "Starting now, this update is rolling out free of charge for hundreds of millions of people across the U.S., Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia, and India, using our new Gemini 4 Hydrogen model," the post states. Then: "We're also rolling out a set of advanced features, starting in the U.S."
The post never itemizes that second set. Its footnotes and closing instructions fill some of the gap. Calling is restricted by US state, texting requires a US phone number, and email forwarding is introduced with the words "Starting in the U.S." For payments, no geography is given at all.
The rest of the post is a sequence of household errands from Woodward's own life, used to show Gemini working as a personal AI agent that holds the user's context and acts on it. "The best assistant knows you and helps you get things done," it opens. "Our goal is for Gemini to save you time and money."
Google published a companion post at the same minute. Written by P.J. Linarducci, VP of Consumer Payments, it announced that Google Wallet is now available on iOS in the US, where it gathers receipts, package tracking, boarding passes and tickets pulled from Gmail and other Google apps. That post notes that tap-to-pay is not supported on iOS. Gemini's purchase receipts are designed to land in the same wallet, which is why the two announcements arrived together.
What is available, and where
From $19.99 to free in five weeks
The speed of the shift is the story. On October 13, PPC Land reported that Google had added a toggle from chat to tasks in the Gemini app, released alongside Gemini 4 Argon, in a post whose only availability sentence named Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. That post said nothing about free users or the AI Plus tier. Google's plan documentation, as reported by PPC Land on October 4, put AI Plus at $4.99 a month, AI Pro at $19.99 and AI Ultra at $99.99 or $199.99, and limited accounts without a subscription to Flash-Lite from October 9.
Today, 35 days later, the same toggle is described as free. The wording has shifted slightly, from "chat to tasks" in October to "Chat to Task" now, but the mechanism is the same switch on the app's home screen.
The model has changed, too. Today's post does not mention Argon. It names Gemini 4 Hydrogen, which Google introduced on November 12 as a faster and cheaper model for agentic work. PPC Land reported that day that Hydrogen had reached Google Search for all users, with developer pricing of $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, and that Google's model post said free Gemini app users would reach Hydrogen only through automatic model selection. Today's post names Hydrogen as the model for the free task rollout. It does not say whether paying subscribers' tasks still run on Argon, or whether free tasks face usage limits.
Google has been moving agent features down its price ladder for several weeks. When PPC Land covered a Google holiday guide on November 16, the help page for AI Mode's monitoring tasks named no paid tier, although Google had said at I/O in May that those agents would launch first for AI Pro and Ultra. On November 10, Google extended Android task automation, which works through food and ride apps, to the UK, India and Japan, with no subscription required according to its help page.
The country list deserves a second look. It includes no European country. The UK, which received Android task automation a week ago, is absent from today's list. So is every member of the European Economic Area. That pattern is not new: PPC Land reported on July 30 that Gemini Spark, Google's background agent, was unavailable in the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and Nigeria even as it expanded to more than 160 other countries. Today's post gives no reason for the selection and no timetable for other markets.
Five errands, as Google tells them
The post demonstrates the product through five tasks. Each relies on a different combination of Google services, and together they show how much of a household's administration Google now proposes to route through one agent.
The phone call
The first example is the most consequential for local businesses. "Because Gemini already knows we drive a 2019 Honda Odyssey and where we live through Personal Intelligence, it found the nearest shops on Google Maps and called both at once," Woodward writes. An image caption adds that Gemini "booked an appointment at the shop that accepts appointments vs. walk-ins only."
On disclosure, the post is specific. "On the dealership's end, Gemini disclosed right away that it's an AI. Businesses can opt out whenever they want, and Gemini won't give out my payment or private account info unless I say so."
The footnote attached to the call reads: "Calling through Gemini is not currently available in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana, and Nebraska." The same five states were excluded when Google introduced AI-powered business calling in US Search on July 16, 2025, a feature that checked prices and availability on a user's behalf. The repetition suggests the exclusions follow from the same underlying calling system, though Google does not say why those states are left out.
Gemini is now the third Google consumer surface that phones businesses. In November 2025, Google launched "Let Google Call" to check stock and prices at nearby stores, alongside agentic checkout. Yesterday, PPC Land reported that Ask Maps had started phoning salons, pet groomers and auto and home repair businesses to find open appointments, starting in the US.
The opt-out link in today's post leads to a Google Business Profile help page titled "About automated calls and texts from Google to your business." That page does not mention Gemini or AI by name. It says businesses can tell Google over the phone or change their Business Profile settings, under Advanced settings and a section called "Google automated calls and text messages," which covers bookings and inquiries from customers, profile updates and posts made on the business's behalf. It also warns that a business that opts out may still receive occasional manual calls from Google to confirm its information.
The bill
The second example introduces a capability that has received less attention: signing into a third-party website with the user's stored credentials. "I gave Gemini permission to access our utility portal with the new Google Password Manager integration and asked for it to analyze our past billing statements in my email," the post says. The analysis found that the family's heat pump was "kicking on around 4 p.m., right in the middle of peak pricing."
The post does not explain how a password moves from Password Manager into the agent's environment, whether the model ever sees it in plain text, or what happens if a portal asks for a second authentication factor. Stored credentials are not new territory for Google's agents: Chrome auto browse, available on desktop in the US, can use saved logins and passwords but hands payment steps back to the user.
The charger
For shopping, the post leans on Google's Shopping Graph, the product data set behind its shopping results. Gemini "quickly compared chargers thanks to Google's Shopping Graph, asked me follow-up questions about the wattage and color, and then helped me check out using the new Google Pay integration," according to Woodward. "The best part: it also found a discount code so I saved an extra $10."
That last detail matters for anyone who distributes promotional codes. A shopping agent that finds and applies codes on its own changes where codes are found and who gets credit for them. When Universal Cart reached US Search and the Gemini app on October 15, it already displayed promo codes and Google Wallet card perks. Today's post does not say where Gemini finds codes, or whether merchants can control which ones it applies.
Groceries by text
The fourth example uses a new channel. Woodward texted Gemini "with the new RCS integration," asking it to add lasagna, strawberries, cucumbers and vanilla ice cream to an Instacart order, and later sent a photo of an empty cereal box with the words "Add this too." Gemini "added it, and then handed me off to Instacart to complete the order."
The handoff is significant. The user, not Gemini, completed the order inside Instacart's own flow, so Instacart kept the checkout. Google says Gemini "connects with more than 60 popular apps," and invites developers to apply to its Connected Apps Platform. That figure has climbed quickly. It was "more than 40" on October 13, and "more than 50" across Search and Gemini when Google opened the Connected Apps Platform preview on October 28, where developers integrate through a cloud-hosted MCP server or Android's AppFunctions APIs.
The school emails
The last example turns the inbox into an input channel. Woodward "forwarded a batch of school emails from Gmail to gemini@google.com. In less than a minute, every date was on my calendar with my wife invited." Gemini also "noticed a conflict during basketball practice and asked if I wanted to text one of the other parents to trade carpool duty."
Payments: AP2, tokens and a spending cap
The payment section is short, but it contains the post's most detailed commitments. "Gemini now uses the Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) to make purchases like this," it says. "We're working with American Express (coming soon), Mastercard, and Visa to keep your purchases secure with digital tokens on supported cards."
On rewards: "when you shop with Gemini, you still earn your card's points, cashback, or purchase protections during checkout." On control: "You can also set a hard spending cap upfront, and Gemini is designed to stay within your budget." The phrase "designed to" is a description of intent, not a guarantee, and the post does not say what happens if a purchase exceeds the cap.
AP2 is not new. When Google launched the Universal Commerce Protocol on January 11, 2026, co-developed with Shopify, Etsy, Wayfair, Target and Walmart, it presented AP2 as the companion protocol for cryptographic authorization of agent transactions.
