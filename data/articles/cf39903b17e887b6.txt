TITLE: Insurance coverage lags behind advances in maternal-fetal surgery
OUTLET: News-Medical

While maternal-fetal surgery has rapidly advanced to treat serious congenital conditions before birth, a new national study led By Penn Nursing reveals that insurance coverage policies and policy transparency have not kept pace. Published in JAMA Network Open, the study is the first to systematically evaluate publicly ascertainable insurance coverage for these specialized, time-sensitive interventions.
Maternal-fetal surgery is a rapidly advancing field that allows clinicians to treat certain serious conditions before birth, sometimes substantially improving outcomes for children and families. However, little has been known about whether insurance coverage policies have kept pace with these advances.
Researchers analyzed coverage policies from March through August 2025 across all 50 state Medicaid programs and 30 large commercial insurers representing roughly 70% of U.S. enrollment. The team evaluated 19 procedure codes, including 15 Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) codes for individual components and four Healthcare Common Procedure Coding System (HCPCS) S-codes for comprehensive fetal interventions.
The research team found:
- Medicaid Disparities: Coverage was high for individual procedural components (e.g., 100% for intrauterine transfusion) but significantly lower for comprehensive fetal surgery S-codes (mean coverage for CPT codes vs. for S-codes).
- Intervention Gaps: Comprehensive procedures like in utero sacrococcygeal teratoma resection were covered in only 16.7% of applicable Medicaid programs, while Tennessee provided no publicly verifiable coverage information.
- Commercial Opacity: Among 30 major private insurers, 40% provided no usable plan-level data, while all five insurers with sufficient data reported covering all four S-codes.
In the first national study of its kind, our team examined coverage policies across all 50 state Medicaid programs and 30 major commercial insurers. We found substantial variation in Medicaid coverage for comprehensive maternal-fetal surgeries, with some procedures covered in fewer than one-third of states where coverage could be determined. We also found significant challenges obtaining coverage information from commercial insurers."
Abigail Wilpers, RN, PhD, Study Lead Author and Assistant Professor in the Department of Family and Community Health and a researcher at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia
Inconsistent code recognition and opaque commercial policies create financial uncertainty and administrative friction, forcing health systems to navigate complex preauthorizations under tight clinical timelines. These hurdles risk delaying prenatal care and may disproportionately impact lower-income and Medicaid-insured families.
These findings raise important questions about how insurance policy and healthcare financing may shape access to emerging, highly specialized treatments. They also highlight the potential consequences for hospitals and clinical teams navigating uncertain coverage for costly, time-sensitive care, particularly for patients who already face barriers to accessing specialized services.
Wilpers, A., et al. (2026) Publicly available information on insurance coverage for maternal-fetal surgery. JAMA Netw Open. DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.42991. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2856576?resultClick=3.
