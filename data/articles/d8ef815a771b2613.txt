TITLE: Grand Theft Auto VI Is Asking You To Hold Multiple Thoughts In Your Head At Once About A Complex Video Game Release
OUTLET: Aftermath

While I've already written about some of the wider political conversations (and that's a generous use of that word) when it comes to Grand Theft Auto VI, a smaller but maybe more topical one--since it hits closer to home for the kind of person reading this website--has been the debate about what GTA VI means to someone who both enjoys good video games but also supports the rights of the people who make them.
Consider, when surveying not just the game that it ended up being, but the work that went into making it, that multiple things can be true about Grand Theft Auto VI at once. Like:
- It is a staggering achievement of scale, fidelity, technical prowess and human creativity.
- The studio responsible for the game stands accused of union-busting, throwing people who spent years making the game into the bin during one of the worst climates for games employment in the industry's history.
- It's currently, if you take any stock in the powers of aggregation, the best-reviewed video game of all time.
- It's a symbol of excess, of bloated AAA game budgets and unsustainable business practices.
Because we are human beings and we can often crave some sense of moral clarity, I've seen a lot of hand-wringing over the last week as folks– particularly on social media, where complicated issues are stripped of all context be design--first acknowledge, then try to decide which of those above factors they care about the most. Should they boycott the game? Be a pithy asshole about it on social media? Just enjoy it? Which, like, I get the drive, but also we are all adults here, we are all capable of formulating then hosting complex feelings, and the fact is that everything I've said above is true, so much so that trying to hold onto just one of them is probably doing the situation a disservice.
Do I, personally, find the actions of Rockstar management abhorrent? Absolutely, and I fully support the affected workers (and the union itself) in their ongoing efforts. But do I also appreciate the work that has gone into this astounding video game, the craft and love and joy that has gone into every NPC interaction, every in-universe radio show and every industry-accurate piece of Florida infrastructure? Most if not all of that thanks to the talents of Rockstar's workers, not higher-ups? Also yes.
Can I reconcile that? I'll be honest with you and say no, I cannot. I can't fully enjoy the game without thinking of its enormous cost and the effects it has had on Rockstar employees (and the rest of the video game industry!), and I can't think about that without being floored by the vision and efforts of the 1000's of workers who actually made what is, at the end of the day, a very good video game.
I'm not alone in having trouble with this; even the affected Rockstar workers themselves have had to issue statements thanking people for their support on one hand while urging them not to boycott GTA VI on the other (you should, instead, support their actions directly). As Jack Hoxby, an IWGB Game Workers Branch member and former Rockstar employee, says:
“We poured years of our lives into helping build this game. We don't want you to boycott it. We want you to help us fight for justice. Since Rockstar fired us suddenly last year, we’ve seen an incredible outpouring of support from gamers and workers around the world.
Some people have asked whether they should boycott GTA 6 to protest on our behalf. But there’s a better way to support us and hold Rockstar to account. Buy one of our T-shirts. You’ll be helping to win justice for us and to defend the right of workers everywhere to come together and unionise.
That way, when GTA 6 comes out in November, you can enjoy it knowing that you had the backs of the people who made it.”
I am sorry that I am not ending this post with some kind of moral walkthrough for you, if that's what you came here to find. I hope I've at least given you the small comfort that if you're conflicted while playing this game, you are far from alone. What I can leave you with instead are the words of recently-retired legend Warren Spector, whose own 'congratulations' on GTA VI's release are as split down the middle as many of your own would be:
First, congratulations to everyone who worked on GTA 6. It looks like it will be a high point of sorts for our medium and our business.
However…
It’s billion-dollar development cost and an undoubtedly crazy marketing budget don’t represent a business model anyone else can or probably should emulate. That’s especially true when thousands of developers are out of work. A billion dollars pays for a lot of games and a lot of salaries.
Personally I’d rather have hundreds of games and full employment than one game no matter how amazing it is.
That said the sure success of GTA 6 proves a point I’ve made for years — that no game that matters ships on time and/or on budget. I’ll say again — a billion dollars! And at least a year late on its original ship date.
Give a game the time and money to prove its potential (key word, potential) and you have a chance of making something great. Underfund a game or force it out the door before it’s ready guaranteed failure no matter how much potential it has.
So, again, congratulations. Ambition and innovation are necessary for the medium to reach its potential. I just hope GTA 6 is the last of its kind even if it’s the best we have to offer.
