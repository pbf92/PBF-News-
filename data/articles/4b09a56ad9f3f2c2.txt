TITLE: Meta-analysis: Pleural injection combo boosts response rates
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and meta-analysis published in the World Journal of Surgical Oncology reports that adding a specific injection to standard chemotherapy improves response rates for malignant pleural effusion. The study, led by Yangsen He and colleagues, analyzed data from nine randomized controlled trials involving a total of 718 patients. The researchers evaluated the efficacy and safety of Sodium Cantharidinate and Vitamin B6 Injection (SC/B6) when combined with intrapleural perfusion of cytotoxic chemotherapeutic agents. The findings suggest that this combination therapy offers significant short-term benefits without increasing the risk of major adverse reactions.
The research team aimed to systematically evaluate the clinical outcomes of this combined approach.
To conduct the analysis, the researchers searched seven major databases, including PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, Web of Science, and three Chinese databases. The search covered literature from the inception of the databases through September 2026. Two reviewers independently screened the literature, extracted data, and assessed the risk of bias in the included studies. The meta-analysis was performed using RevMan 5.4 software and STATA 12, ensuring a rigorous statistical approach to the pooled data.
The results indicated that the SC/B6 combination group achieved significantly higher response rates than the control group, which received only intrapleural chemotherapy. The complete response rate was substantially improved, with a risk ratio of 1.83 and a 95% confidence interval ranging from 1.45 to 2.32. The partial response rate also showed a significant increase, with a risk ratio of 1.23. Overall, the overall response rate was higher in the combination group, with a risk ratio of 1.47, and the disease control rate was also significantly improved.
Quality of life was another key outcome measured in the study. Patients in the combination group reported significantly better quality of life scores compared to those in the control group. The risk ratio for improved quality of life was 1.44, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.20 to 1.72. These findings suggest that the addition of SC/B6 not only enhances tumor response but also contributes to better patient well-being during the treatment period.
Safety was a critical component of the analysis. The researchers examined several potential adverse reactions, including gastrointestinal issues, myelosuppression, chest pain, and fever. The data showed that the combination group did not significantly increase the risks of these side effects. For instance, the risk of gastrointestinal reactions was slightly lower in the combination group, with a risk ratio of 0.79, although this difference was not statistically significant. Similarly, the rates of myelosuppression, chest pain, and fever were comparable between the two groups, indicating that the addition of SC/B6 does not compromise patient safety.
The robustness of the findings was tested through sensitivity analysis, which confirmed that the results were stable. Additionally, Egger’s test was used to assess for publication bias, and the results indicated no significant bias, with a p-value of 0.520. This suggests that the observed benefits are likely genuine and not an artifact of selective reporting. The study provides a comprehensive overview of the current evidence supporting the use of SC/B6 in this clinical context.
Despite the positive findings, the authors noted important limitations. The study primarily focused on short-term objective response rates and quality of life. There was a lack of long-term survival data in the included trials, which limits the ability to determine the long-term benefits of the treatment. The authors concluded that while the combination therapy may serve as a local palliative treatment option, its long-term efficacy requires further validation through larger, longer-term studies. This highlights the need for continued research to fully understand the role of SC/B6 in the management of malignant pleural effusion.
