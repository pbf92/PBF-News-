TITLE: Converted product parent ID
OUTLET: PPC Land

Converted product parent ID is a dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that records the parent ASIN of the product a shopper actually bought after interacting with an ad. An ASIN, or Amazon Standard Identification Number, is the ten-character code Amazon assigns to every item in its catalogue. A parent ASIN sits one level above it: it groups the sizes, colours or flavours of the same product into a single family. The field exists because purchases on Amazon happen at the level of a specific variant, a size 9 in navy, while most brands plan, price and judge performance at the level of the product family. The parent ID lets a report roll thousands of variant-level conversions up into the families they belong to.
The word "converted" matters as much as "parent". Amazon's reports distinguish the product an ad promoted from the product a shopper bought, and the two are often different. Converted product fields describe the second. Their counterparts, the advertised product fields, describe the first.
Where the field sits
Amazon published its unified reporting dimensions reference on November 11, 2026. In PPC Land's field-by-field count of that reference, the "Product" category holds 18 of the 156 attributes: nine advertised product fields, eight converted product fields and a field called Product relevance. The converted set covers ID, name, parent ID, brand, category, subcategory, product group and marketplace.
The converted fields, according to that analysis, apply to all five ad products marked in the reference: DVA+, Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display and Sponsored TV. DVA+ is the AI-driven campaign type that buys across the programmatic platform known as Amazon DSP until September 29, 2026, as well as Sponsored Display and Sponsored TV. The converted fields "mostly inherit DVA+ names such as 'Item name,' 'Parent ASIN' and 'Brand name'", PPC Land wrote. Converted product ID maps to the former DVA+ name "ASIN" and the former sponsored ads name "Purchased ASIN".
The advertised fields reach fewer products. PPC Land lists them for DVA+, Sponsored Display and Sponsored Products, not Sponsored Brands or Sponsored TV, although the same paragraph says advertised product name carries the Sponsored Products marker alone. The converted side is therefore the only product hierarchy available across every ad type in a single report.
How parent ASINs work
Amazon's catalogue is built on parent-child relationships. Each variant that can be bought, a specific colour and size, is a child ASIN with its own detail page, price and stock. The parent ASIN holds them together under a variation theme such as size-colour. Amazon's Creators API documentation describes it plainly: "The abstraction of the variations is called the parent ASIN." It adds that "because the parent ASIN is an abstraction, it can't be purchased and hence is not associated with an offer." Amazon's DSP self-service reporting guide from August 2022 defines a parent ASIN as "a non-buyable entity used to relate child products".
That has one direct consequence for this field. A conversion can never happen on a parent ASIN. Every purchase lands on a child or on a standalone ASIN with no variations, and the parent ID is looked up afterwards from the catalogue.
The DSP guide also explains how its legacy reports handled the two levels. "Parent ASIN level data includes child ASIN data," it states, and "an ASIN with no variations will appear in both 'ASIN level' and 'Parent ASIN' level." Amazon has not said whether unified reporting follows the same rule for standalone items, or returns an empty parent ID.
From an ad to a converted product
The field is filled in at the end of Amazon's attribution chain. The sequence runs roughly as follows:
- A shopper sees or clicks an ad. The ad promotes one or more advertised products.
- The shopper buys something within the attribution window.
- Amazon decides whether the purchase counts for the ad and, if so, which product was bought. That product becomes the converted product.
- The converted product's catalogue data, including its parent ASIN, brand and category, populates the converted product fields on the report row.
Which purchases qualify differs by ad type. Amazon's attribution help page sets a 14-day lookback for Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display, covering promoted products and "any other products from the same brands", which Amazon calls the campaign's "brand halo". Sponsored Products bought by sellers use a 7-day, click-only, last-click window. Vendor Sponsored Products campaigns use 14 days. The DSP guide describes a last-touch model with a 14-day lookback, with brand halo defined as all ASINs carrying the same brand name in the byline as the tracked ASINs.
Amazon ranks competing interactions when a shopper has seen several ads. In order of priority: promoted ASIN clicks, "highly relevant" brand halo clicks, promoted ASIN views, highly relevant halo views, then other halo clicks and views. "Highly relevant" means the same brand and subcategory as a promoted ASIN. Amazon "will attribute a conversion to no more than 1 ad interaction across all campaigns and all advertisers," according to the same page.
Rules for views changed on January 1, 2026. Amazon moved Sponsored Brands, viewable-impression Sponsored Display and DSP ads in the Amazon Store to a "shopping-signal enhanced last-touch attribution model" with a shorter window, a shift PPC Land covered when Amazon tightened view attribution. Click-based attribution was not changed.
A worked example
Consider a footwear brand whose running shoe exists as one parent ASIN with 40 children: eight sizes in five colours. A Sponsored Products campaign advertises the black size 9. Over a month, a report grouped by Converted product ID shows 31 rows, one for each child that sold. The black size 9 is only one of them.
Grouped by Converted product parent ID instead, those 31 rows collapse into one. The brand can see that the campaign drove, for example, 212 orders of the running shoe family, of which 48 were the advertised variant. Adding Advertised product ID and Product relevance to the same report separates sales of the exact advertised item from sales of its siblings.
Product relevance carries the former DVA+ name "ASIN conversion type", according to PPC Land, and indicates whether the product was directly promoted. On Sponsored Brands or Sponsored Display, a navy size 10 would usually be a brand halo purchase. On a seller's Sponsored Products campaign, the legacy purchased product report would have listed it as a halo sale, the column formerly called "7 day other SKU sales". At parent level, all of these appear under the same family.
Why it matters
Variant-level data is noisy for brands with deep catalogues: apparel, footwear and beauty listings can carry dozens of children per product, and a campaign promoting one variant routinely sells others. Amazon's older Sponsored Products purchased product report listed "non-advertised ASIN purchased by the customer" without a parent column. Agencies built their own lookup tables against catalogue exports to make sense of it. A native parent ID removes that join.
Because the converted fields apply to all five products, a single report can also show which product families DVA+, Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands each drove.
Timing adds pressure. Amazon's schedule makes legacy reports read-only on December 17, 2026, and deletes unmigrated reports and their history on January 31, 2027. Teams that relied on custom parent mapping need to move it into unified reports before then.
Limitations and open questions
Catalogue drift is the main weakness. Sellers and Amazon can merge, split or reassign variation families, and Amazon has not stated whether the parent ID reflects the family at the time of purchase or at the time the report runs. If the second, historical totals by parent can shift without any change in sales.
Standalone items are undocumented. The 2022 DSP guide treated an ASIN without variations as its own parent. Whether unified reporting repeats the ASIN, leaves the field blank or does something else is not stated in the material available as of November 2026.
Mixed relevance is a reporting trap. A parent row adds promoted and halo children together. Without Product relevance or Advertised product ID next to it, a parent total can make an ad look as if it sold exactly what it promoted.
Marker conflicts remain. PPC Land's table credits 17 product attributes to DVA+ and Sponsored Display but 18 to Sponsored Products, while its text says Product relevance applies to all five products. Sponsored TV is marked on many attributes although Amazon says it is not yet supported.
Converted product parent ID is not...
Advertised product parent ID. The parent of the product the ad promoted, not the one bought. When the shopper buys the promoted variant or a sibling, the two values match. When a brand halo purchase lands on a different product family, they differ.
Converted product ID. The child or standalone ASIN actually purchased, formerly "Purchased ASIN" in sponsored ads reports. It is the variant. The parent ID is the family.
Brand halo. An attribution rule, not a field. Halo decides whether a purchase of another product from the same brand counts at all. The parent ID only describes the product once it has been counted.
Recent developments
On November 11, 2026, Amazon updated the dimensions reference that lists Converted product parent ID among 156 attributes, and PPC Land's analysis the next day placed the converted fields across all five ad products. The same week, Amazon set December 17, 2026 as the date legacy reports become read-only, with deletion on January 31, 2027. As of November 2026, Amazon has not published a definition of how parent IDs are assigned in unified reports.
