TITLE: Green Week Ahead? Nvidia Futures Rip 6%, Nasdaq and S&P 500 Futures Snap Back 2%
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

Nvidia (NVDA) futures jumped 5.71% to $132.70 on Monday as Nasdaq 100 and S&P 500 futures gained more than 2%. The rebound follows the Nasdaq 100's worst week since March 2020.
The S&P 500 lost ground for seven straight sessions and entered a correction on Friday. However, Nvidia stock remains down nearly 50% since early October, leaving its holders deep in losses.
Is the Nvidia Futures Bounce a Relief Rally or a Reset?
Nvidia shares fell 14% on Friday and more than 26% on the week.
Nvidia's perpetual contract on Binance has no expiry date. Still, it now trades only about 6% above its 52-week low of 124.99.
US media reports cited by AFP say securities regulators are examining Nvidia's accounting. Chief Executive Jensen Huang resigned earlier this month.
The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has not publicly named the issues under review. No wrongdoing has been established.
Can Dip Buyers Hold the Line This Week?
      "A bounce was always likely after seven straight days of losses, and the move in futures reflects how oversold tech had become."
Josh Gilbert, lead analyst for Asia Pacific at eToro Ltd., said in a Bloomberg report.
Nasdaq 100 futures rose 2.71% but still trail their 52-week high of 31,616.50 by about 18%. In contrast, S&P 500 futures rose 2.12% and sit roughly 10% below their 7,897.50 peak.
Last week, a Kobeissi Letter list showed 20 tech stocks trading at least 25% below record highs.
Meanwhile, the Goldman Sachs Panic Index hit 9 out of 10 after Friday's plunge. The bank's trading desk now sees a buying opportunity, but elevated positioning suggests full capitulation may be missing.
At the same time, authorities across Asia stepped in over the weekend. Taiwan widened short-selling curbs, and South Korea is preparing to activate a stock-market stabilization fund last used in 2008.
The Cboe Volatility Index (VIX), Wall Street's fear gauge, surged to its highest level since April 2025 on Friday. Support on that scale suggests officials see risks beyond one chipmaker, though a lasting recovery would weaken that read.
