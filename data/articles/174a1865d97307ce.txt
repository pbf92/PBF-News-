TITLE: Gout Cohort Analysis Links Condition to Elevated Cardiovascular Risk
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and meta-analysis of longitudinal cohort studies has linked gout with higher risks of multiple cardiovascular outcomes among adult populations. Researchers evaluated published longitudinal data to examine how a diagnosis of gout corresponds to broad cardiovascular disease as well as specific clinical presentations, while noting that observational associations do not establish a causal relationship.
To assemble the evidence base, investigators conducted searches across PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library from each database’s inception through July 1, 2026. The literature review was formally registered in PROSPERO under registration number CRD420261404857. Eligible prospective and retrospective cohort reports specifically compared adults diagnosed with gout against individuals without the condition.
The quantitative synthesis incorporated a total of thirty cohort reports, with twenty-nine studies contributing data directly to primary analyses and one report contributing solely to substitution analyses. Study quality across the included reports was formally evaluated using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, while the certainty of the aggregated evidence was evaluated under the GRADE framework.
Primary pooled analyses using DerSimonian–Laird random-effects models identified increased risks across several cardiovascular categories. For overall cardiovascular disease, the pooled adjusted hazard ratio was 1.32 with a 95 percent confidence interval from 1.22 to 1.44. In evaluations of coronary heart disease, coronary artery disease, or ischemic heart disease, the pooled hazard ratio was 1.36 with a confidence interval between 1.27 and 1.46.
Additional outcome assessments revealed elevated risk estimates for heart failure, myocardial infarction, and cardiovascular mortality. The pooled hazard ratio for heart failure reached 1.72 with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning 1.51 to 1.94, representing the highest relative estimate among the surveyed conditions. For myocardial infarction, the pooled hazard ratio was 1.39 with a confidence interval of 1.18 to 1.63, while cardiovascular mortality showed a hazard ratio of 1.23 with a confidence interval from 1.15 to 1.31.
For stroke, the primary random-effects calculation yielded an adjusted hazard ratio of 1.20 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 1.09 to 1.32. However, the analysis demonstrated sensitivity to the chosen statistical model. When researchers applied restricted maximum likelihood estimation paired with Knapp–Hartung adjustment, the resulting 95 percent confidence interval spanned from 0.991 to 1.482 with a hazard ratio of 1.212, crossing the threshold of 1 and showing model-dependent significance.
Across the evaluated outcomes, between-study heterogeneity remained substantial, with I-squared values ranging from 86.64 percent to 95.17 percent. When calculating prediction intervals to gauge expected effects in future comparable settings, only the coronary heart disease prediction interval excluded 1, indicating persistent dispersion in the observed effects across other cardiovascular outcomes.
Further methodological examinations introduced additional qualifications regarding the consistency of the underlying data. Egger’s tests suggested potential small-study effects for stroke and cardiovascular mortality, although tests based on fewer than ten reports remained exploratory. Ultimately, evidence certainty was rated as very low across all outcomes under GRADE assessment, leading the authors to conclude that the findings require cautious interpretation and do not demonstrate cardiovascular benefit from gout treatment.
The systematic review was conducted by researchers Tianyu Xu, Xinyue Zhang, Fengyuan Sun, and corresponding author Dinglei Su from Nanjing First Hospital in Nanjing, China. Published in the rheumatology section of Frontiers in Medicine, the investigation was specifically designed to evaluate the associations between gout and overall as well as specific cardiovascular outcomes using longitudinal cohort evidence.
To examine analytical uncertainty across the included longitudinal studies, the researchers utilized sensitivity analyses alongside restricted maximum likelihood estimation, Knapp and Hartung adjustments, and prediction intervals. By pooling selected adjusted hazard ratios and 95 percent confidence intervals, the investigators assessed whether associations remained robust or demonstrated model sensitivity across the different evaluated cardiovascular outcomes.
