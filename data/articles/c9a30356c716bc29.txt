TITLE: Non-Pharmacological Therapies May Improve Stroke Quality of Life in Network Meta-Analysis
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis evaluating non-pharmacological interventions for stroke patients found that distinct therapeutic modalities yielded the greatest improvements across separate measures of overall quality of life compared with standard care. In analyses examining SS-QOL, high-intensity and moderate-intensity aerobic exercise demonstrated the largest comparative improvement among evaluated options. Concurrently, when overall quality of life was evaluated through SIS, neurostimulation therapy emerged as the intervention showing the greatest improvement relative to standard care.
Beyond aggregate quality-of-life scores, the network meta-analysis identified specific non-pharmacological therapies associated with the most favorable outcomes across individual functional and psychological dimensions. For physical health outcomes, aquatic-land combined exercise demonstrated the most favorable results. Dedicated aquatic therapy produced the most favorable improvements in mental health, social participation, and emotional well-being. Furthermore, strength training showed the greatest relative efficacy for activities of daily living, acupuncture ranked highest for vitality and communication, and traditional Chinese medicine exercise therapy demonstrated the most favorable results for patient mobility.
The evidence synthesis was conducted through a comprehensive literature search across PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from each database’s inception through December 1, 2025, with no language restrictions applied. Studies were selected according to predefined PICOS criteria. The final synthesis incorporated 53 randomized controlled trials encompassing a collective population of 2,639 stroke patients. The investigation focused specifically on randomized controlled trials assessing the impact of non-pharmacological interventions on quality of life and its component dimensions in this clinical cohort.
To synthesize relative clinical efficacy across diverse interventions, the research team conducted a Bayesian network meta-analysis executed in R Studio. Evaluated interventions within each outcome measure were formally ranked using the area under the cumulative ranking curve, or SUCRA scores. Methodological quality across the 53 included randomized controlled trials was evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias Assessment Tool, while the certainty of the overall evidence body was appraised using the GRADEpro framework.
In the quantitative network comparisons against standard care, the investigators analyzed overall quality-of-life outcomes across two independent measurement networks. For the SS-QOL network, high-intensity and moderate-intensity aerobic exercise achieved a mean difference of 42.61, with a 95 percent credible interval spanning from 36.11 to 49.06. Within the separate SIS network, neurostimulation produced a mean difference of 9.58 relative to standard care, accompanied by a 95 percent credible interval ranging from 4.59 to 14.57.
Methodological evaluation across the included trials indicated that risk-of-bias judgments varied across the evaluated domains, with the majority of trial assessments classified as having low or unclear risk of bias.
Based on SUCRA scores and the results of the network meta-analysis, the authors concluded that multimodal rehabilitation strategies may provide potential benefits for improving quality of life.
The authors emphasized that further high-quality clinical studies are required to confirm the comparative effectiveness of multimodal rehabilitation strategies.
