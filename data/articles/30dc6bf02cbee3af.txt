TITLE: APG|SGA wins 193 more Zurich poster sites under five-year city licences
OUTLET: PPC Land

APG|SGA today said it had won both tenders run by Liegenschaften Stadt Zürich, the property company of the city of Zurich, keeping the advertising spaces in the ShopVille passages under Zurich Main Station and adding 193 poster sites along city streets. The Swiss out-of-home company said the new licences take effect on February 1, 2027, for a term of five years, and that all of the spaces can already be booked.
In Short
APG|SGA won both advertising tenders of the city of Zurich's property company, which keeps 23 ShopVille station faces with the firm and gives it 193 more street poster sites. It matters to advertisers and agencies planning outdoor campaigns in Switzerland's largest city, because one seller will now handle both sets of locations from February 2027. The licences run five years, the spaces are open for booking now, and the release discloses no fees, audience figures or bidder details.
What the city put out to tender
The announcement, dated November 23, 2026, and published on the APG|SGA website, describes two separate awards. One covers the existing spaces in ShopVille Zurich, the shopping passages beneath Zurich Main Station. According to APG|SGA, the company "has secured exclusive marketing of the existing analogue and digital spaces in ShopVille Zurich." The other adds street locations across the city. APG|SGA said it "expanded its range of poster locations in public spaces in the city of Zurich by 193 additional spaces along streets in prime locations."
The wording matters. ShopVille is a continuation: the 23 spaces there, according to the release, "remain exclusively in the APG|SGA marketing portfolio." The street package is new inventory for the company. Taken together, the two awards cover 216 advertising faces, a PPC Land calculation from the figures in the release.
Both licences, according to APG|SGA, "come into effect on 1 February 2027 for a term of five years." The release gives no end date. Counting five years from that start would take the licences to the end of January 2032, again a PPC Land calculation rather than a date stated by the company.
The ShopVille spaces
The ShopVille package is small in count but concentrated in one place. The release lists 11 ePanels, 2 eBoards and 10 BacklitPosters in F200 and F12 sizes. The ePanels and eBoards, as the names indicate, are the digital faces in the set, which matches the release's description of "analogue and digital spaces." That makes 13 screens and 10 backlit posters.
APG|SGA described Zurich Main Station as "the busiest transport hub in Switzerland" and said "the continuation of these established premium locations will strengthen the company's presence" there. The release does not give passenger counts, footfall data or audience estimates for the passages.
The 193 street sites by format
The release describes none of the street faces as digital. Swiss poster formats carry codes rather than descriptive names, and the release uses those codes without giving dimensions. According to APG|SGA's own product page, F12 is a landscape format sold with a smallest booking unit of one week, and the F12L and F12LR codes denote illuminated variants. The same logic applies to the F200L and F200LR faces in the package.
The two MegaPosters sit, according to the release, "at the exit from Zurich Main Station to ShopVille." That placement ties the street award back to the station, so a campaign can run on large formats at the entrance and on screens inside the passages under the same seller.
The release does not describe any of the 193 street sites as digital. On a simple count of faces across both awards, 13 of 216 are screens, about 6%, a PPC Land calculation that measures faces rather than revenue or impressions.
How the licences run
According to APG|SGA, the arrangement set out in the release works in four steps:
- Liegenschaften Stadt Zürich, described in the release as "the city of Zurich's property company," ran two tenders, one for ShopVille and one for street locations. APG|SGA won both.
- Under the ShopVille award, APG|SGA keeps "exclusive marketing" of the 23 existing analogue and digital faces in the station passages.
- Under the street award, APG|SGA adds 193 poster spaces in five format groups, including two MegaPosters at the station exit to ShopVille.
- The new licences "come into effect on 1 February 2027 for a term of five years," and "all spaces are now available to book with APG|SGA."
Who holds the 193 street spaces until the new licences start? The release does not say, nor whether that company contested the award.
What the company said
Patrik Denzler, Head of Partner Market East at APG|SGA, gave the only quote in the release. "The two awards strengthen APG|SGA's market position in Zurich for the long term. At the same time, advertisers benefit from an even broader range of attractive advertising opportunities for effective, combined Out of Home communication solutions from a single source," Denzler said.
The phrase "from a single source" is the commercial point. With ShopVille and the street package in one portfolio, buyers who want both the station and the surrounding city book with one seller rather than two.
A contested city
Zurich's outdoor inventory has changed hands repeatedly in the past decade, and the record from Swiss trade press shows how the property company's tenders have moved between sellers.
In January 2016, according to MarketScreener, APG|SGA won a tender of the city's property administration for the exclusive right to place commercial posters on city land, starting January 1, 2017. A larger public-ground tender followed. Horizont Schweiz reported on February 6, 2017, that the city had put out about 1,800 poster spaces in 11 lots for a five-year term from July 1, 2018, to June 30, 2023.
Digital screens on public land came with constraints. In June 2018, according to Netzwoche, APG declined to bid in the city's tender for digital advertising screens and interactive city maps, a package of 16 installations. The company argued that the short contract term would not recover investment and running costs, and objected to the city's ban on moving images and its rule that static images could change only every 24 hours. The city justified the 24-hour rule by the screens' direct orientation toward traffic, Netzwoche reported. Whether those rules still apply in 2026 is not addressed in the release.
The property company's last poster tender went largely to a competitor. Horizont Schweiz reported on December 14, 2021, that Clear Channel had won most of the Liegenschaften Stadt Zürich poster tender, with 216 new poster spaces and three MegaPosters, including two at ShopVille's main entrance on Bahnhofplatz, from February 1, 2022. The new APG|SGA licences start exactly five years after that date, and the release places its own two MegaPosters at the station exit to ShopVille. The release does not say whether these are the same sites.
APG|SGA has also been rebuilding its position around the station in other deals. Horizont Schweiz reported on May 13, 2024, that the company had won back the marketing of 64 premium illuminated F12 posters on Zurich's Bahnhofplatz from the city transport operator VBZ, starting in August 2024, a site it had held from 2004 to 2016.
Why this matters for the market
City and transit concessions remain the backbone of out-of-home businesses in Europe, and the terms are long. JCDecaux's subsidiary Wall was awarded Hamburg's main advertising contract for 15 years, covering about 2,200 bus shelters, in August 2026. In October 2025, JCDecaux returned to Barcelona with a 10-year street furniture contract that includes 300 new 81-inch LED screens. Against those deals, a five-year licence for 216 faces is modest in size, though Zurich is Switzerland's largest city.
The format mix is the more telling detail. Across the large European sellers, growth sits in screens. Ströer's digital out-of-home revenue rose 11.0% in the third quarter of 2026 while classic posters fell 4.4%. JCDecaux reported that digital reached 45.4% of revenue in the same quarter and that programmatic revenue climbed 37.5% organically. The Zurich street package runs the other way: none of its 193 faces is described as digital, and most are F12 and F4 paper formats.
Automated buying of outdoor screens is still a thin slice of the market. The World Out of Home Organization put programmatic DOOH at $1.339 billion in 2025, about 7% of digital out-of-home spend. With only 13 screens in the ShopVille set and no programmatic access mentioned in the release, the Zurich awards show how much prime city inventory is still sold by location, format and week rather than through automated auctions.
Consolidation also shapes who sells what. Bauer Media Group agreed to buy Clear Channel's Europe-North business, adding about 110,000 sites across 12 markets, in January 2025. For buyers, fewer sellers in a city means fewer calls, and also fewer alternatives when prices are set.
What the release leaves out
The release gives no licence fees, revenue guarantees or financial terms, no audience or footfall figures, no number of bidders, no dimensions for the formats and no list of street addresses. It does not name the current holder of the 193 street spaces, say whether the ShopVille screens will be replaced or added to, or mention programmatic access.
