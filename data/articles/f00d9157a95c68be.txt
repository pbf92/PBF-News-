TITLE: Wall Street Futures Fall as Nvidia Reverses Gains and Tariff Concerns Resurface: Dow Jones, S&P, Nasdaq
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance UK

US equity futures declined on Tuesday as investors reassessed the previous session's substantial gains, with renewed weakness in technology shares and reports of potential tariff increases weighing on market sentiment.
The decline followed the strongest daily performance for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite since April 2025, after Nvidia (NASDAQ:NVDA) announced the reinstatement of founder Jensen Huang as its chief executive officer.
Uncertainty surrounding the sustainability of the technology sector recovery was compounded by a Wall Street Journal report that the US was preparing to raise tariffs on China and dozens of other countries, according to people familiar with the plans.
As of 08:30 ET (13:30 GMT), Nasdaq 100 futures were down 2.2%, S&P 500 futures had declined 1.3% and Dow Jones futures were trading 0.7% lower.
Technology Recovery Faces Renewed Selling Pressure
Monday's gains brought an end to seven consecutive trading sessions of losses for the S&P 500, with technology and semiconductor companies recovering from recent declines.
However, analysts remain uncertain whether the rebound signals a broader market recovery or a temporary improvement in investor sentiment.
"If what the markets witnessed yesterday wasn't a clearing event, we do not know what is," analysts at Lynx Equity Strategies said in a research note.
Concerns over technology company valuations, the scale of artificial intelligence investment and the outlook for US interest rates remain relevant factors for investors assessing the market's direction.
Nvidia Falls Nearly 6% in Premarket Trading
Nvidia shares retreated nearly 6% ahead of Tuesday's opening bell, following a 31.5% increase to US$165.05 during Monday's session.
The previous session's advance added more than US$950 billion to Nvidia's market value and represented its largest single-day percentage gain since 2003.
The semiconductor manufacturer's board reinstated Huang as president and chief executive officer less than two weeks after his departure, following pressure from President Donald Trump.
"Imagine a scenario where you're perceived to be so important to a company that your stock increases nearly a trillion dollars on a day that you're reinstated as CEO. That sums up the last 24 hours," analysts at Deutsche Bank said in a note.
Huang's return helped ease concerns surrounding Nvidia's executive leadership and supported a wider recovery among companies exposed to artificial intelligence investment.
