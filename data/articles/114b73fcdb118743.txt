TITLE: INPA carries out controlled explosion of coastal cliff near Hadera
OUTLET: The Jerusalem Post

The Israel Nature and Parks Authority (INPA) carried out a controlled explosion at the Gedor Beach Nature Reserve near Hadera on Thursday morning to remove part of a coastal cliff that was at imminent risk of collapse.
The operation, captured in drone footage, marked the completion of the first phase of an engineering project aimed at eliminating an immediate safety hazard to visitors at the nature reserve near Hadera.
The reserve, located along the Sharon region's coastline, is susceptible to sudden cliff collapses, with chunks of rock and kurkar (calcareous sandstone) potentially breaking away and falling onto the beach without warning.
The INPA deliberately removed the upper section of the kurkar cliff at Tel Gedor after identifying a large rock formation that was at risk of sudden collapse. The operation involved the controlled detachment of the rock, including the use of a controlled explosion, the authority said.
The work was carried out under the close supervision of the Israel Antiquities Authority, in coordination with and with the support of the Hadera Municipality.
Authorities will assess whether additional work is needed to stabilize the remaining cliff. Plans are also underway to make a new hiking trail atop Tel Gedor accessible to visitors.
Authorities warn of cliff collapse risks along Israel's coastline
According to Guy Lavian, deputy director of the INPA's marine unit, the area was fenced off, warning signs were installed, and public access was prohibited during the operation.
"It is important to remember that a similar danger exists along many stretches of coastline between Herzliya and Hadera, and people should stay away from both the edge and the base of the cliffs," Lavian stressed.
The Gedor Beach Nature Reserve attracts numerous hikers, families, and other visitors. However, the kurkar cliffs overlooking the beach can be unstable, with masses of rock and sand weighing hundreds of kilograms potentially breaking away and collapsing without warning.
The danger is particularly significant along narrow stretches of beach at the base of cliffs, where visitors have little room to escape.
In March, the INPA installed warning signs along a roughly 300-meter stretch of the reserve between Chinese Bay and Bay 70 to alert visitors to the risk of collapse and prohibit access to the hazardous area.
The authority also released a public awareness video on social media. Despite the warnings, visitors have continued to spend time beneath the cliffs, disregarding safety signs, the INPA noted.
Coastal cliff collapses recorded across Israel in recent years
The collapse of kurkar cliffs is well documented along Israel's coastline, caused in part by waves striking the base of cliffs, ongoing erosion, slope instability, and surface runoff.
In recent years, cliff collapses have been recorded along the beaches of the Sharon region, Netanya, and Tel Aviv.
In February, part of a cliff collapsed at Sydney Ali Beach in Herzliya. While no injuries were reported, the incident underscored the danger visitors face when spending time beneath coastal cliffs.
The INPA stressed that visitors should stay away from both the edges and bases of cliffs. Members of the public are urged to follow posted warnings and safety instructions, avoid closed stretches of beach, and use designated trails only.
