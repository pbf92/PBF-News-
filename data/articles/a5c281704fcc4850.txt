TITLE: Disney+ turns the Avengers: Doomsday trailer into a ticket counter
OUTLET: PPC Land

The Walt Disney Company today added a movie ticketing feature to Disney+, letting standalone and bundle subscribers in the United States buy tickets to Marvel Studios' Avengers: Doomsday from inside the streaming app, with the purchase completed through Fandango. The feature arrives with the film's newest trailer and is rolling out across Disney+ on televisions, mobile devices and the web, 25 days before Avengers: Doomsday opens in cinemas on December 18, 2026.
In Short
Disney+ now lets US subscribers start buying Avengers: Doomsday tickets from the app itself, by scanning a QR code or sending a phone alert after the trailer, through ads that appear when playback is paused, or from a new Tickets tab, with Fandango handling the sale. It matters because Disney is using its own streaming screens, including ad placements, to push viewers out of the house and into cinemas for its biggest release of the year. What changes is that a subscription service built to keep people watching at home is now a sales route for a theatrical ticket, and Disney has not said whether that route will be offered to other films or brands.
How the ticketing feature works
The announcement, published on Disney's corporate newsroom under the heading "Movie Ticketing Feature Comes to Disney+ for 'Avengers: Doomsday'", frames the launch as a celebration of the new trailer. "In celebration of the newest trailer for Marvel Studios' Avengers: Doomsday, Disney+ is giving fans a new way to buy tickets to the most anticipated movie event of the year," according to Disney. The operative line follows: "Beginning today, standalone and bundle subscribers can purchase tickets to Avengers: Doomsday through the Disney+ app."
According to Disney, a subscriber can reach the ticket purchase from four places inside the service: the trailer, pause ads, a new Tickets tab and a collection banner. Disney describes each one this way:
- The trailer. "After watching the latest trailer for Avengers: Doomsday, viewers can choose to either receive a notification on their mobile device or scan an on-screen QR code to purchase tickets through Fandango."
- Pause Ads. "When viewers pause playback on Disney+, Pause Ads provide more information about Avengers: Doomsday and ways to purchase tickets from mobile or web devices."
- The Tickets tab. Subscribers "can now find a new Tickets tab within the Avengers: Doomsday landing page and on additional Marvel titles."
- The collection banner. A new banner in the Countdown to Avengers: Doomsday Collection is where subscribers "can watch the latest trailer and purchase tickets through Fandango."
In every case the transaction itself is not processed by Disney+. The trailer route and the collection banner name Fandango explicitly. The Pause Ads route speaks only of "ways to purchase tickets from mobile or web devices", and the Tickets tab, in the image Disney published with the post, carries a "Visit Fandango" link. The streaming app is, in practice, the shop window; the till sits elsewhere.
The rollout covers three device groups. "The movie ticketing feature is rolling out today across Disney+ living room, mobile, and web experiences in the U.S.," according to Disney. No other market is mentioned.
What the screens show
The single image Disney published with the post shows two devices and adds detail the text leaves out. On a television, a frame from the trailer shows a hooded, armoured figure, with a panel to the right carrying the film's logo and the lines "IN THEATERS DECEMBER 18" and "GET TICKETS NOW". A small yellow badge in the panel's top corner reads "Ad".
Below the logo, the panel offers "Send an info notification to:" with two choices: a named phone, shown in the image as "IPHONE 16 PRO MAX", and "ACCOUNT OWNER EMAIL". A QR code sits underneath, labelled "Fandango.com". The fine print at the bottom of the panel reads: "By electing to receive a notification about this advertiser, you are agreeing to receive a communication from Disney."
That sentence matters more than its type size suggests. It describes the ticket prompt as a message "about this advertiser", which places the feature inside Disney's advertising framework rather than its product menus, and it makes the follow-up a communication from Disney rather than from Fandango. And who is "this advertiser" - Marvel Studios, Fandango or both? The post does not say. The email option is tied to the account owner, which on a shared household subscription may not be the person holding the remote; the post does not address that case.
On the phone, the Avengers: Doomsday page in the Disney+ app shows three tabs - TICKETS, SUGGESTED and DETAILS - with Tickets selected. The tab carries the headline "Doomsday is coming!" and a line reading "Visit Fandango to buy your tickets and save your seat for Marvel Studios' 'Avengers: Doomsday,' in theaters on December 18th." A "Visit Fandango" link closes the panel.
A familiar format with a new advertiser
Nothing in the mechanics is new to Disney's ad sales team. In June, Disney described a set of interactive formats built on its DXC technology, and PPC Land reported on the four formats now running on Disney+: Gateway Go, an offer or reminder that viewers act on by push notification, email or QR code without interrupting playback, introduced in April 2025; Pause Ads, introduced in October 2025; Pause+, which adds billboard, trivia and carousel units; and Ad Selector, which lets viewers choose which ad to watch. Disney said Gateway Go engagement exceeded industry benchmarks by more than 60%.
The trailer prompt in today's image looks a great deal like Gateway Go: a mobile notification or an email, a QR code, and playback left alone. Disney's post does not name the format, so the link is PPC Land's reading of the screen rather than Disney's description. The pause-screen route, by contrast, is named outright as Pause Ads.
What is new is the advertiser. Disney sells its interactive units to brands as advertising products; here, the company is running the same kind of prompt for its own studio's film and pointing the response to a ticketing site. Disney's streaming supply is also open to outside buyers of interactive formats: Amazon Ads said on July 29 that advertisers could buy BrightLine interactive and enhanced formats against Disney Advertising supply through Amazon DSP, including units that layer QR codes onto existing video.
QR codes and phone notifications are the standard answer to an old problem in CTV advertising. A television has no checkout and a remote control is a poor keyboard, so any purchase has to move to a phone or a laptop. The trailer route is a direct application of the second screen: the television carries the message, and the handset carries the transaction.
Who gets it, and what is left unclear
Disney limits the feature to "standalone and bundle subscribers", a phrase that appears to cover both people who pay for Disney+ alone and those who receive it in a bundle; the post does not list which bundles qualify. Nor does it separate ad-supported from ad-free plans. That distinction matters for one of the four routes. Pause Ads are an advertising product, and Disney does not say whether ad-free subscribers see the ticket message when they pause, or whether the trailer prompt, which carries an "Ad" badge in the image, appears on ad-free plans at all. The Tickets tab and the collection banner are part of the app's interface rather than ad slots, but the post does not confirm that they appear on every plan either.
The scale of the ad-supported audience is substantial. Disney's first-quarter results for fiscal 2026 cited 122 million ad-supported subscribers across its streaming services, a global figure that is not limited to the United States.
A franchise in countdown mode
The ticketing feature sits inside a broader Marvel push on Disney+. Disney gave one number to support it: "Audience excitement around Marvel Studios' titles has been at an all-time high, with August being the highest month ever for Marvel viewing on Disney+ with over 420 million hours."
Alongside the ticket routes, Disney listed four other pieces of programming and promotion for subscribers in the run-up to the release:
Perks is Disney's subscriber loyalty scheme of discounts, sweepstakes and deals, which Disney made an always-on program for US subscribers in 2025. Endgame: Encore began as a theatrical event. Marvel Studios returned Avengers: Endgame to cinemas on September 25, with IMAX audiences shown an extra look at Avengers: Doomsday. On October 4, Disney said Endgame had overtaken Avatar as the highest-grossing film worldwide, at an estimated $2.9255 billion against $2.9237 billion, crediting the Encore release.
The corporate framing is the most revealing line in the post. "As Disney+ continues to evolve into the digital centerpiece of The Walt Disney Company, new features like movie ticketing are bringing fans closer to the stories they love, all in one place," according to Disney. The phrase "digital centerpiece" describes a streaming service whose job now extends beyond streaming: it sells subscriptions, carries advertising, hosts creator videos and, as of today, steers subscribers toward a cinema seat.
Fandango in the middle
Disney+ does not sell the tickets itself. The partner Disney chose is Fandango, which its corporate ownership page describes as one of the digital assets of Versant (Nasdaq: VSNT). Disney does not say whether Fandango paid for the placement, whether Disney earns a commission on tickets sold through the app, or whether ticket data flows back to Disney.
That last point is where the feature becomes interesting for measurement. A QR scan or a notification tap creates a record that links a streaming impression to an intent to buy. Can Disney see the completed purchase on Fandango's side? Could such a signal ever be offered to outside advertisers as proof of shoppable ads working on television? The post addresses neither question.
Why cinema tickets are worth the screen space
Disney's theatrical slate is large enough to justify the space. The studio said on September 3 that it had crossed $4 billion in global box office for 2026, with Toy Story 5 above $1.1 billion and Avengers: Doomsday still to come on December 18.
Cinema attendance itself has been recovering. More than 651 million tickets were sold across 2025 releases, up 9% from 598 million in 2024, according to The People Platform's Cinema Measurement Service. The money still concentrates at the top: the Video Advertising Bureau found that the top 20 films took $4.9 billion, or 57%, of the $8.7 billion 2025 domestic box office, while 650 films outside the top 20 shared the remaining 43%. A franchise release of this size is the kind of title for which a studio builds a dedicated purchase path.
What it means for advertisers on Disney+
Disney's advertising business is under price pressure. Its fiscal 2026 filings showed $11.5 billion of advertising across Entertainment and Sports, up about 4%, with lower rates holding back growth in Entertainment. On the same results call, chief executive Josh D'Amaro said the company was exploring a free Disney+ offering that would give it more advertising inventory to sell, and Disney said TikTok creator content would start appearing on Disney+ in December, timed to the run-up to Avengers: Doomsday. That creator content flows from a content-sharing agreement with TikTok under which opt-in creator videos run in Verts, the vertical video feed Disney+ launched for US mobile users in March.
Falling rates put a premium on formats that can show a measurable response. A ticket purchase is a cleaner outcome than recall or engagement, the metrics Disney cited in June for its interactive units. If the Avengers: Doomsday launch produces numbers Disney is willing to publish, they will show what its own inventory can do when the advertiser is Disney itself.
