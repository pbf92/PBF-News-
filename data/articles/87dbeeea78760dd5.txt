TITLE: Mauricio Pochettino had Tottenham stars ‘literally falling down’ in intense training sessions
OUTLET: Spurs Web

Former Tottenham goalkeeper Michel Vorm has lifted the lid on Mauricio Pochettino’s demanding methods, giving Manchester United players an idea of what awaits them under their new manager.
As Tottenham fans, we’re probably experiencing something of a mixed feeling about Mauricio Pochettino right now.
There is inevitably a burning sensation somewhere in our hearts knowing that the man regarded as one of the greatest managers in Spurs’ history, who gave us so many unforgettable nights and memories, will now be taking charge of a direct rival.
But good for Manchester United, I suppose. Pochettino has long wanted to manage at Old Trafford, and he finally gets to live that dream.
In fact, United were one of three clubs the Argentine specifically wanted included in his US exit clause. Tottenham were another, given his affection for the club and its supporters, while Real Madrid completed the list.
There are plenty of reasons why Pochettino is the right fit for United. He could have to work with tighter spending because of the club’s focus on their new stadium, a situation he knows all too well from his time in north London. More importantly, he will bring a much-needed improvement to the players’ fitness levels.
Towards the end of his predecessor Michael Carrick’s time in charge, one of the biggest complaints was how unfit the squad had become. United’s players looked tired and sluggish on the pitch.
Under Pochettino, however, none of that will be tolerated – at least according to former Tottenham goalkeeper Michel Vorm.
A 32-year-old Vorm was one of Pochettino’s first signings at Tottenham, swapping his role as first-choice goalkeeper at Swansea City for a senior backup position behind Hugo Lloris at White Hart Lane.
Even if he made only 48 appearances in all competitions across seven seasons, Vorm experienced first-hand what it was like to work under the Argentine. Now, he has given United supporters a glimpse of what their players can expect.
How Mauricio Pochettino compares with every Tottenham Premier League manager
Michel Vorm reveals the intensity of Mauricio Pochettino’s Tottenham training sessions
Writing in his exclusive column for The Times, Vorm explained that improving fitness levels will be one of Pochettino’s first priorities at Manchester United, and his methods could come as quite a shock.
He pointed to Harry Kane as an example of how Pochettino transformed players.
‘Harry Kane has spoken about being completely changed by Mauricio, who worked on the striker’s habits on and off the pitch, making him leaner and demanding more of himself.’
Vorm explained that Pochettino believes hard work, extra effort and hunger are the starting points for success. He is also a manager who understands when to offer encouragement and when to dish out tough love. He likes to control the environment, pay attention to the smallest details and is not someone who takes defeat particularly well.
However, it is his training sessions that could really get United’s players sweating. Vorm described them as intense, extreme and heavily focused on small-sided games at a relentless tempo.
‘It was continuously fast, playing one v one, two v two, three v three, four v four. There were a lot of repetitions. I could see the outfield players sometimes struggling, unable to run anymore, literally falling down.’
Vorm added that players would repeatedly be asked to press goalkeepers, with the coaching staff pushing them to go again and again.
‘The coaches would push players, shouting, “One more, harder!”’
And that was not necessarily the end of it.
‘That level of intensity was usually a couple of days before the game, never on the eve of it. Mauricio loved that the sessions prepared the players to be fitter than opponents after 75 minutes. He knew it made players very aggressive to swarm over the other team, especially as the White Hart Lane pitch was tight.’
Vorm also explained how involved the manager was in the day-to-day work. Pochettino would either walk around the pitch observing sessions or join in alongside his coaches. His assistant Jesus Perez was also very important in getting the best out of players, both physically and mentally.
Perez knew how to push players through their limits, sometimes with nothing more than a few encouraging words. As Vorm recalled, he might simply tell a player, ‘Go on, you are strong.’ That little encouragement could be enough to give someone the belief to complete another drill.
The former goalkeeper also appreciated Pochettino’s involvement in his own sessions with goalkeeping coach Toni Jimenez.
‘As a goalkeeper I liked that Mauricio was also involved in my sessions with the goalkeeping coach Toni Jiménez. Not all managers do that. Sometimes Mauricio joined in the shooting drills or he wanted to understand why we did certain exercises.’
His tactical meetings, meanwhile, were short, clear and to the point.
Vorm then touched on Pochettino’s approach to discipline. The Argentine was strict about lateness, but would never embarrass a player in front of the entire squad.
‘Mauricio was strict on lateness, but in a good way. I saw Trippier saying that Mauricio knew everything about us almost as if he had a camera.
‘I played for Louis van Gaal, and he could kill a player in a team meeting if they had done something wrong. Mauricio would never do that. He would call you into his office for a chat with him and Jesús, show you a video of where you could have done better.
‘He asked me many times as I was one of the more senior players and he knew I spoke with players and felt the temperature and energy of the squad. He might identify a similar player he can use at Old Trafford.’
Mauricio Pochettino could make Manchester United players walk on hot coals
The demanding sessions were only one part of Pochettino’s methods. Away from the training pitch, he also had some rather unusual ways of building team spirit and getting his players into the right frame of mind.
Vorm revealed that Pochettino occasionally took the squad out for dinner in London or organised training camps in Barcelona to change the environment and strengthen relationships between teammates.
The Argentine was also interested in psychology, energy and motivational techniques. He believed players could connect with a ‘universal energy’ powerful enough to make them feel invincible. Apparently, he even kept a bowl of lemons in his office because they were supposed to absorb negative energy.
And then, Vorm opened up about Pochettino’s motivational methods behind the scenes, including the unusual measures he took to prepare his Tottenham squad for their historic run to the 18/19 Champions League final.
‘In the build-up to the 2019 Champions League final against Liverpool, he had someone fly in to talk to us about facing our fears. That afternoon we walked on hot coals and broke arrows on our throats.
‘The arrow trial involved placing the tip against the throat and the other end against a wall and pushing until the arrow snapped.’
Pochettino also made changes to the players’ diets at the training ground. Vorm explained that sweet treats were removed, while the chef was instructed to prepare food in a leaner and more controlled way. Of course, there was only so much the manager could do about what players ate at home.
Vorm believes United supporters should be patient with their new manager.
‘Mauricio took time to move on players and bring through younger ones keen to learn at Tottenham. It could take a similar period to do that at Old Trafford and it will be hard to include very intense fitness sessions during the season.
‘But if United’s players buy into his methods, they can achieve big things like we did.’
