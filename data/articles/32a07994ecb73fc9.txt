TITLE: Black and Latina women with diabetes or high blood pressure experience gaps in pregnancy planning
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Type 2 diabetes and hypertension raise the risk of pregnancy complications, and both disproportionately affect Black and Hispanic women. In this qualitative study, researchers at the University of California, Berkeley interviewed 32 Black and Latina patients with type 2 diabetes and/or high blood pressure about their experiences with pre-pregnancy and contraceptive counseling. The findings point to several gaps in family planning care.
The study found:
- Participants said clinicians rarely asked about their pregnancy goals, and conversations usually happened only when the women raised the topic.
- Only one participant with diabetes recalled discussing how blood sugar could affect a pregnancy. Many learned about the risks firsthand: 75% of those who had given birth reported a pregnancy complication, such as preeclampsia or gestational diabetes.
- Many women said they felt pressured to use birth control. Women with high blood pressure described being denied their preferred method after an elevated reading, often with little explanation.
- Women who had trusting relationships with their clinicians were more open to discussing family planning and described more positive birth control counseling.
Why It Matters: The findings point to a need to develop patient-centered approaches, such as decision support tools, that address patients’ information needs and recognize the complexity of pre-pregnancy and contraceptive care and the health care bias and discrimination Black and Latina women report in their health care.
