NIO to Deploy 7,500 AI Agents, Targeting 10-Point Gross Margin Improvement
William Li, chief executive officer of Chinese electric vehicle (EV) maker NIO (09866.HK), said on November 25 that embedding artificial intelligence (AI) across its business units to boost operational efficiency could lift gross margin by 10 percentage points over the long term. He made the remarks at a "Technology Day" event in Guangzhou, ahead of the Guangzhou Auto Show, which opens on November 27.
The company said it has already deployed 7,500 AI agents across areas including research and development, manufacturing, logistics and vehicle sales. Li told reporters that integrating AI into an automaker's operations will be "crucial to maintaining competitiveness and leading the next wave of transformation," signaling that NIO will put AI at the core of both efficiency gains and profitability improvement.
The third-generation "World Model" unveiled alongside the announcement can reduce driver interventions in its advanced driver-assistance system (ADAS) by 40% and also lower the computing power required to train the technology, according to the company. On the same day, NIO officially launched the World Model "NWM 3.0" and its all-scenario emotional intelligence system "NOMI Intelligence 4.0" at its "NIO IN" technology event for the mainland China market, and disclosed rollout plans for each.
On the financial front, NIO posted a net loss of CNY 500 million (approximately $75.0 million) in the second quarter, narrowing the loss by 89.4% from a year earlier. Sales have been strong this year, with deliveries of new vehicles reaching 303,001 units in January-September, up 49.2% year on year, as all three brands set quarterly records.
Note: Delivery figures are cumulative for January-September 2026. The company plans to report third-quarter 2026 results on December 1.
In Hong Kong trading on November 26, NIO shares surged, briefly rising 6.07% to HK$21.32 (approximately $2.7). Turnover swelled to HK$24.372 million (approximately $3.1 million). The buying was driven by the view that an AI-centered overhaul of its cost structure and its new model launch plans will lead to medium- to long-term earnings improvement.
China's EV market is mired in a prolonged price war, forcing automakers to differentiate themselves through software and autonomous driving technology. If NIO achieves the 10-percentage-point gross margin improvement it is targeting, it would significantly strengthen its ability to withstand pricing pressure. However, rolling out AI agents company-wide requires upfront investment, and near-term costs could weigh on profits. Investors will likely look to the December 1 earnings report to confirm the progress of AI adoption and the path to earnings improvement.
