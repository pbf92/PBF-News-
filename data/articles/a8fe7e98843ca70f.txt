TITLE: 2 Minute Medicine Rewind November 16, 2026
OUTLET: 2 Minute Medicine

1. Angina pectoris was associated with a higher incidence of myocardial infarction (MI) or coronary death irrespective of coronary artery disease (CAD) severity.
Evidence Rating Level: 2 (Good)
In recent years, increasing efforts have been made towards evaluating the prognostic value of CAD severity in patients with angina. However, the asociation between angina and subsequent cardiac events stratified by CAD severity has not yet been evaluated in a random sample from the general population. This retrospective cohort study therefore sought to investigate the prognostic value of angina in a cohort from the general population without previous cardiac history, stratified by CAD severity. 24,176 patients (median [IQR] age, 57 [54-61] years; 50.6% female) from a cohort in Sweden between 2013 and 2018 who completed the Rose Angina Questionnaire and underwent subsequent coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) were included in this study. Over a median follow-up time of 7.7 years, the cumulative incidence of MI or coronary death was 2.9% in those with angina and 1.2% in those without (hazard ratio [HR], 2.50; 95% CI, 1.60-3.90; p < 0.001). There was no significant interaction between angina and the severity of CAD (p for interaction = 0.065). Overall, this study found that among a random sample from the general population, angina was associated with a higher incidence of MI or coronary death irrespective of the severity of underlying CAD.
1. In adult patients with sickle cell disease (SCD) and episodic cough and wheeze (ECW) without a formal diagnosis of asthma, long-term use of mometasone (Asmanex) was associated with increased soluble VCAM-1 levels.
Evidence Rating Level: 1 (Excellent)
The lung is a critical organ in patients with SCD, as it is the only place where sickled red blood cells can undergo reverse sickling due to oxygenation. Previous studies have shown that patients with asthma and SCD and patients with ECW and SCD have higher rates of visits to the emergency department due to pain crises. While the use of inhaled corticosteroids in SCD patients with asthma has been investigated, there are no long-term studies evaluating this subject. This randomized controlled trial therefore sought to investigate the safety and efficacy of long-term inhaled mometasone in SCD patients with ECW but without asthma. 77 patients from a single centre in the United States with SCD and ECW without a diagnosis of asthma, between 2018 and 2022, were included in the final analysis. 40 were randomized to a mometasone group (mean[SD] age, 36.0[11.6] years; 65% female) and 37 were randomized to a placebo group (mean[SD] age, 31.5[8.6] years; 49% female). Patients in the mometasone group had a 42% (95% CI: -11% to 96%, p = 0.02) increase in VCAM-1 levels, a marker of SCD disease severity, compared with the placebo group. Patients in the mometasone group had an increased daily pain score compared with those in the placebo group, although this did not reach statistical significance (pain score, 1.59 of 10.0; 95% CI, −1.02 to 4.22; p = 0.11). Overall, this study found that among adult patients with SCD and ECW without asthma, long-term use of mometasone was associated with worsened markers of disease severity.
1. Among adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), blood pressure control with a systolic blood pressure (SBP) target of <120 mmHg did not lead to a reduction in all-cause dementia or mild cognitive impairment compared with standard blood pressure control with an SBP target of <140 mmHg.
Evidence Rating Level: 1 (Excellent)
Cognitive impairment is an important complication of T2DM, with previous studies having shown that blood pressure control in T2DM may reduce dementia risk. However, there remains no consensus on the specific threshold at which blood pressure targets should be set to achieve this goal. This randomized controlled trial therefore sought to investigate the effect of intensive blood pressure control, defined as a target SBP of <120 mmHg, compared with standard blood pressure control with a target SBP of <140 mmHg, on cognitive outcomes and brain structure in adult patients with T2DM. 12,434 participants from seven geographical regions in China (mean[SD] age, 63.7[7.4] years; 44.8% female) were randomized to either the intensive arm or the standard arm and included in the study. After a median follow-up time of 3.8 years, all-cause dementia or mild cognitive impairment occurred at a rate of 3.69 events per 100 person years in the intensive arm and 3.89 events per 100 person years in the standard treatment arm. This difference was not statistically significant (hazard ratio, 0.96; 95% CI: 0.86-1.06; p = 0.38). However, participants in the intensive arm had a statistically significantly smaller increase in white matter lesions when measured with MRI compared with the standard arm (between groups difference, -0.09 inverse hyperbolic sine (cm3), 95% CI, -0.15 to -0.02; p = 0.01). Overall, this study found that among adults with T2DM, intensive blood pressure control did not lead to a reduction in all-cause dementia or mild cognitive impairment compared with standard blood pressure control. However, there was imaging evidence that an intensive blood pressure control strategy may still have structural benefits.
1. In patients with lung cancer on immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) who develop Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) grade 1 or 2 myocarditis, continuation of ICI therapy was associated with low recurrence rates and improved overall survival (OS).
Evidence Rating Level: 2 (Good)
ICIs have dramatically altered the landscape of cancer treatment in recent years across many solid organ malignancies. However, use of ICIs is associated with several adverse events among which ICI-induced myocarditis is one of the most severe. It is currently unclear whether re-challenge with ICI in patients who develop ICI-induced myocarditis is safe. This retrospective cohort study therefore sought to investigate the safety and efficacy of ICI continuation among patients with lung cancer who develop CTCAE grade 1 or 2 myocarditis. Data from 357 patients (median[IQR] age, 67[61-71] year; 84.6% male) from Shanghai, China with lung cancer treated with ICIs who developed CTCAE grade 1 or 2 myocarditis between 2016 and 2024 were included in the study. During a median follow-up of 18 months, 8.8% of patients who continued immunotherapy (20/226) developed recurrent myocarditis. All cases of myocarditis were mild and classified as either CTCAE grade 1 or 2. Under multivariate Cox regression analysis, continuation of ICI was associated with significantly improved OS (hazard ratio, 0.45; 95% CI, 0.30-0.67; p < 0.001). Overall, this study found that among patients with lung cancer who developed grade 1 or 2 ICI-induced myocarditis, continuation of immunotherapy was associated with low recurrence of myocarditis and improved OS.
1. Social isolation and loneliness in middle-aged and older adults were each associated with worse physical and mental health.
Evidence Rating Level: 2 (Good)
Social isolation and loneliness are increasingly prevalent among middle-aged and older adults, and have been associated with worse health outcomes including associations with chronic illness, cognitive decline and all-cause mortality. However, previous studies have not investigated the effects of social isolation and loneliness on health in a multinational fashion. This multi-cohort longitudinal study therefore sought to investigate the associations between social isolation and loneliness with physical and mental health in middle-aged and older adults. 94,122 participants over the age of 50 who completed assessments for social isolation and loneliness from 18 countries were included in this study. Social isolation was associated with a significantly higher probability of physical diseases (pooled average marginal effect [AME], 0.007; 95% CI, 0.003-0.011) and mental disorders (pooled AME, 0.026; 95% CI, 0.014-0.037). Loneliness was similarly associated with a higher probability of physical diseases (pooled average marginal effect [AME], 0.011; 95% CI, 0.006-0.016) and mental disorders (pooled AME, 0.243; 95% CI, 0.214-0.271). Overall, this study found that among middle-aged and older adults across 18 different countries, social isolation and loneliness were associated with increased probabilities of developing both physical diseases and mental disorders.
