TITLE: New Gallup Poll: Americans See LA as Unsafe. But What Do Crime Numbers Show?
OUTLET: Parriva

Only 42% of Americans consider Los Angeles safe, Gallup reports. Official LAPD figures show declines in several major crime categories, but the city’s national reputation tells only part of the story.
Los Angeles is recording fewer homicides, robberies and burglaries. Yet fewer than half of Americans consider it a safe place to live or visit.
A Gallup survey released November 16 found that 42% of U.S. adults rated Los Angeles as safe. That’s nearly unchanged from 41% in 2023.
Meanwhile, official LAPD statistics show declines across several major crime categories during the first seven and a half months of 2026.
So why does Los Angeles still struggle with its national safety reputation?
The answer begins with an important distinction: what Americans believe about Los Angeles is not the same as what crime records show or how Angelenos experience their neighborhoods.
What Gallup found about Los Angeles
Gallup has measured Americans’ perceptions of major cities since 1990, when just 26% considered Los Angeles safe.
The city’s current 42% rating is near its highest in the survey series, but below the 53% average for the 14 cities Gallup has tracked since 1990.
The difference between LA’s 41% rating in 2023 and 42% in 2026 is too small to establish a meaningful change.
Gallup’s October 1–19 survey measures national perceptions, not the opinions of Los Angeles residents specifically. It also does not measure actual crime or individual experiences of victimization.
Those facts are key when the results are compared with police records.
What LAPD’s 2026 crime figures show
An official LAPD crime snapshot for the week ending August 15 compared reported offenses during 2026 with the same period in 2025.
The department reported these changes:
- Homicides: Down 8.1%.
- Robberies: Down 4.3%.
- Aggravated assaults: Down 4.1%.
- Burglaries: Down 27.7%.
- Motor-vehicle thefts: Down 22.6%.
- Larceny theft offenses: Down 0.6%.
Overall, LAPD reported an 11.8% decline in property crimes in the snapshot.
These figures offer stronger evidence than a broad assertion that crime is either rising or falling. They identify specific categories and a comparable reporting period.
But they are not full-year results. They also cover LAPD’s jurisdiction within the City of Los Angeles, not the entire county.
LAPD transitioned to a newer crime-reporting system in 2024, complicating comparisons with older records. The 2025–2026 figures above come from the department’s own current reporting framework.
As Parriva previously found while fact-checking claims about Los Angeles crime, declining homicide totals should not be confused with changes in population-adjusted homicide rates.
Not every neighborhood follows the citywide trend
Citywide figures can conceal differences.
An LAPD Southeast Area report for the week ending October 31 recorded 21 homicides so far in 2026, compared with 43 during the equivalent 2025 period.
But robberies in the same police area increased from 500 to 557.
Both findings can be true: fewer killings and more robberies within the same area.
That is one reason citywide improvements should not be interpreted as proof that every neighborhood or resident is safer.
And as Parriva reported in October, reported hate crimes increased slightly during the first seven months of 2026, illustrating another dimension of community safety that broader crime trends may not capture.
What Angelenos think is a different question
The city’s national reputation also differs from residents’ assessments of daily life.
The 2026 UCLA Luskin Los Angeles County Quality of Life Index, previously examined by Parriva, found deteriorating assessments of public safety and other aspects of life.
That survey concerns Los Angeles County residents, while Gallup surveys adults nationwide about the City of Los Angeles. Their findings cannot be compared as though they asked the same question of the same population.
Still, they demonstrate why national reputation, reported crime and local satisfaction require separate examination.
Parriva previously explored this distinction for Latino families, whose concerns about safety can extend beyond homicide and robbery statistics.
Politics complicates the picture
Gallup also found a 42-percentage-point difference between Democrats’ and Republicans’ perceptions of LA’s safety.
That gap was 53 points in 2023.
The association is significant, but the survey does not establish whether political affiliation, media coverage, personal experience or other factors caused individual respondents to view the city differently.
Reality Check: Is Los Angeles safer than Americans think?
What the evidence supports: LA’s national safety reputation has improved since 1990. Official LAPD figures show declines in several major crime categories through mid-August 2026.
What the evidence challenges: A low national safety rating does not establish that crime is increasing. Nor does a citywide decline mean every neighborhood has improved.
What the evidence cannot tell us: Whether Gallup respondents’ judgments accurately reflect their personal risk in Los Angeles, or how residents in every neighborhood currently feel about safety.
How to check crime in your neighborhood
Residents can use LAPD’s Crime Mapping and COMPSTAT resources to review local patterns.
Compare the same periods and offense categories, and distinguish citywide totals from individual police areas.
Los Angeles’ reputation and its crime statistics tell different stories. Neither should be mistaken for the complete experience of the people who live there.
