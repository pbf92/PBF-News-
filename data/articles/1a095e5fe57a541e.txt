TITLE: Amazon Could Open Its Store to Meta and Defend Its $78 Billion Advertising Business
OUTLET: CTOL Digital Solutions

Amazon’s ad slowdown looks milder across the Prime Day calendar shift, and its new ad software and Walmart’s deal with Meta show ways to protect revenue when shoppers use outside agents.
On November 24, Walmart announced that customers could shop its Black Friday deals through Meta’s Muse. Once Muse hands over the customer’s shopping list, the announcement says, “Walmart then does the shopping, finding products that match your request.” Amazon, which Fortune reports is negotiating its own arrangement with Meta, has a $78 billion trailing advertising business, and part of its future earnings depends on who makes that selection.
A customer who asks Muse to find a television may never see an Amazon search page, and the television manufacturer has one less reason to bid for a sponsored placement. Amazon can keep the merchandise sale and lose the advertising contribution attached to it, and Amazon’s own advertising growth has slowed considerably.
The slowdown, however, looks different when measured over the period in which Amazon actually held its promotional events. According to its third-quarter results, advertising growth excluding currency effects fell from 26% in Q2 to 12% in Q3. Combine the quarters and advertising revenue rose from $33.397 billion to $39.446 billion, an increase of 18.1% in reported dollars. Weighting the constant-currency growth rates by the corresponding prior-year revenues gives approximately 18.6%.
On the October 29 earnings call, Andy Jassy put the sequential impact of moving Prime Day into Q2 in most countries at 850 basis points of year-on-year advertising growth. He also cited the timing of NBA programming on Prime and the anniversary of a change to Amazon’s search engine marketing. Jassy explicitly denied that external shopping agents were materially affecting advertising performance.
Management has an obvious interest in attributing the weakness to timing, and the combined-period calculation still comes in below the preceding 22% growth rate. Blaming Muse would take evidence of customer migration and lost advertising demand, and the results do not yet show either.
Amazon has been building a product that could keep some of the advertiser’s money even if the advertiser has fewer reasons to buy sponsored search. On September 29, Amazon Ads introduced Full-Funnel Campaigns through its Ads Agent. An advertiser supplies a unified budget covering sponsored ads, display, video and streaming television, with an optional return-on-ad-spend threshold. Amazon’s AI allocates that budget across the different advertising formats in real time.
If Muse diverts a meaningful share of product searches away from Amazon, a manufacturer under conventional campaign management might reduce its sponsored-search allocation and review where else to spend the money. Under Full-Funnel Campaigns, Amazon already holds the manufacturer’s budget and the delegated authority to find another use for it within Amazon’s advertising business.
Amazon’s launch announcement described an open beta available to any US advertiser, but its support documentation, updated November 20, says availability remains restricted to some advertisers. Streaming television inventory also has different conversion economics and potentially different margins from sponsored search, so a successful defence of advertising revenue could still leave Amazon with a less profitable advertising business.
An AI optimising Amazon-attributed sales can make different spending decisions from a finance director optimising incremental profit. Amazon reports encouraging returns from its Full-Funnel Campaigns beta, but advertisers deciding how much money to entrust to it will want independent incrementality tests and effective-bid limits.
Walmart already operates the consumer side of such a defence. Under its Agent Gateway and Agent Profiles, customers can set spending limits and require purchase approvals, while the retailer keeps payment credentials within its own systems. Muse handles the conversation and Walmart handles product matching, checkout at Walmart’s prices and returns.
If Amazon negotiated the right to select and order the products returned to Muse, together with some ability to monetise those selections, a merchant would retain a reason to advertise through Amazon even when the eventual purchaser never visits Amazon’s website.
We would therefore not trade an Amazon and Meta agreement as an inherently bearish catalyst. The valuation question is how much advertising contribution Amazon loses for each dollar of shopping expenditure that migrates to an external interface, after accounting for retained advertiser budgets, any payment to Meta and changes in advertising mix. There is insufficient evidence to price in successful substitution of displaced search advertising, but a deal that preserves Amazon’s authority over product selection and sponsored placements warrants a materially different earnings assumption from unrestricted access.
The operating hurdle for the next earnings print was established on October 29, when Jassy forecast a return to high-teens advertising growth, an expectation also recorded in Amazon’s Q3 Form 10-Q. Meeting it would be consistent with the calendar explanation and a business growing more slowly than its earlier 22% rate. A substantial miss would reopen the question of structural impairment, particularly if accompanied by weakness in sponsored search. Before then, any agreement with Meta is worth reading for its provisions on product ranking, paid placements and transaction fees.
