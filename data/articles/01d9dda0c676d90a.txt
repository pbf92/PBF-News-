Dow Jumps 591 Points as Black Friday Shopping Season Lifts Stocks; Nasdaq Surges 6.7% for the Week
On Nov. 27, the day after Thanksgiving, buying in blue-chip stocks dominated the New York stock market, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at 50,774.94, up 591.99 points (1.18%) from the previous trading day. It was the fourth consecutive session of gains. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite rebounded 543.70 points (2.31%) to 24,129.50, rising for the first time in three sessions, and the S&P 500 closed at 7,297.49, up 114.71 points (1.60%). Trading was shortened following the holiday, with the session ending at 1 p.m. New York Stock Exchange volume was 680.25 million shares, down 564.73 million shares from the previous trading day.
On a weekly basis, the gains became even more pronounced. The Dow rose 5.5% for the week, its largest weekly advance since October 2002, about 24 years ago. The S&P 500 gained 5.6%, its biggest weekly rise since April 2025, and the Nasdaq climbed 6.7%, its largest since April this year. Investors moving to buy back shares after the sharp selloff in AI-related stocks helped lift the market in the latter half of the week.
With "Black Friday" marking the full start of the year-end shopping season, consumer-related stocks also drew buying. Online retail giant Amazon.com rose nearly 4%, and construction machinery maker Caterpillar gained 3.3%. Market participants said they are watching retailers' sales trends as a gauge of the outlook for personal consumption.
A rebound in high-tech and semiconductor shares was also notable. Nvidia rose 0.80% to $150.49, gaining for the first time in three sessions. Micron Technology jumped 7.60% to $646.95, Broadcom rose 3.82% to $312.34, and AMD gained 4.56% to $424.54. Expectations for expanding data-center demand were once again in focus.
Among AI-related names, Anthropic, which held its initial public offering (IPO) on Nov. 19, surged 10.98% to $91.88. Buying concentrated in the stock, which had performed poorly since its listing. Tesla rose 5.22% to $400.00, and Palantir gained 5.24% to $172.17, with funds flowing into a broad range of stocks. Apple rose 0.83% to $358.54, and its market capitalization has swelled to $5.21 trillion (about 831 trillion yen).

The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX), known as the "fear index" that signals easing market tension, plunged 11.16% to 23.81, reflecting reduced short-term caution.
Still, the rally has not been enough to recover this month's losses. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq are on track for their worst monthly performance in November since 2008, weighed down by expectations of stricter AI-related regulation and prolonged monetary tightening. This month, all three major indexes entered correction territory, and the Nasdaq 100 at one point fell nearly 20% from its October high.
On monetary policy, with the preliminary reading for U.S. real GDP in the July-September quarter revised upward and October inflation accelerating, the view that the Federal Reserve will raise rates next month has become dominant in the market. Additional rate hikes could push up borrowing costs and act as a headwind for growth-stock valuations.
What shook the market sharply this week was the move by Nvidia Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang. He stepped down on Nov. 12, sending the market sharply lower, led by AI-related stocks. When he made a surprise return on Nov. 23, panic selling was confirmed to have subsided, leading to buying back in the latter half of the week.
Spillover to the Japanese market is also being watched. Following the previous day's U.S. tech rally, buying may lead in the Tokyo market at the start of the week, centering on semiconductor-related stocks. In Tokyo on Nov. 27, even as U.S. markets were closed for Thanksgiving, bargain-hunting led to broad buying centered on tech stocks, and the Nikkei Stock Average extended gains sharply, rising 1,211.95 points from the previous day to 58,711.11, while the Tokyo Stock Price Index (TOPIX) closed at 3,815.20, up 64.80 points, meaning buybacks have already begun in Japan as well. In the Tokyo foreign exchange market at 5 p.m. on Nov. 27, the dollar was in the lower 159-yen range, with the yen remaining weak and the dollar firm. Still, with expectations of a Fed rate hike lingering, it is unclear whether the chase for higher prices will continue, and currency market moves also need to be watched.
