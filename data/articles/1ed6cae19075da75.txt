OpenAI launched OpenAI for Chile on November 17, 2026, a partnership with Chile’s government covering public administration, civil-servant skills and support for local AI startups. The Ministry of Science, Technology, Knowledge and Innovation is leading the initiative for Chile under a memorandum of understanding.
The signing occurred alongside Semafor’s Silicon Valley & The World conference in California, with Minister Ximena Lincolao leading Chile’s side of the agreement. OpenAI for Chile sits within OpenAI for Countries, the company’s global initiative to help governments and local partners apply AI to national priorities, economic development and expanded opportunity.
The U.S. Department of Commerce’s American SI Exports Program is supporting the partnership, with OpenAI serving as an anchor partner. The program describes itself as a national accelerator for Super Intelligence (SI) business opportunities and offers designated consortia coordinated federal support across export licensing, government advocacy and financing.
Chile’s Existing AI Policy
The partnership builds on a national framework already in place. Chile’s AI policy page describes an action plan of 177 initiatives coordinated by 14 ministries. The national policy establishes ethical principles and promotes talent, research and responsible adoption, while the action plan is intended to ensure implementation respects fundamental rights.
Training for public servants, teachers and students is already underway, and the government is promoting AI adoption in health, transport, agriculture and security. The OpenAI agreement therefore connects its proposed training and public-sector activities to an existing national program rather than creating a standalone AI strategy.
Government, Workforce and Startup Plans
The government component is aimed at the ministry’s own operations. The partners will examine where new technology could fit into administrative processes, while OpenAI may assist with model adoption and provide ministry personnel with advice and training.
A second track addresses the wider civil service. Role-specific training would combine practical AI literacy with upskilling and reskilling, potentially through workshops and a train-the-trainer model. Proposed subjects range from effective tool use and output checks to protecting sensitive information and maintaining human oversight.
For startups, the partners are considering access to relevant OpenAI programs and technology, together with practical learning, technical guidance and mentorship. Links to public institutions, universities and other local organizations could help entrepreneurs build applications around Chile’s economic-development and public-service priorities.
Implementation Details Remain Open
Implementation would be coordinated by a joint task force drawn from the ministry and OpenAI. It is expected to set priorities and an initial workplan, assigning leads, milestones and measures of success to each activity.
The commercial and operational scope is not yet fixed. Activities, eligibility, resources and technology access require separate agreement. The MoU includes no commitment to invest or make purchases, and any activities must operate under appropriate safeguards for privacy, information security and responsible use.
As a result, the announcement creates a framework for cooperation rather than a finalized deployment or procurement package. The initial workplan is the next concrete step. Until it and the later agreements are in place, the partnership’s breadth across government, skills and startups is clearer than its committed scale.
