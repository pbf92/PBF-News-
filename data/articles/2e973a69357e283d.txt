TITLE: FGF21 Analogues Significantly Improve Liver Fibrosis in MASH Systematic Review
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Fibroblast growth factor 21 analogues significantly increase the likelihood of histological liver fibrosis improvement among patients diagnosed with metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis, according to an updated systematic review and meta-analysis. The evaluation gathered evidence from ten randomized controlled trials comprising 1,113 adult participants with biopsy-confirmed disease across fibrosis stages F1 through F4. Investigators observed that individuals receiving long-acting analogues of the liver-derived hormone achieved a more than twofold higher rate of at least one-stage fibrosis improvement without disease worsening compared to those receiving placebos.
Metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis, historically termed nonalcoholic steatohepatitis, represents an escalating driver of chronic liver morbidity across the globe. While clinical management expanded following the authorization of resmetirom for noncirrhotic cases demonstrating stage F2 to F3 fibrosis, additional antifibrotic pharmaceutical interventions remain essential. Fibroblast growth factor 21 functions naturally as an endogenous endocrine regulator governing lipid metabolism, insulin sensitivity, and systemic energy balance, prompting clinical development of targeted molecular analogues to mitigate progressive hepatic tissue scarring.
To synthesize therapeutic outcomes, investigators conducted systematic queries across PubMed, Scopus, and the Cochrane Library through February 2026 for randomized controlled trials comparing analogue candidates against placebos in biopsy-confirmed cohorts. The primary analytical endpoint prioritized at least one-stage histological fibrosis regression in the absence of steatohepatitis deterioration. Secondary metrics tracked changes across liver stiffness, hepatic fat accumulation, circulating liver enzymes, and key metabolic parameters, applying random-effects models to pool risk ratios and standardized mean differences alongside ninety-five percent confidence intervals.
The pooled results demonstrated that active analogue administration generated a pooled risk ratio of 2.25 for achieving histological fibrosis improvement compared with placebo, with a ninety-five percent confidence interval spanning 1.25 to 4.03. In addition to tissue remodeling, analogue therapy yielded substantially greater reductions in hepatic fat content, measured liver stiffness, and circulating fibrosis biomarkers relative to control regimens across the evaluated randomized clinical trials.
Safety evaluations revealed comparable incidences of serious adverse events between active treatment and placebo arms across the analyzed trials. The most frequently documented treatment-related side effects involved gastrointestinal symptoms, predominantly mild nausea and diarrhea, alongside local injection site reactions that rarely caused therapy discontinuation. Authors concluded that fibroblast growth factor 21 analogues present a promising and well-tolerated therapeutic strategy for reversing fibrosis, though definitive clinical adoption will require larger confirmatory trials tracking long-term clinical outcomes.
