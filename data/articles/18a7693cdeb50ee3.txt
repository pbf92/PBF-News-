TITLE: 'In faith, we cannot remain upright without moving': Pope Leo meets world-class cyclists at Vatican
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Pope Leo has called cycling a metaphor for "life in abundance" during an audience with champion professional cyclists at the Vatican.
The event was promoted by cycling's governing body the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI), with four-time British Tour de France winner and two-time Olympic medallist Chris Froome and one of the greatest road sprinters Manx Mark Cavendish listening on.
Two-time Tour de France Femmes winner Demi Vollering, eleven-time Grand Tour winning legend Eddy Merckx and French rising star Paul Seixas were also in attendance.
Leo looked to compare the Christian life and the life of a cyclist, according to Vatican News.
“Cycling can therefore be understood as a metaphor, for that life in abundance that is characteristic of every authentic sporting experience. In our journey of faith, rather like a bicycle, we cannot remain upright without moving.”
Several bicycles have been donated Caritas Rome, a charitable organisation founded by the Episcopal Conference of Italy.
Before the audience, UCI President David Lappartient "highlighted the longstanding historical roots between cycling and the Catholic Church".
"From races organised by parish communities and cycling teams inspired by gospel values, to the various meetings with Popes throughout the 20th century and the recent passage of the Giro d’Italia through the Vatican during the 2025 Jubilee," Lappartient said.
The Pope urged cycling to remain promoting the "experiences of authentic humanity" and invited believers to pray for cyclists who have died or been injured in accidents.
The pontiff took time to speak about the story of the Blessed Don Giovanni Fornasini, who travelled on bike from village to village bringing sacraments during World War Two.
He also illuminated why he thought the Blessed Virgin Mary was made the patroness of cyclists in 1949, linking beautiful cycling routes to the chapels and shirines dotted along the way and calling the bike an "instrument of evangelisation".
The Dicastery for Culture and Education and the Vatican's multi-sport association organised the event with the UCI.
Last year, Leo blessed the peloton ahead of the final stage of the Giro d'Italia grand tour, a stage which took place in the Vatican. The stage was dedicated to the late Pope Francis.
Pope Paul VI and Pope John Paul II also addressed Giro pelotons in 1964 and 2000.
