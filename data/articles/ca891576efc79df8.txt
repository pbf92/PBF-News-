TITLE: Walt Disney World Isn’t In Grand Theft Auto 6
OUTLET: Disney Tourist Blog

Today is the eve of the release of the biggest piece of media of the year–arguably ever–and many (most?) Disney fans are probably oblivious to it. No, not Avengers: Doomsday. Even that won’t surpass the release of Grand Theft Auto 6 tomorrow. Unfortunately for Walt Disney World and gamers, the parks and resorts won’t be among the many things parodied on GTA’s vast map of fictional Florida.
Video games are a blind spot for many Disney fans, and we’ve learned over the years that’s even true of DTB readers. This makes sense as Grand Theft Auto is basically the antithesis of Disney (if you’re not familiar with GTA, please do not misconstrue this post as a recommendation for you or your kids to play the game; it is not an endorsement). It’s nevertheless important to remember that one’s own interests and cultural relevance are not necessarily the same. To that end, I want to provide some context as to video games are such a big deal, and Grand Theft Auto 6 in particular is dominating the pop culture zeitgeist.
The gaming industry has grown consistently in the last decade, a trend that accelerated in 2020 when everyone was stuck at home. Just last year, the global games market had $201.6 billion in revenue, crossing the $200 billion threshold for the first time. To put that into perspective, the video games industry is bigger than the Hollywood, streaming, and music industries combined. Forecasts put this year’s video games revenue at $225 billion, fueled in large part by this year’s holiday tentpole release of Grand Theft Auto 6.
According to market intelligence firm Sensor Tower, Grand Theft Auto 6 has already generated more than $1.65 billion in preorder sales, an unprecedented number for a video game. At the same time, these results are expected for GTA 6, which is likely to be limited more by the lower install-base of Playstation 5 and current-gen Xboxes.
It probably doesn’t help that tariffs and rising costs have driven up the prices of consoles, raising the barrier to entry for potential players. On the other hand, it probably does help that the review embargo has lifted, revealing universal acclaim for Grand Theft Auto, which sits at a 99/100 on review aggregator Metacritic.
It’s been 13 years since publisher Take-Two Interactive and developer Rockstar Games released Grand Theft Auto 5 in 2013 for the PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360. Since then, the game has remained among the top 20 bestselling titles every single month for the last decade-plus, with re-releases on pretty much every platform under the sun.
Take-Two says it has sold roughly 235 million copies of GTA 5 as of its November 2026 earnings report, making over $10 billion for the publisher. It hit the $1 billion mark faster than any entertainment release in history, with $816 million of that in its first 24 hours. That’s like three Avengers: Endgames!
Industry estimates indicate Grand Theft Auto 6 could generate up to $5 billion in global sales during its launch week, setting a new record. It is widely expected to be the biggest entertainment launch in history, shattering the records set by GTA 5. That would make it, what, like five Endgames?!
Suffice to say, the world’s biggest current franchises arguably aren’t Avatar, Star Wars, Marvel, Cars, Toy Story, and Frozen. They’re Grand Theft Auto, Fortnite, Call of Duty, Pokemon, Minecraft and Mario. With each passing year, new video game franchises are born or strengthened by crossovers into legacy media. This is precisely why Disney so badly wants to make fetch Fortnite happen with a shared universe or whatever.
For all of his dealmaking prowess and success in fostering the growth of Disney’s business units, Bob Iger failed at establishing the company as a major player in video games. After losing nearly $1.5 billion, Iger shut down the company’s in-house gaming division, Disney Interactive, back in 2016. Iger also conceded during a 2019 earnings call that Disney is “not particularly good” at self-publishing games, and was satisfied with licensing its IP to third parties, like Electronic Arts.
Just wanted to provide a bit of context for those of you who haven’t even heard of GTA 6, don’t “get” why it’s such a big deal, or understand why Disney’s video game gambit is actually integral to the company’s future–and the inability of Iger to get it done was his second-biggest failure as CEO (after Chapek).
For those unfamiliar with the trademark GTA style, the game is based on exaggerated, fictionalized version of the real world. It uses fake brands, media companies, businesses and more to satirize America and our worst impulses. It lampoons crass consumerism, social media addiction, and other things you might consider societal ills. There’s been debate as to whether the game plays the “satire card” to get a pass on its own indulgences (kind of like Wolf of Wall Street, if you’ve seen that), but we’re getting well beyond the scope of this post.
Given Grand Theft Auto’s prominent place in the pop culture zeitgeist and its setting in the Sunshine State, it would be a win for Walt Disney World to be parodied in the game. Free marketing to a younger generation that lacks brand awareness for Disney since the company has done so poorly at building its own games!
After all, Disney just did recently take an ‘any publicity is good publicity’ perspective with “The Kosmic Kruiser” episode of The Simpsons, which parodied the failed Star Wars: Galactic Starcruiser. That was done by an arm of the Walt Disney Company itself, and to market something that doesn’t even exist! GTA 6 could at least feature the Tragic Kingdom or IPCot, or a version of something that still operates.
Meanwhile, the D23 Expo has been more self-aware and willing to poke fun at Disney’s own failures, most notably with this year’s epic opening number, “Disney Animatronic,” by Neil Patrick Harris and other celebrities. In light of all that, I would argue that Walt Disney World being skewered in GTA 6 would actually be a win, even if fans without senses of humor might see it differently.
Unfortunately, the Walt Disney World and Grand Theft Auto crossover that the world needs isn’t happening.
As part of a 14-page cover story on Grand Theft Auto 6, Game Informer magazine asked if the game’s fictionalized version of Florida, known as Leonida, would feature Walt Disney World, Universal Orlando, and other tourist corridor attractions. Rockstar’s senior vice president of environment art, Jack McPherson, said they wouldn’t be in GTA 6.
MacPherson indicated that Rockstar had to be selective about what they included for the sake of narrative and game design, but maintained that the goal was to replicate the Sunshine State and give gamers the representative Florida experience in numerous other ways. He likewise confirmed that GTA 6 also won’t feature SeaWorld or the Space Coast.
“We have to constantly evaluate the diversity of landscapes, towns, structures, landmarks, vistas, plant species, rock formations and everything else before we work with design and narrative to distill a list down that best works with and serves the game’s needs,” MacPherson explained. “So, while we may not have those exact locations, everything we have is geared to getting that full Florida experience!”
This isn’t a huge surprise, as GTA 6 will technically be set in Vice City, which is modeled after Miami. That’s quite the drive (or Brightline ride) from Walt Disney World. However, GTA 5 featured a sprawling map of Southern California beyond its Los Angeles stand-in setting, and there have been reports that GTA 6 will have the largest map for any Grand Theft Auto ever. (Then again, GTA 5 didn’t lampoon Disneyland; the only Disney connection was Fred’s Pictures, with its Mickey Mouse-esque wolf mascot and ostensibly family-friendly films with more mature themes.)
Between that and Rockstar’s penchant for parody, it seemed possible that Grand Theft Auto 6 would feature a satirical theme park. And perhaps it still will, cherry-picking elements of both Universal and Disney to skewer in its own distinct theme park. Or at minimum, maybe there will be billboards, radio ads, or other nods to Walt Disney World in the game.
Knowing what Rockstar has done in the past, right down to the detailed In-N-Out Burger parody in GTA 5, it’s hard to imagine that Florida’s biggest single-site employer and #1 tourist destination won’t be represented at all. That’s doubly true given the low-hanging fruit that Disney offers for parody purposes.
And I say that as a card-carrying Disney Adult! No reason we can’t enjoy others poking fun at Walt Disney World and its fans (us!). Few things are as valuable as a self-deprecating sense of humor, and many Disney Adults would be well-served to learn this. GTA could have a lot of fun with Disney and its fans, and at minimum, it would be amusing to see how they approached the parks.
Perhaps I’m just biased as an alleged adult with responsibilities who badly wants to give GTA 6 a spin, but doesn’t have the free time to do so. But if it were for very important research, I’d have no other choice! (I’m told this post does not meet that bar.)
Then again, I didn’t get around to playing GTA 5 until March 2020 and still managed to log [redacted] hours playing it. But somehow I doubt another COVID is going to happen to free up that much time, and even if it did, I’d still be a parent with other priorities. I hope everyone else enjoys the game, though!
Beyond Walt Disney World’s absence from Grand Theft Auto 6 and the ongoing story of the Fortnite universe, I really find Disney’s bungled approach to video games fascinating. It’s a topic we covered years ago at length in Disney’s Biggest Weakness: Why Mickey Mouse Should Be More Like Mario.
That article advocated for Disney to dive deeper into gaming and discussed how time spent with video games and mindshare is a really big deal. The average kid is consuming more video game content, and actively engaging with it. It’s occupying their minds and imaginations in a way that passive entertainment like movies and streaming simply are not.
It was surprising to me that this hasn’t been more important to Disney, a company that has historically recognized the significance of its theme parks as integral to brand affinity. Suffice to say, Disney needs success and a greater presence in the gaming realm. I’m not sure a parody of Walt Disney World in GTA 6 would’ve served that end, but it certainly wouldn’t have hurt!
