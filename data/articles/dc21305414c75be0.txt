TITLE: Apple TV takes exclusive US rights to all-female F1 Academy from 2027
OUTLET: PPC Land

Apple and F1 ACADEMY today said the all-female racing series will stream on Apple TV in the United States from 2027, with every qualifying session and race of its fifth season available to US subscribers. The announcement landed on the opening day of the Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend, where the series closes its 2026 season with the title already decided.
In Short
F1 ACADEMY, the Formula 1-backed series for women drivers, will be shown in the US only on Apple TV from 2027, and Apple's footnote says watching it requires a US Apple TV subscription. This matters for anyone who has been reaching the series' audience through free outlets, because in 2026 US fans could watch races live on F1 ACADEMY's YouTube and X channels and on a free ad-supported Formula 1 channel, while Apple TV subscribers reached it only through the separate F1 TV app. Apple's post does not say what happens to those free outlets, so the practical change for 2027 is a move of the whole season into Apple's own app, behind its subscription.
What Apple and F1 ACADEMY said
The post appeared on Apple Newsroom labeled "UPDATE" and dated November 19, 2026, under the headline "Apple TV becomes the new U.S. home of F1 ACADEMY, the all-female racing series." Its standfirst says Apple TV "will bring F1 ACADEMY races to fans across the U.S., expanding its Formula 1 coverage to spotlight the next generation of female racing talent and their journey through motorsport."
The scope is set out in two sentences. The series "will stream on Apple TV in the United States beginning in 2027," according to Apple, and "Fans will be able to watch every qualifying session and race throughout the season." The closing line of the post adds the word that carries the commercial weight: "Fans in the U.S. can catch all the F1 ACADEMY action exclusively on Apple TV beginning in 2027."
The sentence on qualifying and races carries footnote 1, and the footnote is short: "Requires a U.S. Apple TV subscription. An Apple TV subscription does not include third-party branded subscription services, or content available for rental or purchase in the Apple TV app." No price appears in today's post. Apple's own Newsroom update on the Las Vegas Grand Prix, published two days earlier, listed Apple TV at $14.99 a month. When the 2026 Formula 1 season opened in March, Apple's post gave the price as $12.99, so the monthly fee rose during Apple's first season of Formula 1 coverage.
Three executives are quoted. Susie Wolff, F1 ACADEMY's managing director, called it a "landmark partnership" and tied it to the series' purpose. "Visibility remains fundamental to our mission," Wolff said. "When young girls can switch on their screens and see women racing, competing, and succeeding, it changes what they believe is possible." She also described "a growing and increasingly engaged audience following our drivers and their journeys" in the US, without giving figures.
Jim DeLorenzo, Apple's global head of Sports, said: "These talented young drivers represent the future of motorsport, and we're excited to give them a bigger platform to share their stories, inspire new fans, and bring even more great racing to viewers on Apple TV."
Ian Holmes, Formula 1's chief media rights and broadcasting officer, linked the deal to the larger contract. "Through our broadcast partnership with Apple, we have a fantastic opportunity to bring even more fans closer to the action, showcase the incredible talent and personalities coming through the series, and inspire more women and girls to see a future for themselves in motorsport," he said.
Apple also promises "more live racing, in-depth programming, and exclusive original content, all complemented by a premium viewing experience with powerful customizable features." The post names no specific programme, feed or feature for F1 ACADEMY.
A new home, or a bigger room in the same house?
The headline frames Apple TV as a fresh destination. The record for 2026 is more complicated.
When the 2026 Formula 1 season began, Apple's Newsroom post of March 5, 2026 said that on Apple TV, iPad and iPhone, US subscribers could also reach Formula 2, Formula 3, F1 ACADEMY and Porsche Supercup events through the F1 TV app, using their Apple TV subscription. That access came from the core rights deal. When Apple secured exclusive US Formula 1 rights in October 2025, the five-year agreement folded F1 TV Premium, Formula 1's own direct-to-consumer streaming service, into US Apple TV subscriptions at no additional cost. Apple's Formula 1 page says US subscribers link their Apple Account to unlock it.
F1 ACADEMY's own "2026 Broadcasters" page lists two US carriers: Apple, and a FAST channel run by C15 Studio. FAST stands for free ad-supported streaming television, the advertising-funded linear streams distributed through smart TVs and streaming platforms. C15's channel is the Formula 1 Channel, launched in 2024. The Desk reported in March 2026 that C15 Studio had renewed its multi-year deal with Formula 1 and that the updated agreement added live rights to the 2026 F1 ACADEMY season, the first time the channel carried the series live. F1 ACADEMY's page also says the Formula 1 Channel shows delayed full session replays and short highlights of all F1 ACADEMY sessions in the US, and that fans "across all regions" can stream qualifying and races live on the series' YouTube and X channels and through F1 TV.

The word "exclusively" in Apple's post sits uneasily with three of those rows. If it means what it says, the free routes US fans used in 2026 would not carry live F1 ACADEMY racing in 2027. Apple does not say so directly, and neither the Formula 1 Channel nor F1 ACADEMY's broadcaster page had published 2027 terms at the time of writing.
How access works, according to Apple
Apple's posts and its Formula 1 support page describe the route to F1 ACADEMY coverage in the US. Set out in order:
- Territory. The coverage is for the United States. Today's post does not mention US territories; F1 ACADEMY's 2026 broadcaster listing for the US includes Guam, the US Virgin Islands and American Samoa.
- Subscription. Footnote 1 of today's post requires "a U.S. Apple TV subscription." It excludes "third-party branded subscription services" and rental or purchase content in the Apple TV app.
- Content from 2027. "Every qualifying session and race throughout the season," according to Apple. Practice sessions are not mentioned.
- Where it plays from 2027. On Apple TV. The post does not say whether coverage sits in the Apple TV app's Formula 1 hub, in the F1 TV app, or both.
- Access in 2026, for comparison. Apple TV subscribers reach F1 ACADEMY through the F1 TV app on Apple TV, iPad and iPhone, after linking their Apple Account to F1 TV Premium, according to Apple's March post and its Formula 1 page.
The current Apple TV subscription is an SVOD product, and nothing in the post describes a free tier for F1 ACADEMY. For Formula 1 itself, Apple's Formula 1 page says every practice session and a select number of race weekends are available without a subscription, including this weekend's Las Vegas Grand Prix.
The series Apple is taking on
F1 ACADEMY was set up in 2022 as a Formula 4-level development series, according to Apple, and it raced its first season in 2023. The 2027 season will be its fifth.
The 2026 title is settled. Alisha Palmowski of Campos Racing, who races with Red Bull's backing, clinched the championship at the Circuit of The Americas on October 24, 2026, RacingNews365 reported, finishing second in the reverse-grid race behind Ferrari-backed Alba Larsen after starting eighth. F1 ACADEMY's site says Palmowski will receive a fully funded Formula Regional seat for 2027, and that Emma Felbermayr will contest a third F1 ACADEMY season with Audi next year. All three drivers are named in Apple's post, which gives Larsen's full name as Alba Hurup Larsen. The season ends this weekend in Las Vegas, which F1 ACADEMY's site bills as the finale.
Formula 1 published the 2027 calendar on November 10, 2026. It keeps seven rounds and two of them are in the US, which is the market Apple's deal covers.
According to Formula 1, the Hungaroring will be the 17th circuit to host an F1 ACADEMY round. The series runs as a support event on Formula 1 race weekends, so its US rounds fall on the same weekends Apple already covers for Formula 1 in Austin and Las Vegas.
The audience behind the claim
Apple's claim that F1 ACADEMY is "the second-most-followed series after Formula 1" comes from the 2025 Global F1 Fan Survey. Formula 1 and Motorsport Network published its findings on July 1, 2025. The study is run every four years and drew more than 100,000 responses from self-identified, highly engaged fans in 186 countries, according to Formula 1. It is not a representative sample of the general public.
Within that group, 23% said they follow F1 ACADEMY, according to Formula 1, rising to 42% among women. The share engaging with the series was 37% among Gen Z respondents and 36% among newer fans. Formula 1 also reported that women make up three in four new fans, and that the US supplied the largest share of respondents of any country. Formula 1 did not publish a figure for how many of those US respondents follow F1 ACADEMY.
The series has already had a long-form showcase on a rival platform. "F1: The Academy," a seven-episode documentary on the 2024 season produced by Reese Witherspoon's Hello Sunshine, launched globally on Netflix on May 28, 2025, according to Formula 1. Wolff and Holmes were executive producers.
Apple's Formula 1 build-out
Today's post presents F1 ACADEMY as one more layer on Apple's Formula 1 rights. It refers to "the global success of Apple Original Films' blockbuster F1," which Apple's boilerplate calls "the highest-grossing sports feature of all time," and to the five-year broadcast partnership that made Apple TV the exclusive US broadcast partner from 2026. Apple also says Apple Original productions have collected 927 wins and 3,928 award nominations since the service launched on November 1, 2019.
The pattern is consistent with how Apple has handled its other sports property. A month after the Formula 1 deal, Apple moved every Major League Soccer match into the base Apple TV subscription from 2026, ending the separate MLS Season Pass. Sports content is used to give a single subscription more reasons to exist, and that subscription has become more expensive. Apple raised the monthly price 30% from $9.99 to $12.99 in August 2025, and the $14.99 figure in its November 17 post is a further increase. By PPC Land's calculation, the monthly fee is about 50% higher than it was before August 2025.
Why the deal matters to marketers
For advertisers, the most concrete change sits in what may disappear. In 2026, F1 ACADEMY's live races reached US viewers through two free outlets: the series' own YouTube and X channels, and a FAST channel that runs on advertising. If Apple's exclusivity removes live racing from those outlets in 2027, the series' US audience moves from ad-supported and social distribution into a subscription service that does not, on the evidence of today's post, sell advertising around the coverage. Apple says nothing about ads either way.
Apple's own advertising business is growing. Its CFO said advertising set an all-time revenue record in the September 2026 quarter, with Services revenue up 10% to $31.6bn. Apple does not publish an advertising figure, and nothing in its disclosures ties the record to Formula 1 coverage.
Formula 1, for its part, has been widening its own ad offer in the US. It started selling audio ads through Consumable's network in October 2026, a network Consumable says reaches more than 220 million US monthly users.
