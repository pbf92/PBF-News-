TITLE: Target's ad revenue climbs 23% to $296m in third quarter
OUTLET: PPC Land

Target's advertising revenue rose to $296 million in the three months to October 31, 2026, from $241 million a year earlier, the Minneapolis retailer reported on November 18, an increase of 22.8% by PPC Land's calculation. The figure sits inside a non-merchandise line that grew 19.3% while total net sales rose 7.1% to $27.1 billion and comparable sales climbed 5.6% on a 5.0% rise in transactions. Diluted earnings per share reached $2.65, against $1.51 reported and $1.78 adjusted a year ago, although $301 million of refunded tariffs accounted for $0.50 of that. Target now expects full-year net sales to grow by around 6%, one percentage point more than it forecast before.
In Short
Target sold more in its third quarter, with more shoppers coming through its stores and app, and its advertising business, Roundel, grew faster than the rest of the company. That matters to brands that buy retail ads, because Target's ad revenue rose about 23% while Amazon's grew 11% in its own July to September quarter, though a $301 million tariff refund did a large part of the work on Target's profit. What changes now is the outlook: Target raised its full-year sales forecast to around 6% growth and plans $5 billion to $6 billion of capital spending in 2027.
The quarter in numbers
The results cover the three months ended October 31, 2026, compared with the three months ended November 1, 2025. Target filed them with the Securities and Exchange Commission on November 18 as Exhibit 99 to a Form 8-K signed by Jim Lee, executive vice president and chief financial officer, and published a summary page titled "A Closer Look at Q3 2026 Results" on its corporate site the same morning.
Non-merchandise sales are the sum of three lines Target reports separately: advertising revenue, credit card profit sharing and an "Other" line. Target attributes the growth in that group to "strong growth in Roundel ad revenue, Target Circle Card profit sharing revenue, Target Circle 360 membership revenue, and the Target+ marketplace."
Michael Fiddelke, chief executive officer, framed the quarter as cumulative. "Our investments are building on one another - creating momentum in our business and further defining our unique lane in retail," he said, according to the release. Net sales grew in all three months of the quarter, with the strongest performance during the back-to-school and back-to-college seasons, and on a two-year basis both net sales and traffic growth accelerated relative to the first half of the year, according to Target.
Roundel: faster than the company, slower than the first half
Advertising grew more than three times as fast as merchandise sales, though not as fast as credit card profit sharing or the Fun 101 category. The $55 million added in the quarter took advertising to 1.09% of net sales, up from 0.95% a year earlier, and to 47.9% of non-merchandise sales, both by PPC Land's calculation.

The nine-month column tells a second story. Over the first half of fiscal 2026, Target booked $525 million of advertising revenue against $380 million a year earlier, growth of 38.2% by PPC Land's calculation. The third quarter's 22.8% is therefore a deceleration, even as merchandise sales accelerated. Target gave no explanation for the change in pace, and it does not break the line down by format, channel or advertiser type.
Card profit sharing moved the other way. The line grew 28.6% in the third quarter after slipping from $276 million to $269 million over the first half, a 2.5% decline by PPC Land's calculation.
What the advertising line counts
The $296 million is not the full size of Roundel. A footnote to Target's sales table reads, word for word: "Primarily represents revenue related to advertising services provided via the Company's Roundel digital advertising business offering. Roundel services are classified as either Net Sales or as a reduction of Cost of Sales or Selling, General, and Administrative (SG&A) Expenses, depending on the nature of the advertising arrangement."
Part of what brands pay Roundel therefore never appears as revenue. It shows up as lower costs, inside gross margin or inside selling, general and administrative expenses, and Target does not quantify that portion. The distinction matters when comparing Roundel with other networks. PPC Land reported in January that Roundel generated over $1 billion in annual revenue as of 2023, a figure that cannot be reconciled with an advertising revenue line of $821 million over nine months without knowing how much was booked against costs. The release does say that "growth in non-merchandise sales" was one of the reasons gross margin expanded in the quarter.
Against the other retail networks
As a retail media network, Roundel remains small next to its two largest US competitors. Amazon reported advertising revenue of $19.64 billion for its July to September quarter on October 29, growth of 10.9% as reported after Prime Day moved into June. Amazon's quarterly ad revenue is about 66 times Target's reported advertising line, by PPC Land's calculation, though the two companies' fiscal quarters do not cover the same months. Walmart said in August that its global advertising business grew 38% in its second quarter, with Walmart Connect in the US up 43% excluding VIZIO, and its full-year 2025 advertising business grew 46% to nearly $6.4 billion.
Set against the wider commerce media market, Target's growth is above the benchmark. The IAB forecast in April that this spend would grow 12.1% in 2026. Target's 22.8% in the quarter, and 32.2% over nine months, outpace that, while trailing Walmart's rate by a wide margin.
Digital: same-day services carry two-thirds
Digital comparable sales rose 9.8%, against 2.4% a year earlier, and 9.1% over nine months, against 3.8%. Store comparable sales rose 4.6% after falling 3.8% in the prior-year quarter. Target defines its terms precisely, and the definitions in the release read: "Comparable sales include all Merchandise Sales, except sales from stores open less than 13 months or that have been closed. Digitally originated sales include all Merchandise Sales initiated through mobile applications and the Company's websites."
Applying the rounded channel shares to merchandise sales puts digitally originated sales at roughly $5.24 billion in the quarter, against about $4.78 billion a year earlier, a PPC Land estimate that carries rounding error.
The stores still do almost all the work. Merchandise sales fulfilled by stores, according to Target, "include in-store purchases and digitally originated sales fulfilled by shipping merchandise from stores to guests, Order Pickup, Drive Up, and Same Day Delivery." The summary page adds detail that is absent from the release:
- Two-thirds of Target's digital business is driven by same-day services.
- Same-Day Delivery is growing at twice the rate of Order Pickup and Drive Up and reaches 80% of the US population.
- Next-day shipping appears twice as often at checkout as it did last year.
- On-shelf availability improved by a full percentage point year over year, including on the most frequently purchased items.
Artificial intelligence features in the holiday plan rather than in the numbers. Target describes Ask Target as a tool that "helps guests discover products and shop with confidence," while AI-powered tools "help teams improve availability and keep shelves stocked." The release refers to "new AI-powered shopping experiences" for the holiday season without naming the platforms involved. Target's earlier move into agentic commerce came on January 11, when it and Walmart announced checkout inside Google's Gemini and AI Mode, built on the Universal Commerce Protocol. How such checkouts convert is still an open question: in March, Walmart said products sold inside ChatGPT converted three times lower than journeys that clicked out to its own site. Target disclosed no AI traffic or conversion figures on November 18.
Categories: Fun 101 up 27%, apparel down
Five of Target's six core merchandise categories grew. The exception was apparel and accessories, which fell 3.8% by PPC Land's calculation, even after Target cut prices on nearly 2,000 home, apparel and accessories items during the quarter.
Dollar figures from Target. Percentage changes are PPC Land calculations; Target's own rounded descriptions are "nearly 27 percent" for Fun 101, "more than 9 percent" for Beauty and "nearly 7 percent" for Food and Beverage.
Fun 101, the hardlines group, sold $856 million more than in the same quarter a year earlier. Target credits "newness in our electronics owned brand, Heyday and record Pokémon sales," and says it reinvented Fan Shops in more than 1,600 stores, nearly doubling space and assortment. Beauty grew on exclusives in skincare, fragrance and haircare, with Target Beauty Studio now in more than 600 stores, bringing emerging and prestige brands together. In food and beverage, snacks, candy and beverages have collectively delivered nearly 10% net sales growth since an assortment reset in the second quarter, according to the summary page. Back-to-school and back-to-college produced mid-single-digit sales growth and market share gains.
For brands that advertise on Roundel, the category mix is the practical map of where shoppers are spending. Hardlines and beauty grew fastest; apparel shrank.
How the tariff refunds flow through the results
Target's profit growth needs to be read with the tariff refunds in mind. According to the release and its footnotes, the mechanism has six steps:
- During the third quarter Target received refunds of tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), and recognized $301 million "as a reduction of Cost of Sales."
- That lowered cost of sales and added 1.1 percentage points to the gross margin rate and the operating income margin rate.
- After tax, the refunds contributed $228 million to net earnings and $0.50 to both GAAP and adjusted EPS. Target did not exclude them from its adjusted measure.
- Over the nine months to October 31, refunds totaled $1.3 billion and added 1.6 percentage points to the same margin rates.
- Full-year guidance includes approximately $2.14 per share of tariff refund benefits recognized to date. Target states that "The Company's guidance excludes any potential future tariff refunds."
- Over the trailing twelve months, the refunds added 3.0 percentage points to after-tax return on invested capital, which reached 16.5% against 13.4% a year earlier.
Without the refunds, the quarter still grew, more modestly. Target says GAAP EPS would have risen 42% and adjusted EPS 21%; EPS excluding the refunds works out at $2.15. Operating income excluding the $301 million was $1,371 million, 23.6% above the prior year's adjusted $1,109 million, and the operating margin excluding the refunds was about 5.1% against an adjusted 4.4%, both PPC Land calculations. Gross margin excluding refunds would have expanded by approximately 1.2 percentage points from 28.2%, according to Target.
Target attributes that underlying expansion to "the comparison against last year's elevated markdowns and tariff rates, as well as growth in non-merchandise sales and the benefit of productivity improvements at supply chain facilities," partly offset by "elevated freight and fuel-related costs, continued investments in value, and modest pressure from category sales mix."
Costs, stores and cash
Selling, general and administrative expenses rose 7.0% to $5,922 million, roughly in step with the 7.1% rise in sales. The SG&A rate of 21.9% matched last year's reported rate, but was 0.6 points above last year's adjusted 21.3%, which excluded $161 million of business transformation costs described as "employee severance and related costs, as well as asset impairments and other charges related to the termination of a commercial partnership."
