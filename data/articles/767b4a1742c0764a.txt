TITLE: Acute Antidepressant Use Slightly Raises Perceived Exertion in Male Athletes
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Acute administration of antidepressant medications leads to a small but statistically significant increase in the rating of perceived exertion among athletes while producing no consistent overall impairment in objective physical performance, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in the International Journal of Clinical Pharmacy. Conducted by researchers including Jeongha Yun, Sohyeon Park, Kalynn Park, Chaeyoon Kim, Margo Mountjoy, Mark Stuart, and Sandy Jeong Rhie, the investigation evaluated whether different classes of antidepressants alter physiological indicators, power generation, time-trial capability, and subjective effort during athletic activity. Across ten double-blind crossover randomised controlled trials involving 99 male athletes, pooled meta-analyses showed that acute antidepressant intake raised perceived exertion by an average of 0.52 points on the 6-to-20 Borg scale without systematically degrading broad cardiovascular or metabolic metrics.
The research was initiated against a backdrop of increasing recognition of mental health conditions among high-performance competitors, where elite fitness does not grant immunity from psychiatric illness. While general population assumptions frequently presume that high activity levels confer resilience against psychological distress, prior meta-analytic evidence indicates that symptoms of depression or anxiety affect up to 34 percent of current elite athletes and 26 percent of former elite athletes. Furthermore, high-performance sports environments present distinct stressors such as Relative Energy Deficiency in sport, major injuries, public scrutiny, and interpersonal violence that can amplify psychiatric vulnerability. In response to these challenges, the International Olympic Committee has recognized mental health as central to overall athlete well-being, yet sports clinicians frequently encounter diagnostic and therapeutic hesitation because pharmacotherapy introduces worries regarding potential athletic disruption, physiological impairment, and anti-doping regulations.
To rigorously quantify the interaction between pharmacotherapy and athletic demands, the review was prospectively registered in PROSPERO under registration code CRD420251123740 and conducted in strict compliance with Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. The investigative team carried out a comprehensive literature search from database inception through March 2025 across PubMed, the Cochrane Library, Web of Science Core Collection, and SPORTDiscus with Full Text. Search terms integrated controlled vocabularies and free-text phrases spanning athletes, psychotropic agents, and exercise performance metrics, restricted to English-language publications. Additional eligible records were sought through hand-searching of reference lists and screening of major trial registries including ClinicalTrials.gov and the World Health Organization International Clinical Trials Registry Platform.
Study selection followed a structured PICOS framework that required randomised controlled trials enrolling elite, competitive, or recreational athletes engaging in regular moderate-to-vigorous exercise with intent to compete. Interventions were required to evaluate defined antidepressant classes, including selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, norepinephrine–dopamine reuptake inhibitors, norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors, serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors, tricyclic antidepressants, or monoamine oxidase inhibitors against placebo or no-treatment controls. From an initial identification of 10,109 database records, the removal of 165 duplicates left 9,944 records for title and abstract screening. Of 77 full-text articles thoroughly assessed, 67 were excluded due to ineligible populations, non-antidepressant interventions, absence of performance outcomes, non-randomised designs, or non-English language, ultimately leaving 10 published studies representing 11 discrete double-blind crossover trials for inclusion.
The included trials were published between 1992 and 2012 and conducted across three countries: Belgium, Canada, and the United Kingdom. All 99 participants were men, primarily competitive cyclists or triathletes, alongside two trials that enrolled recreational athletes. The pharmacological agents evaluated represented three major classes: the selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor reboxetine in three trials, the norepinephrine–dopamine reuptake inhibitor bupropion in four trials, and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, specifically fluoxetine or paroxetine, in four trials. Most protocols examined acute, single-dose administration over one to two days, whereas only two trials tested short-term repeated daily administration over 10 or 14 days. Notably, the search identified no eligible clinical trials examining tricyclic antidepressants or monoamine oxidase inhibitors in athletic cohorts.
Data synthesis and statistical calculations were executed in R version 4.5.1 using the meta package, employing random-effects models across all outcomes to accommodate methodological differences in drug classes, dosage schedules, exercise protocols, and environmental test conditions. Between-study variance was estimated via restricted maximum likelihood, while 95 percent confidence intervals were derived using the Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman method. For crossover studies lacking reported paired data, within-subject correlation coefficients were imputed with sensitivity analyses testing correlations across 0.0 to 0.7. For trials evaluating performance under different environmental ambient temperatures, results across conditions were combined using established Cochrane Handbook procedures, with statistical heterogeneity formally evaluated through the I-squared statistic.
The primary perceptual finding emerged from a pooled meta-analysis of five crossover trials evaluating rating of perceived exertion immediately following exercise protocols. Acute antidepressant administration produced a statistically significant mean increase of 0.52 points on the Borg 6-to-20 scale, with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 0.01 to 1.04 points and a p-value of 0.049. This alteration represents approximately 3 percent of the total Borg scale span and was characterized by low statistical heterogeneity with an I-squared value of zero percent. When stratified by specific drug classes, neither reboxetine nor bupropion independently demonstrated a statistically significant increase in perceived exertion, showing mean differences of 0.15 points and 0.78 points, respectively, although both subgroup analyses also exhibited zero heterogeneity.
In contrast to the perceptual outcome, pooled analyses of objective performance measures failed to show statistically significant overall alterations across the antidepressant classes combined. The meta-analysis of seven crossover trials assessing time-trial completion time revealed an overall pooled mean difference of 0.88 minutes, with a 95 percent confidence interval of minus 1.69 to positive 3.46 minutes and a p-value of 0.43. Similarly, an overall meta-analysis of four crossover trials evaluating mean mechanical power output showed a pooled mean difference of minus 8.65 watts, with a 95 percent confidence interval extending from minus 32.27 to positive 14.97 watts and a p-value of 0.33. However, both overall analyses were marked by substantial statistical heterogeneity, with I-squared reaching 70.0 percent for time-trial duration and 47.6 percent for mean power output.
Pre-specified subgroup analyses resolved the high statistical heterogeneity by revealing divergent, drug-specific impacts on physical capability. Specifically, the norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor reboxetine demonstrated marked impairment of physical capacity, significantly prolonging time-trial completion times by an average of 3.62 minutes, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.18 to 7.06 minutes and a p-value of 0.045. Reboxetine simultaneously reduced mean mechanical power output by a statistically significant 19.97 watts, with a confidence interval between minus 36.87 and minus 3.06 watts and a p-value of 0.042, both displaying zero percent heterogeneity across trials. Conversely, bupropion showed no statistically significant effect on either time-trial performance, yielding a mean difference of minus 0.91 minutes, or mean power output, which showed a mean difference of minus 0.09 watts.
The researchers noted that physiological markers of exertion exhibited minimal systematic disturbance across pooled cohorts. A meta-analysis of five crossover trials evaluating heart rate measured immediately post-exercise demonstrated an overall mean difference of minus 0.09 beats per minute, with a 95 percent confidence interval of minus 4.96 to positive 4.79 beats per minute and low heterogeneity at 21.1 percent. Post-exercise blood lactate concentrations, synthesized across six crossover trials, similarly revealed an overall mean difference of minus 0.31 millimoles per liter, with a confidence interval spanning minus 1.73 to positive 1.11 millimoles per liter. Subgroup evaluations confirmed that neither reboxetine nor bupropion altered heart rate significantly, while blood lactate was not significantly shifted by reboxetine or selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors.
Methodological quality assessments using the revised Cochrane Risk of Bias tool, RoB 2.0, indicated that none of the 10 included studies attained an overall low risk of bias. While all included trials documented adequate randomisation processes, recurrent concerns were identified regarding the underreporting of intervention fidelity, incomplete blinding protocols, and potential selective outcome reporting. Applying the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework resulted in low certainty ratings for heart rate evidence and very low certainty for perceived exertion, time-trial completion, power output, and blood lactate, with downgrades driven primarily by risks of bias, indirectness, and statistical imprecision.
The study authors highlighted critical limitations that constrain the direct extrapolation of these findings to ongoing psychiatric care in sports. The available data reflect acute or very short-term drug exposures in healthy male participants rather than chronic therapeutic regimens administered to patients with clinically diagnosed depressive disorders. Because therapeutic antidepressant response typically requires sustained administration over multiple weeks, initial adverse effects like somnolence or altered effort perception may attenuate as tolerance or physiological adaptation develops. Clinicians are advised to balance performance monitoring with clinical psychiatric needs, recognizing that unmanaged mood disorders impair sleep, motivation, and overall functioning far more severely, while adhering to updated World Anti-Doping Agency guidelines, which currently place bupropion under monitoring rather than on the prohibited list.
