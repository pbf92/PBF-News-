TITLE: Rockstar co-lead explains how GTA 6's prologue was inspired by Red Dead Redemption 2
OUTLET: GAMINGbible

The GTA VI prologue is a masterclass in storytelling. It sets the tone of the game immediately while still having that authentic Grand Theft Auto charm.
My introduction to Jason and Lucia took me by surprise in what could very well be one of the best kept secrets in gaming. No spoilers, of course, but I’m so surprised it didn’t leak ahead of launch.
In the run up to GTA VI’s historic launch, I spoke to GTA VI’s head of development and Rockstar Games co-head Rob Nelson to learn more about the game’s prologue, and what lessons were learned from Red Dead Redemption 2.
For those who may have forgotten. Rockstar’s previous game has one of the most agonisingly slow openings to any AAA game ever made, so much so that the majority of players would probably skip it entirety if they could.

During my entire time reviewing GTA VI, I was probably at my most-critical during the prologue and first chapter – and that’s largely because of Red Dead Redemption 2’s opening crawl.
Red Dead Redemption 2’s painfully slow prologue was originally even longer
While playing for the first time, I kept asking myself this question: “When I inevitably replay this game, will I be okay with doing all of this again?”
Fortunately, Rockstar Games has put considerable effort into speeding up the opening and getting you into the open-world quicker, and fan feedback to Red Dead Redemption 2 had a hand in this.
Rob Nelson told me: “When we were doing Red Dead we had committed to that winter section pretty early on, and we were kind of stuck with it. It was designed to teach you how to play the game, because the game was more complicated than anything that we had done before.”
Even on your first playthrough, Red Dead Redemption 2’s opening feels like a slow-burn, so you’ll probably be surprised to hear it was meant to be even longer.
“We took as much fat out of it as we could if you can believe that. There's a lot of cuts where we skipped the journey, because it was originally longer … We made it as fast as we could.”
Rockstar applied a similar logic to GTA VI, but took the trimming even further to ensure players could dive into Leonida as quickly as possible.
“In Chapter 1, we were very aware of the length. We really didn't want to bog players down with awkward tutorial stuff, so we tried to make it all feel as seamless as possible, and also trust players more," Rob said.
“Your first objective in the prologue is to lose the cops so we teach you how to jump and teach you how to run. That's pretty much it."
Nelson explained this was done to “get you to Jason in the open world as expediently as possible” for those who want to start exploring straight away.
Yet despite quickening the pace compared to Red Dead Redemption 2, GTA VI’s opening doesn’t feel any less impactful.
Somehow, Rockstar appealed to both crowds: the players who want to immerse themselves in a rich narrative and those who just want to drive off and cause some trouble.
GTA VI is without a doubt Rockstar Games’ best work to date, and we’re so glad it’s finally here. It was worth the wait.
