TITLE: OpenAI’s GPT-Live-1.1 tops Voice Arena’s Jarvis Bench, but humans still win the call
OUTLET: Crypto Briefing

OpenAI's newest voice model took first place on a live-call benchmark, though raters still preferred human agents and scored Gemini higher on call handling
OpenAI’s GPT-Live-1.1 has taken first place on Voice Arena’s Jarvis Bench, a benchmark built around live phone calls rather than tidy lab prompts.
There is a catch. The human raters scoring those calls still preferred actual human agents, and they rated Gemini’s call handling higher than OpenAI’s.
What GPT-Live-1.1 actually does
GPT-Live-1.1 arrived in OpenAI’s API on or around November 19, 2026. It is the successor to GPT-Live-1.
The model is full-duplex, meaning it can listen and talk at the same moment. Older voice systems tend to work in turn: you speak, you stop, the machine answers. A full-duplex model can handle interruptions and overlapping speech, which is how people actually talk to each other.
OpenAI has also pointed to better transcription accuracy and more consistent accents across multiple languages. The accent fix targets a familiar quirk of voice AI, where a model’s accent can drift mid-conversation.
The API launch was not the model’s debut. GPT-Live-1.1 had already become the default voice model for ChatGPT Plus and Pro subscribers starting October 15, 2026.

The pricing move developers will notice
Active use of GPT-Live-1.1 costs $0.05 per minute. The more interesting number is the new standby mode, priced at $0.02 per minute.
Standby kicks in after a configurable idle timeout. Developers can decide how long a quiet line sits at full price before the cheaper rate applies.
How Jarvis Bench keeps score
Voice Arena’s Jarvis Bench tests voice agents under practical, real-world conditions. It runs live calls and measures how often agents succeed at tasks such as booking airline travel.
The benchmark also relies on human judgment. Raters cast Elo-style votes, the same basic system used to rank chess players, where head-to-head matchups determine relative standing.
Jarvis Bench also puts AI agents side by side with human-driven interactions. GPT-Live-1.1 beat its machine competitors on the benchmark. When the comparison included real people, raters still sided with the humans.
Gemini, Google’s model family, also earned higher marks than GPT-Live-1.1 on call handling in certain metrics.
As of November 26, 2026, OpenAI had not published detailed Jarvis Bench leaderboard scores or publicly confirmed the model’s results there.
