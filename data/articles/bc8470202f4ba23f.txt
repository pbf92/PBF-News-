Man United row escalates as ‘referees left furious’ and Premier League panel vote leaked
Leny Yoro was sent off just eight minutes into Sunday's clash between Manchester United and Liverpool at Anfield, but will not serve a ban against Brentford this weekend
Premier League referees and officials are said to be 'furious' over the FA's decision to overturn Leny Yoro's red card against Liverpool on Sunday. That's despite the English top flight's own independent panel backing Sam Barrott's decision to dismiss the defender.
The 21-year-old lasted just eight minutes into the clash at Anfield before he was shown an early exit for bringing down Alexander Isak on the edge of his own area as the last man back. Dominik Szoboszlai stepped up to convert the resulting free-kick, with Florian Wirtz, Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz also finding the back of the net in a 4-0 win.
Mauricio Pochettino was quick to slam VAR's decision not to overrule Barrott's judgement, before lambasting Howard Webb and ProRef for backing the judgement. Webb has since noted that the official's opinion was "certainly not wrong," further angering United officials who successfully appealed the sending-off.
A source at Old Trafford told the MEN that the interpretation of ProRef was 'totally wrong' and was compounded by Webb's failure to recognise the error - despite an FA disciplinary panel overturning the decision. A club source also noted how the matter will be raised with the Premier League and ProRef.
Now, as per the Mail, the Premier League's Key Match Incidents panel has voted 5-0 in favour of Barrott's decision to send Yoro off. The panel are also claimed to have backed VAR's decision not to intervene.

Their findings are set to be released next Wednesday. While Yoro's red card has been rescinded, meaning he will not miss this weekend's clash with Brentford, the Premier League and ProRef continue to stand by their original decision.
Top-flight officials are now said to be demanding answers on how such a decision was made, with Webb backing his referees. He said of the matter on Mic'd Up: "That (the red card) was the referee's call, made on the field.

"Then the VAR checks whether the decision made on the field was clearly and obviously incorrect - and formed the opinion it wasn't. I think by and large, most people would see that as a correct decision, with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball.
"The alternative view is that Lammens might get there first, but I think it's a little bit debatable, I guess. Certainly not clearly wrong."
The Premier League and Pro Ref have not commented on the most recent claims. It comes as United sit 19th in the English top flight as a result of Sunday's loss to Liverpool.
Pochettino will be feeling the pressure as he now looks to a home clash with Brentford come Saturday at 3pm.
