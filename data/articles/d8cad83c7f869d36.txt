TITLE: HKU Institute for Precision Prevention launches Protecting.Health Hong Kong Study
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

The Institute for Precision Prevention (IPP) at The University of Hong Kong (HKU) has officially launched the Protecting.Health Hong Kong Study. This long-term research programme is led by Professor Ferenc Krausz, 2023 Nobel Laureate in Physics, Director of the IPP, Chief Principal Investigator of the study and Chair Professor of Laser Physics of the Department of Physics at Faculty of Science. The initiative aims to revolutionise healthcare by enabling the earlier, personalised prevention of major non-communicable diseases, including cardiovascular disease, cancer, diabetes, and chronic respiratory diseases.
Professor Xiang Zhang, President and Vice-Chancellor of HKU, affirmed the University’s strong support for the initiative. "We celebrate this very important milestone as Professor Ferenc Krausz passionately launches this vital initiative with the support of our colleagues in medicine," said Professor Zhang. "He is a scientific giant who has crossed disciplinary boundaries into medicine to make a profound impact on human life and health."
The Protecting.Health Hong Kong Study will enrol 15,000 local participants for a 10-year active follow-up, consisting of regular clinical assessments and blood sampling at partner clinical sites. This will be followed by an additional 10 years of health data collection through data linkage. By establishing each participant’s personal molecular baseline and tracking subtle changes over time using advanced molecular phenotyping and artificial intelligence, the study aims to identify early molecular signatures of disease risk years before clinical symptoms appear. The ultimate goal is to develop an affordable, personalised health screening algorithm (PH1.0).
At the core of the study is Electric-Field Molecular Fingerprinting, a technology developed from Professor Krausz’s Nobel Prize-winning research in fundamental physics. By using infrared light to read molecular patterns in blood, the technology can detect the earliest molecular signals of disease while an individual is still healthy.
The study has already commenced its clinical phase, welcoming its first participants—The Honourable Bernard Charnwut Chan, Dr the Honourable Wing-man Ko, and Dr the Honourable Ching-choi Lam—who have completed their baseline clinical assessments and blood sample collections to establish their personal molecular baselines.
"Looking back, a single guiding thread has shaped my scientific journey: the drive to make the invisible visible again and again," said Professor Krausz. "Modern medicine has become remarkably good at repairing the car after the engine breaks down. But it would be far more efficient if we could recognise the fault while the engine is still running well. That is exactly our ambition."
Professor Sophia Chan, Chief Scientific Advisor of the IPP, Director of HKU Primary Health Care Academy (PHCA) under the School of Public Health, LKS Faculty of Medicine and Director of WHO Collaborating Centre for Health and Nursing Workforce Development in Primary Health Care, emphasised the study’s alignment with Hong Kong’s strategic healthcare reforms. "This research supports Hong Kong’s crucial shift towards primary healthcare and preventive medicine, translating cutting-edge science into direct benefits for our local community," she said.
Professor Dennis Ip, Associate Director of the IPP, and Clinical Professor of Practice and Division Head of Community Medicine and Public Health Practice, School of Public Health, LKS Faculty of Medicine, will oversee the clinical execution and day-to-day operations of the programme.
The Hong Kong study is a key component of the international Protecting.Health Initiative led by Professor Krausz, which bridges curiosity-driven basic science with global human health. The project brings together the IPP at HKU with leading research partners in Germany, Hungary, and Ghana. For more information about the study, please visit: https://phhk.hku.hk/
About The Institute for Precision Prevention
The Institute for Precision Prevention at The University of Hong Kong is dedicated to advancing personalised preventive medicine through the integration of clinical research, advanced molecular technologies and data science. Its mission is to shift healthcare from reactive treatment toward proactive, precision prevention.
