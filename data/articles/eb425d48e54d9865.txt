TITLE: Early pregnancy ultrasounds at rural North Carolina clinic improve care for spanish-speaking patients
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

In rural areas with maternity care shortages, many pregnant patients can’t get an early ultrasound to help assess pregnancy location, viability, and gestational age. Family physicians at Piedmont Health’s Siler City clinic in Chatham County, North Carolina, started a weekly first-trimester ultrasound clinic within their primary care practice. Before the program, many of the clinic’s mostly Spanish-speaking, often uninsured patients did not get imaging until midway through pregnancy or until they went to an emergency department. Since 2022, the clinic has performed more than 650 ultrasounds, typically within a week. Patients and clinicians report high satisfaction, citing less travel, lower costs and care in patients’ own language, though this hasn’t been formally measured.
Embedding First-Trimester Obstetric Ultrasonography in Rural Primary Care
