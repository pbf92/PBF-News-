Goldman Sachs Warns S&P 500 Selloff May Not Be Over
The S&P 500 sits 12% below its Oct. 6 record, a deeper drop than the 10% median calendar-year decline since 1980, and Goldman Sachs (GS) says a few signals could show when selling is exhausted, according to a note to clients.
The pain has spread beyond AI: Goldman's AI basket is down 32% since the peak, and the index excluding AI sits 6% below its high.
Goldman would read any of these as supportive of a low: evidence that AI spending will pay off in earnings, a dovish, or rate-friendly, Federal Reserve signal, or a much sharper pullback in investor positioning.
That last one is not yet in place. Hedge funds have cut net leverage to its lowest since April 2025, yet Goldman's wider positioning gauge remains above average.
Past corrections of at least 10% have usually been followed by gains. Across 22 such episodes since 1980, the S&P 500 added an average 4% over three months and rose in 73% of them.
Goldman's 12-month forecast for the S&P 500 is 8,700, a target set before the latest slide.
