TITLE: TVP wins top spot in Poland's video rankings with 18.28% share
OUTLET: PPC Land

Telewizja Polska returned to first place among Poland's video content distributors in October 2026, taking 18.28% of in-home viewing across television sets, computers, smartphones and tablets, according to Nielsen's All Screens Video Landscape report, published in Warsaw on November 19, 2026. The public broadcaster climbed from third, pushing Polsat into second place at 18.06% and Warner Bros. Discovery into third at 17.93%.
In Short
Nielsen's monthly ranking of the companies behind the video Polish households watch put public broadcaster TVP first in October, just ahead of Polsat and Warner Bros. Discovery, with all three within 0.35 percentage points of each other. Live national football and returning autumn shows gave TVP the edge, while YouTube kept moving onto the living-room TV, where 34% of its Polish viewing now takes place. The month shows three TV groups still holding more than half of measured video time in Poland, and YouTube's growth coming on the big screen rather than the phone.
The October table
Nielsen published the report on November 19, 2026, under the dateline "Warsaw, Poland". The page header carries only "November 2026". The figures cover October 2026, and the individual shares appear only in the chart attached to the release. The text gives rank changes but no percentages.
"The top three content distributors shifted again in October, with TVP climbing from third to first place," according to Nielsen. "Following TVP's surge, Polsat moved into second place and Warner Bros. Discovery took third."

The margins are thin. On PPC Land's arithmetic, TVP led Polsat by 0.22 points and Polsat led Warner Bros. Discovery by 0.13 points. The three largest groups held 54.27% of measured viewing between them, down from 55.49% in September, and the top 10 together accounted for 78.54%, against 78.65% a month earlier.
First place, by subtraction
TVP's climb owes more to its rivals' losses than to its own growth. The broadcaster added 0.25 points. Polsat gave up 0.99 points and Warner Bros. Discovery 0.48. Placed in September's table, TVP's October share of 18.28% would still have ranked third, behind Polsat's 19.05% and Warner Bros. Discovery's 18.41%.
So where did the 1.47 points shed by the two former leaders go? Mostly to other broadcasters. Telewizja Republika gained 0.65 points, Canal+ 0.27, TVP 0.25, Canal+ Content Benelux & Central Europe 0.14 and TV Puls 0.07, a combined 1.38 points on PPC Land's calculation. The seven television groups in the top 10 held 67.19% of viewing in October, against 67.28% in September. The three platforms - Google, Meta and ByteDance - held 11.35%, against 11.37%. Measured this way, October was a reshuffle among broadcasters rather than a movement of audiences between television and online platforms.
The October release does not explain Polsat's fall beyond the change in rank. Its September lead, however, was built on sport: Nielsen said then that Polsat aired eight of the month's 20 most-watched programmes, all of them live volleyball matches, with Poland against France drawing more than 4.8 million viewers on September 26. The October release mentions no volleyball. Reading Polsat's decline as the end of that tournament effect is PPC Land's inference, not Nielsen's.
Football, returning series and reality television
Nielsen gives TVP's result two causes. "TVP's return to the top was driven by two major pillars: live national football and its autumn programming slate," according to the release.
The football was Poland's UEFA Nations League schedule. TVP broadcast the national team's fixtures against Romania and against Bosnia and Herzegovina, which ranked as the second and fifth most-watched programmes of October. The rest came from entertainment. "Alongside live sport, TVP's refreshed schedule saw three series (returning with new episodes) and two reality shows secure spots in October's top 10 most-watched broadcasts," according to Nielsen.
Taken together, that places at least seven of the month's 10 most-watched broadcasts on TVP channels. Polsat's best performer was Dancing with the Stars, the sixth most-watched programme of October, which Nielsen said underscored "the enduring appeal of both live sport and marquee entertainment formats among Polish audiences."
Several things are missing. The release does not name the most-watched programme of the month, the three series or the two reality shows. It gives no audience figures for the matches, either, which leaves the football's weight in TVP's monthly share unquantified.
Lucyna Koba, Market Leader at Nielsen Poland, tied the result to the season. "October's rankings capture the core strengths of the Polish television ecosystem," she said. "As colder weather brings audiences back indoors, Polish viewers consistently gravitate toward shared national moments and familiar local formats - whether that is rallying behind the national football team in the Nations League or tuning in for major prime-time entertainment like Dancing with the Stars and new episodes of successful series."
National team football and a live dance competition are both forms of appointment viewing, watched when they air rather than recorded for later. In two consecutive months, Nielsen has credited live sport with deciding the order at the top of the table: volleyball for Polsat in September, football alongside autumn programming for TVP in October.
The rest of the top 10
The largest gain anywhere in the table went to a distributor the release does not mention. Telewizja Republika, represented in Nielsen's list by a single channel, TV Republika, rose 0.65 points to 5.02% and held fifth place. It now draws more measured viewing than TV Puls, with its two channels, and more than Meta's Facebook and Instagram combined.
At the foot of the table, a name changed. "Canal+ Content Benelux & Central Europe (formerly SPI International) and ByteDance swapped places at the bottom of the top 10, with ByteDance dropping from ninth to tenth," according to Nielsen. The renamed distributor is credited with 11 channels, the same count listed under SPI International a month earlier, among them Stopklatka, Kino Polska, Kino Polska Muzyka, DocuBox, ZOOM, Gametoon and five FILMBOX+ channels.
The top 10 now contains two Canal+-branded distributors, which Nielsen ranks separately. Combined, Canal+ at 2.26% and Canal+ Content Benelux & Central Europe at 1.83% would hold 4.09%, enough to rank sixth, on PPC Land's calculation. The release does not describe the relationship between the two entities.
Among the platforms, little moved. Google slipped 0.02 points to 6.26%, Meta added 0.01 to 3.29% and ByteDance lost 0.01 to 1.80%. Meta and ByteDance again recorded no measured viewing on television sets. Netflix, which dropped out of the top 10 in September, stayed out. Its share in October was therefore below 1.80%, though the release does not mention it.
YouTube moves to the big screen
The flat total hides a change in where YouTube is watched. "Following a 32% TV screen share in September, Google's viewership on the TV screen rose by a further 2% in October to 34%," according to Nielsen. The move described as "2%" is two percentage points; in relative terms, the TV-set share rose by 6.25% on PPC Land's calculation.
The longer series is steadier than it might appear from a single month.
According to Nielsen, the climb from 27% two years ago "highlights a clear shift in how Polish audiences consume YouTube content in the living room."
Applying the screen split to Google's overall share gives a rough sense of scale. On that PPC Land approximation, about 2.13 points of Google's 6.26% came from television sets in October, compared with about 2.01 points of 6.28% in September. Viewing on phones, computers and tablets fell from about 4.27 points to about 4.13. Because Nielsen rounds the screen split to whole percentages, these are estimates, but they point the same way: YouTube's share of total viewing barely changed, while more of it moved to the television.
Koba attributed the shift to deliberate effort on both sides of the screen. "At the same time, we are seeing a notable shift in how audiences use the television set itself, with Google steadily growing its TV screen share. This shows YouTube's strategy to capture more living-room viewing is paying off," she said. "More Polish TV stations are actively collaborating with YouTube and making a wider range of their content available on the platform, which naturally encourages viewers to watch on a bigger screen. Combined with simple, everyday triggers - like dedicated YouTube buttons on modern TV remote controls - the path from traditional channels to YouTube on the main screen has become effortless."
She did not name the stations. And the release does not say how a broadcaster's programme watched on YouTube is credited - to Google, or to the broadcaster that made it. That question matters for any reading of the table, since the more Polish channels publish on YouTube, the more their output may count toward Google's share.
The Polish trend matches YouTube's direction elsewhere. In the United States, YouTube took a record 15.8% of TV viewing time in Nielsen's August 2026 Gauge. The company has also been changing how its connected TV viewing is counted and controlled: since November 2026, its public view counts have included an estimate of co-viewers watching together on TV screens, and in September it removed the shared Wi-Fi requirement for controlling TV playback from a phone, relying on the same account being signed in on both devices.
How Nielsen counts
According to Nielsen, the All Screens Video Landscape report "measures total household viewership - defined as a combination of total reach and time spent - across four screens: TVs, PCs/laptops, smartphones, and tablets." The chart's source line reads: "SOURCE: Nielsen Media; Total Video Consolidated +7 InHome; 4 screens; Target: All4+; metrics: SHR% October 2026; TOP 10 content distributors; Other screens: smartphone, PC, tablet."
In plain terms, the share figure (SHR%) covers viewing by everyone aged four and over, inside the home, and includes time-shifted viewing up to seven days after first broadcast. Viewing outside the home is excluded, which affects the mobile-first platforms most. The chart legend labels the second category in Polish, "pozostałe ekrany", or other screens. Earlier releases in the series described a single-source panel of 3,500 households and nearly 9,700 panelists; the October release does not restate the panel size.
A distributor, in Nielsen's grouping, is a holding company plus every channel or online platform attributed to it. The logic resembles Nielsen's US Media Distributor Gauge, which ranks American media owners by adding up the viewing of everything they control, although the Polish report spans four screens rather than the television set alone. As in September, the broadcaster lists contain only television channels. The streaming services that appeared in Nielsen's lists a year ago are absent, which may partly explain why Polsat shows no measured viewing on other screens.
The report is distinct from Nielsen's Gauge for Poland, which divides time spent in front of television sets by delivery path - cable, satellite, terrestrial and streaming. For September, the Gauge put YouTube at 2.3% of Polish TV time and streaming at 10.4%. The October edition is the subject of a separate PPC Land article.
Both linear and streaming grew
The distributor shares are slices of a larger total, and that total grew. In September, Nielsen said linear television rebounded while streaming engagement softened month over month.
