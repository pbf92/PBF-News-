TITLE: Two years after Baby Ava was found in a snow-covered Salford field, police prepare to lay her to rest
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

Two years after she was found beneath an ash tree plans are being made for the funeral of Baby Ava. It is also expected that an inquest into her death will open soon.
She was buried with a trowel or spade in a 12cm deep grave. But her remains were found on Wednesday November 20th 2024 on top of a snow-covered field.
It is believed a badger, fox, or large bird hunting for food in freezing conditions brought her tiny body to the surface a day or hours after it was left there. Ava was found by a dog being walked at Ashtons Field, Little Hulton, Salford.
Intensive work by scientists and police mean they can be certain as to the 20-day period in which Baby Ava was left at the entrance to the country park, created on the site of a former colliery. But two years on her identity and that of her parents remain unknown.
A thousand homes in the Little Hulton and neighbouring Farnworth area have been visited by police in a bid to find witnesses. They have also followed up and investigated 200 pieces of information which have come in from the public.
Due to leaf litter and the soil type experts are able to say Ava was placed at the site no earlier than the beginning of Novermber 2024. The time frame for the movement of her to the site is from November 1st to November 20th. However it is thought her body may have been kept elsewhere for a substantial period before it was left in the field.
The child was thought to be at 38 to 39 weeks gestation, after work by an anthropologist, archaeologist and dental and bone experts. The location of her discovery, next to Cleggs Lane, suggests whoever left her there may have done so in a panic. They could have walked into the park but instead Ava was left twenty steps from a busy road over the M61 motorway and near an industrial estate.
Detective Inspector Louise Kelly is leading the investigation to solve who Baby Ava is and the circumstances of her death. She said GMP was now liaising with the local authority to arrange for a funeral for the child. An inquest is "likely" to be opened soon and then adjourned for a full hearing.
"My entire team are still working on the investigation. We have continued pursuing multiple lines of inquiry, and making house inquiries in the local area which is quite vast but specific to Little Hulton and Farnworth.
"We are continuing with our forensic inquiries and making sure we have exhausted every possible opportunity we have got," said DI Kelly.
Baby Ava was found wrapped in a distinctive pair of women's briefs. They are a size large, pink and beige in colour and have an unusual donkey design. Police say there is a possibility the mother may have worn them when pregnant. Tests of the fabric have yielded a DNA profile.
Police have established the briefs are not manufactured in the UK but imported wholesale into the country. Police had a male profile from the knickers but when they ran it against the National Database there was no hit.
Police are still working on familial DNA as they have Ava's DNA. They have mitochondrial DNA - inherited exclusively down the maternal line. This means if they find Ava's mother there would be no doubt it was Ava's mother.
DI Kelly said: "There has been no further breakthrough but we are still waiting for the results of some tests and inquiries to come back. We have completed our review of CCTV in the area and that has not given us any further leads unfortunately.
"We have had a variety of pieces of information from the public but nothing which has led us to identifying who Ava's parents might be, or who may be involved in how Ava came to be left at Ashtons Field.
"We have multiple lines of inquiry ongoing and it is our priority to secure justice for Baby Ava. But we have not found Ava's mum and dad and we do not know where she was born."
She added: "To Ava's mum and dad the anniversary of Ava being found in Ashtons Field in Novermber 2024 that is likely to be a significant date for them. My appeal to them is to come forward to the investigation team and allow us to speak to them and help us get justice for Baby Ava.
"We will be here to support them. Obviously we can't ignore any criminality that there may be in this investigation.
"A reward of £50,000 from the Greater Manchester Combined Authority is still on offer for any information leading to the identity of Baby Ava. I have now had confirmation from the Coroner that we can look to organise her funeral.
"A message has been sent to the local authority, Salford, about that. We are hoping it can be organised soon. Ordinarily GMP would not be involved (in the funeral) as the investigation team, that would be with the family. But in this situation because we don't know who Ava's family are we are very mindful that we don't want that funeral to take place with no one there for Ava."
Scientists are still trying to establish when Ava was born. "We don't have that information yet but part of the forensic tests is to see if we can at least establish a time parameter for that. What the forensic evidence is telling us at the moment is that Ava may have been somewhere else before she was at Ashtons Field but at this time we can't say how long for.
"If anyone is sat at home wondering whether they should come forward whether they have information that may or may not relevant, my ask of them is that they do come to us. However small that information is it could help us with our investigation. and help is identify Ava." said DI Kelly.
The owner of the dog which found Ava called a relative in shock then, soon afterwards, 999 at 12.22pm that day. Experts have told police that the level of decomposition of the body indicated she could not have decomposed that quickly from November 1 to November 20.
Detective Chief Inspector, Charlotte Whalley, previously told the Manchester Evening News: "I suspect she has been somewhere else first. If she had been wrapped and put in a freezer the expert says it could have been two to three years before when she was born.
"She could have been kept in a house in a fridge, there are so many options and that is why a forensic pathologist cannot tell me when she was born. I think there would have been a trigger for the movement of her (to Ashtons Field). Something happened - either her family moved or someone else found out."
Detective Inspector Louise Kelly, the Senior Investigating Officer in this case, said: “Two years have now passed since Ava was found in Ashtons Field, and throughout that time our determination to find answers for her has never wavered. Most importantly, we want to secure the justice that Ava deserves.
“We know that the 20th November will be particularly significant for Ava’s parents, family members, and anyone who may have known her. I want to appeal to them to say we would like to speak to you to understand how Ava came to be found in Ashtons Field in November 2024.
“Behind every line of enquiry, every review of evidence, and every piece of information we assess is a baby girl who will never be forgotten.
“We are currently working with local authorities to organise a funeral for Baby Ava, this way she can be laid to rest.
“We continue to consider and explore all investigative opportunities available to us. However, at this stage we still do not have answers.
“Last year, we were able to share details of a distinctive item recovered with Ava - a pair of ladies’ size large underwear featuring what we believed to be cartoon donkeys. Despite the unique nature of this item, and our appeals to identify its origin or owner, we have received no new viable leads.
“We firmly believe that someone may hold information that could help us understand the full circumstances surrounding Ava's death. It may be something that seems insignificant, something seen or heard at the time, or information that has never before been shared with police.
“That one piece of information could prove crucial in helping us uncover the truth and provide the answers that Ava deserves.
“We will not give up on Ava. We simply ask anyone who knows something, no matter how small they believe it to be, to find the courage to come forward and speak to us.”
