TITLE: Campaign bid strategy
OUTLET: PPC Land

Campaign bid strategy is a dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that labels each row of performance data with the bidding rule its campaign was running under. In Sponsored Products, that rule decides whether Amazon enters auctions at exactly the bid the advertiser set or adjusts it up or down on each impression according to its own prediction of a sale. The field exists because the same keyword at the same bid can cost very different amounts depending on that setting, and a report without it cannot separate the advertiser's pricing decisions from Amazon's. According to Amazon's Support Center dimensions page, as read by PPC Land in November 2026, the field applies to Sponsored Products only. It returns nothing for Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display, Sponsored TV (STV) or DVA+, the display, video and audio campaign type that replaced the Amazon DSP name in September 2026.
Where the field sits
Unified reporting arranges an account as a hierarchy: advertiser account, portfolio, campaign, ad group, target, ad. Campaign-level dimensions describe settings that apply to everything below. Campaign bid strategy sits beside Campaign budget amount, Campaign budget type, Campaign cost type and Campaign rule amount, the last described as "the modified campaign budget based on a rule". Of the 156 dimensions on the page, PPC Land found only two that carry the Sponsored Products marker alone: Campaign bid strategy and Campaign rule amount.
The definition Amazon gives is short and, as discussed below, odd. PPC Land reported that the page defines the field through the examples "front loaded or evenly spread".
How a Sponsored Products bidding strategy works
Every Sponsored Products campaign carries one bidding strategy, which acts on the bids set lower down, on ad group defaults and individual keyword or product targets. As of November 2026, Amazon's Support Center lists three strategies:
- Dynamic bids - down only. "We may decrease your bids by up to 100% for all placements based on performance." Amazon lowers the bid when it judges a conversion unlikely.
- Dynamic bids - up and down. "We may increase or decrease your bids by up to 100% for all placements based on performance." Amazon's guide, last updated on October 24, 2026, gives the worked example of a $1.00 bid rising to $2.00 and tells advertisers to "be prepared to potentially spend double the amount of the bid you input."
- Fixed bids. "Amazon will use your exact bid for all opportunities and won't adjust your bids."
A fourth mode, rule-based bidding, sits alongside. The advertiser sets a performance goal such as return on ad spend (ROAS), calculated as "total revenue from ads / total cost of ads", and Amazon adjusts base bids per impression toward it, with the caveat that it "does not guarantee that we'll hit the performance target." Bids can rise up to three times on top of search during high-opportunity periods, two to three times on top of search otherwise, and 1.5 times on the rest of search and product pages. Eligibility is narrow: a campaign must have run for at least 10 days, carry a daily budget of at least $10 and have recorded 10 conversions in the previous 30 days.
In the Sponsored Products API version 3, the setting lives in a dynamicBidding object whose strategy field accepts four values: LEGACY_FOR_SALES, AUTO_FOR_SALES, MANUAL and RULE_BASED. The names correspond to down only, up and down, fixed and rule-based respectively. The same object holds placementBidding, the percentage uplifts by placement.
Placement adjustments stack on top of the strategy. Advertisers can raise bids by up to 900%, ten times the base bid, for top of search on the first results page, the rest of search, and product pages. Audience adjustments, using custom audiences from Amazon Marketing Cloud, compound with them. Amazon's own example takes a $1.00 bid to $4.50 once both are applied. Under up and down, a $1.00 bid with a 50% top-of-search adjustment becomes $1.50, which Amazon may then double to $3.00.
Schedule and event bid rules, which raise bids at set times or before shopping events, work with the three standard strategies but not with rule-based bidding.
Origin and evolution
Amazon introduced Sponsored Products in 2012 with a single behaviour: a cost-per-click (CPC) bid, lowered automatically when a click looked unlikely to convert. In January 2019 the company launched three bidding strategies, placement multipliers between 0% and 900%, and product targeting. At launch, according to trade coverage at the time, up and down could raise bids by up to 100% on top of search and 50% on other placements. Amazon's current documentation states 100% for all placements; the date of that change has not been published.
Other products took different paths. On November 1, 2020, Amazon DSP introduced bid strategies for new self-service orders, with two options: "While spending full budget, maximize performance", the default, and "Maximize performance". On October 25, 2023, at unBoxed, DSP added goal-based bidding, in which an advertiser could "set a target CPA of $10 and choose 'Prioritize KPI target'". Sponsored Products gained schedule-based bid rules on November 6, 2023, and a rest-of-search placement adjustment on January 9, 2024. On August 8, 2024, Sponsored Brands added cost control, in which machine learning "adjusts bids in real-time for every opportunity".
The reporting name is recent. Unified reporting entered beta on November 11, 2025 and reached general availability on June 8, 2026. The PPC Land review of the dimensions page lists no former name for Campaign bid strategy, unlike Campaign name, which was "Order" in the old DSP reports.
Why the field matters
The strategy changes how every cost number on a row should be read. Under fixed bids, CPC can only reach the target bid plus placement uplifts. Under up and down, it can legitimately reach double that before placements. An account mixing both strategies, aggregated without this field, produces an average CPC that describes neither.
The setting also determines who is making the price decision. Amazon's Ad Lab video series compared fixed and dynamic bidding in October 2026 and named four metrics to watch, but published no test results, concluding that "neither strategy is universally better." Under fixed bidding, the video said, "If you've bid a dollar, Amazon enters the auction at a dollar every time." Under dynamic strategies, Amazon chooses the submitted bid, and that choice is not reported.
That gap has acquired legal weight. On August 31, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and 22 states sued Amazon over alleged undisclosed reserve-price surcharges. PPC Land reported the complaint's claim that advertisers paid their full bid 79% of the time in Sponsored Products in 2024. Amazon disputes the reading and says that "in no scenario does an advertiser pay more than their bid." Separating campaigns by bidding strategy is one of the few ways an advertiser can test, from its own data, how often CPC approaches the stated maximum.
Limitations and disputes
The definition does not match the product. "Front loaded" and "evenly spread" are terms that describe budget pacing, how quickly a budget is spent across a day or flight, not how bids are set. Sponsored Products has no pacing setting with those names; its campaigns carry daily budgets and one of the bidding strategies listed above. Amazon has not explained the examples, and as of November 2026 has not published the values the field returns. Whether a row shows "Dynamic bids - up and down", an API enum such as AUTO_FOR_SALES, or a pacing label is unconfirmed.
Timing is a second unknown. Amazon has not said whether the field reports the strategy in force when each impression served or the strategy currently set. If it is the latter, a campaign switched from fixed to dynamic in mid-month would label its whole history with the new value.
Coverage is a third. Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display and DVA+ all have their own bidding or optimisation settings, yet none is reported here. Sponsored Display's equivalent surfaces as Ad group optimization type, formerly "Bid optimization". Ad group bid strategy, the sibling field, carries no ad product marker at all on Amazon's page, according to PPC Land, and no definition.
Commonly confused terms
Campaign bid strategy vs ad group bid strategy. The campaign field covers Sponsored Products only. The ad group field is undefined and unmarked on Amazon's page. Older DSP structures set bidding on line items, now called ad groups, which may be where it applies.
Campaign bid strategy vs portfolio bid strategy. In Google Ads, a portfolio bid strategy is one automated strategy shared across campaigns. Amazon portfolios group campaigns and can cap their combined budget, but each Sponsored Products campaign keeps its own bidding strategy.
Campaign bid strategy vs bid modifier. A bid modifier, such as Amazon's placement or audience adjustment, is a percentage applied to a bid in specific conditions. The bidding strategy is the overall rule under which those adjustments operate.
Campaign bid strategy vs Campaign cost type. Cost type is the billing unit, such as CPC or cost per thousand impressions (CPM). Strategy is how the bid in that unit is set.
Recent developments
Amazon updated its unified reporting dimensions page on November 11, 2026, and PPC Land's review the next day surfaced the pacing-style definition. In February 2026, according to analytics firm Nova, Amazon began testing a Sponsored Products option called "Automatically optimizing ROAS within budget" with select US accounts, in which the advertiser sets only a daily budget and Amazon sets every bid. Legacy Sponsored Ads and DSP report pages are due to stop working on January 31, 2027, after which unified reporting, and this field, become the only source for the setting in console reports. The FTC case continues in the Western District of Washington.
