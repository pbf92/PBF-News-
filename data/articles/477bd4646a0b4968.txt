TITLE: Advertiser account ID
OUTLET: PPC Land

Advertiser account ID is a dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that holds the system identifier of the advertiser account a row of data belongs to. The advertiser account holds one advertiser's campaigns, users, billing and business details, below any manager account and above every campaign. The field exists because a single unified report can now span several accounts, ad products and countries at once. Without a stable account key in each row, spend from two brands would merge into one total.
Until 2025 Amazon ran separate account systems for sponsored ads and for its demand-side platform (DSP), and an advertiser buying both held at least one account of each type in every country. The identifier in this column inherits that history.
Where it sits in the reference
Amazon's dimensions reference for unified reporting, updated on November 11, 2026, lists 156 attributes, each with its former name in the legacy DSP and sponsored ads consoles. PPC Land's field-by-field reading of that reference places Advertiser account ID in the level-of-detail group, marked for all five products the page covers: DVA+, Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display and Sponsored TV. Its former DVA+ name is "Advertiser ID". For sponsored ads no former name is given: legacy sponsored ads reports were pulled per profile, so the account was implied rather than reported.
DVA+ is the AI-assisted campaign type that took over much of what Amazon sold as Amazon DSP until September 29, 2026, when the programmatic product was renamed DVA (display, video and audio). Two neighbouring fields complete the account layer. Advertiser account name is the readable label. Manager accounts records the manager account or accounts through which the advertiser account is reached. A separate Entity ID field ties an advertiser account to a legal or commercial entity.
How the account hierarchy works
Amazon's structure runs in five levels. A manager account sits at the top and holds no campaigns of its own. It links to one or more advertiser accounts. Each advertiser account holds campaigns, formerly called orders in the DSP. Campaigns hold ad groups, formerly line items, and ad groups hold ads.
Manager accounts can be nested. Since April 15, 2024, Amazon has let advertisers and agencies stack them by operating company, region or business unit, a change PPC Land covered in May 2024. Amazon's manager account API accepts two kinds of identifier when linking accounts: an ACCOUNT_ID for sponsored ads accounts and a DSP_ADVERTISER_ID for DSP advertisers, with up to 20 accounts per request, according to the documentation of the open-source python-amazon-ad-api client. That split between two identifier types is the legacy the new advertiser account was built to remove.
Sponsored ads add one more layer underneath: the profile. According to Amazon's profiles reference, as mirrored by the same library, profiles "represent an advertiser and their account's marketplace" and "advertisers cannot have more than one profile for each marketplace". Every sponsored ads API call carries a profile ID in the Amazon-Advertising-API-Scope header, and reports are tied to a single profile. A seller active in three countries therefore had three profile IDs and no single number tying them together.
From many accounts to one
The consolidation came in three steps.
On February 20, 2025, Amazon introduced a global seat for the DSP, letting users register once while still creating "country specific advertiser accounts", invoiced from a single Amazon legal entity, with Singapore becoming the 18th invoice country.
On September 30, 2025, Amazon announced a closed beta for a new kind of advertiser account under the line "One account. Every ads product. Global reach." Before it, according to the announcement, customers had to create "1 Sponsored ads, and 1 Amazon DSP AA per country", AA meaning advertiser account. The beta was open to existing DSP seat holders who had "opted-in for the beta via their sales team", and Amazon published API operations to create, query and update advertiser accounts and to query linked selling accounts. On October 24, 2025, PPC Land reported an open beta for new registrations, quoting Pratham Sarin, a product and engineering leader at Amazon: "One account to access all Amazon Ads products - no more separate registrations." Region is chosen at the advertiser account, in one of three (Americas, EMEA and APAC), and the country at the campaign.
On July 30, 2026, Amazon upgraded existing DSP advertiser accounts to the new model across 34 countries without re-registration. According to Amazon's announcement, "Users will receive a new advertiser account ID that replaces legacy Amazon DSP advertiser account IDs." The same notice says existing identifiers, which it calls CFIDs, "continue to work without modification" in the API, and that each upgraded account "receives a new Global Account ID and additional regional identifiers". Campaigns created without a country code default to the account's original country.
What the ID represents now
For an account created or upgraded under the new model, one advertiser account ID covers sponsored ads and DVA+ across all the countries in its region. For an advertiser still running separate legacy accounts, each account keeps its own ID, and the unified report shows them as separate values. Amazon's guide to the enhanced advertiser account says a self-serve workflow to combine separate sponsored ads and DSP accounts "will be available in a future release". Until it arrives, two IDs for one brand are normal.
Creating the account fixes the business identity attached to the ID. Amazon's Support Center page on creating an advertiser account, updated on November 16, 2026, describes an account that manages "all products and campaigns (sponsored ads and DVA+) from a unified account, across multiple countries", and asks for a business name, phone number and website. The business name is "the company headquarters that is shown on official documents and tax forms", and partners are told to enter "your advertiser's details here", as PPC Land reported on November 17, 2026. Billing can sit with the manager account or the advertiser account, but pay-by-invoice is available only when the advertiser account is billed.
A worked example
A household goods company works through an agency. The agency's manager account links to two advertiser accounts: one created under the new model for the company's main brand, covering Germany, France and Italy, and one legacy sponsored ads account for a smaller brand in Germany only.
A unified report grouped by Advertiser account ID returns two values. The main brand's DVA+ streaming TV campaign and its Sponsored Products campaigns share one ID in all three countries; the legacy account's rows carry the other. Adding Campaign country splits the main account into its three markets. If the second brand later moves into the main account, its new campaigns take the main ID while its history stays with the old one.
Why it matters
Advertiser account ID is the key that external systems use to separate clients. Agencies reconcile invoices by it and data warehouses partition tables by it. Amazon's legacy campaign-level agency fee for DVA ends on January 31, 2027, after which manager-account fees set at account, campaign or ad group level take over, making the account ID part of fee calculation.
The renaming also carries a migration task. DSP dashboards built on "Advertiser ID" must be remapped before legacy reports close: under the timeline Amazon published on November 12, 2026, creation and editing of legacy reports stop on December 17, 2026, and unmigrated reports are deleted on January 31, 2027.
Limitations and disputes
Amazon's own pages disagree on what happened to the old number. The July 30, 2026 announcement says the new ID "replaces" legacy DSP IDs. Amazon's library guide says "your existing Amazon DSP advertiser account ID is now your advertiser account ID" and "remains your identifier". Since the API still accepts legacy identifiers, both may be partly true, but a data team cannot tell whether historical rows will show the old value or the new one.
The beta's status is also contested. Amazon's September 30, 2025 notice describes a closed, opt-in beta, while PPC Land dated an open beta to October 24, 2025 on the strength of Sarin's post. Amazon has published no general availability date for the advertiser account.
Amazon publishes no format for advertiser account IDs, Global Account IDs or regional identifiers, so systems storing them as numbers risk truncation. And Sponsored TV, though marked on the field, sits under Amazon's caveat: "We do not support Streaming TV Smart mode/ Sponsored TV yet."
Advertiser account ID is not...
Advertiser account name. The editable label. Names can repeat across countries and change; the ID should not.
Profile ID. The per-marketplace sponsored ads scope used in API headers. One advertiser account can sit behind several profiles, one per country.
Manager account ID. The identifier of the parent layer an agency or holding company uses to reach many advertiser accounts. One manager can link many advertiser accounts, and one advertiser account can sit under several managers.
Global Account ID and Entity ID. The Global Account ID was added on July 30, 2026 to upgraded accounts alongside regional identifiers. Entity ID ties an advertiser account to a business entity. Neither is the advertiser account ID itself.
Recent developments
The November 11, 2026 dimensions reference marked Advertiser account ID for every ad product, and five days later Amazon's account creation page put the client's legal details at the centre of each new account. DVA+ remains in US closed beta, with general availability in 35 markets planned for the first quarter of 2027, and Amazon has yet to ship the promised tool for combining legacy accounts under one ID.
