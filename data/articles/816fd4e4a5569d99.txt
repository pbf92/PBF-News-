TITLE: Glycosylation and cardiovascular disease: a bibliometric map of 1,441 publications over fifteen years
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Background
Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Glycosylation, one of the most abundant post-translational modifications of proteins, plays an important role in the onset and progression of cardiovascular disease. The field's evolution and hotspot distribution, however, had not been systematically quantified.
Methods
The study applied bibliometric methods to 1,441 publications retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection, published between 1 January 2010 and 31 December 2024.
The analysis covered publication trends, country and institutional distribution, keyword co-occurrence and clustering, and burst detection of cited references, with visualization performed in CiteSpace.
Results
Research activity spanned 71 countries or regions. By publication volume, the United States ranked first (526), followed by China (338) and Germany (122), consistent with a triennial analysis.
Ranked by average citations per publication, the order differed: Canada, France, and the United States led.
A total of 612 institutions participated, with the University of Alabama at Birmingham making the most important contribution.
Publication volume rose slowly from 2010 to 2015 and fluctuated notably between 2016 and 2024. Annual output averaged around 100 papers, with an annual growth rate of 3.62%.
Keyword analysis built a co-occurrence network from 615 keywords. Key hotspots included O-GlcNAcylation, biomarkers, angiogenesis, α-dystroglycan, potassium channel, heart failure, gene expression, glycosylation, and cardiac glycosides.
Burst detection showed "mechanisms" as the strongest term, surging from 2012 and persisting through 2024.
Implications
The authors conclude that research on glycosylation in cardiovascular disease has grown steadily, with O-GlcNAcylation playing a pivotal role. The analysis offers an objective overview to support future investigations.
