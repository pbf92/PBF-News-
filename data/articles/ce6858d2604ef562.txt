GTA 6 Sales Reach an Estimated 47.8 Million Copies in Four Days
GTA 6 sales reached an estimated 47.8 million copies by the end of its launch weekend, making Rockstar Games’ latest release the best-selling video game of 2026 in just four days. Its arrival has also pulled millions of daily active players away from competing titles, including Call of Duty and Fortnite.
New data released on 24 November by market intelligence firm Sensor Tower offers a closer look at how players spent their first weekend with Grand Theft Auto 6. From console installations and hours played to food delivery orders and Twitch viewership, the game’s launch has affected businesses well beyond its immediate competitors.
GTA 6 Sales Breakdown Across Its Launch Weekend
Before GTA 6 even went live on 19 November 2026, Sensor Tower estimated that Rockstar had already secured 24.6 million digital pre-orders. That figure excludes physical pre-orders, which were only accounted for in the firm’s sales estimates from launch day onwards.
By the end of Thursday, combined physical and digital sales had reached an estimated 35.3 million copies. Another 12.5 million copies were sold over the following three days.

Rockstar Games and parent company Take-Two Interactive previously reported US$3.8 billion in first-day sales, describing GTA 6 as the “biggest entertainment launch in history”. The game also debuted with a Metacritic critic score of 99 out of 100, following a 13-year wait since the release of GTA 5 in 2013.
GTA 6 Is Already Installed on 35% of Active PS5 and Xbox Series Consoles
According to Sensor Tower, approximately 35% of active PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S consoles had GTA 6 installed by the end of its opening weekend.
That’s more than one in every three active consoles across the two platform families. For a game that had been available for only four days, the installation figures give some indication of just how quickly Rockstar has reached its potential console audience.
Players have also been putting considerable time into exploring Leonida. By Sunday evening, 37% of GTA 6 players had already spent at least 20 hours in the game, including 7% who had clocked up 40 hours or more.
Across the four-day launch window, reaching that 40-hour threshold would mean spending an average of at least 10 hours per day playing GTA 6.
Call of Duty and Fortnite See Significant Player Count Drops
Call of Duty appears to have taken the hardest hit among the four competing AAA titles tracked in Sensor Tower’s report. Its average daily active users across consoles and Steam fell from approximately 7.3 million to 3.9 million following GTA 6’s release, a decline of 46% compared with its previous 30-day average.
Fortnite experienced a similar, albeit smaller, decline. Its daily active player base dropped from approximately 13.9 million to 10.5 million, representing a 25% reduction.
Interestingly, Call of Duty and Fortnite each saw a reduction of approximately 3.4 million daily active users, despite the considerable difference in their percentage declines.
The drops in Crimson Desert and 007 First Light also suggest that GTA 6 is competing for players’ time across genres, rather than simply drawing audiences away from established multiplayer games.
Whether those players return to their usual games after finishing GTA 6’s campaign remains an open question. The figures measure short-term engagement during the launch period, rather than permanent player losses.
GTA 6 Launch Drives Food Delivery Orders and Record Twitch Engagement
Some of the more unusual findings from Sensor Tower’s report concern what players were doing outside the game, or rather, what they were ordering while playing it.
Among GTA 6 buyers in the United States, DoorDash orders increased by 32% on launch day compared with Thursday, 12 November. Uber Eats recorded a 23% increase over the same period.
Food delivery services and restaurants had already been targeting GTA 6 players through dedicated marketing campaigns ahead of release. The New York Times previously reported that DoorDash orders in Miami increased by 25% on the night of the game’s launch, with Philadelphia, Detroit and Atlanta also recording substantial increases.
Workplaces felt the effects too. According to data shared with Forbes by BambooHR, which represents more than 3.4 million US-based employees, approximately 18% of salaried workers took time off on the Thursday GTA 6 launched.
For those who weren’t playing GTA 6 themselves, watching others explore Rockstar’s new open world proved popular. Sensor Tower estimated that both Twitch mobile app downloads and daily active users increased by more than 80% between 18 and 19 November.
The surge pushed Twitch’s mobile daily active user count to an all-time high, with the launch-day figure climbing to approximately 34 million worldwide.
