TITLE: GTA 6 runs better on Xbox Series X than on PS5
OUTLET: mobidevices.com

Digital Foundry compared four console versions of GTA 6. Xbox Series X dropped frames less often during high-speed driving than the base PS5, although the graphics are nearly identical on the two consoles. PS5 Pro delivered the most stable 30 fps and the sharpest image.
GTA 6 compared on Xbox Series X and PS5
Based on gameplay footage, Digital Foundry determined that both consoles render GTA 6 at 1440p. Rockstar separately confirmed to Wccftech that the game uses its own TAA-based upscaling algorithm to output at 4K. The key graphics settings are the same: ray tracing is used for global illumination and reflections, while shadows are rendered using shadow maps.
Both consoles held 30 fps for long stretches in ordinary scenes. The difference emerged during high-speed driving: in comparable sequences, the PS5 more often dipped below the target frame rate. At the airport, when NPCs scattered after a gunshot, both versions ran at around 25-29 fps. A series of car explosions near the terminal brought the frame rate down to around 20 fps on both consoles.
With a compatible TV, Xbox Series X can output the game with variable refresh rate (VRR) at 120 Hz. This makes minor dips less noticeable. The game itself is designed to run at 30 fps. A Quick Resume bug discovered after release can remove the frame-rate cap, but the game also speeds up as the frame rate rises.
PS5 Pro’s advantages in GTA 6
PS5 Pro also renders the game at 1440p. Upscaling to 4K with PSSR 2 preserves fine details in foliage and textures better. Reflections look sharper, and ray tracing is also used for shadows from the sun and local light sources.
In sequences where the base PS5 dipped to 27-28 fps, PS5 Pro usually held 30. In the most demanding airport scenes, the frame rate fell to around 20 fps on the more powerful console as well. Rockstar explained that 40 fps and 60 FPS modes are not planned for current consoles because of the CPU load involved in simulating the open world.
How GTA 6 runs on Xbox Series S
On Series S, the game renders at 720p and is upscaled to 1080p using FSR 4.1. Rockstar retained ray tracing for lighting and reflections, but reduced the detail of shadows, clouds, and hair. Objects in the distance pop into view more noticeably, especially while driving.
Like the other console versions of Grand Theft Auto VI, the Series S version is designed to run at 30 fps. In testing, it held that frame rate in cutscenes and while moving on foot, but often dropped frames behind the wheel. In the airport stress test, the frame rate fell to around 15 fps. Digital Foundry also noticed brief audio dropouts in its Series S recording.
Will FSR 4.1 and QSSR come to GTA 6?
Rockstar could add FSR 4.1.2 to Xbox Series X. Microsoft and AMD have already provided developers with two versions of the upscaler for the console. In Microsoft’s demo, processing a frame for 4K output took 2.75 ms on the GPU with the Lite model and 3.86 ms with Full. For comparison, at 30 fps, 33.3 ms is available for each frame. On Series S, at 1080p output, the same models took 1.9 and 2.9 ms, respectively.
Rockstar has not explained why it chose TAA on Series X. Digital Foundry estimates that switching to FSR could require lowering the internal resolution or additional optimization to maintain 30 fps. At a lower resolution, reconstruction can sharpen edges, but the quality of ray-traced reflections and global illumination may suffer. On Series S, FSR serves a different purpose: restoring detail when upscaling from a native 720p resolution to 1080p output.
Technically, Rockstar could add QSSR (Quick Spectral Super Resolution) to the base PS5. QSSR shares a software interface with PSSR, which the studio already uses on PS5 Pro. There is also a precedent for adding QSSR after release: Insomniac added it to Marvel’s Spider-Man 2 in an update. However, in Digital Foundry’s test of Marvel’s Wolverine, switching to QSSR for 4K output added around 4.4 ms to frame time compared with Insomniac’s built-in upscaler.
