TITLE: Adding Shexiang Baoxin Pill to Standard Therapy Shows Potential Heart Failure Benefits
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Adding the traditional Chinese patent medicine Shexiang Baoxin Pill to standard sacubitril and valsartan therapy was linked to improvements in cardiac function and ventricular remodeling in heart failure patients, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine. However, investigators emphasized that these findings must be interpreted with caution because the overall certainty of the available evidence remains low to very low across most evaluated outcomes.
Shexiang Baoxin Pill, derived from the historical Suhexiang Pill formulation, has long been administered in clinical management for heart failure due to its proposed cardioprotective actions, which encompass anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidative, and anti-fibrotic biological mechanisms.
To evaluate the comparative efficacy and safety profile of the combined regimen, researchers conducted a comprehensive database search spanning PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, CNKI, Wanfang, VIP, and the Chinese Biomedical Literature Database from their inception through June 19, 2026. This electronic query was supplemented by manual searches of trial registries including the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry and ClinicalTrials.gov. Two independent reviewers handled record screening, eligibility confirmation, data extraction, and methodological quality assessments using the Cochrane risk of bias tool for randomized trials.
The meta-analysis ultimately synthesized data from 15 randomized controlled trials encompassing a total of 1,483 patients diagnosed with heart failure. Statistical evaluations were performed using R software, comparing individuals receiving Shexiang Baoxin Pill alongside sacubitril/valsartan against control groups administered sacubitril/valsartan monotherapy. The certainty of evidence for each measured clinical endpoint was formally evaluated according to the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework.
Patients allocated to the combination therapy demonstrated statistically significant enhancements in primary measures of cardiac performance compared with standard therapy alone. The pooled analysis revealed a mean difference increase in left ventricular ejection fraction of 5.06 percentage points, with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 4.23 to 5.90. Stroke volume increased by a mean difference of 6.72 milliliters, with a 95 percent confidence interval between 2.72 and 10.71, while cardiac output improved by a mean difference of 0.77 liters per minute, with a confidence interval of 0.50 to 1.05, all yielding significance levels below 0.001.
Functional exercise capacity and circulating neurohormonal biomarkers also showed notable changes in the combination treatment cohorts. In physical performance testing, the 6-minute walk distance expanded by an average mean difference of 52.12 meters, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 34.90 to 69.33.
Structural dimensions reflecting adverse left ventricular remodeling were consistently reduced across the included studies following combination therapy. End-diastolic diameter decreased by a mean difference of 5.57 millimeters, with a 95 percent confidence interval of minus 6.53 to minus 4.61, and end-systolic diameter contracted by a mean difference of 5.42 millimeters, within an interval of minus 6.63 to minus 4.20. End-diastolic volume also diminished significantly, showing a mean difference reduction of 14.73 milliliters, with a confidence interval of minus 25.67 to minus 3.80.
Evaluation of safety outcomes demonstrated no significant statistical difference in the overall incidence of adverse events between patients receiving the combined regimen and those on standard sacubitril/valsartan monotherapy.
Despite these favorable physiological and functional observations, the authors stressed that the certainty of the evidence was graded as low to very low across the majority of analyzed endpoints.
