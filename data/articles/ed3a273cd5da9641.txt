TITLE: This entrepreneur from small-town Maharashtra outearned Meta on BJP’s election ad spend
OUTLET: Scroll.in

A small firm based in an outer suburb of Pune earned nearly double in advertising revenues from the Bharatiya Janata Party in recently-held assembly elections than Meta, the Silicon Valley behemoth which owns Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp.
The firm, CNX Media, registered with tax authorities in May 2025. Over the next year, it earned Rs 28.5 crore from the BJP for “media advertisements” during the assembly elections of Bihar, West Bengal, Kerala and Assam, according to expenditure reports filed by the party with the Election Commission of India. In contrast, the party paid Meta just Rs 15.2 crore.
CNX Media is owned by Sarjerao Dhage, a 32-year-old political consultant from Umri in Maharashtra’s Nanded district.
Initially, media reports linked CNX Media to CNX Media Private Limited, a Delhi-based firm led by pollster Bhawesh Jha. But in emails to Scroll, Jha denied the link. Scroll found that the Jha-owned firm had been struck off the company registrar’s list in 2017. Further, tax records showed that Jha had stopped using the tradename “CNX Media” in 2021.
Scroll then found a partnership firm registered with the same name on the government’s Goods and Services Tax website. This led us to Sarjerao Dhage, who confirmed that his firm had executed digital campaigns for the BJP.
“No other company is able to execute the work we do,” he said. “We don’t make much noise. You wouldn’t have seen any publicity about what we have done.”
But Dhage is not just a political consultant. Since February, on his social media accounts, he has presented himself as a Hindutva youth leader. In June, he organised a grand celebration in Umri to mark the birth anniversary of Shambhaji, the 17th-century Maratha king. One of its attendees was Ravindra Chavan, the Congress MP from Nanded.
Sarjerao Dhage’s cousin, Rajesh Patil Dhage, was part of the Congress party’s social media team in Nanded till 2025.
“I am not for BJP and I’m not against BJP,” Sarjerao Dhage told Scroll. “I am only for Hindutva.”
‘We don't make much noise’
In 2026, the Bharatiya Janata Party told the Election Commission that it spent Rs 530 crore on the election campaigns in Bihar, held in November 2025, and West Bengal, Kerala and Assam, held in April and May 2026.
A third of this expenditure was on media advertisements – Rs 174 crore.
About 16% of this media spend, or Rs 28.5 crore, was cornered by a partnership firm registered in Talegaon in Maharashtra’s Pune district – CNX Media.
It is second to only Google India Private Limited, which got 26% of the spend.
According to the government’s Goods and Services Tax website, the firm was registered with tax authorities on May 26, 2025. Its services include advertising, sale of internet advertising space and advertising on commission.
The tax management website Jamku lists Sarjerao Dhage and Ganesh Digabhar Dhage as its partners.
Sarjerao Dhage told Scroll that his strength was influencer marketing. Specifically, he worked on campaigns that involved taking a political party’s manifesto to a targeted audience. “We take the narratives, briefs and creative material that clients give us, and get people to work on them,” he said. “Our unique selling point is that in the digital space, we can take anything that is based on a visual concept and execute it.”
The Election Commission’s expenditure disclosures require parties to disclose ad spending by the central headquarters and the state unit separately. The BJP’s disclosures show that CNX Media was paid by the central headquarters during the assembly elections.
Dhage did not go into details of his dealings with the BJP, citing non-disclosure agreements with his clients, but said that his clientele included parties other than the BJP.
A political consultant who spoke to Scroll on the condition of anonymity, citing business interests, said that before Dhage got involved with political clients, he had worked with brands like the social media platform Snapchat. Today, even though he seemed to have a significant foothold in the BJP’s digital campaigns based on ECI disclosures, these figures hardly reflect the actual extent of the expenditure, the consultant added.
“He [Dhage] has been in this space for the last three or four years,” said the consultant. “If a party spends Rs 100 on digital campaigns during elections, Dhage’s market share is typically between two to three rupees.”
Dhage describes himself as a well-connected “freelancer” in the digital political campaign industry. He said clients would approach him with what they wanted done and he in turn engaged other consultants and firms to deliver on it. But when late payments from clients made the other consultants jittery, he decided to go solo.
Dhage, the Hindutva activist
Dhage is originally from Umri, a subdivision in Maharashtra’s Nanded district. A local government official told Scroll on the condition of anonymity that he comes from a family of wealthy landowners.
The region was a Congress stronghold for decades until one of its tallest leaders, former chief minister Ashok Chavan, switched to the BJP in February 2024.
Even though the Congress won the Nanded parliamentary constituency in the 2024 general elections and retained it in a bye-election months later, the BJP and its ally, Shiv Sena, swept its six assembly seats during the assembly polls held later that year. The Hindutva party also won a majority of seats in the municipal elections in Nanded held in January 2026.
Around the same time, Dhage, who had moved to Pune for work, returned to his hometown to become a political activist, the local government official said.
The CNX Media founder’s new life is thoroughly documented on Instagram, where his activism is advertised through high-quality reels shared by a network of local content creators who address him as “Hindu dharm rakshak” or “Hindu sevak” – the protector and servant of the Hindu faith.
This shift from behind-the-scenes political consultancy to front-and-centre politics is part of a larger Indian trend in recent years. Prashant Kishor, who founded the political consultancy firm IPAC, started his own political party and is now a legislator in Bihar.
Sandeep Pathak, a data strategist for the Aam Aadmi Party, was nominated to the Rajya Sabha and is now in the BJP. Sunil Kanugolu worked for the Congress and is now an advisor to Karnataka chief minister DK Shivakumar with a cabinet-level rank.
The actor Vijay-led Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam, or TVK, has appointed two former political consultants – Aadhav Arjuna and S Keerthana – as cabinet ministers in Tamil Nadu.
Compared to them, Dhage has a long way to go. A glance at his Instagram shows that his Hindutva avatar was only launched in February this year.
During the Hindu festival of Navratri in October, Dhage’s team distributed pamphlets in Umri that gave an insight into his politics. “Let us protect Hindu society from love jihad and completely eradicate this perversion from Hindu society,” said the first agenda. It also mentioned the creation of a “Legal Hindu Defence Force” to “teach a lesson to the wolves who make insulting statements or engage in insulting acts against Hindu gods and goddesses”.
Dhage told Scroll that his political ambitions were national, not regional or state-level. But there already was a national force taking up his anti-Muslim and Hindu-first agenda – the BJP. “I am taking business risks and doing my own work,” said Dhage. “I don't care whether the BJP remains my client tomorrow or not… I am only for Hindutva in the true sense.”
