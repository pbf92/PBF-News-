TITLE: Four in ten chronic pancreatitis patients develop exocrine pancreatic insufficiency
OUTLET: News-Medical

Chronic pancreatitis (CP) is a progressive fibro-inflammatory disorder in which persistent inflammation causes acinar cell loss, ductal distortion, and interstitial fibrosis, culminating in impaired synthesis and secretion of digestive enzymes, a condition known as exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI). EPI manifests through steatorrhea, weight loss, malnutrition, and micronutrient deficiencies. Previous studies have reported EPI incidence in CP ranging from 14% to 66%.
This review, published in Volume 9, Issue 3 of the Journal of Pancreatology in September 2026, was registered in PROSPERO (CRD42024618687) and followed PRISMA guidelines. Searches covered PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science for English articles published from November 27, 2004, to November 27, 2024. Fourteen studies with 7,663 patients were synthesized with STATA 18.0 using random-effects meta-analysis, subgroup analyses by geography and diagnostic method, sensitivity analysis, and publication bias assessment.
The pooled EPI prevalence was 40.0% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 33.5%–46.7%), with substantial heterogeneity (I² = 96.713%, P < 0.001). Diagnostic method significantly affected estimates: the fecal elastase-1 (FE-1) test yielded the highest rate (52.1%, 95% CI: 44.2%–59.9%), exceeding the steatorrhea or fecal fat test (21.1%) and the 13C-mixed triglyceride breath test (31.6%). Geographic differences were not statistically significant (Asia 31.3%, Europe 44.5%, and North America 39.8%). Smoking emerged as a risk factor (odds ratio = 1.51, 95% CI: 1.25–1.76) based on four studies. Other reported risk factors included low body mass index, male sex, and diabetes.
The authors note that the higher FE-1 prevalence may reflect its sensitivity to mild-to-moderate dysfunction, while a key limitation is the dilution effect in watery diarrhea that can cause false-positive results; fecal fat tests are specific for overt malabsorption. Chinese cohorts showed a lower prevalence (21.1%), which the authors attribute to diagnostic methodology. Substantial heterogeneity persisted across subgroup analyses, and disease duration data were incomplete. They conclude that EPI prevalence in CP is globally high, that smoking is a risk factor, and that diagnostic variability calls for standardized screening.
