TITLE: Man Utd Player Ratings vs. Liverpool: Pochettino Era Starts With Embarrassing Defeat
OUTLET: Sports Illustrated

Playing with 10 men for almost the entirety of the match, the Mauricio Pochettino era at Manchester United started with a humiliating 4–0 defeat against a far superior Liverpool on Sunday evening.
Any hope of Pochettino enjoying a honeymoon phase upon arrival to United emphatically evaporated within the opening 13 minutes of his first match at the helm.
Leny Yoro was sent off eight minutes in after denying Alexander Isak from going through on goal. Dominik Szoboszlai added insult to injury when he buried the ensuing free kick, and three minutes later Florian Wirtz scored a beauty of a volley to double Liverpool’s lead inside the opening 15 minutes.
The visitors went into complete survival mode the rest of the match, but Liverpool were far superior and could’ve easily further humiliated Pochettino’s men had they been more clinical. Still, after plenty of wasted chances, the Reds finally buried the dagger when Bradley Barcola squared to Rio Ngumoha for a tap-in in the 66th minute.
United’s nightmare was completed in the 82nd minute when another careless giveaway in the build-up allowed Wirtz to slide a nice ball for a charging Víctor Muñoz, who tucked in Liverpool’s fourth with an emphatic finish.
It was yet another dreadful performance from United, who have now failed to win any of their last nine Premier League games and are now 19th in the standings. Pochettino faces the daunting task of fixing a broken team that continues to perform like a side bound to get relegated.
The Moment That Defined the Match
Expectations were sky-high ahead of the opening act of the Pochettino era at United. But the Argentine’s first match at the helm was essentially over inside the opening 10 minutes of his tenure.
United have no one to blame but themselves for their Anfield demise. Matthijs de Ligt inexplicably opted against clearing a routine lobbed pass from Ronald Araújo and instead simply opened his arms as he let Isak run by him to collect the ball and go through on goal.
Isak almost made a mess of his golden opportunity when he took a heavy touch, but Yoro arrived at the scene—unfortunately for United—and ran over the charging forward from behind just as he was about to enter the penalty area. It wasn’t the most violent of challenges, but Yoro left match referee Sam Barrott no choice but to send him off just eight minutes into the match.
If being condemned to play with 10 men for the rest of the game wasn’t enough punishment, Szoboszlai compounded United’s misery and converted the resulting free kick into the first goal of the match. Pochettino's debut was completely spoiled in a matter of seconds.
Liverpool smelled blood in the water and doubled their advantage just three minutes later, virtually clinching the victory against their historic rivals in less than 15 minutes.
All the excitement the dawn of the Pochettino era produced disappeared almost instantly as De Ligt and Yoro made a mess of a routine action. Undermanned, down two goals and in one of the most hostile grounds in Europe, Pochettino’s focus shifted from trying to upset the Reds to trying to avoid an embarrassing, lopsided defeat in his first match at the helm—and United couldn’t even do that.

What the Ratings Tell Us
- After spending almost a year on the sidelines, its clear that Matthijs de Ligt simply isn’t at the level to start for United. The Dutchman struggled all game and was absolutely to blame for Yoro’s red card. De Ligt’s confidence looks absolutely shot and is making countless routine mistakes as a result. Pochettino surely regrets including him in his first United XI.
- De Ligt’s mistakes were the most egregious, but Lisandro Martínez and Diogo Dalot were just as poor. The pair of defenders were completely outclassed by Liverpool’s forwards and there was a clear lack of intensity from both. Two veterans expected to help the Red Devils overcome adversity instead further contributed to their capitulation.
- Bruno Fernandes was one of the lone bright spots of an otherwise disastrous effort. The skipper created five chances, and his contributions out of possession, despite the lopsided scoreline, surely will be valued by Pochettino.
The Numbers That Explain Man Utd’s Humiliating Defeat
- The Red Devils allowed Liverpool to register 28 total shots, nine big chances and an xG of 4.38—perfectly encapsulating United’s dreadful defensive performance. Meanwhile, United had an xG of 0.63 and didn’t craft a single big chance.
- Pochettino’s men could only muster nine touches inside Liverpool’s penalty area, meanwhile the hosts had 60 inside United’s box. That sums up how uneven the game was.
- United’s lone shot on goal from inside the penalty area came in deep into stoppage time.
