Blame game emerges after Anthropic's IPO market value plunge - WSJ
Anthropic ANT and its advisers faced intense volatility during the company’s initial public offering, resulting in a sharp decline in its market value and sparking a wave of finger-pointing across Wall Street. Despite careful planning, the IPO encountered unexpected challenges, with Anthropic losing approximately $109B of its market value on its second day of trading.
The stock dropped as much as 15% on Friday—briefly trading just 5 cents above its IPO price—before closing down 8.5% at $88.91. Even so, shares remain 7% above the IPO price. The company’s turbulent debut has led some investors and observers to blame angry fund managers excluded from the offer, shortcomings by underwriters, and the overall climate for artificial intelligence stocks for the damage.
The allocation of IPO shares has drawn scrutiny. People familiar with the matter told The Wall Street Journal more than half of the $81 billion in IPO shares went to existing investors such as Altimeter Capital, Lightspeed Venture Partners, and Menlo Ventures, reflecting an effort by Anthropic to reward early backers. Venture capital firms, which were not permitted to sell their pre-existing holdings, typically are not granted substantial IPO share allotments that could be sold for immediate gains. Additionally, major investment firms like Citadel received significant allocations, while around 60% of firms who sought IPO shares reportedly received nothing.
Other concerns have centered on the decision-making behind when and how shares were made available for public trading. Questions have been raised about whether the opening price was set too high, potentially creating selling pressure that was difficult to stem. Anthropic also allowed some current and former employees to immediately sell about 300 million shares—worth approximately $26 billion at the IPO price—a break from usual lockups that may have further contributed to the downward momentum, the Journal reported.
Despite these challenges, those close to Anthropic and its lead underwriters emphasized to the Journal the success of the offering, highlighting the more than $100B raised and the stock’s resilience above the IPO price even amid broad market selloffs. However, stabilizing mechanisms such as the green-shoe option, which was exercised immediately, have now been exhausted.
