Colombia Ranks Top for Sexual Satisfaction in Survey
An Ipsos survey of 23,765 people in 30 countries found Colombians the most satisfied with their sex and relationship lives.
Updated on Nov. 28, 2026 in Sexual Health

An Ipsos survey of 23,765 people across 30 countries, conducted between December 2024 and January 2025, ranked Colombia first, with 74 percent of respondents saying they are satisfied with their sex and relationship life. The 30-country average was 59 percent.
Why it matters
The ranking offers a snapshot of how people in different countries rate their intimate lives, which can inform readers about shifting patterns in relationships and sexual wellbeing worldwide. Separate research from Britain adds context on how sexual frequency has changed over time.
The figures come from a self-reported Ipsos survey of 23,765 people in 30 countries, with Colombia at 74 percent against a 59 percent average. The survey does not explain the reasons behind the country differences.
The players
Ipsos
Ipsos is a global market research and polling company headquartered in Paris that conducts surveys across dozens of countries.
The Lancet
The Lancet is a long-established, peer-reviewed general medical journal published in the United Kingdom.
The details
Ipsos asked respondents how satisfied they are with their sex and relationship life across 30 countries. Separately, a Lancet study of British adults found that those under 45 now have sex about twice a month, down from roughly four times a month in 1990-1991.
Timeline
- December 2024 - January 2025: Ipsos conducts its survey of 23,765 people in 30 countries.
- 1990-1991: Adults under 45 in Britain reportedly have sex about four times a month.
- 1990-2010: The share of 16-to-24-year-olds in Britain who never had sex is about one in five, according to the Lancet study period.
Big Picture
The Lancet's British sexual-behaviour surveys, which compare 1990-1991 with later periods, show sexual frequency among under-45s falling from about four times to about twice a month. The new Ipsos ranking adds a cross-national measure of satisfaction that those British trend lines do not capture.
The survey results suggest that satisfaction with intimate life is not tied only to how often people have sex, since countries with lower average frequency can still report high satisfaction. Readers can use these findings as a reference point when considering how their own relationship expectations compare with broader patterns.
The takeaway
Satisfaction with intimate life appears to depend on more than frequency alone, so individual wellbeing may be better judged by personal fulfilment than by comparison with national averages.
