TITLE: Study of 20 African megacities spotlights coastal land sinkage as an underestimated flood risk
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

An assessment of 20 coastal megacities across Africa reveals the dire impact of local land subsidence on flood risk. The research spotlights the underestimated influence of this phenomenon, called vertical land motion (VLM), on flood risk assessment models. “Beyond Africa, the framework demonstrated here constitutes a scalable, transferable methodology for first-order risk assessment in other data-scarce coastal regions across the Global South,” Oluwaseyi Dasho and colleagues write. VLM contributes to coastal flood risk alongside sea-level rise. Yet, in Africa, this phenomenon had not been thoroughly mapped across different regions. Now, Dasho et al. have developed a high-resolution database recording VLM across 20 African coastal megacities, which are home to roughly 90 million people in total. They used Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar (InSAR) data to quantify ongoing land subsidence specific to each city. For example, the median subsidence rate in Alexandria was 6.0 millimeters per year, while in Lagos it was 5.0 millimeters per year. Both rates sizably exceed current estimates, which don’t fully account for VLM. The authors then incorporated cities’ respective VLM rates into an Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change climate model to investigate how this sinkage changed sea-level projections. The adjusted simulations showed that, under an intermediate emissions (SSP2-4.5) pathway, relative sea level would rise by 35.8% and flood-exposed areas would expand by 15% by 2050 in Alexandria. These numbers varied based on city. Overall, though, throughout the 20 cities, an extreme sea-level event could cost 180 billion US dollars and imperil more than 7 million people and 1 million buildings – spotlighting what the authors deem “an urgent imperative for action.”
