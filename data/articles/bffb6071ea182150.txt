TITLE: Microsoft says its bidding upgrade started in summer, not November 18
OUTLET: PPC Land

Microsoft Advertising's bidding upgrade, presented on November 18 as the biggest upgrade to its bidding models in a decade, has no single go-live date and has been rolling out since the summer, according to a LinkedIn post published today by Navah Hopkins, Microsoft's Ads Liaison.
Hopkins wrote that "a few questions" had been "circling around the Microsoft Advertising bidding updates," along with "some conflation of improvements," and that the post was meant to make sure "everyone is clear about what is happening and how to measure results in your accounts." It covers what the blog's three performance figures measure, when the changes reached accounts and how the five-conversion threshold is counted, and it states that Maximum CPC, which the blog called "not recommended," can still be used through portfolio bidding.
In Short
Microsoft's Ads Liaison said today that the bidding upgrade Microsoft described on November 18 has no single start date and has been rolling out since the summer, so that date does not mark a switch in how accounts bid. This matters to anyone comparing results before and after November 18, or logging the date as an algorithm change, and to advertisers deciding when to leave manual or Enhanced CPC bidding. The post also fills gaps in the blog: five conversions count at the account level, accounts with no conversion data have four starting strategies rather than one, and Max CPC is still available through portfolio bidding.
No single go-live date
The November 18 blog post, written by Carlo Buchmann, Principal Product Manager, said "now we're rolling out the biggest upgrade to bidding models in a decade" and called the release "the first stage of our updates." PPC Land's report that day described the rollout as beginning on November 18, a reading the blog's present-tense wording supported.
Today's post sets out a different timeline. "Several folks have asked if last week's announcement should be marked as a major algo switch in the books," Hopkins wrote. "The truth is we're always working hard to make bidding work more efficiently for you." Then the central sentence: "There isn't a single 'go live' date for any one of these updates, and they've been rolling out since this Summer."
That fits Microsoft's own footnotes. The blog's 18% figure rests on data from June and July 2026, the 10% figure on August and the 8% figure on August and September, all before the blog appeared. The blog did not say whether the upgraded models were in place for those campaigns; Hopkins now writes that "the results shared reflect improvements rolled out." Which improvement reached which accounts, and when, is not stated.
Hopkins offered another reference point: "it can be really useful to earmark the date you begin testing in earnest Microsoft conversion-based bidding." By Microsoft's account, then, November 18 is not a date on which account behaviour changed, and a before-and-after comparison built around it would not isolate changes that had been arriving for months.
Those months were crowded. AI Max began rolling out to all Microsoft Advertising accounts on August 19, 2026. Max CPC left new campaigns on standalone automated strategies on October 1. The Conversions API became generally available on October 28. All of them reached accounts while, according to Hopkins, the bidding improvements were rolling out, and Microsoft has published no schedule that separates them.
What changed between the blog and the post
The blog and today's post agree on direction but differ on several details. Side by side:
Three figures, three parts of the update
"The proof points are specific to different parts of the bidding updates," Hopkins wrote. The post repeats the blog's three figures word for word and adds one clarification: "To be clear on eCPC, the proof point is about upgrading from eCPC to Maximize Conversions with a tCPA."
The clarification ties the 18% to one move only, from Enhanced CPC to Maximize Conversions with a target CPA, and not to Enhanced CPC itself or to any other switch away from it.
Hopkins also explained what the figures are for. "The proof points are there to help you feel confident testing bidding strategies you might not have felt comfortable testing before," the post says. That frames the numbers as a reason to test rather than as a forecast for any given account. Neither the blog nor the post gives sample sizes or a definition of "similar," whereas in September Microsoft tied its 8% AI Max figure to 44 advertiser-run A/B tests.
Five conversions, counted at the account level
"Conversion-based bidding unlocks at 5 conversions at the account level, and the more conversions you can bring, the better!" Hopkins wrote. The blog had already tied the threshold to the account, and an exchange under today's post made the point concrete. Nirar Punj, Marketing Manager at Auxe, wrote: "I've seen accounts sit on Max CPC for months because they thought they didn't have enough data yet. Does that 5 count sit at the account level even if a campaign's only got one or two itself?" Hopkins replied: "yes - so long as there are at least 5 conversions at the account level, you can begin testing conversion-based bidding." The post adds that past that point "the need to work harder with manual bidding and bid adjustments isn't there anymore."
Below the threshold, the post lists more options than the blog. The blog said new accounts with fewer than five conversions "can start with Enhanced CPC to gather conversion history." Hopkins' post addresses advertisers "brand new to Microsoft Advertising (i.e. your conversion tracking isn't set up yet and/or there's no conversion data)" and names four strategies: "eCPC, Max Clicks, Target Impression Share, or a Portfolio bidding strategy to help build conversion data." The groups differ slightly: the blog's takes in any new account with fewer than five conversions, Hopkins' only accounts with no tracking or no data at all.
The path to conversion-based bidding, as Microsoft describes it
According to the November 18 blog and today's post, the sequence from a new account to conversion-based bidding has five stages.
- Tracking. The blog calls conversion tracking "a prerequisite for automated bidding" and names Universal Event Tracking (UET), the Conversions API and offline import, recommending UET.
- Before five conversions. eCPC, Max Clicks, Target Impression Share or a portfolio strategy (Hopkins, for accounts with no data); Enhanced CPC (blog, for new accounts below five conversions).
- At five account-level conversions. New campaigns start on Maximize Conversions with a target CPA or Maximize Conversion Value with a target ROAS, even when the campaign itself has one or two conversions.
- Changing targets. The blog describes target changes "in 15% increments" with a review "after one conversion cycle," which it defines as "the time period it typically takes for your business to move a click to conversion."
- Judging results. The post gives "at least 30 conversions in 30 days or have gone through 2-3 conversion cycles." The blog gives "ideally 30 conversions in 30 days" and "1-2 conversion cycles after major changes are made," with evaluation "at the campaign or portfolio level (not at the ad group or keyword level)."
The last stage is where the documents diverge. Hopkins joins the two conditions with "or," so either would be enough; the blog lists both and ties its cycle count to major changes. For a 14-day conversion cycle, two to three cycles is 28 to 42 days and one to two is 14 to 28 days, a PPC Land calculation. Neither document explains the difference.
Max CPC, still available through portfolios
"The choice remains for all advertisers, and while we don't recommend using Max CPC with conversion-based bidding since it overrides tCPA and tROAS goals, you can still access it through portfolio bidding," Hopkins wrote.
The blog's wording was broader: "Keeping a Maximum CPC set on your campaigns is not recommended," because caps "override advertisers' own CPA/ROAS targets," even "when the cap is above average CPC." Today's post narrows that to conversion-based bidding and names the route that remains open.
That route matches the structure Microsoft set in August, when it said Max CPC would leave new campaigns on October 1. The setting was removed from new campaigns on standalone automated strategies, while portfolio strategies, Enhanced CPC and Target impression share kept it. A portfolio bid strategy applies one automated strategy and one target across several campaigns, so a per-click ceiling alongside a target remains possible at that level.
Why this matters
Bidding changes without a date are harder to account for. When an agency explains a shift in cost per acquisition, a dated platform change is one of the first things it checks. Google supplied one this year: on August 17 it began pushing overperforming CPAs up across five campaign types, a change it had announced weeks in advance. Microsoft now describes continuous change since the summer, with no single date on which anything was switched on.
That puts more weight on account-level reporting. Microsoft's November 9 product newsletter, which PPC Land covered when Microsoft Advertising overhauled its interface after advertiser complaints, introduced Bid Strategy Reports with average target CPA and ROAS, target-versus-actual comparisons and target trend lines. Without a rollout date, those reports and the date an advertiser changes strategy are the remaining anchors.
The direction has held since July 2025, when Microsoft folded Target CPA and Target ROAS into Maximize Conversions and Maximize Conversion Value. Today's post keeps manual routes open while making clear which way Microsoft points.
What the post does not say
The post gives no dates for individual improvements, does not say which markets or accounts have received them, and does not say how an advertiser can tell whether an account has. It does not explain how the summer rollout relates to the blog's "first stage," why its evaluation window of two to three conversion cycles differs from the blog's one to two, or what Microsoft plans for 2027.
