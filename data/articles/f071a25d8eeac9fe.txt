TITLE: Gallup: Support for Childhood Vaccines Rebounds
OUTLET: Yahoo

The percentage of Americans who say it is extremely important for parents to vaccinate their children has increased by 7 percentage points since 2024, according to a Gallup poll released Tuesday.
The survey found that 47% of Americans consider childhood vaccinations extremely important, while 25% say they are very important.
The combined 72% is up from 69% in 2024 but remains well below the record high of 94% recorded in 2001.
The increase comes as Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and his Make America Healthy Again movement continue to scrutinize vaccine safety, while the Trump administration pushes to overhaul federal childhood vaccination guidelines.
The survey also revealed a wide partisan divide, with 91% of Democrats and Democrat-leaning independents saying childhood vaccinations are extremely or very important, compared with 50% of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents.
Overall, 78% of Americans disagree with the statement that vaccines are more dangerous than the diseases they are designed to prevent, while 17% agree.
Republicans are more likely to view vaccines as posing a greater risk than the diseases they prevent, with 32% expressing that view, compared with 5% of Democrats.
Meanwhile, 46% of Americans say vaccines do not cause autism, up from 36% in 2024, while 16% believe they do, and 38% are unsure. Extensive scientific research has found no causal link between vaccines and autism.
Support for government-mandated childhood vaccinations also increased, with 56% of Americans favoring such requirements, up from 51% in 2024, while 40% oppose government involvement in the decision.
The survey was conducted before the Nov. 17 announcement that the United States had lost its measles elimination status, a designation it had held since 2000.
The Gallup poll was conducted Oct. 1-19 among 1,200 U.S. adults and has a margin of sampling error of plus or minus 4 percentage points.
