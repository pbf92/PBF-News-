TITLE: The staggering surge in physical video game sales following GTA 6 launch

New data has revealed physical video game sales skyrocketed by 409 per cent following the release of Grand Theft Auto 6, even though that format of the game doesn’t even come with a disc.
Boxed software revenues, covering discs and “code-in-a-box” (the format Rockstar chose for GTA 6), hit an estimated £43 million during launch week, according to figures compiled by NielsenIQ (NIQ). It was the strongest week for physical games in the UK since Christmas 2018, when FIFA 19 was released.
GTA 6 alone accounted for £32 million of the physical sales during its launch week, NIQ said.
The firm added that its figures were estimates for the wider retail market based on shopping data.
The game was released on 19 November, ending a 13-year wait among fans worldwide for the next instalment of Rockstar's video game series.
The standard version carried a price tag of around £69.99, while the more premium version cost around £89.99.
Buyers who want a physical copy get a box containing a code to download the game, rather than a disc.
Digital versions, by contrast, can be bought and downloaded onto gaming consoles straight away.
Dorian Bloch, senior client insight director at NIQ, said the sales performance for physical copies was “particularly impressive” against a market that is increasingly geared towards digital downloads.
“There have only been 16 games released since 1995 that generated higher boxed software revenue in their opening week,” he said.
“This demonstrates the enduring appeal and retail power of one of gaming’s biggest franchises.”
The release also sparked a wider resurgence in demand for gaming consoles, NIQ's data showed.
PlayStation 5 sales rose 46 per cent during launch week compared with the previous week.
Xbox Series console sales jumped by 75 per cent week-on-week.
Demand for other gaming accessories like headsets, joypads and chairs also soared, according to the figures.
Joost van Dreunen, an Adjunct Assistant Professor at NYU Stern School of Business who teaches the business of video games, said physical sales would be just the beginning for a game like GTA 6.
“GTA V made $1bn in three days in 2013, which turned out to be the small part. GTA Online kept millions of people paying every month for thirteen years and became the real business. So we’d do well to judge GTA 6 accordingly: the launch is act one, the online world that follows is act two, and act two is where the money is.”
Although there is no official word on a release date for a “GTA 6 Online”, when it does arrive, there will be years of revenue waiting for Rockstar to cash in on.
