TITLE: Police 'working to establish whether crime committed' after Co-op Live evacuated
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

Police 'working to establish whether crime committed' after Co-op Live evacuated
The force said 89 people 'displayed symptoms believed to be linked to the exposure' after a substance was released into the air
Police are working to establish whether any crime was committed after Co-op Live was evacuated when a mystery substance was apparently dispersed into the air amid the crowd during a gig by US rapper Don Toliver.
Greater Manchester Police, in an update on Monday evening, said more than 12,000 people were inside the venue as an evacuation was ordered.
The force said a total of 89 people 'displayed symptoms believed to be linked to the exposure'. One woman was taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary for further assessment and treatment, GMP added. There's been no update on her condition.

In a new statement, GMP said its major incident status - initiated as the incident unfolded - was lifted at 4am on Monday.
The substance believed to have been dispersed hasn't been confirmed. The force said enquiries remained ongoing 'to establish the nature of the substance, where it came from and any associated risk'.
Police said 'further testing' would take place on items found at the venue in the aftermath on Monday, but refused to say what those items are.
GMP said: "The remaining 88 people underwent dry decontamination at the scene and were subsequently accommodated within the Etihad Stadium while the incident was managed."
The force described the incident as 'isolated' - and said there was no wider threat to the public.
Assistant Chief Constable Tom Harding said: "Enquiries remain ongoing to establish the nature of the substance, where it came from and any associated risk. Testing will take place throughout the day as the investigation into the cause of this incident progresses. We are trying to establish whether any criminality has occurred in relation to this incident.
"Anyone at last night's event who has since noticed side-effects or become ill should attend their local hospital, contact their GP or call 101.
"I would like to thank the public who attended for their patience whilst we responded to this incident. There were over 12,000 people in attendance during this concert who were safely escorted from the arena and made their way home.
"Our coordinated response meant colleagues from the emergency services and partner agencies could tackle this incident swiftly and safely."
Mobile phone video footage showed concertgoers struggling to breathe and coughing as they walked out of the venue.
Don Toliver's show at the O2 in London on Monday, meanwhile, has been rescheduled.
A Co-op Live spokesman said: "Following an incident in the crowd at last night's show, we are working closely with Greater Manchester Police to identify the cause and action appropriately. Our priority remains the safety and security of all visitors to Co-op Live, and we will provide more information once we have an update from GMP."
