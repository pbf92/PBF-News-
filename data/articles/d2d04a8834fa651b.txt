TITLE: Christians branded national security threats in Turkey take case to European court
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Christian missionaries have taken another step in a legal challenge against the Turkish government's ban on some believers re-entering the country.
Christian legal advocacy group, Alliance Defending Freedom (ADF), has said more than 200 Christians and their families have been “unjustly” banned from Turkey after being labelled "national security threats".
The cases concern long-time legal residents of Turkey, who ADF alleges were expelled solely for their Christian faith and evangelistic efforts.
The charity has now filed a reply before the European Court of Human Rights on behalf of multiple Christian missionaries and is representing three of the cases.
The filing submitted on 18 November urged the Court to acknowledge the violations of religious freedom and order the security codes under which the missionaries were banned to be lifted.
ADF said that since 2020, around 350 people (the Christian workers and their families) have reportedly been expelled from Turkey, many after living there for decades.
According to the organisation, the bans have left families separated from communities they helped build, but also left local churches without stable leadership, weakening the already-small Christian presence in the country.
One Christian missionary couple, Pam and Dave Wilson, had lived in Turkey for almost 40 years when they were abruptly prohibited from re-entering.
Pam said: “Dave and I made Turkey our home for decades, and we came to love not just the country but its people. Then in February 2019, we landed in Istanbul and were turned away at the airport, branded a threat to national security. We have never been told what we are supposed to have done. The only thing the authorities have ever pointed to is the sharing of our Christian faith”.
She added that they are “grateful that the European Court of Human Rights is examining our case, and we pray it leads the Turkish authorities to respect the religious freedom of all who call Turkey home, as we once did”.
Previous statements from the Turkish government alleged that the missionaries’ religious affiliation had nothing to do with the security codes assigned to them. However, ADF disputes this.
Senior Counsel Kelsey Zorzi said: “There are several hundred documented cases of Christians banned from Turkey because of their faith. While the government claims that there is no religious bias involved in these decisions, the pattern clearly shows otherwise.
“Labelling these Christians as terrorist threats constitutes egregious discrimination on the basis of their faith and a total violation of their human rights. It has tragically caused these people to lose their homes, debilitated their church communities, and undermined their ability to share the gospel. The Turkish government must acknowledge that these lawful residents have been severely discriminated against for being Christian and rescind the bans in the interest of religious freedom.”
