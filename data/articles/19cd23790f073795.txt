TITLE: Continuous Sutures May Increase Complications in Paediatric Hypospadias Repair
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials indicates that using continuous subcuticular sutures during primary tubularized incised plate urethroplasty for paediatric hypospadias is linked to a higher risk of overall postoperative complications compared with interrupted sutures. The investigation, conducted by researchers at Guangzhou Medical University and reported in Frontiers in Pediatrics, evaluated pooled clinical outcomes to assess the comparative efficacy and safety of both suturing techniques in reconstructive surgery for male children undergoing primary repair.
To directly evaluate how continuous closure compares against interrupted closure, the research team conducted comprehensive literature searches across PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, the Cochrane Library, and ClinicalTrials.gov from inception through 23 July 2026. The systematic search identified six randomized controlled trials meeting the inclusion criteria, encompassing a combined sample size of 589 paediatric patients.
Study selection, data extraction, and quality assessments were performed independently by two reviewers, who appraised the risk of bias across the included trials using the Cochrane ROB2 tool. The primary and secondary clinical outcomes targeted for comparative evaluation included overall postoperative complications, urethrocutaneous fistula formation, glans dehiscence, meatal or urethral stricture, and superficial wound infection. Pooled relative risks were calculated across datasets using both fixed-effect and Hartung-Knapp random-effects models, with statistical heterogeneity measured via the I² statistic.
Analysis of the primary aggregated outcome revealed that continuous subcuticular suturing significantly increased overall postoperative complications across the six included trials. Under the Hartung-Knapp random-effects model, patients receiving continuous closure experienced a relative risk of 1.76, with a 95% confidence interval spanning from 1.17 to 2.65, achieving statistical significance with a P value of 0.007. The elevated composite risk was confirmed to be stable through sensitivity evaluations, including leave-one-out and trim-and-fill analyses designed to test robustness and publication bias.
When examining specific adverse events individually, differences between continuous and interrupted closures yielded nuanced statistical patterns. For urethrocutaneous fistula, the fixed-effect model demonstrated a statistically significant elevated risk for continuous closure, showing a relative risk of 1.71 with a 95% confidence interval of 1.12 to 2.60 and a P value of 0.013. However, under the more conservative corrected random-effects model, the finding shifted to a non-significant trend, producing a relative risk of 1.59 with a 95% confidence interval of 0.87 to 2.91 and a P value of 0.130.
No statistically significant difference between surgical groups emerged for any single adverse event under the random-effects analysis, including glans dehiscence, urethral stricture, or superficial infection. Nevertheless, the point estimates for every evaluated individual adverse event consistently favored interrupted closure over continuous closure. The authors noted that the significantly elevated composite complication rate observed across the pooled data reflects an accumulation of these individually non-significant trends rather than a single dominant surgical failure mode.
A pre-planned subgroup analysis specifically examined procedures performed using Vicryl suture material, pooling results from five randomized controlled trials with a cumulative cohort of 509 patients. In this stratified cohort, continuous suturing similarly demonstrated a statistically significant elevation in overall postoperative complications, generating a relative risk of 1.55 with a 95% confidence interval of 1.09 to 2.21 and a P value of 0.015, mirroring the pattern found in the broader trial synthesis.
Despite the observed association with higher composite complication rates, the investigators emphasized that the GRADE certainty of evidence across all measured outcomes was classified as low. The authors concluded that while individual adverse events did not demonstrate statistically significant divergence in conservative modeling, the aggregated data suggest interrupted subcuticular closure may be cautiously preferred over continuous suturing to reduce cumulative morbidity, particularly during primary tubularized incised plate urethroplasty utilizing Vicryl sutures.
