TITLE: From lighthouse keeper's daughter to Exeter's first female dean: 'The light belongs to Christ'
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The first female Dean of Exeter believes that the Church can be a beacon of light and "transformative healing" inspired by her personal journey in the Christian faith.
Mother Arwen Folkes, currently Vicar of Eastbourne at St Mary the Virgin in East Sussex, will replace the Very Rev Stephen Waine in May, returning to her South West roots.
Born and baptised in Barnstaple and Bideford in North Devon, she grew up in Cornwall at Lizard Lighthouse in Helston, following her father's occupation as a working lighthouse keeper in the Duchy.
Mother Arwen has called Exeter Cathedral her "new lighthouse" and said that the sea is intrinsically linked to her faith.
"My whole childhood was governed with how you keep the light, how you magnify the light for others, how you make sure it can shine as brightly, boldly and as clearly. I have long reflected on ministry as having very similar attributes," she told Premier Christian News.
"The light belongs to Christ and we do all that we can to not get in the way of that shining and being seen by others. Every church that I have had the privilege of leading has been a lighthouse of some description, it just happens that Exeter Cathedral is slightly larger and has the potential to shine its light not just across a parish but actually across a whole county.
"The sea is in my veins really. Growing up beside the sea you sort of take it for granted, but actually, the rhythm of it but also the perspective that it gives you on creation and on God's magnitude.
"My best place to pray other than in front of the Blessed Sacrament or at the altar in Mass, is actually sitting beside the sea. The ebb and the flow of it has, the book of nature is very powerfully expressed in the sea I think."
The former Cornwall district councillor was raised Catholic and recounted receiving first Holy Communion as day she vividly remembers. She also founded Real Baby Milk, a social enterprise project in Cornwall, where she served her curacy at St Just in Roseland.
Speaking after Safeguarding Sunday, she illuminated her perspective on how the Church can heal the hurt and what her mission in Exeter is as she leaves the Diocese of Chichester.
"I came back to my faith and it was that early experience of the Eucharist that brought me back, it was kind of like the sea in my veins," she revealed.
"One of the things that I am most passionate about within the Church is that every church is a place of transformative safety and healing. When we take safeguarding really seriously, remarkable things are possible because it becomes a place that knows its boundaries, knows its limitations, but also has the liberty to be able to really minister in a way without distraction. My own faith journey of returning to my faith has been one of transformative healing.
"My job, if you call it a job - my calling - really is to try and make that available to as many people as possible. Sometimes that means transforming the structures of a place before that work can begin, but I genuinely think that a genuine encounter with our Lord Jesus Christ, is seriously one of the most foundational, incredible, transformational things that can happen in the life of a person."
