TITLE: On the other hand
OUTLET: J-Wire

A 2,000-year-old Roman Street in the Christian Quarter of Jerusalem’s Old City is being reconstructed to improve wheelchair and stroller accessibility as part of a multi-year NIS 80 million ($24.7 million) renovation project, the East Jerusalem Development Company (PAMI) said last week.
Despite facing “nearly every possible constraint” because of the area’s high population density and historical sensitivity, accessibility experts from other regions have been impressed with the project, which PAMI said has been planned since 2009.
The work on Christian Quarter Street involves removing about 50 ancient stones dating to around 135 CE, during the early Roman period, and reinstalling them along the street’s eastern side. This will allow the western side of the street to be levelled so wheelchairs and strollers can pass easily, PAMI said. The project will also replace water and electricity infrastructure.
The Israel Antiquities Authority will document each ancient paving stone before it is lifted. Once removed, the IAA will assess whether the stone can be safely returned to the street or must be preserved elsewhere. Some of the stones are already fragile, PAMI noted.
The project is part of a broader Old City accessibility initiative that was first conceived in 2009. Since implementation began in 2019, about six kilometres of alleyways have been made accessible, including the Via Dolorosa and David Street, PAMI said.
“Making the Old City accessible is much more than adapting sidewalks and paving,” said Jerusalem Mayor Moshe Lion. “It is an initiative that expresses a fundamental value of equality, dignity and every person’s right to experience Jerusalem’s most important places for themselves.”
