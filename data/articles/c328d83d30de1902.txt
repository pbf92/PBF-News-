TITLE: Priest murdered in Birmingham had fled conflict in South Sudan
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A priest who was murdered in Birmingham earlier this month is understood to have been granted asylum in the UK after fleeing conflict in South Sudan.
Rev Jacob Yong Den Joh Yong who was ordained in the Episcopal Church of South Sudan was fatally stabbed at a property in Edgbaston on 5 November.
According to Church Times, the 37 year old had begun attending Holy Trinity West Bromwich in September after travelling from South Sudan where he had lived in refugee camps. It's understood he had plans to hold regular gatherings at the church for the local South Sudanese community.
Rev Neil Robbie, vicar of Holy Trinity, described his death as "tragic" especially knowing that Rev Yong had survived the civil war and life as a refugee but had been killed within six months of finding refuge in the UK.
He said it was devastating not just for his family but for the whole South Sudanese and Dinka community who are said to be "reeling" from the news.
Holy Trinity West Bromwich is now raising funds to enable Yong's family to travel from South Sudan to attend his funeral in Birmingham. His family released a statement describing him as a "man of God and a beloved son and brother”and said he would be "dearly missed by everyone who knew him.”
West Midlands Police have charged a 28-year-old man, Muner Yahya, with his murder. But they're keen for anyone with information which could help their inquiries to contact them on 101.
