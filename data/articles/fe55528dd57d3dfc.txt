TITLE: Maurcio Pochettino offers Tyler Adams a USMNT plaudit for the road
OUTLET: New York Post

CINCINNATI — Mauricio Pochettino delivered what sounded a lot like a parting message to U.S. national team captain Tyler Adams on Monday, telling the midfielder both how proud he is of him and how important he is for the program’s future.
“He’s the example. … He’s the captain,” Pochettino said to a small group of reporters, including The Post, on Monday afternoon. “He’s the player that can be key for the future of this country.
“I said to him, ‘How you make [Adri] Mehmeti play next to you so good, or Sebastian [Berhalter] so good, or Julian Hall so good, how you help, you don’t realize because you are young and you are playing. But you don’t realize how you can make your teammates better. Acting, showing an example, always being the first to go and play and fight.’ ”
While there might technically be a chance the next coach would go with a different captain, such a move would be highly unlikely given the respect Adams — who also wore the armband at the 2022 World Cup — has throughout the program.
Adams, who was renamed the national team’s captain in late September, was one of the only high-profile members of Pochettino’s squad who never seemed to be in his doghouse. While most of the other big names in the pool skipped the 2025 Gold Cup, Adams showed up and was a key piece of the group that made it to the final before losing to Mexico.
At the World Cup, Adams started every game save for the one in the group stage against Turkey, when the U.S. had already clinched a spot in the knockout rounds, and continued playing an important role as a defensive safety valve in Pochettino’s system.
Now, as the U.S. team opens the door to a series of younger faces, Adams has an important mentorship role in the dressing room as well, particularly for players like Mehmeti, who could form a midfield partnership with him throughout this cycle.
“A unique thing for me, with a lot of these kids is I came through the same system that a lot of these kids are coming through right now,” Adams told The Post in September. “I can talk to them about playing in the U.S., I can talk about them being a homegrown player, about them making a transition to Europe and what that looks like, what I found helped me, what hurt me, these kinds of things. So I have these conversations with the guys. They shouldn’t be afraid to come talk to me.”
