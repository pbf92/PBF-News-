TITLE: Stocks Win Back Most of Their Morning Losses After Chipmakers Slide
OUTLET: Stock Titan

On this page
The short answer. Chipmakers led the market lower for a second day this morning, but stocks won back most of that fall by the close, and the Dow fund turned higher. The US Trade Representative proposed new tariffs of up to 35% on goods from 14 economies, and the plans are not final. Earlier, government reports at 8:30 a.m. ET showed that the economy grew faster last quarter than first estimated, and that prices in October were 3.8% higher than a year earlier.
Every company reporting this week, with its date and time, is in StockTitan's Earnings Calendar for the Week of November 23, 2026.

8:03 p.m. ET
Closing summary: stocks won back most of a chip-led morning fall by the close
Stock funds rose a little in late trading, after a day in which they won back most of a morning sell-off led by chipmakers. At the 4:00 p.m. ET close, QQQ, the Nasdaq 100 fund, was down 0.65% from Tuesday's close, after a 4.38% fall at 11:02 a.m. ET. SPY, the S&P 500 fund, closed down 0.29%, and DIA, the Dow fund, closed up 0.31%.
Among companies with news, Pharming had the largest fall in the movers table. It reported higher third-quarter revenue and a new share buyback (Kanzhun release).
After the close, at 7:59 p.m. ET, SPY was up 0.52% from its regular-session close, and QQQ was up 0.42%. Nvidia and Micron also rose in late trading. Vertex was down 7.26% from its close at 7:56 p.m. ET. At 4:02 p.m. ET, it had said the FDA had moved back its decision on its kidney drug (Vertex release).
6:03 p.m. ET
Vertex says the FDA has moved back a decision on its kidney drug, and its shares fall in late trading
Vertex Pharmaceuticals fell in trading after the close. At 5:57 p.m. ET, its shares were down 7.68% from the regular-session close.
Vertex said at 4:02 p.m. ET that the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) had moved back its decision on povetacicept, a drug for the kidney disease IgA nephropathy (Vertex release). The FDA now plans to decide by February 28, 2027, instead of November 30, 2026. Late in its review, the agency asked for more information. It treated Vertex's reply as a major change to the application, which needs more time to review.
The wider market was quiet after the close. At 6:02 p.m. ET, QQQ, the Nasdaq 100 fund, was down 0.22% from its close, and SPY, the S&P 500 fund, was down 0.04%.
4:19 p.m. ET
Stock funds end the day near Tuesday's levels after winning back most of a chip-led fall
Stocks ended the regular session far above their morning lows. At the 4:00 p.m. ET close, QQQ, the Nasdaq 100 fund, was down 0.65% from Tuesday's close, after a 4.38% fall at 11:02 a.m. ET. SPY, the S&P 500 fund, closed down 0.29%, and DIA, the Dow fund, closed up 0.31%.

Why are chip stocks down today
Chip stocks jumped on Monday, the day Nvidia brought back its chief executive. They fell on Tuesday and fell further on Wednesday morning. For several of them, the whole Monday jump was gone by late morning.
On Monday, Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, and QQQ closed up 7.00%, as StockTitan's Monday live page shows. Chip stocks gave back part of that on Tuesday, as the Tuesday live page shows. At 11:02 a.m. ET on Wednesday, QQQ had given back about 96% of Monday's price gain. At the 4:00 p.m. ET close, QQQ had won back ground, and about 40% of Monday's gain was gone.
The give-back is deepest in the chipmakers. At 11:02 a.m. ET, AMD, Broadcom and Micron all traded below their Friday closes, so for them Monday's rally was gone. At the close, Micron was back just above that level, while AMD and Broadcom were still below it. Nvidia had given back about 40% of Monday's 31.48% jump.
AI stocks beyond the chipmakers fell too. Anthropic, the maker of the Claude chatbot, traded $3.35 below its $83.00 IPO price at 11:02 a.m. ET, the price set in its final prospectus dated November 18. It climbed back above that price in the afternoon, then closed at $82.79, just below it.
