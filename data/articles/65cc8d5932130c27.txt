TITLE: Christian apologist Wes Huff responds to claims of plagiarism in his papers
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Christian apologist Wes Huff has responded to allegations he plagiarised parts of a paper he wrote for his master’s degree.
The vice president of Apologetics Canada has said he takes the concerns “seriously”, and is stepping back from “public comment” while the organisation’s board conducts a review.
It comes as Huff and his family care for their three-year-old daughter who was hospitalised following seizures last week.
Dan McClellan, a Mormon scholar, posted a YouTube video on 8 November in which he alleged Huff “plagiarised significant portions” of a paper titled “Christological Claims: A Textual Consideration of John's Gospel”, which he submitted as part of studies at Tyndale Seminary, Toronto, in 2016 and uploaded to Academia.edu.
McClellan compares Huff’s work to “Jesus as ΘΕΟΣ: A Textual Examination,” written in 2010 by Brian J. Wright, author and founding pastor of Redeemer Community Church in Pensacola, Florida. He alleges that there is crossover in numerous details, including wording, structure of argument and bibliographic details, with Huff not citing Wright.
A number of academics then raised similar concerns. Stephen C. Carlson, associate professor at Australian Catholic University in Melbourne, shared screenshots on X alleging Huff had plagiarised his work.
Responding to the claims on X, Huff said: “I take the concerns about plagiarism in my written work seriously. Honesty and integrity matter deeply to me, and not correctly crediting others’ work, in any setting, falls short of those standards.
“I'm committed to full accountability and am walking closely through this with my pastor, church elder board, and the Apologetics Canada Board of Directors.
“Right now, my family's priority is caring for our young daughter as she recovers at home following her recent hospitalization. I'm very grateful for the support and prayers we've received. I will be stepping back from further public comment while the Apologetics Canada Board conducts its thorough review,” he posted on Friday.
Huff responded to the allegations in a YouTube channel which has since been deleted. He acknowledges past “poor and problematic work” which had been cited badly, saying he would be minded “to cancel Wes Huff from 2016 or maybe even 2017 and 2018”, as he believed some social media users wanted to do.
Wright, who Huff allegedly copied, told The Blaze that he believes “in extending grace and forgiveness, recognizing that forgiveness does not excuse wrongdoing or eliminate accountability.”
Apologetics Canada said it was “aware of the concerns raised regarding plagiarism in some of Wes Huff's written work.”
A statement added: “We are committed to truth, the highest level of integrity, and loving God and people. Our Board is reviewing the matter carefully. We will share an update once the review is complete.”
