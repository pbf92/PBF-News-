TITLE: Flipkart CEO Kalyan Krishnamurthy exits after more than 11 years
OUTLET: PPC Land

Flipkart today said Kalyan Krishnamurthy is concluding his tenure as chief executive after more than eleven years with the company, leaving Walmart's Indian e-commerce group without a permanent leader in the quarter that Walmart expects Flipkart's biggest sale to lift its results.
No successor has been named. The company will announce one "in the coming weeks," according to the release, which was published on Walmart's corporate newsroom under a Bengaluru dateline. Until then, Balaji Thiagarajan, Flipkart's Chief Product and Technology Officer, and the Flipkart board will run the business.
In Short
Kalyan Krishnamurthy, who has run Flipkart since January 2017, is stepping down as chief executive, and Flipkart will name a replacement in the next few weeks. Walmart owns most of Flipkart, one of India's leading online stores, and has credited its ad business, Flipkart Ads, with leading Walmart's advertising growth outside the US. For now, product and technology chief Balaji Thiagarajan and the board are in charge, and the release gives no reason for the exit, no departure date and no sign of what the next chief will change.
What Flipkart announced
The release is short, about two minutes of reading by Walmart's own estimate, and most of it is a tribute. It credits Krishnamurthy with a "defining decade of growth" in which, according to Flipkart, "the business grew more than fivefold." It does not say which measure grew fivefold, or from what starting point.
Flipkart also credits him with making The Big Billion Days, its annual sale, "synonymous with India's festive shopping season." The release says Flipkart "pioneered several innovations and industry firsts" under his leadership and built new services for consumers and sellers. It names none of them in the body. The boilerplate at the bottom lists services Flipkart says it pioneered: Cash on Delivery, No Cost EMI (monthly instalments with no interest charge to the buyer), Easy Returns and UPI payments.
One word comes back three times. The release praises his "focus on running a business with strong governance." Chris Nicholas, president and CEO of Walmart International, thanks him for "the governance he instilled." And Krishnamurthy's own statement puts it at the centre: "Strong ethics, integrity and high standards of governance have been central to Flipkart's growth."
The full statement from Nicholas reads: "Both Flipkart and Walmart are better because of Kalyan. We are grateful for his leadership, the governance he instilled and his many contributions to the company. We wish him nothing but the best as he moves on to his next adventure." He added: "We know great things are ahead for Flipkart and we're excited by the potential that exists and the opportunity to continue helping customers in India save money and live better."
Krishnamurthy said: "It has been a privilege to be part of Flipkart's journey. I am proud of the scale of the business we have built, but equally proud of how we have built it." He went on: "Our team has helped change how millions of Indians shop, expanding choice and creating opportunities for sellers, entrepreneurs and businesses across the country. I'm grateful to the colleagues, sellers and partners who have been part of this journey. Flipkart has a strong foundation and an exciting future, and I wish the team every success."
Walmart's position is set out in a single paragraph: "Walmart remains committed to Flipkart and its long-term opportunity in India." The release describes Flipkart as having "a strong market position, a well-established business and significant opportunities ahead."
How the handover is set up
According to the release, the transition has three parts:
- Krishnamurthy is "concluding his tenure as CEO." The release gives no final working day.
- In the interim, Balaji Thiagarajan, "currently Chief Product and Technology Officer at Flipkart, and the Flipkart board will lead the organisation."
- "The company will announce a successor in the coming weeks." The release does not say whether candidates come from inside Flipkart, from Walmart or from outside.
Thiagarajan is a relatively recent hire. Flipkart named him Chief Product and Technology Officer in September 2025, according to YourStory, which reported that he had held senior roles at Google, Microsoft, Uber and Yahoo and had most recently founded and run Kasu.ai, a US artificial intelligence startup. He replaced Jeyandran Venugopal, who had left earlier that year to join Reliance Retail, and reported directly to Krishnamurthy, the outlet said.
Flipkart by the numbers
The release's company description gives the only hard figures in the document. None of them is new, and none comes with a date or a comparison period.
The tenure figure does not line up neatly with Walmart's own leadership page. That page dates the start of Krishnamurthy's current association with Flipkart to 2016, says he was appointed CEO of Flipkart in January 2017 and lists him as group CEO since 2018. It also records an earlier spell, in 2013, when he served as Flipkart's interim chief financial officer. Quartz reported in January 2017 that he had joined in June 2016; from then to today is about ten years and five months, by PPC Land's calculation. The release does not explain how it counts eleven.
The road to the top job
Krishnamurthy came to Flipkart from the investor side. Walmart's leadership page lists him as a former managing director at Tiger Global Management, where he managed the fund's India portfolio companies, with earlier finance roles at eBay and Procter & Gamble. Quartz reported in January 2017 that he had joined Flipkart in June 2016 from Tiger Global and headed its category organisation when he replaced Binny Bansal as chief executive, with Bansal moving to a group-level role.
That group role ended in November 2018. Binny Bansal resigned as CEO of Flipkart Group with immediate effect after an independent investigation, carried out on behalf of Flipkart and Walmart, into an allegation of serious personal misconduct, according to a joint statement published by Walmart on November 13, 2018. The statement said the investigation did not find evidence to corroborate the allegation, which Bansal denied, but did find other lapses in judgement, including a lack of transparency in how he responded. It said Krishnamurthy would continue as CEO of Flipkart, now including Myntra and Jabong.
Walmart has since increased its stake. In 2023 it spent $3.5 billion buying shares from Flipkart investors including Tiger Global, Accel and Binny Bansal, and resolving liabilities with some PhonePe shareholders, bringing its ownership of Flipkart to about 80%, TechCrunch reported in September 2023, citing a Securities and Exchange Commission filing.
Why it matters for advertisers
Flipkart is not only a shop. Its advertising business, Flipkart Ads, is a retail media network: it sells ad space on Flipkart to the brands and sellers who trade there. Walmart has said Flipkart Ads led its international advertising growth, and its results move Walmart's reported figures from quarter to quarter.
Walmart does not publish Flipkart's advertising revenue. It reports growth rates for Walmart International's advertising as a whole, and PPC Land has tracked them across the last four reports. The swings are large, and most of them trace back to when the Big Billion Days falls.
Walmart's full-year fiscal 2026 results put its global advertising revenue at $6.4 billion, with international advertising up 19% for the year. In August, Walmart International's advertising grew 20% in the second quarter, led by Flipkart Ads. Six days ago, international advertising fell 15% in the third quarter as the timing of Flipkart's Big Billion Days pulled global ad growth down to 25%. Walmart's finance chief said the shift would add about one percentage point to sales growth in the fourth quarter, which runs from November to January.
So the chief executive is leaving in the very quarter that Walmart expects Flipkart's sale to lift its numbers. Will a new leader change how Flipkart sells its ad space? The release does not touch advertising at all.
Flipkart's own accounts give a longer view. Flipkart Ads grew 31% in the year to March 2026, faster than the group's 18% revenue growth, according to exchange4media, which reported on October 15 on the group's consolidated statements filed with India's Registrar of Companies. Group revenue from operations reached ₹1,45,880 crore and the net loss narrowed to ₹8,468 crore from ₹8,646 crore, the outlet reported. The release under review mentions none of these figures.
The festive season is the period that matters most for that ad business. Industry estimates cited by Adjust put festive advertising spend in India at ₹48,000 to ₹51,000 crore for 2025 and ₹60,000 to ₹62,000 crore for 2026, and Adjust's data showed e-commerce app installs in India running 29% above the daily average in the two weeks before Diwali 2025, after the Amazon and Flipkart sales started on September 22 and 23 that year.
Amazon, which competes with Flipkart for the same sellers, is pitching its own ads to newcomers. In October, PPC Land reported that Amazon Ads told new Indian sellers to budget about ₹500 a day for 45 to 60 days, while naming a ₹15,000 starting figure that falls short of that advice. Flipkart's 1.4 million sellers are the advertisers Flipkart Ads depends on, and its base of more than 500 million registered users is the source of the first-party data that a retailer's ad business sells against.
A run of senior departures
Krishnamurthy's exit is the most senior in a string of leadership changes at Flipkart over the past two years. They come from several sources, and the release itself mentions none of them.
Outlook Business tied the Myntra change to a push into quick commerce through M-Now, Myntra's fast-delivery service, which it described as offering delivery in 30 to 60 minutes across 10 cities.
Then there is the stock market listing. When the CFO change was announced in March, Business Today reported that Flipkart was planning a stock market debut by March 2027, that Krishnamurthy was in early talks with banks including Goldman Sachs, Kotak Mahindra Capital, Morgan Stanley and JP Morgan, and that nothing had been finalised. It also reported that Flipkart had recently moved its headquarters from Singapore back to India. By August, Outlook Business, citing Moneycontrol, described the listing as delayed so the company could focus on profitability. Today's release does not mention a listing.
What the release does not say
The release gives no reason for the departure, no effective date, no word on whether Krishnamurthy keeps a board seat or an advisory role, and no detail of his "next adventure." It does not say whether the fivefold growth refers to revenue, GMV or another measure, how Flipkart counts "more than eleven years" against Walmart's 2016 date, who will choose the successor, or whether any change is planned for Flipkart Ads.
