TITLE: Tim Weah won't 'beg' for USMNT return after Mauricio Pochettino snubbed Marseille captain
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

Since making his U.S. Men's National Team debut at just 18 years old in 2018, Tim Weah has gone on to appear in two FIFA World Cups, winning three CONCACAF Nations League titles.
However, the now 26-year-old's international career has tapered off in recent years. Weah struggled to nail down a starting spot under now-former head coach Mauricio Pochettino, who was appointed in September 2024 and recently left to take charge of Manchester United.
The Marseille captain appeared in just two games during this summer's World Cup on home soil, as the USMNT wound up being knocked out in the Round of 16 by Belgium.
Despite this, Weah appears defiant in his desire not to let this setback get in the way of his overall career. In a recent interview with The Guardian, the former Juventus star said, "In every career you have moments where you're a bit down, and you're a bit in your head about things. I think the World Cup was definitely that for me.
"On a personal level, I had to take a back seat and figure out where my place was in the squad."
Weah went on to note that there were "never any excuses," even criticizing his only tournament start against Türkiye, explaining, "I went into the game overthinking a lot. When you're overthinking, your feet stop working. It was rough for me. After the game I knew that's not how I want to perform for my national team."
Despite performing for Marseille, Weah failed to make the cut for either of Pochettino's post-World Cup rosters, in which the USMNT went on a run of six wins prior to his departure.
But while this could be deflating for some, Weah noted that he has no intention of 'begging' for a return. "I don't want to force anything or beg," he said. "I'm good where I'm at. If I end up back in the national team, and I don't know when that will be, I'll go. I'm focused 100% on club football."

Whenever Weah does make his return to the USMNT, he'll be hoping to see the culture and foundations laid by Pochettino being built upon by his successor. "He set the foundation and whoever comes in is going to ride that wave," Weah said.
"It's an amazing group that they have and hopefully, they can continue levelling up and getting results against bigger opponents. The next goal for us is to win against top European sides and top South American sides."
As for the ongoing domestic campaign, during which Weah has missed just one game in all competitions due to a suspension, the 53-time international admitted that being seen as a leader in what is his second season in France is "an amazing feeling."
"It's an amazing feeling to be someone that's looked upon as a leader, especially when it's coming from the head coach [Bruno Génésio]," he said. "I've worked super hard here and the fact that everyone sees me as someone that can lead the team and continue to lead the team is an amazing feeling."
Marseille sits fifth in Ligue 1 after 11 games, while they're a lowly 31st in the UEFA Europa League four games in, with PFC Levski Sofia traveling to France on Thursday.
