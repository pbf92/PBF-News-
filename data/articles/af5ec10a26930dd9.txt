Potential Altrincham Grammar School for Girls admissions policy change sparks concern
Some think the change is unfair
A proposal to change the admission policy at a Trafford grammar school has prompted concerns it could disadvantage local girls.
The trust behind Altrincham Grammar School for Girls (AGGS) is considering an update that would see priority given to pupils at its feeder primary schools over those living closer to the school should it be oversubscribed for the new academic year. Those pupils would still need to pass entrance exams.
AGGS has four feeder schools - Acre Hall Primary School, in Urmston; Barton Clough Primary School, in Stretford; Elmridge Primary School, in Hale Barns; and Lime Tree Primary Academy, in Sale.

Concerns have been raised that girls could miss out on a place simply because they have not attended the right primary school. Those speaking against the move have pointed out this could include those living and studying in Altrincham itself, as well as nearby areas such as Broadheath, Timperley and Bowdon.
Looked-after children, and those living in the catchment area who receive a ‘pupil premium’ or who have a sister already at the school still make up the top three priority categories under the proposed change.
If space remains after feeder school pupils are then taken into account, places will then be given to those in the catchment area with those living closest offered places first.
The school’s admission policy defines its catchment area as an eight-mile radius, with each of the feeder schools sitting within that. However, that does not necessarily mean pupils attending that school will live within the catchment radius.
No decisions have yet been made, the Bright Futures Educational Trust has stressed, saying it is consulting with parents. The four feeder schools sit within the trust’s portfolio.
Among those raising concerns are elected members from the Liberal Democrat and Conservative groups who sit on Trafford council.
Broadheath Lib Dem councillor Ulrich Savary said in a letter to the trust: “Grammar schools are founded on the principle of academic selection. Children who demonstrate the required academic ability should have a fair opportunity to secure a place, regardless of which primary school they attend.
“Under the proposed arrangements, a girl living outside the catchment area who passes the entrance examination could receive priority over another equally academically qualified girl living just a few hundred metres from AGGS, simply because she attends one of four primary schools belonging to the same academy trust.
“I believe this raises serious questions about fairness and equal opportunity.”
Conservative councillor for Bowdon Phil Eckersley has also raised concerns. He said: "I've been contacted by a number of local residents who are understandably worried that their daughters could lose out on a place at their local grammar school, despite passing the entrance exam and living close by. Under the proposals, children attending four primary schools within the same academy trust could receive priority over other qualifying applicants, even those living much closer to AGGS.
“I struggle to see how that can be considered fair. AGGS has been part of the Bowdon and Altrincham community for generations. Its relationship with local families matters, and I don't believe membership of an academy trust should automatically take priority over where a child lives.”

He added: “Opportunities must be allocated through a fair and transparent admissions process. Unless the trust can provide convincing evidence that these proposals are fair, I will oppose them [...] Our local schools should serve their communities, not simply prioritise the interests of an academy trust.”
Bright Futures Educational Trust said it is proposing to give priority to girls at the four feeder schools because they ‘already have established educational links’ with AGGS. This includes working together through transition activities, curriculum collaboration and wider Trust initiatives.
“As Trafford schools within the Trust, they represent the primary phase of the Trust's local educational pathway, and the proposal seeks to recognise those existing relationships within the admissions arrangements,” the trust has said.
It believes the changes would impact just a ‘small number’ of admissions.
The matter was raised at this week’s full council meeting at Trafford town hall. Lib Dem councillor Jane Brophy asked the ruling Labour administration whether it would be responding to the consultation and what their position was on the proposed changes.
Council leader Tom Ross said the authority was a statutory consultee for matters such as this, meaning they have to be consulted. He added: “We will be looking at the implications of the changes to school admissions, and that will inform our formal response that we will place with the proposals.”
Following the meeting, Coun Brophy said: “"Parents deserve to know where their elected representatives stand on an issue that could affect their children's education. We asked Labour a very simple question: do you support these changes or not? Instead of giving us a straight answer, they hid behind technicalities.
"This is about fairness. Academically able girls should not be disadvantaged simply because they attend the wrong primary school.”
A spokesperson for Bright Futures Educational Trust said: "We welcome feedback from all interested parties and encourage anyone with a view on the proposal to take part in the consultation. No decision has been made, and all responses received will be considered by the Trust Board before any determination is reached."
