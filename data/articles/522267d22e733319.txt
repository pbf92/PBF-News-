TITLE: Jordanian parliament debates tougher terror sentences after deadly church attack
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Lawmakers in Jordan are considering changes to anti-terror legislation in the wake of the deadliest attack on Christians in the country.
Nine people were killed and 34 others wounded after gunmen opened fire on a funeral being held at St George’s Greek Orthodox Church in the Christian village of Hamoud, Karak, on 23 October.
Three men have been sentenced to death for their role in the massacre.
In response to the church attack, King Abdullah II said “the treacherous hand of terrorism” had reached out “to test the resilience of our state and the resolve of our people”.
The House of Representatives voted to refer draft amendments to the Anti-Terrorism Law 2006 to a review committee on Wednesday.
The government is seeking to double minimum prison sentences for terror-related offences, according to the Jordan News Agency. It would make most offences punishable by at least ten years in jail, rising to 15 in some cases, with the death penalty on the table for serious offences.
Some parliamentarians have also called for tighter hate speech laws to clamp down on extremism, although this has been criticised by opponents as being too vague and infringing on freedom of speech.
Churches in Jordan have cancelled Christmas celebrations out of respect for those murdered and their families. Congregations will also honour a security officer killed outside an embassy on 5 November, which has been linked to terrorism.
The Council of Church Leaders in Jordan have called for Christmas to be marked with “simplicity and dignity”, praying for those affected and including space next to Christmas trees for a memorial to those killed.
