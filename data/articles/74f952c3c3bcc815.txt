TITLE: Hosting World Cup means decade of excitement - Weir
OUTLET: BBC Sport

- Published
Scotland captain Caroline Weir says hosting the 2035 Women's World Cup along with England, Northern Ireland and Wales will usher in "an amazing decade of excitement" for the game in her homeland.
World governing body Fifa confirmed on Monday that the United Kingdom will host a football World Cup for the first time since England won the men's tournament on home soil in 1966.
Scotland's national stadium at Hampden Park in Glasgow and Hibernian's Easter Road Stadium in Leith are among 19 venues chosen for the 39-day competition involving 48 teams.
At the age of 31 now, Weir would have to show unusual longevity to still be playing at that point, but she still considers it "amazing news, not just for young players that hope to play there but for us, in this period in between, to develop the women's game in Scotland - and to push it.
"So I think it's amazing news and I think it's going to be an amazing decade of excitement leading into this World Cup".
The Lyon midfielder has already played for Scotland the only time they have so far qualified for a World Cup finals - in 2019.
"As a young, aspiring footballer watching football on TV growing up, for your home country to actually host a tournament itself, and the World Cup itself, the biggest tournament in football, is the most amazing thing," Weir said.
"So, if I was a 10 or 11 year old girl now, I would be thinking this is the best news ever and I'm going to work and practice and hope that, one day, I am representing Scotland in a home nations tournament playing at Hampden Park in a World Cup for Scotland.
"I don't think it gets any bigger or better than that."
Angie Hind, Scotland Under-23 head coach and Scottish FA player pathway manager, also thinks it will prove inspirational to the young players under her charge.
"If you think of the young players who are involved in our pathway right now, from 13 years old right through the 23s that I take, that's the future," the former Scotland midfielder told BBC Scotland.
"That's very much those players who will be pulling on a Scotland jersey when we are part of that World Cup."
Hind pointed out that it "fits perfectly" with Project 2035, announced this month by SFA and Scottish government, designed to boost the long-term growth of the girls and women's game.
For SFA head of women's and girls football Shirley Martin, it is a "tremendous milestone" considering holding a World Cup had been such "a game changer" for previous hosts.
"Ten years ago, a home World Cup have felt like a dream and now it's happening," she told BBC Radio 5 live. "I think it shows how far women's football has come."
Martin pointed out that the SFA hopes to "double our participation over that time" as well as improving coaching, referees, clubs and facilities - and are already doing so.
However, she admitted: "The one thing we need to do is increase the crowds that come to matches."
