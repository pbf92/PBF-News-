Anthropic's Autonomous AI 'Claude Duet' Lands in Japan as US-Origin Research Preview Expands to 4 Countries
The research preview of "Claude Duet," the AI agent offered by US-based Anthropic, became available in Japan starting November 26 (Japan time). The move follows Anthropic's Karan Ahuja announcing on X the previous day, November 25 (US time), that the company would expand the offering to four countries—Japan, India, Brazil, and Indonesia—marking its decision to go overseas roughly one month after launch in the United States.
Claude Duet is an application in which Anthropic's conversational AI Claude functions not merely as a passive responder but as an agent that acts proactively. It supports the iOS/Android mobile apps and the web version "claude.ai," and its defining feature is a dedicated web browser on a virtual machine. It is designed to continue processing even while the user's PC or smartphone is powered off, periodically patrolling connected accounts such as Gmail, Slack, and calendars to handle tasks like replying to unread messages and preparing for meetings.
In the United States, the offering began on October 21 (local time) as a waitlist-based research preview. In Japan, invitations have begun reaching users of the "Pro," "Max," and "Ultra" plans who had registered for the waitlist in advance, and social media has seen a wave of posts reporting receipt of invitations.

For the corporate "Team" plan, Anthropic said it is proceeding with a phased rollout so that administrators can enable Claude Duet.
The Japan rollout is seen as aimed at letting Anthropic collect real-world operational data on agentic AI outside the United States. As a research preview, the offering is at a stage where features and specifications are subject to change at any time, and usage-limit reset credits are being distributed for a limited time. For Japanese companies, use cases in which tasks are processed in the background while remaining integrated with existing business tools such as Slack and Gmail are becoming more realistic. At the same time, permission management for connected accounts and audit frameworks for operations the agent executes autonomously will be issues to consider at adoption.
Anthropic has also been beta-offering a feature that lets users install "Artifacts" created on Claude as desktop apps for Mac and Windows. Small tools generated in a chat can be launched with their own icons and windows, and if the user instructs revisions in the original conversation, the changes are reflected without reinstalling. Alongside the agent features, the push to expand Claude from a mere conversational tool into an execution platform for business operations continues.
