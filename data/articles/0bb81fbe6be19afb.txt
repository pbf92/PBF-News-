TITLE: Police probe suspected robberies at Co-Op Live after gas incident sparks chaos at Don Toliver gig
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

Police probe suspected robberies at Co-Op Live after gas incident sparks chaos at Don Toliver gig
GMP have issued an update following the Don Toliver concert
Greater Manchester Police are investigating a possible link between an unknown substance being released at Co-op Live and a series of suspected robberies during a concert on Sunday evening (November 22).
The force confirmed that nine reports of jewellery theft were received following the event, with detectives now examining whether the alleged release of the substance was connected to the suspected offences.
No arrests have been made.

Tests are ongoing to identify an unknown substance that sparked the evacuation of Co-op Live during a gig by US rapper Don Toliver, Manchester council's leader has said. More than 12,000 people were inside the venue as a major incident was declared on Sunday night.
One person was taken to hospital and 89 concert-goers were treated at the scene. Police initially said tear gas was thought to have been released inside the arena, but later described it as an 'unknown substance'.
Mobile phone video footage showed concert-goers struggling to breathe and coughing as they walked out of the venue.
The police scene at the Manchester arena has now been lifted, but enquiries remain ongoing as officers investigate the circumstances surrounding the incident.
Greater Manchester Police also revealed that similar robberies have reportedly taken place at two European concerts featuring the same artist.
Assistant Chief Constable Tom Harding said: "We know that what happened on Sunday night and the imagery seen on social media will bring back painful memories of 2017.
"Thankfully, we are not dealing with an incident of such a devastating nature or motive and we maintain our belief that, based on the information available to us now, there is no wider ongoing risk to the public.
"We are aware similar robberies have been reported at two European concerts for the same artist and will be linking in with our European counterparts to better understand the wider picture of this suspected offending. We'll also continue to work closely with the venue and local authority partners over the coming weeks as events begin to return to the Co-Op Live arena.
"I'd like to thank everyone who attended the concert on Sunday evening for their patience and cooperation while emergency services responded. We're continuing to advise anyone who has developed symptoms or become unwell since attending the event to seek advice from their GP, NHS 111, or to attend their local hospital if necessary."

Speaking to the Manchester Evening News on Monday, Don Toliver concert-goers said they heard people shouting 'I can't breathe' after the substance was released into the crowd. A major incident was declared following a call to emergency services.
Other gig goers have raised questions over the security measures in place at the Co-op Live arena following the incident.
Mobile phone footage seen by the Manchester Evening News also clearly shows a flare being ignited and emitting red-tinged smoke in the crowd during the concert. Greater Manchester Police (GMP) says it has been liaising with the venue over security as part of its ongoing investigation into the incident.
One concertgoer said he was 'sprayed' directly in the face with what he believed was tear gas by someone holding a 'canister'. Shouts of 'what's that smell', 'I can't breathe' and 'I can taste it' could be heard as concertgoers flocked through the doors to get outside.
Charlie, from Cheshire, was at the gig with his girlfriend. Speaking to the MEN in the aftermath, he said: "I was in a mosh pit and I heard someone shouting behind me. Someone then tapped me on the shoulder in an aggressive manner. When I turned around, I saw a white or silverish canister in my face. A split second after I turned around, someone sprayed out of it.
"My eyes were on fire. I couldn't see anything. It went all down the back of my throat, nose and mouth. At that moment, I just ran out. I was screaming for help from medics as I really couldn't breathe or see anything. All the effects I have had, they are the effects of tear gas."
He told how his eyes were washed with saline and of being given antihistamines and water. Charlie said he was then taken to the sports hall where, wrapped in tin foil, he was checked over again before the effects began to die down.
Charlie accused the venue of 'failing in its duty of public safety.' "How has someone actually taken that into the venue?" he asked.
Co-op Live has been approached from comment by the Manchester Evening News.
Anyone who has developed symptoms or felt unwell since attending the concert is being advised to seek medical advice from their GP, NHS 111 or their local hospital if necessary.
