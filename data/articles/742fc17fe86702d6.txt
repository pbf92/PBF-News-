TITLE: Most Overviews of Acupuncture Reviews Fail to Prevent Overlapping Outcome Data
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

In a meta-research study of 169 overviews of systematic reviews assessing acupuncture across diverse health conditions, researchers found that active management of overlapping primary studies is infrequent, leaving double-counting of outcome data widespread. Although overviews of systematic reviews synthesize evidence from multiple reviews, overlapping primary studies present a methodological challenge that may produce artificially precise estimates of clinical effects. Across the examined literature, only seventy overviews, or 41.4 percent, utilized any strategy to visualize, quantify, or resolve data overlap. Furthermore, only 43 overviews, representing just 25.4 percent of the sample, fully avoided double-counting underlying primary evidence.
The investigation was designed to address two primary methodological objectives. First, the team sought to quantify the impact of different overlap management strategies on both effect estimates and the evidence base in acupuncture overviews that resolved duplicate data. Second, the investigators assessed the degree of overlap underlying overall effects in overviews that did not resolve duplicate studies. To assemble the evidence base, the authors conducted comprehensive searches across five electronic databases from their inception through June 1, 2026. The search identified overviews of systematic reviews evaluating acupuncture effectiveness in participants with any health condition while describing the frequency of overlap strategies applied.
For the 126 overviews with unresolved overlap, which comprised 74.6 percent of the sample, the researchers categorized outcomes into four effect levels and calculated the corrected covered area. Across all 771 evaluated outcomes, the median overall corrected covered area was 3.6 percent, while the median of pairwise corrected covered area medians was 0.0 percent. Despite these low medians, substantial concentrations of repeated primary research were observed across categories. Specifically, moderate-to-very-high overlap was present in 21.7 to 52.4 percent of outcomes across effect levels, indicating that unresolved data duplication remains widespread beneath reported summary results.
In the subset of 22 overviews, representing 13.0 percent of the total, that resolved overlap by performing new meta-analyses, the authors replicated four alternative overlap strategies. These strategies involved retaining only the Cochrane review, the most comprehensive review, the highest quality review, or the most recent systematic review. Implementing these four alternative approaches generated 302 results across 108 distinct outcomes. By comparing these alternative strategy results directly with findings from the newly conducted meta-analyses, the investigators systematically assessed how choosing specific reviews influences the resulting evidence base and numerical conclusions.
The replication demonstrated that selecting individual reviews via alternative overlap strategies caused measurable data loss and altered interpretations. Across comparisons, adopting an alternative strategy led to a median loss of 4 primary studies and 316 participants relative to new meta-analyses. Furthermore, 31.8 percent of outcomes showed changes in statistical significance, and 7.6 percent exhibited an outright shift in the direction of effect.
Despite these outcome-level shifts, overall pooled estimates showed no statistically significant difference between new meta-analyses and alternative overlap strategies. The combined ratio of odds ratios was 1.01 with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.83 to 1.24, and the difference in standardised mean differences was −0.20 with a 95 percent confidence interval from −0.44 to 0.05. The authors concluded that although alternative strategies produce similar overall pooled estimates, they affect the comprehensiveness and interpretation of individual outcomes, underscoring the necessity of careful selection and transparent reporting. The study protocol was registered on the Open Science Framework under the identifier https://osf.io/w7rzh.
