TITLE: Apple lets Mac users limit apps to chosen folders, not the whole disk
OUTLET: PPC Land

Apple told Mac developers on November 16, 2026, that macOS 27.3 beta 2 changes how two of the most far-reaching permissions on the Mac are granted. Device Control and Data Access, the setting introduced with macOS 27 that governs apps controlling other apps, is split into System & App Protection Override and Accessibility. Full Disk Access requests now steer users toward granting only the folders an app needs. Users can make an app ask every time, and an indicator appears when one app is controlling another. The notice, titled "Updates to Device Control and Data Access, and Full Disk Access in macOS," arrives 45 days after Apple warned that the risks of Full Disk Access would grow as AI agents became more autonomous.
In Short
Apple's macOS 27.3 beta 2, released to developers on November 16, 2026, changes how Mac users hand apps two sweeping powers: control over other apps, and Full Disk Access. The change reaches AI agents, automation tools, backup software and security products that depend on those permissions, and the people and companies who install them. New permission requests will push users toward granting specific apps or folders instead of everything, while permissions already granted stay in place.
What Apple published
The notice sits on Apple's developer news site, dated November 16, 2026. It runs to about 350 words and names no app, developer or company. It opens on a conciliatory note about the Mac's standing with people who "build, automate, and innovate using cutting-edge technologies and tools," before getting to the point.
"In macOS 27.3 beta 2 today, we're introducing increased transparency and more granular options for two of the APIs: Device Control and Data Access, and Full Disk Access," Apple wrote. The justification follows in one sentence: "Before granting an app such extraordinary access, users should understand that it puts all of the data on their Mac at risk."
Apple frames the changes as a matter of informed consent. They are, in the company's words, "designed to help them do that and to empower them to make informed decisions about their own data and privacy." Nothing in the November text mentions artificial intelligence. That is a change of emphasis from October, when AI agents were the stated reason for acting.
The six changes, in Apple's words
Apple lists six changes. The wording below is copied from the notice; the right-hand column records which permission each item refers to, based on the notice's own text.
Three of the six concern the moment of consent: one way to grant, more information before granting, and a nudge toward granting less. One concerns duration, through the ask-every-time option. One concerns what happens afterwards, through the indicator. The remaining item, the split, changes the map of settings itself.
One switch becomes two
Device Control and Data Access is a young setting. Before macOS 27, software that needed to observe or operate other apps asked for the Accessibility permission under Privacy & Security, according to Microsoft documentation for its Purview Endpoint data loss prevention product, last updated August 26, 2026. In macOS 27, Microsoft wrote, that capability moved under Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Device Control and Data Access. Administrators on the Jamf Nation community forum reported new Device Control and Data Access pop-ups on August 13, 2026, during macOS 27 beta 5; one reply said the change appeared in the beta 5 release notes. By macOS 27.2, the setting was a single unified toggle in System Settings, according to 9to5Mac.
Microsoft's documentation gives a sense of what that toggle unlocks. When the permission is off, Endpoint DLP can no longer obtain URL and browser context for Safari, Chrome and Firefox, according to Microsoft. In other words, the same permission that lets assistive software read a screen also lets a program see which web pages are open in other apps.
macOS 27.3 beta 2 separates those uses. The System & App Protection Override setting covers one app controlling another, and it is the setting the new indicator watches. The Accessibility setting returns as a separate entry. Apple's entitlement paragraph describes it as "the new Accessibility settings to provide assistive technologies," which ties the name back to its original purpose. The indicator appears in the Mac menu bar, according to MacRumors.
The split matters for any software that leaned on the old Accessibility route without being an assistive tool. That group includes computer use agents, which operate apps by reading the screen and clicking, as well as automation utilities and security products. The notice does not say which of the two new settings such software should request.
Full Disk Access, one folder at a time
Full Disk Access was the subject of Apple's October 2 notice, "Updates to Full Disk Access in macOS." In it, Apple said the permission largely bypasses the controls that protect private data so that backup apps can work. It added that some developers were using it in ways that could expose files, mail, messages and browsing history without users fully understanding what they had agreed to. Apple promised additional controls so that only deliberate, explicit action by a user could grant such access, but gave no date and no software version.
Part of that arrived before the November notice. In macOS 27.3 beta 1, released on November 2, apps could no longer add themselves to the Full Disk Access list for users to switch on, according to Macworld. Users instead had to open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Full Disk Access, and add the app manually. Macworld also cited a developer who described a folder-by-folder permission called Private Folders, configured like Full Disk Access, which did not yet appear to be fully working in that build.
Beta 2 adds the guidance Apple describes: users are encouraged to grant Full Disk Access to the specific folders a workflow requires. A user can choose to give an app access to specific folders instead of the entire Mac, according to 9to5Mac. The same outlet gave an example of the app-level choice under System & App Protection Override: an app can be allowed to work with Mail but not with Messages.
The prompt itself has become more explicit about what is at stake. macOS 27.3 displays a more detailed explanation of the protections being bypassed and the types of personal information that could be exposed, according to 9to5Mac. Macworld reproduced a screenshot, posted on X on November 16, of the ChatGPT app requesting access. The listed categories included reading, changing, deleting and exporting all data, viewing hidden and recently deleted photos, and viewing history and browsing as the user, and each type could be allowed or denied separately, according to Macworld.
How the new permission process works
According to Apple's notice, supplemented by reporting on the beta, a permission request in macOS 27.3 beta 2 moves through these stages:
- Trigger. The changes apply "when approving new app permission requests." Grants made before the update are untouched.
- Grant method. Apple describes "a single method for granting permissions by selecting the requesting app from a list." In beta 1, Full Disk Access required adding the app manually at System Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Full Disk Access, according to Macworld.
- Disclosure. Before a full grant, the system shows "the wide range of data apps will receive access to or control over," per Apple. Reports describe category-by-category descriptions of the data involved.
- Scope. The guidance points users toward "just the specific apps (for System & App Protection Override) or folders (for Full Disk Access) that are required for their workflows." Full permission remains available.
- Duration. Users can "configure apps to ask every time they require access," instead of granting standing access.
- Monitoring. When an app controls another app through System & App Protection Override, a transparency indicator appears, placed in the menu bar according to MacRumors.
- Exceptions. Developers of disk backup apps using Full Disk Access, and of assistive technologies using the new Accessibility setting, "can request entitlements to deliver the same experience for new installations that they offered before macOS 27.3." For now, "known apps aren't affected and will keep the same experience without an entitlement."
What developers are told
The instruction to developers is one sentence long: "Developers should be prepared to adjust how they onboard users and make sure their apps can gracefully handle having access to only a limited set of folders or apps when utilizing these APIs."
That sentence carries two separate burdens. Onboarding flows that walk a new user through switching on Full Disk Access, often with screenshots of System Settings, will need rewriting. And software written on the assumption that it can see everything must now cope with partial access, whether that is a backup tool that can reach only some folders or an agent allowed to drive only some apps.
The entitlement route is narrow. Apple names two categories: disk backup, and assistive technologies. No category is named for automation tools, security software, data loss prevention products or AI agents. The exemption for "known apps" is undefined and, by Apple's own word "Currently," provisional. "Additional entitlement details will be available soon," Apple wrote, without a date.
Managed fleets are not mentioned at all. Companies typically pre-approve these permissions on employees' Macs through mobile device management profiles, and Jamf administrators said in August that they saw no way to manage the Device Control and Data Access pop-ups introduced in beta 5. The November notice says nothing on whether administrator-granted permissions follow the new rules. PPC Land raised the same question about Apple's October Full Disk Access notice, and it remains open.
From warning to beta in 45 days
The October notice, dated October 2, 2026, was short and undated in its consequences. MacRumors published it that day alongside the observation that it arrived amid always-on AI agents such as Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots. Apple named neither.
The background was a dispute. Inc. columnist Jason Aten said Meta's Muse had synced about 187,000 lines of his local Messages database although he had kept Full Disk Access switched off, as AppleInsider reported on September 28, 2026. Meta's communications chief Andy Stone said the Messages integration in the Muse Mac app was opt-in and worked only with both Full Disk Access and an in-app Messages connector enabled. PPC Land set out both accounts on October 4; the sources did not reconcile them. Thirteen days after Apple's notice, Apple and Meta worked together to run Meta's Muse Spark Open model on two linked Mac Studios.
What changed between the two texts is specificity. October stated a problem and an intention. November names settings, a version, an exception process and a rule for existing grants. Apple has not said when macOS 27.3 will reach the public; Macworld expects it in early December.
Why this matters for the marketing community
The Mac has become a place where marketing work is handed to software agents. Meta connected Muse to ad accounts and 15 business tools on September 29. Google brought Gemini Spark to the Mac on June 30 for Ultra subscribers, with file sorting among its functions. Apple's own Keynote, Pages and Numbers let outside AI agents read content, make edits and run actions on macOS 27. Each of these is more useful the more it can reach. macOS 27.3 does not take that reach away, but it makes every new grant slower, narrower by default and more visible.
