TITLE: Former Bishop of Liverpool to chair government review into impact of nuclear weapons testing
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Former Bishop of Liverpool, Rt Rev Bishop James Jones has been announced as the chair of an independent government review into the impact of UK nuclear weapons tests on veterans and families.
Bishop James, whom also led the milestone Hillsborough Independent Review, will chair the Independent Panel into Nuclear Test Veterans to investigate the impacts on veterans and families of the UK's nuclear tests.
The panel is working towards presenting an interim report for the 75th anniversary of the UK's first nuclear test on 3 October 1952 in Western Australia.
Nine countries are known to possess nuclear weapons today - the UK became only the third nuclear power in 1952.
The PM is backing Bishop James to be "relentless" and determined to find the truth, examining blood tests of those potentially impacted.
Prime Minister Andy Burnham said: “This is a campaign close to my heart, and I pay tribute to the courage and dedication of the community and campaigners. This announcement is the result of their tireless work.
“Nuclear test veterans and their families have waited too long for the truth, and that’s exactly what the Independent Panel will establish.
“Bishop James Jones will be relentless in his pursuit of the facts, bringing unparalleled independence, determination and expertise, and I’m delighted he has agreed to act as chair.
“My message to the community is this: we will be led by the truth, and I am clear that the government must act on the recommendations of the panel.”
In the meantime, Rt Rev Bishop John Arnold, Bishop of Bolton, has criticised the government for acquiring fighter jets capable of deploying nuclear weapons, saying that it slides back on commitments to gradually disarm the UK of nuclear weaponry under the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty.
The national president of Pax Christi England & Wales, a Christian non-violence campaign group, has supported the new Treaty for the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons (TPNW), which will be reviewed by the United Nations for the first time at the end of this month.
The Catholic Bishops of England & Wales are supporting the treaty which has been signed by 96 countries inclduing the Vatican.
Bishop John said: "Marking the anniversary of the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, Pope Leo stressed that 'true peace demands the courageous laying down of weapons - especially those with power to cause an indescribable catastrophe.
"Through prayer and public witness, we seek for the UK to sign and ratify the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons, and until this point, engage meaningfully with the treaty framework including participating as an observer in future meetings of signatories."
