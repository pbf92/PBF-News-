TITLE: ChatGPT ad reports add 9 metrics built on how many people see the ads
OUTLET: PPC Land

OpenAI on November 25, 2026 told advertisers that ChatGPT's ad reports can now estimate how many different people saw their ads, and how many times each of those people saw them on average. Until then, Ads Manager counted impressions and clicks - the number of times an ad was shown and the number of times it was clicked - but not the people behind those counts.
The change came in an email titled "ChatGPT Ads Product Update," which said the week's changes offer more ways to "connect with customers, understand ad reach, and manage product-feed promotions." It listed five updates. Business Agents, the branded chat assistants that companies can build inside ChatGPT, are now open to eligible businesses outside the United States. Single image ads carry a "Learn more" link by default. Advertisers running ads from a product catalog can hide sale prices once a promotion is over. And a rejected ad now comes with more detail on which policy applied. Barry Schwartz of Search Engine Roundtable published the email at 5:40 pm US Eastern time the same day.
In Short
OpenAI's ad tool for ChatGPT now estimates how many different people saw an ad and how often, along with seven related numbers, in Ads Manager and through its Ads API. That matters to any business buying ChatGPT ads, because until now the reports counted showings and clicks but not people, so a large audience and a small one seeing the same ad again and again could look alike. The same update opens Business Agents to some countries outside the US, puts a "Learn more" link on single image ads, lets shops hide expired sale prices and explains ad rejections in more detail, while OpenAI says the approval rules themselves have not changed.
The five changes
From showings to people
An impression is one showing of an ad. If one person sees the same ad five times, that is five impressions but one person. Since ChatGPT Ads Manager opened to US businesses in May, its reports measured only the first of those two things.
The two lead metrics in the update are reach and frequency, the standard measures in television and online video buying. According to OpenAI, Reach "estimates how many unique people saw your ads at least once" during the reporting date range an advertiser selects. Frequency "estimates how many times those people saw your ads on average, calculated by dividing total impressions by Reach for the same period."
A simple example shows the arithmetic. An ad shown 10,000 times to an estimated 2,500 people has a frequency of 4. The same 10,000 impressions spread over 9,000 people would give a frequency of about 1.1. Both campaigns look identical in an impressions column. (These figures are PPC Land's illustration, not OpenAI data.)
OpenAI uses the word "estimates" for both metrics. The email does not explain how the estimate is built, or how a unique person is identified - by account, by device or by some other signal.
Seven more metrics arrived with the first two: Click Reach, Click Frequency, Total Reach, Total Frequency, Unique CTR, Unique CPC and Cost per 1,000 Reached. The email names them without defining them. Read by their names alone, Click Reach and Click Frequency would apply the same people-based counting to those who clicked. Unique CTR would be a people-based version of the click-through rate, the share of showings that end in a click, and Unique CPC a cost per person who clicked rather than per click. Cost per 1,000 Reached would be the price of reaching a thousand different people, the people-based counterpart of the cost per 1,000 impressions that Ads Manager already reports. That reading is PPC Land's, not OpenAI's, and what separates Total Reach from Reach is not explained at all.
OpenAI's help documentation had not caught up by the evening of November 25. Its "Measure Results" page, updated that day, still listed seven reporting metrics - impressions, clicks, spend, click-through rate, average cost per click, average cost per 1,000 impressions and conversions - and none of the new ones.
Reporting in ChatGPT Ads Manager over time
The May entries come from PPC Land's report on the day OpenAI opened ChatGPT Ads Manager to all US businesses with cost-per-click bidding, which listed impressions, clicks, spend, click-through rate, average cost per click, average cost per 1,000 impressions and conversions, and made no mention of reach or frequency. In August, ChatGPT Ads added view-through conversions with a fixed one-day window, kept out of the main conversion total and limited to eligible accounts.
Why a people count matters
Until now, the only reach figures around ChatGPT advertising came from outside OpenAI. When Similarweb and Graphite released "The State of ChatGPT Ads" in late September, PPC Land's coverage of the study noted that its reach numbers - 43.4% ad-eligible reach in the US, 45.7% in the UK - came from a panel of users and measured ad-eligible reach across ChatGPT, not the reach of any one advertiser's campaign. The new metrics come from OpenAI's own delivery data and apply to each advertiser's own ads.
Third-party measurement has been OpenAI's other answer. In October, OpenAI named 20 measurement partners for ChatGPT Ads, from attribution firms to incrementality testers, with results that were reported by the vendors themselves and came without spend figures or sample sizes. Reach and frequency fill a more basic gap. A brand that buys television, YouTube or Meta campaigns plans on how many people it reaches and how often; until November 25, ChatGPT reports could not be set against those plans.
The numbers also bear on cost. A campaign with a high frequency is spending part of its budget on people who have already seen the ad several times. Without a frequency figure, an advertiser had no direct way to see that inside Ads Manager.
Granularity remains an open question. In August, ChatGPT Ads added targeting for iOS apps, Android apps and the web while reporting stayed at two device groups, Mobile and Desktop. The email does not say whether reach can be split by device, country or audience, or whether it removes duplicates across campaigns.
Developers get the same numbers. The email says the metrics are available "in Ads Manager and the Ads API," OpenAI's programming interface for running campaigns through software rather than the web dashboard. The interface already carried campaign management functions: in August, OpenAI moved ChatGPT ad budgets from fixed daily caps to seven-day averages in the same email that announced a Bulk API through the Ads API. Reporting tools such as Supermetrics, which added ChatGPT Ads as its seventh ad platform on November 10, draw on that kind of access. The email does not give the API field names.
Business Agents leave the United States
Fifteen days separate the two announcements. On November 10, OpenAI opened Businesses in ChatGPT, and businesses gained click and mention counts in OpenAI's Business Hub. Business Agents - assistants that answer questions about a company's products in a separate, labeled conversation - were then limited to eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the US.
The November 25 email widens that. "Eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in supported markets outside the U.S. can now create and publish a Business Agent in Business Hub," according to OpenAI. People using ChatGPT can choose to chat with such an agent to learn about a company's products and services.
Which markets are supported? The email does not say. As of the evening of November 25, OpenAI's help page "Setting up your business on ChatGPT," also updated that day, still described business agents as available to eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the US, and said availability depends on a business's location and type.
The paid route sits behind two conditions. "Advertisers with access to the feature who meet our ad policy requirements can also add their agent to an eligible campaign in Ads Manager as a Sponsored Business Agent," the email says. OpenAI began testing Sponsored Agents with select US advertisers on September 16, where a click on the ad opens a separate, labeled conversation with the advertiser's agent.
OpenAI's help page on setting up a Sponsored Business Agent, which the email links to, adds detail. According to that page, the feature is rolling out to eligible advertisers in Ads Manager over the coming weeks. Only campaigns with the Conversion optimization objective can use it. There is no separate charge for agent chats; the campaign's billing settings apply. People who see the ad can either start a chat or visit the website. An Insights tab shows patterns once an agent has had more than 25 conversations. The page also says ads do not influence ChatGPT's answers.
One detail sits awkwardly with the email. The email speaks of advertisers adding "their agent" to a campaign, which reads like the agent built in Business Hub. The help page says agents built in Business Hub and in Ads Manager do not sync, and the sponsored version is drafted in Ads Manager's own Agent builder. Whether a business outside the US needs both, and whether the advertising side is open outside the US at all, is not spelled out.
A Learn more link, on by default
Single image ads now show a "Learn more" link below the description by default, according to OpenAI. The link opens the landing page already set in Ads Manager, "so no additional setup is required." OpenAI's example ad, for a shop called Heirloom Grocery, has the headline "A fresh idea for tonight," the description "Make dinner with ripe tomatoes and fresh basil," and the Learn more link with an arrow beneath.
The ad unit itself has changed little since spring. When Ads Manager opened to US businesses in May, an ad consisted of the advertiser name, a small site icon, a headline of about 16 characters, a description of about 32, an image and a landing page, and no button was described. Weeks later, ChatGPT Ads Manager tested dynamic CTAs - buttons reading Shop Now, Book Now, Sign Up and Learn More - on a small share of ads, with OpenAI choosing the wording automatically. The mock-ups for the ad format OpenAI tested during ChatGPT image generation in October also showed a "Learn more" button.
The November version is narrower and fixed: one wording, on one format, on by default. The email does not say whether advertisers can turn it off, choose other wording, or see clicks on the link reported separately from clicks elsewhere on the ad.
Hiding sale prices after a promotion
Retailers that build ChatGPT ads from a product feed - the file listing each item with its title, image, price and stock - can now stop showing a sale price without touching that file. According to OpenAI, sale prices can be hidden in Ads Manager for a product-feed ad group, so the ads show the regular price again. Both prices can stay in the feed.
Feed ads have carried discounts for months. ChatGPT Ads Manager added product feeds in June, after OpenAI closed in-chat checkout. In August, an OpenAI email showing a carousel test displayed prices such as $1,299.00 against an earlier $1,899.00, according to PPC Land's report on how ChatGPT advertisers faced 10 days to opt out of automatic advanced matching. The higher figure in such a pair is the reference price, the benchmark a discount is judged against.
Google deals with stale discounts through rules rather than a switch. PPC Land reported on November 22 that Merchant Center sales running past 30 days may miss Google's sale badge, after Google's help page added a note that a sale price should not run longer than 30 days to count as a temporary reduction. OpenAI's email sets no such limit; it gives the advertiser a setting.
The timing is plain on the calendar. The email arrived the day before US Thanksgiving, November 26, and two days before Black Friday, when many retailers run short promotions. The email itself does not mention the holidays.
