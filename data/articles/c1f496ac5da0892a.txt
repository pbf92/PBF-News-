TITLE: Advertised product brand
OUTLET: PPC Land

Advertised product brand is a reporting dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that records the brand of the product an ad promoted. Each product sold on Amazon carries a brand name in its catalogue record. When a report is broken down by advertised product, this field attaches that brand to every row, so impressions, clicks, spend and sales can be totalled by brand rather than product by product. It exists because one account often promotes hundreds of products across several brands, and budgets and client contracts are usually set by brand.
The field sits on the promoted side of the ledger. It describes what the advertiser chose to advertise, not what the shopper eventually bought. On Amazon, a click on one product often ends in the purchase of another.
Where the field sits
Amazon's dimensions reference for unified reporting, updated on November 11, 2026, lists 156 dimension attributes across 11 categories. One of those categories is Product, with 18 attributes split into two sets of nine. PPC Land's reading of the reference lists the advertised set as ID, name, parent ID, brand, category, subcategory, product group, marketplace and SKU, and reports that these apply to DVA+, Sponsored Display and Sponsored Products, but not to Sponsored Brands or Sponsored TV. The nine converted product attributes, the mirror image describing what was purchased, apply to all five ad products.
The field therefore covers Sponsored Products (cost-per-click listing ads), Sponsored Display (self-service display ads) and DVA+, the campaign type announced on September 29, 2026 that merges Amazon DSP, Sponsored Display and Sponsored TV buying. Amazon's reference states that "when a dimension does not apply to an ad product, its value appears as null", so a Sponsored Brands row shows an empty cell.
The reference gives Advertised product brand no former name. Its neighbour Advertised product ID was called "Advertised ASIN" in the legacy sponsored ads reports; ASIN stands for Amazon Standard Identification Number, the 10-character code Amazon assigns to each catalogue item. The old DSP console's "Brand name" dimension maps to the converted product set, not the advertised one.
How the value is populated
Amazon has not published a field-level definition, but adjacent documentation shows the mechanics. The chain starts with the catalogue. A product listing carries a brand attribute set by the brand owner or seller. Brands enrolled in Brand Registry, Amazon's programme for verified trademark owners, gain control over that attribute; unbranded goods may be listed as generic, as Amazon describes in its guidance for sellers of generic products. An advertiser then picks the products a campaign will promote. In DVA+, sellers pick ASINs as the "promoted item" during setup.
When a report is generated at advertised-product grain, each row is keyed on the promoted ASIN, and the brand field is filled from that ASIN's catalogue record. Amazon's 2022 self-service DSP reporting guide states the rule plainly for the older console: "A brand is identified by the name in the byline", the brand line printed under a product title on the detail page.
A worked example: a household goods company runs one Sponsored Products campaign promoting six ASINs, four under its "Brightwash" detergent brand and two under its "Edge" razor brand. Six rows by Advertised product ID roll up into two by brand: Brightwash with 1.2 million impressions and $8,400 in spend, Edge with 300,000 impressions and $2,100. Without the column, that split needs an external ASIN-to-brand table. The figures are illustrative.
The field does not change which sales are credited. A Brightwash row can still include razor sales, depending on the metric selected; the label stays fixed to the promoted product.
Promoted, halo and converted
Amazon's attribution separates sales of the exact promoted product from sales of other products of the same brand, known as brand halo. The 2022 DSP guide says brand halo "expands attribution to all ASINs that have the same brand name(s) as the ASINs tracked by the order", and that halo metrics measure "how a campaign contributed to an overall lift in brand-wide conversion events". Total sales combine the two.
Sponsored Products and Sponsored Display used a similar split. Amazon's help page for the Sponsored Display advertised product report defines its 14-day click-attributed total sales as covering "promoted products and products from the same brands as the promoted products". The Sponsored Products purchased product report captures what Amazon now labels "Sales (halo)": purchases within seven days of a click "where the purchased SKU was other than the one advertised", formerly called "7 day other SKU sales".
Brand therefore does two jobs: it labels a row, and it decides which purchases count as halo. Advertised product brand exposes only the first.
Origin and evolution
Product-level reports predate unified reporting by years. The Sponsored Products and Sponsored Display advertised product reports carried Advertised ASIN and Advertised SKU columns but no brand column, according to Amazon's help pages and Funnel's field list, updated August 31, 2026. Agencies joined report data to catalogue exports to get brand totals.
Amazon DSP took a different route. Its product reports included Brand name, Parent ASIN, Product category, Product subcategory, Product group and an "ASIN conversion type" column distinguishing promoted from brand halo conversions, according to Amazon's August 2022 DSP reporting guide and Funnel's DSP field list.
Unified reporting brought the two traditions together. Amazon launched the system in open beta on November 11, 2025 at unBoxed. It reached general availability in the console on June 8, 2026, when PPC Land reported a template gallery that included Advertised Product and Converted Product templates. The November 11, 2026 dimensions reference is the first public listing of Advertised product brand by name.
Why it matters
The brand is where most advertising decisions on Amazon are made. Budgets and agency fees are set by brand, and Brand Registry ties formats and metrics to a registered brand. New-to-brand metrics, which count shoppers who had not bought from a brand in the previous 12 months, are also defined at brand level.
Timing matters too. Amazon's Support Center page, updated November 12, 2026, says the legacy sponsored ads and DVA report pages become read-only on December 17, 2026 and shut down on January 31, 2027, with saved reports and history deleted.
Comparing the two product sets is the analytical use. Grouping by Advertised product brand and then by Converted product brand shows how much of one brand's advertising ends in purchases of another brand from the same owner.
Limitations and disputes
The definition gap is the first problem. Amazon's public reference does not define the field, state which catalogue attribute it reads, or say whether the value is captured at the time of the impression or at report time.
Coverage is contested. PPC Land's count of the November reference shows the advertised product set unavailable for Sponsored Brands, while its June report on general availability listed Sponsored Brands among the products covered by the Advertised Product template. Sponsored Brands ads also feature several products at once; since January 2026, product collections ads require between three and 10 ASINs. A reply in the discussion forum of Amazon's advanced tools documentation repository in October 2023 said there was "no ASIN-grain reporting for Sponsored Brands since there can be multiple ASINs in a single ad", which is a plausible reason for the null.
Brand data is uneven. The 2022 DSP guide warns that if an ASIN's "brand is not specified or registered with a different brand", other brands can appear in halo reporting. Inconsistent spellings and generic listings could likewise split or merge brand rows.
Advertisers that do not sell on Amazon have no catalogue brand. DVA+ campaigns for such brands promote a landing page URL and an off-Amazon conversion event, so the field has nothing to read.
Sponsored TV support is unresolved. Amazon's page says it does "not support Streaming TV Smart mode/ Sponsored TV yet", while marking many dimensions for Sponsored TV, a contradiction PPC Land documented.
Advertised product brand is not...
Converted product brand. The converted field describes the brand of the product a shopper bought after an ad interaction. One click on a Brightwash ad that ends in an Edge razor purchase produces Brightwash on the advertised side and Edge on the converted side.
Benchmark brand name. The benchmark field names the "focus brand" a peer group was built for, derived from the promoted products. It labels a competitive comparison, not each promoted product, and has a companion identifier, Benchmark brand ID.
Brand halo. Halo is a class of attributed sales, purchases of non-promoted products from the same brand. Advertised product brand is a label on a row. The brand sets the perimeter for halo, but the dimension does not measure it.
Recent developments
The field arrived as part of a dense autumn for Amazon reporting. PPC Land's analysis of the November 11 reference found that 85 of the 156 dimensions apply to Sponsored Products, against 137 for DVA+, 81 for Sponsored Display and 73 for Sponsored Brands.
DVA+ is still in closed beta in the United States, with general availability in 35 markets targeted for the first quarter of 2027. The Reporting API and Amazon Marketing Stream versions of unified reporting remained in beta as of November 2026, so the API column name for the brand field and its behaviour in automated pipelines have not been confirmed publicly.
