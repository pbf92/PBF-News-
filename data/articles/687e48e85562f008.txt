TITLE: From 1956 to 1971 to Today
OUTLET: Christianity Today

“Between my husband and I, we have subscribed to Christianity Today since 1971,” Cheryl Stacey tells me. “I have been a longtime supporter of Christianity Today.”
Cheryl Stacey and her late husband, Dennis, have subscribed to Christianity Today for 55 years. “[During] one of our moves, I found ten boxes of old Christianity Today journals because he wouldn’t throw them away, but I think they have been digitized since then,” she said, chuckling. Their oldest subscriptions are in Dennis’s name.
Fifty-five years is a long time to read any publication. But for Cheryl, those years represent much more than a subscription. They represent a lifetime of questions asked, ideas explored, faith deepened, and conversations shared. Christianity Today was there as she and Dennis began their life together, and it has remained a steady presence through the many seasons that followed.
Over the years, the Staceys cultivated a passion for apologetics, for C. S. Lewis, and for Christian journalism. Their Christianity Today subscription began around the same time as their marriage.
“My husband was still in college; we were newlyweds. He was president of InterVarsity Fellowship at Western Michigan University, and we were also involved in a Christian bookstore startup,” Cheryl says. “At that time, we were collecting books and reading all of C. S. Lewis and John Stott and J. I. Packer, etc., and Christianity Today just became part of that whole approach to thoughtful Christianity. … It was all a part of the beginning of our life together as well as with our journey with Jesus.”
In those early years, reading CT was something they did together. They were building a library, wrestling with big questions, and developing a faith that welcomed thoughtful engagement with the world around them. Christianity Today became part of that shared pursuit—not simply something to read, but a resource that helped shape the conversations and convictions that would define their life together.
Dennis passed away 15 years ago. He had a passion for authentic and meditative Christianity. Cheryl said about him, “Part of his whole identity was around reasoning together, why is it that you believe, and answering questions thoughtfully. I think that informed the rest of our life in terms of how we interact with other believers and how we interact with the world. And Christianity Today has always been there and always been a part of that.”
For the past few years, Cheryl has been a part of The Thread, CT’s community of partners who give monthly or quarterly. Members of The Thread gain access to monthly CT Partners updates, prayer requests, organizational updates, and invites to CT webcasts.
Over the decades, Cheryl’s relationship with Christianity Today has evolved. What began as a subscription shared by two newlyweds has become something she now helps sustain. Having spent much of her life benefiting from CT’s journalism and its commitment to thoughtful Christian conversation, she sees an opportunity to ensure that resources remain available for others.
For Cheryl, it’s a joy to be able to give money now in retirement, especially as she reflects on being a young newlywed wanting to make enough extra income to subscribe: “Even when we didn’t have any money. We used to have to scrape the money together [for our subscription].”
Beyond her giving, Cheryl leans on Christianity Today’s place in the world to continue the conversations she and her husband engaged in so deeply throughout their marriage. She also appreciates looking back through the archives to remember the conversations in the Christian faith that were happening in the past. She remembers how intentionally Christianity Today covered the inerrancy of Scripture in the early years of their subscription, as well as the ongoing coverage of pro-life issues.
“I think CT is a consistent voice in a world that is polarizing people of faith. Society today is trying to polarize us around cultural ideas and not bring us together in our faith. That is of great concern to me.”
