Man United make two rare transfer moves as Mauricio Pochettino and board 'reach agreement'
Manchester United are expected to be active in the January transfer window after coming to an agreement with new boss Mauricio Pochettino
Mauricio Pochettino and Manchester United's board agree that the club's squad must be improved as they fight to emerge from the Premier League's relegation zone. United sit in 19th place, having won just once in 11 matches.
Pochettino's side will prioritise adding a striker during the January transfer window, with United's only out-and-out forward Benjamin Sesko sidelined until next year with a shin injury. The club have so far utilised the likes of Bryan Mbeumo and Matheus Cunha to lead the forward line.
However, United could also look to sign a left-back in the coming weeks, reports The Times, having been unable to add in that area over the summer, with Newcastle reluctant to sanction the sale of Lewis Hall. Pochettino is noted as an admirer of Luke Shaw, who he worked with at Southampton, but the 31-year-old has missed the last eight matches.

Barcelona's Alejandro Balde is noted as a potential target after the club looked at the Spaniard over the summer. The 23-year-old has fallen behind Joao Cancelo at Camp Nou and is considering an exit after failing to start a match this term.
United have also been linked to making a move in the centre of defence. Fulham star Calvin Bassey has been touted as a potential target, but the Nigeria international has opened talks over a new deal at Craven Cottage.
The Old Trafford club's intention to move in January runs counter to its preferences, having not operated in the winter window in 2026, 2024, or 2022. United have focused on the summer for their transfer activity in the main.
In 2019/20, United signed Bruno Fernandes to make an immediate impact, while Odion Ighalo joined on loan. The following year, Amad arrived from Atalanta as an 18-year-old prospect.
Two years later, Wout Weghorst, Marcel Sabitzer and Jack Butland came into Old Trafford during a busy window in Erik ten Hag's first season at the club. It was then two years before Patrick Dorgu arrived at the club from Serie A side Lecce.
And another two years on, Pochettino is hopeful of an injection of quality and wants to be involved in United's transfer dealings despite his title of head coach rather than manager.
"I was head coach, coach, manager, and in the end it's not changing too much, because I think the way that we work is indifferent to the title description," Pochettino said.
"I know that being a head coach, I am the guy that is going to decide the squad for Sunday and I'm going to pick the starting XI.
"Of course that in the future, I would like to be involved in all the main decisions that will happen on the squad. That is in the end, Manchester United is a football club, no?"
