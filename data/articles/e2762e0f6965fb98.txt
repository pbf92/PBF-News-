TITLE: AI system developed to recommend content for improving emotional health
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Researchers at the Universidad Carlos III de Madrid (UC3M), in collaboration with the company Psiconnea, have developed an artificial intelligence (AI)-based system capable of detecting emotional patterns in individuals and recommending tailored content to improve their emotional well-being. The platform, available via the EMOTINET app, enables users to perform self-assessments to spot early signs of psychological distress and—through exercises, visualizations, and professionally guided series—overcome mental blocks, reduce anxiety, and gain emotional clarity.
The contribution of UC3M's Robotics Lab to this university-to-society knowledge transfer project consists of a recommendation system similar to those used by streaming platforms, adapted specifically to the mental health field. "Just as multimedia platforms recommend series and movies based on our preferences and viewing history, this system works in a similar way," explains José Carlos Castillo, a researcher in the Social Robotics group at UC3M's Robotics Lab.
To achieve this, the app developed by Psiconnea (a Spanish company of psychotechnologists specializing in emotional health) processes various types of data, including the user's psychological profile, their interaction with the platform, and their self-reported needs. The AI system designed by UC3M integrates all this information to generate personalized multimedia recommendations. Specifically, these consist of brief explanatory videos—acting as "micro-learning modules"—that help users better address their specific challenges and have been pre-validated by Psiconnea.
A First Step, Not a Substitute for a Professional
The researchers emphasize that the tool is not intended to replace mental health professionals. "It really serves as a preliminary step: when someone senses they have a problem, the app allows them to evaluate it and offers an initial approach. If the issue persists or cannot be resolved directly, the system facilitates referral to a specialist and access to a consultation," clarifies José Carlos Castillo.
EMOTINET also features emotional self-assessments, psychologist-guided exercises, and an anonymous support community. According to Psiconnea CEO Rosa Becerril, more than 80,000 people have used the platform, and 80% of users reduce their levels of stress, anxiety, or depression.
The UC3M team highlights the value of this type of collaboration. "We are deeply grateful for the company's commitment to university knowledge transfer, because for us, these synergies are key: universities hold vast amounts of knowledge that need to reach society, which is often no easy task. Through initiatives like this, we can translate research into something tangible that directly helps people," concludes José Carlos Castillo.
