TITLE: Fecal Transplants Aid Colitis Remission but Reviews Heavily Overstate Trial Evidence
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Fecal microbiota transplantation may improve short-term clinical remission in selected adults with active ulcerative colitis, but the sheer volume of published reviews substantially overstates the independent evidence, according to an umbrella review published in BMC Gastroenterology. Investigators led by Assylzhan M. Messova and colleagues evaluated methodological quality, primary-study overlap, efficacy, safety, and certainty across existing systematic reviews, assessing adult and paediatric evidence separately.
The authors noted that multiple meta-analyses have evaluated fecal microbiota transplantation for ulcerative colitis, but these papers repeatedly include the exact same primary trials. This pattern prompted the researchers to assess whether the apparent abundance of scientific literature reflects genuine clinical replication or redundant reporting, while examining methodological quality, primary-study duplication, and treatment outcomes across both adult and paediatric patient populations.
To establish the evidence base, the investigators conducted an umbrella review of systematic reviews with quantitative synthesis reporting ulcerative colitis-specific outcomes. Searches across PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, and Cochrane Library CENTRAL covered publications from 1 January 2020 through 28 February 2026. In addition, the team conducted a supplementary verification search of the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews.
The initial electronic searches identified 525 records. Following the removal of 182 duplicates, 343 records were screened. The researchers sought full reports for 41 publications; four were not retrieved and 37 were formally assessed, resulting in 11 exclusions. Ultimately, 26 eligible reviews were identified through database searches, and one current Cochrane update was carried forward manually, yielding a total of 27 included reviews.
Methodological quality was appraised using the AMSTAR 2 measurement instrument. Methodological confidence proved notably weak across the literature, rating as high for only one review, low for seven reviews, and critically low for 19 reviews. Certainty ratings were extracted from the Cochrane review. Because study overlap and clinical heterogeneity precluded valid statistical re-pooling, the authors synthesized all outcome estimates narratively.
Overlap of primary studies was measured using the corrected covered area metric after consolidating companion reports. In the adult literature matrix, 18 reviews encompassed 25 unique primary studies across 149 occurrences, generating a corrected covered area of 29.18 percent, categorized as very high overlap. In paediatric literature, six comparative reviews reused a single trial, yielding a 100 percent corrected covered area, whereas two non-comparative reviews included four distinct studies without overlap.
Across the reviewed literature, syntheses generally favoured fecal microbiota transplantation for short-term adult clinical remission. For example, a 2025 review by Zhang T favoured adjunctive transplantation for clinical response with a relative risk of 1.71, clinical remission with a relative risk of 1.92, and endoscopic remission with a relative risk of 1.81. Crucially, however, that review added no new adult trial family to the evidence base.
The team concluded that while transplantation may improve short-term remission in selected adults with active ulcerative colitis, endoscopic healing, uncommon serious harms, long-term maintenance, paediatric use, and protocol superiority remain uncertain. Future trials and reviews must standardize protocols and outcomes, prospectively register methods, consolidate companion reports, and account for overlap and bias. The review was retrospectively registered on Open Science Framework under doi10.17605/OSF.IO/PW7DJ on 10 March 2026.
