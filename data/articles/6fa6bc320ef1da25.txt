TITLE: Anthropic Prices IPO at $83, Topping Range on AI Frenzy
OUTLET: The Tech Buzz

Anthropic just priced its initial public offering at $83 per share, above the expected $70 to $78 range, according to a source familiar with the matter who spoke to CNBC. The AI safety startup's stock will begin trading Thursday morning on the Nasdaq under ticker symbol ANT, capping one of the most anticipated tech debuts of 2026. The premium pricing signals overwhelming institutional appetite for exposure to the company behind Claude, one of the few credible challengers to OpenAI's dominance in large language models.
Anthropic is going public at a premium. The AI safety company priced its initial public offering at $83 per share late Wednesday, above the $70 to $78 range it had marketed to investors, according to a person familiar with the deal who spoke to CNBC. Shares are set to begin trading Thursday morning on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol ANT.
The above-range pricing caps a whirlwind IPO process that drew record retail investor demand matching the enthusiasm typically reserved for companies like SpaceX. Institutional investors clearly shared that appetite, pushing the company to price at the high end and then some. The $83 price point values Anthropic's offering higher than the company's most recent private funding rounds, a notable achievement given the choppy IPO market of recent years.
Anthropicoriginally filed to sell 825 million shares in a range that would have valued the offering between $57.8 billion and $64.4 billion. At $83 per share, the company is now raising approximately $68.5 billion, assuming the full allocation sells. That's a significant uptick from the IPO parameters disclosed earlier this month, which detailed cornerstone investments from Google, Nvidia, and AMD totaling $26 billion.
The pricing decision reflects a calculated bet by Anthropic's bankers that public markets are hungry for pure-play AI exposure. While Nvidia and Microsoft offer indirect ways to invest in the AI boom, Anthropic represents one of the few opportunities to own a piece of a frontier model developer. The company's Claude assistant competes directly with OpenAI's ChatGPT and has gained traction among enterprise customers seeking alternatives to Microsoft-backed solutions.
Timing matters here. Anthropic is hitting the public markets just days after launching Claude Meetings, a new feature that lets the AI assistant participate in live calls, draft plans, and update collaborative whiteboards in real time. The product launch served as a convenient reminder to IPO investors that Anthropic isn't sitting still while competitors iterate.
But the company faces real headwinds that sophisticated investors are surely pricing in. Anthropic disclosed an $895 billion compute bill in its initial prospectus, a staggering figure that underscores the capital intensity of training and running frontier AI models. Those costs aren't going down anytime soon, especially as chip giants like Nvidia accelerate production despite safety warnings from Anthropic itself and OpenAI.
There's also regulatory uncertainty hanging over the debut. New York's RAISE Act enforcement began earlier this month, requiring AI companies including Anthropic to register and disclose certain practices. How that and other pending legislation might constrain the company's operations remains an open question for public shareholders.
The $83 pricing also reflects confidence in Anthropic's leadership structure. In a rare move, all seven founders are cashing out $250 million each in identical share blocks while maintaining control, an equal split that signals internal cohesion at a time when many AI startups are dealing with founder drama.
Investors will be watching Thursday's opening trade closely for signals about how public markets value AI companies relative to their private funding rounds. Recent IPOs in adjacent spaces have sent mixed messages. Nvidia-backed Nscale updated its IPO filing with widening losses, raising questions about when AI infrastructure plays can achieve profitability. Meanwhile, Oura postponed its $2.2 billion IPO and pivoted to acquisitions instead.
Anthropicis betting it's different. The company's focus on AI safety and constitutional AI - an approach that tries to make models behave according to predefined principles - differentiates it from pure performance plays. Whether that's worth a premium valuation in the eyes of public market investors will become clear within hours of the opening bell Thursday morning.
Anthropic's above-range IPO pricing at $83 per share demonstrates that institutional investors are willing to pay up for direct exposure to frontier AI development, even with massive compute costs and regulatory uncertainty on the horizon. Thursday's trading debut will reveal whether that confidence extends to the broader public markets, or if the premium pricing leaves early investors holding an overvalued position. Either way, the Claude maker's public offering marks a pivotal moment for the AI industry, offering the first real price discovery for a company competing head-to-head with OpenAI in the race to build artificial general intelligence.
