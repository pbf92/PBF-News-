TITLE: From Friday night lights to Monday morning care
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

From Friday night football injuries to Monday morning health concerns, a new initiative from The University of Texas at Arlington and Medical City Healthcare aims to help school nurses and athletic trainers improve student care through communication and coordination.
“If a student sustains a concussion at a Friday night game, they may be evaluated by an athletic trainer that night. On Monday, that same student may visit a school nurse with headaches or difficulty concentrating,” said Adam Annaccone, a clinical associate professor of kinesiology at UT Arlington. “Both clinicians are providing care, but are they communicating? Who is coordinating the student’s recovery? That is the problem we’re trying to address more effectively.”
The collaboration began in 2025, when Dr. Annaccone saw an article about Medical City Healthcare’s existing school nurse education series and suggested expanding it to include athletic trainers. Since then, the program has evolved into Medical City Healthcare’s School Health Professional Education Series. Through its Master of Science in Athletic Training program, UTA is an approved continuing education provider, allowing eligible athletic trainers to earn credits through the series.
“Since 2017, Medical City Healthcare has provided annual continuing education opportunities for school nurses across North Texas. We have long wanted to expand this program to include athletic trainers but faced challenges with continuing education accreditation,” said Ryan Eason, director for community impact at Medical City Healthcare. “Through our important partnership with UTA, we are proud to offer continuing education credits to athletic trainers, expanding our reach and strengthening our support for school health professionals.”
The impact is tremendous. During the 2026-27 school year, the program is expected to educate more than 1,800 school nurses and athletic trainers from more than 40 North Texas school districts.
Working with healthcare professionals from several organizations, Annaccone led the development of a two-hour presentation for this year’s series titled “Friday Nights to Monday Mornings.” Using clinical scenarios such as a student experiencing sudden cardiac arrest, it guides participants through emergency response, injury assessment, communication, clinical handoffs and strategies for improving continuity of care.
“We begin with a scenario in which a student collapses on campus and both the athletic trainer and school nurse respond,” Annaccone said. “We ask participants to consider what happens next. Who takes responsibility? How do they communicate? Have they practiced responding together? In an emergency, you don’t want professionals figuring out their roles for the first time.”
Annaccone goes on to say, “it starts with understanding each person’s role, sharing information effectively and knowing who is responsible for the next step in a student’s care. We use athletic trainers and school nurses as an example, but the same principles apply to other professionals who care for students.”
The sessions also introduce school nurses to how athletic trainers assess injuries, make clinical decisions and determine when further evaluation is needed. By better understanding one another’s roles, participants can identify opportunities to strengthen coordination among athletic trainers, nurses, physicians, counselors and other members of a student’s healthcare team.
The partnership demonstrates how UTA faculty expertise and academic resources can support healthcare professionals serving students and families beyond campus.
“We are excited to expand our partnership with Medical City Healthcare and the clinicians caring for students across our surrounding communities,” Annaccone said. “This collaboration demonstrates how UTA’s faculty expertise and academic resources extend beyond campus to strengthen connections among healthcare professionals and support the health and well-being of students and families.”
