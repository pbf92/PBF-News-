TITLE: Which of the Top 5 Large-Cap Gainers Beyond Nvidia on November 23 Has the Most Earnings Support: Micron (NASDAQ:MU), Sandisk (NASDAQ:SNDK), Bloom Energy (NYSE:BE), Marvell Technology (NASDAQ:MRVL) or Lumentum (NASDAQ:LITE)?
OUTLET: kalkine.com

Key Highlights
- Five companies valued at about USD 50 billion or more joined Nvidia on the gainers list on November 23, 2026: Bloom Energy rose 20.00%, Lumentum 19.27%, Marvell (NASDAQ:MRVL) 19.03%, Micron 17.93% and Sandisk 16.58%.
- None of the five had company news dated November 21 to 23; the gains came as Nvidia's reinstatement of Jensen Huang reversed part of Friday's sell-off.
- Moves of this size are rare for companies this large, so the ranking asks which valuations are best supported by reported or guided earnings, using our own arithmetic where noted.
- Prices are intraday figures from the morning of November 23 and are not closing prices.
1. Micron (NASDAQ:MU)
Micron (NASDAQ:MU) ranks first because its Earnings power is enormous relative to its share price, even after a 17.93% gain to USD 701.09. At a Market Value near USD 793.2 billion, the move added roughly USD 120 billion of value in a morning on our arithmetic. Guidance for the fiscal first quarter calls for Revenue of USD 61.5 billion and non-GAAP earnings of USD 38.15 per share. Annualizing that quarter puts the stock at less than five times earnings on our arithmetic, a sign of how cyclical the market believes memory profits to be. Fiscal fourth quarter revenue was USD 54.23 billion, against USD 11.32 billion a year earlier, and GAAP gross Margin was 86.8%. Total cash, investments and restricted cash were USD 73.48 billion against Debt of about USD 5.2 billion, and customer contract liabilities of USD 12.9 billion show buyers prepaying for supply. The case against is that memory pricing is cyclical after a steep rise, and margins near 87% leave little room to improve. Even so, no other company here has as much verified profit behind its price. For the fiscal year, revenue reached USD 133.19 billion and Operating Cash Flow USD 89.68 billion, which funds both Capital spending and a quarterly Dividend of USD 0.15 per share.
2. Sandisk (NASDAQ:SNDK)
Sandisk (NASDAQ:SNDK) ranks second for the same reason as Micron, on a smaller scale. The stock rose 16.58% to USD 92.38, for a market value near USD 105.2 billion, in its first full week after an 8 for 1 split. Fiscal first quarter revenue was USD 11,031 million, up 378%, with a GAAP gross margin of 85.0%, and second quarter guidance calls for revenue of USD 12.2 billion to USD 12.6 billion and non-GAAP earnings of about USD 6.88 to USD 7.13 per share after the split on our arithmetic. That implies a low multiple of annualized guided earnings, again reflecting doubt that NAND pricing will last. Free cash flow of USD 6,309 million funded USD 5.0 billion of Buybacks in the quarter, which shows the cash is real. Sandisk trails Micron because its Balance Sheet is smaller, with cash and equivalents of USD 5,136 million, and because it is more exposed to one product type. The shares fell sharply in the weeks before Monday, so the cycle risk is already visible in the price history. Datacenter revenue rose 61% from the prior quarter to USD 4,802 million, which shows AI storage Demand is now the main driver.
3. Bloom Energy (NYSE:BE)
Bloom Energy (NYSE:BE) had the largest gain of the five, 20.00% to USD 170.94, for a market value near USD 50.54 billion. It ranks third because its growth is genuine but the valuation is far higher than the memory makers. Third quarter revenue was USD 1,235.8 million, up 138.1%, with an 18.2% Operating Margin and non-GAAP earnings of USD 0.94 per share. Guidance for 2026 calls for non-GAAP earnings of USD 3.10 to USD 3.40 per share, which puts the stock at more than 50 times this year's earnings on our arithmetic. Revenue guidance of USD 4.4 billion to USD 4.6 billion implies about 122% growth, and a 900 megawatt agreement with NTT DATA adds shipments from 2027, so investors are paying for several years of expansion. Operating cash flow was negative USD 27.1 million in the quarter as the company spends on capacity, and cash of USD 2.49 billion roughly matched recourse debt of USD 2.47 billion. The stock fell more than 13% on Friday and remains about half its June record, so the multiple can compress quickly when sentiment turns. Some headline projects, such as a Google related item in Wyoming, relate to orders placed earlier in the year and are not new bookings.
4. Marvell Technology (NASDAQ:MRVL)
Marvell Technology (NASDAQ:MRVL) rose 19.03% to USD 145.55, for a market value near USD 127.83 billion, after falling 11.69% on Friday. The rebound added about USD 20 billion of value in a session. Trailing twelve month revenue is about USD 9.45 billion, up 30.6%, with Net Income of about USD 2.64 billion and earnings of USD 2.98 per share, so the shares trade at roughly 49 times trailing earnings. It ranks fourth because that multiple is high against verified profits, and quarterly figures and guidance were not available in the material reviewed. The December 1 report will carry most of the new information, especially on custom silicon for hyperscale customers, Data Center growth and gross margin. The settlement of Patent disputes with Credo on November 17 removed one legal overhang. The 52 week range of USD 70.69 to USD 329.88 shows that sentiment toward the stock has swung widely, and a move of similar size around the report is plausible in either direction. Marvell is a credible AI supplier, but its earnings support is weaker than its peers here. About 878 million shares are outstanding.
5. Lumentum (NASDAQ:LITE)
Lumentum (NASDAQ:LITE) rose 19.27% to USD 549.27, for a market value near USD 49.82 billion, after a 13.94% drop on Friday. Revenue growth is strong, with trailing twelve month sales of about USD 3.74 billion, up 103.2%, and fiscal fourth quarter revenue above USD 1 billion, driven by lasers and modules for AI networks. It ranks last because its earnings support cannot be verified from the material reviewed. The reported trailing net income figure is deeply negative and looks distorted by unusual accounting items, so a price to earnings multiple is not meaningful, and cash and debt were not confirmed. The stock trades at less than half its 52 week high of USD 1,143.00 but more than twice its low of USD 229.59, a range that shows how much of the value rests on confidence in AI spending. About 90.7 million shares are outstanding. A long term indium phosphide Supply agreement with AXT through 2031 secures a key input. Investors should check the latest filing before relying on any profit figure. Peers in optical components also rose sharply on Monday, which points to a sector move rather than company news.
Where Things Stand
Of the top 5 large-cap gainers beyond Nvidia on November 23, Micron has the most earnings support, trading below five times annualized guided earnings on our arithmetic. Sandisk offers similar support on a smaller base, Bloom Energy trades above 50 times 2026 earnings, Marvell near 49 times trailing earnings, and Lumentum's profits could not be verified.
