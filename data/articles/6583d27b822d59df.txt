TITLE: GTA 6 Criminal Profile explained: morality levels and how to avoid Irredeemable status
OUTLET: GAMINGbible

GTA 6 introduces a new take on Red Dead Redemption 2’s morality system called Criminal Profile, and gamers have been struggling to wrap their heads around it.
It’s much easier than you think though, GTA 6 just does a poor job at explaining it. Worry not, we’re on hand to tell you everything you need to know about it.
We’ll be going over what the Criminal Profile system is, how it’s measured, what it unlocks and why Rockstar Games added it to the game with words from Rockstar’s co-lead Rob Nelson.
What is GTA 6’s Criminal Profile system?
Simply put, the Criminal Profile system measures what kind of character your version of Jason and Lucia are.
There are five tiers; Professional, Hardened, Violent, Psycho, and Irredeemable. Where Jason and Lucia land on this scale depends entirely on your in-game actions.

How GTA 6 determines your Criminal Profile
Professional and Hardened relate to criminal activities that show restraint, or sometimes mercy.
If you rob a jewellery store with a mask on, disable the alarm, keep every hostage alive and make a clean getaway in your escape vehicle, GTA VI will deem you a Professional criminal. Should things go wrong, as they often do, you might end up with Hardened instead. This is fine, it just means you’ve made some sloppy mistakes, but nothing you can’t do better next time.
Violent and Psycho will cater more to the classic Grand Theft Auto players. In these scenarios, you’ll have barged into the jewellery store guns-blazing, bodied the hostages and made off with the cash as every alarm blares and the police hot on your heels.

What Irredeemable means in GTA 6 and can you come back from it?
The worst tier on GTA VI’s Criminal Profile system is Irredeemable. As the name implies this is the last-leg on any deplorable player’s journey, as there’s literally no natural way of coming back from it.
You’ll only attain this status if you kill anyone and everyone, with no regard for professionalism or human life.
The only way to reverse it is to revert to a previous save where you don’t possess this status, so if you do have a rampage planned be sure to save the game first.
How to influence your Criminal Profile in GTA 6
There are several ways to alter your Criminal Profile in GTA VI. Casualties are the main one. Generally speaking any kill the game deems unnecessary is the main driving factor. Killing random civilians on the street is an easy path to the Dark Side, but killing in self-defence tends to be more understandable.
Killing a few police officers while making a getaway is also viewed as a necessary action unless you go out of your way to slaughter dozens and dozens of them.
If you want to stay in the Professional and Hardened tier you’ll want to focus on non-lethal takedowns. Shooting enemies in the legs, arms, or sometimes the main body if they’re wearing a bulletproof vest, it will incapacitate, but not kill. Headshots remain an instant-kill and will be noted for your profile.
Wider criminal activities like stealing vehicles, selling drugs or robbing stores won’t be recorded unless you kill a bunch of civilians in the process.
Again though, you have to really go out of your way to reach the Psycho and Irredeemable tiers.
Throughout my 100-plus hours I’ve generally left civilians alone, but when I’m raiding gang hideouts or have the police on my back I always go weapons-free. At the time of writing both Jason and Lucia are labelled as Hardened criminals.
What’s doesn’t count towards GTA 6’s Criminal Profile system
I’ve seen a lot of people confused about this online, so hopefully this clears things up a bit. Violent action that takes place within one of the game’s main story missions doesn’t count towards your Criminal Profile.
There are several moments where Jason and Lucia are presented with a choice to kill or spare an individual, but how you play this is entirely up to you. It won’t move the needle.
The game won’t penalise you for blowing up police cars during an action set-piece, as that’s something Jason and Lucia have to do in order to progress the story.
It’s only when you hit free-roam that GTA VI will start paying attention to what kind of player you are.
Does the Criminal Profile system determine what ending you’ll get?
The short answer is yes, sort of. Your Criminal Profile definitely plays a role in what sort of ending you get, with Professional and Hardened players getting a slightly better outcome. Violent and Psycho players aren’t automatically locked out of good endings though.
In the grand scheme of things your BuckMe account is more important if you’re going for a certain ending. Maxing that out is highly-recommended.
Can you turn the Criminal Profile system off in the settings?
At the time of writing there’s currently no way of disabling the Criminal Profile system, and Rockstar Games has made no mention of introducing the option in the future.
Rockstar co-lead Rob Nelson explains why the Criminal Profile system was introduced
While speaking to Rob Nelson in the build-up to GAMINGbible's GTA 6's review, and GTA VI’s launch, I asked for more information on the Criminal Profile system and whether Rockstar thought it was an unfair limitation for players who just want to cause chaos.
Nelson explained how the development team went out of its way to not remove player choices, but actually provide more choices for your preferred gameplay style.
“It's not called Karma or a Morality System because you're still a criminal. That's your job.
“We’re trying to respond to the way that the player is playing, and give options for people who want to use restraint. I think those choices give more agency to make these characters yours.”
The team apparently looked at the simple “demi-god” rampages of GTA V and wanted to do more than just give players more tools of destruction. The goal was to add “more layers and options” to the world you’re exploring, rather than slaps on the wrist for being bad.
“It gives all of the acts more meaning, because you have to approach them with a little bit more thought and intention … I don't think there's a bad ending or a good ending, but you will reap what you sow”, said Nelson.
