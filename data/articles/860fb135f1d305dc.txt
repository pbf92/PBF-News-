TITLE: Red Wednesday: Catholic sister honoured for courage in ministering to vulnerable Syrians
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A religious sister who risks her life ministering to families, the elderly and sick in war-torn Syria has been honoured for her courage.
Sister Annie Demerjian has been awarded the Courage to be Christian Award from Aid to the Church in Need (ACN), as the Catholic charity marked its tenth Red Wednesday campaign for the persecuted church.
A Mass at St George’s Cathedral Southwark was the main event for ACN in the UK, as churches across the world illuminated red in solidarity with those who suffer for their faith.
Having originally trained as an engineer, Sr Annie decided to stay in Aleppo at the height of the civil war. She provides lifelines to the vulnerable by sharing emergency aid, food, and medicines, as well as offering spiritual guidance and leading initiatives that support children affected by trauma.
Speaking to Premier Christian News at the service, Sr Annie said Red Wednesday was an opportunity to tell persecuted Christians that they are not alone: “We are beside you with our prayers and with our hearts.”
“I recall the message of Saint Paul saying, ‘If one part of the body is suffering, the whole body is suffering’. We cannot say, ‘this has nothing to do with us’. Prayer is very important as it can change the minds and hearts of people, especially world leaders. We need the grace of God to change their hearts and minds so they can, instead of going to war, be a peacemaker.”
Sr Annie said prayers were needed for those persecuted: “So they will not feel abandoned and alone. They will feel that they have brothers and sisters concerned about them, thinking about them. That puts hope in their hearts.”
Christian freedoms in Syria have declined further since the fall of the Assad regime in 2024, according to ACN’s report Persecuted and Forgotten? There have been attacks on churches, including Mar Elias Greek Orthodox Church in Damascus last June, suspected to be perpetrated by Islamist terrorists. The country’s Christian population has declined by 80% since 2011, driven by insecurity and poverty.
Sr Annie still believes there is a role for believers to play in Syria’s reconstruction: “To be a Christian, that means to be a bridge of hope and love, and to build bridges of forgiveness and reconciliation in a time of hatred or revenge, full of revenge and violence.”
Accepting the award, Sr Annie spoke of the times “when the convent shook around us as shells fell nearby”, yet they “rose together to clear the ruins, encouraging one another”. She thanked her fellow religious sisters, the support of ACN, and God “who never leaves us and is with us in times of weakness; strengthening, supporting, and teaching us.”
The Homily on Wednesday was delivered by Father Firas Lutfi, Custodian of the Province of Saint Paul covering Syria, Lebanon and Jordan. Prayers were said for those who have been killed because of persecution; clergy, religious, faithful and Christians who face hostility for their faith; and for the protection of women and girls from abduction and all forms of violence.
