TITLE: Victorian Socialists are polling at a record high. Here’s where they could break through
OUTLET: Crikey

As the Victorian election campaign lumbers to the finish line, a wildcard party is going under the radar: the Victorian Socialists.
VicSoc only has one local councillor in office, so it is easy to dismiss it as just another minor party in a crowded field. But its campaign is resonating in Melbourne’s inner and outer suburbs to the point of being competitive in multiple seats, and with new polling showing them at a record-high vote, there’s good reason to take them seriously.
Formed just two state elections ago in 2018, VicSoc is running candidates in all 88 lower house seats and eight upper house regions this year. A YouGov poll released yesterday showed the party — in a rare instance of it being named in a published poll — at 4% statewide. The party’s statewide upper house vote in 2022 was just 1.4%, and it received 1.5% in the Senate at last year’s federal election.
So where could VicSoc actually perform well? Helpfully, its best prospects can be split into two of Victoria’s upper house regions: Western Metropolitan and Northern Metropolitan, where the YouGov poll had the party polling at 7% and 8%, respectively.
In Western Metropolitan, VicSoc’s presence is most felt in Labor-held Footscray, where Jasmine Duff, who has served as the national co-convenor of Students for Palestine and contested Fraser at the federal election, is running as the party’s candidate.
VicSoc received 9% of the vote in Footscray at the last state election. With a strong ground game, campaigners on the ground think they could double it. But the electorate is a chaotic, multi-cornered race, one also targeted by the Greens, with the Liberals, One Nation, independent Jess Marsh and possibly even the West Party also set to pull notable vote shares that would drag out counting well beyond this weekend.
Speaking to 6 News earlier this month, Duff said she’s been “really overwhelmed by the amount of support” the party has received for the Footscray campaign, which has allowed them to knock on every single door in the electorate. VicSoc has previously received more than 20% of the vote in multiple booths within Footscray.
Other Western Metro lower house seats where the Socialists could perform reasonably well include St Albans (6.6% in 2022, just behind the Greens), Werribee (7.2% in a 2025 by-election) and Kororoit (third-placed on primary votes with 6.9% in 2022).
As for their chances of winning one of the five upper house seats up for grabs here, the YouGov poll remarkably showed Labor, the Liberals, One Nation and the Greens receiving 20-21% of the vote each, leaving the fifth seat as a fight involving Socialist lead candidate Anneke Demanuele. The 8% for “others” in the poll also opens the door, even if only slightly, for either Legalise Cannabis or Family First to retain a Western Metro seat too.
Over in Northern Metropolitan, VicSoc has a good chance of reaching double-digits in outer suburban seats, including Broadmeadows (8% in 2022), Greenvale (fourth placed with 6.9% in 2022; the Greens finished sixth), and Thomastown (third placed 7.7% in 2022). The latter of those three may very well be the strongest, with a highly visible corflute presence for the Socialists observed locally — combined with a weaker Greens vote (in contrast to Broadmeadows) — that allows Socialist candidate Priya Reddy to take a good chunk of Labor’s 52% primary vote in the seat.
In the inner-suburbs of the north, VicSoc will likely receive a decent vote in Brunswick (held by the Greens) and Northcote (Labor), as well as Preston (Labor). Their vote should also be boosted by the candidacy of Jordan van den Lamb (aka PurplePingers) — arguably the party’s public face — in Greens leader Ellen Sandell’s seat of Melbourne. Walking around many of the suburbs within these seats shows a large Socialist campaign that seems to eclipse visible support for Labor.
As for the party’s Northern Metro upper house chances, lead candidate Omar Hassan has a realistic path to victory based on YouGov’s poll. As in Western Metro, the other four main parties will be able to gain one seat each, but the fifth will probably be a race between a second Labor seat, a second Greens seat and the Socialists.
The Greens’ bid to win two seats in Northern Metro has been pushed recently, with Sam Drummond (second on the party’s ticket) telling 6 News that it was the first time the Greens had run an open preselection process for the second position. Sandell said last week she would love to see Drummond elected despite being “really up against it”.
The Socialists have reason to be annoyed at not already holding this seat. They claimed 4.7% of the vote in 2022, just behind Democratic Labour Party member Adem Somyurek on 4.8%, who went on to win the seat with the help of preference deals organised by Glenn Druery. The group voting ticket system that allowed that to happen has since been abolished, and Somyurek quit the DLP in March 2024, leaving the race primed for a potential Socialist victory.
Victorian Labor’s complete collapse in the polls — a trend showing no sign of abating — means we may be in for some unexpected results on Saturday night. It’s not yet clear if that will include any Socialist victories, but the party has good reason to be optimistic about its chances in both houses, as well as their long-term future in the state.
