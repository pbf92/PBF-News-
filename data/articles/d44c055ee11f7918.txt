TITLE: Alternative Bone Grafts Show Favourable Volume Preservation in Cleft Palate Review
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Alternative grafting techniques for secondary alveolar bone grafting in patients with unilateral cleft lip and palate demonstrate favorable volumetric preservation relative to conventional autogenous cancellous iliac crest bone grafts, according to a systematic review published in BMC Oral Health. The peer-reviewed investigation analyzed comparative radiographic evidence to evaluate how alternative bone graft substitutes perform against traditional autogenous grafting.
Secondary alveolar bone grafting represents an established clinical cornerstone in the comprehensive management of unilateral cleft lip and palate. Historically, the procedure has been routinely performed using autogenous cancellous bone harvested from the iliac crest; however, recognized clinical limitations, including donor-site morbidity and unpredictable graft resorption, have encouraged the exploration of iliac crest graft alternatives.
To identify and evaluate emerging alternative approaches, a comprehensive systematic literature search was conducted across three biomedical databases: PubMed, LILACS, and the Cochrane Library. The search protocol specifically identified and screened scientific studies published between 2016 and 2025 that examined alternative materials applied in secondary alveolar bone grafting procedures for cleft lip and palate patients.
The systematic review established rigorous inclusion criteria focused on studies evaluating volumetric changes of iliac crest graft alternatives compared directly to autogenous cancellous bone grafts. To maintain objective measurement standards, the review required volumetric assessments to be conducted using either cone beam computed tomography or computed tomography imaging, and only randomized controlled trials meeting the criteria were incorporated.
Following comprehensive screening, six randomized controlled trials satisfied the formal inclusion criteria for the systematic review. To evaluate study quality and determine the methodological rigor of the included trial evidence, the review authors assessed the risk of bias across all six randomized controlled trials utilizing the Cochrane RoB 2.0 tool.
The evaluated alternatives encompassed double iliac cortico-cancellous block grafts, mineralized plasmatic matrix, and tissue-engineered substitutes and constructs. Results from the synthesized trials indicated that these emerging grafting approaches achieved promising outcomes, demonstrating acceptable, comparable, and favorable volumetric preservation when assessed relative to traditional cancellous iliac crest bone grafts.
In addition to volumetric retention, the alternative grafting approaches were associated with fewer donor-site complications overall compared to traditional grafting methods. Nonetheless, the authors emphasized an important surgical consideration: modified autogenous approaches, such as double iliac cortico-cancellous block grafts, still require surgical intervention at the iliac crest donor site. The systematic review was formally registered in PROSPERO under the identifier CRD420251130777.
