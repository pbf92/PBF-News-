TITLE: OpenAI, Google and Anthropic Gain Edge as China's AI Race Slows
OUTLET: Coinpaper

Key takeaways
- Chinese AI developers face delays launching flagship models
- US chip restrictions threaten access to overseas computing
- OpenAI and Anthropic strengthen defenses against unauthorized distillation
OpenAI, Google (NASDAQ: GOOGL) and Anthropic could gain additional time to consolidate their positions in the global artificial intelligence market as Chinese competitors encounter growing technological and regulatory obstacles.
According to an exclusive Reuters report published on November 26, Chinese AI developer Z.AI has fallen behind schedule on its upcoming flagship model, while Moonshot AI faces potentially longer development cycles.
China's AI Development Faces Growing Obstacles
Z.AI, also known as Zhipu AI, reportedly intended to launch its GLM-5.5 flagship model in November. However, development challenges and restrictions on computing resources could delay its release until 2027.
Moonshot AI faces similar challenges. Sources familiar with its operations indicated that the interval between major model releases could increase from six months to a year or longer if access to overseas computing infrastructure becomes restricted.
The US Department of Commerce introduced new export regulations on November 19, expanding licensing requirements for advanced computing technology and targeting remote access to restricted processors.
The measures have already contributed to a reported AI server shipment suspension affecting Nvidia's manufacturing partners across Southeast Asia.
Restricted access to advanced computing infrastructure could undermine one of the principal advantages of Chinese AI developers: their ability to produce competitive models at relatively low costs.
OpenAI and Anthropic Strengthen AI Model Defenses
Computing restrictions represent only one challenge facing Chinese artificial intelligence companies.
OpenAI and Anthropic have also strengthened their defenses against unauthorized model distillation, a process in which developers use outputs from more capable AI systems to train other models.
Although distillation is a legitimate machine learning technique, unauthorized large-scale extraction can violate platform usage policies and undermine intellectual property protections.
In a November 20 publication, OpenAI outlined its efforts to prevent unauthorized distillation and coordinate safeguards with other AI developers.
These developments could increase the financial and technological barriers facing competing laboratories, particularly as the AI infrastructure debt boom highlights the substantial costs of developing frontier models independently.
What Does This Mean for Google and US AI Stocks?
Longer development cycles among Chinese competitors could provide OpenAI, Google and Anthropic with additional opportunities to expand their enterprise businesses, improve existing models and strengthen customer relationships.
For Google, reduced competitive pressure could support its Gemini ecosystem and cloud computing operations, although the company's substantial infrastructure investments remain a financial consideration.
Meanwhile, the recent Anthropic stock correction demonstrates that technological leadership does not necessarily guarantee strong investment returns.
The restrictions could also accelerate China's development of domestic semiconductor alternatives, creating longer-term competitive risks for American chipmakers and complicating bullish Nvidia stock forecasts.
