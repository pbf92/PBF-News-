OUTLET: Luxurylaunches

Have you heard the adage, “Fortune favors the bold”? It certainly has favored Emirati billionaire Hussain Sajwani in more ways than one. Over the past year, we saw his 27-year-old son, Abbas Sajwani, take the reins of the seized and spectacular $325 million Amadea superyacht at a massive $138 million discount. If you think that was a good deal, imagine the gains when his father, Hussain Sajwani, turned an early $50 million investment in Anthropic into $1.4 billion in paper profit in less than two years.
The Dubai-based developer was one of three billionaire investors in the AI giant that gave the world Claude, something most of the world can no longer function without. He acquired his original $50 million stake in 2024 through a court-supervised sale of shares from collapsed crypto exchange FTX’s bankruptcy estate. The windfall was the result of Anthropic’s valuation skyrocketing to an insane $1.5 trillion, more than the annual GDP of the UAE, where Sajwani is one of the wealthiest individuals, and even Saudi Arabia.
According to ForbesMiddleEast, the IPO was priced at $83 per share on Wednesday evening and forever changed the fortunes of its founders and early investors; boosting the wealth of CEO Dario Amodei and his six cofounders by $40 billion, to an estimated $140 billion, or roughly $19.9 billion each. It also added a total of $9 billion to the fortunes of three billionaires, Sajwani, former Google CEO Eric Schmidt, and Skype cofounder Jaan Tallinn.
Hussain shared at Bloomberg Invest Dubai on November 12, “What I have made in 30 years, I made in Anthropic in less than two years.” His broader U.S. technology portfolio also includes OpenAI, SpaceX, and Databricks. Evidently, both father and son have had major wins recently, first with Amadea and then with Anthropic, but the disparity is unmissable. While the junior Sajwani secured a $138 million bargain when he picked up Amadea for $187 million, it looks modest compared to his father’s $50 million Anthropic deal. Not only is the estimated paper profit roughly ten times the yacht’s discount, but a hypothetical $138 million invested in the firm behind the Claude chatbot at his father’s original 2024 entry terms could have grown to roughly $4 billion on paper.
Billionaire assets have their charm, but when compared to life-changing sums, even floating palaces can feel like a costly indulgence. Lucky for the well-heeled Sajwanis, they don’t have to choose between luxury and making a living, as there is plenty more where that came from. At 73, Sajwani has experienced both financial setbacks and extraordinary success. From being born into a modest family in Dubai to a local trader, to building his first fortune in the 1980s through a catering venture serving clients like the U.S. military and Bechtel, before launching DAMAC Properties in 2002 during Dubai’s real estate boom, he has done it all.
Superyacht Amadea is just a little diamond in the necklace of niceties he has created. His life and achievements can be best summed up in his own words: “It [success] has been a combination of luck and vision. Someone would open the door, and I would run and grab the opportunity.” Looks like he is practicing what he preaches.
Note- At the time of writing, Forbes estimated Sajwani’s net worth at $16 billion.
