Shopify Stock: What the Company Does and What Drives the Shares
Shopify Inc. is a Canadian company that sells software merchants use to run online and in-store sales. Its shares trade under the ticker SHOP on Nasdaq in US dollars and on the Toronto Stock Exchange (TSX) in Canadian dollars. In the third quarter of 2026, about 78% of revenue came from payments and other merchant services, with the rest from subscriptions.

What is Shopify?
Shopify was founded in Ottawa in 2004 and is still headquartered there. Co-founder Tobi Lütke is the chief executive, according to the StockAnalysis company profile. The company says millions of businesses in more than 175 countries use its platform, according to its Q3 2026 results release.

What Shopify does and how it makes money
Shopify gives merchants the tools to sell online and in physical shops. Revenue comes from two segments. The Shopify business model links most of its income to how much its merchants sell.
Subscription Solutions
Merchants pay monthly fees for plans, from basic shops up to Shopify Plus for large brands. Apps and themes also sit here. Monthly recurring revenue reached $230 million in Q3 2026, up from $193 million a year earlier, as stated in the Q3 2026 release.
Merchant Solutions
This larger segment is mainly payment processing through Shopify Payments and Shop Pay. It also includes shipping and merchant loans. These fees grow with gross merchandise volume (GMV), the total value of sales made on the platform.

Risks to keep in mind
The main Shopify stock risks are summarised below.
Volatility
The shares fell 74.8% in 2022 and rose 124.4% in 2023. In 2026 alone, single sessions brought a gain of 17.0% and a fall of 8.7%.
Valuation and profitability
A trailing P/E above 90 can make the shares sensitive to any slowdown in growth. Small misses against estimates have moved the stock sharply. Reported profit can also turn into a loss when the value of equity investments drops, as in Q1 2026.
Dependence on merchants
Revenue depends on the sales of millions of mostly small and mid-sized merchants. Merchant failures in a downturn could reduce GMV. Shopify's merchant loans also carry credit risk.
Competition
Large platforms and cheaper website builders compete for the same merchants. Price pressure could slow growth or squeeze margins.
Macro and trade rules
Weaker consumer spending could reduce merchant sales. Tariffs and changes to trade rules can also raise costs for cross-border sellers.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does Shopify do?
Shopify sells software that lets businesses run online stores and shop tills. It also processes payments for those merchants.
Where is Shopify stock traded?
Shopify shares trade on Nasdaq in US dollars and on the Toronto Stock Exchange in Canadian dollars.
What is Shopify's ticker symbol?
The ticker is SHOP on both Nasdaq and the TSX. Canadian data services often show the Toronto line as SHOP.TO.
Is Shopify profitable?
Yes, on an operating basis in every 2026 quarter. Investment losses pushed net income into a loss in Q1 2026.
What drives Shopify's share price?
Quarterly GMV and revenue growth have moved the shares around results. Consumer spending and technology share valuations also matter.
