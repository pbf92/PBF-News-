TITLE: 4 women arrested in India over alleged conversion of slum children to Christianity
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Four women have been arrested for allegedly encouraging children to convert to Christianity.
The women were providing books, food and outings at a makeshift school in Ghata village in Haryana, located in northern India. Police were alerted to their activities by a man who said they were providing education for children from slum settlements The Times of India reports.
Asianet News also reports police saying the women offered notebooks, books, food items and other assistance while encouraging the children to embrace Christianity
Forced conversion is currently illegal under anti-conversion laws which means converting or attempting to convert someone is illegal by force, threat, misrepresentation coercion, allurement, fraud, or marriage. Religious conversion itself is not illegal and Article 25 of the Indian Constitution guarantees freedom of religion.
Following a raid on the temporary classrooms police found the women had been visiting the children every Saturday for a year.
The women have been identified as 21-year-old Suman Kumari a recent graduate, 31-year-old Lalnunpuii, 35-year-old Ashmita Chahar a Nepal native living in Kotla and working as a volunteer with cancer patients and Rajkumari Chaoba Singh who is 36-years-old.
Police say they are recovering CCTV to help with the case.
