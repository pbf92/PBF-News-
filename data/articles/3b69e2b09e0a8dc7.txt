TITLE: The 6 Most Fun Things To Do in the First Few Hours of ‘GTA 6’
OUTLET: Collider

Introducing Grand Theft Auto 6 is difficult, because there is so much to talk about, if you want to keep things broad. Broad is not the word of the day here, though. The only people who can go broad and talk about everything are reviewers who got a copy of the game some weeks early, and then the review embargo was lifted right as the game came out. Some of those people claim to have played GTA 6 for 100 to 200 hours, and many of them still claim they're finding new things. Some remain feeling overwhelmed, when it comes to talking about the whole thing and breaking down just what the game means. So, the person writing this… he’s only had the game for about two days. He’s devoted many hours of those two days to it, but he’s only about 11% of the way through the story. That does mean there won’t be spoilers here, at least as far as the narrative is concerned, but being in this position does mean only a few things can be talked about.
To spin something out of a lack of experience, though, how about this: GTA 6 is a slow-paced game at the beginning, not to the same extent as Red Dead Redemption 2, but it takes a while to really get going. But because it’s not as patient as Red Dead Redemption 2, there are still some fun things you can do once you’ve beaten the early tutorial missions (the ones that are brief and don’t allow freedom). The next few hours you spend with Jason, because the story kicks off with Lucia, the other protagonist, in prison (that was in the trailer! Not a spoiler). People have made fun of GTA 6 for having some tedious early missions, and also some activities that make you do chores and housework in preparation for Lucia getting out of prison, but alongside those slower parts, there are still some pretty wild things you can do in GTA 6 not long after you start playing the game for the first time. These are some of the most fun activities that are possible to do early in GTA 6, within the first area of the (absolutely massive) map, basically in the space between finishing the tutorial and seeing Lucia once she’s released from prison.
6 Interactions in Key Lento
It’s tempting to go to Vice City early on, because that’s the big city in GTA 6. And that’s where 2002’s GTA: Vice City was set, so seeing how the city’s changed in 40 years (since that game was set in the 1980s, though if you want to get technical, they're not really in the same continuity anyway) is tempting. You can go there early, but you're not really given a reason to until later in the game, when the story takes you to other areas of the giant map. And the map is giant, because GTA 6 gives you various locations within the whole state of Leonida, which is the GTA universe’s version of Florida, so Vice City lines up with Miami. But there’s a lot to do in the first area of the game, beyond the early (and slow-paced) story missions.
There are also lots of places to go around Key Lento, but you can wander around the town for an hour or two and just get entertainment from that alone.
Key Lento is where the game wants you to stay for the first few hours, and even if it’s a small town, it’s got a lot of character and things to do. There are also lots of places to go around Key Lento, but you can wander around the town for an hour or two and just get entertainment from that alone. Some story requirements do get you interacting with some townspeople, including Mandy (pictured above), but there are things you can do by way of interacting with her and other people beyond the story that are interesting (Mandy’s memorable because of her strange dynamic with Jason, owing to him being with Lucia, but only kind of, since she’s in prison and all). And then your neighbor Shirley is an interesting character, too, asking you to get her rum and then steal a mobility scooter off another neighbor. And then there are all the other interactions you can have with NPCs who aren’t really characters, like the guy who you might see accidentally light himself on fire by spraying gasoline around at a fuel station, Zoolander-style. Just running into people around this small town is a blast, even if it’s so much smaller and low-stakes than what you'll ultimately get into later.
5 Checking Out the "Nightlife"
Well, you know, it’s a Grand Theft Auto game. There is, uh, “nightlife” to see. These parts of the game are controversial to some extent, but the first club you can go to that’s a bit risqué is a little more wholesome than similar clubs in past games. Unless you ask for a private dance, NINE1NINE is basically a normal nightclub, and going there as Jason, without Lucia, is pretty funny. You won’t have much money to spend, but you can get drunk and dance (and interact) with other people there to a greater extent than clubs in previous games. Everything is expensive, it's too loud, and you leave feeling dizzy and looking sweaty. In other words, it is a great simulation of going to an actual nightclub in real life.
It does involve venturing into Vice City, but NINE1NINE is the one nightclub you can get into without progressing through the story. And it’s a great showcase for the game’s lighting and capacity to have countless NPCs on screen at the same time. There is a real spectacle to the place, and sure, if you want to look at the risqué stuff, that option’s there as well (but not as out in the open, which is good to keep in mind if you yourself are playing GTA 6 out in the open… you know).
4 Taking On Gangs as an Underdog
Like going into Vice City itself, to see a nightclub, maybe it’s better to wait a little while before doing too much by way of combat, but there are benefits to tackling some of the earlier Illegal Operations locations ASAP. If you have a gun, you can probably make some progress. Being stealthy early on helps, and then you can pick up better firearms from the first few people you take out.
Basically, it’s an adrenaline rush to take on gangs of criminals before you have many abilities and easy access to firearms. Further, if you do succeed in getting through some of these early gang hideouts, it’s the only real way you're going to find semi-advanced weapons or genuinely fast cars early on (and the latter is especially thrilling to take control of for the first time, well before any story mission lets you drive something so high-class).
3 Beginning the Search for Bigfoot (AKA Skunk Ape)
This builds on that previously mentioned “meeting strangers” thing, which sure, was also a feature in previous Rockstar games, but it’s elevated in GTA 6 for sure. You can tell as much just from the early area of the game. It’s worth drawing attention to one particular encounter, though, because GTA 6 does not really waste time letting you get a glimpse of Bigfoot, or the Leonida equivalent, referred to sometimes as Skunk Ape.
He’s in the swamps around Key Lento, and look, this particular player hasn’t encountered him again, so there’s clearly more going on with him and his deal, but running into him just a few hours into the game is wild. The hunt for Bigfoot, or rumors around him being in some previous GTA games, has plagued players for years (see GTA: San Andreas, especially). But GTA 6 isn't afraid to let you see/investigate him almost right from the start. If the first 10% of the game can let you look into Bigfoot/Skunk Ape, it gets the mind racing about what weird secrets and unsettling easter eggs might be experienced dozens of hours into the game.
2 Robbery Sprees
If the Illegal Operations side missions seem a bit daunting, one thing you should do (and get used to) is robbing places. Like, almost any building that can be entered has things you can steal in it, and there are so many more interiors than could be found in GTA 4 and GTA 5. GTA 5 did have those big heists, and you won’t be a stranger to committing crimes if you’ve played other GTA games, but the emphasis on all the smaller and spur-of-the-moment robberies in GTA 6 is something special, not to mention unique.
You do learn which kinds of stores are better to rob than others, and it’s always tense to go into a new one and scope it out, because they’ll usually have different backrooms and things like safes (and monitors where you can delete CCTV footage) in different places. You'll also be kept on your toes because cashiers react differently… like, one had a severe panic attack once, and that forces you to make a decision about helping him by getting his medical vape, or leaving him to pass out. And sometimes, you go to steal from a place and someone will already be in there trying to rob the place. For example, this player once had an NPC doing so with a banana instead of a gun, and you can embarrass him by telling the cashier that the amateur robber actually has a banana, sending the robber on the way before robbing the place for real with your gun… which is emotional whiplash for the poor cashier, but hey, it’s literally all in the game.
1 Driving and Browsing All the Radio Stations
Driving around is what you do a lot in every GTA game, and GTA 6 being a leap forward technically makes going for aimless drives even more enjoyable and immersive. You do have to worry about fuel, but genuinely running out without a fuel station around is rare, plus you can get to most places without having to fill up. All the cars handle in interesting and different ways, and then driving is also the easiest way to get control of all the radio stations in GTA 6.
And the radio… well, yeah, like driving, listening to fake radio shows/news reports and real-world music is fun in every GTA game, but there’s a larger variety of songs here, and just an overall higher quantity of music. It would take hours upon hours of driving to hear every song, since there are more than a dozen radio stations, and some stations themselves have dozens of songs they’ll cycle through in random order. There are also all the songs written for GTA 6, and that’s impressive, plus in-game podcasts if you ever get sick of the radio stations, by any chance, but some of the most satisfying moments of the game’s first few hours involve driving and/or listening to music. Go figure.
