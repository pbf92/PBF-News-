TITLE: Redfin Reports U.S. Home Prices Ticked Up 0.14% in October
OUTLET: TradingView

Redfin Reports U.S. Home Prices Ticked Up 0.14% in October
- Nationwide, home prices inched up 0.14% month over month in October, the smallest increase in six months.
- Home prices increased in 30 of the 50 most populous U.S. metros, with Miami and Montgomery County, PA posting the fastest price growth.
- Home prices declined in 20 metros, with Seattle and Riverside seeing the steepest declines.
SEATTLE, Nov. 24, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- U.S. home prices ticked up 0.14% month over month in October on a seasonally adjusted basis. That is the smallest increase in six months, according to a new report from Redfin, the real estate brokerage powered by Rocket.
Prices rose 3.5% from a year earlier in October, the smallest increase since May 2026. Annual price growth has slowed for three straight months.
This is according to the Redfin Home Price Index (RHPI), which uses the repeat-sales pricing method to calculate seasonally adjusted changes in single-family home prices. The RHPI measures how sale prices of homes have changed since their previous sale. October data covers the three months ending October 31, 2026.
Home prices continued to rise in October, but at a slower pace. Buyers gained more negotiating power than at any point in Redfin's records, which date back to 2013. Only three of the 50 most populous U.S. metro areas tracked by us were seller's markets in October.
However, a buyer's market doesn't necessarily signal strong buyer's demand. "Mortgage rates are not that volatile right now, but they're still higher than they used to be. That's holding back demand and putting some downward pressure on prices, which have started to fall in many parts of the country," said Chen Zhao, Redfin's head of economics. "On a national level, we don't think prices will fall very much."
There's also more supply available now, but sellers are not willing to cut prices deeply. "Many sellers are still anchored to the prices they've seen over the past few years, and because few sellers are under pressure to sell, that puts a floor under prices," said Zhao.
Home Prices Are Rising Most in Miami
Home prices rose in 30 of the 50 major U.S. metros month over month in October.
The biggest increase was in Miami, where home prices rose 1.5% month over month on a seasonally adjusted basis. It's followed by Montgomery County, PA (1.2%), Chicago (1.1%), Pittsburgh (1.0%) and Cincinnati (0.9%).
On a year-over-year basis, prices rose most in San Francisco, up 12.7%. It's followed by Miami (11.3%), Chicago (9.5%), West Palm Beach (8.9%) and Philadelphia (8.5%).
Home Prices Are Falling Most in Seattle
The biggest price decline was in Seattle (-1.2% month over month). Next come Riverside (-0.7%), Detroit (-0.6%), Las Vegas (-0.6%) and Washington, D.C. (-0.5%).
Prices declined year over year in six major metros, four in Texas plus Seattle and Phoenix. San Antonio had the biggest drop (-3.0%), followed by Seattle (-2.1%), Dallas (-1.3%) and Austin (-1.1%). Houston (-0.2%) and Phoenix (-0.2%) also declined.
