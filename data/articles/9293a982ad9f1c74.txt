TITLE: Paris Fury retracts claim that Tyson Fury was diagnosed with schizophrenia
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The wife of former world heavyweight champion Tyson Fury has retracted a claim that her husband was diagnosed with schizophrenia.
A new Netflix documentary exploring the life of the two-time king of the heavyweight division and born-again Christian has explored his struggles with mental health over a decade ago.
In the self-titled documentary TYSON FURY, released before Fury takes on Anthony Joshua at Principality Stadium in Cardiff on 11 December, it was suggested that he had been diagnosed with schizophrenia.
However, on the day of the documentary's release, Paris Fury, who has been married to Tyson since 2008, clarified that he had never been clinically diagnosed.
Paris Fury said: "I want to clarify a statement I made in the original version of the Tyson Fury documentary. I recalled a conversation from many years ago when discussing Tyson's condition, and I used the word 'schizophrenic'.
"Whilst Tyson did show some symptoms associated with schizophrenia, he doesn't have this condition and has never received a diagnosis. Mental health is complex, and it isn't always easy to find the right words, especially when you're living it with someone you love.
"I'm so proud of Tyson for really opening up in this documentary and want to say thank you for all the support we've received as a family."
The NHS website describes schizophrenia as a form of psychosis when a person may not be able to distinguish their own thoughts and ideas from reality. Delusions, hallucinations and wanting to avoid people and interests are symptoms.
The British Boxing Board of Control, which hands out licences to fight in boxing matches, reviewed Fury's medical record in light of the documentary's release. They have, however, handed the Lancashire-raised sportsperson a licence to fight Joshua.
A former unified world title holder, his mental health worsened significantly after winning the unified world title against Wladimir Klitschko in 2015.
He struggled with drug and alcohol addiction after winning the world title and gained weight.
In June 2016, he was admitted to hospital with a suspected heart attack and heard voices in his head during this hospital stay.
"It was like a living version of the pits of hell. I thought I would end up in a padded room with the door locked, and that scared me so much," Fury said.
"The anxiety was crippling me. I just could not overcome it, and that's when I went and saw the psychiatrist down in London.
"She asked me a gazillion questions, and I ticked all the boxes. Depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder."
"I was so scared, scared for him. I could see he was gone, he'd flipped the switch," Paris Fury added.
