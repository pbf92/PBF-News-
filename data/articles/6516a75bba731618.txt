TITLE: Christian woman to stand trial over silent prayer outside Birmingham abortion clinic
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A Christian woman charged with silently praying outside an abortion clinic in Birmingham will stand trial in January 2027.
Isabel Vaughan-Spruce appeared at Birmingham Magistrates’ Court on 25 November, where a senior district judge ruled that the case will not be dismissed.
Vaughan-Spruce pleaded not guilty last month to five charges under Section 9 of the Public Order Act 2023, which legalised buffer zones around abortion facilities in England and Wales.The charges relate to five occasions when she allegedly stood and prayed silently within the designated safe access zone outside the Robert Clinic in Birmingham.
Her legal team, Alliance Defending Freedom (ADF), argued that the prosecution amounted to an abuse of process and breached her rights to freedom of thought and religion.
They also argued that she had been singled out because of her known pro-life beliefs, and that silent prayer should not fall within the scope of the legislation. However, the judge declined to dismiss the charges.
Vaughan-Spruce said: "Silent prayer can never be a crime in the UK. My criminal prosecution for alleged ‘thoughtcrime’ is clear evidence of the UK’s censorship crisis.
“I sincerely hope and pray the court will uphold my fundamental human rights to freedom of thought and religion, and rule in my favour following my trial in January.”
ADF legal counsel Jeremiah Igunnubole said the prosecution demonstrated that Christians and pro-life supporters were being treated in a discriminatory way by UK authorities.
"Equality under the law and freedom of speech, thought and religion are core British values," he said in a statement. "The case against Isabel undermines all of these, highlighting the vital importance for our society that she is vindicated in court.
Vaughan-Spruce was previously acquitted in 2023 after being arrested for praying silently in a local buffer zone established under a Public Spaces Protection Order (PSPO).
She was arrested again weeks later, prompting a police investigation which was eventually dropped.
In August 2024, she successfully challenged both arrests and received a £13,000 settlement from West Midlands Police.
She is the first person to face prosecution under the national buffer zone legislation in England and Wales.
Her trial will take place between 25-29 January 2027.
