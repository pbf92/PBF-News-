TITLE: Lego, Uggs and a kimono: what 14 countries asked Google on Black Friday
OUTLET: PPC Land

Shoppers from Tokyo to Mexico City spent the first hours of Black Friday typing shop names, games consoles, boots and a fair number of questions about the calendar into Google today. According to the Google Trends Trending now lists for 14 countries, covering the 24 hours up to about 15:35 Central European Time, 202 searches rose sharply enough to count as trends - and 101 of them, exactly half, carried the words Black Friday either in the trend itself or in the related searches Google grouped beneath it.
The lists cover the United States, Canada, Mexico, Brazil, the United Kingdom, Ireland, Germany, France, Spain, Portugal, Italy, Poland, Japan and Australia. Together they hold 202 trends and 254 further related searches, 456 search terms in all, by PPC Land's count. Every one of them is recorded below, country by country, with Google's volume band and the time each trend started and, where it had, ended.
The spread is wide. In France, the online retailer Cdiscount reached the 100k+ band, ahead of the electronics chains Boulanger and Darty at 50k+. In Brazil, Magalu and Amazon both sat at 100k+. Five US trends reached 100k+, from the plain phrase "black friday" to Uggs, Lego, Walmart and TJ Maxx. Japan logged Black Friday at 200+, below a search for furisode, the formal kimono. The biggest trend in Spain was an actress. And in Mexico, not one of the five trends mentioned Black Friday at all.
In Short
Google's Trending now lists for 14 countries recorded 202 searches that jumped in the 24 hours up to Black Friday afternoon in Europe, and half of them mention Black Friday. Shop names did much of the work, with Cdiscount in France and Magalu and Amazon in Brazil in the 100k+ band, while people in the US asked about store opening hours in 56 different ways. For anyone buying search ads, the lists show demand gathering around the names of retailers and products such as Lego, Apple, Nintendo, Zara and H&M more than around the generic phrase. Google's volumes are rough bands rather than counts, so they cannot be used to compare the size of one country's market with another's.
How Google's Trending now list works
Google Trends publishes Trending now as a running list of searches that have jumped above their normal level. According to Google's help page for the feature, the list is built from six elements:
- Location. Each list is set to one of more than 100 countries and regions. All 14 lists in this article are national.
- Time window. The filter for when a search started trending defaults to the past 24 hours, with 4 hours, 48 hours and 7 days as alternatives. All 14 lists use the 24-hour setting.
- Search volume. Google shows traffic in buckets for the chosen period, together with growth against a predicted baseline. A figure such as 20k+ is a band, not an exact count. When Google upgraded the page in 2024, PPC Land described the volume figure as an approximation of actual Google searches.
- Start time and status. Each row records when the query started trending and whether it is active, meaning still searched more than usual, or ended, meaning back to its typical level. An ended trend can become active again.
- Trend breakdown. Each trend carries the related queries that make it up. One row can group variants of the same search, or searches Google considers related.
- Refresh and export. The data refreshes on average every ten minutes, and the page can be exported to Google Sheets, downloaded as a CSV file, copied or followed by RSS. The redesign of the Trending now page in August 2024 brought in search volumes, start times and trend breakdowns.
Every start and end time in this article is Central European Time (UTC+1), whatever the country. A US trend that started at 17:00 therefore began at 11 a.m. on the US East Coast, and a Japanese trend that started at 16:10 began ten minutes after midnight in Tokyo. Local-time conversions in the text are PPC Land's calculations. A row marked "Still active" had not ended by the close of the period covered, between 15:32 and 15:35 Central European Time.
Two limits shape what follows. Google does not publish how it selects trends, and its description of an active trend - one searched more than usual - points to jumps rather than totals, so a name that people search heavily every day can be missing even on a busy day. Amazon, for instance, is absent from the US and UK lists but present in the Brazilian, German, French and Polish ones. And the volume bands are no guide to market size: a 1k+ trend in Ireland and a 100k+ trend in the US reflect population, search habits and Google's banding, not the relative value of the two markets. Google Trends figures carry quirks of their own, too. An ExpressVPN study published in October found that Google Trends scores differed by IP address in 60% of weekly checks, depending on the country a request came from.
The 14 lists at a glance
The table counts trends per country, how many mention Black Friday in the trend or its related searches, how many were still active at the end of the period, and the highest volume band reached. The counts are PPC Land's.
Across all 14 lists, 94 of the 202 trends were still active at the end of the period. Nine lists include the plain phrase "black friday" as a trend of its own: the US, Brazil, Ireland, Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Poland and Japan. The UK, Canada, Portugal and Australia do not, although retailer-specific Black Friday searches appear in all four. Mexico has neither.
The size of the lists varies as much as their content. The US list runs to 39 trends and 237 search terms, more than half of all the terms in the 14 lists combined. Ireland and Portugal have four trends each.
Names that crossed borders
A handful of brands appear again and again. By PPC Land's count, counting both trend names and related searches, these names showed up in more than one country:
- Apple products (iPhone, iPad, AirPods, Apple Watch, MacBook): 11 countries - the US, Canada, Brazil, the UK, Germany, France, Spain, Portugal, Italy, Poland and Japan.
- Lego: 9 countries - the US, Canada, the UK, Ireland, Germany, France, Spain, Italy and Poland.
- Nintendo Switch consoles and games: 8 countries - the US, Canada, Brazil, the UK, Germany, France, Spain and Poland.
- Lululemon: 6 countries - Canada, Mexico, the UK, Ireland, Germany and France.
- Zara: 6 countries - Brazil, the UK, Germany, Spain, Portugal and Poland.
- UGG: 5 countries - the US, Canada, the UK, Germany and Poland.
- H&M: 5 countries - Brazil, Germany, Spain, Portugal and Italy.
- Amazon: 4 countries - Brazil, Germany, France and Poland.
- PlayStation 5: 3 countries - the US, Brazil and the UK.
- Samsung: 3 countries - Canada, Brazil and Spain, plus a search for "s26" in Germany that probably refers to Samsung's Galaxy S26.
- Sephora, Costco, IKEA and Best Buy: 3 countries each - Sephora in Canada, France and Italy; Costco in the US, Canada and Australia; IKEA in the US, the UK and Germany; Best Buy in the US, Canada and Mexico.
- Cyber Monday: 3 countries - the US, Germany and Italy.
- Aritzia, Walmart, Target and Kate Spade: 2 countries each - Aritzia and Walmart in the US and Canada, Target in the US and Mexico, Kate Spade in Canada and Australia.
The list is heavy on toys, phones, consoles and clothing, categories for which Adobe's forecast of $275.1 billion in US online holiday spending projected discount peaks of 30% for electronics, 29% for toys and 23% for apparel. Lidl's own brands surfaced twice: Silvercrest appliances in France and Parkside tools in Italy.
Shoppers asked when, not only what
Five of the 14 lists include questions about the date or length of Black Friday itself:
- United States: "what is black friday", "is today black friday", "when does black friday start" and "when does black friday end", alongside 56 searches about store opening hours, covered below.
- United Kingdom: "when does black friday end", trending between 11 p.m. on Thursday and 1:30 a.m. on Friday UK time.
- Australia: "when does black friday sales end" and "when do black friday sales end", trending from 7 p.m. on Friday in Sydney.
- Germany: "wann ist black friday 2026" (when is Black Friday 2026), "ist heute black friday" (is it Black Friday today) and "wie lange geht black friday" (how long does Black Friday last), the last active since 20:00 on Thursday.
- Brazil: "qual o dia da black friday" (what day is Black Friday), "quando começa a black friday" (when does Black Friday start) and "dia da black friday" (Black Friday day).
The questions come in a year when the event has spread well beyond one day. Amazon's Black Friday Week runs from November 19 to November 30, ending on Cyber Monday, the last day of the Cyber Week stretch that starts on Thanksgiving. Searches for "cyber monday" were already trending today in Germany, from 07:40, and in Italy, from 13:50.
United States
The US list is the longest of the 14 and the most commercial. Of its 39 trends, 33 mention Black Friday in the trend or its related searches, and 35 were still active at 15:32 Central European Time, which was 9:32 a.m. on the East Coast. The 24-hour window therefore runs from Thanksgiving morning to the start of the Black Friday shopping day in the eastern US.
The first trends began on Thanksgiving itself. "shark vacuum" and "patagonia black friday" started at 16:00 Central European Time, 10 a.m. in New York; "black friday" and "uggs" followed at 17:00. By the end of the period five trends had reached the 100k+ band: "black friday", "uggs", "lego", "walmart black friday 2026" and "tj maxx black friday". Six more sat at 50k+, among them PS5 deals, the Nintendo Switch, the Canadian clothing retailer Aritzia, Academy Sports and Kohl's.
Opening hours dominate the related searches. By PPC Land's count, 56 of the 237 US search terms ask when a store opens or what its hours are, naming Walmart, Target, Kohl's, Costco, Sam's Club, Best Buy, Lowe's, Home Depot, TJ Maxx, Marshalls, Old Navy, JCPenney, Michaels, Hobby Lobby and Barnes and Noble, along with "the mall". One trend, "what time does old navy open on black friday", ran from 3:50 a.m. to 8:50 a.m. Eastern. Another related search asked "what stores open at midnight on black friday". Google Maps, for its part, put the heaviest Black Friday road traffic between 11 a.m. and 3 p.m., later in the day than most of these searches.
Google's grouping is not always intuitive. The "tj maxx black friday" trend carries 44 related searches, of which only two mention TJ Maxx; the rest ask about hours at Costco, Kohl's, Target, Walmart and others. The "academy black friday" trend carries 30 related searches, and 19 of them name Target, asking about its opening hours, its Black Friday ad and gift cards. Twelve searches under that trend, and 17 across the US list, ask about gift cards, giveaways, freebies or Lowe's buckets. Ten use the retail term doorbusters, the steep, limited-quantity offers meant to bring people into stores early, and 44 include the year 2026.
Walmart's own trend, at 100k+, carries 18 related searches, from "walmart black friday hours" to "walmart doorbusters 2026" and "what is walmart doing for black friday". None of the 456 terms across the 14 lists names an AI shopping assistant, although Walmart on November 24 let Meta's Muse agent shop its site, with Walmart choosing the products.
Games and devices make up a second cluster. "ps5 black friday deals" reached 50k+, with "gamestop black friday" among its related searches, and "gamestop black friday 2026" carried "gta 6 black friday". Rockstar Games released Grand Theft Auto VI on November 19. The Nintendo Switch trend included "donkey kong bananza $30". Apple's deals trend reached 20k+, and a later search for "macbook neo black friday deals" started at 13:30. Television played a part as well: "gma deals and steals", the deals segment of ABC's Good Morning America, started trending at 8:30 a.m. Eastern.
