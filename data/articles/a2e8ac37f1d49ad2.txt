TITLE: TTUHSC Garrison Institute on Aging’s Brain Bank provides answers for families, hope for future generations through research
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

While the season of giving is here, so is Alzheimer’s Awareness Month.
Beyond financial gifts to groups related to the disease, there is a uniquely personal donation that can make a significant difference in the fight against Alzheimer’s and other forms of dementia.
The Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center (TTUHSC) Garrison Institute on Aging’s Brain Bank is a repository for these profound gifts.
The Garrison Brain Bank provides donor families with a free brain autopsy, offering definitive diagnostic answers that may help guide their own healthcare decisions. At the same time, donated brain tissue provides researchers with an invaluable resource to better understand dementia and advance efforts to prevent, treat and ultimately cure the disease.
As the baby-boomer generation continues to age, researchers are intensifying efforts to better understand Alzheimer’s disease and other forms of dementia, which are becoming increasingly prevalent. TTUHSC scientists say that because Alzheimer’s is a uniquely human disease, advances in research depend heavily on studying human brain tissue, donated after death.
“The more the population ages, the more cases we're going to see, unfortunately,” Boris Decourt, Ph.D., associate director of Basic and Translational Research for the TTUHSC Garrison Institute on Aging, said. “If we want to prevent disaster, almost an epidemic, we need to find treatments that at least slow the progression of the disease from the early stage.”
Established in 2007, the Garrison Brain Bank provides researchers with access to donated brain tissue to help advance a variety of studies focused on improving diagnosis, treatment and potentially prevention of Alzheimer’s and other neurodegenerative disorders. Donations of brains from individuals both with and without dementia helpscientists better understand the differences between normal aging and disease-related changes in the brain.
For Ruben Gonzales, senior director of the TTUHSC Garrison Brain Bank, his work is inspired by a personal connection. “My grandmother had Alzheimer's disease, and it just amazed me how she was still communicating with me in both English and Spanish. It just made me wonder, ‘Why is she still able to speak to me in both languages, but yet she doesn't remember other things?’ I've always found it interesting how the brain works,” he said.
Gonzales said the TTUHSC Garrison Brain Bank ensures each donation contributes to ongoing research while also providing valuable answers to donors’ families. Brain autopsy is the only definitive way to confirm many clinical dementia diagnoses. Through the Garrison Brain Bank, this service is provided free of charge. Families receive a detailed neuropathology report at no cost, providing greater clarity about the type and severity of a loved one’s condition when they died.
“They want answers for their future generations," Gonzales said. "That becomes part of their family medical history going forward.”
Gonzales noted that these findings often provide families with a deeper understanding of their loved ones’ journey while offering valuable medical history that may help surviving relatives make informed healthcare decisions, including preventive care.
Gonzales also added that brain donation does not prevent families from choosing an open-casket funeral service, as the procedure leaves no visible signs that would affect their loved one’s appearance.
Decourt said every Garrison Brain Bank donation contributes to a better understanding of aging-related brain diseases.
“My objective in conducting this kind of research is really to help in preventing the disease in the future and, if possible, cure patients at an early stage of the disease before the brain atrophies too much,” Decourt said.
Gonzales emphasized the importance of planning ahead to ensure a timely, smooth and respectful brain donation process. Families interested in learning more about the program can contact the TTUHSC Garrison Brain Bank for additional information, enrollment assistance and guidance throughout the donation process at gia@ttuhsc.edu or (806) 743-2408.
