TITLE: Filipino bishop pleads for poor and future generations as COP31 collapses
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A Filipino bishop pleaded with delegates at the COP31 conference to remember "the poor, our common home and future generations" as climate negotiations collapsed.
Bishop of San Carlos, Rt Rev Bishop Gerardo Alminaza also spoke about how the Global South bears the brunt of climate impacts, which he believes are acclerated by the use of fossil fuels.
Talks to form an international agreement on climate change policy at the United Nations' climate forum fell apart in Antalya, Türkiye, as the European Union among others refused to sign an agreement that omitted mention of phasing out fossil fuels.
Disagreements over commitments for richer countries to provide "climate finance" to help less-developed countries tackle the effects of climate change also became a sticking point.
Bishop Gerardo highlighted the story of Palawan, the Phillipines' “last ecological frontier”, which is being threatened by major ecological threats due to mining, land conversion and marine degradation.
"Fossil fuels drive global warming, placing enormous pressure on coral reefs, marine life, and coastal ecosystems," he said. "Offshore exploration and drilling threaten these same ecosystems through underwater noise, pollution, habitat destruction, and the risk of devastating spills.
“The plight of Palawan makes us ask what kind of development puts at risk the very ecosystems that sustain life. For fishing communities and indigenous peoples, the sea is a source of food, livelihood, cultural heritage, and life itself. Yet they are often expected to bear environmental risks in the guise of development without receiving its promised benefits."
The COP31 negotiations were the longest-running, lasting nearly a fortnight, and they are the first talks to collapse since 2009.
Outgoing UN Secretary General Antonio Guterres is "deeply disappointed".
Bishop Gerardo said Filipino Catholic churches are already serving Christians who are suffering due to climate change.
He added: “Fossil gas has been extracted from [Palawan's] waters for decades. Yet the province continues to struggle with costly electricity. Who benefits from the development? Who bears its burdens? And who will answer when the damage becomes irreversible?
“Our churches serve communities already suffering from the climate crisis. Farmers struggling with drought, fishing families facing declining catches, indigenous peoples defending ancestral territories, and families displaced by disasters. A just energy transition must protect both people and creation."
Director of advocacy at Catholic aid charity CAFOD, Neil Thorns, has called for more financial help for developing countries unable to invest into protective measures.
Thorns said: “Remembering the Nepal landslide this year and the impending El Nino, the response from governments to protecting and supporting people living in poor communities who done least to cause the climate crisis is hugely disappointing.
"The UK has an opportunity to make a big difference in the financial debates as it takes over the G20 Presidency next month. At COP the PM said debt and development were key priorities for the UK Presidency and we look to them to deliver meaningful action on debt, making polluters pay and illicit financial flows.”
Public policy lead at Christian international development group Tearfund, Sam Perriman, echoed CAFOD's words while shining a spotlight on smallholder farmers.
"What happened at these talks is not just a failure to respond to the scale of the climate crisis, but a betrayal of smallholder farmers and their futures," Perriman said.
"Despite recognising the crucial role of agriculture and smallholder farmers in delivering a just transition, wealthy countries have only offered crumbs in climate finance to help them adapt to the extreme heat, severe droughts, and erratic floods destroying their crops."
The UK Government have said that not recognising the role of fossil fuels upon climate change moves the debate "backwards on the science when we muct accelerate implementation".
