TITLE: Brand suitability inventory tier
OUTLET: PPC Land

What the field is
Brand suitability inventory tier is a reporting dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that labels each row of Amazon DSP delivery data with the suitability tier an advertiser had selected when the impression was bought. Amazon sells three tiers: Expanded, Standard and Restrictive. Each one excludes progressively more content that is legal and within platform policy, but that some advertisers would still rather avoid. The field exists so that the setting chosen in the campaign interface can be checked against what actually ran: how many impressions, clicks and conversions came through under each tier, and at what cost.
The dimension is one half of a pair. Its sibling, brand suitability content exclusion category, reports the on-off topic switches an advertiser can add on top of a tier. Both sit under the brand safety floor, the set of content categories that Amazon blocks for every advertiser regardless of preference. The tier is a dial that moves above that floor; the floor itself does not move.
Amazon's November 14, 2025 launch announcement describes the control as one that "provides graduated control across content types, balancing reach with brand safety." The reporting dimension is the audit trail for that dial.
How the tiers work
The three tiers are cumulative. A campaign set to Restrictive excludes everything that Standard excludes, plus more; Standard excludes everything Expanded does, plus more. Expanded is the widest setting and sits closest to the floor.
Amazon's public launch page does not publish a content definition for each tier. The closest primary description comes from Twitch, where the tiers were first used. Twitch's advertising blog, announcing the controls for Twitch Premium Video, says the widest tier "includes all content that adheres to Twitch's brand safe Community Guidelines which map to the GARM Brand Safety Floor." The middle tier "excludes content that advertisers may consider the most sensitive", while the narrowest "has the same exclusions as Moderate and, in addition, excludes even more content", with significant profanity cited as an example.
The signals behind the classification differ by supply. On Twitch, creators must apply Content Classification Labels to streams with sensitive material, and Amazon's Twitch brand safety guide says those labels "inform Twitch's brand suitability tools", alongside Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB) ratings for the games being played. Twitch's blog adds that the controls weigh "both the content of a livestream and streamer behavior during the broadcasts." For third-party websites and apps, Amazon has not published an equivalent description of how pages are sorted into tiers.
A worked example shows how the field reads in a report. An advertiser runs one line item, now called an ad group in unified reporting, set to Standard, and a second set to Restrictive. A report broken out by brand suitability inventory tier returns two rows, one per tier, each with its own impressions, spend and conversions. If the Restrictive row shows a sharply higher cost per thousand impressions (CPM) or far lower delivery, the advertiser can see what the narrower setting cost in reach.
Where it sits in the buying flow
The tier is set before any bid is placed. Amazon's launch page lists three places to configure it: the Amazon DSP console under "Inherited settings", then "General", then "Brand suitability"; the public application programming interface (API); and the bulk tool spreadsheet. Because it lives under inherited settings, a tier chosen at advertiser level cascades down to campaigns and ad groups unless overridden.
PPC Land reported on the API fields when the settings opened in beta on November 18, 2025: brandSafetyTierInheritedSettingDetails at advertiser level and brandSafetyTierTarget for ad group targeting. The same report noted that only display and video media types were supported at the time.
A bid request arrives from Twitch or from third-party exchanges. Amazon's systems check the page or stream against the floor, then against the tier, then against any content exclusion categories. Only inventory that clears all three is eligible for a bid. After delivery, the reporting layer stamps each impression with the tier that applied, which is what the dimension exposes.
Publishers do not choose a tier; Amazon's classification sorts them into one. The dimension is a buy-side tool, used by advertisers, agencies and the brand safety teams that audit them.
Origin and evolution
The tiered model predates Amazon's version. The Global Alliance for Responsible Media (GARM), a World Federation of Advertisers (WFA) initiative, published a framework in September 2020 with a floor and three graded risk levels above it, as PPC Land's explainer on brand safety sets out. Google used the labels Expanded, Standard and Limited inventory for YouTube; its Google Ads API enum still carries the values EXPANDED_INVENTORY, STANDARD_INVENTORY and LIMITED_INVENTORY, as PPC Land's explainer on inventory modes notes. Amazon's naming follows that vocabulary closely.
Amazon's own path began on Twitch. On November 6, 2023, Amazon announced Content Controls for Twitch Premium Video in Amazon DSP. The controls "replaced the currently available Content Ratings Filter" and were offered in three tiers named Expanded, Moderate and Restrictive. According to Amazon, "each successive tier progressively excludes more inventory, allowing customers to balance suitability and reach." PPC Land's explainer on brand suitability dates the extension to display line items to April 11, 2024.
The decisive change came in November 2025. On November 14, Amazon announced the inventory tier control for all self-service Amazon DSP advertisers, now covering Twitch and third-party web and mobile inventory, and with the middle tier renamed Standard. Days later the API fields entered open beta. On December 18, 2025, Amazon added content exclusion categories, which PPC Land described as binary on-off switches, and extended the brand suitability report to cover them.
The tier moved into the unified reporting field list as Amazon consolidated its reporting. Unified reporting launched in beta on November 11, 2025 and reached general availability on June 8, 2026. Amazon DSP itself was renamed DVA on September 29, 2026, but the field name was not changed.
Why it matters for marketers
Suitability settings are a trade-off between reach and risk, and until the reporting dimension existed that trade-off was mostly invisible. An advertiser could choose Restrictive and see delivery fall, without knowing whether the tier or something else was responsible. With the dimension, the tier becomes a variable that can be compared like any other.
The field also matters for accountability. Brands that commit publicly to placement standards need evidence. A report showing that every impression ran under Restrictive, combined with the floor, gives an auditor something to check. The launch page states that the enhanced report lets advertisers "verify ad placements across different inventory tiers and content exclusion categories."
Limitations and disputes
The largest gap is definitional. Amazon has not published, on its launch pages, what content each tier removes on third-party web and mobile inventory. Advertisers can see which tier ran, but not exactly what each tier excluded.
Naming and defaults conflict across Amazon's own sources. The November 2023 Twitch announcement uses Expanded, Moderate and Restrictive, and its screenshot caption marks Moderate as the default. Twitch's blog for the same launch says all new campaigns "will default to the Expanded tier." Amazon's current Twitch brand safety guide and the November 2025 launch use Standard, not Moderate, and state no default.
Enforcement is qualified. Amazon says it applies suitability measures "on a best-effort basis", a phrase PPC Land quoted in both its beta and content exclusion coverage. Amazon's brand safety methodology, which PPC Land covered when Amazon said under 0.1% of DVA ads on outside websites hit unsafe pages, submitted only English-language third-party web inventory for Media Rating Council (MRC) accreditation and was not accredited at the time. Brand suitability controls, including inventory tiers, were explicitly outside that scope.
Market coverage is also unclear. Amazon's launch page lists 34 countries, while PPC Land's December 2025 report cited 43 markets for the related exclusion categories.
Finally, the broader framework is contested. The WFA discontinued GARM on August 9, 2024, after litigation from X, and US regulators have questioned whether shared suitability standards restrict competition. The tiers survive, but the industry body whose floor they reference no longer operates.
How it differs from similar terms
Brand suitability content exclusion category reports the individual topic switches, such as specific sensitive subjects, that an advertiser turns off. The inventory tier is a single graded setting; exclusion categories are separate binary choices layered on top.
Brand safety floor is the fixed set of content Amazon blocks for all advertisers. The tier cannot loosen it. Amazon's methodology lists nine floor categories, including hate speech, terrorism and illegal content.
Inventory modes is Google's term for its equivalent setting in Google Ads and Display & Video 360 (DV360), now named Maximum, Moderate and Limited. The concept is similar, but Google's classification and tier definitions are not interchangeable with Amazon's.
Third-party verification tiers from vendors such as DoubleVerify or Integral Ad Science (IAS) apply the vendor's own classification. They can run inside Amazon DSP, but they are not reported through this dimension.
Recent developments
The field is part of the reporting migration Amazon is forcing through this winter. PPC Land reported that Amazon's dimensions reference, updated November 11, 2026, listed 156 unified report dimensions, and that advertisers will lose unmigrated reports on January 31, 2027. Brand suitability reports built in the legacy console need rebuilding in unified reporting before then.
On November 16, 2026, Amazon updated its brand safety methodology, which PPC Land covered the following day. The document claims a brand-unsafe impression rate below 0.1% on in-scope inventory, but again excludes suitability controls from the accredited scope. As of November 2026, there is no independent measurement of how accurately the three tiers sort third-party pages.
