Gold, silver prices today: What is weighing on bullion ahead of US inflation data
Gold and silver prices edged lower in international markets on Wednesday, November 25, after retreating sharply in the previous session, as expectations of another US Federal Reserve rate hike in December weighed on investor sentiment.
Market participants are now awaiting US inflation data for fresh clues on the central bank's interest-rate trajectory.
Spot gold fell 0.3% to $3,544.54 per ounce by 0024 GMT, while US gold futures for December delivery declined 0.5% to $3,542.60 per ounce.
Spot silver was down 0.2% at $40.79 per ounce.
The latest decline follows a sharp reversal in domestic bullion prices after Monday's (November 23's) recovery. Gold had gained more than 2% in the previous session, supported by bargain buying and a pause in the dollar's rally, following a steep correction that had pushed prices to a 14-month low.
The outlook remains sensitive to expectations of US monetary policy. Markets are pricing in a 96.4% probability of a 25-basis-point rate increase at the Fed's December meeting, while the probability of a larger 50-basis-point hike stands at 3.6%, according to the CME FedWatch tool.
Higher interest rates tend to weigh on gold, which does not offer interest income, by increasing the opportunity cost of holding the metal.
US inflation data in focus; what experts say
Investors will closely track the US Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index for October, scheduled for release on Wednesday (November 25). The measure is closely monitored by the Federal Reserve as it targets inflation of 2%. US GDP, durable goods orders, initial jobless claims and new home sales data are also due during the day.
NS Ramaswamy, Head of Commodity at Ventura, said the recent recovery in gold followed a pullback in technology stocks and a moderation in rate-hike expectations, which had contributed to the earlier correction. However, he cautioned that the rebound remains tentative, with a firm US dollar, elevated Treasury yields and possible equity-related forced selling continuing to limit gains.
Ramaswamy said softer US inflation or weaker growth data could ease pressure on Treasury yields and support bullion, while stronger-than-expected readings could revive hawkish rate expectations and push prices lower.
Rajeev Sharan, Head of Research at Brickwork Ratings, said gold's near-term movement would depend on inflation data, the US labour market and the Fed's December policy decision. He expects the metal to trade in a broad range of $3,450-$3,750 per ounce in the near term, with the dollar, bond yields and central bank buying remaining key factors to watch.
Vikram Subburaj, CEO of Giottus.com, said the recent rebound reflected the return of bargain buying after the sharp sell-off. However, he cautioned that it was too early to interpret the recovery as a sustained reversal, particularly with the December rate decision still posing a significant risk to bullion prices.
Prithviraj Kothari, Managing Director of RiddiSiddhi Bullions Ltd., President of the India Bullion and Jewellers Association Ltd. and Chairman of the Jain International Trade Organisation, said gold and silver had faced selling pressure as investors raised cash amid weakness in technology stocks and expectations of tighter monetary policy. He said a recovery could extend if gold holds the $3,500-per-ounce level and silver sustains above $41, although a breach of these levels could expose both metals to further declines.
Ashish Rajodiya, Head of Commodities at PL Capital, said the direction of precious metals would depend on movements in the US dollar, bond yields and equity markets. Continued easing in yields and the dollar could support a recovery, while fresh hawkish signals from the Federal Reserve could bring renewed selling pressure.
