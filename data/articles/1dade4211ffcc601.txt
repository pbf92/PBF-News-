TITLE: GTA 6 Is Overrated, and the User Reviews Prove It
OUTLET: PCMag

Like many of us, I preordered the $100 edition of Grand Theft Auto 6. I stayed up until midnight on launch day to check it out, and grew increasingly excited as the overwhelmingly positive reviews poured in. Even now, at the time of writing, GTA 6 has a Metacritic score of 99 based on 152 critic reviews, making it the highest-rated video game in history.
Meanwhile, across over 100,000 Metacritic user reviews, the game sits at a paltry rating of 5.7 out of 10. I'm sure many of these reviews are simply backlash over the near-unanimous praise or come from those who think Rockstar went 'woke' because you can't hire prostitutes or treat the female protagonist, Lucia, like one. But I think the rest of them have a point. I'm talking about the complaints about clunky gameplay, pacing problems, and gross monetization, which I think the critic reviews largely ignore.
Editors' Note: This article contains spoilers.
I'm Not a Hater, and GTA 6 Isn't a Bad Game
Admittedly, I'm not the biggest GTA (or Red Dead Redemption) fan, even if I grew up playing the games and having fun wreaking havoc in virtual metropolitan centers. But it's not because I don't enjoy Rockstar titles or open-world games in general. In fact, I love Rockstar's L.A. Noire and Max Payne 3, while Cyberpunk 2077 (probably the closest analog to GTA 6 aside from GTA 5) is an all-time favorite.
Even if GTA isn't my favorite thing, that doesn't mean I can't appreciate it. Its graphical fidelity and sheer scope are peerless, and we might never see a bigger, more polished game, considering the amount of development time and money that went into its creation. How could you not be impressed by a game world that has virtual video game consoles with playable games, after all? And, of course, GTA 6's soundtrack is pure magic.
But none of that means it's faultless, or that I have to agree with the critics' reviews. Because despite all of what it gets right, I think a Metascore rating of 99 is absolutely nuts.
Inconsistent Frame Rates and Latency Make Gameplay Choppy
If you weren't aware, a common criticism of Rockstar's last game, Red Dead Redemption 2, was that it was difficult to play. Cumbersome controls, lengthy animations, and a 30-frame-per-second target on consoles, among other things, all added up to an experience that just wasn't responsive or smooth. GTA 6 plays much better than Red Dead Redemption, but it still suffers from similar issues.
For instance, GTA 6 doesn't just have a 30fps cap; it often doesn't even maintain 30fps. If you're playing on a PS5, Xbox Series S, or Xbox Series X, just driving around GTA 6's admittedly beautiful world can drop you down into the mid-20s. Even the PS5 Pro can't lock to 30fps in the most stressful situations. And you don't have to take my word for any of this: Digital Foundry tested the game thoroughly across platforms to deliver these conclusions.
Then, you need a healthy amount of motion blur to compensate for GTA 6's frame rate, especially on an OLED display, and the game already looks quite soft (except on the PS5 Pro), courtesy of its upscaling tech. Naturally, the game also uses depth of field and other kinds of blurring, like when drinking, all of which make it tough to see clearly. It's much harder to appreciate how gorgeous a world is when you can't get a good look at it. It's like trying to take in a beautiful, scenic vista when you've forgotten your glasses at home.
Even at an unstable 30fps, GTA 6 has some significant latency, too. Digital Foundry measured 180ms of latency end-to-end in GTA 6. To put that into perspective, Skyrim on the Switch 2 clocks in at around 140ms and 150ms on the original Switch. Lag in GTA 6 is closer to what you get when streaming games from the cloud than other console games.
I can feel that latency, too. GTA 6 requires a variety of button presses for everything from lifting weights to playing pool, and it also relies heavily on relatively lengthy animations you can't cancel. Driving feels more realistic than it did in GTA 5, too, and that change also adds to a lack of responsiveness. That's a problem, of course, because GTA is an action-packed third-person shooter: You need to be able to quickly peek out from behind a piece of cover, just as easily as you need to be able to deftly swerve in and out of traffic.
None of these problems are deal-breakers by themselves, and even collectively, they don't mean I'd turn my nose up at a game I'd otherwise love. And, of course, what you consider to be acceptable performance might differ entirely from me. But they do make me less willing to forgive other issues, such as the game's pacing.
The Pacing Is Inconsistent and Often Exhausting
I like that GTA 6 starts slow. I have no problem hanging drywall, patching up a roof, and otherwise attending to household tasks in a game. What bothers me is the whiplash I get from breaking off a piece of that cozy vibe only to end up in a shootout over a drug deal gone wrong moments later. Then, of course, a few minutes later, I'm back to watching TV with my girlfriend before a late-night workout and a protein shake.
Even when I'm expecting the unexpected, GTA 6 is relentless. Rockstar carefully crafted an enormous sandbox for me to get lost in, but I never seem to have the time to explore it. Every two minutes, I get a call or text telling me to go somewhere and do something. If I don't go, more urgent calls and texts arrive soon after. Sometimes these are time-sensitive tasks I simply must do, such as preparing for Lucia's arrival, but other times not doing them is immersion-breaking, so I do them anyway. This means I can scarcely drive a few miles to an interesting icon on my map before I turn around and go somewhere else.
When I finally do have a few seconds to breathe and go wherever my heart desires, random events pop up. Take, for example, the first time I went to a gun shop to add a rifle to my arsenal. The second I left the store, a car pulled up, a bunch of thugs jumped out, and I saw the flicker of a screen telling me about the hand-to-hand combat controls before shots started ringing in my ears and I died. The first time I went to an ATM to deposit some cash into my freshly opened bank account, somebody waiting outside the ATM held me up.
I don't even have the time to enjoy missions. For example, my homie Cal came over to relax and play some video games, as in actually play games on GTA's version of a PlayStation. Rather than give me an option to leave the game when I'm finished, I only got to play for a few minutes before a cutscene interrupted me. And then suddenly it was the next morning, and I had a call I needed to go deal with, so there wasn't any time to jump back in. Not to mention, I had to go through my whole workout routine, shoot some hoops, eat something, and shower before I headed out.
Once again, none of this stuff is necessarily bad in isolation. It's immersive, even. But taken as a whole, the way GTA 6 paces its enormous store of content makes me feel the same as when I'm starting a new MMO, when it hits me with dozens of screens to click through and systems I need to learn before I get a chance to play.
The Costs Just Keep Adding Up
In a perfect world, I could separate a game's monetization from its content, but that's not the world we live in. No review of Genshin Impact ignores the fact it's a gacha game, after all. And given that GTA 6 is the best-rated video game in history and made by one of the biggest developers in the world, I'm less inclined to forgive its faults than some indie game in Early Access.
GTA 6's list price is $80, and that doesn't include a disc even if you get it in stores. But GTA 6 doesn't really cost $80. It costs $100 because the Ultimate Edition not only includes extra cars and weapons but also unlocks access to map locations. For the most anticipated video game in history, who wants to experience a lesser version of it?
Again, I'm not saying GTA 6's monetization makes it a bad game. But it's not unreasonable to think that an all-digital, $100 single-player game deserves to lose, at least, a couple points off a review score.
No, Even GTA 6 Isn't Above Reproach
GTA 6 might not be for me, and that's fine. Not every game has to appeal to everyone. But take an honest look at the game, free of the hype, reviews, and success, and ask yourself whether it truly is almost perfect, as the Metacritic rating suggests. I don't agree with the Metacritic user score of 5.7 out of 10, but these reviews do make a point worth taking seriously: GTA 6 isn't perfect.
