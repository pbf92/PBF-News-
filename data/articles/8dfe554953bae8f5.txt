Anthropic capped IPO deal teams at four bankers and sought six-month bars on working for OpenAI and other rivals
- Anthropic told some of its 22 underwriters to limit deal teams to four senior bankers, according to people familiar with the matter cited by Reuters [1].
- It asked some senior bank officers to sign documents keeping core deal-team members from working with rivals including OpenAI, Cursor, DeepSeek and Hugging Face for six months after the listing; one banker declined to sign, Reuters reported [1].
- Most banks reviewed the prospectus on NetRoadshow, which logs who views each page, watermarks it with the banker's name and blocks saved copies [1].
- Anthropic and SpaceX both negotiated zero underwriter spread on greenshoe shares, which University of Florida professor emeritus Jay Ritter said most companies could not demand [1].
Anthropic told some of the 22 banks underwriting its initial public offering to limit their deal teams to four senior bankers, and asked some senior bank officers to sign documents barring core team members from working with rivals including OpenAI, Cursor, DeepSeek and Hugging Face for six months after the listing, Reuters reported on Sunday in an exclusive citing people familiar with the matter [1]. One banker declined to sign, according to the report [1].
Bankers and lawyers told Reuters it is rare for an IPO to impose exclusivity that broad, especially on individual bankers [1]. In past rivalries such as Uber and Lyft or Trulia and Zillow, banks could pick a side; Anthropic needed a broad syndicate and still asked for the restrictions, the report said [1]. One person said the four-banker cap likely stemmed from fear of leaks, and that banks found ways to work around it [1]. Reuters had reported details of Anthropic's draft S-1 nearly four weeks before it became public [1].
Watermarks and a 2 a.m. filing
The deal, code-named "Project Lantern," kept most banks away from PDFs [1]. Anthropic shared documents through NetRoadshow, which logs who viewed which page, stamps pages with bankers' names and does not let users save copies, people familiar with the matter told Reuters [1].
Anthropic planned to file its amended S-1 with a price range and launch marketing on Nov. 10 [1]. The night before, a law firm sent banks a PDF about a share sale in Canada, then an email telling them to disregard it because it "contains incorrect price range and share information," according to the email, which Reuters saw [1]. The correct filing went out through NetRoadshow around 2 a.m. New York time, leaving banks a few hours to sign off [1].
Bankers' complaints, per the report, center on the compressed timeline, last-minute changes, delays in sharing financial projections and rushed approvals [1]. Anthropic, Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, the lead underwriters on both the Anthropic and SpaceX deals, declined to comment to Reuters; OpenAI did not respond [1].
The SpaceX template and who holds the power
Banks still did well. Anthropic's IPO, which eclipsed SpaceX's on Thursday, earned them more than half a billion dollars in fees, against about half a billion from SpaceX's June listing, Reuters reported [1]. MLQ.ai reported earlier that the final prospectus put Anthropic's fee pool at about $550 million (our report). For SpaceX, Elon Musk's team dictated how the shares were marketed, to whom and in what order sizes, and banks signed on without being told what they would be paid, according to the people Reuters spoke with [1].
"These companies certainly did have some market power to tell underwriters that it's not going to be business as usual," said Jay Ritter, a professor emeritus at the University of Florida who studies IPOs [1]. Both companies negotiated zero underwriter spread on greenshoe, or overallotment, shares, meaning banks earned no fee on those extra shares. "I don't think most companies are going to be able to demand zero spread on overallotment shares," Ritter said [1].
The share slide
Anthropic shares jumped 17% on their first day and closed down 8.5% on Friday, Reuters reported [1], at $88.91, erasing about $109 billion of market value, according to a summary of a Wall Street Journal report that described finger-pointing among Anthropic's advisers after the drop [2] (our report). The IPO was priced at $83. SpaceX peaked at $201.80 on June 16 and fell below its $135 IPO price in mid-July, Reuters noted [1]. The Anthropic slide came in a week when the Nasdaq lost about 10% (our weekly wrap).
