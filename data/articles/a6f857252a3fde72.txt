TITLE: Digital Ad Study Maps Performance Across Targeting Breadth and Creative Depth
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A longitudinal field study examining digital advertising configurations has evaluated how targeting breadth and creative personalization depth jointly shape return on ad spend across different stages of the marketing funnel. Conducted within a coffee-focused e-commerce setting, the investigation tracked twenty-four distinct campaign configurations over fifty-two consecutive weeks from August 2024 through July 2025. Rather than utilizing user-level random assignment, the researchers employed a quasi-experimental design using campaign-level variations implemented through native advertising infrastructure. The study collected a balanced panel of 1,248 weekly campaign observations across Meta Ads Manager and Google Analytics 4. The empirical findings reveal that while audience engagement operates as the primary positive driver of campaign revenue efficiency, increasing creative personalization depth produces nuanced and competing performance outcomes. Furthermore, high-performing campaign configurations formed distinct strategic profiles rather than conforming to a single universally optimal advertising strategy across marketing funnel stages.
The operational context of contemporary digital commerce has heightened the urgency of understanding tactical personalization mechanisms. Global investments in digital advertising exceeded 790 billion United States dollars in 2024, representing 72.7 percent of worldwide advertising expenditures. This rapid expansion has been supported by algorithmic targeting technologies, mobile commerce adoption, and automated optimization tools embedded in commercial platforms. Despite these advanced platforms, marketing practitioners regularly encounter substantial uncertainty when configuring campaign parameters across progressive marketing stages. Prior literature has documented that while personalized messaging can increase relevance, its effectiveness remains heterogeneous and context-dependent. Studies have noted that excessive or ill-timed personalization may trigger psychological reactance, while narrowing audience segmentation to heighten relevance can reduce overall reach and scalability. However, integrative field studies evaluating targeting breadth, retargeting mechanisms, and creative depth within a unified empirical framework have remained comparatively sparse in advertising literature.
To investigate these dynamics, the researchers established an analytical framework distinguishing between personalization width and personalization depth. Personalization width captures audience selection scope and funnel positioning, encompassing the structural contrast between first-party custom audiences and algorithmically generated lookalike audiences calibrated at one percent and five percent similarity thresholds. In contrast, personalization depth reflects individual-level content adaptation, contrasting standard retargeting based on general behavioral signals against dynamic retargeting driven by granular interactions such as cart abandonment or product viewing recency. Depth also encompasses creative complexity, distinguishing static, standardized creative assets from dynamically assembled, behavior-contingent creatives. The theoretical foundations of this framework draw upon the Elaboration Likelihood Model, which posits that personalization acts as a central informational cue when personal relevance and motivation are high, but functions as a peripheral heuristic cue when consumer motivation is low.
The twelve-month quasi-experimental field study was situated within an online coffee retailer, selected because coffee represents a repeat-purchase, moderate-involvement category with short decision cycles and diverse product options suitable for testing varied creative and retargeting treatments. The researchers executed twenty-four campaign configurations concurrently, maintaining identical weekly budgets and normalized audience sizes through fixed targeting buckets to prevent scale disparities from distorting performance comparisons. Data aggregated at the campaign configuration-week level yielded 1,248 observations spanning thirty-six internal campaign identifiers. Meta Ads Manager supplied measurements regarding impressions, clicks, advertising spend, conversions, and return on ad spend, while Google Analytics 4 provided aggregated tracking for behavioral events including pageviews, add-to-cart actions, and verified purchases. Because campaign delivery relied on native platform algorithms without individual randomization, the investigators explicitly treated all estimated relationships as associational and predictive rather than strictly causal treatment effects.
Systematic data preprocessing prepared the balanced panel for predictive machine learning and econometric analysis. Categorical indicators comprising funnel stage, retargeting logic, audience segment, and creative personalization depth were transformed using one-hot encoding via a scikit-learn ColumnTransformer pipeline to avoid imposing artificial ordinal relationships. Screening eliminated edge-case observations with zero audience size or implausibly high click-through rates. The resulting categorical distribution across the 1,248 configuration-weeks included 624 top-of-funnel observations, 936 standard retargeting instances compared to 312 dynamic retargeting instances, 624 custom audience deployments, and 936 low personalization creative deployments. Numerical features were standardized where appropriate to ensure algorithm convergence. Descriptive statistics across the panel revealed a normalized mean audience size of 1,159 individuals with a standard deviation of 200, spanning from a minimum of 800 to a maximum of 1,499.
Additional summary metrics detailed baseline campaign performance across the observed year. Across all 1,248 weekly observations, click-through rates averaged 1.19 percent with a standard deviation of 0.56 percent, spanning an empirical range from 0.30 percent to 2.50 percent. Weekly conversions averaged 1.19 per campaign-week with a standard deviation of 1.15, while weekly advertising spend averaged 231.70 United States dollars with a standard deviation of 78.40 dollars, ranging between 81.60 and 446.20 dollars. Return on ad spend exhibited an average of 2.43 with a standard deviation of 1.10, ranging from 0.80 to 5.00 across the panel. Diagnostic evaluations confirmed that multicollinearity was minimal, with variance inflation factor values for click-through rate, audience size, spend, and creative depth all measuring below 1.50. Furthermore, one-way analysis of variance tests across funnel stages revealed no statistically significant isolated differences for click-through rate, conversions, return on ad spend, or spend.
Predictive modeling using a Random Forest Regressor and complementary standardized linear regression demonstrated that engagement metrics and campaign configurations jointly explain return on ad spend. Within the fitted Random Forest model, feature-importance scores identified retargeting type, advertising spend, audience size, and click-through rate as prominent predictive components, whereas conversion counts and isolated funnel-stage indicators contributed more modest predictive utility. In the standardized linear regression model, click-through rate emerged as the single strongest positive predictor of return on ad spend, confirming that capturing audience attention represents a critical prerequisite for revenue efficiency. Audience size also maintained a positive association with return on ad spend, indicating that broader reach supports economic efficiency when paired with appropriate messaging. Conversely, total advertising spend demonstrated a negative standardized relationship with return on ad spend, reflecting diminishing marginal revenue efficiency at elevated expenditure levels.
The empirical models uncovered notable nuances regarding creative execution and retargeting mechanisms. Dynamic retargeting consistently outperformed standard retargeting within middle-of-funnel and bottom-of-funnel campaigns, confirming the value of behaviorally aligned delivery when users exhibit established brand familiarity. However, regression models revealed that campaigns utilizing low-complexity, static creatives slightly outperformed high-complexity dynamic creatives in overall direct comparisons. To unpack this unexpected dynamic, the researchers conducted a formal mediation analysis specifying creative personalization depth as the predictor, click-through rate as the mediator, and return on ad spend as the primary outcome. Results showed that high creative personalization depth was positively associated with click-through rate with a path coefficient of 0.607, while click-through rate related positively to return on ad spend with a path coefficient of 0.091.
Crucially, the mediation analysis revealed that once click-through rate was controlled, the direct path coefficient between high creative personalization depth and return on ad spend became negative at minus 0.083. A nonparametric bootstrap test using 5,000 resamples estimated an indirect effect of approximately 0.056 with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning from minus 0.009 to 0.123. Because this confidence interval crossed zero, the indirect pathway was statistically unstable, generating an estimated total effect of approximately minus 0.027. This empirical configuration illustrates competitive mediation, wherein the positive performance contribution transmitted through heightened click-through rates is partially offset by an opposing direct negative influence on revenue efficiency. Although unmeasured in the field data, the authors noted that cognitive load, perceived manipulation, or privacy reactance provide theoretically plausible mechanisms that could explain why heightened creative complexity suppresses direct efficiency.
To investigate operational heterogeneity, an exploratory k-means clustering analysis with three clusters was performed on the top quartile of campaign-week observations ranked by return on ad spend. Cluster 0, designated as a defensive portfolio, demonstrated an average return on ad spend of 1.88 with a low standard deviation of 0.72, accompanied by an average click-through rate of 0.98 percent and mean spend of 186 dollars. This configuration combined broad audiences with low-complexity creatives in upper-funnel settings, delivering stable acquisition and reach efficiency. Cluster 1, designated as a core portfolio, achieved a balanced profile with a mean return on ad spend of 2.27, a standard deviation of 0.96, an average click-through rate of 1.07 percent, and higher spend averaging 305 dollars, leveraging custom audiences and dynamic retargeting across middle and lower funnel stages.
Cluster 2, representing an opportunistic portfolio, achieved the highest return on ad spend of 3.36 alongside elevated volatility indicated by a standard deviation of 1.08, paired with a 1.63 percent click-through rate and 205 dollars average spend. Characterized by high engagement and selective targeting, this portfolio delivered maximum conversion efficiency but exhibited greater sensitivity to performance variance. The authors emphasized that the resulting clusters represent heuristic strategy profiles rather than discrete campaign archetypes. Taken together, the findings demonstrate that advertising performance does not depend on universally maximizing personalization depth. Instead, optimal execution requires balancing targeting breadth and creative complexity across the marketing funnel, coordinating simple, fluent creatives for low-involvement acquisition while reserving dynamic, behaviorally contingent retargeting for audiences demonstrating verified brand familiarity and cognitive readiness.
