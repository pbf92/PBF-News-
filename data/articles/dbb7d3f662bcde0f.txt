TITLE: School cellphone restrictions may improve math and reading, study finds
OUTLET: KOTA Territory News

RAPID CITY, S.D. (KOTA) - Limiting students’ access to cellphones during the school day may do more than reduce classroom distractions. A new study suggests strict school cellphone policies could also lead to stronger academic growth.
Researchers from Harvard University, NWEA and Dartmouth College analyzed data from 3.3 million students at 4,600 schools across the country.
They found students at schools with strict cellphone restrictions showed greater improvement in math and reading than students at schools without those policies.
The strongest gains were reported at schools requiring students to put their phones in lockable pouches during the school day. Those students made academic progress equivalent to one to two additional weeks of learning each year.
Researchers also found fewer students at those schools experienced significant declines in reading achievement. The benefits appeared to continue beyond the first year after the restrictions were introduced.
The findings come as cellphone use among children continues to grow. Data cited by researchers shows nearly one in four children in the United States owns a cellphone by age 8.
The study adds to broader research examining how screen time affects children. A separate review published in JAMA Pediatrics analyzed information from 2.8 million children and adolescents across 64 countries. It found greater screen exposure was associated with lower academic achievement, weaker language skills and more emotional and behavioral difficulties.
Supporters of school cellphone restrictions say limiting access during class can help students focus and stay engaged. The new findings suggest those policies may also have measurable academic benefits as schools continue to weigh how to manage cellphone use.
