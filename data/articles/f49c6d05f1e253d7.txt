TITLE: GTA VI: How one school is battling the 'GTA flu' with warnings to parents and prizes for pupils
OUTLET: BBC

School offers prizes to pupils for turning up to class on day GTA 6 comes out
Belfast Boys' Model school issued letters to parents, saying that the release of the game on Thursday coincided with GCSE maths exams.
The school said that "activities such as playing Grand Theft Auto can wait until the weekend".
On Monday, the school added that pupils who attended school on both Thursday and Friday would be entered into a draw for a £70 gift card for either PlayStation or Xbox.
It also said that "all Year 11 students and some Year 12 students" would be sitting a GCSE maths exam on Thursday, the same day the game comes out.
"Good attendance is one of the strongest indicators of academic success," the letter added.
Mary Montgomery, who is principal of the school, said "the primary focus" of her letter was Thursday's Mathematics examination and "encouraging our boys to be in school and ready to achieve their best".
"The results at GCSE have been fantastic in recent years and we want to continue that success for the boys," she said.
"It also highlighted the launch of GTA as something which might entice some boys to stay at home. However, the games vouchers were a pupil-led incentive."
On Monday, the school announced that all pupils in attendance on Thursday and Friday would be entered into a draw to win one of two £70 gift cards "for PlayStation/Xbox".
The school's head boy said "the idea came from us as pupils".
"We wanted to do something positive to mark International Men's Day and, importantly, to raise money for a charity that supports male victims of domestic violence," he said.
"For us, the most important thing was having as many boys as possible in school so everyone could get involved and support the fundraising."
He added that gaming can help the pupils "switch off and manage some of the stress around exams, which is important with GCSE modules this week".
Speaking to The Nolan Show, a former school principal Kathleen O'Hare said the decision to send the letter to parents was "very wise".
"The cult of this game is so large that parents will be put under extreme pressure at times," she said.
O'Hare added that it has been "proven" that missing a day of school "really does matter".
Pete Wray, a Ulster Unionist Party councillor who works with children, said "there is a sizable amount of young people that are in our education system and completely disengage".
"They take an odd day off, and once they've got one day off, they're anxious about going back because they know they're going to be behind, and that leads to more and more problems," he said.
Wray said he will be imploring young people not to miss school to play the game, but that he "completely understands how people can become engrossed in it".
What is GTA 6?
Considered one of the most highly-anticipated video games in history, the game is the latest in the long-running Grand Theft Auto series.
The game series, which has attracted controversy in the past over its depiction of violence and crime, is also the most expensive game ever made, with estimates putting development costs at more than $1bn (£866m).
The previous game in the series, GTA 5, sold nearly 230 million copies, making it one of the best-selling games of all time, which has generated billions of dollars in revenue for developer Rockstar.
GTA 6 will feature its first ever playable female protagonist in a 3D setting - Lucia - alongside her partner in life and crime, Jason, as a second playable character.
Like other GTA games before it, the sixth chapter is set in a fictional US state - this time in Leonida, which is Rockstar's version of Florida.
