TITLE: Trade Desk picks Attain, with 15 million consumers, to anchor ad measurement
OUTLET: PPC Land

Attain, a US company that collects purchase data from people who use its apps, today said it will be the inaugural data partner for Open Measurement, the advertising measurement framework that The Trade Desk plans to launch before the end of the year. The arrangement makes Attain's transaction records a "foundational input" to how The Trade Desk will judge which media drove sales. It comes eight days after chief executive Jeff Green told analysts that the product was on its way and that, unlike Amazon and Google, his company does not grade its own homework.
The announcement, datelined New York, November 17, 2026, carries statements from Brian Mandelbaum, Attain's chief executive and co-founder, and Kristi Argyilan, chief commercial officer of The Trade Desk. Neither company gave a price, a methodology, a list of supported channels or an exact launch date. What the release does settle is where the numbers will come from: purchases observed among people who have given Attain explicit permission to use their data, rather than retailer feeds, panels projected onto the population, or surveys asking people what they remember buying.
In Short
The Trade Desk, one of the largest independent ad-buying platforms, will measure what ads bought through it achieve partly with purchase data from Attain, a company whose apps give consumers everyday value in exchange for permission to use their spending data. If it works as described, credit for sales could move from the last ad seen or clicked before a purchase toward the brand campaigns and premium publishers that came earlier, which matters to advertisers, publishers and agencies weighing the open web against Amazon and Google. The product launches later this quarter, but the method, the price and any outside check on the results have not been published, so the first campaigns measured with it will be the real test.
What Attain announced
Attain describes its role in three different ways. The headline calls it the "Inaugural Data Partner." The body names it "the foundational data partner of The Trade Desk's Open Measurement framework," and a later paragraph says Attain is "the early design partner." According to Attain, the collaboration has run for nearly a year. "Inaugural" implies that other data partners will follow, but the release names none and does not say whether Attain's position is exclusive.
The stated purpose is twofold. "Open Measurement will launch later this quarter and is designed to give advertisers an objective view of what drives business growth and give publishers full credit for the value their media creates," according to the release. Later this quarter means by December 31, 2026. The document uses "framework" and "initiative" interchangeably; on the November 9 earnings call, Green called it an open measurement product and said it would formally launch later this quarter.
On Attain's contribution, the wording is specific. "As the early design partner with The Trade Desk, Attain is contributing its permissioned transaction data as a foundational input to Open Measurement's methodology," the release states. It goes on to describe the effort as "a step toward a more connected approach to advertising, where real-world outcomes can inform not only how media is valued, but how future investments are planned and optimized."
Mandelbaum drew the same line between looking back and looking forward. "Advertisers want to know what their media actually drove, and that answer starts with real transaction data," he said. "No single platform or brand has the complete picture. Attain brings an independent view of real consumer spending that connects media investment to business outcomes, helping advertisers invest with confidence and publishers get credit for the value they create. But the real opportunity goes beyond measuring what happened. It's using that same purchase intelligence to inform what happens next, so every campaign can make the next one smarter." The second half of that statement matters commercially. Measuring a campaign is one use of purchase data; deciding where the next budget goes is another, and it is the one that moves money.
The name may cause some confusion. IAB Tech Lab's Open Measurement SDK, usually shortened to OM SDK, is the industry software that runs verification scripts inside apps to check whether ads were seen; in October, PPC Land reported that a sandbox version had cut Android ad session start times to 11 milliseconds. The Trade Desk's Open Measurement is about sales outcomes. The release does not mention the IAB standard or suggest any connection to it.
What has been said about Open Measurement so far
The call column paraphrases Green's remarks as PPC Land reported them on November 10. The release column quotes the release.
Where the data comes from
The release is built around a contrast with how sales measurement is often done. "Attain's transaction data is sourced directly from millions of consumers with their permission, with no dependence on retailer data feeds, panel projections or survey recall," it states. According to Attain, the data "captures observed purchases in real time across every merchant and category," with CPG, retail, QSR, travel and e-commerce named among them, connecting "marketing investment to what people bought, wherever they bought it."
Each of the three alternatives it names has a known limit. A retailer's data feed describes only the purchases made at that retailer, so a campaign that sends people to a rival store or a restaurant shows no sale. Panel projections take a recruited sample and weight it to stand for the whole population, which introduces modelling error. Survey recall depends on what respondents remember and are willing to report. Attain's claim is that it sees purchases directly, across merchants, as they happen.
Attain's data also comes from a defined group of people. According to the company's description, it connects "directly with over 15 million consumers" through "owned and operated apps, spanning financial wellness and shopping rewards." Those consumers "receive everyday value in exchange for explicit permission to use their data for research, insights and targeted advertising." The apps are not named, and the release does not say what the everyday value is. Fifteen million people who installed a rewards or money app are a large group, but they are a subset of American shoppers. How results observed among them are applied to campaigns that reach mostly other people is the problem panel projections were designed to solve, and the release does not say how Open Measurement handles it.
How the data reaches Open Measurement, according to Attain
The release describes a chain with five links:
- Consumers install one of Attain's owned and operated apps. According to Attain, these span "financial wellness and shopping rewards."
- In return for "everyday value," those consumers give "explicit permission to use their data for research, insights and targeted advertising."
- Attain observes their purchases "in real time across every merchant and category." The release lists CPG, retail, QSR, travel and e-commerce, and says the data does not depend on retailer data feeds, panel projections or survey recall.
- Attain contributes that transaction data to The Trade Desk "as a foundational input to Open Measurement's methodology."
- Open Measurement, due later this quarter, connects media investment to those purchases. According to the release, the aim is to show advertisers what drove growth, credit publishers for the value their media creates, and let outcomes "inform not only how media is valued, but how future investments are planned and optimized."
One step is missing from this description. Measuring an ad's effect on sales requires knowing which of the people in Attain's data saw which ads bought through The Trade Desk. The release does not say how ad exposures are matched to Attain's consumers, which identifiers are used, or whether the matching happens in a clean room or elsewhere.
Attain's description changed in 12 days
Twelve days ago, Attain announced that its purchase-based audiences were available inside Amazon Ads DVA+. The two announcements describe the same business slightly differently.
Neither announcement explains the change from 14 million to 15 million or the difference between daily refreshes and real-time capture.
The same data for targeting and for measurement
Attain is not only a measurement supplier. Its company description lists "audience activation, insights, in-flight optimization and measurement," which means its purchase data is used both to choose who sees ads and to judge whether those ads worked. On November 5, PPC Land reported that Attain had also been, by its own account, one of the first automated offline sales lift measurement partners of what was then Amazon DSP in 2025. So the company that will anchor The Trade Desk's case against the walled gardens also sells into Amazon, one of the two rivals Green named. The release does not suggest any conflict between the two relationships, and nothing in it says the Trade Desk role is exclusive.
The Trade Desk has used Attain's measurement before. In an October 5 post on retail data, the company described an analysis of 11 brands and eight retailer datasets that used Attain as a measurement layer independent of any single retailer, and claimed that 90% of campaigns beat Attain's benchmarks for new-buyer ROAS by an average of 122%. That post did not name the brands or retailers, give a study period, or describe the control group.
When one dataset both selects an audience and scores the result, buyers usually ask how the method avoids measuring the people it was built to find. The standard answer is incrementality testing, which compares people who saw a campaign with a similar group who did not. Green described exactly that approach on November 9. Argyilan's statement today points the same way, criticising measurement that takes "credit for sales that were often going to happen anyway." The release itself does not say whether Open Measurement uses holdout or control groups.
The case against the last click
Argyilan, who joined The Trade Desk as chief commercial officer in July after leading Uber's advertising business, put the argument plainly. "Last-click and last-view measurement reward whatever ad ran closest to the purchase and take credit for sales that were often going to happen anyway. That's left branding underfunded for years, because the premium media where brands actually get built rarely gets credit for the heavy lifting," she said.
Last-click attribution gives all the credit for a sale to the final ad a buyer clicked; last-view gives it to the final ad they saw. Both favour search ads, retargeting and retail media placements at the bottom of the funnel, because those ads appear just before a purchase. The alternatives are well established. Multi-touch attribution spreads credit across the touchpoints a buyer encountered. Marketing mix modelling estimates the effect of each channel statistically from aggregate spend and sales. Incrementality tests measure the difference that exposure makes. A guide published by the analytics company Funnel, which PPC Land covered on November 9, argued that confusion arises when a single method is asked to answer questions it was not built for, and showed how a healthy 3.0 average ROAS can hide a return of $0.40 on each extra dollar.
Argyilan's second remark carried the competitive message. "Open Measurement is built on real transactions, not self-graded homework, and Attain's purchase data is a big part of why we can say that. Together we're setting a new standard for advertising accountability," she said. The homework line repeats Green, who told analysts on November 9 that the walled gardens of Amazon and Google grade their own homework and that The Trade Desk does not own media it is trying to sell.
