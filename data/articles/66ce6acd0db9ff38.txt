TITLE: Tears in court as husband who drugged and raped wife for 20 years gives evidence
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

Perhaps it was the moment that the enormity of his crimes finally hit him. As he made his way into the witness box, where his wife had calmly answered questions for 45 minutes the day before, her husband broke down on several occasions as he sought to explain himself.
Why, after initially pleading not guilty, then changing his plea, was this the moment he wanted to speak out? “So the court knows the truth of what happened to my wife, so my wife knows and I’m here to tell the truth and give my account of what actually happened," he said.
In a navy suit and tie, the husband, a man in his 60s from Stockport, faced questioning from prosecutors all day on Thursday (November 26). "For you, why is it important that you are here in this trial?"," Louise Oakley KC, the lead prosecutor, asked.

It was then that he broke down for the first time, appearing visibly emotional. He apologised, as he took a few seconds to compose himself. "Take your time," Judge Maurice Greene told him. The husband said: "So I can tell the truth of what happened to [his wife]."
Then, the prosecutor laid out charge after charge which he has admitted, 60 crimes committed over a 20 year period until his arrest in October last year. They included conspiracy to rape, rape, assault by penetration, and conspiracy to administer a substance with intent.
Minutes after, he again broke down as he recalled the process he underwent as he was interviewed by police, before being called as a witness for the prosecution. He told how he found it 'very difficult'.
Becoming emotional, he paused and explained that he felt 'guilt and shame' for causing his wife 'pain'. Ms Oakley asked: "Who is responsible for that pain?" "I am," the husband said.
After a marriage that lasted for decades, he recalled first starting using online chat sites in 2004. "It was for the sexual thrill, I suppose," asked why he did so.
At first he posed online as his wife, or a woman more generally, to get access to chat sites, he said. "It's difficult to say," he said when asked how he started. "The first thing was talking about sex with women, group sex. Things like that to start off with, and it developed from there."
Asked what it developed to, the husband said: "To be there when somebody was having sex with [his wife]. And I was there." "What was it that you wanted to happen?," the prosecutor asked. "What was your fantasy?"
He replied: "I wouldn't really say it was a fantasy when things changed. It made me feel bad about myself." "What did you want to happen?," the prosecutor asked.
The husband said: "It was to make me feel irrelevant while somebody else was having sex with [his wife]. To cause my pain." The prosecutor asked: "Did you want other men to have sex with your wife?" He replied: "I didn't want it but I thought it had to happen." The husband then became emotional again.
It eventually led to the husband inviting 'complete strangers' into his home, jurors heard. He said he had only messaged two men, Graham Brougham and Sean Peers, for a matter of hours before they attended.
"So you invited a complete stranger?," the judge asked, interjecting. The husband replied: "I had a message on Fabswingers, when Brougham had left I looked at my phone. He had messaged me saying could he come around."
"But you knew nothing about him?," the judge asked. "No, I didn't know anything about him," the husband said.
The husband added: "I didn't know him, any of them, they were all strangers. Every person I have messaged on a website, any person that attended, I didn’t know any of them.” After spending about four hours being in the witness box, the husband is set to return tomorrow for further, searching questioning about his crimes.

The Manchester Evening News and other media organisations are not naming the husband in order to protect his victim's anonymity. The nine men on trial deny the charges they face. Seven of the nine defendants have been described as 'contact offenders', who jurors heard visited the couple's home expecting her to be 'wasted' and 'out of it'.
It is alleged the 'contact offenders' are taxi driver Philip Wild, 59; recruitment agency worker Mohammed Sabir, 28; building supplies employee Jordan Wallace, 31; industrial cleaning firm worker Alan Keelan, 42; retiree Robert Stewart, 70; sheet metal worker Richard Townsend, 38; and senior paramedic Jonathan Kirk, 43.
The Crown alleges 'non-contact' offenders, who allegedly 'hid behind a screen' as they conspired to administer substances to the wife, are kitchen firm sales rep David Graves, 60; and self-employed mechanic Daniel Rayner, 42. Four other men - Keith Fotheringham, Karl Lindsay, Sean Peers and Graham Brougham - pleaded guilty at earlier hearings to various offences linked to the case.
- David Graves, 59, of Milton Avenue, Derbyshire, denies one count of conspiracy to rape; one count of conspiracy to commit assault by penetration; and one count of conspiracy to administer a substance with intent
- Robert Stewart, 70, of Sandpiper Drive, Stockport, denies one count of conspiracy to rape. He admitted conspiring to administer a substance with intent and two counts of rape
- Philip Wild, 58, of St Davids Road, Cheadle, denies one count of conspiracy to rape; one count of sexual assault; one count of attempted rape; and one count of assault by penetration
- Daniel Rayner, 42, of Sherwood Drive, Whitstable, denies one count of conspiracy to rape; one count of conspiracy to commit assault by penetration; and one count of conspiracy to administer a substance with intent
- Mohammed Sabir, 28, of no fixed address, denies one count of conspiracy to rape and three counts of rape
- Jordan Wallace, 31, of Hindsford Close, Wythenshawe, denies one count of rape and one count of attempted rape
- Alan Keelan, 42, of Foscarn Drive, Wythenshawe, denies two counts of attempted rape and one count of assault by penetration.
- Richard Townsend, 37, of Woodland Street, Heywood, denies one count of assault by penetration
- Jonathan Kirk, 43, of Telford Close, Macclesfield, denies one count of conspiracy to rape and two counts of rape
Five other men pleaded guilty at earlier hearings to various offences linked to the case, the jury has been told. They are Keith Fotheringham, 59, of Cleghorn Street, Dundee; Karl Lindsay, 56, of Wordsworth Drive, Taunton; Sean Peers, 38, formerly of Sandwich Road in Eccles, now of Stockport; Graham Brougham, 74, of Birtwistle Road, Northwich, Cheshire, and Tommy Golden, 59, of HMP Risley.
