UK public verdict on Gordon Ramsay pick for Top Gear presenting role revealed in YouGov poll
Viewers are split down the middle on the BBC's choice to drive the show's 2028 comeback.
The British public are divided on the BBC's decision to hand Gordon Ramsay the keys to a revived Top Gear, according to new polling from YouGov.
The survey found 35 per cent of Britons think Ramsay is a good choice to front the motoring show, while 34 per cent see it as a bad choice. A further 31 per cent said they did not know.
The BBC confirmed earlier this week that the celebrity chef will be the lead presenter when Top Gear returns in 2028, with filming expected to begin in 2027 and co-presenters still to be announced.
Opinion remains split even among the show's existing audience. Of those who describe themselves as fans of at least one previous series, 44 per cent called Ramsay a good choice, against 40 per cent who thought it was a bad one.
The poll also underlines the scale of the challenge facing the BBC as it tries to recapture the show's peak popularity.
Some 31 per cent of Britons say they are fans of the Jeremy Clarkson, Richard Hammond and James May era, which ran from 2002 to 2015, compared with just 4 per cent who say they are fans of the later revival fronted by figures including Chris Evans, Matt LeBlanc and Freddie Flintoff.
A further 6 per cent remain fans of the original Top Gear that aired from 1977 to 2001. Another 13 per cent said they liked the show but would not call themselves fans, 30 per cent expressed indifference and 16 per cent said they actively dislike it.
YouGov surveyed 5,545 adults in Great Britain aged 18 and over, with fieldwork conducted online on 26 November.
Following the announcement this week, Ramsay said: "My love affair with cars began as a child, growing up a field away from Silverstone, where I would climb over the fences and sneak in for a glimpse of the racing. That early excitement sparked a lifelong passion for cars, motor sport, beautiful design and extraordinary engineering.
"At its very best Top Gear has always been about the unexpected, surprising, entertaining and just the right amount of chaos. That's the spirit we want to build on, bringing a fresh energy and ambition to create something truly thrilling for a whole new generation of audiences. It's an incredibly exciting opportunity, and I genuinely can't wait to get started!"
Top Gear has been off air since production was halted following a serious crash involving Freddie Flintoff at the Dunsfold test track in December 2022. The BBC paused season 34 and, in November 2023, said it would rest the UK show for the foreseeable future.
