TITLE: Nasdaq Plunges 5.3% on AI Selloff; Nvidia Tumbles 14%, Down 10% for the Week
OUTLET: BigGo Finance

AI Bubble Fears Wipe Out New York Stocks: Nasdaq Plunges 5.3%, Nvidia Sinks 14%
Doubts about overinvestment in the artificial intelligence (AI) industry slammed New York stocks. On the 20th (local time), the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite plunged 5.28% from the previous session to close at 22,617.73, giving back all of its gains for the year. The one-day drop exceeded 1,200 points, and the index fell about 10% for the week, its largest weekly decline since March 2020.
The S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.39, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 949.73 points (1.93%) to close at 48,147.33. Both indexes are now more than 10% below their recent closing highs, putting them in a technical correction. The S&P 500 fell for a seventh straight session, its longest losing streak since February 2020.
Selling was concentrated in AI semiconductor and data center stocks. Nvidia, the flagship AI stock, plunged 14.01% to a new 52-week low. Reports that the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is investigating Nvidia's accounting practices added to the selling pressure. The company fell about 23% this week alone, its worst weekly performance since 2008, and is down about 48% from its all-time high on the 6th of last month. The market value wiped out since that peak amounts to $2.5 trillion.
Other chip stocks also suffered double-digit declines. AMD fell 13.26%, Micron Technology 13.86% and Broadcom 12.12%, while storage-related names such as SanDisk (-16.05%) and Western Digital (-12.02%) also plunged. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index closed down 10.73% at 8,408.59.
Anthropic, the generative AI developer that listed on the Nasdaq the previous day, also fell 9.1% to $88.30, just two days after its debut. It fell as low as $83.05 during the session, approaching its $83 IPO price. It had surged 17% above its IPO price on its first day, but gave back most of those gains in a single session.
Big Tech was not immune. Meta fell 5.85%, Tesla 7.54%, Amazon 3.98%, Alphabet 2.54% and Apple 1.82%. By contrast, defensive stocks rose as money rotated into them, with Walmart (1.52%), Costco (0.76%), Johnson & Johnson (1.28%) and Procter & Gamble (0.91%) all gaining.
Reflecting the market turmoil, the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) surged 26.20% to 35.89. It was the first time the VIX has topped 30 since March.
At the heart of the selloff is not AI demand itself but questions about investment costs and the timing of returns. Enormous sums are being poured into data centers and AI infrastructure, but skepticism is growing over when profits commensurate with that scale will materialize. That was compounded by rising borrowing costs, credit market stress and the possibility of slowing earnings growth at technology companies.
Richard Saperstein, chief investment officer at Treasury Partners, described an "AI repricing" under way that reflects a slowdown in data center construction, reduced funding for AI projects and a weakening path for tech earnings growth. Massimo Santichia, head of U.S. equities at Procyon, called it "not simply a valuation-driven correction but a risk-off phase spreading across the market."
Companies heavily dependent on external funding saw the steepest declines. CoreWeave, which rents out AI computing capacity, fell 12.1%, and Nebius dropped 16.1%. Because their business model requires securing chips and equipment before collecting fees from customers, borrowing costs and the timing of facility activation feed directly into results.
The possibility of further tightening by the U.S. Federal Reserve also weighed on sentiment. Fed Governor Lisa Cook said in remarks prepared for a speech at the Central Bank of Chile's annual conference that the current policy rate is not enough to curb inflation. "In a different environment, the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) could afford to be patient, but in the current economic situation we do not have that luxury," she said. She also noted that AI infrastructure expansion is pushing up semiconductor and equipment prices, construction labor costs and power costs, adding to inflationary pressure.
According to the CME FedWatch tool, the market is pricing in a 91.5% probability of a 0.25 percentage-point rate hike next month. The current U.S. policy rate is 3.75% to 4.00%.
Analysts say the political sphere has limited room to respond. President Donald Trump warned that he could impose hefty taxes on overseas data centers, but stocks failed to rebound. The power to levy taxes rests with Congress, making the feasibility itself uncertain. Tobin Marcus, head of policy and politics at Wolfe Research, said "the White House has no obvious switch to send the market soaring again."
Nvidia's management vacuum is another variable. After former CEO Jensen Huang resigned on the 12th, the board's refusal to allow his return has heightened uncertainty. President Trump has said he wants him back, but the board sees it differently.
Market participants are divided on the outlook. Chuck Carlson, CEO of Horizon Investment Services, sees the move as closer to "a correction within a bull market" than a bear market. Jonathan Krinsky, chief technical analyst at BTIG, noted that major indexes are below their 200-day moving averages, leaving downside risk even if a short-term rebound occurs.
Next week brings the release of the personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index, which the Fed watches closely. If inflation stays elevated, rate pressure could persist and financing conditions for AI companies could tighten further. What the market wants to confirm goes beyond whether stocks rebound after the plunge: it is whether credit stress and deteriorating earnings outlooks come to a halt.

The fallout is not confined to U.S. markets. Ripple effects are expected for Asian tech stocks deeply tied to the AI semiconductor supply chain and for emerging-market capital flows sensitive to a stronger dollar and rising rates. In particular, semiconductor sectors that rely on AI infrastructure demand such as high-bandwidth memory (HBM) will see whether data center investment plans are adjusted become the key to future earnings.
