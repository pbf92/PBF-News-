TITLE: Patzer calls again for agricultural research funding to AAFC sites to be restored
OUTLET: CochraneNow

MP says decades of regenerative organic research lost
The debate over federal agricultural research funding was back in the spotlight this week as Swift Current-Grasslands-Kindersley MP Jeremy Patzer challenged the government over cuts affecting research sites across Canada.
Speaking in the House of Commons, Patzer said agriculture remains one of Canada's most important economic sectors, supporting one in seven jobs and contributing significantly to export earnings and economic growth.
Patzer focused much of his criticism on the closure of a long-running research project at the Swift Current Research and Development Centre.
Patzer says a 20-year regenerative organic agriculture study was terminated after funding was withdrawn, resulting in the loss of research plots that had been maintained for two decades.
Long-term soil research a key concern
Patzer argued the project was designed to study how soil health, nutrient cycling and crop production evolve over many years under zero-till and regenerative management systems.
He said the findings were valuable not only for organic farmers but also for conventional producers looking to improve soil productivity and sustainability.
The Saskatchewan MP also questioned why the government continues to require environmental assessments and reporting from producers applying for certain business risk management programs while reducing support for agricultural research with environmental benefits.
Committee recommendation ignored, says Patzer
Patzer noted that members of the House of Commons Agriculture Committee from all recognized parties previously agreed on a recommendation calling for the restoration of agricultural research funding.
However, he said Budget 2026 did not include a commitment to reinstate those funds.
The Conservative MP repeatedly pressed government representatives for a timeline on when funding might return.
Government cites savings and broader investments
Responding on behalf of the government, the Parliamentary Secretary to the Minister of Finance Ryan Turnbull defended the expenditure review process that led departments to identify operational savings.
The government says spending growth has been reduced significantly and that departments were required to find efficiencies while maintaining core services.
Turnbull also argued the federal government continues to support agriculture by expanding market access, strengthening regional food systems, and investing in long-term food security initiatives.
Consultants versus research
In his final remarks, Patzer challenged government spending priorities, arguing that reducing spending on consultants could preserve research programs.
He claimed the annual cost of operating the affected research sites was comparable to departmental spending on consulting services.
The exchange highlights growing concerns within the agriculture sector over the future of publicly funded research programs and the potential impact on innovation, productivity and environmental sustainability.
