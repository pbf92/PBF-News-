TITLE: Amazon's ad status page still tracks Posts, a feature shut in July 2025
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon Ads revised its Support Center article on the Amazon Ads status page on November 20, 2026, and the revised text describes a smaller, newer-sounding service than the one it documents. According to the help article, the status page shows "the current status of services like campaign creation and campaign management, ad delivery, and reporting for sponsored ads and DVA+." It lists nine features in four groups and names 19 countries across three regions. On the morning of November 21, the page at status.ads.amazon.com displayed 17 components in six groups, filed programmatic buying under the heading "Amazon DSP", and kept a row for Posts, the brand content feed Amazon closed in July 2025.
In Short
Amazon updated the help page for its Amazon Ads status page, the public site that shows whether campaign tools, ad delivery and reports are working, on November 20, 2026. The page is what advertisers, agencies and software makers check when something breaks, yet the help text lists nine monitored features in 19 countries while the live page shows 17 components, still says Amazon DSP instead of DVA+, and still tracks the discontinued Posts feature. The status page's machine-readable feed uses the older names as well, so monitoring tools built on it see "Amazon DSP" and "Creative management", not the labels in Amazon's newer documentation.
What the November 20 help page says
The article sits in the Support Center under Troubleshooting, beneath the Amazon Ads Agent header carried by Amazon's recent help pages. Its summary line is a single sentence: "Stay updated with the latest health status information of Amazon Ads services using the Amazon Ads Status page." When a disruption occurs, according to the page, the status site gives "more details about the event, including the description and status updates for that service."
Coverage is organised by region rather than by country. "The page shows the status of Amazon Ads at a region level," according to Amazon. "The status will apply to all countries in the region unless mentioned otherwise."
The page then states: "Current status will be provided for the following features." Four groups follow. Sponsored ads carries campaign creation, campaign management and ad delivery. DVA+ carries campaign creation, campaign management, "Ad (formerly known as Creative) Management" and ad delivery. A group headed "Measurements and Reports" carries Reports. Brand content carries Stores. That is nine features in all.
Four status levels can appear, according to the help page: No known issues, marked with a green tick; Informational, with a blue "i"; Degraded, with an orange warning triangle; and Disrupted, with a red exclamation mark. A note explains that the Last updated field records the time the status was last refreshed on the page, and that every time is displayed in the viewer's local time zone.
How Amazon says a status entry reads
The help page walks through one example entry and labels five parts of it. The example is a sponsored ads campaign creation problem, flagged with a red exclamation mark, carrying the message "We're having some issues with campaign creation and are working to fix them. We're sorry for any inconvenience." Its timestamp reads March 08, 2023 at 12:02 - two months before Amazon's public launch note for the status page, dated May 10, 2023. According to Amazon, the five parts are:
- Impacted area: "The status and message apply to sponsored ads."
- Impacted activity: "The status and message apply to campaign creation."
- Date: "The date and time the status and message were published."
- Impact: "The status is applicable to both Amazon Ads console and Amazon Ads API." In the example, this line reads "Impacts console and API".
- Message: "This is the current published message and additional information about the status."
The fourth field is the one that matters most to agencies running campaigns through software rather than the browser. The status page's data schema allows three values for it: console only, api only and both console and api. An outage that hits only the API would leave a team clicking through the console untouched, and the reverse is also possible.
Seventeen rows on the live page
At 08:24 GMT+1 (07:24 UTC) on November 21, 2026, the status page showed green ticks for all three regions and for every component in the North and Latin America view. It also showed two tabs, Current Status and History, an English (US) language selector, and three links at the foot of the page: Status JSON, Schema and RSS. Every component carried the same 08:24 timestamp except Streams, which read 08:25.
The live page is organised into six groups, not four. The comparison below sets the help page against the page it describes and against the published data schema behind it.
Eight live components do not appear in the help text: bulksheets for both sponsored ads and Amazon DSP, Amazon Marketing Cloud, Posts, Streams, and the three account rows. Some are long-standing products. Spreadsheet-based bulk editing arrived in the DSP in January 2024, when Amazon DSP introduced Bulksheets for contextual campaign creation, and the Sponsored Products version picked up an off-Amazon serving column on June 8, 2026. Amazon Marketing Cloud is Amazon's clean room for querying advertising and shopping signals. Amazon Marketing Stream pushes hourly advertising metrics into advertisers' own AWS accounts, so a stalled stream can leave bidding software working from stale numbers without any visible error in the console.
Posts, nearly 16 months on
The Posts row is harder to explain. Posts was Amazon's free, social-style feed for brands. In June 2025, PPC Land reported that Amazon was discontinuing it, citing Amazon's advertising API release notes: deprecation announced on June 3, 2025, existing users barred from creating new posts from June 16, and complete termination on July 31, 2025. The status page still lists Posts under Brand content, 478 days after that termination date (PPC Land calculation), and its schema still accepts Posts as a subcategory. Neither the page nor the help article says what the row now monitors.
Two names for the same row
The help page's DVA+ group lists campaign creation, campaign management, ad management and ad delivery. Those are the Amazon DSP rows on the live page, under different names, minus bulksheets.
The new labels follow Amazon's own rebranding. Amazon retired the Amazon DSP name on September 29, 2026, the same day it put a four-step DVA+ campaign builder into US closed beta. DVA+ buys display, video and audio from one objective, one budget and one campaign. The switch from "creative" to "ad" shows up in reporting too: the Support Center page on unified reporting dimensions, covered by PPC Land on November 12, maps Ad ID and Ad name back to their former DVA+ names, Creative ID and Creative name.
The status page has not followed. Fifty-two days after the rename (PPC Land calculation, September 29 to November 20), the live page and the schema both say Amazon DSP and Creative management. That is not the only place where Amazon's two vocabularies overlap. An Amazon launch note for six new DVA+ locales, dated November 10, pointed advertisers to an Amazon DVA+ console while the console view it illustrated still carried the Amazon DSP header. And a Support Center primer updated on November 19 gave Amazon DSP two successors, describing DVA+ campaigns as formerly known as Amazon DSP in its subtitle and Amazon Ads Agent as formerly known as Amazon DSP in a worked example.
For a person reading the page during an outage, the mismatch is a minor translation exercise. For software, it is a contract question. A monitoring script written against the documentation would look for DVA+ and find nothing; one written against the schema will keep receiving Amazon DSP.
Nineteen countries, 15 fewer than in 2023
The country list is shorter than any Amazon has published for the service before. Amazon's launch note for "Amazon Ads Status (beta)", dated May 10, 2023, listed 34 countries in five regional groups. A second note, dated November 13, 2024, listed 33, the same set without Switzerland.
Every country on the November 20 list also appears in the 2023 list. The 15 that dropped out are Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Luxembourg, Ireland, Denmark, Finland, Norway, Turkey, Kuwait, Bahrain, Morocco, Israel, New Zealand and China (PPC Land count). The 2023 list matches, country for country, the 34 markets in which Amazon offered VAST 4 support on third-party supply from September 2, 2026, and its size equals the 34 countries covered by a single global DSP account from July 30, 2026.
Germany is the most conspicuous gap. The help page's own rule - status applies to all countries in a region "unless mentioned otherwise" - suggests that a missing country is not necessarily an excluded one, but the page does not say whether the shorter list is deliberate. Ireland and South Africa, two of the six countries Amazon named for DVA+ on November 10, are both absent. India has also changed region, from Asia Pacific in both earlier notes to Europe, Middle East, and North Africa.
The feed behind the page
The status page is a JavaScript application, and the data that drives it is published in machine-readable form. At 07:20:33 UTC on November 21, the file status.json contained three fields: a timestamp, a language value of english, and an empty status array. According to its schema, an empty array means exactly what the green ticks mean: where no status is present for a region, category or subcategory, the status can be taken as "No known issues".
The schema, written to the JSON Schema 2020-12 standard and titled Amazon Ads Status json, sets out what an incident record looks like. Five fields are required for each entry: timestamp, statusType, region, systemImpacted and category. Three status types are allowed: Informational, Degraded and Disrupted. Six categories are defined: Sponsored ads, Amazon DSP, Measurement & Reporting, Status announcement, Brand content and Amazon Marketing Stream. The nine subcategory values are Ad delivery, Bulksheets, Campaign creation, Campaign management, Creative management, Reports, Stores, Posts and Streams. A "Status announcement" entry carries no subcategory, and its message applies to any entry that arrives without one of its own.
Three details in the schema do not line up with the rest. It has no value for the Account Management & Access group or for Amazon Marketing Cloud, so it does not describe how an incident in billing, user permissions, registration or the clean room would appear in the feed. It caps the status array at 27 entries while its own explanation gives a maximum of eight statuses in each of three regions, which is 24 (PPC Land calculation). And it describes the language field as an ISO 639-1 code, giving en as the example for English, while the live file says english. The schema also states that English is the only supported language for the feed, although the page itself has offered a language switcher since November 2024.
The RSS feed is thinner still. Titled "Amazon Ads Status" and generated with a Node.js library, it contained no items on November 21, and its last build time was 17:29:10 GMT on Wednesday, November 4, 2026.
History is published separately. According to Amazon's November 13, 2024 note, the History tab shows status updates from the past six months, the same record is available as history.json with its own schema, and a language choice made on the page is saved for later visits. Until then, according to the note, all status messages had been available only in English. The 2023 launch note pitched the service at advertisers and API integrators who needed status information without logging in to the advertising console or the DSP.
