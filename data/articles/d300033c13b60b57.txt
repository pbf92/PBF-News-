TITLE: Cochrane proposes nearly $150,000 more for library after warning of reduced hours, services
OUTLET: CochraneNow

Cochrane town administration is recommending an additional $149,456 for the Cochrane Public Library in 2027 after the library board warned the three per cent increase in the draft budget could force it to close at least two days a week and reduce services.
The 11.6 per cent increase presented to council Monday would cover the $52,000 cost of implementing Bill 28 and allow the library to fill one of the two positions it requested. It also includes the three per cent base increase.
Board chair Chuck Love and trustee Tom Hovland asked council on Nov. 16 for up to an additional $233,000, an 18 per cent increase. The board said the original three per cent increase would fall short of what it needed to stay open seven days a week and limit its ability to tests the recommendations of a recent needs assessment.
New role would start with communications
Michelle Ambrose told council the fundraising and marketing position would initially focus on marketing and communications, duties now shared across the library team. The role would also help the library develop a fundraising plan with the goal of building a new facility.
In the original budget submission, the library sought funding for the facility and its amenities themself. The library requires new computers, new computers, a paint job and the replacement of a 20-year-old carpet. Library officials say patrons commonly praise the wide range of services offered but have commented its starting to look run-down.

Ambrose said the town wants to work with the library on an agreement that separates facility maintenance and capital costs from day-to-day operating expenses.
Bill 28
During Monday’s budget talks, some councillor questioned why the library should have to absorb the estimated $52,000 cost of implementing Bill 28 in its operating budget and believed the increase was inadequate for what they view as an essential service.
Bill 28 gives Alberta’s municipal affairs minister more oversight of public libraries, including the power to order inspections. Related regulations restrict minors’ access to library materials containing certain visual depictions of sexual acts.
Starting Jan. 1, staff will have to manage access to restricted materials, review new purchases and regularly check shelves for restricted items that may have been returned to general circulation.
Lasr week, the delegation told council that said Cochrane is the largest user of the Marigold Library System because its own collection is limited. Under the new requirements, staff will have to screen incoming holds from other libraries and flag restricted items Cochrane sends to other libraries in the system.
Wilson wants current hours protected
Coun. Patrick Wilson said he supported the recommended increase and asked whether the town could make keeping the library’s current hours a condition of the additional funding.
“As long as service hours aren’t touched, I like exactly where you just fell and I appreciate you looking into it,” Wilson said.
Ambrose said the town could explore that through the agreement, subject to the requirements of Alberta’s Libraries Act.
Town CAO Drew Hyndman said it was their understanding it would ensure the exitsing operations would continue.
The proposed increase remains part of the 2027 budget process. Final budget calculations are expected to return to council Dec. 7.
