Deere earnings: What to look for
Agricultural and construction machinery company Deere DE will be reporting results this Wednesday morning. Here’s what to look for.
Deere beat analysts’ revenue expectations last quarter, reporting revenues of $12.61 billion, up 4.9% year on year. It was a solid quarter for the company, with a beat of analysts’ EPS estimates.
Is Deere a buy or sell going into earnings? Read our full analysis here, it’s free for active Edge members.
This quarter, the market is expecting Deere’s revenue to decline 2.7% year on year, a reversal from the 11.2% increase it recorded in the same quarter last year.
Analysts covering the company have generally reconfirmed their estimates over the last 30 days, suggesting they anticipate the business will stay the course heading into earnings. Deere rarely misses Wall Street’s revenue estimates.
Looking at Deere’s peers in the agricultural machinery segment, some have already reported their Q3 results, giving us a hint as to what we can expect. Lindsay delivered year-on-year revenue growth of 5.2%, beating analysts’ expectations by 7.3%, and AGCO reported a revenue decline of 8.6%, falling short of estimates by 2.7%. Lindsay’s stock price was unchanged after the resultswhile AGCO was up 8.6%.
Read our full analysis of Lindsay’s results here and AGCO’s results here.
In the last year or so, investors have shifted their focus from one macro dynamic to the next (AI disintermediation and AI investment to geopolitical conflict, interest rates, and the health of the wider economy). While some of the agricultural machinery stocks have shown solid performance in this choppy environment, the group has generally underperformed, with share prices down 2.8% on average over the last month. Deere’s stock price was unchanged during the same time and is heading into earnings with an average analyst price target of $694.01 (compared to the current share price of $636.00).
