TITLE: Advertised product subcategory
OUTLET: PPC Land

Advertised product subcategory is a dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that labels each row of a report with the subcategory of the product being advertised. Amazon files every item in its catalogue under several layers of classification, from broad groups such as kitchen or books down to narrow classes such as chef's knives or crime thrillers. The subcategory field exposes one of the lower layers. It exists so that an advertiser promoting hundreds or thousands of ASINs (Amazon Standard Identification Numbers, the retailer's product codes) can add up spend, clicks and sales by type of product, rather than reading results one product at a time. Amazon assigns it from catalogue data; the advertiser does not set it.
Where the field sits
Amazon replaced its separate sponsored ads and programmatic report pages with unified reporting, which opened in beta on November 11, 2025 and reached general availability on June 8, 2026, as PPC Land reported when the product left beta. Its dimensions reference, updated on November 11, 2026, lists 156 attributes in 11 categories.
One of those categories is Product, with 18 attributes. Nine describe the advertised product: ID, name, parent ID, brand, category, subcategory, product group, marketplace and SKU (stock keeping unit, a seller's own product code). A parallel set describes the converted product, the item a shopper eventually bought. According to PPC Land's count of the reference, the advertised product fields apply to DVA+ (Amazon's programmatic display, video and audio buying product, formerly called Amazon DSP), Sponsored Display and Sponsored Products. They carry no marker for Sponsored Brands or Sponsored TV. Converted product fields apply to all five ad products.
Where a dimension does not apply to an ad product, Amazon's reference says the value "appears as null". A report mixing Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Products campaigns, broken down by advertised product subcategory, will therefore show blanks for the Sponsored Brands rows.
How the value is assigned
The subcategory is a property of the product, not of the campaign. When a campaign promotes an ASIN, unified reporting looks up that ASIN's classification and writes the subcategory onto every row for it.
The field belongs to a three-level ladder that unified reporting exposes in full:
- Advertised product group - the broadest division, roughly a department.
- Advertised product category - a class within the group.
- Advertised product subcategory - a narrower class within the category.
Amazon's public documentation does not publish the list of values, the codes behind them, or the rule that assigns them. What is documented is that Amazon runs more than one classification system. Its Selling Partner API (SP-API), the interface sellers use to manage listings, describes a catalogue item with a website display group, defined as the "website display group associated with an Amazon catalog item", and a browse classification, defined by the "browse node identifier" and display name, with a parent node above it. Browse nodes form the tree shoppers navigate on the retail site. Display groups are coarser and are used for sales rank.
Which of these systems feeds the subcategory field is not stated. The three-level group, category and subcategory naming matches the language of Amazon's older programmatic and attribution reports rather than the browse node tree, which has no fixed depth. That is an inference from naming, not something Amazon has confirmed. A reporting tool, Two Minute Reports, describes the sibling advertised product category field as the "category (browse node) name of the advertised product", which points the other way. The two readings conflict, and Amazon has not settled the question in public.
A worked example
A kitchenware brand runs Sponsored Products campaigns across 40 ASINs: 25 knives, 10 cutting boards and five knife sharpeners. Without product taxonomy fields, a weekly report lists 40 rows of spend and sales.
Adding the advertised product subcategory dimension collapses the 40 rows into three or four, one per subcategory, each with summed impressions, clicks, spend and attributed sales. If sharpeners spent 12% of the budget but delivered 4% of sales, the gap is visible in one line. Adding advertised product category above it shows whether all three subcategories sit in the same category or are split. Adding the converted product subcategory in a separate report shows what shoppers actually bought after clicking, which may be a different subcategory altogether.
The split between the two halves matters for metrics. Impressions and clicks belong to the advertised product, the one shown in the ad. Amazon's data availability page, updated on November 17, 2026, states that "impressions, clicks, and other traffic metrics are not specific to any 1 conversion", so traffic metrics are unavailable when a report is broken down by converted product, as PPC Land reported on November 18. The advertised subcategory is the only one of the two that can carry cost-per-click and click-through rate.
Origin and evolution
Category, subcategory and product group were not invented for unified reporting. The legacy Amazon DSP Product report offered "Product category", "Product group" and "Product subcategory" alongside brand name and parent ASIN, according to the data connector documentation of Funnel, a marketing data company. Amazon Attribution, the free tool that measures how off-Amazon ads drive sales on Amazon, has a Products report with "Product Group", "Product Category" and "Product Subcategory" fields, according to the same vendor. In Amazon's Attribution API those fields appear as productCategory, productSubcategory and productGroup, restricted to the PRODUCTS report type, according to the API reference republished by data integration company Data Virtuality, whose copy was last modified in September 2025.
Sponsored ads reporting took a different path. Its advertised product report keyed on "Advertised ASIN" and "Advertised SKU", with no category breakdown, according to Funnel's documentation of the Sponsored Products and Sponsored Display reports. Analysts who wanted category totals had to join an ASIN list to catalogue data themselves.
Unified reporting brought the DSP-style taxonomy across to sponsored ads. In the November 2026 reference, the advertised product ID carries the former name "Advertised ASIN" for Sponsored Products, and converted product fields "mostly inherit DVA+ names such as 'Item name', 'Parent ASIN' and 'Brand name'", in PPC Land's reading. Amazon's data availability page sets February 13, 2025 as the earliest date for which advertised and converted product data can be pulled in unified reporting.
Why it matters
Large sellers organise their business by product type, not by campaign. A field that writes Amazon's own classification onto ad data removes a manual join and fixes the taxonomy to one source.
The field also matters for comparison. Amazon's benchmark reports judge a campaign against peer brands "within a category", and the benchmark browse category path field names the category used. Setting the advertised product subcategory beside it shows whether a campaign's products sit inside the benchmark category or straddle several.
Timing adds pressure. Amazon's migration page, as summarised by PPC Land, schedules legacy report pages to go read-only on December 17, 2026 and to shut on January 31, 2027. Dashboards built on the old DSP product report fields have to be remapped to the advertised and converted product fields before then.
Limitations and disputes
The taxonomy is Amazon's. Values are not published as a list, codes are not documented, and Amazon can reclassify a product. A subcategory total for one month may not match the next if items moved.
Coverage is uneven. The dimensions reference gives the advertised product fields no Sponsored Brands marker. Yet the unified reporting template gallery at launch offered an "Advertised Product" template for Sponsored Products, Sponsored Display and Sponsored Brands, according to PPC Land's June 2026 coverage. Whether Sponsored Brands rows return a subcategory or a null depends on which document holds. Sponsored TV is not yet supported in unified reporting at all.
Off-Amazon products. DVA+ campaigns can advertise products sold outside Amazon's store, where no Amazon catalogue entry exists. The documentation does not say what the subcategory field returns in that case; null is the likely outcome under Amazon's general rule, but it is unconfirmed.
History is short. Product dimension data starts on February 13, 2025, and daily pulls are capped at 120 days at a time. Year-on-year subcategory comparisons before 2026 have to come from legacy exports.
Advertised product subcategory is not...
Converted product subcategory. The converted field describes the item purchased, which can differ from the item advertised when a shopper buys a related product. It carries sales but not traffic metrics.
Advertised product category. The category is the level above. A category contains several subcategories.
Benchmark browse category path. That field names the single category Amazon used to find peer brands for benchmarks. It describes the campaign's comparison set, not each product.
Category targeting. In Sponsored Products and Sponsored Display, advertisers can target shoppers browsing chosen categories. That is a setting the advertiser picks; the subcategory field is a label Amazon assigns for reporting.
Recent developments
The field has been documented only in the past few weeks. Amazon updated the unified reporting dimensions reference on November 11, 2026, giving the 18 Product attributes and their ad product markers. On November 17, its data availability page fixed February 13, 2025 as the start of product dimension history, set the 120-day limit on daily pulls, and confirmed that traffic metrics cannot be combined with converted product fields. Earlier in the year, on April 14, 2026, Amazon added category breakouts to its Omnichannel Metrics measurement product for US DSP campaigns, at study and campaign level, a sign that category-level analysis is spreading across its measurement tools.
