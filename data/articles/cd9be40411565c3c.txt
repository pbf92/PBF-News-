TITLE: Multicriteria Analysis Rates Ambrisentan Favorable Among Key Pulmonary Hypertension Drugs
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

In an indirect comparative evaluation integrating clinical trial evidence, expert consensus, and regional economic data, the endothelin receptor antagonist ambrisentan achieved the highest overall score when weighed against bosentan and macitentan for treating pulmonary arterial hypertension. The investigation, published in the International Journal of Clinical Pharmacy, combined meta-analytic evidence from twelve randomized controlled trials with multidimensional scoring to assist clinical pharmacy decision-making and formulary management within the Chinese healthcare context. Endothelin receptor antagonists serve as central pharmacotherapies for pulmonary arterial hypertension, a progressive condition characterized by sustained elevations in pulmonary artery pressure that can lead to right heart failure. Although these three pharmaceutical agents share therapeutic goals targeting endothelin-1 pathways to improve hemodynamics and exercise capacity, differences across efficacy, safety profiles, monitoring demands, drug interactions, and treatment costs present complex trade-offs for clinicians and hospital committees.
To navigate these multifaceted clinical and practical distinctions, the researchers developed a structured multidimensional framework through a two-round Delphi consensus process involving nineteen multidisciplinary experts recruited from tertiary hospitals and academic institutions across China. The panel represented specialists across cardiology, pulmonology, clinical pharmacy, pharmacoeconomics, and health policy, with each expert having at least ten years of professional experience in pulmonary arterial hypertension or related disciplines. The formal consensus procedure established a hierarchical structure comprising six core evaluation domains: clinical efficacy, drug safety, economic value, treatment suitability, real-world accessibility, and therapeutic innovation. These broad categories were further delineated into thirteen distinct subdomains and thirty-two specific indicators. Statistical quality control metrics confirmed robust consistency across the consensus rounds, yielding an expert authority coefficient of 0.82, a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.864, and a Kendall’s coefficient of concordance of 0.412 with statistical significance below the 0.01 threshold.
Indicator weighting within the hierarchical structure was subsequently established using the Analytic Hierarchy Process, prioritizing safety and clinical efficacy to reflect their primary importance in guideline-directed therapeutic selection. To populate the quantitative dimensions of the decision framework, investigators conducted a systematic literature review and meta-analysis across PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from database inception through October 2024. The quantitative synthesis included twelve randomized controlled trials assessing monotherapy against placebo in patients diagnosed with pulmonary arterial hypertension. Trial quality was appraised using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2.0 tool, under which all twelve trials were assessed as having a low risk of bias. Clinical outcomes were quantitatively synthesized using random-effects models via the DerSimonian–Laird method, summarizing exercise capacity and hemodynamic measures as pooled mean differences with ninety-five percent confidence intervals, while safety parameters were summarized as pooled odds ratios.
The quantitative meta-analysis demonstrated that all three endothelin receptor antagonists yielded improvements in exercise capacity and pulmonary hemodynamics compared to placebo, though the relative magnitude varied among the individual agents. For the primary functional endpoint of six-minute walk distance, ambrisentan demonstrated a pooled mean improvement of 43.89 meters with a ninety-five percent confidence interval ranging from 29.97 to 57.80 meters, while bosentan demonstrated a pooled mean increase of 40.95 meters with a confidence interval between 18.02 and 63.88 meters. In contrast, macitentan demonstrated a smaller, non-statistically significant improvement in the six-minute walk distance analysis, showing a mean difference of 9.73 meters with a ninety-five percent confidence interval spanning negative 8.42 to positive 27.88 meters. When examining mean pulmonary arterial pressure, bosentan and macitentan showed comparatively larger reductions than ambrisentan, indicating that individual agent selection may depend partly on specific clinical priorities such as maximizing functional exercise tolerance versus hemodynamic reduction.
Safety and tolerability evaluations derived from drug labeling and trial synthesis highlighted substantial differences in clinical risk profiles and monitoring requirements. In the pooled meta-analysis of treatment-related adverse drug reactions, ambrisentan showed an odds ratio of 0.57 with a ninety-five percent confidence interval of 0.31 to 1.06, reflecting a numerical trend toward reduced adverse event odds, whereas bosentan and macitentan demonstrated higher odds point estimates that did not attain statistical significance. Regulatory data indicated that ambrisentan exhibited fewer clinically significant drug–drug interactions, though it carries standard precautions regarding pregnancy and requires dosage adjustments when administered concurrently with cyclosporine. Bosentan involves a recognized potential for drug interactions due to the induction of cytochrome P450 3A4 and 2C9 enzymes and requires mandatory regular liver function monitoring. Macitentan requires caution with cytochrome P450 modulators, while its safety profile in pediatric populations remains an area under active investigation.
Economic parameters were evaluated from the perspective of the Chinese healthcare system using standardized 2024 Chinese Yuan values drawn from national pricing databases, hospital procurement records, and official per capita disposable income statistics. Substantial differences emerged across baseline treatment expenditures. The annual drug acquisition cost was calculated at 24,600 Yuan for ambrisentan, 17,820 Yuan for bosentan, and 57,240 Yuan for macitentan. Cost-effectiveness ratios, assessed as the drug expenditure incurred per meter of improvement gained on the six-minute walk distance, revealed closely comparable economic efficiency for ambrisentan at 140.12 Yuan per meter and bosentan at 142.38 Yuan per meter. Conversely, macitentan was associated with a substantially higher expenditure ratio of 1470.71 Yuan per meter improvement, reflecting its higher retail acquisition price paired with the smaller functional walk gain observed in the pooled trial analysis.
Patient-centered suitability and real-world accessibility metrics further shaped the comparative scoring. Both ambrisentan and macitentan offer the convenience of once-daily oral administration, which supports improved medication adherence and long-term treatment persistence relative to bosentan, which necessitates a twice-daily dosing schedule alongside routine laboratory liver monitoring. In terms of accessibility, all three endothelin receptor antagonists are listed in China’s National Reimbursement Drug List, confirming baseline coverage. However, substantial variations remained regarding personal financial burden when measured against economic benchmarks. The ratio of annual drug acquisition cost relative to per capita disposable income was calculated at 63.13 percent for bosentan and 87.15 percent for ambrisentan, whereas macitentan imposed a substantially heavier financial burden with an annual cost-to-income ratio of 202.78 percent, presenting a practical obstacle to sustained long-term access.
Evaluation of pharmacological innovation highlighted distinct developmental trajectories among the three molecules. Bosentan represents the first-in-class endothelin receptor antagonist that established targeted therapy in this disease pathway, while ambrisentan and macitentan represent subsequent drug generations characterized by refined target selectivity and pharmacokinetic properties. Macitentan demonstrated technological advantages in receptor binding and pharmacokinetic optimization, while ambrisentan was distinguished by favorable safety and interaction characteristics. However, the comprehensive multicriteria decision analysis framework revealed that technological innovation did not automatically translate into the highest overall therapeutic value once real-world economic costs, affordability burdens, and practical clinical suitability factors were weighted into the integrated scoring model.
Following the final integration of weighted scores across all six domains, ambrisentan achieved the highest cumulative ranking, followed in descending order by bosentan and macitentan. The authors noted that this composite ranking reflected ambrisentan’s balanced performance across clinical exercise capacity, favorable tolerability trends, manageable once-daily administration, and relatively moderate treatment expenditures. The authors emphasized that the structured multicriteria decision analysis framework provides hospital Pharmacy and Therapeutics committees and clinical practitioners with a systematic, transparent tool to evaluate complex therapeutic trade-offs beyond isolated clinical trial metrics, aiding value-oriented prescribing and formulary prioritization.
The study authors documented several methodological limitations that govern the interpretation of their findings. Primarily, the comparisons among ambrisentan, bosentan, and macitentan were indirect and derived from placebo-controlled trials rather than direct head-to-head randomized trials or formal network meta-analyses; therefore, the observed variations cannot be construed as definitive proof of comparative clinical superiority. Furthermore, the economic evaluations relied on six-minute walk distance gains as a surrogate outcome rather than quality-adjusted life years, leaving long-term survival and comprehensive health utility unmeasured. Finally, economic and affordability metrics were derived specifically within the framework of Chinese procurement and reimbursement pricing, meaning cost-effectiveness conclusions cannot be generalized directly to other healthcare settings, and the expert-derived weights in the decision model inherently retain an element of clinical judgment.
