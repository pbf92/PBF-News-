TITLE: Why is There No Disney World Parody in GTA VI? Rockstar Explains
OUTLET: Yahoo Tech

Grand Theft Auto VI gives players a sprawling version of Florida to explore, but one of the state's best-known attractions has no playable counterpart. Rockstar Games left a Walt Disney World-style theme park out of Leonida because building one would have taken substantial work, and the developers had little reason to send Jason and Lucia there.
Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson told VGC that the decision came down to what the team needed for the story and what would make Leonida feel like a real place. A functioning theme park was a big commitment for a location the two leads could easily skip.
A Theme Park Would Have to Do More Than Look the Part
Rockstar has spoofed Disney World before. As VGC notes, the original Grand Theft Auto: Vice City featured advertisements for Fun World, a fictional park complete with a fairytale castle. A billboard can get that joke across in seconds. Put the castle on the map, though, and players will want to see what happens when they walk through the gates.
VGC asked Nelson whether players would expect a selection of working rides. "Yeah, that's the challenge: where do you stop? When do you feel like it's enough?" he replied. Nelson said the team faced a similar problem with building interiors. Once players can go inside, the rooms need enough detail and activity to feel natural.
That leaves Rockstar with a choice about how far to take the joke. A fake park could be funny from the highway. One players can spend an afternoon in would need attractions, crowds and enough to do between rides to make the trip worthwhile.
A playable Disney World parody could also have pushed the series into more sensitive territory. If players could bring the same violence to a park modeled on a family vacation destination that they can to the streets of Vice City, that alone might have sparked a backlash. However, Nelson's explanation focused on the workload and what the story called for.
Even a Decade of Development Has Limits
The Guardian reported that work on GTA VI dates back to research trips to Miami in 2015. Independent estimates cited by the paper put the cost of making the game at more than $1 billion, a figure Rockstar has declined to confirm.
Some of that effort shows up in small interactions. During The Guardian's demo, Jason asked a pedestrian if he could pet her dog. He could also identify a gull with binoculars and add it to a wildlife log. The open world is massive, and even a short walk through Vice City can give players a reason to stop. A theme park built to that same standard could demand a great deal of work for a single destination.
The Florida Joke Some Players Wanted
GTA VI had a massive launch, and early player reactions have been mixed. Some players have praised Leonida's scale and detail. Others have criticized the pacing and questioned whether the push for realism leaves less room for the series' freewheeling play. The missing theme park brings that debate down to a simple question. What do players want to find when they stray from the main story?
