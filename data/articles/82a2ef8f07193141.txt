TITLE: Inside GTA 6 – How Rockstar Made the Game of the Generation
OUTLET: GamingBolt

It’s never easy to wait with patience for something you’ve been anticipating for a very long time. The last leg of a long journey is sometimes the one that brings the most challenges, and perhaps an added emotional weight that comes with the melancholy of something ending, and the joy of something new that begins because of it.
The folks over at Rockstar are probably going to agree with us the most right now (if any of you are reading this.) Their responses to our questions, alongside additional comments supplied by Rockstar, prompted us to explore the work behind GTA 6.
What stands out across these comments is that even the game’s biggest names share the same hopes and anxieties as the players on the other side of the screen. We’re looking back at a journey that has lasted many years and crossed many obstacles and milestones to get here, so get comfortable.
Note: This feature includes quotes from our interview with members of the Rockstar team, alongside selected quotes provided directly by Rockstar. These comments are presented with additional context and editorial analysis. Some quotes have been edited for clarity without changing their original meaning.
Worth the Wait
Many of us wondered if GTA 6 was going to be good enough to justify a thirteen-year-long wait. For the folks over at Rockstar, the question was whether years of hard work was going to be enough to meet expectations that had certainly snowballed in the years after a couple of delays to GTA 6’s highly anticipated launch date.
That’s now come and gone, and Leonida has cast a spell on us, but Rob Nelson, the game’s Head of Development, spoke to GamingBolt ahead of that day. And his response to questions about how the team felt about the game in the run-up to its release showcases that there was a bunch of talented folks waiting with equally bated breath for that launch, a curtain that they’d been meticulously working behind finally about to lift and reveal a stage that’s now become a benchmark.
“It is a surreal feeling after working on it for so long! We are excited and anxious!! We have made something that we believe in and are incredibly proud of, but you never know how people are going to respond, so… we are nervous! We cannot wait until next week, when hopefully people will feel we have delivered for them, it was worth the wait and that they will love this game as much as we do.”
There was hope in that statement, and one that reflected a bunch of people we hope have had as much fun making GTA 6 as we’ve enjoyed playing it since it’s come out. Rockstar’s co-founder and president, Sam Houser outlined the studio’s vision, and it’s one that sheds light on how it has managed to consistently churn out titles that push the boundaries of what a video game can achieve just a little further every time.
“Rockstar Games was founded on the belief that games could achieve the same distinction as all other forms of storytelling, become essential to modern culture, and to be thought of as stories and worlds that live on with all of us. This week, Grand Theft Auto returns to Leonida and modern-day Vice City. While things have changed and time has passed, one aspect holds true: We have tried to create an experience that will exceed your expectations while showing what this medium is capable of, and why we believe games are so special,” Houser stated.
Nelson and Houser’s statements showcase a vision, and a team that has clearly kept it in mind as it went about creating Leonida. But how did it manage to get there?
The Art of the Craft
As a version of Florida tailored to the game’s premise and systems, it only made sense for the team to begin crafting Leonida by visiting the place it’s meant to imitate. But how did it approach the time it spent there? What would one look out for on its streets when they seek to craft an open world that’s going to raise the bar for all the others that follow it?
John Macpherson, the game’s Senior Design Director of the World and Population, looked for details like the music, people, and the way they moved. But those weren’t random parameters aimed at creating a crowd that would simply populate Loenida’s streets. They were aimed at making them feel alive, and claimed by the people who belonged to them.
“We had a lot of fun trying to capture the raw energy and vibrant chaos that Florida is known for. By immersing ourselves in a number of these locations on reference trips, spending time with locals, and experiencing as much as possible firsthand, we gathered a direct sense of what would make Leonida feel truly alive. We tried to match that in-game as much as possible, hopefully resulting in something that feels full of life,” Macpherson stated.
That’s insightful, and considering how the team approached what happened once somebody moved off the streets and into a new space is going to help us see how seemingly disparate approaches begin to come together.
Miriam Bellard, the Vice President of Art: Visual Development, outlined how there was a thought process that began long before any environment was decorated by the team. With the kind of detailed interiors we’ve been seeing in Leonida, a facet that’s so darn consistent across the city’s vast map, it’s nice to see how those environments came to contain the details that they do.
“For every visual design, we are getting into the heads of the people who would have inhabited that place and role-playing how the final look would have come about. Would they have hired an architect or DIY’d it? Are they trying to express their status and prestige, or is it all about comfort and practicalities? Are they messy or tidy? What is their taste? I love that we get to do that for such a wide variety of people and places. Everything from industrial to scruffy to decadent to dangerous. We get to design all of that – and it’s lots of fun!,” Bellard stated.
We’d hoped the team enjoyed their work as much as we currently do, and it seems like that’s the case. We’d call that a win-win, something made even sweeter by Macpherson’s insights on how Florida’s scale became something special since Rockstar came with the means to make something of it this time around.
“The most exciting thing about building this world is how immense and varied it is,” Macpherson stated. “There is so much flavour and personality to discover across the map, it seems endless, and it gave us a lot of room to work with. That scale, alongside all the new assets, tools, and tech, provided us with the freedom to create something unparalleled,” Macpherson added.
But we can’t help but wonder how all those little ideas, and the details that were needed to drive them forward, could be possible in a world that was already going to be one of the biggest ones Rockstar had made, and we now see that it’s also one that’s just dense.
How could Rockstar weave all those systems into the fabric of Leonida and keep them working together in a world where anything could go wrong? How does the energy of inspired ideas be transformed into foundational pillars that let those ideas fuel a sort of controlled chaos for Jason and Lucia to navigate?
It’s time to look at how Rockstar managed to weave in those production values we’ve been marvelling at over the days we’ve been spending in Leonida.
A World That Tells Its Stories With Pride
During our playthrough of GTA 6, we saw how the stories of its inhabitants helped shape its world. Now, it’s time to explore how those stories became gameplay systems that many of us may never fully uncover. For that ambition to pay off, the details have to be there for curious players willing to follow the breadcrumbs. It would have been easy to assume that only a handful would ever see these threads through and decide the extra work wasn’t worth it.
However, Rockstar doesn’t work like that now, does it? We’ve seen it before, and we had Adam Cochrane, a Vice President of Art: Environment, reveal how getting even the smallest details right made all the difference.
“The life is in the detail of Grand Theft Auto VI. The subtle sway of trees or bunting swaying in the wind. A puddle of bin juice around the bottom of a dumpster, or an overgrown garden in a property that is unoccupied. The personalization of each home and business so that it all feels unique and there’s no repetition,” Cochrane stated.
“Then you zoom out, and you look at the sheer scale of the world, and you realize that the detail you’ve been discovering on such a small scale is quite literally everywhere across the vast landscape of this world. Then you realize that it’s all been curated, considered, and created by a human hand, and you can’t quite believe it,” Cochrane further added.
The details might be in place, and the stories they’re bringing are now ready to unfold. But the challenge of bringing them to life is only beginning.
Chaos, Its Catalysts, And Consequences
Any bunch of inspired ideas is sure to hit snags when it comes to bringing them all together. Forging a path forward through such clashes, through coordinated integration of what works into a coherent experience, is a very important part of any successful project, and we’re seeing how GTA 6 has done very well on that front.
Stephen Donkin, the Vice President of Design, had this to say about how the challenge was not just challenging, but also rewarding.
“The most exciting part of building out the world of Grand Theft Auto to me was exploring the connections between all the various strands of development we had going on at any one time, and looking for ways to make a minor tweak in one part that could have an influence in another, and so on, until eventually finding a spot where it felt like everything from the environment to the character art, vehicles, NPC dialogue, Missions, events, score, player systems, in-game media, overarching narrative, and more were all coming together and driving towards a coherent play experience,” Donkin stated.
He went on to elaborate on how getting systems to work without one of them tripping up another was a recurring problem, but it’s clear to see that there is a sense of accomplishment underlining how the team has managed to bring things together into what we’re now experiencing as a very engaging world.
“The interconnected nature of all the systems running in the game meant that it was never a case of just making a single change to fix or improve something we had picked up on; there was always some unintended consequence that occurred elsewhere in the game because everything speaks to everything else..If a cop is doing a stop-and-search on another criminal in the world, how do they behave if you commit a crime in front of them? Do they switch their attention to you? Do they compare the severity of the crime? If they come after you, what does the original criminal do? Does he drive off? What happens if you follow him? Every event, encounter, interaction had to be considered with this level of depth, as there are so many possible inputs to any given situation, and every new feature or mechanic that is added becomes a new input we must collectively consider,” Donkin stated.
We’re glad Donkin touched upon what we’re doing in front of the cops, since it helps us move on from the stage and props on display in Leonida, as well as how they all come together to make it a very lively platform for the story Rockstar had crafted for us.
It’s now time to move the spotlight onto the actors on the game’s stage, and to look at how they were brought to life over GTA 6’s development.
