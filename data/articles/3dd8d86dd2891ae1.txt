Dollar Moves Higher With T-Note Yields
The dollar index DXY rose by +0.37% on Friday. The dollar rose Friday amid higher T-note yields, which strengthened the dollar’s interest-rate differentials. Also, expectations for the Fed to raise interest rates by +25 bp at next month’s FOMC meeting are supporting the dollar. Friday’s trade news showing the US Oct trade deficit widened more than expected to a 19-month high was bearish for the dollar.
The US Oct trade deficit was -$149.5 billion, wider than expectations of -$136.0 billion and the largest trade deficit in 19 months.

The markets are pricing in a 98% chance of a +25 bp rate hike at the FOMC's next policy meeting on December 8-9.
EUR/USD E rose by +0.18% on Friday. The yen was under pressure Friday from a stronger dollar and higher T-note yields. Also, Friday’s +2% rally in the Nikkei Stock Index curbed safe-haven demand for the yen. Yen losses were limited Friday after Japan’s Tokyo Nov CPI rose at the fastest pace in 16 months, a hawkish factor for BOJ policy.
Japan's Tokyo Nov CPI rose +2.8% y/y, the highest in sixteen months but below expectations of +3.1% y/y. Tokyo Nov CPI ex-fresh food and energy unexpectedly weakened to +2.5% y/y from +2.6% y/y in Oct, weaker than expectations of +2.7% y/y.
The market is pricing in a 78% chance of a +25 bp BOJ rate hike at its next meeting on December 18.
December COMEX gold (GCZ26) closed up +103.80 (+2.94%) on Friday, and December COMEX silver (SIZ26) closed up +3.109 (+7.76%).
Precious metals prices rallied sharply on Friday, with silver soaring to a 1-week high. Metal prices surged Friday as a sharp stock rebound boosted risk-on sentiment in asset markets. Silver prices also found support Friday from a PBOC statement saying it will work to stabilize its capital markets and expand domestic demand, which could boost China’s industrial metals demand. Negative factors for precious metals on Friday included a stronger dollar and higher global bond yields.
Recent fund support for precious metals is bullish for prices, as long holdings in gold ETFs climbed to a 4.25-year high on October 21, although those holdings have since backed off. Long holdings in silver ETFs rose to an 8.5-month high on Tuesday.
Strong central bank demand for gold is supporting gold prices. Recent World Gold Council data showed cumulative gold purchases by global central banks in Q3 were 321,000 MT, the most since Q4 of 2024. Also, recent news showed that bullion held in China's PBOC reserves rose by 830,000 ounces to 78.30 million troy ounces in October, the largest increase in three years and the twenty-fourth consecutive month the PBOC boosted its gold reserves.
