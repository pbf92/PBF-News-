TITLE: Amazon's Fire tablet ad impressions can arrive after a campaign ends
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon Ads on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, updated a Support Center page that sets out how advertisers build campaigns for the screen a Fire tablet shows when it is switched on. Titled "Create a Fire tablet campaign," the guide names three ad types, confines the campaigns to four marketplaces, sets a 15-second ceiling for video and closes with a note on measurement specific to the device: because Fire tablets are only intermittently connected, impressions can keep turning up in campaign data after a campaign has ended.
The page sits under Building campaigns and Setup guides: Display in the Support Center, beneath a header that reads Amazon Ads Agent, the name Amazon now gives its advertising console. Its own summary line reads: "Learn how to create a Display, Video, and Audio campaign for Fire tablet." A notice under the update date sets its scope: "This page only lists settings specific to creating an ad campaign for Fire tablets." Everything else, from budgets to targeting, is left to Amazon's general campaign guides.
In Short
Amazon has published instructions for building ads that fill a Fire tablet's screen when the device is turned on, using muted video or image ads, in the US, the UK, Germany and Japan only. It matters to brands buying Amazon's own device inventory, because the setup now runs through the ad group structure of Amazon's renamed Display, Video, and Audio product, with fixed rules such as a 15-second video limit and an ad review of up to 12 hours. The biggest practical change is in reporting: impressions and clicks can take up to 24 hours to appear, and some impressions arrive only after a campaign is over, when a tablet that showed the ad finally comes back online.
Three formats on a screen that wakes up
The guide opens with a definition: "A Fire tablet campaign is a full-screen advertising experience that displays ads when customers turn on their Fire tablet devices." It then lists three ad types, each in a single line:
- "OLV: Video ads (muted) that autoplay on wake"
- "Display: Image ads that appear on wake"
- "Responsive eCommerce (REC): Image ads that appear on wake"
A note follows: "Fire tablet campaigns are available for US, UK, DE, and JP marketplaces only."
The list of three is not the full picture. Further down, in the section on building an ad, the page says: "The ad types that you can create depend on which ad group type you set up. When you create an Online Video (OLV) ad group, you can only create OLV ads. When you create a Display ad group, you can create Amazon Video Ads, Image, and REC ads." So there are four creatable formats, not three, and video can be bought through either kind of ad group. A Display ad group carries video in the form of Amazon Video Ads, which the opening list does not mention. The page does not explain when an advertiser would pick one video route over the other.
Amazon's separate specification page for Amazon Video Ads on Fire tablet gives one clue. Besides the video, it asks for background and button images at 1200x1920 pixels in portrait and 1920x1200 in landscape, according to Amazon. That format sits on the Component-based tab of the New ad page, while the OLV route runs through the Video tab and a standard video file.
The four-market limit applies to campaigns built in the console. Amazon's ad specification page for Fire tablet with Special Offers, the ad-supported version of the tablets, names 19 locales: Canada, Mexico, the US, Brazil, Germany, Spain, France, Italy, the Netherlands, Poland, Sweden, Turkey, the UK, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Australia, India, Japan and Singapore. That page lists formats with production turnaround times of 4 to 18 business days, plus a custom option handled by sales, and states that the ads run on devices from the seventh generation onward, according to Amazon. It does not say how buyers outside the four console markets reach the inventory.
From line items to ad groups
Fire tablet wake-screen ads are not new to Amazon's self-service tools. The guide records the route they now take, which differs from the one Amazon described when it first opened them up.
On June 19, 2023, Amazon opened what it then called the Fire Tablet lockscreen to self-service advertisers as an open beta, with full-screen display and video ads in the US, Germany, the UK and Japan, according to its launch note. Buyers set it up through a Type dropdown on line items in the Amazon DSP, choosing between two templates: "Fire Tablet - Amazon Video Ads" and "Image - Fire Tablet." Those two names survive almost unchanged in the November 17 guide, where they now sit behind a "Show other creatives" link on the Component-based tab.
In February 2024, Amazon expanded its Responsive eCommerce Creative format to nearly all traditional ad sizes, with variations such as Shop Now, Add to Basket, Voucher and Customer Review. On May 27, 2024, the Fire tablet lockscreen began accepting that format without a custom Fire tablet template, for vendors, sellers, managed service and self-service buyers, through the Amazon DSP and the Amazon Ads API, according to Amazon.
Video came later. When Amazon added its owned properties to the self-serve DSP for sellers in January 2025, Fire TV, Fire Tablet and Grocery online supported only display advertising. On November 10, 2025, Amazon made Fire Tablet a video supply source in the Amazon DSP, checked by default for self-service online video line items, and said the ads would extend to the lockscreen placement with no extra setup, according to its launch note.
Then the buying system itself changed. On September 29, 2026, Amazon Ads put a four-step DVA+ campaign builder into closed beta in the US, with general availability in 35 markets targeted for the first quarter of 2027. The November 17 guide is written for that structure. Line items have become ad groups, the Amazon DSP has become the Display, Video, and Audio tab on the Campaigns page, and the beta label from 2023 is gone. The four markets are the same as in 2023.
One sentence in the new guide does not fit the rest. Under the heading "Create a Fire tablet campaign with these settings," it reads: "To advertise with DVA+, create a campaign and select Streaming TV as your ad group type." Every later instruction on the page uses OLV or Display ad groups, and none mentions Streaming TV. The page does not reconcile the two.
The documented path, step by step
The guide splits its instructions into three stages: the campaign and ad group, the ad itself, and activation.
Campaign and ad group
The campaign stage refers elsewhere. According to the Campaign tab, the advertiser follows the steps in Amazon's general "Create a campaign" guide, and that step can be skipped if an existing campaign is to be used. The tab then reads: "Next, set up your ad group and attach it to your campaign." The choice of ad group type at this point, OLV or Display, decides which ad formats can be built afterwards.
Building the ad
According to Amazon, creating a Fire tablet ad takes nine steps:
- In the navigation side menu, the advertiser clicks Campaigns.
- At the top of the Campaigns page, the advertiser clicks the "Display, Video, and, Audio" tab (the comma after "and" is on the page).
- In the Campaigns page side menu, the advertiser clicks Ads, then Create ad.
- An advertiser is chosen, where applicable.
- On the New ad page, the advertiser picks the tab that matches the ad group already created: the Video tab and Standard Video for OLV, or the Component-based tab for the three Display formats, as set out in the table above.
- On the next page, three fields are completed: Name, Language and, except for REC, Age range. The page recommends a unique ad name.
- The page points to Amazon's Fire tablet creative standards and guidelines.
- Settings specific to the ad type are configured, in tabs for OLV ad groups and Display ad groups. For OLV, the page lists only the fields unique to Fire tablet, says standard ad settings also apply and refers to Amazon's "Create a Standard Video ad" guide. The OLV tab names two settings: an Asset (the video, added through Upload file or Choose from creative assets) and Ad experiences, set to Online Video. The Display ad group settings sit in a separate tab of the page.
- The advertiser clicks Save, then follows Amazon's "Assign ad to ad group" guide to attach the ad to the campaign.
Review and activation
After saving, each ad goes through review "to make sure it meets our policies and any legal restrictions," according to the page. "The process may take up to 12 hours to complete. For high traffic events, the review may take longer." Amazon adds: "We recommend submitting ads early to avoid any campaign delays." The page was updated two days before Amazon's Black Friday deal event, scheduled to run from November 19 to November 30.
Activation then takes four steps, according to the page:
- In the navigation side menu, the advertiser clicks Campaigns.
- At the top of the Campaigns page, the advertiser clicks the "Display, Video, and, Audio" tab.
- On the Ad groups page, ad groups are activated with the toggle in the Active column.
- The campaign is activated with the toggle at the top of the window, next to the campaign name.
Specifications and the wording of the rules
The OLV asset rules on the page are short. The video must meet Amazon's ad specifications "to avoid rejection," and two lines set the resolution, aspect ratio, file type and length: "Resolution: 1280x720px or higher with 16:9 or 16:10 video aspect ratios" and "Format: .mp4 format with audio and be 15 seconds or less."
A muted video that must carry audio looks odd at first. Amazon's specification page for Amazon Video Ads on Fire tablet explains the logic: the clip starts muted when the screen turns on, and the customer can tap to unmute it, according to Amazon. That page sets out a fuller list of technical requirements than the Support Center guide, and the two can be compared line by line.
Two other fields carry rules worded in full on the page. On language: "Choose the language for your ad. The options vary depending on your region. Ads must be in the primary language of the country in which the content is streaming." The reference to streaming content is another phrase that reads as if written for television rather than a tablet's wake screen.
On age range, which does not apply to REC: "Required for ads for apps, films, TV shows, or video games that are rated appropriate for ages 12/13 and older (such as PG-13, TV-PG, and Teen ESRB). If you select Mature only, the content won't be shown on devices that have parental controls turned on." The parental controls filter is the only targeting consequence the page spells out. Amazon's video specification page adds that ads with trailers for content rated 12 to 15 or 15 and above are filtered from devices with active parental controls, and that trailers for 15-plus content can play only when a customer starts them on a custom landing page or external site, according to Amazon.
The creative standards linked in step 7 set limits on copy and calls to action. Amazon's standards page says wake-screen content must be appropriate for audiences of all ages, that the advertiser's logo or name must appear in the creative, and that film, TV and game ads should carry the appropriate rating icon. Its numeric limits are below.
The standards page is not fully consistent with itself. One of its examples rejects an ad for supporting copy of more than 20 words, three words below the 23-word limit the same page sets.
Twelve hours in, twenty-four hours out
The page's final paragraph deals with what happens after launch, and it is the part with the most direct consequence for reporting: "After a Fire tablet campaign starts running, data and metrics such as impressions and clicks can take up to 24 hours to appear in campaign data. Due to the semi-connected nature of Fire tablets, impressions can continue to be reported after the campaign ends."
