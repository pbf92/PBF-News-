TITLE: The Gospel According to Anthropic
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

The Anthropic IPO is now the largest stock offering in human history, despite nearly $1 trillion in construction commitments and widespread opposition to new data centers. Is its $1.5 trillion valuation the harbinger of a new era or another sign of an AI bubble?
The housing bubble was disastrous, but at least people shared a common understanding of "houses" and their use. With AI, we don't even have that. The industry's large-language model programs (LLMs) are technologically impressive, with many potential applications. But Anthropic goes even further than its competitors in arguing that LLMs have human-like, and even potentially superhuman, qualities that will transform humanity. It represents the cutting edge of the belief system behind tech's massive investment wave, and its S-1 document (the prospectus and supporting documents a corporation must file when going public) is the closest thing that system has to a written creed.
The first thing an observer might notice about Anthropic's filing is that it's long—very long. Anthropic's team is known for issuing lengthy statements, screeds, and philosophical reflections. (You might say they put the "chat" in "chatbot.")
As a literary text, Anthropic's S-1 lacks the "Robert Heinlein on amphetamines" quality that characterized SpaceX's. Its tone oscillates between dewy-eyed reverence and Calvinist-sounding omens of doom. The reader can imagine it being recited someday in hushed tones within candle-lit rooms. The reverence is appropriate—if you accept Anthropic's extreme beliefs about its product. If you don't, however, the reading experience becomes off-putting and even disturbing.
The name "Anthropic" is from the Greek word for "human." The corporation is firm in its beleief that its Claude chatbot is, or could become, something akin to human. The world got a glimpse of this in 2025 when someone obtained an internal Anthropic paper known as its "soul document," the company's in-house nickname for a draft of Claude's operating instructions.
The word "soul" does not appear in the S-1, but it characterizes Claude with traits that until recently were considered strictly human. Foremost among them is intelligence. "For the first time in history," the prospectus declares, "intelligence is something humans can build."
This is a hotly debated question, however. LLMs sometimes replicate human intelligence by producing text, pictures, or program code. But these things are outputs of human intelligence, not intelligence itself, and LLMs draw on massive amounts of human-generated data.
