TITLE: 'Dignity, trust, equity': Christian Sir Stephen Timms shares insight into landmark PIP report
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Christian advocates have welcomed a damning report which has found the main disability benefit is “not fit for purpose” and puts disabled people through a “dehumanising” process, urging the government to ensure the vulnerable are included and protected in future reform.
The Timms Review into Personal Independence Payments (PIP), claimed by more than four million people in England and Wales, has called for an overhaul of the system.
The government has committed to immediately adopting two recommendations: minimising unnecessary reviews for those with life-long conditions, and fast-tracking applications for those with the most severe conditions and people nearing the end of their life.
'Living up to values of dignity and trust'
Sir Stephen Timms, minister for social security and disability, “co-produced” the review with disability charities. The report sets out how any reform will be guided by principles including dignity, trust and equity.
The minister, who is a Christian, told Premier Christian News those values were “important because the Department for Work and Pensions is not always trusted and is not felt to always behave in the way that those principles set out. So we’ve spelt them out, and they need to be at the heart of the changes that we deliver in the future.
He said: “Applying for disability benefits shouldn’t be a dehumanising, traumatising, or humiliating experience. We want to have another assessment which is more about needs and the costs arising from those needs. For some people, that assessment will establish that actually there aren’t any costs arising from their needs, in which case they won't be eligible for the benefit. But I think we can do a better job in a way that isn't dehumanising like the current one.
As part of the review process, Sir Stephen gathered evidence from more than 40,000 organisations and individuals. This included a visit to a Christians Against Poverty debt centre in Bradford and taking submissions from the Church of England’s Disability Task Group and Deaf Ministry Task Group.
Sir Stephen said churches were “playing a bigger role in supporting disabled communities”, drawing on experience of services in his constituency which focus on those with learning disabilities. “This review is about rehumanising PIP, and I think rehumanising is a good message for churches because there's so much they have to contribute,” he added.
‘Giving disabled people dignity and respect’
Rachael Maskell, Labour MP for York Central and Christian, prompted the review to be established after leading a backbench rebellion against former Prime Minister Sir Keir Starmer’s proposed welfare reforms last year.
She told Premier: “What a difference it makes, having the voice of disabled people put at the heart of a report about them. We’ve seen a real sea change. Eighteen months ago we saw about 800,000 people expecting a cut in their social security, their PIP which optimises their independence. It's really welcome that the government has recognised the system is dehumanising, and it doesn’t work for disabled people, and needs to be completely refashioned.”
Maskell urged ministers to “ensure that disabled people remain engaged and co-produce the next phase, which is when the hard miles are going to be done,” as changes are made to the assessment process which the report deemed “dehumanising”. She said MPs were still waiting on further detail about how reforms could impact those aged under 25, after the Milburn review last week suggested they should not be able to claim PIP or the health element of Universal Credit.
Speaking about the co-produced nature of the report and its guiding principles, she added: “I know that the minister is himself a Christian, but also somebody of deep conviction and value-based in his approach. In welcoming this report, we can actually also welcome a change in societal attitudes that he is bringing about around disabled people, to ensure that people are really given that dignity and respect that they should in our society.”
Christian anti-poverty charities respond
Christians Against Poverty, welcomed proposed changes to remove barriers in the application process but raised concerns about “an important gap at the heart of today’s response” over how the government may look to reduce the cost of PIP by restricting eligibility.
Stewart McCulloch, the charity’s chief executive officer, said: “These factors risk leaving millions of people in limbo over potential cuts or tighter eligibility criteria and could create deep anxiety for many of our clients who are already in financial distress.”
Trussell, the anti-poverty charity which runs foodbanks at churches, has urged the government to act in response to the review. Helen Barnard, director of policy and research, said: “Too many disabled people have faced incorrect decisions, stressful appeals and months without the support they need to meet the extra costs of disability,"
Opposition view
Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has said the government has “no plans to cut welfare” and will keep “taxing everyone else”. The party has proposed restricting eligibility for what it calls “low-level mental health PIP claims”, particularly among young people.
