'A sad day for College Bank and for Rochdale' as Seven Sisters tower blocks to be demolished
After a decade of limbo for residents living in the iconic tower blocks - a decision on the future of the Seven Sisters has been made
Residents of the Seven Sisters tower blocks in Rochdale have been left saddened, angered and disappointed after learning their homes are going to be bulldozed.
Rochdale Boroughwide Housing (RBH) have made the decision to demolish the 766 flats and replace them with a mix of 601 new flats and townhouses. The decision brings to an end an era of uncertainty for the towers, actually called College Bank, which were first earmarked for demolition a decade ago.
What used to be a bustling community on the outskirts of Rochdale town centre now sits silent. That’s because only around 80 households remain from the original 766 since the original demolition announcement 10 years ago.

The uncertainty about the blocks' future has led many tenants to move on. Even hardened campaigners from the Save the Seven Sisters (ST7S) group have gone.
The campaigners have fought to stop demolition ever since it was first mentioned in 2016. They want to see the 1960s block refurbished instead.
RBH deemed it too expensive and believed it would take too long to refurbish the blocks, so opted to redevelop. They estimated that it would take 12 years to refurbish and cost £269m – around £388,000 per flat.
Then, even after that, homes would have an expected lifespan of around 50 years, according to RBH.
Joan Wainwright was lost for words when she read RBH’s letter outlining their plan. She is angry.
Joan owns her flat in Town Mill Brow, so is in a different position to other tenants who would need to be offered new accommodation by the social housing landlord. RBH have said previously they are offering to buy out leaseholders and vowed to support them in their move.
“Here we go again,” the 72-year-old said. “We’re not playing. Just no.
“There are too many anomalies with what they are saying and what they’re doing. They’re quoting figures that are totally off the planet.
“Whatever they’re going to put in their place is going to be totally inferior.
“We’ve had offers to buy, but they’re offering peanuts. The size of these flats are the equivalent of a terraced house. What they’re offering wouldn’t buy a terraced house.
“As far as I’m concerned I am too old. At 72 why should I have to go property hunting. Thinking about selling all my furniture, which I’m not prepared to do, and starting all over again – no.
“I moved in here when I was 36 and then 10 years later I bought it. I thought this is it – I’ve got the hospitals, I’ve got the shops – if I need a wheelchair I just need a little ramp and the flat is big enough.
“Then someone thinks they’re going to pull the rug out. I don’t think so.”
The decision has been made by landowners RBH after more than a year of working together with partners in the College Bank Task Force – which includes the council and Greater Manchester Combined Authority. The work included “a detailed review of all available scenarios”, says RBH.
This decision does not change the ongoing rehousing programme, which will see residents all moved out by next summer. Customers have already been moving out of College Bank since October 2025, due to safety concerns, and our priority remains supporting every remaining customer to find a home that is right for them.
Amanda Newton, RBH’s chief executive, said: “Today marks an important step forward for College Bank and for the future of housing in Rochdale.
“College Bank has been home to generations of Rochdalians, and we recognise the important place the blocks hold in many people’s memories. I want to thank our customers for the patience, honesty and resilience they have shown throughout what has been a long and difficult process.
“This decision gives us a way forward together and allows us to focus on the opportunities ahead. This includes creating hundreds of new, safe, affordable homes on the site that meet Rochdale’s current and future housing needs, while continuing to support our existing customers every step of the way.”

Mark Slater, chair of the ST7S campaign group, says the cost includes a plan for adding a second fire escape to the blocks, which he feels is not necessary under current housing regulations. So he is still confident they can stop the demolition.
Mark said: “The RBH board states that given the condition of the buildings they have a responsibility for the safety for the people that live there, of course this is true, Paul Roberts, chair of the RBH board states that the board ‘have no appetite for the risk’.
“What is clear is the condition of the buildings is a result of the neglect and poor management over a number of years by RBH, and there are cost effective alternatives that could see the blocks refurbished.
“The surveys, as presented, would seem to show that there were issues with the fabric of the buildings, but nothing that could not be refurbished. The community that have lived on the estate for the period managed by RBH are clear that since the demolition was proposed in 2016, there has been a policy of managed decline, with RBH consciously running down the blocks and doing the bare minimum.
“What is not clear is why RBH have refused to release their surveys for scrutiny and why they will refuse to consider the viable options that would see the Seven Sisters retained in Rochdale with a vibrant community as in years gone by.
“If RBH has no appetite to retain social housing in Rochdale, our political representatives must step in and take control of College Bank and Lower Falinge to retain these valuable homes for the community.”
RBH, say they will be involving the community in their plans for redevelopment and intend to consult with them over the coming weeks. They believe the new scheme would actually add to the housing provision to the area. They say, because of the increased number of family homes in the plan, a ‘minimum of an additional 249 bedspaces’ would be created.
By comparison, newly-built homes are expected to last significantly longer and better meet the current and future housing needs of people living across the borough of Rochdale.
Long-time campaigner and Tentercroft resident Roy Kitcher said: “I was disappointed when I saw the letter from RBH, because we were hoping they might do them up. So we’re going to have to try and fight on and get onto the councillors.
“It’s just a matter of regrouping and moving on with what we’ve heard today. It’s a big setback, yes, but we’ve got to try and move on with it.
“We can’t just sit back and let them take our homes. I am worried, because where are we going to go?
“We’re not going to get another property this size anywhere. They’re keeping people on housing waiting lists to move us into another property when we’ve got a perfectly good one here – it’s scandalous.”
Christine Hartley added: “When I read the letter I was quite angry, annoyed, upset and all kinds of emotions. I am still trying to figure it out in my head.
“I am unsure what is next. We’ve been told we’ve got until August to move out. But I can’t just comprehend what they might offer us. It doesn’t bear thinking about – I can’t.
“It’s more than upsetting, I’m really devastated. It’s just so hurtful.
“This is mine and Roy’s forever home – that’s what we said when we moved in. But yeah, we’re going to fight this.
“There isn’t a viable replacement for this. What they’re hoping to replace it with, I think it’s quite comical.”
A decision on the future of the Seven Sisters has been up in the air ever since demolition was put back on the table in 2024 – when the new regime at RBH came in. The social housing provider say they have conducted surveys that suggest there are a number of structural issues with the blocks and have costed up options for various forms of refurbishments and also demolition.
Following a year-long review of those findings, a decision has finally been made by RBH.
RBH chief executive Ms Newton added: “Our top priority is our customers, and our focus remains on supporting every remaining customer to find a home that is right for them.
“Over the last year, we have worked closely with partners through the College Bank Task Force to explore every possible option for the site. After carefully examining all the available evidence, we have concluded that redeveloping the site with new homes is the only realistic and affordable way to deliver the quality neighbourhood that residents deserve.
“This decision creates the opportunity to deliver a new generation of safe, warm, sustainable and affordable homes, while helping to secure the long-term future of the area. It also ensures we can continue investing in the homes and services our more than 12,000 customers across the borough rely on every day.
“We know there is still much work to do, but we are excited about the opportunity to create a positive new chapter for College Bank and a neighbourhood that can thrive for generations to come.”
Rochdale MP Paul Waugh said: “This is a sad day for College Bank and for Rochdale. It’s hard to ignore that a lack of investment over many years has contributed to where we are now, and residents have paid the price for that.
“I pressed for an independent review of the survey work, and I raised with the Housing Minister whether the blocks could be retrofitted and saved. I’m disappointed that the review has concluded redevelopment is the only viable option. What matters now is that residents get certainty and a real say in what comes next.
“RBH has said it will consult residents ahead of a planning application early next year. That consultation needs to be genuine, and it must include tenants who have already been moved out.
“I also want the council and other providers involved in shaping the future of the site, so that it delivers the good-quality homes Rochdale needs.”
