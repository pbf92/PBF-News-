TITLE: Stellate Ganglion Block May Ease Pain and Early Cognitive Decline After Laparoscopy
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis published in Frontiers in Medicine has evaluated whether perioperative stellate ganglion block can improve multiple postoperative recovery outcomes in patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery. Researchers led by Zhiyang Li, Yubo Feng, Dongmei Shan, and corresponding author Tiejun Li from The Fourth Affiliated Hospital of China Medical University synthesized data from 43 randomized controlled trials encompassing 3,733 surgical patients. The quantitative investigation demonstrated that administering local anesthetic around the stellate ganglion was associated with statistically significant reductions in resting pain intensity at 24 hours postoperatively, decreased requirements for supplemental rescue analgesics, attenuated declines in Mini-Mental State Examination scores at 24 and 72 hours, shorter durations to the return of bowel sounds and passage of first flatus, and reduced incidences of postoperative nausea and vomiting as well as rescue antiemetic administration. However, the study authors emphasized that the overall certainty of evidence was rated as low or very low across most evaluated clinical endpoints under the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework, with all included trials conducted exclusively within healthcare institutions in China.
Laparoscopic surgical approaches have become firmly established throughout contemporary surgical specialties, including general surgery, gynecology, and urology, because of well-documented clinical advantages such as decreased surgical trauma, reduced intraoperative blood loss, and accelerated postoperative recovery compared with traditional open procedures. Despite these recognized benefits, the perioperative course of laparoscopy presents several distinct physiological challenges that can hinder an uncomplicated recovery trajectory. Prolonged operative durations, prolonged steep Trendelenburg positioning, carbon dioxide insufflation inducing pneumoperitoneum, systemic neuroendocrine stress responses driven by tissue injury, and excessive perioperative opioid administration frequently trigger complex adverse events. Acute postoperative pain remains common and can impede early physical mobilization, promote hemodynamic instability, heighten cardiovascular risks, and potentially transition into persistent postsurgical pain syndromes. Concurrently, transient postoperative neurocognitive deterioration, characterized by diminished memory recall, impaired executive attention, and slowed psychomotor processing speed, can complicate the convalescent phase. Postoperative nausea and vomiting further compromise patient well-being, risking severe complications such as gastric regurgitation and pulmonary aspiration while prolonging hospital stays and elevating overall institutional healthcare costs.
Standard clinical management of these postoperative complications has historically relied on systemic sedative, analgesic, and antiemetic pharmaceuticals, yet these conventional medications carry inherent clinical liabilities, including respiratory depression, sedation, and opioid-induced delays in gastrointestinal motility. In response, perioperative medicine has focused on multimodal, opioid-sparing adjuncts designed to suppress pathological stress responses and expedite functional recovery within enhanced recovery after surgery pathways. Stellate ganglion block is an established regional anesthesia technique involving the targeted injection of a local anesthetic agent into the fascial plane surrounding the cervical sympathetic trunk and stellate ganglion. The physiological rationale underlying this intervention involves the reversible suppression of excessive sympathetic outflow, modulation of autonomic nervous system balance, attenuation of systemic neuroendocrine stress and inflammatory cascades, and potential enhancement of cerebral blood flow and sleep architecture. Although numerous primary trials previously investigated the technique, earlier systematic reviews pooled heterogeneous mixtures of open and minimally invasive surgical operations across diverse anatomical sites or examined isolated endpoints, leaving its specific efficacy across interrelated recovery domains in laparoscopy undefined.
To rigorously resolve this knowledge gap, the investigators conducted their systematic review in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses statement and prospectively registered their methodological protocol in the PROSPERO database under identifier CRD420261441978. Two reviewers independently executed systematic literature searches from database inception through July 13, 2026, across eight major medical databases: Embase, Web of Science, PubMed, the Cochrane Library, the China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Wanfang Data, the VIP Database, and SinoMed, utilizing Boolean combinations of relevant medical subject headings and text words. Potential study records were deduplicated in EndNote 21 and screened against strict PICOS criteria. Eligible studies were restricted to randomized controlled trials comparing perioperative stellate ganglion block combined with routine perioperative care against sham block or non-blocked controls among adult patients undergoing any form of laparoscopic surgery. Methodological quality was scrutinized using version 2 of the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool, which evaluates five specific domains of trial design, conduct, and reporting to generate outcome-specific and time-point-specific assessments rather than single study-wide summary ratings.
The screening and retrieval process yielded 43 eligible randomized trials published between 2016 and 2026, collectively randomizing 1,871 patients to receive stellate ganglion block and 1,862 patients to control cohorts. All 43 trials were conducted in mainland China, with individual study sample sizes ranging from 40 to 200 participants. Across the 42 trials reporting detailed demographic profiles, there were 1,246 male and 2,407 female patients, while one 80-patient trial omitted sex breakdowns; study-level mean or median participant ages ranged from 32.18 to 73.50 years, with American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status classifications primarily spanning grades I through III. Methodological evaluation using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool across 85 outcome-specific assessments revealed that 79 determinations, or 92.9 percent, exhibited some concerns, whereas six assessments, or 7.1 percent, demonstrated a high risk of bias, and none satisfied the criteria for low risk. High-risk judgments arose in three of seven trials assessing 24-hour resting pain, two of 23 trials reporting time to first flatus, and one of ten trials evaluating bowel-sound recovery, largely reflecting challenges in masking observable clinical signs, deviations from intended protocols, and gaps in outcome reporting.
Synthesis of the primary analgesic endpoints demonstrated that stellate ganglion block exerted a favorable effect on resting pain scores at 24 hours postoperatively across seven trials enrolling 651 patients, yielding a pooled standardized mean difference of -0.67 under a random-effects model, with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning -1.16 to -0.18 and substantial heterogeneity of 84.8 percent. Furthermore, meta-analysis of nine randomized trials involving 877 patients demonstrated a significant reduction in the requirement for postoperative supplemental rescue analgesia, showing a pooled risk ratio of 0.63 with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.41 to 0.97 and moderate heterogeneity of 54.7 percent, representing an absolute rescue analgesic event rate of 16.1 percent in the intervention arm versus 26.1 percent in control groups. Prespecified subgroup analyses examining the presence of patient-controlled intravenous analgesia identified non-significant between-group differences, with standardized mean differences of -0.45 in the patient-controlled analgesia group and -0.98 in non-user cohorts. Subgroup comparisons based on local anesthetic selection similarly revealed no statistically significant interaction between lidocaine and ropivacaine regimens, although the authors cautioned that the standardized mean difference cannot be directly benchmarked against established clinical thresholds such as the 10-millimeter visual analog scale minimal clinically important difference.
Evaluation of the second primary outcome, early postoperative cognitive function, was conducted by synthesizing changes in Mini-Mental State Examination scores, calculated as baseline preoperative scores minus postoperative scores such that negative mean differences reflect smaller cognitive declines. Meta-analysis of ten randomized controlled trials involving 850 participants revealed that patients receiving stellate ganglion block experienced significantly smaller cognitive score drops at 24 hours postoperatively, with a pooled mean difference of -2.16 points, a 95 percent confidence interval of -2.97 to -1.36 points, and substantial between-study heterogeneity of 94.0 percent. At 72 hours postoperatively, analysis of 11 trials comprising 930 participants similarly demonstrated preserved cognitive scores, showing a pooled mean difference of -2.86 points, a 95 percent confidence interval of -4.03 to -1.68 points, and 96.6 percent heterogeneity. Stratified analyses based on whether trials exclusively enrolled elderly patients revealed consistent cognitive score preservation across both cohorts without statistically significant between-subgroup divergence at 24 or 72 hours. Subsequent subgroup analyses comparing laparoscopic gastrointestinal resections against laparoscopic cholecystectomy procedures also demonstrated comparable protective associations across surgical types without significant interaction, while an outlier trial presenting opposite trends was noted to have enrolled exclusively elderly gastrointestinal patients with slight baseline cognitive imbalances.
Regarding secondary functional outcomes, gastrointestinal motility recovery was evaluated through time to first postoperative flatus across 23 randomized controlled trials incorporating 2,051 surgical patients. Random-effects meta-analysis demonstrated that stellate ganglion block significantly shortened the time to first flatus by a pooled mean difference of -9.03 hours, with a 95 percent confidence interval of -12.50 to -5.56 hours, accompanied by very high between-study heterogeneity of 98.0 percent. Subgroup stratification according to patient-controlled intravenous analgesia usage demonstrated flatus time reductions of -10.76 hours in analgesia users and -7.56 hours in non-users without statistically significant subgroup differences, and comparisons between sham interventions and untreated controls showed similar consistency. However, exploratory subgrouping across surgical categories yielded a highly significant interaction, demonstrating that the reduction in time to first flatus was most pronounced in laparoscopic radical resections for gastrointestinal malignancies, reaching -19.53 hours, compared with -5.73 hours in gynecological laparoscopy and -2.29 hours in laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Complementing these findings, synthesis of ten randomized controlled trials encompassing 824 patients demonstrated that stellate ganglion block shortened the time required for recovery of audible bowel sounds by a pooled mean difference of -9.27 hours, with a 95 percent confidence interval of -13.33 to -5.21 hours.
