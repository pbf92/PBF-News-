TITLE: Dreaded word Utd can no longer ignore as unthinkable $565m catastrophe explained
OUTLET: Fox Sports

Mauricio Pochettino has been shown the sheer scale of the job at hand at Manchester United after suffering a chastening 4-0 defeat to Liverpool at Anfield, while remaining winless during the week following a Champions League draw with Sporting.
While he could blame a controversial early red card to Lenny Yoro on the Liverpool result, the way in which they surrendered to their great rivals has caused deep concern. The Red Devils have now conceded not just the second most goals in the Premier League this season, but the most in all competitions across any team in Europe’s top five leagues.

Ex-United captain and right back Gary Neville was in despair on Sky Sports following his beloved side’s defeat to their hated foe.
“This is the worst Manchester United have ever been in 53 years," he said.
“There's no time to work on this. Maybe this will be a good thing in the long run that Pochettino sees the worst of it.”
While he and fellow host Jamie Carragher stopped short of tipping the Red Devils for relegation, the nerves are clearly there and questions are being asked.
Former United goalkeeper Peter Schmeichel is under no illusions as to the level of trouble his side is in - and he explained recovery needs to start as soon as possible.
"This team looks like a team that is in danger of relegation, so the aim now has to be avoiding relegation," he said.
"We've all played in the Premier League, so we know we're in November, approaching December. It's not now, it's when we get to March. If they keep doing this for a long time, I mean, there will be a problem."
Hundreds of millions have yet again been spent in the off-season, to seemingly little effect, and now that dreaded word is now a genuine possibility - relegation.
Tottenham came dangerously close last season, but with just eight points from their opening 11 games, United could be flirting with even more danger.
Outside of the on-pitch impact, the off-pitch financial fallout could be disastrous. 
The Premier League is one of the great sporting financial institutions. The estimated prize money pay outs within the league sit between 112.06 million GBP and 186.3 million GBP. Missing out on these would be disastrous for United’s balance sheet. 
Just in outstanding transfer fees, Manchester United owe other clubs an estimated sum of 416 million GBP which is due to be paid off over the length of the effected players’ contracts.
Financial analyst Paul Quinn, writing for the Esk blog says the possibility of relegation would be, while survivable, “a solvency and financing event for Manchester United, not merely a revenue shock”. 
According to Quinn, If United are relegated, their revenue would drop from 676 million GBP to a projected 390 million GBP within a season - a decline of about A$565 million.
Consider the fact the Red Devils are currently in 1.1 billion GBP of debt, it shines a light on how catastrophic relegation would be.
The Premier League heavyweight would be forced to make major reductions in the wage bill - but shifting high-earning players is extremely difficult for any club, especially given the lucrative contracts handed out.
United’s current fixed wage bill for their squad stands at an estimated total of 195.93 million GBP.
The team with the current highest wage bill in the EFL Championship Wolverhampton Wanderers, by contrast, have a fixed wage bill of around 44.3 million GBP a year.
Given the Championship’s rules around the wage budget being capped at 85 per cent of the club’s net income, United’s bills will have to drop drastically.
From here, the question becomes, who will buy the players that Manchester United have under contract?
Excluding the players whose deals are set to finish at the end of the current season, United have an estimated 15 players earning over 100,000 GBP per week. For context, there are two players in the rest of the Championship who are thought to earn over 100,000 GBP per week.
According to Quinn, there could be another severe consequence for United if they are relegated. The sponsorship deals which have made them the commercial juggernaut they are off the pitch could be altered or even withdrawn.
“Adidas is the single largest relegation lever in the commercial contracts. The deal carries a £900m minimum guarantee over 2025/26–2034/35 (£90m a year)," he explained.
"£10m is deducted for each season without Champions League football; adidas may cut its payment by 50 per cent in any year outside the Premier League and may terminate on one full season’s notice following relegation.”
There is also the spectre of the planned new stadium to replace the decaying icon that is Old Trafford. Once famed for its grandeur, the once glorious stadium is now prone to leaks, leading to the plans to replace the ageing monolith with a two-billion-pound, 100,000 capacity monster. Those dreams would be under serious threat should relegation materialise.
It’s a long way before the death knell rings out for Manchester United. They have international stars littered through their squad, including the reigning PFA Player of the year in Bruno Fernandes.
They should be able to recover from this. However, if they are unable to arrest their dizzying slide, there are real concerns, not just for their status in the Premier League, but their status as a global footballing giant.
