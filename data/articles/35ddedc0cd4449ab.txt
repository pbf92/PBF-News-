TITLE: Poch insists: 'We will find a way to win'
OUTLET: China Daily

New Manchester United manager Mauricio Pochettino insisted his team would find a way to win games after a tricky start to life at the helm of the Red Devils.
United was held to a chaotic 2-2 Champions League draw at Sporting Lisbon on Wednesday, after a 4-0 thrashing by rival Liverpool in the Argentine's first match on Sunday.
United, 19th in the Premier League, was upset with several refereeing decisions across both matches, but Pochettino said his team had to take responsibility for its situation.
"I'm happy to be here," said Pochettino, when asked about the various decisions that had gone against his team in his first week in charge.
"We are here to take responsibility... we are, for sure, going to improve and to put this team in the place that, because of its history, we deserve to be.
"Nothing is easy, but we have the energy and I think we have the capacity, and, most importantly, the players — and the power and the energy from our fans, that is massive — and I think united, all together, I think we will find a way to perform and win games."
United had two goals controversially ruled out against Sporting, but might have won the game were it not for a mistake from goalkeeper Senne Lammens.
The Belgian dropped the ball for Colombian striker Luis Suarez to score his second goal of the night, canceling out Bruno Fernandes' double against his old side.
Lammens also saved a Suarez penalty, but then gave away a second penalty, which the Colombian converted.
"This type of thing happens, that is football," said Pochettino.
"Football is about making mistakes — I'm always going to support my player if something happens."
The Argentine said United's defenders might have done better to help protect Lammens before he spilled the ball.
"When you are a keeper, it's not easy, that ball. And you always need help from your teammates," added Pochettino.
"But, of course, we need to accept it, we accept the challenge and we are here to try to improve in all the small details that, for sure, we will improve."
Fernandes shines
Fernandes set aside questions about his future at United by producing his best display of the season against his former club Sporting.
Fernandes' goals looked set to earn Pochettino a first win a week after taking charge, but United was undone by the shambolic mistakes of Lammens in a contest that featured three penalties.
While frustrated not to claim all three points, both Fernandes and Pochettino found reasons for encouragement in a performance that suggested the troubled side may finally be moving in the right direction.
"So proud of my players, it was a really tough game, and I like the team, the personality, the character," Pochettino said.
"We were again punished a lot in different actions that were key. I think we showed the way that we want to play, it's only one week working together. I feel so proud. I told them after the game."
With speculation over his United future, Fernandes had said on Tuesday that contract talks could wait until results improve.
The Portugal midfielder, whose contract expires at the end of the season but includes a one-year option, had said he was focused on helping lift United out of its malaise.
"(My) contract, that's not important now; it's irrelevant," Fernandes said.
"I'm more worried about getting results, trying to bring the club back into a position to be proud of."
Lammens' mistake
The 32-year-old more than did his part in a match that swung wildly.
Lammens first rescued United by saving a poor Suarez penalty attempt, but barely a minute later conceded a second spot kick by fouling Maxi Araujo, allowing Suarez to put Sporting into the lead.
Amad Diallo had a goal disallowed for a foul in the buildup, and then Fernandes scored twice in three minutes after the break to put United in front, his first from the penalty spot and his second a long-range shot that wrong-footed Sporting goalkeeper Kaique Pereira after hitting a divot in the pitch.
Lammens allowed Suarez to level when he inexplicably spilled a high ball in the 80th minute and the Colombian forward pounced to make it 2-2. United's frustration was compounded when a late Sporting own goal was ruled out following a VAR review.
Fernandes refused to blame United's 24-year-old keeper. "It's not an individual sport. The most important thing is that we understand that we need to be together in the good and in the bad moments, and it's not going to change the belief we have in Senne," he said.
"Now (it is time) to get back to the Premier League ... we need to really stick together, understanding that we need to fight for our lives, because at the moment that's our situation."
