TITLE: CofE Redress Scheme set to open in January
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The Church of England’s Redress Scheme for survivors of church-related abuse will open in January 2027.
Preparations for the long-awaited scheme are now in the final phase and from 7 December, an independent Redress Helpline will be available to provide practical and emotional support to people considering whether to apply. More information about how to apply and the support available will also be available from this date.
In a statement, the Church of England said it recognises that many people have been waiting for the scheme to open and its focus remains on ensuring that it is “ready to provide the fair, compassionate and well-supported experience that victims and survivors deserve”.
The scheme was originally announced in 2020 in the wake of calls from victims and survivors, recommendations from the Independent Inquiry into Child Sexual Abuse (IICSA), and other safeguarding reviews.
It’s aimed at enabling survivors and victims of church-related abuse to have an opportunity to seek acknowledgement of the harm they have experienced and access the support and redress available through the scheme, including financial payments and other forms of redress.
In a letter to parishes, cathedrals and other church bodies, the lead bishop for safeguarding Rt Rev Robert Springett said while "it cannot undo the harm that has been done, it seeks to provide a practical and meaningful response through recognition, support and redress”.
The Church of England said it remains “fully committed to delivering a Scheme that acknowledges harm, provides meaningful redress and support, and treats applicants with dignity, respect and compassion throughout the process”.
An independent Appeal Authority has been appointed and guidance for parishes, dioceses, cathedrals and other Church bodies about the scheme is available.
