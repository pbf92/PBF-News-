TITLE: Mauricio Pochettino scraps Man Utd rule after Liverpool loss as players face wake-up call
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

Mauricio Pochettino has wasted no time making his mark at Manchester United, following through on his intention to scrap the team's post-match day off.
Under Michael Carrick, the entire squad was granted time away in the 24 hours following a game, but the Argentine has eliminated that practice and brought the playing group back into the Carrington training facility.
Players were also instructed to arrive an hour earlier than previously required on training days, with cars pulling in from 8:30am. Pochettino will oversee a recovery day with his players at the club's training complex, according to The Mirror.
The approach mirrors that taken by Ruben Amorim during his troubled tenure. Pochettino is well known for his demanding style, which places a heavy emphasis on player conditioning, and it is evident that he wants to take every measure possible to ensure his squad is ready for the challenges ahead.
United have long drawn criticism for posting some of the lowest — and at times the very lowest — running stats in the league. Gary Neville was particularly vocal in his condemnation of the team's lethargic display in a draw with Fulham earlier this season.
Pochettino, particularly during Tottenham Hotspur's peak years, built a reputation on high-intensity pressing soccer. That brand of play has rarely been witnessed on the red side of Manchester, and implementing it would signal a significant change in direction.
The Argentine watched his side suffer a heavy defeat on his first game, with Liverpool romping to a 4-0 victory over the Red Devils, who remain rooted in 19th place as their winless run in the Premier League stretched to nine matches.
Pochettino, with virtually no preparation time at his disposal, was always going to face an uphill battle on Sunday, and his task was made considerably harder when Leny Yoro was shown a red card after just ten minutes. That gave the new United boss ample opportunity to focus on the controversy surrounding the dismissal, rather than his team's overall display.

He said: "It's clear that after eight minutes the plan was in the bin. Do you think that is easy? Even the best team in the world playing in this situation concedes four goals or maybe more. We are not going to lose more than one game how we lost today. I take the responsibility, and we keep pushing.
"We feel pain for this defeat, but we need to keep our heads up and translate that anger into energy to start to work from tomorrow for the next game. We are in a situation that this is very complicated. It's difficult, yes, but I think we fight, we made the effort, and I know how they feel now in the dressing room.
"The players are feeling the pain of the result. I told them to be calm: it was an unfair situation that happened. We need to accept that reality. We need to try to increase our focus, concentration and make better decisions on the field. We also need to be critical on ourselves but some situations happened in the game that we are not happy."
