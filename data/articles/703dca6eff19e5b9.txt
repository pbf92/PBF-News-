TITLE: Subtly, Manchester United looks like the new Tottenham, but can Spurs become the old United?
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

Tottenham Hotspur watched Mauricio Pochettino take on the Manchester United job in his latest Big Six appointment other than a Spurs reunion, as the Lilywhites had already hired Roberto De Zerbi instead while Poch was otherwise indisposed this summer as the manager of World Cup host nation the United States. And in truth, Spurs have no regrets here, because as much as Pochettino is loved, De Zerbi has been exceptional in turning things around for Spurs, both saving them from relegation and getting them to play a more modern and dominant style.
Pochettino lost his debut with Man United by a score of 4-0, as his side were decimated by Liverpool at Anfield. The loss has more to do with what came before for Manchester United, and Pochettion is going to find himself in a situation akin to what he faced under Daniel Levy at Spurs. Because looking at the summer business, Spurs fans can already see the parallels.
What makes Mateus Fernandes's excellent at Tottenham so rich for Spurs fans is the fact that Man United supporters were, in full on copium, deriding the 85 million pound transfer as overpay. They were boasting about how every other Big Six club was spending big on the likes of Fernandes, Sandro Tonali, Morgan Rogers, and Elliot Anderson while they were the geniuses for getting bargain bin buys like Carlos Baleba, Youri Tielemans, and Andrey Santos.
Tottenham want to win everything, ever year now
You get what you pay for. Manchester United have gotten nothing out of their signings and are near last place, in as grave threat of relegation as Spurs were last season. And given their start to the season is even worse than the 2025/26 Lilywhites under Thomas Frank, a convincing argument can be made that the Red Devils are in even worse shape.
Manchester United are the new Tottenham. Mismanaged for years, led astray by terrible management, cycling through managers left and right, burning through big names now like Mauricio Pochettino, and yet busy trying to find random scraps from the bargain bin to cobble together a mediocre side.
Lest Tottenham Hotspur fall into complacency again by reveling in someone else's schadenfreude, they have to focus on their own mission. And the clear goal of the project under Roberto De Zerbi and after canning that very same Daniel Levy is to take Tottenham to where they have never been before: to become the powerhouse of football in London that they really should be based on their popularity.
De Zerbi wants to be the new Sir Alex Ferguson, molding a squad of proven talent and rising stars into his own image to dominate a decade or two. Man United are the new Tottenham, but De Zerbi wants to turn Spurs into the old Manchester United - a side capable of winning everything year after year.
