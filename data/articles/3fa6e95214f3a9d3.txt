TITLE: Killer's rapist past revealed as he's sentenced for stabbing pregnant girlfriend to death
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

A murderer who stabbed his pregnant girlfriend to death is a convicted rapist, it was revealed as he was jailed for life. Stephan Garner knifed Amber O'Brien more than 60 times before stripping her, wrapping her in a tarpaulin and binding her in gaffer tape.
The callous 31-year-old left Ms O'Brien's body on the bathroom floor of his eighth floor Blackley flat before confessing what he had done to a relative. Police found Ms O'Brien naked and 'cold to the touch', lying in 'pools of water' on April 19 this year.
The 32-year-old had been stabbed repeatedly in the neck and face, trial jurors heard, with a pathologist recording more than 60 separate puncture wounds. Ms O'Brien was nine weeks pregnant with twins.

Garner was arrested and later charged, before going on trial at Manchester Crown Court. He found found guilty of murder earlier this month. Garner swore at jurors as the verdict was returned following five hours of deliberations.
As he was handed a life sentence, with a minimum 23-year-term, more shocking details in the case were heard. Prosecutor Nigel Powers KC said it is believed Garner put Ms O'Brien's cat Squeak in the freezer and left the animal to die as he cleaned up following the murder.
The court was also told he was convicted of seven counts of rape following a violent attack when he was 18. Garner was jailed for 12 years in 2015 following the incident in 2013. He was released in 2023 and was still on licence when he killed Ms O'Brien.
Mr Powers KC said Garner received a referral order for battery in 2011 and was convicted of grievous bodily harm in 2015 following an attack on a fellow prisoner. There were shouts of 'yes' and clapping from the public gallery as Garner was sentenced.
Judge Suzanne Goddard told him he had presented a 'carefully constructed tissue of lies', rubbishing his claims he 'lost control' during an argument and that Ms O'Brien had 'pulled a knife' on him. Garner claimed Ms O'Brien was verbally and physically abusive and had told him she was getting an abortion.
'We're done... leave me alone'
Trial jurors were told Garner met Ms O'Brien in Middleton. They started a relationship around the beginning of this year. It was described as being 'in flux', with repeated 'petty' arguments.
Ms O'Brien told her brother she and Garner 'argued a lot over silly things' and that she was 'unhappy about his mood swings'. The court heard two weeks before her death, she told her sibling: "Something ain't fitting with me."
There was also said to have been a 'running dispute' over Ms O'Brien's flat in Middleton. Jurors were told Garner wanted her to give it up and move in with him, but that she felt 'pressured'. Ms O'Brien did move into the Blackley flat three weeks prior to her death.
In the early hours of April 18, Ms O'Brien called her brother and said: "I can't stand him, I'm not staying here." Ms O'Brien told health professionals she was in an 'abusive' relationship, though no formal reports were made to police.
On the afternoon of April 17, Ms O'Brien had a scan which showed she was pregnant with twins. That evening, Garner drank alcohol and took cocaine before returning to his Somerton Court flat with a friend. Ms O'Brien arrived later.
Arguments ensued, with Ms O'Brien telling her brother she would go to her mother's house. Garner's friend was said to have witnessed Ms O'Brien 'throwing things' at him, but Garner 'brushed this off'.

At 3.42am she sent a text to a friend's boyfriend reading: "You up, need help." Mr Powers KC said: "At 4.06am she left the building alone, in her long coat, carrying her pet cat with her. The defendant went after her in his car."
At 4.29am Ms O'Brien was seen on a doorbell camera close to her mother's home, saying: "We're done Stephan, leave me alone."
"At 4:45am she is back in the lift to the flat with the cat on her back and she is not seen alive again," Mr Powers KC added. The defendant's friend left at around 5.30am, with Garner believed to have killed her some time after 6.20am on April 18.
Garner's trial heard he spent a day-and-a-half stripping and cleaning Ms O'Brien's body; bleaching the flat; bagging her bloodstained clothes; destroying her phone and getting rid of his own'.
"The truth caught up with him," Mr Powers KC said. Garner later told a relative he had been 'cleaning up' the bathroom after buying bleach and scouring sponges from Poundland and that he'd 'stabbed [Ms O'Brien] in the neck twice'.
Police forced their way into the flat just after 6pm the following day, met with the 'overpowering smell of bleach'. Garner was arrested at Blackley Cemetery. Twice interviewed by police, he stayed quiet. He told jurors he had 'lost control'.
"She had been threatening me," Garner - who pleaded guilty to manslaughter, but denied murder - claimed. Pushed to explain his claim he 'lost control', he said: "The constant abuse throughout the relationship, whether it was verbal or physical. Second was her telling me that she had aborted my two children. I felt manipulated and betrayed.
"Her slashing me and trying to stab me with a knife. I snapped because of all this stuff that has happened. When you look at all these factors as a whole that's how it has made me respond." Jurors rejected his defence and found him guilty of murder.
'A carefully constructed tissue of lies'
"It is impossible to know what prompted you to do it," Judge Goddard told Garner as he was sentenced. "You suggested she told you she was planning an abortion, that she came at you and you lost control.
"You were an unconvincing witness. If she came at you with a knife I find you would have said that to the police. There is no significant degree of provocation that could serve as mitigation. There was evidence that she had threatened you but nothing came of this.
"Nothing she said or did could have justified the brutal attack on her. I find your account to be a carefully constructed tissue of lies."
Speaking about Garner destroying Ms O'Brien's phone and dumping his own, Judge Goddard said: "There must have been messages that you did not want the police to see... that would have shed some light on what happened that night."
Statements written by Ms O'Brien's parents Amanda and John were read to the court. She was described as a 'happy' and 'bubbly' person with 'lots of friends'.
"On April 17 she went for a scan and asked me to meet her," her mother said. "She was so excited to tell me she was having twins. I will never forget her face as she walked towards me, arms raised in triumph, smiling from ear to ear.
"She came around that night so excited talking about what to buy. She left at around 10.30pm and I never saw her again. I had to go and identify the body, praying that the police had been wrong. I carry that image of her body lying there wherever I go and will never get over it.
"She had an infectious laugh, a beautiful smile, a loving and caring person who doted on her family. How things could have been so different if she could have got into my house or if he could have just left her alone. She loved me and I am thankful for that. Nothing the defendant can do can take away my memories of her."
Addressing Garner, Judge Goddard said: "Her family spoke of the joy she brought to their lives and how desperately she will be missed. She was a woman with hopes and dreams for the future. They will feel immense loss and also distress at knowing how she died.
"You chose to insult the jury and the family as you were led to the cells. This shows a lack of remorse you have felt throughout proceedings. You wanted to underplay the amount of alcohol and cocaine you took that night. You told a friend you were planning to break up with her.
"I find you killed her having lost your temper during an argument after many hours of drug taking. The precise reason is known only to you."
Stephan Garner, 31, of Somerton Court, Blackley, was jailed for life with a minimum term of 23 years. He will serve the entirety of the term before being considered for parole and will remain on licence for the rest of his life.
