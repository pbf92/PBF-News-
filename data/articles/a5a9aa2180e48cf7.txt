Mauricio Pochettino has ‘look of regret’ after first Man United meeting
Mauricio Pochettino arrived at Carrington for the first time as Manchester United boss and conducted a number of his obligations ahead of the weekend
Mauricio Pochettino's smiles after officially becoming Manchester United manager on Wednesday seemed to disappear for his presentation photograph alongside Jason Wilcox. Some fans have even suggested that he has already shown a hint of regret after meeting the club's director of football.
Pochettino has signed a two-and-a-half-year contract to succeed Michael Carrick and arrived in Manchester on Wednesday following the conclusion of his duties with the United States. As part of his first duties, Pochettino told fans this season would be special.
“This is going to be a special season to support and to make our players fight on the field and to get the results that we deserve," he said.

The 54-year-old flew straight into the country following the USA's match against Haiti in the early hours of Wednesday morning, and could be forgiven for lacking the energy to conduct all of his media obligations at Carrington. The former Spurs and Chelsea boss had a big smile on his face on the training pitch, but it had worn off by the time United published the photograph with Wilcox.
"Pochettino is not very happy having to take a picture with Wilcox," said one supporter.
"Look at his face. He hates Wilcox already. Poch is the guy for the job," said one fan in response to the photograph.
"Poch has been smiling all day until he met Wilcox," added another.
"Unbelievable that United had the gall to put Wilcox in the same photo with Poch. Clueless," said a further frustrated social media account.
"Look at his face. He knows the enemy is near," said another. "You can tell by his body language that he doesn't want to stand next to the loser called Wilcox," added another fan.
"He’s already wearing a look of regret on his face," theorised one supporter. Another said: "Already looks unhappy after meeting Wilcox."

Wilcox has drawn criticism from United supporters this season, having played an important role in United's hierarchy as they cycled through Erik ten Hag, Ruben Amorim and Michael Carrick in recent seasons, with unconvincing transfer business to moot.
Amorim famously called out Wilcox in his final press conference before being sacked in January. "I just want to say I'm going to be the manager of this team, not just the coach. I was really clear on that," he fumed.
"That is going to finish in 18 months and then everyone is going to move on. That was the deal. That is my job, not to be a coach.
"If people cannot handle the Gary Nevilles and the criticisms of everything, we need to change the club. I just want to say that I came here to be the manager of Manchester United, not to be the coach.
"Every department - the scouting department, the sport director - needs to do their job. I will do mine for 18 months, and then we move on."
