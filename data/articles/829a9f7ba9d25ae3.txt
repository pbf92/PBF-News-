TITLE: Roasting Partner Launches an Operating System for Roasters
OUTLET: Daily Coffee News by Roast Magazine

A new software platform called Roasting Partner opened to outside coffee roasting businesses in October, offering tools designed to organize production, inventory, sales, cafe operations and other day-to-day roastery data within a single system.
A three-tiered subscription program includes the Basic level at $449 per month, Pro for $749 and an Enterprise level for customized platforms supporting larger and multi-location operations.
Designed as a central operating hub for a roasting business, Roasting Partner uses tables, charts and dashboards to organize information across production, inventory, sales, purchasing, staffing and other functions as operating data changes.
For roasting production, this can include updated queues based on open orders, batch sizes and inventory age and volume, while bagging and fulfillment are tracked downstream. Green coffee tools track lots by location, age and volume, while incorporating purchasing, receiving, contracts, landed costs and green coffee market data.
Additional modules cover roast logging, wholesale sales, cafe operations and staffing, with integrations including QuickBooks Online and Shopify. A compliance section tracks records including pest-control, sanitation and temperature logs.
The software natively supports logging data from Mill City Roasters machines and can log roasts on any other machine with a TC4-style board or Modbus controller. It also interacts with Artisan roast-logging software.
“Roasting Partner is not a roast logger with extras. It’s a complete operating system built specifically for coffee, an ERP made for roasters,” Roasting Partner Co-Founder Tyler Reaska told Daily Coffee News. “A roast logger tells you what happened in the drum. Roasting Partner connects that roast to the green lot it came from, what it cost, the bag it went into, the customer who bought it and the records an inspector will ask for.”
Roasting Partner is owned by St. Louis-based Thomas Acquisition, the company behind St. Louis-based legacy coffee brand Thomas Coffee. Reaska is also the founder of coffee packaging company Modern Eco Packaging and AI company Percy Intel and previously founded and roasted for Sally Sue’s Coffee, which was acquired by Thomas Acquisition.
Reaska co-developed Roasting Partner with Rachel Tucker, an entrepreneur who previously worked in the salon business before moving into coding and coffee operations, according to Reaska. The software was tested in collaboration with St. Louis-area roasting company La Finca Coffee, which operates a production roastery and three retail locations.
“Every part of Roasting Partner was built and proven in that operation before any outside roaster used it,” Reaska said.
The platform began with forecasting tools for green coffee prices and inventory before expanding into other areas of roastery operations.
“It helped the roaster decide whether to buy on the spot market, lock in a contract or wait on purchases,” Reaska said. “That roaster’s-eye view of cost is still the core of the platform.”
From there, the system expanded into packaging materials, including purchasing, costs and inventory. Cafe operations followed, along with roast logging, batch planning and lot tracking.
“Once it ran every part of the business, the gaps showed us what came next: compliance and traceability,” Reaska said. “Roasters can trace green and finished coffee lot by lot, and every FDA-required record is a few clicks away.”
As the platform expands beyond its initial users, future plans include lab and quality-reporting tools, including certificates of analysis generated from lot records, as well as a marketplace connecting roasters with vendors for green coffee, packaging and freight.
“The marketplace is the big next step. It turns Roasting Partner from software roasters use into the place where roasters and vendors do business,” Reaska said. “Combined with Modern Eco Packaging and our own roasting operation, the goal is to be a one-stop partner for small and mid-sized roasters. Above all, we want to build software that stays relevant and adapts as the industry changes.”
