TITLE: Alexi Lalas sets USMNT benchmark for 2030 World Cup following Mauricio Pochettino's exit
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

After winning his final six games in charge, Mauricio Pochettino has cut short his tenure as U.S. Men's National Team head coach, opting instead to attempt to save the sinking ship that is Manchester United.
In just over two years in charge, the 54-year-old Argentine won 24 games, losing 12 and tying just one. Three of those wins came at this summer's FIFA World Cup, while two games ended in defeat, including their Round of 16 match against Belgium.
Despite the fact that they failed to reach the quarter-finals for the first time since 2002, winning their newly implemented Round of 32 match meant Pochettino had succeeded in guiding a USMNT side to a World Cup knockout round win for the first time in 24 years.
Regardless, reflecting on Pochettino's tenure, USMNT legend turned analyst Alexi Lalas argued on talkSPORT that this defeat to Belgium meant his time in charge was arguably "a failure," although he added that "we can say on the outside that [the team] is in a better position."
With this in mind, Lalas believes that the benchmark for Pochettino's replacement at the next World Cup is clear. "In 2002, we were a handball call away from possibly going to the semifinals of a World Cup," he recalled. "So that is kind of where everything is measured at.
"But really what it comes down to is the Round of 16. I know there was a Round of 32 here that we got through, but for all intents and purposes, he ended in the place that we have ended numerous times with other less high-profile managers, with other managers that haven't been paid as much and in a much more honorable way, if you will, going out in the Round of 16.
"So we will be getting past the Round of 16 and then at some point beating teams that you and I would look at as better than the U.S. and having that one moment in that day. Whether it's fair or not, that's what international managers, or certainly the international manager for the U.S. is judged upon.
"European, it's a little different because you have Euros and that high-profile type of tournament. But these are the big moments, and there's an Olympic feel to it where you have all of this time and all of this work goes into being successful in a very short period of time. And everything has to come together. It doesn't always happen, but that's what you're judged on, fair or not."

The USMNT is scheduled to return to action in a friendly on December 13 before a lengthy break, after which they take on Mexico in the CONCACAF Nations League semifinal in March.
It remains unclear whether or not U.S. Soccer will have appointed Pochettino's successor in time for their next game against Jamaica; however, as far as Lalas is concerned, plenty of coaches are likely to throw their hats into the ring.
"I think there will be a lot of coaches, managers who look at this as a plum gig," he said on talkSPORT. "The money we talked about, it's not a question of paying. And whether it's Pep [Guardiola] or somebody else, we can pay, and we've proven that we can pay the biggest and the best out there.
"That doesn't necessarily mean that that's the appropriate choice going forward. I think there will be names that have already been bandied about out there when it comes to foreign managers, American managers, some high profile, some not so high profile.
"But I think that there's a lot of I think there are a lot of calls that are happening right now, either from the agents or the actual managers out there saying, 'Hey, I would like to interview.'"
