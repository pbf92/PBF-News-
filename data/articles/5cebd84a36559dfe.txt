TITLE: Better cardiovascular health halves mortality risk in US adults with clinical obesity
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Clinical obesity, recently redefined by the Lancet Diabetes and Endocrinology Commission as excess adiposity with endorgan dysfunction, carries substantial cardiovascular risk. Yet whether modifiable cardiovascular health metrics can stratify mortality risk within this highrisk population remains unknown.
Using data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) 2005–2018, researchers analyzed 8,767 adults with clinical obesity. Cardiovascular health was assessed using the American Heart Association’s LE8, a 100point composite of four behaviors and four physiological factors. Over a median followup of 78 months, 758 deaths occurred, including 256 from cardiovascular causes. After full adjustment, moderate-to-high LE8 (score ≥ 50) was associated with a 32% lower risk of allcause mortality and a 32% lower risk of cardiovascular mortality compared with low LE8. A doseresponse relationship was evident, with each 10point increment in LE8 reducing mortality by 22%. These associations were consistent across subgroups but stronger in younger adults and those with higher education.
This study demonstrates that even among individuals with clinical obesity, cardiovascular health status meaningfully stratifies prognosis. For clinicians, LE8 offers a practical, multidimensional tool for risk assessment and targeted lifestyle intervention. The findings reinforce that improving diet, physical activity, sleep, and metabolic control can reduce mortality, suggesting that CVH optimization should be a core component of obesity management.
The work titled “Life’s Essential 8 and Mortality in U.S. Adults With Clinical Obesity: A Cohort Study”, was published on Cardiac Research (accepted on Mar. 3, 2026).
