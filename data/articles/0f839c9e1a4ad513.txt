TITLE: NI secretary draws on Christian faith in defence of Troubles legacy plans
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The Northern Ireland secretary has drawn on his Christian faith in defending the government’s approach to Troubles legacy issues.
Sir Chris Bryant, a former curate in the Church of England, told the Combatting Impunity, Memorialisation and Transitional Justice Under Threat conference in Belfast that plans were “human rights” compliant, drawing on a biblical reference in doing so.
“When Jesus is on the cross, the Roman centurions cast lots for his robe because it’s a seamless garment and they don’t want to tear it apart.
“I think human rights are a seamless garment… you can’t just pick and choose. It’s all of humanity that has to be embraced in that human rights,” he told attendees, The Irish News reported on Tuesday.
The Northern Ireland Troubles Bill, currently in the House of Lords, seeks to repeal a ban on civil action and inquests linked to the Troubles.
The government believes it will provide consistency in dealing with the unresolved legacy of violence. Opponents fear it could lead to ex-soldiers being pursued through the courts when there is little evidence.
Bryant was ordained as a deacon in 1986, serving his curacy in High Wycombe. He left ministry in 1992 over the Church’s position on same-sex relationships.
