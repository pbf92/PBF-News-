OUTLET: BigGo Finance

Dow Falls Over 8% for the Week, Nasdaq Erases Year-to-Date Gains; U.S. Stocks Face Dual Test of PCE and Black Friday Next Week
After a bruising selloff, all three major U.S. stock indexes have slid into technical correction territory. As of Friday's close, the Dow Jones Industrial Average had fallen 8.02% for the week, the S&P 500 was down 8.61%, and the Nasdaq Composite had dropped 10.02%, erasing all of its gains since the start of 2026. The S&P 500 is now 11.6% below its record high set on October 6, while the technology sector has fallen nearly 24.7% over the same period.
This selloff is not confined to U.S. markets. Asia-Pacific markets on November 20 directly followed the previous day's selloff in U.S. AI-related stocks: the Nikkei 225 fell 7.70% this week, and the Taiwan Weighted Index dropped 17.50%, marking its worst weekly decline in nearly 36 years.
U.S. stocks next week enter a Thanksgiving-shortened trading week. Key data including the U.S. October personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index and the revised third-quarter gross domestic product (GDP) reading will be released in a concentrated window, and the annual "Black Friday" shopping peak arrives on Friday. After the concentrated selloff in the AI sector, the direction of inflation and the resilience of American household consumption will be the two main threads for judging whether the market can stem its decline.
Key schedule for next week (U.S. Eastern Time):
AI Trade Retreats, Technical Pressures Release in Force
The market had hoped two major events this week would stabilize sentiment: Nvidia delivering strong quarterly earnings and Anthropic completing its closely watched initial public offering (IPO). But neither reversed the selloff. Anthropic rose 17% on its first trading day, then fell about 9% the next day.
Concerns continued to simmer over the financing needs surrounding massive AI infrastructure spending, the outlook for investment returns, and technology stock valuations. Reports about management changes and a regulatory probe at Nvidia further amplified selling pressure.
Technical indicators also point to market stress. The S&P 500 has fallen for seven consecutive trading sessions and broke below its 200-day moving average this week for the first time since April. The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) briefly topped 30 and closed at 35.89, its highest close since April 2025. The S&P 500's relative strength index (RSI) fell below 30, reflecting both rising fear and short-term oversold conditions. Oversold readings may create conditions for a technical rebound, but they do not confirm that a bottom has been reached. Meanwhile, safe-haven flows moved into U.S. Treasuries, with the 10-year yield falling to 4.912% from 5.154% the prior week, making long-dated government bonds one of the few havens this week.
Katie Stockton, founder of Fairlead Strategies, noted that the long-term uptrends in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 have not been fully broken, but weakening momentum means subsequent rebounds are less reliable. Mark Hackett, chief market strategist at Nationwide, said market fundamentals still have positive elements, but with sentiment swinging sharply, calling a bottom is not easy.
PCE Data Takes Center Stage, Inflation Reading to Shape Rate Expectations
The most closely watched macro data next week is the U.S. October PCE price index, due Wednesday. Market expectations compiled by U.S. Bancorp show headline PCE may rise to 3.8% year-over-year from 3.6% in September, while core PCE is expected to climb to 3.2% from 3.1%, with a 0.3% month-over-month increase.
Note: Figures are market expectations compiled by U.S. Bancorp; actual values are subject to U.S. Department of Commerce releases.
The rates market has almost fully priced in a rate hike: the CME FedWatch tool shows the market sees about a 97.5% probability that the Federal Reserve will raise rates by 25 basis points in December.
UniCredit economists believe goods price pressures may strengthen further, and the October PCE data could prompt the Federal Reserve to maintain its tightening bias. If inflation comes in above expectations, fears of further policy tightening will intensify and weigh on highly valued technology stocks; conversely, it could provide an opportunity for a short-term rebound.
On the same day, the U.S. will also release the second estimate of third-quarter GDP, with the market expecting annualized quarter-over-quarter growth to be revised slightly upward to 3.5% from the initial 3.4%. October personal income and spending, durable goods orders, new home sales, and weekly initial jobless claims will also be released. In the early hours of Thursday Beijing time, the Federal Reserve will publish its latest Beige Book on economic conditions, from which investors will seek clues on regional economic activity, employment, and price changes.
In other markets, major European and U.S. economies will release November flash manufacturing and services purchasing managers' indexes (PMIs) on Monday, and New York Fed President John Williams will speak on the economy and monetary policy on Monday U.S. Eastern Time. On Tuesday, the U.S. releases the November Conference Board Consumer Confidence Index, with a market expectation of 92.1 versus a prior reading of 91.7. In Europe, Germany's final third-quarter GDP and France's and Spain's November flash inflation readings will be in focus; in Asia-Pacific, Australia's October CPI, the Bank of Korea's rate decision, and Tokyo's November core CPI will draw attention.
How Strong Will "Black Friday" Be? Consumer Resilience Faces a Test
Beyond macro data, the performance of the U.S. holiday shopping season is also on the market's mind. Next Friday's "Black Friday" brings the traditional shopping peak, and investors will watch whether high prices, borrowing costs, and the stock market decline have already affected households' willingness to spend.
Oxford Economics expects U.S. holiday-season retail sales to grow 4.2% year-over-year in November and December, but real growth after adjusting for inflation to be only 2.2%. A survey by the National Retail Federation shows about 182.7 million consumers plan to shop during the five days from Thanksgiving through "Cyber Monday," slightly below last year's 186.9 million.
Jim Baird, chief investment officer at Plante Moran Financial Advisors, said that if American consumers remain strong, it would help support economic growth, corporate revenue, and earnings, and provide upward momentum for sectors beyond AI. However, rising energy and food prices and elevated borrowing costs could still squeeze household purchasing power.
On the earnings front, this reporting season is nearing its end, with next week's focus on selected technology and retail companies. Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) and Zoom will report after Monday's U.S. close; Analog Devices (ADI), Best Buy, and Dollar Tree will report before Tuesday's open, while Dell Technologies, HP, and Workday will report after Tuesday's close, and Deere will report on Wednesday. Among them, the AI server businesses of Dell and HPE, chip demand for Analog Devices, and the consumer outlooks from Best Buy and Dollar Tree will offer more clues for assessing the AI investment outlook and U.S. consumer resilience.
Note: Times are U.S. Eastern Time; actual arrangements are subject to company announcements.
In addition, U.S. markets will be closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and will close early at 1 p.m. ET on Friday. Shortened trading hours combined with lower liquidity around the holiday could amplify short-term market volatility.
For U.S. stocks, the key question next week is not only whether inflation data can ease rate-hike concerns, but also whether American consumption and corporate earnings can provide new support for a market under sustained pressure.
