TITLE: ‘More than just a game’: How GTA VI’s blockbuster launch fuels a booming content economy
OUTLET: CNBC

Grand Theft Auto VI has exploded out of the gate after a 13-year wait, drawing millions of buyers while fueling a parallel economy of livestreams, clips and other content built around people watching the game as much as playing it.
GTA VI has delivered a blockbuster debut, with about 19.1 million copies sold — including preorders — by Thursday morning, representing roughly $1.84 billion in sales, according to Sensor Tower. The GTA franchise itself has sold more than 470 million units worldwide.
Take-Two Interactive, which owns the studio behind the game, has not yet disclosed a profit figure for the title.
Ross Gerber, president and CEO of Gerber Kawasaki Wealth & Investment Management, told CNBC's "Squawk Box Asia" that he sees further upside for Take-Two, which his firm holds in its portfolio.
"It's crazy good. I've never seen anything like it," Gerber said, hailing the title as "more than just a game" and pointing to the franchise's potential to expand into film and television.
But that launch is also spawning a second economy around the enormous global hype and demand for GTA VI content.
Viewers watched 125.5 million hours of the title's content in its first 24 hours across platforms including Twitch, YouTube and Kick, according to Streams Charts.
YouTube creator Darren "IShowSpeed" Watkins Jr. led streamers with 4.47 million hours viewed across YouTube and Twitch. Separately, Twitch tracker CastAccord recorded about two million hours watched over roughly 36 hours of streamer Kai Cenat's launch broadcast.
Both creators had built anticipation with trailers for their own GTA VI coverage. As of Nov. 19, IShowSpeed's trailer had drawn 3.7 million YouTube views, while Cenat's had amassed 2.7 million.
According to Forbes, IShowSpeed and Cenat are estimated to earn about $30 million and $8.5 million a year, respectively, from their broader creator businesses.
Rockstar Games, the studio behind the franchise, has also moved to capitalize on the surge in viewer interest, partnering with YouTube on a 24-hour launch broadcast and introducing themed gifts that viewers could send during select streams.
Outside creators and agencies are also trying to capture the surge in demand for Grand Theft Auto content.
Agency Clipped & Shipped is offering a $10,000 prize pool to editors who can drive audiences to clips from Kai Cenat's streams, with qualifying submissions distributed across YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram and X.
A separate campaign offered $1 per 1,000 views for clips of streamer KreekCraft, with individual submissions eligible to earn up to $400.
The game has also helped smaller channels reach audiences far beyond their subscriber bases.
A single GTA VI YouTube Short drew 1.5 million views for Meda, a channel with about 1,220 subscribers and just 12 uploads. Four Shorts from Skorpy V drew about 5.1 million views combined, despite the channel having roughly 12,100 subscribers.
Longer videos have broken out as well. Vice City Dispatch drew about 900,000 views on a launch preview and another 264,000 on a follow-up, according to analytics firm Returbe, which recorded about 1,140 subscribers for the channel.
Those audiences could translate into thousands of dollars in creator revenue.
Using AIR Media-Tech's U.S. YouTube Shorts benchmark, 1.5 million eligible engaged views could generate about $490, while 5.1 million would yield roughly $1,670.
The two Vice City Dispatch videos could generate about $3,840 based on higher benchmark rates for long-form content. The channels' actual earnings could not be independently verified.
Moreover, the content economy is becoming increasingly automated.
On X, users are pitching artificial intelligence-assisted clipping operations that scan livestreams for spikes in chat activity, automatically cut highlights and distribute them across multiple accounts.
One user claimed an operation generated $21,000 overnight from 2.7 million views, equivalent to about $7.78 per 1,000 reported views.
The earnings claims could not be independently verified. X bases payouts on qualified impressions from Premium users, which are not reflected in public view counts.
And appetite for more content — and more revenue — may only be starting.
Wedbush analyst Alicia Reese told CNBC that in-game purchases could generate "years of high-margin recurring revenue" for Take-Two, which she believes investors have yet to fully price in.
She expects the console launch to be followed by a PC release and then an online multiplayer mode, mirroring the rollout of the previous title in the franchise.
Rockstar's launch announcement listed PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X and S, with no timetable for either potential follow-on. GTA VI has also picked up eight nominations for the Game Awards, including game of the year, best game direction, narrative, art direction, score and music, audio design, action/adventure and performance.
