TITLE: CryptoStar USA shuts down its last Bitcoin miners in Utah
OUTLET: TradingView

CryptoStar USA shuts down its last Bitcoin miners in Utah
CryptoStar USA switched off its remaining Bitcoin mining equipment on November 23. That ends active self-mining for a company that once had operations in both Quebec and Utah.
The final shutdown covered roughly 100 machines.
CryptoStar Corp., which trades on the TSX Venture Exchange under the ticker CSTR, describes itself as a Canadian data center operator focused on Bitcoin mining. Its US arm ran its last active miners out of leased facilities in Utah.
The November 23, 2026 shutdown hit about 100 mining units. It also took offline the cooling and networking gear those machines needed to run.
The company pointed to two pressures. Electricity got more expensive under an updated utility rate structure, and the Bitcoin price declined.
CryptoStar said the two together made its mining operations unviable.
Some systems are still on. Essential safety and security systems at the Utah sites stay operational while the company works through the transition.
The shutdown follows a long retreat. CryptoStar closed its Quebec operations in September 2024, leaving Utah as its only mining site.
As of late 2024, the company reported self-mining hashrate of 21.26 PH/s from the Utah site.
In February 2026, CryptoStar raised $634,500 through a private placement funded by insiders. The money was earmarked for operational adjustments.
Now the company wants to repurpose Utah. The plan is to host customer-owned artificial intelligence (AI) and high-performance computing (HPC) equipment.
CryptoStar says it has approximately 12 megawatts of spare capacity it could put toward that work.
On November 19, 2026, four days before the miners went quiet, CryptoStar Holdings entered a strategic partnership with a US-based AI infrastructure platform provider. The partner's name was not disclosed.
The AI hosting plan has conditions. CryptoStar needs financing, customer contracts and regulatory approvals before the new business can run.
Those approvals include land-use permissions and business licenses. Timing is also an issue: a temporary data center regulation is set to expire in March 2027.
So the company has stopped mining but has not yet started hosting.
For shareholders, the near-term picture is a company with no active mining revenue and a hosting business that depends on deals not yet signed. The undisclosed partnership shows what CryptoStar intends to do; it is not a contract with paying customers.
Watch three things from here. First, whether CryptoStar names its AI partner or signs actual hosting customers. Second, whether it secures the land-use and business licenses before the temporary data center rule expires in March 2027. Third, whether it raises fresh financing, and on what terms, given that its last raise was $634,500 from insiders.
