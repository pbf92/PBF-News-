TITLE: JPMorgan slashes Meta price target to $775 from $940 as AI spending concerns return
OUTLET: Business Upturn

JPMorgan has sharply reduced its price target for Meta Platforms, bringing it down to $775 from $940. The $165 reduction represents a cut of approximately 17.6% and signals a more cautious outlook from the investment bank on the social media company's valuation.
The revised target is particularly notable because JPMorgan had raised its price objective to $940 from $920 in October, maintaining an Overweight rating at the time. That earlier bullish view reflected confidence in Meta's advertising business and the company's growing use of artificial intelligence to improve ad performance.
The latest move suggests that expectations around Meta's future growth and investment requirements are being reassessed. However, a lower price target does not automatically mean the bank has turned bearish on the stock. The updated rating and the analyst's explanation for the reduction are important details to distinguish from the target change itself.
Meta has been navigating a difficult balance between maintaining strong advertising growth and committing enormous amounts of money to artificial intelligence infrastructure. Investors have increasingly focused on whether those investments will produce enough additional revenue to justify their cost.
Meta's AI investment plans are attracting greater scrutiny
One of the biggest questions surrounding Meta is the scale of its spending on AI infrastructure. The company has been investing heavily in data centres, computing capacity, chips and the technology needed to develop its artificial intelligence models.
These investments are intended to strengthen Meta's advertising systems, improve recommendations across Facebook and Instagram, and support new AI-powered products. The company believes these capabilities can improve user engagement and create additional commercial opportunities.
However, the financial commitment is substantial. Higher capital expenditure can put pressure on free cash flow, particularly when infrastructure spending increases faster than the revenue generated by new products.
JPMorgan has previously raised concerns about this issue. In April 2026, the bank downgraded Meta from Overweight to Neutral and reduced its price target from $825 to $725. Analyst Doug Anmuth cited heavy infrastructure spending and limited visibility into the company's AI product pipeline.
The bank also questioned how quickly Meta could generate returns from its AI investments beyond its established advertising business. Its concerns included growing competition from companies such as Google and Amazon, which have substantial AI and cloud infrastructure businesses.
Advertising remains a major strength for Meta
Despite concerns about spending, Meta's advertising business continues to provide an important financial foundation. The company operates Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, giving it access to a massive global audience.
Artificial intelligence has already helped improve the effectiveness of its advertising systems. Better recommendations and targeting can help businesses reach more relevant audiences, potentially increasing the value of advertisements displayed across its platforms.
This is one reason analysts have remained interested in Meta despite the pressure created by its infrastructure spending. The company's established advertising operations give it a revenue base that many newer AI businesses do not have.
JPMorgan had previously highlighted this strength when it placed Meta on its Analyst Focus List in November, retaining a $940 price target at the time. The bank pointed to AI-driven advertising growth as an important factor supporting its investment case.
The challenge is whether advertising revenue can continue expanding quickly enough to offset the rising cost of developing AI products. Investors are also looking for clearer evidence that Meta's newer offerings can become meaningful sources of revenue rather than remaining expensive development projects.
What the lower price target means for Meta investors
The reduction from $940 to $775 puts Meta's future valuation under renewed scrutiny. Investors will want to understand whether the revised target reflects higher spending expectations, a change in earnings estimates, a more cautious valuation multiple or a combination of these factors.
It is also important to remember that analyst price targets are estimates, not predictions of where a stock is guaranteed to trade. Different investment banks can reach very different conclusions based on their assumptions about revenue growth, costs and future profitability.
For Meta, the next major test will be its ability to demonstrate that its AI investments are translating into measurable business results. Strong advertising performance may continue supporting the company, but investors are likely to demand greater clarity on spending and returns. The central question is no longer simply how much Meta can invest in artificial intelligence, but how much additional profit that investment can ultimately deliver.
