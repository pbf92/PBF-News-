TITLE: Surprising No One, GTA 6's Comedy Is All Over the Place
OUTLET: Polygon.com

From the moment Grand Theft Auto 6 was first announced, I had little doubt that developer Rockstar Games would be able to deliver a high-quality game. Any minor worries I had that the studio’s approach to open-world design might have become dated in the eight years since Red Dead Redemption 2 went away as soon as I started exploring the impressively constructed world of Leonida. But there’s one thing I was far less certain of: the satire of it all.
More than being deviously fun crime sandboxes, every Grand Theft Auto game also acts as a snapshot of American culture at the time of its release. These games have never pulled any punches, taking swings at everything from the paparazzi to politics to the most innocuous corners of pop culture. GTA 6 was sure to take the same shots, but at a time when the targets are moving faster than ever. Could a game that has been in the works for more than a decade possibly have humor that felt up to date? Would it be careful to punch up rather than turn the country’s most vulnerable people into punchlines? And would it have the stones to take on President Donald Trump, a man who has dominated America’s political culture throughout the game’s entire development cycle?
If you’re going into GTA 6 looking for a specific satirical worldview, you’re bound to be disappointed. Rockstar’s continued commitment to making fun of literally everyone ensures that it will throw our current culture war for a loop. Everyone will have a reason to be mad about it — and everyone will have a reason to claim it as a victory, too.
Historically, GTA’s humor can be described as “South Park-core.” The series found its voice in an early 2000s era where the dominant philosophy of comedy was that it was okay to make fun of everything, so long as the ridicule was handed out in equal measure. There was little difference between going after George W. Bush or Terri Schiavo; both were treated as current events to be riffed on despite the gulf of difference between them. GTA was built on this kind of edgy topical humor, for better and certainly worse at times.

Comedy has evolved since then, and GTA 6 does reflect that change… up to a point. It’s a little more careful in its choice of targets, largely keeping its focus on rich weirdos, crypto scammers, conservative conspiracy theorists, and more of modern culture’s biggest weirdos. For instance, turn on the radio and you might catch a right-wing sportscaster ranting about trans athletes in sports. The punchline comes when the host starts talking about how much he cares about girls’ sports, creepily making a point to note how “beautiful” all those young ladies are. The intended target of that bit is transphobes, as GTA 6 paints men who care too much about youth sports as pedophiles using hate as a cover.
That brand of humor already seems to be striking a nerve with the right people. Search “GTA 6 woke” on X, if you dare, and you’ll find plenty of engagement-baiting comments from the usual suspects whining about it. It will be tempting to pull out some isolated moments, or reactions to them, and declare that GTA has undergone a full change in consciousness that sticks it to the bigots rather than punching down on the people they target.
That sunny read will only get you so far, though. GTA 6 still does have the same approach to satire that the series has always taken, and some weird targets wind up in its crosshairs. Gags about hippie animal rights activists who want to free chimpanzees from an animal sanctuary, for instance, feel oddly dated, a vestige of 2000s cultural satire — not offensive, just ancient. GTA 6 also doesn’t spare a lot of sympathy for those battling substance abuse, giving Jason and Lucia stereotypical “addicts” to rough up. Caricatures are still doled out in abundance in a game that goes out of its way to include a loaded scene where Lucia strikes back against a bigot who launches a racist tirade against her. Anyone who wants to celebrate that GTA hasn’t lost its edgy tone will have plenty of data on the other side of the political spectrum for confirmation.
Then there’s the gray area between those two extremes. There are moments where GTA 6’s satire will draw fire from all sides. At one point while chilling in Jason and Lucia’s apartment, I flipped on the TV to catch a cartoon. It starred Right-Wing, a superhero who serves the president. The episode revolved around the president tasking Right-Wing with rounding up gay people using a "gaydar." It’s not clever comedy, but its intent is to lampoon homophobic conservatives. The episode hurtles towards a low-hanging punchline, though, where it implies that Right-Wing is a closeted gay man himself. It’s the kind of backwards humor built to pop a certain kind of neoliberal who thinks that the best way to stick it to Donald Trump is to imply that he’s secretly having sex with Vladimir Putin.
The scattershot collection of jokes shows why social satire can be such a delicate operation. There’s a fine line between making a point through heightened comedy, and just repeating the thing you’re making fun of, but with a knowing wink. There’s not always a punchline when shock jocks are on air ranting about immigrants; sometimes the joke is just that guys like that exist. Yes, and? Like previous games in the series, GTA 6 can feel more invested in farce than satire, and that’s the point where players would be right to ask who’s really getting the last laugh.
Even if the punchlines aren’t always clean, there’s undeniably a function to all of GTA 6’s political gags on a broader level. In a series of developer quotes sent out to reviewers, senior vice president of narrative Rupert Humphries explained how every bit of satire is working in service of a powder-keg atmosphere that’s crucial to understanding Jason and Lucia’s journey.
“Living in the actual world in this present moment, it feels like there is so much noise and so much pressure on everyone — politics, the environment, media, social media. It’s coming at you from everywhere,” Humphries wrote. “It seems like we are all about two bits of bad luck away from completely losing it. That is the world we are showing you on the streets of Vice City and in the rest of Leonida — everyone’s been driven to a kind of madness that comes out in lots of different ways, from conspiracy theories to self-help cults to get-rich-quick schemes and drug habits.”
That approach may work for players long-term in context of GTA 6’s big picture, but the reactions to its moment-to-moment gags are bound to stir up some heated debates — as they should. Satire is meant to provoke and push us to ask questions about the world that is being lampooned. We stand to learn more about our cultural climate from how GTA 6 is dissected and remembered 13 years from now. If it doesn’t feel dated by the time GTA 7 comes out, it will be a bad sign for modern society. But in the meantime, everyone is going to find something to get mad about in GTA 6.
