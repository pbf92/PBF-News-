TITLE: EXPERT PITCH: WVU gaming expert predicts GTA VI will be popular, pricey, worth the wait
OUTLET: WVU Today

A West Virginia University gaming expert says demand is high for Rockstar Games “Grand Theft Auto VI,” which is scheduled to be released on Nov. 19, more than a decade after the arrival of its predecessor.
Heather Cole, teaching associate professor for game design and interactive media in the WVU College of Creative Arts and Media School of Art and Design, is available to discuss Grand Theft Auto VI as a generational touchpoint in the gaming industry, and the factors driving its price.
Quotes:
“I do an assignment at the start of one of my game design courses which requires students to make a list of 75 games they’ve played, along with an anecdote of each, over their life. Nearly everyone’s list contains ‘My parents finally said I was old enough to play Grand Theft Auto V’ or ‘I snuck over to my friends to play Grand Theft Auto V because my parents wouldn’t let me.’
“Development for Grand Theft Auto VI has been underway for more than a decade. It was only essential personnel involved for the first few years, but eventually the developers had to hire a lot of people to make this game happen. It became as large of a production as major Hollywood films with thousands of people involved.
“With so many people working on the project, it shouldn’t be surprising that the developers invested heavily in GTA VI’s production. A game developed at this scale needs to pay back its investment plus enough to fund the studio’s next project.
“For avid players that are going to spend a lot of time in the game, I’m sure the deluxe edition will be worth it to them. The company has bundled all the extra content you’d get through microtransactions — an online transaction involving a small amount of real-world currency within the video game’s ecosystem — up into a content pack. They’ve explicitly stated that this single player version will not have microtransactions, which I think a lot of gamers are sick of anyway.
“Grand Theft Auto changed the gaming industry. Playing on a console, you were usually just a soloist, and social gaming wasn't much of a thing back then. This game influenced ‘couch co-op’ and trying to figure out how to play with people over distances. It was one of the first few games that you could play with your friends and hang out.
“Grand Theft Auto V was one of first games that was accessible for people who with disabilities in motor function. The game had enough settings to adjust sensitivity and controls; not many games did that back then. It was a pivotal, revolutionary kind of game just for people with disabilities, and it profoundly influenced a lot of the industry standards.” — Heather Cole, teaching associate professor, game design and interactive media, WVU College of Creative Arts and Media
