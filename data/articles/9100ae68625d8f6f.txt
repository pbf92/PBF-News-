TITLE: Alibaba's Taobao and Tmall merchant revenue drops 5% as cloud jumps 53%
OUTLET: PPC Land

Alibaba Group's customer management revenue, the line that records what merchants pay its Taobao and Tmall marketplaces, fell 5% to RMB75.0 billion (US$11.2 billion) in the three months ended September 30, 2026, while revenue from its AI Cloud and Compute Services segment rose 53% to RMB61.0 billion. Sales and marketing expenses dropped 38% to RMB41.3 billion, partly because subsidies once counted as marketing costs are now deducted from revenue, according to results the company announced in Hong Kong on November 19, 2026, and furnished to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission as Exhibit 99.1 to a Form 6-K.
In Short
Alibaba's group revenue grew 10% to RMB271.5 billion in the September quarter, but the fees it collects from sellers on Taobao and Tmall fell 5%, and the growth came from its cloud and AI business and from quick delivery services. The drop in seller fees is mostly an accounting effect: Alibaba now subtracts some subsidies from revenue instead of counting them as marketing costs, and without that change the line would have grown 1%. For brands and agencies that buy media on Taobao and Tmall, the numbers describe a marketplace whose merchant income is flat, a company spending far less on reported marketing, and a new AI agent, QwenWork E-commerce, designed to automate advertising and other tasks for sellers.
The quarter in numbers
All figures come from Alibaba Group's results announcement for the quarter and six months ended September 30, 2026, unless marked with an asterisk as a PPC Land calculation. Percentage changes without an asterisk are the company's own, which Alibaba says are calculated on exact amounts. Merchant revenue and the subsidy reclassification
Customer management revenue is the closest thing Alibaba publishes to an advertising number for its domestic marketplaces. It does not separate marketing services from other merchant charges, and it is not comparable line for line with the advertising revenue of Amazon or Meta. In the September quarter it came to RMB75,033 million, against RMB78,855 million a year earlier, a decline of RMB3.8 billion.
The release explains the gap between that figure and Alibaba's own preferred view in one sentence: "Excluding the contra revenue impact from the new business development program, customer management revenue on a like-for-like basis would have grown by 1% year-over-year." The same wording covers the six months ended September 30, when the line fell 6% to RMB157.6 billion. Alibaba's June quarter announcement of August 20, 2026, had reported a 7% decline and the same 1% like-for-like growth.
What the program is, the release does not say. Its only description sits in the explanation of sales and marketing costs, which fell "primarily due to more efficient investment in Taobao Instant Commerce and the impact of our new business development program, where relevant subsidies previously recorded as sales and marketing expenses are now recorded as contra revenue item." Cost of revenue as a share of revenue also rose, to 62.0% from 60.8%, which Alibaba attributes in part to "the contra revenue impact from the new business development program."
How the reclassification moves the numbers
According to the release, the change works through three lines of the income statement:
- Subsidies that Alibaba previously recorded as sales and marketing expenses are now recorded as a contra revenue item, which reduces reported revenue.
- Customer management revenue therefore falls on a reported basis. Alibaba states that, excluding the program, the line would have grown 1% in the quarter and 1% over six months.
- Sales and marketing expenses fall by a matching amount, on top of the reduction Alibaba attributes to "more efficient investment in Taobao Instant Commerce."
Alibaba gives no renminbi figure for the contra revenue. A rough estimate is possible from the two growth rates: a 1% increase on RMB78.9 billion would have produced about RMB79.6 billion, so the program reduced the quarter's customer management revenue by something in the region of RMB4 billion to RMB5 billion, once rounding of the published percentages is allowed for*. On the same arithmetic, the six-month effect is in the region of RMB11 billion to RMB13 billion*. Neither estimate comes from Alibaba.
That matters for anyone reading the 38% fall in sales and marketing expenses. The line dropped by RMB25.2 billion*, to RMB41.3 billion, and fell to 15.2% of revenue from 26.8%. Only a part of that is the subsidies that moved into contra revenue; the rest, on Alibaba's description, comes from spending less to win orders in Taobao Instant Commerce. Excluding share-based compensation, sales and marketing costs fell to 15.1% of revenue from 26.6%.
The note on revenue by type in the Hong Kong version of the announcement gives a wider view. Revenue from customer management services across the whole group, which includes marketplaces outside China, was RMB217.1 billion in the six months, down 2.8%* from RMB223.4 billion. Over the same period, cloud services revenue rose 55.6%* to RMB82.2 billion and sales of goods rose 17.1%* to RMB127.0 billion. Customer management revenue from China E-commerce made up 27.6%* of group revenue in the September quarter, against 31.8%* a year earlier.
The release gives no gross merchandise value, no take rate and no split between search, display and commission income, so it is not possible to say whether merchants spent more or less on Taobao and Tmall advertising in the quarter. The like-for-like 1% is the only underlying growth figure Alibaba offers.
Against other Chinese platforms and Western retail media
The pattern of shrinking marketing budgets runs through China's large platforms this autumn. JD.com cut its marketing expenses by 26.2% to RMB15.5 billion in the September quarter as losses in its food delivery business narrowed by more than half, while its marketplace and marketing revenues rose 9.6% to RMB28.1 billion. Baidu's online marketing revenue fell 22% to RMB11.9 billion as its AI cloud infrastructure revenue rose 94%. Alibaba's reported decline in customer management revenue is smaller than Baidu's, but unlike JD's merchant income it is not growing.
Western comparisons show the scale. At US$11.2 billion, Alibaba's quarterly customer management revenue sits below the US$19.64 billion that Amazon reported in advertising revenue for the same quarter, up 11%, and far below Meta's US$63.06 billion in advertising revenue, up 26%. Alibaba's line is not limited to advertising, so the gap in pure advertising is likely wider than these figures suggest, although the release does not allow that to be measured.
QwenWork E-commerce: an agent that runs merchant advertising
The most direct change for sellers is a product rather than a number. Alibaba says it launched QwenWork E-commerce for merchants in October 2026, describing it as "an AI product powered by our agentic AI capabilities." According to the release, the product works as follows:
- It sits inside QwenWork, which Alibaba calls "our one-stop AI agent platform."
- It is "designed to automate end-to-end merchant workflows, including store operations, business analytics, advertising and customer service."
- It "connects directly to Taobao and Tmall data" by drawing on Alibaba's existing merchant tools.
- It "will further support data access across other platforms, enabling more seamless cross-platform operations." The release gives no date or list of platforms for that step.
On the earnings call, Chief Executive Eddie Wu said: "We recently launched QwenWork E-Commerce, and the percentage of paying e-commerce users continues to rise." He gave no price, user count or share of merchants. Chief Financial Officer Toby Xu said on the call that the product "is able to automate end-to-end cross-platform operations for e-commerce merchants," according to a transcript published by Investing.com.
On the shopper side, Alibaba says the Qwen model family's multimodal capabilities help it "better understand complex consumer needs and deliver more relevant product recommendations with knowledge-based reasoning." The release adds: "These enhanced capabilities have driven a notable increase in general knowledge-related queries, creating more opportunities to identify latent consumer demand, facilitate product discovery, and drive sales conversion." No query volumes or conversion figures accompany the claim.
Automated bidding on Taobao is not new. In June 2026, PPC Land reported that Alibaba's Bid2X model lifted GMV by 4.65% on Taobao in a two-month online test covering about one million campaigns. QwenWork E-commerce moves the automation from bidding to the wider set of tasks a merchant's operations team performs, advertising included, which raises the question of how much budget control sellers keep when an agent built by the platform also manages their campaigns on that platform. The release does not address it.
Shoppers, members and the consumption climate
Alibaba tracks its highest-spending customers through 88VIP, its membership program. The number of members "continued to increase by double digits year-over-year to approximately 66 million as of September 30, 2026," up from approximately 64 million at June 30, according to the June quarter announcement. The release says the ARPU of new 88VIP members, "defined as those who joined the membership program within the past year, increased significantly year-over-year during the 11.11 Global Shopping Festival." No figure is given.
Xianyu, the second-hand marketplace, grew daily active users by 23% and paid orders by 29%, according to the release, which credits AI features "including smart pricing, automated product listing descriptions and precise buyer targeting."
The outlook for the December quarter, which includes the 11.11 festival, was cautious. Asked about Taobao and Tmall on the call, Jiang Fan, Chief Executive of Alibaba E-commerce Business Group, answered in Chinese. The Investing.com transcript prints the English interpretation of his answers under Eddie Wu's name, but the Chinese text it carries is attributed to Jiang. Through the interpreter, he said: "Consumers are becoming more cautious in their spending, and certain categories continue to face intense competition. Quarter to date, our key operating metrics have been in line with the trend of national online physical goods retail sales." He added that "consumer staples are showing healthier growth than discretionary products."
Quick commerce: more revenue, smaller losses
Alibaba's quick commerce segment, China Quick Commerce, combines Taobao Instant Commerce, the Freshippo grocery chain and the on-demand delivery business of Tmall Supermarket. Revenue rose 22% to RMB58.8 billion, "primarily driven by Freshippo," and rose 10.2%* from the June quarter. Year-on-year growth slowed from 45.1%* in the June quarter.
Alibaba does not publish a loss figure for the business. The release says Taobao Instant Commerce "continued to improve its unit economics quarter-over-quarter while maintaining market share," driven by higher average order value and "an increasing focus on high-value food and non-food categories."
