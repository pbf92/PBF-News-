TITLE: Christian woman killed in South Sudan for refusing forced marriage
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

An 18-year-old Christian girl has been beaten to death in South Sudan, after refusing to enter a forced marriage arranged by her family.
International Christian Concern (ICC) said Ajak Mabior had been expected to marry an older Muslim man, but objected to the marriage because of her faith and the forced nature of the arrangement.
After attempting to escape, it's been alleged she was killed by her own brother and cousin in Jonglei. Days later, police arrested the two men but released them without charges after family members demanded their freedom, arguing that “there was no case” because they were relatives.
The killing has raised serious concerns among Christian leaders about the pressure Christian girls face when their faith and personal convictions conflict with family expectations.
“When a young girl is killed for refusing a forced marriage, and the killers walk free because they are family, the message is clear: Tradition is stronger than the law, and stronger than the value of a life,” a local pastor said. “Many of our Christian daughters live under this same shadow.”
Mabior’s case raises concerns about forced interfaith marriages, religious pressure, and violence against young Christian women in South Sudan. For Christian girls, forced marriages can become a matter of religious persecution when they are pressured to marry outside their faith and abandon their Christian convictions.
Those who refuse may face rejection, threats, intimidation, and violence from their own families. Mabior’s death highlights the vulnerability of young Christian women who seek to remain faithful to Christ while facing pressure to submit to marriages that violate their faith and conscience.
An International Christian Concern (ICC) staffer said: “We call on the wider Body of Christ to pray for Ajak’s justice, and for the protection of Christian girls facing forced marriages because of their faith.
“Pray that the Church will have the courage to speak against religious coercion and violence, and that the authorities will uphold the law and protect vulnerable believers.”
