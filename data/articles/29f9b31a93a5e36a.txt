TITLE: 6 Arrested After Illegal Dig Damages Ancient Cistern in Judean Hills
OUTLET: Charisma Magazine Online

Authorities arrested six people after Israel Antiquities Authority inspectors caught them using a jackhammer inside an ancient cistern in the Judean Hills, CBN News reported. Investigators believe a campfire above the cistern was meant to make the gathering look like a picnic.
Inspectors found the diggers nearly 30 feet underground at Horbat Adash, where a settlement stood during the Second Temple period. By the time inspectors arrived, the digging had damaged the cistern’s walls and floor and broken through centuries-old plaster.
The cistern held fragments from hundreds of ancient pottery vessels. Among the pieces recovered was the neck of an animal-shaped vessel from the Islamic period.
CBN News correspondent Joshua Swanson also revisited a separate case on Jerusalem’s Mount Scopus, where Eitan Klein and his team caught looters at night. Klein pointed to an ancient quarry where the looters had been looking for the entrance to a cave. When Swanson asked what the damage meant for archaeologists, Klein called it “a total loss.”
Klein told CBN News that someone tries to steal antiquities somewhere in Israel almost every night. The thefts have continued during wartime.
Ancient coins are a common target, Klein said. Archaeologists can use them to date the layer of earth where they were found and identify who ruled at the time. When looters remove coins and other artifacts, they take away clues archaeologists need to reconstruct the site’s history.
Klein said an untouched burial cave can hold objects that, studied together, tell a story about the people who left them behind. He said the biblical and historical accounts of Israel and its people inspired him to protect the places where those events unfolded.
“This is the place where Jesus walked,” Klein told CBN News.
