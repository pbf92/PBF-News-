OUTLET: TradingView

Gold slumps below $3,800 as Fed officials signal more rate hikes
Gold dropped below $3,800 an ounce for the first time since September 2025 after Federal Reserve officials made the case for more interest-rate hikes to tackle stubborn inflation.
Bullion fell as much as 1.5% to near $3,790 an ounce, a third day of declines, after Fed Bank of St. Louis President Alberto Musalem said the US economy would likely need “several interest-rate increases” before spring. His counterpart at the New York Fed, John Williams, said the central bank still has work to do to bring inflation back to its 2% target.

“It is a tough environment for gold, particularly with buying interest still subdued,” said Christopher Wong, a strategist at Oversea-Chinese Banking Corp. “The longer gold remains below $4.000, the greater the risk of renewed selling pressure,” he said, adding that the market would stay cautious in the absence of any catalyst for a reversal.
For the next clues on the Fed’s rate path, investors will be focused on minutes from the central bank’s October meeting due for release later Wednesday. In remarks earlier this week, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh steered clear of offering any direct comment on monetary policy, in line with his stated preference not to provide near-term guidance on rates.
Spot gold fell 1.4% to $3,795.55 an ounce at 10:08 a.m. in Singapore. Silver dropped 2.6% to $46.18 an ounce. Platinum and palladium also declined.
