TITLE: Domestic abuse victim characteristics, England and Wales: year ending March 2026
OUTLET: Office for National Statistics

The statistics in this release are taken from the Crime Survey for England and Wales (CSEW) and police recorded crime. They refer to people aged 16 years and over, for the year ending (YE) March 2026, unless otherwise stated.
Our Domestic abuse in England and Wales overview article summarises our statistics on domestic abuse, while our Domestic abuse prevalence and trends, England and Wales article presents headline prevalence measures.

Crime Survey for England and Wales
According to the CSEW, 11.5% of females aged 16 years and over and 7.6% of males experienced domestic abuse in the last year. This is equivalent to 2.8 million females and 1.8 million males.
There was a statistically significant increase in domestic abuse prevalence in the last year for both females and males in YE March 2026, compared with YE March 2025. Prevalence increased from 9.1% to 11.5% for females and from 6.5% to 7.6% for males.
Our best assessment is that this increase includes real change. However, changes to the questionnaire and its implementation may have affected the scale of the increase. In addition, as a single year's data point, it is too early to say whether this increase will come to represent a new, higher level of prevalence.
A significantly higher proportion of people aged 20 to 24 years were victims of domestic abuse in the last year, compared with those aged 25 years and over.
Information on the terminology used in this release is available in Section 12: Definitions.
Police recorded crime
The police recorded that the victim was female in 71.6% of domestic abuse-related crimes, in YE March 2026.
Police recorded crime data do not provide a measure of domestic abuse prevalence. Changes in police recording practices have affected the comparability of domestic abuse-related police recorded crime data over time.
71.6% of victims of domestic homicide were female, compared with 10.7% of victims of non-domestic homicide between YE March 2023 and YE March 2025.
Statistical significance
In this release, "significant" means "statistically significant" unless otherwise stated.

Crime Survey for England and Wales
The Crime Survey for England and Wales estimated that 11.5% of females and 7.6% of males aged 16 years and over experienced domestic abuse in the last year. This is equivalent to 2.8 million females and 1.8 million males.
A significantly higher proportion of females were victims of domestic, partner and family abuse than males.
Split by the different types of abuse, prevalence rates for females were significantly higher, compared with males for all abuse types (Figure 2).

Domestic abuse prevalence in the last year was significantly higher in year ending (YE) March 2026 than in YE March 2025 for both females and males. Prevalence increased from 9.1% to 11.5% for females and from 6.5% to 7.6% for males.
Partner abuse increased significantly in the last year, from 7.3% to 9.5% for females and from 4.9% to 6.3% for males. There were no significant changes in family abuse in the last year for females and males.
The significant increase in partner abuse was attributable to a significant increase in abuse by an ex-partner, which increased from 6.2% to 8.6% for females and from 3.7% to 5.1% for males (Figure 3). For females, all types of abuse by an ex-partner increased significantly. For males, only emotional abuse increased significantly compared with YE March 2025.

Police recorded crime
Data supplied from 34 police forces showed that the victim was:
- female in 71.6% of domestic abuse-related crimes
- male in 28.4% of domestic abuse-related crimes
This proportion was similar for most offence categories. However, for domestic abuse-related sexual offences, the proportion of female victims was 90.5%, compared with 9.5% for males (Figure 4). This pattern was similar to that seen in previous years.

Crime Survey for England and Wales
Crime Survey for England and Wales estimates showed that a significantly higher proportion of people aged 20 to 24 years (13.8%) were victims of domestic abuse, compared with those in age categories of 25 years and over (Figure 5).
In contrast, the proportion of people who were victims of domestic abuse, and who were aged 60 to 74 years (7.6%), or 75 years and over (3.6%), was significantly lower than people in age groups between 16 and 59 years.
While the broad pattern hasn't changed, compared with year ending March 2025, domestic abuse significantly increased for people in age groups between 35 and 74 years (Figure 5). For all other age groups, the differences were not significant.

Police recorded crime
Data supplied from 33 police forces allow analysis for all police recorded violence against the person offences by sex. These data showed that for females in age groups between 20 and 44 years, over half of the offences (between 50.5% and 52.9%) were domestic abuse-related.
Among domestic abuse-related crimes, there was a higher proportion of violence against the person offences with male victims in older age groups than in younger age groups.

Estimates from the Crime Survey for England and Wales for year ending March 2026 show that a significantly higher proportion of people in the Mixed (13.5%) and White ethnic groups (10.4%) experienced domestic abuse in the last year, compared with those in the Black or Black British (7.5%), Asian or Asian British (4.9%), and Other ethnic groups (5.4%, Figure 7). There was no significant difference in the prevalence of domestic abuse in the last year between the Mixed and White ethnic groups.

The Crime Survey for England and Wales year ending March 2026 estimated that 4.5% of people aged 16 years and over experienced partner abuse by a male perpetrator in the last year, while 3.0% experienced partner abuse by a female perpetrator (Figure 8).
Split by sex of victim, the prevalence of partner abuse among females was higher where the perpetrator was male (8.6%) than where the perpetrator was female (0.2%).
The prevalence of partner abuse among males was higher where the perpetrator was female (6.0%) than where the perpetrator was male (0.3%).
Estimates exclude victims who have had both male and female partners, as it is not possible to determine whether the perpetrator was male or female, or whether multiple perpetrators were of both sexes.

Crime Survey for England and Wales estimates showed that a significantly lower proportion of people who were married or in a civil partnership (5.9%, Figure 9) experienced domestic abuse in the last year than those who were either:
- cohabiting (12.2%)
- single (13.7%)
- separated (22.6%)
- divorced (17.6%)
Marital status may have changed as a result of the abuse experienced.
