TITLE: Anterior Temporal Lobectomy Yields Higher Seizure Freedom Than Selective Amygdalohippocampectomy in Review
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis comparing two prominent neurosurgical approaches for drug-resistant mesial temporal lobe epilepsy indicates that anterior temporal lobectomy provides a statistically significant advantage in achieving postoperative seizure freedom over selective amygdalohippocampectomy, while the selective procedure reduces the risk of surgical complications, verbal memory decline, and visual field impairments. Mesial temporal lobe epilepsy represents the most prevalent form of focal epilepsy in adult populations and is frequently linked to hippocampal sclerosis. For approximately one-third of all individuals diagnosed with epilepsy, and up to two-thirds of those experiencing mesial temporal lobe epilepsy specifically, standard antiseizure medications fail to provide adequate relief. In these drug-resistant scenarios, surgical resection stands as the primary evidence-based therapeutic pathway capable of offering long-term seizure control and potentially complete seizure freedom.
The two surgical techniques evaluated in the synthesis stem from distinct operative philosophies developed over several decades. Anterior temporal lobectomy, introduced clinically by Wilder Penfield during the 1950s, has served as a benchmark intervention for drug-resistant temporal epilepsy, historically demonstrating long-term seizure-freedom rates between 60 and 80 percent. In modern surgical practice, an anterior temporal lobectomy involves resection of the hippocampus, radical removal of the amygdala, and removal of surrounding temporal piriform circuitry. Research has increasingly identified the piriform cortex as an essential component of the epileptogenic network, showing that incomplete excision of this region correlates with less favorable seizure outcomes. Conversely, selective amygdalohippocampectomy was devised in 1958 by Niemeyer with the explicit objective of resecting mesial temporal structures while sparing the lateral neocortex. Delivered through transcortical or transsylvian corridors, the selective method sought to diminish cognitive and visual morbidities, sparking an ongoing neurosurgical debate over whether its functional preservation offsets a potential compromise in seizure eradication.
To rigorously evaluate the comparative performance of both procedures, researchers conducted an extensive literature search across PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library spanning records from September 2023 to August 2024. Eligible publications were restricted to direct comparative cohort designs—either prospective or retrospective—evaluating patients with mesial temporal lobe epilepsy undergoing either anterior temporal lobectomy or selective amygdalohippocampectomy. Studies were required to report documented surgical outcomes across seizure control, Engel III-IV outcomes, postoperative surgical complications, standardized neuropsychological assessments, or visual field deficits. Of an initial pool of 520 records, duplicate removal and rigorous screening led to the detailed full-text review of 92 articles. A total of 22 studies met all formal inclusion criteria, representing 20 completely independent cohorts comprising 2,482 patients, of whom 1,275 underwent anterior temporal lobectomy and 1,207 received selective amygdalohippocampectomy.
The primary analytical endpoint was seizure freedom at one year of postoperative follow-up, chosen intentionally to maintain time-point consistency across disparate studies and reduce attrition bias caused by uneven participant drop-out in longer monitoring windows. In this pooled analysis, patients undergoing selective amygdalohippocampectomy showed a lower probability of attaining Engel class I seizure freedom compared to those treated with anterior temporal lobectomy, yielding a risk ratio of 0.92 with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning 0.87 to 0.97 and a p-value of 0.05. Due to low heterogeneity among the included cohorts, reflected by a Q-test p-value of 0.09 and an I-squared value of 32 percent, the authors applied a fixed-effects model to synthesize the data. When evaluating institutional cohorts with overlapping publications, such as reports from Tanriverdi and colleagues, only the most complete dataset was incorporated to prevent artificial inflation of statistical weight and avoid double-counting participants.
In addition to primary seizure freedom, the meta-analysis investigated persistent postoperative seizure activity by assessing Engel class III and IV outcomes across 14 comparative studies at the one-year landmark. This secondary evaluation indicated that persistent, non-favorable seizure outcomes occurred more frequently among patients who underwent selective amygdalohippocampectomy than among those who received anterior temporal lobectomy. The pooled relative risk for Engel III-IV classification was 1.22 with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 1.00 to 1.50. Although this relative risk suggested a higher frequency of residual seizures following the selective procedure, the observed difference did not reach formal statistical significance, underscoring the nuance required when evaluating surgical failure versus partial improvement across observational groups.
While anterior temporal lobectomy showed superior rates of seizure freedom, functional and safety endpoints consistently favored selective amygdalohippocampectomy across multiple domains. An analysis of postoperative complications compiled from four studies demonstrated that adverse surgical events were significantly less frequent among patients managed with selective amygdalohippocampectomy compared with anterior temporal lobectomy, yielding a risk ratio of 0.62 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.47 to 0.83. The source authors noted that complication reporting in temporal lobe epilepsy surgery lacks universal standardization, resulting in descriptive variability among original studies, yet the aggregate findings demonstrated a lower complication burden when neocortical resection was avoided.
Neuropsychological outcomes focused predominantly on verbal memory changes assessed through the Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test across five comparative investigations. Rather than examining raw variations in continuous test scores, which varied widely in presentation across centers, investigators extracted the documented proportion of patients exhibiting post-intervention performance deterioration relative to their preoperative baseline. The synthesis revealed that individuals who received selective amygdalohippocampectomy had a significantly reduced risk of verbal memory worsening compared to those undergoing anterior temporal lobectomy, reflected by a risk ratio of 0.76 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.59 to 0.99. Sparing the lateral temporal neocortex, particularly within the language-dominant hemisphere, appears to provide measurable protection against verbal memory decline.
Visual outcomes similarly demonstrated a relative advantage for the selective surgical technique. To preserve methodological uniformity across studies, investigators restricted their visual meta-analysis to three studies that specifically utilized Goldmann kinetic perimetry to assess visual field deficits, excluding patients with preexisting visual deficits where necessary. Within this subgroup, the risk of acquiring postoperative visual field defects was significantly lower following selective amygdalohippocampectomy than after anterior temporal lobectomy, presenting a risk ratio of 0.85 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.73 to 0.99. The researchers cautioned, however, that because this analysis rested on only three cohorts, the finding should be interpreted as exploratory. They also highlighted earlier observations by Schmeiser and colleagues noting that only a minor subset of patients with perimetrically confirmed visual defects experience symptomatic impairment interfering with daily activities such as reading or driving.
Methodological quality assessments conducted using the ROBINS-I tool revealed that most included studies carried moderate to serious risks of bias, largely attributable to confounding and non-randomized patient selection. In clinical practice, surgical allocation was consistently guided by anatomical presentation rather than random assignment: patients exhibiting broader epileptogenic zones or extensive sclerosis typically underwent anterior temporal lobectomy, whereas individuals with well-circumscribed mesial foci were directed toward selective amygdalohippocampectomy. This baseline divergence creates inherent confounding, as broader pathological networks inherently influence surgical success independent of operative technique. Additionally, several surgical series reflected chronological shifts wherein anterior lobectomies were conducted earlier in a center’s experience and selective procedures were introduced later, introducing operator learning curve effects over time.
The authors also emphasized that incomplete operative reporting represents a critical confounder throughout the historical literature, particularly regarding the precise anatomical extent of amygdala and piriform cortex resection. Emerging anatomical investigations have identified radical removal of the piriform cortex as an important determinant of favorable seizure outcomes, yet historical surgical cohorts frequently failed to document whether piriform structures were systematically excised or preserved. Furthermore, the reliance on a one-year follow-up interval, while methodologically necessary to limit attrition bias and pool disparate reports, may not fully capture the long-term durability of seizure control or delayed cognitive and visual trajectories inherent to chronic mesial temporal lobe epilepsy.
Ultimately, the meta-analysis concludes that neither surgical technique is universally superior for every individual with mesial temporal lobe epilepsy. Anterior temporal lobectomy provides the highest documented probability of achieving complete seizure freedom, likely facilitated by broader resection of temporal networks and piriform cortex circuitry. Conversely, selective amygdalohippocampectomy serves as a valuable clinical alternative when neurocognitive preservation, functional safety, and protection of the visual pathway are prioritized. These findings provide an evidence-based framework to guide multidisciplinary counseling, reinforcing that optimal surgical choice relies on balancing individual functional demands against the anatomical extent of the epileptogenic zone while newer prospective studies and minimally invasive techniques continue to evolve.
