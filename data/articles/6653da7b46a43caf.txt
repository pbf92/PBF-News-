TITLE: Sky Media shifts addressable TV ads to the homes that respond, mid-campaign
OUTLET: PPC Land

On November 18, 2026, Sky Media introduced Outcomes Accelerator, a capability that lets advertisers plan addressable TV campaigns against business outcomes and adjust them while they are still on air, drawing on an effectiveness databank of 2,400 historic addressable campaigns from more than 500 advertisers. The advertising partnerships arm of Sky unveiled the product at its 2027 Sky Media Showcase at Ministry of Sound in London, alongside three new publisher partnerships with Platform Media, Goalhanger and 1071 Media that put YouTube and Sky VOD inventory from podcasts including The Rest Is Football and The Rest Is History into Sky Media's sales portfolio. Outcomes Accelerator is already in beta across live campaigns with selected advertisers, according to Sky Media, ahead of a full rollout in 2027.
In Short
Sky Media has a new tool, Outcomes Accelerator, that adjusts targeted TV ad campaigns while they run, steering them toward the households that respond best instead of judging results only once the campaign is over. It matters to brands and agencies buying UK television, because TV has mostly been planned on reach and judged after the fact, while search and social campaigns can be adjusted daily against results. The tool is bookable through Sky Media now, with a full rollout in 2027, and the same sales team will also sell ads on YouTube and Sky VOD shows from Goalhanger, Platform Media and 1071 Media, including The Rest Is Football and a new podcast from Ben Stokes and Mark Wood.
A performance pitch from a broadcaster
Television has long sold itself on scale and on the durable effect it has on brands. What it has rarely offered is the loop that performance marketers take for granted: spend goes out, results come back, and the next day's spend follows the results. Sky Media's commercial chief acknowledged the gap in plain terms.
"TV has a strong legacy of creating enduring connections between brands and engaged audiences - making it a powerful tool for advertisers. But it hasn't traditionally offered the accountability, addressability and in-flight optimisation associated with lower-funnel media. Outcomes Accelerator changes that, combining the power of TV with the performance capabilities advertisers increasingly expect," said Steven McHenry, Executive Director Commercial at Sky Media.
The announcement describes Outcomes Accelerator as extending Sky Media's role "further down the marketing funnel by enabling advertisers to plan and optimise addressable TV campaigns in-flight against business outcomes, rather than reach and impressions alone." Campaigns on linear TV are typically planned against audience targets and assessed after they finish, in effectiveness studies that arrive too late to change the media already bought. According to Sky Media, the capability is designed to give advertisers "a more responsive approach to TV performance, moving beyond traditional post-campaign effectiveness analysis and allowing campaigns to be optimised while they are live."
Addressable TV, in this context, means delivering different advertisements to different households watching the same programme, based on data about those homes. Sky has sold that kind of household-level targeting in the UK for years. What is new in the November 18 announcement is the attempt to close the feedback loop during the campaign itself, rather than reporting on it afterward.
How it works, according to Sky Media
According to Sky Media, Outcomes Accelerator runs in four stages.
- Booking. Outcomes Accelerator campaigns "are available to book through the Sky Media team." The announcement does not describe a self-service route or a programmatic route.
- Initial plan. Sky Media's effectiveness databank, "comprising 2,400 historic addressable campaigns from more than 500 advertisers, forms the predictive engine behind the initial media plan." In other words, the plan for a new campaign starts from the record of earlier addressable campaigns.
- Live feedback. Once the campaign is running, Outcomes Accelerator combines that plan with "a live feedback loop, using in-flight data on the characteristics of the most and least responsive households."
- In-flight optimization. The campaign is then optimized "towards homes driving results" while it is still live. Sky Media says this "creates a clearer connection between media investment and advertiser outcomes, while retaining the upper-funnel capabilities of TV."
On timing, the capability "is already in beta across live campaigns with selected advertisers, ahead of a full rollout in 2027," according to Sky Media.
The databank figures put a scale on Sky's history with the format. Spread across more than 500 advertisers, 2,400 campaigns works out to fewer than five campaigns per advertiser on average, by PPC Land's calculation. That is a broad base across brands rather than a deep record for any single advertiser.
The phrase "most and least responsive households" carries most of the technical weight. Ranking homes by response while a campaign is on air requires a household-level measure of response that arrives during the flight, not weeks after it. The announcement does not identify that measure, how it is matched to homes, or how often it feeds back into the plan.
Three publishers, two platforms
The second half of the announcement moves away from television sets and toward the creator-led video that now competes for the same screen. Sky Media will represent advertising inventory for three publishers, selling their YouTube inventory and, for two of them, Sky VOD inventory as well.
Ten programmes are named across Platform Media and Goalhanger, by PPC Land's count, plus the three Sky Sports formats from 1071 Media. Most are podcasts that also run as video podcasts on YouTube. That's Not Cricket is described as a brand new podcast from "former England teammates and current Durham stars Ben Stokes and Mark Wood." 1071 Media is described as a new media company co-founded by Sir Gareth Southgate, the former England manager.
The platform split is worth noting. Goalhanger's deal covers YouTube only, while Platform Media and 1071 Media also bring Sky VOD inventory, the on-demand service that sits in the category UK buyers call BVOD. None of the three deals mentions audio podcast inventory distributed through Apple Podcasts, Spotify or other audio apps.
Pippa Scaife, Director of Digital Advertising at Sky Media, framed the deals around audience behaviour rather than channels. "Audiences don't think in platforms. They follow the moments, content, and voices they love, wherever those are. We're delighted to be working with some of the biggest names in the industry with the most viewed and listened to shows. We're also thrilled to be able to make premium digital advertising simpler for advertisers, bringing the best of premium digital content into one place so they can be with their audience as they follow their passions across platforms."
A Showcase built for agencies
Sky Media held its 2027 Showcase in November 2026, ahead of the year the event is named for. According to Sky Media, the event brought together more than 350 media agency executives. Talent from upcoming programming appeared, including HBO's Harry Potter, which airs on HBO Max on Christmas Day; Baywatch, which airs on Sky early next year; and Sky's Saturday Night Live UK. Sir Gareth Southgate and Ben Stokes attended from sport, both of them also tied to the new digital partnerships.
Why this matters for advertisers
Television is being sold on outcomes
Outcomes Accelerator joins a crowded effort to make television answer to performance metrics. In October 2025, Teads launched CTV Performance in beta, a product linking TV exposure to site visits and sales through a household graph and a universal pixel. In April 2026, Moloco launched a Performance CTV product for app marketers, with attribution through mobile measurement partners. Those products come from ad tech companies working across streaming inventory. Sky Media's version comes from the broadcaster's own sales house, built on its own campaign history.
The buyer side remains cautious. In July 2026, a Jamloop survey of 120 senior brand and agency marketers found that just 33% fully trusted most platform-reported CTV performance claims, with 62% showing some scepticism. Jamloop sells outcome-based CTV advertising itself. The same question of independent verification applies to a broadcaster-built engine that draws on the broadcaster's own campaign data to optimize the broadcaster's own inventory. Whether Sky Media plans to support third-party measurement or incrementality testing with control groups is not addressed.
Sky's commercial year
The November 18 announcement lands in a year of rapid change at Sky. On October 6, Sky Media and Amazon Ads made Sky's streaming inventory in the UK and Ireland available through Amazon Ads, covering Sky Go, NOW, Sky Glass, Sky Q and Sky Stream, with reserved-price Programmatic Guaranteed deals live at launch and Private Marketplace deals to follow. Scaife spoke for Sky Media on that deal too.
The ownership picture is also moving. In June, Reuters reported that Sky had agreed terms to buy ITV's broadcast and streaming arm for £1.6 billion, and on July 6 the two companies confirmed that Sky would acquire ITV Media and Entertainment for up to £1.6 billion. On June 29, Comcast announced it would spin off NBCUniversal and Sky in a tax-free transaction expected to take about 12 months.
The commercial backdrop is not easy. ITV, the business Sky is buying, cut £30m of fourth-quarter spending as it guided TV advertising revenue down by 5% to 10% in a trading update on November 11, after a third-quarter fall of 8%. ITV said then that it expected the Sky deal to complete in the second half of 2027 - the same year Sky Media plans the full rollout of Outcomes Accelerator. Separately, Sky, Channel 4 and ITV announced plans for a joint self-service TV advertising marketplace in June 2025, built on Comcast's Universal Ads and FreeWheel. Sky Media's announcement mentions neither the ITV deal nor the marketplace.
Podcasts on the television screen
The publisher deals reflect where podcast viewing has gone. A Signal Hill Insights and FlightStory survey of 1,003 UK monthly podcast consumers, published in May 2026, found that smart TVs had overtaken computers as Britain's second podcast screen, used by 45% of respondents against 44% each for computers and tablets, with smart TVs accounting for just over 52% of UK podcast watch time. PPC Land's October 2025 coverage put YouTube's podcast audience at more than 1 billion monthly active viewers.
Audience growth has not translated neatly into advertising. In August 2026, MillionPodcasts found that 77% of 34,208 active English-language video podcasts carried no detectable advertising. Meanwhile, third-party sales houses have been taking on YouTube and video podcast inventory. In April 2026, SiriusXM Media became the exclusive US representative for YouTube's audio-first ad inventory, and on July 21 it took exclusive ad sales rights to both the audio and video versions of "The Bald and the Beautiful". Sky Media is now doing something similar in the UK from the television side: packaging podcast producers' YouTube inventory alongside its own on-demand service, so that a buyer can reach the same sports and entertainment audiences whether they watch through Sky or through YouTube.
What the announcement does not say
Sky Media does not define the business outcomes Outcomes Accelerator optimizes against, the data used to measure household response, or any measurement partner. It publishes no pricing, minimum spend or guarantee terms, no count of beta advertisers and no beta results. It gives no household footprint or addressable reach for the product, and does not say which Sky platforms or partner homes it covers.
