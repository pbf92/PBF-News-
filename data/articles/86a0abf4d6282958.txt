Wigan council adopts controversial new planning rules despite furious opposition
The planning changes were reluctantly backed by the controlling Labour group and angrily opposed by Reform UK and Independent councillors
Wigan council has adopted controversial new planning rules at a stormy town hall meeting. Since October 31, new government reforms on how planning applications are approved or denied have been in place.
To comply with the law, the council was required to change its scheme of delegation for planning matters, otherwise the planning committee would not be allowed to sit and any decisions made after October 31 would be unlawful.
Under the changes, more applications will be decided by planning officers rather than by a planning committee of elected members.

The Labour government has said this would make planning applications quicker, more efficient and reduce the number of decisions overturned on appeal. Larger and more contentious applications would still need approval from planning committees.
Opposition councillors argued that the changes should be rejected. Speaking at a full council meeting, Reform UK leader Coun Paul Watson, said: “We are not simply elected to rubber stamp the wishes of national government.
“We are elected to represent our residents, to defend their interests, to challenge decisions and to scrutinise. We are here to be their voice.
“We are being asked to approve constitutional changes which undermine that principle. How can we represent out communities when our voice has been taken away?”
Previously, if there were 10 or more public objections to an application, that would trigger a planning committee referral. That has now been removed. Ward councillor call-ins and planning committee member call-ins will also be removed with the new legislation.
Independent councillor Stuart Gerrard also opposed the changes. He said: “By voting this through we are taking away people’s voices.
“An unelected officer, along with the chair of planning will be able to usher through applications. It will be their decision alone. Let’s look at the track record of this council, Astley warehouses, the Galleries, we’ve got a list of blunders.”
Labour member Lol Hunt said: “This piece of legislation will hinder us, there’s no doubt about that. But the ramifications of not doing this have been set out. We’ll challenge the legislation.”
Deputy leader of the council, Keith Cunliffe, said ‘there is nobody in this chamber who doesn’t have serious concerns about some of the issues here’.
He added: “The reality is this came into force on October 31, it is law. If we do not apply the national scheme of delegation we will be acting unlawfully. If we don’t abide by the law we’re responsible for the consequences. We can’t vote for this council, a public body, to carry out unlawful acts.
“The consequence would be a planning department in paralysis, no planning committee can legally take place and no planning decision could be made as it would be unlawful.
“What they would be leading us into is this council making unlawful decisions which are subject to legal challenge which could cost us millions of pounds.

Reform’s Coun Rob Kenyon said: “This is about far more than planning procedures and changes to our constitution, it’s about democracy. It’s about whether the people of Wigan have a meaningful say in what happens in our communities.
“We’re told these changes will make planning decision quicker, more efficient and more consistent. When did efficiency become more important than democracy? Since when did the opinions of local residents become an inconvenience?”
Independent Coun Maureen O’Bern said: “Every councillor has a choice today, you either back the government or you back residents. One planning officer, unelected, could just ignore objections. I’d advise you all, vote against this or lose your seat.”
The vote to adopt the new scheme of delegation was passed by 36 votes to 31.
Later in the meeting, a motion submitted by Labour was passed which expressed ‘concern about aspects of the new national scheme of delegation for planning decisions and how they reflect the needs of communities across Wigan borough’.
The motion added: “While recognising the importance of an efficient planning system, the council does not fully agree with the new arrangements. However, as a public body, we must operate within the now current law, recognising that any unlawful decisions could expose residents to significant financial costs.
“We believe the scheme should better reflect local circumstances and strengthen the role of elected members in planning decisions.”
The motion compels the council to write to the government requesting that HMO planning applications where appropriate, would be referred to the planning committee and request an amendment that where the nominated elected member and nominated planning officer disagree on whether an application should be referred to the planning committee, the nominated elected member has the final say.
