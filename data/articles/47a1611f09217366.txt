TITLE: Bishop orders review of church response to priest's abuse case
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

An independent review into how the Diocese of Derry handled concerns about a priest who admitted over 100 child sex offences has been ordered.
Bishop Michael Router has commissioned a review which will examine how the diocese dealt with concerns about Father Edward Gallagher, including matters beyond those covered by the criminal proceedings.
The 59-year-old is due to be sentenced next month after admitting 109 offences involving children between 2023 and 2025. He is currently in custody.
The review will also look at allegations relating to Gallagher's time running a diocesan mission in Belize between 1998 and 2003.
A volunteer teacher who worked in the Central American country during that period told the Irish News that Gallagher had befriended young boys and offered them the opportunity to move to Ireland when they turned 18.
The Diocese of Derry said it had received "no formal complaint from any source alleging criminal conduct on the part of Fr Edward Gallagher during his time in Belize".
It had previously said it had "no knowledge, suspicions, concerns or allegations" involving minors connected to Gallagher before his arrest in April 2025.
However, the diocese confirmed it had received a concern relating to an adult on 18 March 2025, shortly before his arrest.
In a statement, it said: "The information now being raised publicly extends beyond the matters already dealt with through the criminal proceedings and the separate canonical process, which remains ongoing."
It added: "Bishop Michael Router has decided that an independent external review should be commissioned."
The bishop has also asked the Vatican to dismiss Gallagher from the priesthood. That process is ongoing.
The diocese says the review will be carried out by a "suitably qualified person entirely independent of the Diocese" and that it will provide all relevant records and cooperate fully.
It has encouraged anyone with information about safeguarding matters to contact the appropriate civil authorities.
"The priority of the diocese remains the safeguarding and welfare of children, young people and all those who may have been affected," it said.
