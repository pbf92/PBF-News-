Wall Street’s earnings mood is starting to improve. Is the stock market finding a bottom?
Exclusive investingLive research reveals early signs of recovery in post-earnings stock performance. Meanwhile, hedge funds have increased their short exposure, potentially setting the stage for a sharper rebound if market conditions stabilize.
November 26, 2026
Wall Street is showing signs that the selling pressure of recent weeks may be losing some of its effectiveness. Our latest earnings sentiment research, Wednesday's remarkable stock market recovery and the subsequent rally in European equities provide reasons for cautious optimism. But underneath the surface, plenty of stocks are still struggling.
investingLive Earnings Mood: 4.5 out of 10
Our proprietary earnings research shows an interesting discrepancy between how larger companies and the average stock are reacting to quarterly results.
Wednesday's average stock reaction, giving every company equal importance regardless of its size, was actually negative 4%
Deere gained approximately 4.4%, while HP jumped 11.5%. Excluding just these two companies would turn Wednesday's capitalization-weighted earnings reaction from positive 1.73% to negative 0.50%.
That is a fairly concentrated recovery.
Dell stock offers an encouraging clue
Dell Technologies (DELL) is one of the more interesting stocks to examine following Wednesday's trading session.
After reporting earnings on Tuesday evening, Dell fell more than 10% from its pre-earnings closing price before recovering the entire decline.
The initial selling was effectively rejected. For investors looking to identify a potential change in market sentiment, these failed selloffs can be particularly informative.
Dick's Sporting Goods and Abercrombie & Fitch also extended their initial earnings gains, bringing their cumulative advances to approximately 18.6% and 26%, respectively.
There are less encouraging examples, of course. Nutanix, Ambarella and Urban Outfitters suffered double-digit earnings declines.
Nasdaq and S&P 500 futures are showing early signs of stabilization
Wednesday's selloff presented bears with an opportunity to establish fresh lows below those recorded last Friday. Neither of the two major equity futures markets did so.
Both markets have recovered approximately 5% from their November 20 lows.
For traders, two upside references are particularly interesting:
- Nasdaq futures: 27,137, Monday's high.
- S&P 500 futures: 7,313.25, Monday's high.
Reclaiming and sustaining prices above these levels would strengthen the recovery case. Conversely, breaking last Friday's lows would undermine it considerably.
European markets are also participating in the relief rally, with the Euro Stoxx gaining 1.8% and Germany's DAX rising 1.6% at Thursday's opening, as Justin Low reported in his European stock market update.
Hedge funds are positioned for trouble. Could that help the bulls?
According to Goldman Sachs, hedge fund clients reduced their exposure for five consecutive weeks before aggressively increasing their short positions.
As Eamonn Sheridan reported, ETF short exposure recorded its third-largest weekly increase in a decade.
Such positioning creates an interesting possibility. If stocks continue stabilizing, some short sellers may begin buying back shares to close their positions, while other fund managers may gradually increase their market exposure.
That could accelerate a recovery. It does not necessarily require a major improvement in corporate fundamentals to get started, although sustained gains would need broader support.
Nvidia remains central to the recovery
Earlier today, our Nvidia stock analysis identified several encouraging technical developments, including its rebound from $141.17 and a break above its descending price channel.
The fundamental picture is more complicated.
S&P has placed Nvidia’s AA credit rating on negative watch, while Moody’s has maintained its Aa1 rating and stable outlook. Both have raised concerns surrounding governance and dependence on Jensen Huang.
Nvidia's ability to sustain its recovery despite these lingering concerns could provide another indication of whether investors are becoming more comfortable taking risk again.
What stock market investors should watch next
Three developments would make the bullish case more convincing:
- More companies attracting buyers after earnings, rather than a handful of influential stocks supporting the averages.
- Recent earnings winners retaining their gains and more initial selloffs reversing, as Dell's did.
- Nasdaq and S&P 500 futures establishing higher highs and higher lows.
With US stock exchanges closed for Thanksgiving and reopening for a shortened Friday session, next week should provide a better test of conviction.
There is enough evidence to start questioning the bearish narrative. There is not yet enough to declare that the correction is over.
