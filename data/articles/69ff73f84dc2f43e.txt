TITLE: Are multiple short walks better than one long walk for controlling blood sugar?
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

A randomized trial published in Obesity indicates that interrupting prolonged sitting with frequent brisk walks can improve blood glucose regulation to a greater extent than the same amount of walking performed as a single continuous bout.
In the trial, 30 adults with overweight or obesity who were sedentary (self-reporting >6 hours/day of sitting) were randomized to 45 minutes/day of brisk walking, 5 days/week for 6 weeks, performed either as short bouts throughout the day or as a single bout.
Compared with the continuous walking group, the short bouts group experienced greater improvements in glucose regulation during an intravenous glucose tolerance test, as well as increased time spent within the target glucose range and reduced glycemic variability during daily living, as measured by continuous glucose monitors.
“Our findings suggest that how we distribute physical activity throughout the day may be just as important as how much we do,” said corresponding author Audrey Bergouignan, PhD, Director of Research at the French National Centre for Scientific Research (CNRS) and Associate Professor at the University of Colorado Anschutz Medical Campus. “Rather than relying solely on a single exercise session, regularly interrupting prolonged sitting with short walks may offer additional benefits for blood glucose regulation, especially in people at risk of developing type 2 diabetes.”
