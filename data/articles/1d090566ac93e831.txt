TITLE: Network Meta-Analysis Identifies Ongericimab as Most Potent Novel PCSK9 Inhibitor
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive network meta-analysis evaluating four next-generation proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 monoclonal antibodies has found that ongericimab administered at a dose of 150 milligrams every two weeks achieved the most substantial reductions in low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, apolipoprotein B, and lipoprotein(a) among Chinese patients with hypercholesterolemia. While traditional first-line statin therapy remains the clinical foundation for lowering circulating atherogenic lipoproteins, therapeutic gaps persist for patients who experience adverse effects, statin intolerance, or insufficient response at high-intensity doses.
The clinical landscape of lipid-lowering pharmacotherapy has expanded substantially in East Asia following regulatory decisions by China’s National Medical Products Administration, which granted marketing approvals to four novel monoclonal antibodies over an eighteen-month period: tafolecimab in August 2023, ebronucimab in September 2024, ongericimab in October 2024, and recaticimab in January 2025. Although global clinical guidelines issued by the American College of Cardiology, the American Heart Association, the European Society of Cardiology, and the European Atherosclerosis Society have long recognized the established antibodies alirocumab and evolocumab as proven interventions for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk management, the comparative clinical performance and relative tolerability of these four newer agents had remained largely undefined. Because manufacturers evaluated each novel molecule against placebo rather than against active competitor compounds, the research team designed a systematic review following Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses standards and registered the study protocol in the PROSPERO international prospective register under registration number CRD42025646055.
To assemble the evidence base, investigators conducted systematic electronic searches across the Cochrane Library, PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and ClinicalTrials.gov through February 1, 2025, without language restrictions, identifying 123 initial publications. Following the elimination of 73 duplicate citations and the screening of 21 candidate reports, eight distinct phase 3 randomized controlled trials encompassing 3,975 individuals were selected for quantitative synthesis. The trials included CREDIT-1, CREDIT-2, and CREDIT-4 for tafolecimab; a multicenter phase 3 trial for ebronucimab; two dedicated trials for ongericimab; and REMAIN-1 and REMAIN-2 for recaticimab. The pooled study population consisted entirely of Chinese adult patients with hypercholesterolemia, primary mixed dyslipidemia, or heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia receiving stable baseline lipid-lowering background therapy. Mean participant ages across the individual trial cohorts ranged from 44.45 years to 59.76 years, and baseline low-density lipoprotein cholesterol concentrations varied between 2.70 millimoles per liter and 4.25 millimoles per liter, with female representation ranging between 31 percent and 48.24 percent across the included cohorts.
Methodologically, the review employed a frequentist network meta-analysis framework executed with the netmeta package in R version 4.4.1 to simultaneously integrate direct within-trial comparisons and indirect across-trial evidence across ten distinct dosing schedules. The assessed regimens comprised tafolecimab at 150 milligrams every two weeks, 450 milligrams every four weeks, and 600 milligrams every six weeks; ebronucimab at 150 milligrams every two weeks and 450 milligrams every four weeks; ongericimab at 150 milligrams every two weeks and 300 milligrams every four weeks; and recaticimab at 150 milligrams every four weeks, 300 milligrams every eight weeks, and 450 milligrams every twelve weeks, each benchmarked against placebo control arms. Continuous lipid alterations were measured as mean percentage differences from baseline alongside 95 percent confidence intervals, while binary safety end points were quantified as risk ratios. Inconsistency between direct and indirect evidence was formally tested using both design-by-treatment interaction models for global coherence and node-splitting models for local loops, with random-effects models accounting for between-study variance.
In the primary analysis of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol reduction, ongericimab administered at 150 milligrams every two weeks demonstrated the greatest magnitude of lipid clearance, producing a statistically significant mean reduction of 74.21 percent relative to placebo, with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning from negative 79.69 percent to negative 68.73 percent and an adjusted p-value of less than 0.001. In comparative network estimates against other active regimens, this biweekly ongericimab schedule demonstrated statistically superior low-density lipoprotein cholesterol reductions compared to nearly all competing active dosing schedules, although differences did not reach statistical significance when compared directly to tafolecimab at 150 milligrams every two weeks, which yielded a mean difference of negative 15.79 percent with an adjusted p-value of 0.076, or tafolecimab at 450 milligrams every four weeks, which yielded a mean difference of negative 10.43 percent with an adjusted p-value of 0.182. Tafolecimab 450 milligrams every four weeks secured the second-highest therapeutic ranking for low-density lipoprotein cholesterol lowering, exhibiting a statistically significant advantage over the longest-interval regimen, recaticimab 450 milligrams every twelve weeks, with a mean difference of negative 15.68 percent and an adjusted p-value of 0.001.
For apolipoprotein B clearance, ongericimab 150 milligrams every two weeks achieved a mean reduction of 64.36 percent versus placebo, with a 95 percent confidence interval from negative 68.58 percent to negative 60.13 percent, significantly outperforming ongericimab 300 milligrams every four weeks as well as all evaluated dosing schedules of recaticimab. Tafolecimab 450 milligrams every four weeks similarly maintained statistically significant superiority in apolipoprotein B reduction over all recaticimab dosing groups. For lipoprotein(a) lowering, ongericimab at 150 milligrams every two weeks produced a mean difference of negative 50.93 percent relative to placebo, with a 95 percent confidence interval from negative 56.24 percent to negative 45.61 percent, showing statistically superior clearance relative to all recaticimab regimens. Across all three lipid parameters, recaticimab 450 milligrams every twelve weeks ranked lowest among active dosing regimens, with reductions versus placebo of 48.10 percent for low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, 41.14 percent for apolipoprotein B, and 22.79 percent for lipoprotein(a), while still remaining markedly superior to placebo controls.
Safety outcomes were synthesized from seven trials reporting incident treatment-emergent adverse events and serious treatment-emergent adverse events, with one ongericimab trial omitted from the quantitative safety network because its safety data were not stratified by specific dosage groups. The analysis revealed comparable incidence rates of adverse occurrences across all active therapeutic regimens and placebo arms, with adjusted p-values exceeding 0.999. No evidence was detected of dose-dependent escalation in safety signals, and none of the active interventions produced a statistically significant increase in the risk of serious treatment-emergent adverse events compared with control cohorts. Cumulative P-score rankings identified tafolecimab 450 milligrams every four weeks as possessing the most favorable overall safety score among the active treatments. In synthesizing efficacy and tolerability, the investigators observed that while biweekly ongericimab offers maximal numerical lipid suppression, the once-monthly administration of tafolecimab 450 milligrams presents a highly balanced risk-benefit profile for clinical practice, reducing annual injection frequency while preserving potent lipid-modifying capacity.
Methodological quality assessments conducted with the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool indicated that deviations from intended interventions and outcome selection bias represented the most common methodological vulnerabilities among the included studies, resulting in an overall judgment of some concerns across the trial base. Substantial statistical heterogeneity was observed in both the low-density lipoprotein cholesterol analysis, with an I-squared statistic of 56.7 percent, and the treatment-emergent adverse events analysis, with an I-squared of 54.4 percent. Sensitivity analyses demonstrated that the observed heterogeneity in adverse event reporting was driven primarily by the CREDIT-4 trial; excluding that single outlier resolved the residual heterogeneity entirely, yielding an I-squared of zero percent without destabilizing pooled efficacy conclusions.
To evaluate whether cumulative trial data were statistically sufficient to withstand random error, the researchers applied trial sequential analysis to both key efficacy and safety end points. Conversely, trial sequential analysis for treatment-emergent adverse events revealed that existing safety data remain severely underpowered: the calculated diversity-adjusted required information size for the biweekly ongericimab comparison was 20,771 participants, of which only 1.23 percent has been accumulated across available trials, while the required sample size accrued for monthly tafolecimab stood at just 2.02 percent. Because the cumulative Z-curves failed to cross formal monitoring boundaries, the authors cautioned that definitive conclusions regarding rare or long-term adverse events cannot yet be established.
The authors highlighted several critical constraints that temper the immediate generalizability of these findings to broader clinical contexts. Because all eight included trials enrolled exclusively Chinese patients, external validity to other ancestral populations remains unverified, requiring dedicated multinational trials to assess potential pharmacogenomic and pharmacokinetic variations. Furthermore, because fewer than ten trials met inclusion criteria, formal statistical testing for publication bias through funnel plots or Egger’s regression was precluded, and assessments using the Confidence in Network Meta-Analysis framework rated evidence certainty for many safety comparisons as very low due to wide confidence intervals. Finally, while older established agents such as evolocumab and alirocumab have definitively demonstrated hard clinical reductions in major adverse cardiovascular events across multiethnic populations, no dedicated cardiovascular outcome trials or head-to-head randomized studies have yet documented long-term cardiovascular morbidity or mortality reductions for these four newer therapies, underscoring the necessity of ongoing surveillance and expanded trial programs.
