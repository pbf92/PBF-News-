TITLE: Pochettino suffers 4-0 defeat in Manchester United debut as fans taunt him
OUTLET: Chosunbiz

Mauricio Pochettino's time at Manchester United turned into a nightmare from the start. Just 14 minutes into his first match in charge, opposing fans were already chanting for him to be fired.
The British newspaper The Sun reported on the 23rd, Korea time: "Liverpool fans cruelly taunted Pochettino after his nightmarish start at United," adding, "The Anfield crowd wasted no time mocking Pochettino with a song: 'You'll be sacked in the morning.'"
United suffered a 4-0 loss to Liverpool in the "Northwest derby" in the 11th round of the 2026-27 Premier League season at Anfield in Liverpool, England, that same day. The defeat extended United's winless run in the league to nine matches. The team also fell to 19th place, in the relegation zone, with one win, five draws, five losses and eight points.
The match turned in just 13 minutes. United center back Leny Yoro was sent off in the eighth minute after bringing down Alexander Isak from behind. The referee ruled that he had denied a clear scoring opportunity.
Dominik Szoboszlai scored a brilliant opener from the ensuing free kick. United's nightmare did not end there. In the 13th minute, Florian Wirtz finished a cross from Rio Ngumoha to make it 2-0.
Less than 15 minutes into his United managerial debut, Pochettino had conceded two goals and lost a player to a red card. Taunts then poured down from the Anfield stands. Liverpool fans began singing, "You'll be sacked in the morning," in the 14th minute.
Liverpool fans kept taunting United. The Independent reported that, after singing, "You're going down," fans also chanted, "Poch's at the wheel." The chant mocked one United fans had previously used to support former manager Ole Gunnar Solskjaer.
It was the worst possible start for Pochettino. He took charge at United as the successor to Michael Carrick, who was fired last week. United had put Carrick in charge after parting ways with Ruben Amorim in January, but he did not last long either, and the club changed managers again.
Pochettino had promised change, raising the intensity of training as soon as he arrived, but United's longstanding problems were on full display from the first match. Pochettino ended his debut with a 4-0 defeat, matching the heaviest loss by a United manager in his first game. It was the club's first such humiliation in exactly 100 years, since manager Lal Hilditch lost 4-0 to Bolton in his first match in 1926.
According to British broadcaster Sky Sports, Pochettino also became the first manager in Premier League history to concede two goals and have a player sent off in the first half of his opening match in charge. United's appearance in the bottom two places after at least 10 league games was its first in 40 years, since 1986.
The Independent wrote: "Pochettino suffered a heavy defeat to a rival in his first game as United manager. In just 13 minutes, he received a brutal crash course in the situation he was facing. Welcome to Manchester United, Mauricio. For someone who had long yearned to manage United, this was a humiliating start to his debut."
Pochettino also expressed his anguish. "It's so painful. I'm so disappointed. It's hard to accept starting a Premier League match in this situation," he said after the game. He also protested the decision, saying, "Yoro's sending-off was unfair. Anyone who has played football can see that it wasn't a sending-off."
United legends were also unable to hide their anger. Roy Keane, in particular, told Sky Sports: "United were desperate, and the performance was awful. Are we saying 4-0 isn't so bad because they played with 10 men? Has no team ever gotten a result with 10 men? It's entirely possible. We blame the club's leadership, the owners and the manager. When will the players take responsibility for their decisions and performances?"
Keane's anger did not stop there. "Liverpool probably should have scored eight or nine goals. They created an incredible number of chances," he said. "And yet everyone says 4-0 isn't so bad. It's enough to make you swear."
Gary Neville was also stunned by United's situation. "We're now trying to accept a 4-0 defeat at Anfield as if it were an expected result. The club's standards have fallen to an unbelievable level," he said.
Neville added: "But the fans' standards haven't fallen. They expect United to be competitive every week. United now has the worst defense in all of Europe and is second from bottom in the Premier League. It's truly unbelievable," he lamented. <
