Routine Invasive versus Conservative Management Shows Similar Long-Term Survival in High-Risk Chronic Coronary Disease: JAMA
USA: A recent study, published in JAMA, has found no significant difference in long-term all-cause mortality between routine invasive management and an initial conservative treatment strategy in patients with high-risk chronic coronary artery disease. These findings support shared decision-making between patients and clinicians, considering individual risks, preferences, and clinical circumstances.
- Patient characteristics: The median age was 64 years, 23% were women, 42% had diabetes, and the median left ventricular ejection fraction was 60%.
- All-cause mortality: At 11 years, mortality was 25.8% in the invasive group and 26.9% in the conservative group. The difference was not statistically significant (adjusted hazard ratio [AHR] 0.97).
- Cardiovascular mortality: Deaths from cardiovascular causes occurred in 15.8% of patients receiving invasive management and 17.1% receiving conservative treatment (AHR 0.88).
- Noncardiovascular mortality: Corresponding rates were 9.1% and 8.1%, respectively, without a statistically significant difference.
- Survival duration: Restricted mean survival differed by an estimated 14 days between strategies, with the confidence interval including no difference.
