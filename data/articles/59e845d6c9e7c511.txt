TITLE: Anthropic IPO: ANT Stock Opens at $111.50 After Record $81.3 Billion Offering
OUTLET: Eurasia Business News

By William Collins, consultant in stock markets – Eurasia Business News, November 19, 2026. Article no 3277
Anthropic shares opened at $111.50 on Thursday, November 19, 2026, surging approximately 34% above their $83 offering price after the largest initial public offering on record. The Nasdaq debut gave investors a new way to buy directly into the booming market for artificial intelligence.
Trading under the ticker ANT, the company behind Claude briefly commanded a fully diluted valuation of approximately $2 trillion. Shares subsequently retreated toward $100, however, illustrating the volatility surrounding a listing widely viewed as a major test of investor confidence in AI. Those figures are intraday observations, not closing prices.
Anthropic, the developer of Claude, filed for an initial public offering on October 26.
Anthropic Stock Price Jumps Above IPO Expectations
Anthropic’s opening trade represented a gain of about 34.3% from the IPO price. The company had already exceeded expectations Wednesday by pricing shares at $83, above its previously indicated range of $70 to $78.
Demand also supported a larger transaction. The offering increased to 980 million shares from an initially planned 825 million, bringing its total value to approximately $81.34 billion before fees and expenses.
That combination—more shares sold at a higher price—demonstrated substantial demand before public trading began. Yet the subsequent retreat toward $100 showed that enthusiasm did not eliminate price sensitivity. At that level, ANT remained approximately 20% above its offering price but below its opening trade.
Largest IPO Ever Surpasses SpaceX’s Record
Anthropic’s approximately $81.3 billion transaction surpassed SpaceX’s earlier listing to become the largest IPO in history. The record concerns the value of shares sold in the offering, not the company’s overall stock-market valuation.
According to the supplied transaction breakdown, Anthropic sold 956.35 million shares, while existing shareholders sold another 23.65 million. That distinction matters: proceeds from newly issued shares go to the company, whereas proceeds from secondary shares go to selling shareholders.
Consequently, describing the entire transaction value as cash available for Anthropic’s operations would overstate the company’s gross proceeds. Underwriting discounts and other offering expenses would further separate headline transaction size from net funding.
Why the $2 Trillion Valuation Needs Context
Anthropic’s debut generated different valuation figures because reports used different share counts. At the $111.50 opening price, its fully diluted value reached approximately $2 trillion, including stock options and unvested stock awards.
Counting only shares already issued produced a lower market capitalization, closer to $1.5 trillion. Investors comparing Anthropic with other technology companies therefore need to use consistent definitions rather than treating every published valuation as directly interchangeable.
The distinction does not diminish the scale of the debut. It clarifies what investors were pricing: both current ownership and potential additional shares associated with employee compensation and other equity arrangements.
Wall Street Analysts Set Bullish ANT Price Targets
Several research firms began coverage with positive recommendations. Needham assigned a Buy rating and a $150 price target, while Citizens initiated coverage with a $125 target. Melius also recommended buying the stock, setting its target at $155.
These targets stood above the opening price, but they remain analyst judgments rather than guaranteed outcomes. They depend on assumptions about revenue expansion, competition, operating costs and the durability of demand for AI products.
Needham estimated that generative AI’s addressable market could reach $5.6 trillion as businesses adopt tools designed to improve productivity and automate processes. That estimate describes a potential market opportunity, not revenue already secured by Anthropic.
Claude and Enterprise AI Underpin the Growth Story
Founded in January 2021 by Dario Amodei and six other former OpenAI employees, Anthropic built its business around the Claude family of AI models. The supplied company history identifies enterprise customers and public administrations as important early priorities.
Claude Code, launched in 2025, expanded that strategy into software development. Its significance for investors lies in whether coding and other workplace applications can produce sustained customer spending rather than short-lived experimentation.

Amazon and Google are major Anthropic investors, according to the supplied account. Their involvement adds another consideration: shareholders must assess both the advantages of powerful technology partners and the commercial dependencies accompanying those relationships.
A Strong Debut Does Not Remove AI Risks
The offering arrived during a difficult period for technology shares. Against that backdrop, Anthropic’s successful pricing provided evidence that investors still had appetite for a major AI listing, even as they scrutinized valuations.
However, public-market demand and business performance are different tests. Anthropic must convert enthusiasm into recurring revenue while funding model development, computing infrastructure and expansion. Analyst forecasts of rapid growth cannot substitute for reported results.
The supplied account also describes tensions with the U.S. government over military uses of its models. Alongside cybersecurity concerns and disagreements about safeguards, those issues highlight how policy decisions can affect customer access, reputation and commercial opportunities.
What Investors Should Watch After the IPO
The next phase will turn attention from opening-day demand to execution. Revenue growth, customer retention, computing costs, cash consumption and the terms of major partnerships will help investors evaluate whether Anthropic can justify its valuation.
For now, the record offering establishes strong demand for ANT shares. Maintaining that confidence will require results that support the expectations embedded in its extraordinary debut.
