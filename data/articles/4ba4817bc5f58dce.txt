TITLE: OpenAI says a spy handed Apple secret plans for AI devices that could 'outcompete' the iPhone maker on its 'own turf'
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

OpenAI sued Apple (NASDAQ:AAPL) on Friday, accusing the Big Tech giant of stealing its trade secrets and discouraging hardware suppliers from working on future OpenAI devices, after Apple sued OpenAI for allegedly poaching its employees to gain access to its technology.
The ChatGPT maker filed counterclaims in California federal court on Friday, saying that Apple's employment practices were designed to "trap" employees and prevent them from working on competitors' technology.
OpenAI is developing AI-enabled personal electronic devices that threaten to "outcompete Apple on what Apple views as its own turf," the company said in its Friday court filing.

OpenAI calls 'timing' of re-hire suspicious
OpenAI said in the filing that Apple gained confidential information by re-hiring a former employee who worked at OpenAI for six months. In that time, the employee gained access to specific information on a new device as well as OpenAI's roadmap for future hardware products.
"Given the timing, it now appears that stealing those documents may have been his reason for joining OpenAI in the first place," OpenAI said.
Apple and OpenAI did not immediately respond to Reuters' requests for comment.

The struggle for talent and tech
Tensions between the two tech companies have escalated as the intensifying race to develop AI products fuels competition for talent and proprietary technology.
Earlier this year, Apple sued OpenAI and former Apple employees Tang Tan and Chang Liu in July, alleging misappropriation of its trade secrets to benefit the ChatGPT owner's foray into consumer hardware. Tan, a 24-year Apple employee who was a vice president of product design for iPhone and Apple Watch, was part of the group that launched io Products before it was acquired by OpenAI in 2025.
OpenAI and the employees have denied those allegations, and said that Apple's lawsuit depended on its unauthorized monitoring of Liu's personal messages after he stopped working for Apple. Attorneys for Tan and Liu did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
