TITLE: S&P 500 & Nasdaq Live: US Stocks Drop After Monday’s 4.7% and 6.5% Rally
OUTLET: Coinpaper

Key takeaways
- US stock futures retreat following Monday's historic rally
- Nvidia and other AI stocks face renewed selling
- Investors await Dell earnings and Wednesday's inflation report
US stock futures fell on Tuesday, November 24, after the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite recorded their strongest daily gains since April 2025. Technology and artificial intelligence stocks came under renewed pressure as investors assessed corporate developments and upcoming inflation data.
The S&P 500 closed Monday at 7,237.17, gaining 4.73%, while the Nasdaq Composite surged 6.51% to 24,089.56. The Dow Jones Industrial Average advanced 3.01% to 49,595.31.
However, Tuesday's premarket trading saw S&P 500 futures decline approximately 1%, while Nasdaq-100 futures dropped nearly 2%, threatening to reverse part of Monday's gains.
Nvidia and AI Stocks Face Renewed Selling Pressure
Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) fell approximately 5% in premarket trading after surging 31.48% on Monday following Jensen Huang's reinstatement as chief executive.
The decline comes as investors assess unresolved corporate governance concerns alongside the broader financial risks associated with artificial intelligence infrastructure spending.
Other semiconductor companies, including AMD, Micron and Marvell, recorded premarket losses ranging from approximately 4% to 6%. AI infrastructure providers CoreWeave and Nebius also declined around 6%.
Meanwhile, Anthropic stock briefly fell below its $83 IPO price, extending its disappointing performance following last week's record-breaking market debut.
The renewed weakness follows last week's AI stock selloff, which pushed the S&P 500 into correction territory and left investors questioning elevated technology valuations.
Trump Tariffs and Inflation Return to Focus
Beyond corporate developments, Wall Street faces renewed uncertainty surrounding US trade and monetary policy.
The Trump administration is reportedly preparing additional tariffs targeting China, the European Union and other major trading partners, potentially restoring import duties closer to levels seen in 2025.
The prospective measures arrive as the US dollar reaches a 20-month high, while elevated Treasury yields continue to constrain equity valuations.
The next major economic catalyst arrives Wednesday. According to the Bureau of Economic Analysis, October's Personal Consumption Expenditures inflation report will be released at 8:30 a.m. ET.
Stronger-than-expected inflation could reinforce expectations of further Federal Reserve tightening, potentially increasing financing costs for technology companies.
Dell Earnings Could Provide Another Test for AI Stocks
Dell Technologies is scheduled to report its third-quarter fiscal 2027 results after Tuesday's closing bell. Investors will be watching its AI server revenue, profit margins and order backlog for indications of sustained infrastructure demand.
The report follows Monday's sharp recovery in AI infrastructure stocks, which saw investors return to several previously battered companies.
With US markets closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and operating a shortened session on Friday, investors have limited time to respond to this week's earnings and economic releases.
Monday's historic market rebound demonstrated that buyers remain willing to return to beaten-down technology stocks. Whether that recovery can continue will depend partly on corporate earnings and whether inflation data reinforces expectations of higher interest rates.
