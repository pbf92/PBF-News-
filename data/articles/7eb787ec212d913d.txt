TITLE: Cochrane International Mobility - Rawan Hamamreh
OUTLET: Cochrane Collaboration

Getting involved in Cochrane’s work means becoming part of a global community.
The Cochrane International Mobility programme connects successful applicants with a placement in a host Cochrane Group, learning more about the production, use, and knowledge translation of Cochrane reviews. The programme offers opportunities for learning and training not only for participants but also for host staff.
In this series, we profile those that have participated in the Cochrane International Mobility programme and learn more about their experiences.
Name: Rawan Hamamreh, MD
Country of origin: Jordan
Current location: Chicago, Illinois, USA
Cochrane International Mobility location: Cochrane Sweden
Tell us a little about yourself and how your journey with research began.
My name is Rawan Hamamreh, and I am a medical doctor from Jordan, currently living in Chicago, USA.
I have always been the kind of person who wants to do more than what is expected. During medical school at The Hashemite University, I became interested in research because I loved the idea of contributing to something meaningful.
Like many medical students, I started with more questions than answers. Research seemed like a huge world, and honestly, I didn't know where to begin. But I knew I wanted to be part of it. Looking back, one of the best decisions I made was to stop waiting until I felt completely ready and simply start learning.
How did you become involved with Cochrane Sweden?
My journey with Cochrane Sweden began after attending a live educational session about Cochrane. Inspired by the opportunities it offered, I reached out to Dr. Matteo Bruschettini, to whom I remain incredibly grateful for his guidance and support.
That simple step led to an opportunity to collaborate on the Cochrane review “Acupuncture for procedural pain in newborn infants.”
I was still living in Jordan, yet I was contributing to an international research project alongside experienced researchers from different countries.
I contributed to protocol development, literature searching, study screening, data extraction, risk-of-bias assessment, and meta-analysis using RevMan. I was also introduced to research tools I had never used before. With guidance and practice, I gradually became confident using them, and today I use these skills in my own research and teaching.
What did you learn from this experience?
One of my greatest lessons was that distance should never be a barrier to meaningful scientific collaboration. A great team, a clear plan, and genuine hard work can make an incredible difference.
Research can be demanding, especially when dealing with extensive literature and detailed assessments, but supportive and knowledgeable colleagues make the process much smoother.
How did Cochrane influence your journey afterward?
Cochrane had a lasting influence on both my research journey and my passion for teaching.
I continued working on systematic reviews and meta-analyses, applying the skills I had developed and becoming more independent as a researcher.
Eventually, I founded 911 Research Academy, my own virtual educational initiative, where I teach medical students and aspiring researchers across the Middle East how to conduct scientific research, from developing research questions to performing meta-analyses and writing manuscripts.
What makes me particularly proud is that I can now teach others the same research methods and tools I once learned through Cochrane.
I started as someone eager to learn, and eventually became someone who could help others learn, too.
What advice would you give to students interested in Cochrane International Mobility?
Never miss an opportunity to learn, participate, or challenge yourself. Do not wait until you feel completely ready.
Ask questions, welcome feedback, and make the most of every opportunity, even if it seems small at first. You never know how much it might shape your future.
If you could describe your Cochrane experience in one sentence, what would it be?
Cochrane helped me grow into the researcher I had always aspired to become, and I truly believe I would not be where I am today without this experience.
