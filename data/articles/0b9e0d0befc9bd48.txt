TITLE: Systematic Review Finds Tocilizumab Shows High Response in Refractory Neuro-Behçet’s Disease
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic literature review published in Rheumatology and Therapy indicates that targeted interleukin-6 receptor blockade using tocilizumab achieved a 91.7 percent response rate among evaluable patients suffering from neuro-Behçet’s disease that had proven refractory to conventional immunosuppressive treatments and tumor necrosis factor inhibitors. Neuro-Behçet’s disease represents an uncommon but debilitating manifestation of Behçet’s disease, a systemic inflammatory disorder primarily defined by recurrent oral aphthae, genital ulcerations, uveitis, and cutaneous or articular lesions. When Behçet’s disease involves the central nervous system, it can cause irreversible neurological damage and significant physical impairment. The clinical presentation is broadly classified into parenchymal disease, which manifests as abnormal magnetic resonance imaging lesions within the brainstem, spinal cord, and cerebral hemispheres, and non-parenchymal disease, which is characterized by cerebrovascular abnormalities including intracranial aneurysms and cerebral venous thrombosis. While acute episodes often respond to standard anti-inflammatory regimens, chronic progressive forms typically feature insidious motor, cognitive, and neuropsychiatric decline that resists established therapeutic options and carries an unfavorable long-term prognosis.
Standard medical management for neuro-Behçet’s disease historically relies on high-dose glucocorticoids, traditional synthetic immunosuppressive agents such as azathioprine and methotrexate, and biological therapies targeting tumor necrosis factor alpha, such as infliximab. Despite aggressive regimens, a notable subset of individuals experiences persistent or relapsing neurological dysfunction, creating a critical therapeutic dilemma. Experimental and clinical studies have established that interleukin-6 levels in the cerebrospinal fluid become substantially elevated during active neuro-Behçet’s disease episodes, particularly among individuals undergoing ongoing inflammatory destruction within the central nervous system. Interleukin-6 serves as a key driver of blood-brain barrier breakdown and local inflammatory amplification, while also functioning as a reliable surrogate biomarker for disease activity and therapeutic response. Tocilizumab, a humanized monoclonal antibody directed against the interleukin-6 receptor that is widely approved for conditions like rheumatoid arthritis, has consequently emerged as an off-label candidate for refractory neuro-Behçet’s disease. However, prior evidence supporting its deployment remained confined to scattered individual case reports and small uncontrolled series without systematic qualitative and quantitative synthesis.
To rigorously evaluate the therapeutic efficacy and safety profile of interleukin-6 inhibition in this difficult setting, investigators conducted a systematic literature review in strict accordance with Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. The research protocol was prospectively registered in the University Hospital Medical Information Network Center Clinical Trial Registry under identifier UMIN000058445. Researchers comprehensively queried the PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science Core Collection databases up to July 12, 2025. Two independent reviewers screened titles and abstracts using Rayyan software to identify studies of any design reporting clinical efficacy or safety outcomes of interleukin-6 blockade in clinically diagnosed neuro-Behçet’s disease. Out of 429 initial candidate publications, eleven studies met the eligibility criteria. Although the database search was structured to capture any agent targeting interleukin-6 pathways, tocilizumab was the only therapeutic molecule identified in the eligible medical literature, as other interleukin-6 receptor antagonists like satralizumab had not yet been documented in published clinical neuro-Behçet’s reports.
Across the eleven identified publications, twenty-six individual patient cases were initially documented. A critical examination of institutional origin and clinical details revealed that one case described by Liu and colleagues and another reported by Yan and colleagues shared identical cerebrospinal fluid white blood cell counts, lesion topologies, and neurological presentations during overlapping institutional periods. To eliminate duplicate data from the statistical synthesis, this individual was counted only once using the richer dataset from Liu’s group, establishing a final pooled cohort of twenty-five patients, comprising fourteen men and eleven women. The median patient age across the pooled cohort was 33 years, with an interquartile range extending from 28 to 38 years and an overall range from 10 to 50 years. Before commencing tocilizumab, these individuals had undergone extensive treatment with conventional immunosuppressants, including systemic glucocorticoids in twenty-four cases, azathioprine in seventeen, cyclophosphamide in fifteen, infliximab in thirteen, methotrexate in eleven, and cyclosporine A in five, all without achieving sustained disease control.
Assessment of therapeutic efficacy required careful categorization due to the varied outcome measures utilized across the original source papers. Response was formally evaluated across three clinical domains: clinical response, defined as documented symptomatic improvement in neurological signs; radiographic response, defined as regression or resolution of magnetic resonance imaging abnormalities; and cerebrospinal fluid response, defined as normalization of laboratory parameters. Radiographic stability was tracked separately and not counted as an imaging response. For studies employing composite response definitions, classifications respected the original authors’ criteria, although symptom-specific benefits were not assumed if not explicitly reported. Of the twenty-five pooled patients, twenty-four were evaluable for therapeutic response, while one patient could not be assessed due to treatment cessation following a severe acute infusion reaction. Among the twenty-four evaluable individuals, twenty-two were classified as responders, yielding an overall response rate of 91.7 percent with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning 74.2 percent to 97.7 percent. One patient exhibited stable disease without clinical improvement, and one experienced disease relapse within months.
Beyond symptomatic neurological stabilization, tocilizumab demonstrated meaningful steroid-sparing efficacy across the assembled cohort. All twenty-four evaluable patients were receiving concomitant systemic glucocorticoids at baseline. Following the introduction of tocilizumab, documented glucocorticoid dose reductions occurred in fifteen patients, complete discontinuation of glucocorticoids was achieved in three patients, glucocorticoid dosage remained unchanged in one patient, and specific tapering data were unavailable for five patients. Consequently, at least 75.0 percent of patients receiving glucocorticoids, representing eighteen of the twenty-four individuals with a 95 percent confidence interval of 55.1 percent to 88.0 percent, successfully reduced their corticosteroid burden. Serial radiographic monitoring was documented or evaluable in thirteen of the twenty-five patients. Among these thirteen cases, nine individuals, or 69.2 percent, demonstrated clear regression of previous intracranial abnormalities, while four patients, or 30.8 percent, demonstrated radiographic stability without new lesion formation. Baseline lumbar punctures recorded elevated cerebrospinal fluid interleukin-6 levels in six patients, showing a median value of 285 picograms per milliliter with a range of 187 to 1000 picograms per milliliter, and follow-up cerebrospinal fluid testing in two patients confirmed biochemical improvement.
The reported administration regimens for tocilizumab consisted of either eight milligrams per kilogram administered intravenously every four weeks or 162 milligrams delivered via weekly subcutaneous injections. The median follow-up duration across the cohort was 13.5 months, with an interquartile range of 7 to 26 months, though follow-up duration was unstated in seven cases. Documented adverse reactions were infrequent but included one case each of severe infusion reaction, transient leukopenia accompanied by neutropenia, pneumonia, scrotal abscess alongside condyloma acuminata, and systemic sepsis. Interestingly, one treatment responder developed an acute recurrence of painful oral ulcerations after starting tocilizumab. Previous literature corroborates that while tocilizumab provides potent neuroimmunological control, it is occasionally ineffective against mucocutaneous and articular manifestations in Behçet’s disease, and rare paradoxical oral or cutaneous flares have been observed, indicating that organ-specific inflammatory pathways may diverge within the same systemic syndrome.
To provide clinical context regarding the unmet therapeutic need in this population, the authors presented detailed courses of two institutional patients managed at Yokohama City University between 2021 and 2025 who fulfilled both International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease and Japanese ministry guidelines. Both patients exhibited progressive neurological decline and persistently elevated cerebrospinal fluid interleukin-6 concentrations despite intensive tumor necrosis factor alpha blockade, yet neither received interleukin-6 targeted therapy. Case 1 involved a 44-year-old man who developed brainstem lesions, dysarthria, dysphagia, and gait instability nine years after his initial Behçet’s diagnosis. Despite sequential therapy with adalimumab and infliximab combined with glucocorticoids, his cerebrospinal fluid interleukin-6 levels remained persistently elevated, cerebral atrophy progressed, and he eventually became wheelchair-dependent and required gastrostomy placement, although confounding factors including Li-Fraumeni syndrome chemotherapy and suspected Wernicke’s encephalopathy complicated his clinical course. Case 2 involved an 18-year-old man who developed central nervous system involvement including gait impairment and bilateral lower-limb hyperreflexia while receiving escalating infliximab infusions for refractory uveitis; notably, his serum C-reactive protein remained entirely normal while cerebrospinal fluid interleukin-6 levels remained markedly elevated.
These institutional clinical trajectories demonstrate that tumor necrosis factor inhibitors do not invariably guarantee long-term neuroprotection, even when they demonstrate initial effectiveness. While prior short-term studies by Hirohata and Kikuchi documented rapid suppression of cerebrospinal fluid interleukin-6 within three weeks of starting infliximab in five patients with chronic progressive neuro-Behçet’s disease, the Yokohama cases reflect disease trajectories over several years of established therapy. The two observations are not necessarily contradictory, as initial cytokine suppression may fail to prevent late immunological breakthrough over extended observation windows. The biological plausibility of interleukin-6 blockade in this setting was further underscored by an external case report published by Benyarou and colleagues after the review search window closed. In that report, an individual with advanced refractory neuro-Behçet’s disease treated with tocilizumab showed marked radiographic lesion clearance despite failing to recover functional neurological deficits, reinforcing the concept that initiating interleukin-6 inhibition before irreversible axonal loss occurs is paramount for optimal clinical recovery.
