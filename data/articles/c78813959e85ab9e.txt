Anthropic Shares Climb 11% to $91.88, Reclaiming $83 IPO Price After Rocky First Week
Anthropic PBC (ANT) closed Friday at $91.88, up 10.98% on the session, pushing back above the $83 per-share price set in its record initial public offering and ending a two-day stretch of closes below that level.
The gain gave the artificial intelligence developer a full-week advance of roughly 3.3% and capped its first complete week of Nasdaq trading. U.S. markets closed early for the Thanksgiving holiday, and the stock rode a broad rebound in technology shares as risk appetite recovered after a selloff that had been led by Nvidia (NVDA) over much of the past month.
At Friday's close, Anthropic carried a fully diluted market value of about $1.7 trillion, according to one calculation, though another pegged the figure closer to $1.3 trillion. Either way, it remains well below the more than $2 trillion it briefly touched on its Nov. 19 debut, when the stock reached $115.10 intraday before pulling back. Shares closed that first session at $97.17.
The offering itself was the largest IPO on record, raising $119.5 billion, according to a Chinese financial news outlet. The company sold public stock alongside private placements from backers including Nvidia and Alphabet (GOOGL). At the $83 offer price, the deal valued Anthropic at $1.49 trillion on a fully diluted basis, or 18 times the $81 billion annualized revenue run rate it reported for October.
A bumpy opening stretch raises questions about the deal's mechanics
The first week's volatility has drawn scrutiny to how the offering was structured. Some investors have asked why underwriting banks were permitted to exercise the over-allotment option immediately after the first trading day rather than holding that capacity in reserve to help steady the stock later.
Attention has also focused on the supply of insider shares under Anthropic's staggered lockup schedule. The prospectus provides for insider holdings to be released in stages over a year, with as many as 315 million shares becoming eligible for sale on day one. Another 303 million shares are scheduled to clear restrictions on Dec. 15.
The co-founders' stakes are locked up far longer. CEO Dario Amodei and President Daniela Amodei were among seven co-founders who each sold 3,378,378 shares at $83 during the offering, a combined $1.96 billion that represented just 1.4% of their holdings, according to SEC Form 4 filings. The seven still hold roughly 1.69 billion shares and equity awards, equal to 12.7% of shares outstanding, with a value near $140 billion at the offer price.
The first selling window for the co-founders opens two trading days after first-quarter 2027 results or on May 17, 2027, whichever comes first, when up to 29% of their locked stake, about 490 million shares, becomes available. The remaining 71%, roughly 1.20 billion shares, stays restricted until after third-quarter 2027 results or Nov. 18, 2027. A single Class F share preserves 50.1% voting power with the co-founders, whose pledge to donate 80% of their personal Anthropic stock is described in the prospectus as "revocable and not legally binding."
Public trading covers only about 6% of Anthropic's fully diluted stock, with 15.5 billion pre-IPO shares subject to lockups across 23 release dates.
What could move the stock next
Two catalysts sit on the December calendar. Index providers including Nasdaq could add Anthropic to benchmarks such as the Nasdaq 100, which would trigger buying from funds that track them. The Dec. 15 lockup release, meanwhile, could add to the available supply of shares.
The broader AI trade has been unsteady. Before the listing, concerns about the pace of AI spending had already rattled technology stocks, and the mood darkened this week after Jensen Huang returned to Nvidia, prompting investors to weigh how the controversy around him might affect the wider AI ecosystem. Worries about cloud-provider debt and data-center spending dragged Anthropic shares down 15% earlier in the week, while persistent U.S. inflation lifted expectations for further Federal Reserve rate increases.
Against that backdrop, demand for the company's products has been the central pillar of the bull case. Rapid adoption of tools such as Claude Code, its software-engineering agent, helped push annualized revenue to $81 billion by the end of October. That growth has helped investors look past the company's long-term compute commitments, which the prospectus lists at $900.9 billion, with $70.7 billion due within 12 months.

Analyst sentiment remains constructive. On TipRanks, ANT holds a Strong Buy consensus rating built on 10 Buy ratings and one Hold, with an average 12-month price target of $150.50, implying upside of about 82% from Friday's close.
For investors, the coming weeks present a test of whether index inclusion demand can absorb the December unlock and whether the AI spending debate that has whipsawed megacap technology names will continue to dictate the stock's direction. The company's first quarterly report as a public issuer, expected in the new year, will offer the first hard look at whether the revenue trajectory that underpinned the offering is holding.
