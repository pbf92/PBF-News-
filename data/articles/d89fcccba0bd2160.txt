Investing.com — Big Tech's AI spending race has become a funding question this autumn, as investors weigh how much of the data center build-out the largest platforms can pay for from their own cash. Even Microsoft (NASDAQ:MSFT) has slipped 4.3% over the past month. Yet Meta Platforms (NASDAQ:META)—the owner of Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp—has fared far worse, dropping 12.6% over the past month to $616.38, including a 1.2% slip in the past week.
On paper, Meta looks like the one Big Tech stock that didn't get caught in a valuation bubble, but its low multiple hides an AI bill that has already pushed free cash flow below zero. Shares trade at just 20.9 times trailing earnings with a 0.3% dividend yield, while analysts forecast 27.8% revenue growth and 25.8% EPS growth. More Users, Higher Ad Prices
Meta makes its money selling advertising against the attention of the world's largest family of social media and messaging apps, and its moat is pricing power at that scale. Daily active people across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger, and Threads reached an all-time high of 3.64 billion in the third quarter, with record time spent on Instagram and Facebook. Advertisers paid more to reach them: ad impressions rose 10% year-over-year while the average price per ad climbed 14%.
Third-quarter results proved that pricing power is intact. Revenue surged 26% year-over-year to $64.6 billion, topping the $63.34 billion forecast, and EPS of $3.83 beat the $3.57 consensus by 7.3%. Advertising revenue climbed 26% to $63.1 billion, partnership ads passed a $15 billion annual run rate after growing 97%, and management guided fourth-quarter revenue to $73 billion to $76 billion.
