TITLE: Gold rises as rebound in tech stocks offsets rate-hike concerns
OUTLET: TradingView

Gold rises as rebound in tech stocks offsets rate-hike concerns
Gold rose as a tentative recovery in technology stocks and a weaker US dollar offset the prospect of interest-rate hikes by the Federal Reserve.
Bullion climbed as much as 1.7% to near $3,627 an ounce, on track for a weekly gain after rebounding from a 15-month low two days ago. Asian stocks advanced for a second day, extending a rally following weeks of volatility in the artificial intelligence trade, while a gauge of the dollar fell 0.1%, making bullion priced in the US currency cheaper for most buyers.
Sentiment remains fragile, however, at the end of a choppy week of trading. The yellow metal has remained below $4,000 an ounce since Nov. 11 as traders gauged the Fed’s path for combating sticky inflation. It’s still on track for a third monthly loss of more than 6%, extending a fall from a record high in January that gained pace after the US-Iran war sent energy costs soaring.
The Fed’s preferred measure of underlying inflation for October, released Wednesday, reinforced expectations that the central bank will tighten policy when it meets in December. Higher borrowing costs are typically negative for gold, which doesn’t pay interest. The market is also weighing the likelihood of further rate hikes early next year if price pressures continue.
“Gold faces a critical support zone between $3,450 and $3,500, the area from which prices broke out in August 2025 before staging their historic rally to record highs,” said Hebe Chen, senior market analyst at Vantage Global Prime. “This is now the line in the sand for gold — holding above this zone could provide a foundation for stabilization, while a decisive break below would raise the risk of a deeper correction.”
Spot gold climbed 1.4% to $3,616.36 an ounce at 10:24 a.m. in Singapore. Silver surged 3.6% to $43.04 an ounce. Platinum and palladium also advanced.
