TITLE: Another AI company is about to be worth more than $1 trillion. What that means for the rest of us
OUTLET: NBC News

Leading AI lab Anthropic is expected to make the largest-ever public market debut when it starts trading on the Nasdaq stock exchange Thursday.
Founded in 2021 by brother and sister Dario and Daniela Amodei, both former employees of rival OpenAI, Anthropic and its main product, Claude, are seen as the gold standard of AI models, despite lacking the household-name cache of ChatGPT.
With a target market value of $1.5 trillion — larger than SpaceX’s record setting IPO just a few months ago — Anthropic’s public arrival will serve as more than just a measure of what Wall Street thinks the company is worth. It will be a test of current sentiment around AI itself at a pivotal moment for the industry.
For much of the past year, the AI investment wave has powered the stock market to new heights and sustained U.S. economic growth. But there are signs the wave may be starting to break.
“Investors have over recent weeks become increasingly concerned around the sustainability of the investment boom,” James McCann, senior economist for investment strategy at Edward Jones, said in a client note Wednesday ahead of Anthropic’s debut.
The market gains notched this spring and summer thanks to the AI boom have now largely been wiped out.
Fears of over-spending on infrastructure, rising debt loads, ballooning costs and uncertain demand have grown. At the same time, public opinion has turned against data centers and AI-skeptical Democrats won control of Congress earlier this month.
Then, the Jensen Huang scandal broke, creating a cloud of uncertainty over Nvidia, a company at the heart of the AI economy. The tech heavy Nasdaq was up 19% for the year recently. As of Wednesday, it’s higher by less than 5%.
“The leadership change at Nvidia meanwhile adds to short-term uncertainty around the sector, particularly given the important role this giant company has played in financially supporting a range of smaller technology firms in recent years,” said McCann.
“With the selloff in technology stocks continuing this week investors will be watching to see how well the Anthropic IPO is received.”
The offering priced Anthropic at $83 a share, above the company’s intended range, financial news outlets reported Wednesday afternoon, citing sources.
There is little debate over how uniquely successful Anthropic has been. The latest edition of Claude, dubbed Fable, ranks No. 1 on Artificial Analysis’ leaderboard of top-performing large language models as it is able to handle complex computer engineering tasks.
And its Claude Code application is now used throughout the tech industry and by some accounts became the fastest-growing product in enterprise software history when it was released in Feb. 2025, hitting $1 billion in revenues within six months of launching.
Thursday’s IPO is also expected to mint a new crop of billionaires and multi-millionaires as Anthropic’s co-founders and early employees see their interest in the company turned into shares with a defined cash value.
Part of that will be fueled by a massive appetite for investing in the shares among mom-and-pop investors. According to Vanda Research, a markets consultancy, Anthropic could see approximately $250 million worth of buying among non-institutional or “retail” investors when it starts trading Thursday. That would be double the magnitude of interest following SpaceX’s IPO.
Other major winners will include Amazon, Google-parent Alphabet, Microsoft, and Nvidia, which will each own billions-worth of Anthropic shares.
Some observers see Anthropic’s debut as more of a major milestone than a make-or-break moment. In their view, there are plenty of other investments backstopping the AI boom.
“A strong IPO would help resurrect the AI trade, but it does not mean a weaker-than-expected IPO would bury that trade,” said Renée Friedman, global head of research at EXANTE, a Europe-based finance and technology group.
“There are significant commitments into the AI cycle by some of the world’s largest companies over a multi-year period. These are not going to just disappear,” he said.
In the first half of 2026, Anthropic saw total revenues of more than $16 billion. That compares with $41 million over the same period just three years ago. While Anthropic was technically operating at a loss to start the year, investors expect it to be profitable soon.
Those heady figures capture a tension at the heart of Anthropic’s IPO. In addition to being the leading AI lab, current and former Anthropic employees have also been some of the most vocal proponents of AI safety, giving voice to fears that, should the technology become too advanced, it could start to run amok.
“I think that without international coordination we will incur a risk of causing human extinction,” former Anthropic employee Jacob Coxon wrote of his concerns about AI shortly before resigning.
Yet some critics have said those concerns have proven no match for the seemingly unstoppable momentum of creating a company publicly valued at more than $1 trillion.
Dario Amodei has responded, arguing building safe AI and creating the world’s most advanced — and valuable — AI models are not necessarily mutually exclusive.
When a user criticized Anthropic for seeming to plunge ahead with its attempt to create “superhuman” AI despite the risks to humanity doing so, Amodei replied in an X post that “a list of things that Anthropic did in the past year that were pro-safety and commercially costly to us” would be much longer than a list of risks it posed to mankind.
