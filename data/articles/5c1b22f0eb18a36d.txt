TITLE: Coming home to the Jerusalem Hills
OUTLET: The Jerusalem Post

For generations, “next year in Jerusalem” was a prayer. Today, for a growing number of Jewish families around the world, it has become a reality. Rising antisemitism, a sense of unease on the streets and on campuses, and the simple wish to be close to children and grandchildren who already live here have led many to ask a question they once put off: If not now, when?
Many of those making the move are not twenty-somethings. They are retirees and empty nesters, people with full careers and rich lives behind them, who feel that their place - and their future - is in Israel. Ask them why, and you will rarely hear that they are running away from something. Most will tell you that they are finally coming home.
The real question: Not why, but where
Aliyah later in life brings its own challenges. A new language. Unfamiliar systems, from National Insurance to the health funds and the banks. And perhaps hardest of all, building a social life from scratch in a country where everyone else already seems to know each other.
This is exactly where Beresheet comes in. Opened in November 2025 in Ramat Motza, at the western entrance to Jerusalem, Beresheet is a senior living community built around one simple idea: That no one should have to start a new chapter alone.
Straight from the airport to a new home
Beresheet has already become a first home in Israel for many new olim. About 30 percent of the residents made aliyah directly to Beresheet - landing at Ben Gurion and arriving straight at their new apartment in the Jerusalem Hills.
They did not have to do it alone. The Beresheet team accompanied them throughout the aliyah process - from the first conversations and the paperwork, through planning the move, to their first days in the country - and continues to support them, in English, long after the boxes are unpacked.
An international community, from day one
Walk through the lobby at Beresheet and you will hear English and Hebrew, often in the same conversation. Residents have come from the United States, Canada, South Africa, the UK and Europe, and live side by side with Israelis who have spent their whole lives here. Each learns from the other.
For new olim, the support starts before the boxes are unpacked. English-speaking staff, a welcome week with a dedicated team to help set up the apartment, and a “welcome buddy” - an English-speaking resident who helps newcomers settle in. A personal service manager is the single point of contact for every request, and the team helps residents in English with National Insurance, the health fund and the banks.
And because learning Hebrew is part of feeling at home, Beresheet runs its own Hebrew ulpan on site, for all levels - so residents can study at their own pace, with their neighbors, without leaving home. Alongside this, there are lectures and classes in English, an English-language library and book club, a film club with English subtitles, and an activity schedule and House updates provided in English.
Here, language is not a barrier – it is a bridge.
Where pilgrims once stopped on the way to Jerusalem
Beresheet sits in the Jerusalem Hills, surrounded by forest and open views, just minutes from the city. Right next to the complex, an ancient Roman pool was uncovered with the Israel Antiquities Authority - a water station where pilgrims once rested on their way up to Jerusalem. Today it is at the heart of a new shopping center with cafés and restaurants.
The apartments, designed by architect Oren Shapira, combine traditional Jerusalem arches with clean modern lines: High ceilings, large French windows facing the hills, and wide balconies - with room for a sukkah. Mornings begin with coffee facing the view; evenings end with the sunset over the forest.
A full life, every single day
Life at Beresheet is designed to keep body and mind active. There is a gym with a personal trainer, an indoor semi-Olympic pool, reformer Pilates, balance training and a dry sauna. Guided Nordic walking and bird watching take place on the nature trails, and residents grow vegetables and herbs together in the community garden.
The cultural program includes regular lectures in cooperation with the Hebrew University, concerts in the culture hall, a choir and a residents’ theater, art, ceramics and woodworking, bridge in cooperation with the Israel Bridge Federation, folk dancing, and weekly trips to the most beautiful places in Israel.
The synagogue, named in loving memory of Rachel Lichak, the mother of the family that founded Beresheet, holds daily minyanim, a festive communal Kabbalat Shabbat, kiddush and Oneg Shabbat - and many residents say that Shabbat is when the community feels most like family.
Perhaps most importantly, much of this was created by the residents themselves. A retired professor gives a lecture, a former lawyer leads a discussion group, a lifelong music lover runs the choir. Residents are not guests here - they are partners in building their home.
Peace of mind for the years ahead
Most Beresheet residents are independent and active, and plan to stay that way for many years. But aliyah is also a decision about the future, and peace of mind matters. A nurse is on site 24 hours a day, seven days a week, alongside an on-site clinic with a focus on geriatric medicine, a licensed social worker every day, emergency call buttons in every apartment and round-the-clock security.
If and when more support is needed, it is available in the same home: Medical support services in the apartment, and a supported living unit, rehabilitation and nursing department on site. Behind it all stands a family network with more than 30 years of experience in medical and rehabilitation care for older adults.
The whole family is part of the picture
For olim, family is often what brought them here. Beresheet was built with the whole family in mind. Furnished guest apartments allow children and grandchildren flying in from abroad to stay for a weekend or a month. Private halls host family celebrations, and residents have already marked a grandson’s bar mitzvah and a Shabbat chatan at home.
Grandchildren have a playroom, a VR center and a set day each week at the pool, and once a month the House holds a family Shabbat. There is even a co-working space, so that visiting family members can keep working while they are here.
Choosing Israel - even when it is hard
Some of the olim who made aliyah directly to Beresheet arrived during difficult days for Israel. What happened next surprised even them. Rather than feeling isolated, they found themselves held by the community: Neighbors who accompanied them, explained and translated - not just the Hebrew, but the whole of Israeli life.
And the olim gave something back. A fresh perspective, gratitude for what long-time Israelis take for granted, and a living reminder that Israel is worth choosing. In a world that feels less certain every day, that may be the most powerful thing of all: Knowing that there is a place where you belong, and a community waiting to welcome you home.
