TITLE: Lung Ultrasound Outperforms Chest X-Ray in Diagnosing Pneumonia in Older Adults
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Lung ultrasound demonstrates substantially higher sensitivity and overall diagnostic accuracy than conventional chest radiography for detecting pneumonia in older adults, while delivering comparable specificity, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in European Geriatric Medicine.
Diagnosing pneumonia in older patients presents notable clinical hurdles because illness presentations in this population are frequently atypical. Furthermore, the diagnostic utility of standard chest radiography is often compromised by clinical and technical limitations that commonly affect older individuals. Chest X-ray examinations can be impeded by poor inspiratory effort, physical immobility, and the confounding presence of chronic comorbidities. Because these factors can reduce the interpretability of radiographic images, researchers have increasingly evaluated lung ultrasound as an alternative modality capable of providing more accurate diagnostic assessment.
To evaluate the comparative diagnostic accuracy of both modalities, the investigators performed an extensive literature search across four biomedical citation databases: PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and the Cochrane Library. The research team supplemented this search with backward citation tracing to identify all relevant published investigations. Studies were eligible for inclusion if they evaluated both lung ultrasound and chest X-ray in patient cohorts aged 60 years or older. All candidate records were screened, selected, and reviewed through an independent process executed by two researchers.
From an initial total of 2,148 retrieved records, nine studies satisfied all inclusion criteria and were incorporated into the meta-analysis. Methodological quality and risk of bias across the included studies were evaluated using the QUADAS-2 tool. For statistical synthesis, the authors utilized random-effects models to calculate pooled diagnostic odds ratios. In addition, diagnostic sensitivity, specificity, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve were pooled utilizing a bivariate random-effects meta-analysis framework.
The quantitative meta-analysis demonstrated that lung ultrasound achieved markedly superior diagnostic odds compared with standard chest radiography in older patients. Across the included investigations, lung ultrasound yielded a pooled diagnostic odds ratio of 93.35, with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 27.54 to 316.45. In contrast, standard chest X-ray imaging demonstrated a pooled diagnostic odds ratio of 8.33, with a 95 percent confidence interval extending from 3.09 to 22.46.
Summary estimates from the bivariate random-effects meta-analysis further demonstrated the diagnostic strength of lung ultrasound. Lung ultrasound showed high performance, achieving a pooled area under the curve of 0.97, a sensitivity of 0.94, and a specificity of 0.88. In contrast, chest radiography showed lower diagnostic accuracy, yielding an area under the curve of 0.69, a sensitivity of 0.63, and a specificity of 0.86. While both imaging modalities demonstrated comparable specificity, lung ultrasound identified pneumonia cases with substantially greater sensitivity.
The authors concluded that lung ultrasound is a rapid and radiation-free diagnostic tool that is more sensitive than chest X-ray for detecting pneumonia in older adults. The researchers noted that lung ultrasound represents a suitable alternative or complementary imaging modality to chest radiography in patients aged 60 and older. However, the study emphasized that clinical application requires appropriate operator expertise, noting that the diagnostic advantages of lung ultrasound depend on trained personnel being available to conduct and interpret the examinations.
