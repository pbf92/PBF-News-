TITLE: Rockstar Next Game After GTA 6: L.A. Noire 2 Leak
OUTLET: shattered.io

Rockstar Games’ next project after Grand Theft Auto VI may skip both of its biggest franchises. According to ComicBook.com, a resurfaced leak claims the studio’s next game is not GTA 7, not Red Dead Redemption 3 and not Bully 2, but a sequel to L.A. Noire. The claim landed three days after GTA VI went on sale, which is why it spread so fast.
Treat it as a rumor. Rockstar has not announced its next internally developed game, and the company has not confirmed L.A. Noire 2, Bully 2, Grand Theft Auto VII or Red Dead Redemption 3. The leak says the sequel would move to San Francisco, build its story around the Zodiac Killer and aim for 2030. None of those details carries an official stamp from Rockstar or its parent company, Take-Two Interactive.
The timing matters because GTA VI is a record-setting launch. Portal GTA 6 reports that critics gave it a 99 on Metacritic, a score we examined in our piece on GTA VI tying Ocarina of Time at 99. Every fan, analyst and investor now wants to know what comes next. This analysis sorts what is confirmed from what is rumor, explains why the leak got attention and lays out what could change the picture.
What the Leak Claims About Rockstar’s Next Game
The core claim is simple. An anonymous source says Rockstar’s next Rockstar-developed release is a new L.A. Noire. ComicBook.com framed it as a break from the pattern fans expect, since Grand Theft Auto and Red Dead Redemption are the studio’s most lucrative and most celebrated series. The outlet also noted that the leak rules out Bully 2, a sequel fans have wanted for two decades.
Three supporting details travel with the claim. The setting would shift from 1940s Los Angeles to San Francisco. The plot would center on the Zodiac Killer, the unidentified serial killer tied to Northern California. The target window would be 2030. GamesRadar picked up the story under a headline telling readers not to expect to play the game before at least 2030, and Dexerto and other outlets ran their own versions.
Each of those details is unconfirmed. They come from a forum leak, not from a press release, a trailer, a job listing or a regulatory filing. That distinction defines how to read everything else in this story. A rumor can be interesting, internally consistent and even partly right without becoming a fact on a Sunday afternoon in November.
What Rockstar and Take-Two Have Actually Confirmed
The confirmed list is short. Grand Theft Auto VI carries a November 19, 2026 release date. Take-Two Interactive owns Rockstar Games. Remedy Entertainment is developing Max Payne 1 & 2 Remake, with Rockstar financing and publishing it. That remake combines the first two Max Payne games into one title for PC, PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, runs on Remedy’s Northlight engine and entered full production in 2024. No release date exists.

Where the L.A. Noire 2 Leak Came From
Provenance is the weakest link. The source is an anonymous 4chan or GTAForums user, and no outlet has identified a verifiable insider. Dexerto’s account traces the claim to a December 2023 GTAForums post that relayed information from an anonymous 4chan poster. ComicBook.com, by contrast, describes the leaker’s report as resurfacing in September of this year. Outlets disagree on the timeline, and that gap alone should slow the celebration.
The 4chan and GTAForums Trail
Anonymous message boards produce both the best and the worst gaming leaks. They cost nothing to post on, they reward attention and they carry no accountability. A poster who gets one detail right earns a reputation, and a poster who gets ten wrong rarely gets mentioned again. Readers see the hits and forget the misses, which is a classic survivorship problem.
GamesRadar labeled its source a GTA 6 leaker, which is accurate but vague. It tells readers the person claims knowledge of GTA VI, and it says nothing about knowledge of Rockstar’s internal planning for a game several years away. Those are different kinds of information, and a source can hold one without the other.
Why the GTA VI Ending Revived It
The story returned to the front page because players concluded the same source had described part of GTA VI‘s ending correctly. ComicBook.com said the details held up, which pushed fans to re-read everything else the leaker said. That is a reasonable reaction, but it proves less than it seems. A correct claim about a game that shipped on November 19 shows access to some information. It does not show access to a studio’s 2030 roadmap.
How Much Weight a Single Accurate Leak Deserves
Forecasters use a simple test for sources like this one. Ask whether the claim was checkable at the time it was made and whether it was specific enough to fail. A prediction about an ending was both. A prediction that a game will arrive around 2030 is neither, because the year sits far enough away that nobody can score it for years, and an unnamed studio plan can shift without notice.
Rockstar also has a long record of changing course. According to Portal GTA 6, GTA VI first aimed for late 2025, then moved to May 2026 before settling on November 19, 2026. If the studio’s flagship title slipped twice, a sequel placed four years out has plenty of room to move, change genre or vanish entirely. Early-stage projects get cancelled, retooled and renamed all the time across the industry.
That does not make the leak false. It makes the leak a low-confidence data point. The right posture is to hold the claim loosely, watch for corroboration from named sources and avoid building plans or purchases around it.
L.A. Noire’s History Makes the Rumor Plausible and Awkward
ComicBook.com supplies the background. L.A. Noire arrived in 2011 as an action-adventure game that Rockstar published but did not develop. Team Bondi built it, and the studio closed the same year. The game scored 89 on Metacritic and, as of 2017, had sold more than 7.5 million copies. A VR spin-off, L.A. Noire: The VR Case Files, followed in 2017, and the series has sat dormant since.
Those numbers explain the appeal. A critically strong, commercially solid detective game with a loyal audience is an obvious candidate for a revival. A fresh setting like San Francisco gives designers a new city and a famous unsolved case to build around. The 1960s Zodiac story supplies mood, period detail and a mystery structure that suits the series’ interrogation gameplay.
The awkward part is ownership of the work. Rockstar published the original but did not build it, so a sequel would need either an internal team or an outside partner. ComicBook.com itself urged caution and said it would be surprising to see L.A. Noire revived unless Rockstar contracted it out, since the Grand Theft Auto and Red Dead Redemption franchises earn too much to give way. The outlet told readers to take the whole leak with a grain of salt.
Max Payne 1 & 2 Remake Is the Next Rockstar Release With a Name
One Rockstar-linked project already has confirmed status, and it complicates the question of what counts as the next game. Max Payne 1 & 2 Remake is a Remedy production. Rockstar finances and publishes it, and it entered full production in 2024. Because Remedy develops it, the project does not fit the leak’s framing of Rockstar’s next internally developed game.
Headline writers have noticed the gap. TweakTown ran a story arguing that Rockstar’s next game after GTA 6 is already confirmed but is not Red Dead Redemption 3. Whether a published remake counts as the next Rockstar game depends on your definition. For release order, the remake looks like the likelier next arrival, since it has been in full production for roughly two years and a four-year gap to 2030 for the leaked title would leave a lot of calendar to fill.
No release date exists for the remake. That means no one can say with confidence whether it lands before or after any Rockstar-developed sequel. The claim that an L.A. Noire sequel would be Rockstar’s next release after GTA VI stays unconfirmed, partly because the remake has an open calendar slot of its own.
Rockstar’s Eight-Year Promise Changes the Timing Math
The leak’s 2030 target sits awkwardly beside what Rockstar’s own leaders said before launch. Portal GTA 6 reports that development leads Phil Hooker and Aaron Garbut told Bloomberg’s Jason Schreier that the engine, tools and team built for GTA VI should let the studio make its next project faster. The studio spent nearly eight years on the game, and Beebom summarized the message as a promise that fans will not face another wait that long.
Hooker described a plan to build sideways rather than upward, meaning the team would extend the existing technology instead of starting over. Garbut said the team changed a lot at a foundational level for GTA VI and does not intend to rip it apart now that the work is done. Neither executive named a game, a platform or a date, and Portal GTA 6 stressed that any title floated for the follow-up is commentators’ speculation.
This is the strongest argument for treating a 2030 target with suspicion, and also for taking a shorter cycle seriously. Faster does not mean soon. A cycle of four years would cut the GTA VI timeline roughly in half, which fits the executives’ words without implying a release in 2027 or 2028. The leak and the Bloomberg comments can both be true, but neither proves the other.

Why a Smaller Game Could Fit Rockstar’s Strategy
A sequel to a mid-sized detective game would signal a shift in how Rockstar spends its people. The studio’s headline projects consume enormous teams for years. Our look at Rockstar’s 1,000 veterans who passed 10 years at the company shows how much accumulated experience sits inside one release. Spreading that talent across a second franchise could shorten the gap between releases.
There is a financial logic as well. A narrower project with a defined audience carries less capital risk than another open-world epic. In our coverage of Strauss Zelnick and the $1 billion-plus cost estimates for GTA VI, those numbers show why a publisher might want a cheaper follow-up. That argument is analysis, not a Rockstar statement.
The counterargument is just as strong. Rockstar has built its reputation on scale, and a smaller game would break from that identity. Whether the studio changes course depends on internal priorities that outsiders cannot see.
Market Impact: Take-Two, the Pipeline and a $3.79 Billion Backdrop
Investors care about this story because Take-Two’s growth story now rests on what follows GTA VI. Portal GTA 6 reports that Take-Two said the game earned $3.79 billion on day one, which makes it the biggest entertainment launch ever. We broke down why that figure measures retail sales and not company income in our explainer on GTA VI’s $3.79 billion and Take-Two revenue. That distinction shapes how analysts value the pipeline.
