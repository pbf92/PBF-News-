TITLE: S&P 500, Dow, Nasdaq Futures Power Ahead On Nvidia Outlook Beat — NVDA, TSLA, ANT In Focus
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

- The S&P 500 eased about 1%, the Nasdaq 100 lost nearly 0.7%, and the Dow Jones index fell 1.6% on Tuesday.
- Nvidia stock gained after-hours after interim CEO Colette Kress raised the chipmaker's FY28 revenue growth outlook to 75%, up from 70% during the earnings call.
- Investor attention now on Anthropic IPO listing due Thursday.
U.S. stock futures gained on Tuesday following a strong earnings update and outlook from Nvidia amid an ongoing leadership transition, with investor attention on the Anthropic IPO due later this week.
Nasdaq-100 futures were up 1.4%, while S&P 500 futures added 0.8%. Dow Jones Industrial Average futures traded up 151 points, or 0.3%.
Meanwhile, the S&P 500 eased about 1%, the Nasdaq 100 lost nearly 0.7%, and the Dow Jones index fell 1.6% on Tuesday. The Russell 2000, which tracks small-cap stocks, ended 1.5% lower.
The SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY) rose about 0.7% after-hours, and the SPDR Dow Jones Industrial Average ETF (DIA) added 0.3%. Meanwhile, the Nasdaq-100 tracking Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ) gained 1.4% after-hours. All three ETFs ended Tuesday's session lower.
Meanwhile, the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) and the iShares Semiconductor ETF jumped about 4% in after-market trading on Tuesday, as investors cheered Nvidia's robust outlook that was well above analyst expectations. The broader Vanguard Information Technology ETF (VGT) fell about 3% in the regular session.
"The fact that they gave guidance is huge," Gene Munster, managing partner at Deepwater Asset Management, said in a televised interview on CNBC. "That is a very bullish sign. I expect the stock to move higher based just on that alone."
Retail sentiment on Stocktwits was 'bullish' across SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY), Invesco QQQ (QQQ) and SPDR Dow Jones ETF (DIA), which track the benchmark indices at market close on Tuesday.
US Market Drivers
Nvidia's robust guidance provided fresh confidence to investors that the artificial intelligence demand can be sustained, helping cool off some of the capex jitters that have weighed on investor sentiment over the past few weeks.
"Nvidia's results were solid and largely in line with our expectations, which should help ease some of the concerns created by the recent leadership turmoil," Brian Colello, senior equity analyst at Morningstar, told Bloomberg. "The next CEO will have big shoes to fill, but Nvidia's long-term opportunity does not hinge on one person."
