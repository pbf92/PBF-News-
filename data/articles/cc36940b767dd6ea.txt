TITLE: YouGov Poll: 60% of Americans Call High Ticket Prices a Major Air Travel Problem Days After Nationwide Ground Stop
OUTLET: Avi-Go

YouGov Poll: 60% of Americans Call High Ticket Prices a Major Air Travel Problem Days After Nationwide Ground Stop
Why It MattersFare levels now rank above infrastructure and safety concerns in public sentiment, while price-collusion lawsuits add legal pressure, so pricing transparency is becoming as exposed a front for airlines as operational reliability.
A YouGov poll conducted November 17–19 found that high ticket prices were the issue most often named as a major problem in US air travel, cited by 60% of respondents. The survey came days after a November 13 nationwide ground stop caused by telecoms upgrade work, which delayed 8,500 aircraft and led to 8,000 canceled flights.
More than 1,000 US citizens aged 18 or older took part, drawn from YouGov's opt-in panel and weighted by gender, age, race, education, US region, 2024 presidential vote and other factors to approximate a representative sample. The top five issues rated a major problem were: high ticket prices (60%), air traffic control staffing shortages (58%), outdated air traffic control technology (55%), cyberattacks on aviation computer systems (48%), and flight delays or cancellations (45%). Extra and hidden fees also scored highly.
Ticket pricing has also drawn legal scrutiny this month. Two proposed class action lawsuits allege that seven major US airlines, including the three legacy carriers, collaborated to raise domestic fares by sharing confidential pricing information, preventing undercutting. The industry-owned Airline Tariff Publishing Company and its subsidiary 3Victors are also named in the controversy. Seattle-based firm Hagens Berman is leading the action; managing partner Steve Berman said the airlines are "basically eliminating competition."
On government performance, 50% of respondents said the Federal Aviation Administration is doing a good job regulating commercial aviation safety, 17% said a bad job and 33% were not sure. Thirty percent approved of Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy's job performance and 32% disapproved, including 22% who strongly disapproved. On President Donald Trump's handling of air travel, 47% disapproved (39% strongly) and 33% approved. Forty-three percent said US air travel has gotten worse under Trump, versus 15% who said it has improved.
Passengers in this poll ranked what they pay above the state of the air traffic control system that had just grounded flights nationwide. For the commercial airline segment, that ordering matters: operational disruptions are episodic and can be attributed to infrastructure or contractors, whereas fares are a standing, airline-controlled variable. Pricing therefore carries the reputational exposure that an outage does not, and the high showing for extra and hidden fees points the same way.
The class action allegations sit on the same fault line. Claims that carriers shared confidential fare data through an industry-owned intermediary, if they proceed, would turn a public-opinion complaint into a discovery and compliance question about how fares are filed and coordinated. A poll cannot establish that any such coordination occurred, and the lawsuits are proposed actions, not findings.
The next concrete marker is whether the Hagens Berman actions advance past their initial filings and what, if anything, the named airlines and the Airline Tariff Publishing Company say in response. Until a court or regulator acts, the poll measures sentiment only, and sentiment toward the FAA, the Transportation Secretary and the administration remains split among approval, disapproval and uncertainty.
