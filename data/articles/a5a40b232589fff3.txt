Pochettino Rejects Claims He Has Become Too Nice at United
Manchester United manager Mauricio Pochettino has pushed back against criticism of his leadership style, insisting he remains capable of demanding high standards despite his friendly approach to players. Speaking at a press conference on Friday, November 27, 2026, ahead of his first home match against Brentford at Old Trafford, the Argentine coach addressed comments made by former footballer Jamie Redknapp.
Redknapp had questioned whether Pochettino had lost the intensity that characterized his earlier managerial spells at Southampton and Tottenham. The criticism focused on his more relaxed public demeanor, including his habit of high-fiving players, wearing bracelets, and sporting a different hairstyle.
Pochettino rejected the suggestion that being approachable meant being soft. He explained that maintaining positive relationships with players was different from avoiding difficult decisions.
"Sometimes we are confusing two words that maybe have a similar meaning. Nice and good. I am not nice.
I am not nice. We have to be good, that is a different thing. I am not nice.
I am trying to be good. Be careful because we know when we need to push, when we need to be strong, and when we need to build something to improve the atmosphere and relationship and everything. These two things are different." said Pochettino, Manchester United manager.
His comments came after a difficult opening period at Old Trafford, with United suffering a 4-0 defeat against Liverpool in his first match. Leny Yoro received an early red card during that game, although the decision was subsequently overturned.
United also drew 2-2 against Sporting Lisbon in the Champions League during the week. The match included two penalties conceded by United, two disallowed goals, and a controversial dismissal for Youri Tielemans.
Despite those setbacks, Pochettino urged his players to remain mentally strong rather than become distracted by contentious decisions. He acknowledged that frustration would not help the team recover.
"We need to accept that. What we are going to do? Start to cry? No, be strong, believe, keep going." said Pochettino, Manchester United manager.
He also addressed the controversial refereeing decisions involving Liverpool and Sporting Lisbon, arguing that mistakes were part of football and should not become an excuse for the team's difficulties.
"With this type of situation you can always think, 'oh, always things happen against us', but it's not true. That is not true. These type of things happen.
There was a big mistake from the referee against Liverpool, but some things happen. It's happened now with us, it can happen in the future, it happened in the past. The mistake on Wednesday was the same." said Pochettino, Manchester United manager.
The manager also described his approach since accepting the United position, emphasizing the need for commitment and hard work while attempting to rebuild the squad's mentality.
"I need to shut my mouth and say nothing and start from now. When I accepted the invitation to come to Manchester United and start to work in this circumstance, shut my mouth and work 12 hours every day; try to recover the feelings, the emotions, and build our mentality again and really believe in what we are going to do." said Pochettino, Manchester United manager.
Pochettino acknowledged that restoring confidence would take time, particularly because several players have struggled with form and belief this season. United have also slipped into the relegation zone, increasing pressure on the squad and coaching staff.
"I think it's easy for us that we arrive one week ago. It's easy to arrive here with full energy and being positive and try to build that belief that we need, no?" said Pochettino, Manchester United manager.
He said the players faced additional difficulties because of the scrutiny surrounding the club. According to Pochettino, poor results at major clubs often create an atmosphere that makes it harder for footballers to perform confidently.
"But, of course, for the players it's so tough because we cannot make all that noise go away that is around this football club. But it's not only in this football club, it's in every single club that is not performing well or getting the result that maybe everyone expects. That is it's so tough for the player.
What we need to understand is the players need to play. They are going to play tomorrow, and the next one, and the next one, and the next one. The man-management is important, individually and collectively.
We need to recover our confidence, our trust and our belief." said Pochettino, Manchester United manager.
He added that introducing his preferred playing methods would require a gradual process rather than an immediate overhaul. Pochettino said he wanted to avoid increasing the pressure already affecting players, staff, the organization, and supporters.
"And, of course, to implement our method little by little. It's not to implement everything. Be careful.
What we don't want is to add more pressure. The players are under pressure, we are under pressure, the organisation is under pressure, the fans are under pressure. Everyone is under pressure, but we need to understand that the players need to perform and feel the trust of the people in charge." said Pochettino, Manchester United manager.
Redknapp had originally raised his concerns earlier in the week on The Overlap podcast. He contrasted Pochettino's current public image with the more aggressive personality he remembered from the manager's previous positions.
"I've got a little bit of a bee in my bonnet about Pochettino. When I saw Pochettino at Southampton and I saw him at Tottenham, he looked to me like he had that Argentinian dog in him. I look at him now and he's high-fiving everyone, he's got the bracelets on, he's got the barnet. I think he's gone too nice, I do." said Redknapp, former footballer and television pundit.
Pochettino's immediate challenge is to improve Manchester United's performances and restore confidence as the team prepares to host Brentford at Old Trafford.
