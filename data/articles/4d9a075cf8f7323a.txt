TITLE: Nasdaq Plunges 5.28% in One Day, Erasing Year-to-Date Gains; Nvidia Sheds Over $2.7 Trillion in Market Value
OUTLET: BigGo Finance

Nasdaq Plunges 5.28% in One Day, Erasing Year-to-Date Gains; Nvidia Sheds Over $2.7 Trillion in Market Value
On November 20 (US Eastern Time), US stocks suffered a rare and severe selloff, with artificial intelligence-related assets at the epicenter of the flight from risk. The Nasdaq Composite tumbled 5.28% to close at 22,617.73, erasing all of its 2026 gains and turning negative by 2.69% for the year. The S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.39, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 1.93% to 48,147.33. Both the S&P 500 and the Dow have fallen more than 10% from recent highs, putting them in technical correction territory.
The weekly picture was even grimmer. The Dow fell 8.02% for the week, the S&P 500 lost 8.61%, and the Nasdaq dropped 10.02%, with the Dow and S&P 500 posting their largest weekly declines since April 2025. The Nasdaq 100 plunged 10.43% for the week and is about 20% below its all-time high, becoming the first to enter a technical bear market. The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX), a gauge of market turbulence, briefly broke through the 40 level intraday before closing at 35.89, up 26.2% for the day.
The AI Trade Narrative Takes a Collective Hit
The trigger for this correction was not a single event but the simultaneous loosening of multiple pillars underpinning AI-sector valuations. Nvidia's latest earnings report this week showed fiscal 2027 third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year-over-year, with data center revenue of $107.6 billion and fourth-quarter revenue guidance of $130 billion, plus or minus 3%—all beating market expectations. Yet the strong results failed to stabilize the stock. Nvidia closed Friday down 14.01% at $125.53, its worst weekly performance since October 2008 and its seventh consecutive weekly decline.
Since hitting a record market capitalization of about $5.76 trillion on October 6, Nvidia has fallen roughly 47%, shedding more than $2.7 trillion in market value—a decline larger than the entire market capitalization of Amazon (about $2.67 trillion). The company is now worth about $3.03 trillion, having been overtaken by Google and slipping to the fourth-largest US company by market value. Reports said US Securities and Exchange Commission enforcement staff are reviewing potential accounting issues at the company, though the reports did not specify the matters or when the probe began; an investigation itself does not indicate wrongdoing.
Anthropic's highly anticipated initial public offering also failed to play the role of market savior. The developer of the Claude large language model listed on the Nasdaq at an offering price of $83 per share, closing up about 17% at $97.17 on its first day of trading, with its intraday valuation briefly touching $1.5 trillion. But the stock reversed course the next day, falling as low as $83.05 intraday—just 5 cents above the offering price—before closing down 8.5% at $88.91. Underwriters fully exercised the greenshoe option; the IPO raised about $93.5 billion in total, alongside a concurrent private financing of about $26 billion involving Microsoft, Nvidia, Google and others.
Market participants said investors raising cash ahead of the mega-IPO actually intensified selling pressure on other assets. Gold, cryptocurrencies, copper and Nvidia shares were all sold off in the days before the listing.
Chip Stocks Post Worst Week Since 2001
The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, a bellwether for AI hardware, plunged 10.73% on Friday and fell more than 10% for the week, with the chip sector losing about $2.3 trillion in market value overall—its worst weekly performance since 2001. Among individual names, ARM fell 14.09%, AMD dropped 13.25%, Broadcom lost 12.19%, TSMC fell 9.48%, Intel declined 7.97% and Qualcomm dropped 7.45%. Memory and optical communications names also came under pressure, with SanDisk down 16.05%, Micron Technology down 13.89% and South Korea's SK Hynix down 13.11%.
Large-cap technology stocks fell across the board, with the Wind US Magnificent Seven Index down 5.12%. Tesla dropped 7.64%, Meta fell 5.93%, Amazon lost 4.1%, Microsoft declined 2.77%, Google Class C fell 2.59% and Apple dropped 1.9%. The selloff has spread from chips to cloud services and data center infrastructure, with cloud computing company CoreWeave briefly falling below $40 intraday, below its March 2025 IPO price.
China concept stocks were broadly hit, with the Nasdaq Golden Dragon China Index falling 3.37% to 5,018.26. XPeng fell 13.86%, NIO dropped 7.89%, Li Auto lost 7.56%, Baidu declined 6.75%, Xiaomi fell 5.18% and Tencent Holdings dropped 3.76%.
Safe-haven assets offered no shelter. COMEX gold futures fell 3.98% on Friday to $3,539.00 an ounce, down 8.94% for the week; COMEX silver futures dropped 7.39% to $41.08 an ounce, down 16.37% for the week. David Meger, director of metals trading at High Ridge Futures, said losses in the stock market prompted some investors to sell gold to meet liquidity needs. International oil prices rebounded modestly, with WTI crude futures up 0.93% at $83.98 a barrel and Brent crude up 0.64% at $92.57.
Hawkish Fed Stance Compounds Fragile Market Structure
Monetary policy added another layer of pressure. Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson said intraday that her top priority is curbing stubbornly high inflation and that she is open to considering consecutive rate hikes at future policy meetings to bring inflation back to the 2% target. Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook warned that inflation has been above target for more than five and a half years and that delayed policy action would raise the cost of future adjustments, expressing doubt about whether a single 25-basis-point hike in September was sufficient.
According to the CME FedWatch tool, traders' odds of a 25-basis-point hike at the December meeting fell to 91.4% from 100%, while the probability of a cumulative 50 basis points of hikes by the end of January next year dropped to 57.7% from more than 70% a day earlier.
Market structure further amplified the decline. Goldman Sachs' trading desk said the selloff has spread to all types of systematic investors, with the bank's panic index rising to 9 on a 10-point scale, approaching an extreme. It estimated that trend-following traders, including commodity trading advisors, sold a combined $57 billion of stocks over the past month, flipping their net US equity position to short by $19 billion. The bank's model predicts that if the market stays flat, CTAs could still sell $65.8 billion of stocks over the next week; if the decline continues, potential selling could reach $80.6 billion; even a rebound could trigger $28.6 billion in selling.
The bond market sent a different signal. Despite a barrage of hawkish comments from Fed officials, US Treasury yields moved lower, with the 2-year at 4.63%, the 10-year at 4.92% and the 30-year at 5.31%, down 13, 12 and 9 basis points respectively from the prior session. Goldman Sachs strategist Rich Privorotsky said long-end yields may already be starting to reflect the risk of policy error, with the market's focus shifting from inflation to economic growth and liquidity.
Views Diverge on the Outlook
Institutional views are clearly split on whether the correction is nearing an end. Bill Northey, senior investment director at US Bancorp Wealth Management, said sentiment is clearly in the driver's seat, but fundamentals tend to prevail over time, citing factors such as economic strength and solid corporate earnings. Chris Larkin, managing director of trading at E*Trade, noted that of 15 corrections since 1983, only five ultimately became bear markets.
Cautious voices are equally loud. Bank of America's strategy team warned that the market is severely underestimating the risk of a hard landing for the US economy, and that if the Fed maintains its hawkish stance, US stocks could trigger a "doom loop." James Reilly, senior markets economist at Capital Economics, expects the S&P 500 to fall to 6,500 by the end of next year.
Mark Hackett, chief market strategist at Nationwide Financial Services, said the current action recalls market reactions to earlier tariff shocks and the early days of the Middle East conflict—when investor anxiety rose quickly but subsequent developments did not fully bear out the most pessimistic expectations.
Near-term catalysts to watch include: Anthropic stock options begin trading next Monday, which will reveal the strength of retail participation; and November personal consumption expenditures (PCE) data due next week, which will offer more clues on the Fed's policy path after October inflation came in above expectations. Separately, US President Trump said that day that he would tax companies building data centers outside the United States, though the specific rate, timing and legal mechanism remain unclear.
As of Friday's close, all three major US stock indexes had fallen below their 200-day moving averages. Jonathan Krinsky, chief market technician at BTIG, noted that once indexes break below this long-term trend indicator, the risk of further declines typically increases.
