OUTLET: American College of Cardiology

CV Safety of Survodutide in Patients With Obesity and Established CVD or Risk Factors
Treatment with survodutide, a novel glucagon receptor-GLP-1 receptor dual agonist, was not associated with increased cardiovascular risk among patients with obesity and established cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease (CKD) or at increased risk for cardiovascular disease, according to findings from the SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT trial published in The Lancet.
Biykem Bozkurt, MD, PhD, FACC, et al., randomized 5,530 patients (40% female, 79% White) with a BMI ≥27 kg/m2 with either established cardiovascular disease, two risk factors for cardiovascular disease, or CKD with a BMI ≥30 kg/m2 to survodutide 3.6 mg, survodutide 6 mg or placebo. The study was conducted at 524 sites in 34 countries from Nov. 20, 2023, to Nov. 8, 2024. Seeking to determine the cardiovascular safety of survodutide, the study's primary composite endpoint was death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal stroke, nonfatal MI, ischemia-related coronary revascularization or heart failure event.
Over a median time in study of 22 months, 3.7% of patients in the treatment group experienced the primary endpoint compared to 4.1% in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 0.89; 95% CI, 0.67-1.18; p<0.0001 for noninferiority).
Adverse events were observed in 93% of patients receiving survodutide 3.6 mg, 93.1% of patients receiving survodutide 6 mg and 85.6% of patients receiving placebo, with gastrointestinal adverse events occurring more frequently among patients taking survodutide. Treatment discontinuation due to gastrointestinal adverse events were seen in 19.3%, 20.8% and 1.9% of patients, respectively. Both serious and fatal adverse event rates were similar among groups.
"SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT was the largest and longest or the SYNCHRONIZE phase 3 trials and provided a robust assessment of cardiovascular safety of survodutide," write the authors. "The safety profile was characterized by higher rates of gastrointestinal events and treatment discontinuation during dose escalation than those reported for some other therapies containing GLP-1 receptor agonist activity."
