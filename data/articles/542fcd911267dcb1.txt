Meta Pushes Muse Into Instagram DMs and Saved Posts, Tying AI Shopping to Its Ad Engine
Meta Platforms is embedding its personal AI agent Muse directly into Instagram, adding a chat thread in direct messages and prompting users to hand their saved posts over to the assistant for sorting, the company said Tuesday. The rollout covers iOS and Android users in the U.S., Canada, Mexico, Japan and Singapore, with the save-prompt feature initially limited to the U.S. and Canada.
The move puts an ad-free product at the center of Meta's advertising machine. Roughly one in five people who open Instagram on a given day save at least one post, according to a person familiar with the matter who was not authorized to speak publicly, and a large share of those saves are shoppable items or ads. A senior executive, speaking on condition of anonymity because the details have not been disclosed, said Muse is "still very valuable for our ads business because it raises conversions for advertisers" by shortening the path from discovery to purchase and letting shoppers return to items they set aside. "It gives advertisers a way to get more ROI for the dollars they're already spending, and that's all because Muse is helping people shop," the executive said.
On Meta's most recent earnings call, Chief Executive Mark Zuckerberg said a quarter of Muse users shop through the agent, making it the most common use case, with an average purchase above $200. He also said the company intends to collect a small fee on transactions completed through the platform. Everything the agent learns about what people are buying gives Meta another signal for targeting Instagram ads.
Muse has also become the pacesetter in agentic commerce. Forter Intelligence, an e-commerce analytics and cybertechnology firm that tracks such transactions, attributed 51% of all agentic orders in the week ended Nov. 22 to Muse, against 24% for SpaceX's Grok, 22% for OpenAI's ChatGPT and 3% split between Google and the startup Instinct.
Note: Figures from Forter Intelligence, which measures transactions completed through AI shopping agents.
Inside Instagram, Muse will appear as a side chat that syncs with the main Muse app, letting users ask about a post, place or product they encounter, catch up on conversations they missed, or hand over a saved reel with an instruction -- adding a coffee shop to a Mexico City itinerary, for instance, or hunting down a Black Friday price on a pair of shoes. Users can also have the agent group bookmarks into Collections, convert a recipe video into a grocery list, or build a travel plan from clips posted by creators they follow. Starting Tuesday, saving a post triggers a prompt offering to file it away.
The company says users keep control: nothing is shared, published or sent without approval, and they can tell Muse to forget information or reverse an action. Instagram's separate Meta AI chatbot, a general-purpose question-and-answer tool, is not being retired, a spokesperson said; Muse is positioned as more of an assistant.
The Instagram version is deliberately narrower than the standalone app and website. Users can forward to-dos or track Instagram-specific tasks, but seeing what the agent does with their messages still requires leaving for the full Muse product.
The integration follows a pattern Meta has used before, pushing one property's content into another's feed to speed adoption -- the tactic that helped Threads climb past 500 million users. Muse crossed 10 million users last month, and Zuckerberg has said he wants it to reach billions of people and could eventually out-earn Meta's existing family of apps.
Advertising inside Muse is expected eventually, though people familiar with the plans said there is no financial pressure to move quickly and that Meta tends to build an audience before monetizing. A feed-style surface in Muse, closer to a newsfeed than to answers for specific queries, would be the obvious place to test it.
For advertisers, the near-term effect is less about new inventory than about efficiency: saved posts become a measurable funnel, and purchase data from the agent sharpens targeting on Instagram, the company's main revenue engine. For rivals building shopping agents, the Forter numbers show how quickly Meta has converted a large existing user base into transaction volume -- and how much of the agentic checkout market is already spoken for.
