TITLE: Debate on banning CofE clergy from Restore Britain heats up
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Some clergy have begun taking sides on a motion proposing the Church of England distance itself from far-right political party Restore Britain.
The Private Member’s Motion was moved by a lay member of the General Synod, proposing the Church affirm racism is a sin and the its teachings are incompatible with Restore.
If passed, it would mean clergy have to distance themselves from the political party.
Restore Britain's leader, Rupert Lowe, posted a video on social media with a vicar saying the motion needs to be stopped.
“We need conversations, important conversations that Restore are highlighting, in regard to immigration, in regard to grooming gangs, in regard to our Christian heritage,” Rev Luke Dobson said, standing in front of St Paul’s Cathedral.
If the motion reaches at least 100 signatures, the Business Committee can consider putting it before the General Synod for debate. The next Synod is in February.
A Church of England spokesperson said it may take weeks before they can provide an update on how many signatures it has received to date.
Fr Ben Edwards, a clerical member of Synod, told Premier Christian News he is supporting the motion because the public voice of clergy should point people towards unity.
"When political rhetoric undermines the dignity of our neighbours on the basis of race, it runs directly counter to the gospel we are ordained to proclaim," he said. "Passing this motion isn't about party politics - it's about ensuring our public witness remains faithful to Jesus."
Lowe has labelled the Church “woke” over the motion, making several posts about it online.
“If the Church tries to proscribe Restore Britain, they should expect an almighty backlash,” he threatened in one post.
The Church of England’s Clergy Conduct Measure 2026 allows the House of Bishops to make a written declaration that “constitution, policies, objectives, activities or public statements of a political party or organisation specified in the declaration are incompatible with the Church of England’s teaching on the equality of persons or groups of different races.”
Sam Wilson, who brought the motion, said he wants Synod to debate and decide whether Restore meets these criteria.
“Restore knows that when you refer to people's colour, in particular, that it is seen by society and by the law as talking about race.
“They often, though, talk about people's national origins in a way that is just as discriminatory, but allows us often to ignore what is really at the root of what they're saying about people,” he added.
Speaking with Premier, Restore Britain’s head of local government, Maria Bowtell, said she doesn’t see the party’s policy of mass deportation as racist.
“The argument of like loving your neighbour, to me that's very much a priority list where it starts with your family and then it starts with your actual neighbours and your actual community and your county and then your nation,” she said.
When asked by Premier about the parable of the Good Samaritan, Bowtell drew a distinction between the Party’s policy and “personal mercy”.
“God hasn't placed me in India to look after people in India," she said. "God hasn't placed me in Palestine to heal wounded people. God's placed me here to look after people here.”
She backed her leader’s comments about the Church being woke and the motion being “rubbish” and suggested those supporting the motion don’t understand the Party’s policy.
“It's political. This is not based on doctrine. There's no doctrine about that motion.”
In ane earlier statement to Premier, a Church of England spokesperson said: “The submission of a Private Member’s Motion does not constitute a decision of the General Synod or a position of the Church of England.”
