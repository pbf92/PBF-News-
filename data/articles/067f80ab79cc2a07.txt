TITLE: Qualitative Meta-Synthesis Identifies Four Key Unmet Need Domains in COPD Care
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and qualitative meta-synthesis reveals that patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease face persistent, interrelated unmet needs across their care trajectory. Synthesizing data from twenty-two qualitative studies involving 342 patients, investigators identified substantial gaps spanning disease information, emotional well-being, care continuity, and clinical access. These findings show that despite growing attention toward person-centred care, individuals with the condition continue encountering obstacles that hinder self-management and personal well-being. Understanding where healthcare systems fail to meet these essential needs remains vital for improving overall disease management.
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease is a progressive condition requiring sustained long-term management and coordinated clinical support.
To examine patient experiences, the authors searched eleven electronic databases: PubMed, Web of Science, the Cochrane Library, CINAHL, Embase, PsycINFO, MEDLINE, Scopus, CNKI, Wanfang, and VIP. The team gathered qualitative studies reporting patient perceptions and direct experiences of unmet care needs. Data were synthesized using the Thomas and Harden thematic synthesis approach.
The thematic synthesis identified four major interrelated domains of unmet needs. These included unmet informational and understanding needs, unmet emotional and social needs, unmet needs for coordinated and continuous support, and unmet needs in healthcare access and quality.
The authors concluded that overcoming these deficits requires targeted, continuous, and person-centred approaches across healthcare systems. Key priorities include providing ongoing patient education, empathetic social support, coordinated care models, and the active reduction of structural barriers. The investigators noted that their qualitative findings provide direct evidence to inform healthcare policy, optimize service organisation, and assist in designing clinical interventions. Such measures aim to improve the accessibility, continuity, and overall quality of care delivered to patients.
To ensure rigorous methodological standards, the review followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines and the Enhancing Transparency in Reporting the Synthesis of Qualitative Research statement. The study protocol was registered in the PROSPERO database under registration number CRD420261297657 on January 31, 2026.
