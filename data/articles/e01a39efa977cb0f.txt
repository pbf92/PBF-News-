TITLE: Fast fashion, slow parcels: SHEIN to pay the FTC $100m
OUTLET: PPC Land

SHEIN will pay $100 million to settle Federal Trade Commission allegations that the fast-fashion retailer broke its delivery promises, made its customer service almost impossible to reach, held back negative product reviews and could not support what it told shoppers about its suppliers and its cotton. The FTC announced the settlement on November 24, 2026, the same day it filed a complaint and a proposed stipulated order against three SHEIN companies in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California. The money is consumer redress, meaning refunds for affected buyers, not a fine. The order binds SHEIN only once a judge signs it.
In Short
The FTC says SHEIN advertised fast, reliable delivery, shipped nearly one million parcels a day into the U.S., and then left buyers with late or missing orders, no legally required delay notices and almost no way to reach a person. It also says SHEIN fast-tracked glowing reviews while holding back or deleting critical ones, and made claims about factory audits and American-grown cotton that it could not back up. SHEIN neither admits nor denies the allegations, but has agreed to pay $100 million for refunds and to follow detailed rules on delay notices, customer service and reviews for years to come. For any online shop, the order is a plain written record of what the FTC expects when a parcel runs late or a one-star review comes in.
What the FTC says went wrong
The FTC's complaint runs to 38 pages and sets out five counts. It names SHEIN Distribution Corporation, SHEIN US Services LLC and SHEIN Technology LLC, all registered in Delaware and based at 777 Alameda Street in Los Angeles. According to the complaint, the three companies operate as a single business with shared owners, officers and offices. It describes SHEIN as a company founded in China that brings in billions of dollars in revenue a year and accounts for nearly half of fast-fashion sales in the United States.
"SHEIN attracted consumers with promises of affordable clothing, fast delivery and use of suppliers that earned above-average ratings on standards related to child and forced labor, among other representations," said Christopher Mufarrige, Director of the FTC's Bureau of Consumer Protection, in the announcement. "In reality, the company misled millions of consumers about its products, its suppliers and the authenticity of its customer reviews. SHEIN further compounded consumer harm by making it extraordinarily difficult to obtain customer service."
The case rests on three sets of rules, according to the FTC: Section 5 of the FTC Act, which bans unfair and deceptive business practices; the Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule, usually called the Mail Order Rule, which covers anything ordered online, by phone or by post; and the Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, which took effect on October 21, 2024, according to the complaint.
Delivery promises
SHEIN put delivery times almost everywhere a shopper looked, according to the complaint: on every product page, on a dedicated shipping information page, on a QuickShip label for some items and again at checkout. It described its delivery as extremely fast and offered free standard shipping on orders over $29. Order confirmation emails mentioned a processing and shipping time of one to three days.
The reality, as the FTC describes it, was different. "The complaint alleges that SHEIN marketed fast, transparent and reliable shipping but tens of thousands of consumers reported that their orders arrived days, weeks or months late, while some consumers never received their orders at all," the announcement states. Between 2021 and 2024, according to the complaint, complaints about logistics at times made up 53% to 54% of all consumer complaints SHEIN received. Customers turned to the Better Business Bureau and asked their banks for a chargeback, the complaint says.
The Mail Order Rule requires a seller that cannot ship on time to tell the buyer and offer a choice: accept the delay or cancel for a full refund. According to the complaint, SHEIN in many cases did not send notices that met the rule, and it had no records showing systems designed to comply with it. The complaint also says SHEIN changed some practices only after the investigation began. It removed the one-to-three-day processing timeline from its shipping page, and it renamed the QuickShip label "Local".
Five agents, one million parcels
The customer service allegations contain the starkest figures in the case. "According to the FTC's complaint, SHEIN ships nearly one million packages to the United States each day, yet, as of September 2025, the company employed only five customer service agents to handle telephone inquiries from American consumers and failed to provide a phone number for those agents on its website," the announcement states.
The complaint adds that SHEIN received more than 1.2 million customer service inquiries from U.S. customers in September 2025 alone. Customer service ran only through email and chat, according to the FTC. The chat feature rarely worked, sometimes switched off during advertised live-chat hours and ended conversations midway, and answers were generic and often written by AI, the complaint says. SHEIN's own FAQ told customers to wait for a U.S. call center without giving a start date. A 2024 internal analysis cited in the complaint found that 58% of complaints about poor customer service related to slow responses.
Packages marked as delivered created another dead end. According to the complaint, a delivery photo was enough for SHEIN to treat an order as delivered, with no check that it reached the right address. SHEIN told customers to take the problem up with the shipping company or file a chargeback with their bank, even though, under SHEIN's contracts with some shippers, only SHEIN could issue the refund.
Reviews that waited, and reviews that vanished
Reviews sat on every product page, next to star ratings and a review count. SHEIN rewarded them with loyalty points: five points for a written review, 10 more for a photo and two more for size details, according to the complaint, at a rate of 100 points to $1 in 2023. Reviews could be posted only within six months of an order. SHEIN's own research found that fit was one of the top three factors in buying decisions, according to the complaint, which made reviews about sizing especially valuable to shoppers.
Behind the page, every review passed through a system SHEIN called the Global Comment Management System. The complaint describes three stages: an automated screen for irrelevant and sensitive content, an automatic rejection for banned words, and a check against two word lists, one of helpful words and one of unhelpful words.
SHEIN's published review policy said reviews were not removed simply for being critical of a product, according to the complaint. The FTC's announcement summarises the result: SHEIN was "publishing positive reviews while suppressing or deleting reviews from customers who criticized or returned SHEIN's products."
evoluSHEIN and the missing audits
evoluSHEIN is the company's line of products sold on social and environmental claims. According to the complaint, it launched in 2022 with 150 products and at times listed more than 15,000 products a month. SHEIN told shoppers that every evoluSHEIN product came from suppliers that earned above-average scores in social compliance audits, which check factories for child labor, forced labor, wages and safety.
SHEIN said those audits were unannounced, on-site and covered 141 inspection points, and that it ran 2,812 of them in 2022 and almost 4,000 in 2023, according to the complaint. The FTC says SHEIN never produced a single one. "Although the company marketed evoluSHEIN products as sourced from suppliers with above-average ratings in social-compliance audits designed to protect against child and forced labor, the FTC alleges that SHEIN failed to provide a single audit that supported those claims," the announcement states.
Instead, SHEIN relied on letters from its accounting firm, according to the complaint. Those letters only listed how audit grades were spread across the A to D scale. The accounting firm told SHEIN it had not checked the conclusions of the audits themselves. Its 2024 letter found that 29% of the audit scores on SHEIN's list were an A or a B, and did not link any of those scores to evoluSHEIN suppliers.
Cotton labelled American, tested as Chinese
SHEIN told shoppers its suppliers sourced cotton only from Australia, Brazil, India, the United States and other approved regions, and that it kept strict documentation tracing materials back to their source, according to the complaint. The FTC says SHEIN's contracts with its cotton testing services listed one other approved region: eastern China. The complaint notes that SHEIN had faced questions from several governments about cotton grown by forced labor.
Testing undercut the claims, according to the FTC. "For example, cotton that SHEIN represented as U.S.-grown was found to originate from China, and cotton used in at least some of its products could not be traced to any country of origin at all," the announcement states. The complaint says more than 7% of the cotton samples SHEIN submitted could not be traced to any country, and that for the other 93% the testing firm could not fully establish where the cotton came from, including whether any came from China, because the data could not be separated.
How the order handles a late parcel
Section I of the proposed order sets out what SHEIN must do when an online order cannot ship on time. Its wording closely follows the Mail Order Rule itself, but writing it into a court order means a breach would also be a violation of that order. According to the order, the process has five steps.
- Before taking the order. SHEIN must have "a reasonable basis to expect" it can ship by the date it states. If it states no date, the deadline is "within 30 days after Receipt of a Properly Completed Order", extended to 50 days when the buyer applies for credit from the seller to pay for the order.
- The first delay notice. If SHEIN cannot meet that date, it must offer the buyer, "Clearly and Conspicuously and without prior demand, an option either to consent to a delay in shipping or to cancel the buyer's order and receive a Prompt Refund." The offer must come no later than the original shipping date, and it must give a new definite shipping date or say that SHEIN cannot predict the length of the delay.
- What silence means. This depends on the length of the delay. If the new date is no more than 30 days after the original one, a buyer who does not reply "will be deemed to have consented to a delayed Shipment." If it is more than 30 days later, or open-ended, the order "will automatically be deemed to have been cancelled" unless SHEIN ships within 30 days of the original date or the buyer specifically agrees to wait.
- Further delays. If SHEIN misses the revised date, it must offer a renewed choice. This time silence works the other way: unless the buyer specifically agrees to wait longer, the buyer "will be deemed to have rejected any further delay, and to have cancelled the order."
- The refund. A cancelled order must be refunded promptly, which the order defines as "within seven (7) working days" for most payment methods, or within one billing cycle where SHEIN itself extended the credit. SHEIN must provide a way to cancel "at the Defendants' expense", and under Section II that mechanism "must not be difficult, confusing, or time consuming."
