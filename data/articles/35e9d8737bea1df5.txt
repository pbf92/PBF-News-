OUTLET: India Today

Pochettino hits back at criticism of his Man United management style: I'm not nice
Manchester United manager Mauricio Pochettino has hit back at criticism of his management style after Jamie Redknapp suggested he had become "too nice". Pochettino dismissed the suggestion ahead of United's Premier League clash against Brentford.
Manchester United manager Mauricio Pochettino has hit back at suggestions that he has become too soft, insisting that there is a difference between being nice and being good.
Pochettino made the comments during his press conference ahead of Saturday's Premier League clash against Brentford at Old Trafford. His response came after former England midfielder Jamie Redknapp questioned whether the United boss had lost the edge he showed during his spells at Southampton and Tottenham.
"When I saw Pochettino at Southampton and I saw him at Tottenham, he looked to me like he had that Argentinian dog in him," Redknapp said on The Overlap with Tim Sherwood.
"I look at him now and he's high fiving everyone, he's got the bracelets on, he's got the barnet. I think he's gone too nice, I do."
Pochettino was not asked directly about Redknapp's comments but made it clear that he did not agree with the assessment.
"Do not misunderstand my words. Sometimes we are confusing two words that mean maybe something similar; nice and good," he said.
"I am not nice. I am not nice. We have to be good. That is a different thing. I am not nice. I am trying to be good.
"We know when we need to push, when we need to be strong and when we need to build something to improve the atmosphere and relationship and everything. These two things are different."
The Argentine has had little time to settle into his new job, with United playing Liverpool and Sporting CP in his first week in charge. They lost 4-0 at Anfield before drawing 2-2 against Sporting in the Champions League.
Pochettino has already stepped up the workload at Carrington, introducing longer training sessions as he looks to turn things around. He also acknowledged that lifting the players' confidence will take time, given the pressure surrounding the club.
"It's easy to arrive here with full energy and be positive and try to build that belief that we need. But of course for the players it's so tough because we cannot go away from all that noise that is around this football club," he said.
"It's not only in this football club. It's in every single club that is not performing well. That is why it's so tough for the players."
Saturday's game against Brentford will be Pochettino's first home match as United manager. He is also yet to meet former United boss Sir Alex Ferguson, whom he counts among his managerial role models.
Meanwhile, captain Bruno Fernandes is a doubt for the game after picking up a virus in Portugal and finishing the Sporting match with a physical problem. The midfielder was unable to train on Friday.
"We don't know yet. He picked up a virus in Portugal, he finished the game with a problem and he couldn't train today. We'll see how he is during the day and tomorrow when he wakes up, and we won't take any risks with him," Pochettino said.
