TITLE: Anthropic IPO: 6-Month Banker Rival Ban Explained
OUTLET: explainx.ai

Reuters on November 22, 2026 reported new details about how Anthropic ran its IPO, internally called Project Lantern, after Nasdaq trading began under ticker ANT. The news agency says Anthropic capped some underwriting teams at four senior bankers, asked some bank officers to sign six-month restrictions on work with OpenAI, Cursor, DeepSeek and Hugging Face, and used NetRoadshow watermarks to track who read which pages. Anthropic, Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley declined to comment, per the same report summarized by Unite.AI.
If you build on Claude, none of this changes your API bill. It does explain why the largest AI listing of 2026 felt unlike a standard tech IPO — and why banks complained privately even while earning more than $500 million in fees.
TL;DR
What Reuters reported about banker rival curbs
The November 22 Reuters story, as relayed by Unite.AI and trade press, sits after Anthropic priced and began trading. That timing matters: these are process disclosures, not offering terms in an S-1.
Reuters attributes four concrete claims to people close to the deal:
- Team caps. Anthropic told some underwriters to keep no more than four senior bankers on the transaction. One source interpreted that as leak prevention; another said banks found workarounds.
- Signed rival limits. Anthropic asked some senior officers at participating banks to accept legal documents covering core team members who could not work with OpenAI, Cursor, DeepSeek or Hugging Face for six months post-listing.
- NetRoadshow controls. Decks went through NetRoadshow with watermarked names, page-view telemetry, and no local saves — a reaction, the report says, to Reuters itself publishing draft registration details weeks before the public S-1.
- Compressed approvals. Before the planned November 10, 2026 marketing launch, banks received a wrong Canadian share-sale PDF, then a corrected registration statement near 2 a.m. New York time, leaving hours to approve before SEC filing — per an email Reuters says it reviewed.
Anthropic needed a wide bank group because it was raising at a scale closer to SpaceX's June 2026 IPO than a typical SaaS debut. Reuters frames investor demand as so strong that banks accepted friction they would reject from smaller issuers — a point Jay Ritter, the University of Florida IPO researcher, makes in the piece.

Why banks say broad rival bans are rare
Summaries of the Reuters piece quote bankers and lawyers who stress that conflict walls are normal — you do not let Team A hand Team B live draft numbers. What is unusual here is breadth and duration:
- The named rivals span frontier labs, coding agents, and open-model hubs — categories that share underwriters because they all need growth equity and IPO paths.
- A six-month personal restriction on covering or engaging named peers is tighter than a standard Chinese wall between two live deals.
- At least one banker declined to sign, Reuters says, because staffing could not promise zero contact while rivals pursue their own listings.
IPO scholar Ritter told Reuters that SpaceX and Anthropic both negotiated zero spread on greenshoe shares — another sign issuers with oversubscribed books can rewrite fee norms. He doubted most companies could copy either tactic without similar demand.

Leaks, watermarks and why NetRoadshow showed up
Reuters ties Anthropic's caution to its own earlier exclusives on Anthropic finances and risk factors before the effective S-1. That feedback loop explains NetRoadshow:
- Per-user watermarks make post-leak forensics easier.
- Page analytics show which sections banks actually read before 2 a.m. approvals.
- No download pushes work into controlled rooms — inconvenient for bankers, aligned with issuer paranoia.
Leaks continued anyway, the report says. Technical controls do not replace insider discipline; they only raise the stakes after a breach.
