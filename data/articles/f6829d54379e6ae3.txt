TITLE: Converted product name
OUTLET: PPC Land

Converted product name is a dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that labels each credited purchase with the title of the product the shopper actually bought. It exists because the product in an ad and the product in the basket often differ: a click on an ad for a vacuum cleaner may end in the purchase of a replacement filter, and Amazon will usually credit that sale to the campaign. The item's identifier, the Amazon Standard Identification Number (ASIN), is a 10-character code that few people can read at a glance. The name field puts a human-readable title beside it, so a report can show that a campaign sold filters, not vacuums, without a separate catalogue lookup.
Where the field sits
Amazon's unified reporting dimensions page, a Support Center reference updated on November 11, 2026, lists 156 attributes in 11 categories. The Product category holds 18 of them. Nine describe the advertised product, the item an ad promoted. Eight describe the converted product: ID, name, parent ID, brand, category, subcategory, product group and marketplace. The eighteenth, Product relevance, records whether a credited purchase was of a promoted product or a halo product.
PPC Land's field-by-field reading of the reference found that the converted product attributes apply to all five ad products listed on the page - DVA+, Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display and Sponsored TV - and that they "mostly inherit DVA+ names such as 'Item name,' 'Parent ASIN' and 'Brand name.'" DVA+ is Amazon's AI-led campaign type for display, video and audio buying; Amazon renamed its demand-side platform (DSP) to DVA on September 29, 2026. The name field descends from the DSP's "Item name" column, while Converted product ID was "ASIN" in the DSP and "Purchased ASIN" in the sponsored ads consoles.
That breadth is the main structural difference from the advertised side. Advertised product attributes carry no Sponsored Brands marker, and Advertised product name carries the Sponsored Products marker alone, by PPC Land's count. Converted product name therefore reaches ad products whose advertised titles the report cannot show. A Sponsored Brands ad features several products at once, but every purchase it is credited with still has one converted product.
Amazon's reference also says: "We do not support Streaming TV Smart mode/ Sponsored TV yet." As of November 2026, a Sponsored TV marker is not a promise of populated rows.
How a value reaches the row
The field is a label describing figures in metric columns such as purchases, units sold and sales.
Step one is the ad interaction. A shopper clicks or views an ad. Lookback windows vary by product. Amazon's attribution documentation sets 14 days for Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display and vendor Sponsored Products, and 7 days for Sponsored Products run by sellers, with Sponsored Products counting clicks only. The DSP used a "last-touch model with 14-day lookback window," according to Amazon's August 2022 self-service reporting guide.
Step two is the credit. If a purchase follows within the window, Amazon decides which campaign receives it. Its documented order ranks promoted product clicks first, then clicks on "highly relevant" halo products, then views, and other halo interactions last. Highly relevant means products within the same brand and subcategory as a promoted ASIN.
Step three is the identifier. The ASIN of the purchased item fills Converted product ID.
Step four is the name. The title attached to that ASIN in Amazon's catalogue fills Converted product name. Brand, category and subcategory come from the same record.
A worked example shows why the title matters. A vendor runs a Sponsored Products campaign promoting one coffee machine. Over 14 days it is credited with 200 purchases. Grouped by Converted product name, the report shows 120 for the machine itself, 50 for a descaling kit from the same brand, and 30 for a milk frother. Product relevance marks the first row as promoted and the other two as halo.
There is one structural restriction. "Traffic metrics such as impressions and clicks aren't available with Converted product," according to Adbrew, an Amazon advertising software vendor whose October 2026 reporting guide maps legacy reports to unified ones. Impressions and clicks belong to the ad; a converted product exists only once a purchase has happened.
Origin and evolution
The field has two ancestors, one on each side of Amazon's advertising business.
On the sponsored ads side, the ancestor is the purchased product report. Amazon's Support Center describes it as detailing "the non-advertised SKUs a customer purchased after clicking your ad," available to sellers running Sponsored Products and Display campaigns, with a 60-day lookback. Its key column was Purchased ASIN, "Non-advertised ASIN purchased by the customer attributable to your campaign." It carried halo metrics but no product title. Sponsored Brands had a parallel Attributed Purchases report, with a column separating promoted from brand-halo purchases. On June 6, 2023, Amazon extended that report to book publishers, giving an ASIN-level breakdown for Sponsored Brands campaigns created after February 11, 2023.
Titles had to be joined after download: in October 2023, Amazon staff on the company's advertising API forum on GitHub pointed developers to the product metadata API for product names.
On the DSP side, product names were already in the report. Amazon's August 2022 DSP reporting guide shows a product dimension report organised by ASIN, parent ASIN, brand name and "ASIN conversion type," with "Promoted" and "Brand halo" as values. Brand halo, the guide says, "expands attribution to all ASINs that have the same brand name(s) as the ASINs tracked by the order."
Unified reporting merged the two. Amazon opened the system in beta on November 11, 2025 and moved it to general availability on June 8, 2026. ASIN conversion type became Product relevance. Adbrew states that advertised and converted product data are available "from February 13, 2025," with earlier dates returning blank. Amazon has not published that date in the pages consulted for this article.
Why it matters
Halo sales decide whether a campaign is selling what it advertises. For Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display, Amazon attributes purchases of any product from the same brands as the advertised products. For seller Sponsored Products, it credits other products sold by the same merchant. A campaign can look efficient on return on ad spend (ROAS) while most of its credited sales come from items it never featured.
The name field turns that from an abstract percentage into a list. Merchandising teams can see which accessories ride on which hero products and whether a promotion for a new item is mainly moving an old one.
The deadline makes this practical. Amazon's legacy report pages stop accepting new or edited reports on December 17, 2026 and close on January 31, 2027, when unmigrated reports will be lost. Teams that relied on the purchased product report need the unified field instead.
Limitations and disputes
The definition is thin. Amazon's reference page renders client-side and did not load for this article. PPC Land's analysis quotes the definition of Converted product marketplace, "the marketplace where the conversion event for the attributed product occurred," but not a separate definition of the name field. Whether it holds the full product title or the shorter "Item name" is not documented publicly.
Titles move. Amazon introduced a two-part title format in April 2025, separating the item name from marketing highlights. It then enforced a 75-character item name from July 27, 2026, with AI rewrites and a 14-day review window, and more than 984 million titles had been updated by the end of July. Amazon has not said whether a report row shows the title at the time of purchase or the title when the report runs. A 15-month daily report could show both.
Names are not keys. Several variants can share a title, and titles differ by marketplace and language.
Attribution is not causation. The field names the product Amazon credited, under Amazon's rules. PPC Land's halo effect explainer notes the same limitation for halo reporting generally: a credited purchase is not proof the ad caused it. From January 1, 2026, Amazon moved view-based attribution in its store to a shorter, shopping-signal model, which changes which purchases, and so which product names, appear.
Commonly confused terms
- Converted product name vs Advertised product name. The advertised field is the title of the product in the ad, marked for Sponsored Products alone. The converted field is the title of the product bought, marked for all five ad products. In a promoted row the two match; in a halo row they differ.
- Converted product name vs Converted product ID. The ID is the purchased item's ASIN and the stable key. The name is the readable label attached to it.
- Converted product name vs Converted product parent ID. The parent ID rolls sizes and colours into one variation family; the name describes a single child item.
- Converted product vs Converted currency. Converted currency names the currency in which money metrics are displayed. It has nothing to do with products bought.
Recent developments
PPC Land published its count of the 156 dimensions on November 12, 2026, a day after Amazon updated the reference, recording the converted product attributes' DSP-derived names and their reach across all five ad products. On November 17, Amazon revised its data availability rules, and PPC Land reported that daily unified reports are capped at 120 days per pull from 24 months of history, with monthly data reaching back 72 months. As of November 2026, Amazon's general availability notice still gives December 31, 2026 for the legacy sunset, while its Support Center gives January 31, 2027.
