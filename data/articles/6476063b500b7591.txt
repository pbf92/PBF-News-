TITLE: Amazon draws 24% of retailer searches but 11% of AI shopping answers
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon accounts for 24% of retailer searches in US on-site search data but is named in only 11% of answers produced by AI assistants in US shopping conversations, according to a 30-page holiday report that advertising technology company Verve released today. The report arrives eight days before Black Friday on November 27; its figures cover September 1 to November 16, 2026.
In Short
Verve compared how often US shoppers search for big retailers with how often AI assistants name those retailers in their answers, and Amazon came out with the widest gap: 24% of retailer searches against 11% of AI answers. Target went the other way, with 8% of AI answers on a 5% search share, while Walmart sat at 9% against 12%. The report measures mentions, not recommendations or sales, and it does not explain the gap, so it shows where AI assistants put retailer names this season rather than why.
The retailer gap
The comparison sits on a single slide in the report's AI conversations section, under the headline "Amazon Is the Most Frequently Named Retailer in AI Shopping Answers." That headline is accurate. Amazon is still the retailer AI assistants name most often in Verve's data, ahead of Walmart and Target. What stands out is the distance between that position and the company's weight in search.
According to Verve, the chart sets the percentage of AI answers naming each retailer against that retailer's share of retailer searches, both measured between September 1 and November 16, 2026. Eight retailers appear.
Source: Verve, US AI conversation data and on-site search data, 09/01/2026 - 11/16/2026. The last column is PPC Land's arithmetic on the rounded figures Verve published.
Only Amazon and Walmart appear in AI answers less often than their search share would imply. Every other retailer on the list matches or exceeds it. Etsy, which registers 0% of retailer searches after rounding, is named in 2% of AI answers, and Target, a distant third among the eight in search, nearly draws level with Walmart once the assistant starts talking.
Verve's footnote to the slide carries a caveat in six words: "a mention is not a recommendation." An answer that says an item is cheaper elsewhere than on Amazon still counts as a mention of Amazon. So does an answer that names Amazon only to steer a shopper away from it.
Two bases, one comparison
The two columns are not built on the same denominator. The search figure is a share of searches that name a retailer, so it describes how attention divides among retailers once a shopper has a store in mind. The AI figure is a percentage of AI answers, and the report does not say what share of those answers name any retailer at all. If a large portion of holiday answers name no store, every retailer's AI percentage is pushed down by the same base effect.
The eight AI figures add up to 39%, against 47% for the eight search figures (PPC Land calculations; an answer can name more than one retailer, so the AI total is not a share of answers that name any store). That does not dissolve Amazon's gap. Walmart, with half Amazon's search share, sits only two points behind it in AI answers, and Target overtakes its own search share outright. But it does mean that a simple ratio between the two columns should be read as a rough measure of position, not a precise discount.
Verve presents the gap as an early sign that AI assistants are changing which retailers shoppers hear about this holiday season. The report contains no earlier period for AI answers by retailer, so it does not show a change over time. It shows a difference between two channels in the same ten-and-a-half-week window.
There is also a second Amazon search figure in the report. On a separate slide, Amazon accounts for 18.5% of Black Friday and Cyber Monday searches that name a retailer, followed by Costco at 9.7% and Best Buy at 7.9%. That chart is limited to Black Friday and Cyber Monday queries, and its footnote restricts it to "retailers whose searches do not come mainly from one site." Walmart does not appear on it. Costco, second in that ranking, does not appear among the eight retailers in the AI comparison. The report does not explain either omission.
How the report was built
The report, titled "Holiday Shopping Warms Up as Black Friday Searches Surge" and marked "Insights · US" and "November 2026" on its cover, draws on two data sets. According to the source notes on its 30 pages, the method works as follows:
- On-site search data (US). Searches typed into the search boxes of websites, labeled "on site search data (US)" and described as a "same-site panel," which indicates the same set of sites is compared across periods. The number of sites and searches is not given.
- US AI conversation data. Prompts written to AI assistants and the answers returned, labeled "US AI conversation data." The report does not name the assistants or state how many prompts or users the data covers.
- Periods. Most charts cover September 1 to November 16, 2026. Momentum charts compare the "latest 14 days" (November 3 to November 16, 2026) with September 2026 (September 1 to 30). The monthly AI chart runs from January 1 to November 16, 2026.
- Momentum index. A topic's share of holiday shopping searches in the latest 14 days divided by its share in September. According to the report, 1.00x means "keeping pace" or "no change."
- Personas. Four shopper types defined by keywords. According to Verve, "Personas are keyword-defined and can overlap; shares are of shoppers with any holiday-shopping search since 09/01/2026." Persona indexes divide a persona's share by the share among all holiday shoppers.
The report does not name the products behind either data set. Verve bought Captify, a London-based search intelligence company, in September 2025 for a total of 25.6 million euros, and at the time described Captify as analyzing the search behavior of up to one billion daily searches. On March 10, 2026, Verve announced that it had begun selling advertisers signals from AI chat activity, drawn from opted-in users who consent to share pseudonymized chat activity through apps they already use, and aggregated before use. Captify sells ad targeting built on search intent, which Verve said works without third-party cookies, a search-driven form of contextual advertising. Neither the March announcement nor the holiday report names the AI platforms from which the conversations are drawn.
Black Friday searches at 11.8 times September
The report's opening section tracks how fast event-driven searches are rising. Combined Black Friday and Cyber Monday searches reached 11.82 times their September share of US search activity over November 3 to 16, and more than tripled compared with the preceding two weeks, according to Verve.
Source: Verve, on-site search data (US), same-site panel.
Cyber Monday's rise outpaced Black Friday's. Broader holiday shopping, at 1.40x, grew far more slowly, which Verve reads as acceleration concentrated around the two sale events rather than spread across the season.
AI conversations moved in the same direction, though less sharply. Black Friday prompts in the US AI data reached 4.30 times their September level in November, against 11.31 times for Black Friday searches over the same comparison, according to the report. The 11.31x figure is a November-versus-September comparison and differs from the 12.28x figure above, which uses the latest 14 days.
The monthly AI series also complicates the idea of a holiday surge in chat. Holiday-shopping prompts per 10,000 US AI prompts stood at 21.6 in January 2026, fell to 16.5 in April, and climbed to 22.0 in November through the 16th. November is about 20% above September's 18.4 but less than 2% above January (PPC Land calculations). According to Verve, "Holiday gift planning also appears throughout the year in AI conversations."
Source: Verve, US AI conversation data, 01/01/2026 - 11/16/2026.
What shoppers ask AI assistants
Gift ideas dominate. Of US holiday-shopping AI prompts between September 1 and November 16, 42.2% asked for gift ideas, 31.5% hunted for deals or asked whether a price was good, and 27.8% concerned a budget or spending plan, according to Verve. A prompt can carry several requests, so the categories overlap.
Source: Verve, US AI conversation data, 09/01/2026 - 11/16/2026.
The contrast with search is in the detail. A median AI gift prompt runs to 24 words; a median gift search, nine. Of AI gift prompts, 7.2% state a budget, compared with 0.49% of gift searches. The median budget stated to an AI assistant was $50, against $57 in gift searches that named a price, according to the report.
Recipients show the same pattern. Depending on the recipient group, between 4% and 14% of AI gift prompts name it, while no group appears in more than 1% of gift searches.
Source: Verve, US AI conversation data and on-site search data, 09/01/2026 - 11/16/2026.
That level of detail is what Verve sells to advertisers as conversational intent signals. A prompt that names a recipient, a budget and a category carries more purchase intent signal than a nine-word search.
When an AI gift answer named no brand in the prompt, Xbox was the product most often suggested, in 1.4% of unbranded gift answers, followed by iPad and LEGO at 0.7% each, AirPods at 0.5%, Pokémon cards at 0.4%, PlayStation 5 and Apple Watch at 0.3%, and Squishmallows at 0.2%. In search, by contrast, Xbox's share of holiday shopping fell to 0.91x its September level and PlayStation 5's to 0.82x.
Retailers in search: Amazon leads, others gain faster
Amazon's lead in search is not matched by momentum. Its share of holiday shopping searches rose only 1.09x between September and the latest 14 days, the smallest increase among the retailers tracked, according to Verve. Searches naming Kay, Zales, Pandora and Kendra Scott, counted together, gained 2.74x.
Source: Verve, on-site search data (US), share of holiday-shopping searches naming the retailer with a shopping word.
The report also splits retailer searches by what shoppers attach to the name. Best Buy is the most deal-led: 89% of its shopping searches include a deal word. Amazon is the most research-led, with 27% containing a compare, best-of or review word, while 70% of its shopping searches include a deal word.
Source: Verve, on-site search data (US), 09/01/2026 - 11/16/2026, retailers named in at least 1% of retailer shopping searches.
The research-led profile matters for the AI comparison. Shoppers who search "Amazon" plus "best" or "review" are doing the same kind of comparison work that AI assistants now absorb. If that work migrates to chat and the assistant names Amazon less often than the search box does, the retailer loses a slice of visibility at the point of consideration. The report does not test that sequence. It only shows the two snapshots side by side.
Gifts, recipients and price points
Beyond retailers, the report tracks what shoppers search for. Among gift searches naming a product category, books, e-readers and media lead at 1.0%, followed by video games and consoles at 0.9% and beauty, skincare and fragrance at 0.6%. Beauty has gained the most ground since September, at 1.62x, ahead of laptops and computers at 1.41x and clothing and outerwear at 1.32x. Video games and consoles (0.73x), shoes, boots and slippers (0.71x) and books (0.84x) lost share.
Teens and tweens are the fastest-rising gift recipient in search, at 6.15 times their September share, followed by parents, in-laws and family (4.32x), her or women (3.62x), partners (3.39x) and friends and siblings (2.89x). Searches for gifts for him or men fell to 0.54x and for mom to 0.78x.
Among gift searches that state a price, such as "gifts under $50," the median is $57, according to Verve. Some 48% fall between $51 and $100, 24% between $26 and $50, 22% at $25 or less, 1% between $101 and $250, and 4% above $250.
Products tell a narrower story. Nintendo Switch 2 (1.09x) and LEGO (1.07x) slightly outpaced
