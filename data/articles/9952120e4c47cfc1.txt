Disney Stock Moves Higher: What's Going On?
Walt Disney Co. DIS shares are rising Wednesday. Analysts have been raising their price targets after the company delivered better-than-expected fiscal fourth-quarter results.
- Walt Disney stock is showing upward movement. Why is DIS stock advancing?
Disney Stock Rises After Earnings Beat and Target Raises
Disney delivered adjusted earnings of $1.77 per share, beating the $1.68 consensus in Disney’s fiscal fourth quarter. Revenue grew 16% from a year earlier to $25.95 billion, clearing the $25.16 billion analysts projected.
Operating income across the company’s segments totaled $4.97 billion, up 43%. Revenue from on-demand video subscriptions rose 19%, and the Experiences unit, which spans its parks, resorts, cruise lines and consumer-products business, brought in $10.1 billion, a 15% gain.
Management repeated its goal of double-digit adjusted per-share growth in fiscal 2027, excluding last year’s longer calendar.
Seven analysts responded with price target increases. The stock carries a Buy rating with an average price forecast of $133.50.
Needham analyst Laura Martin maintained a Buy and raised the target to $135 from $125, while Rosenblatt analyst Barton Crockett also kept a Buy and lifted the target to $141 from $126. B of A Securities analyst Jessica Ehrlich likewise maintained a Buy and raised the target to $135 from $125.
Guggenheim analyst Michael Morris kept a Buy and moved the target to $135 from $120, and Barclays analyst Kannan Venkateshwar kept an Overweight and went to $135 from $115.
Wells Fargo analyst Steven Cahall maintained an Overweight with a $140 target, up from $132, while JP Morgan analyst David Karnovsky kept an Overweight and raised the target to $140 from $137.
Analysts Point to Content, Parks and Cost Discipline
Morris said every business segment brought in more revenue than expected. Toy Story 5 has passed the $1 billion mark worldwide, and Disney+ audiences for Marvel content rose more than twofold in the run-up to Avengers: Doomsday on Dec. 18.
Crockett calculated that sales rose 8% once the extra week is removed, with adjusted earnings up 43% on the same footing. Crockett wrote that streaming growth continues “to offset maturity in linear TV.”
Martin said Disney plans to channel $3 billion in savings into marketing, content and technology, a move “suggesting an ROIC discipline.” The catch, Martin noted, is that Disney will stop reporting direct-to-consumer profit and that most of its fiscal 2027 growth will arrive in the first six months.
