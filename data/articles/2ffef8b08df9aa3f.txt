TITLE: Bev Craig calls for powers to help 40,000 youngsters into work
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

Bev Craig calls for more mayoral control to help 40,000 Greater Manchester youngsters not ‘learning or earning’ into work
The city region Mayor said the current system is ‘letting young people down’
Bev Craig has said a joined up Greater Manchester system is ‘best placed’ to help the 40,000 youngsters in the city region who are not ‘earning or learning’. The Greater Manchester mayor’s comments come after the government backed plans by former minister Alan Milburn for sweeping reforms to prevent ‘losing a generation’ of young people to a life on benefits.
Milburn has suggested spending billions on tailored support for under 25s not in employment, education or training (NEETS), to be part-funded by cuts to some benefits.
He claims his plan, which proposes tax breaks for firms and major education reforms, will get 440,000 young people into jobs by 2036.

Speaking on her weekly phone in programme on BBC Radio Manchester, Ms Craig said the current system is ‘letting young people down’.
“Across Greater Manchester, there are just under 40,000 young people who aren’t earning or learning,” she said.
“Most of them want to be. But we see a system that nationally is just broken. “It doesn’t support people, and I’ve long made the case that be it support from 14, be it work experience, work placements, or the chance of an apprenticeship, there’s an opportunity for Greater Manchester to have a system that we design that actually supports people, gets them into work, and gets them earning.
“I think we’re best place to be able to do that, rather than at the minute the system where there’s a little bit in the Department of Work and Pensions, a bit at the job centre, a bit at school, a bit at college, and young people don’t know where to turn.
“I think we can join that all up, do something better, and get the kids into work.”
Prime Minister and Makerfield MP Andy Burnham said he endorsed the ‘principles’ of the Milburn review and was confident he could persuade Labour MPs to back legislation to implement its measures.
Ms Craig was challenged on the expense of a Greater Manchester system.
She said: “No, it doesn’t come free. “What Milburn’s done is made the case across the country.

“For every £25 we spend on welfare in this country, we spend only a £1 getting people into work. “That just doesn’t make sense in terms of finances.
“You put a bit more money up front in terms of the support you offer people, you give them priority access.
“We shouldn’t have young people waiting for years just to access mental health support or get an ADHD diagnosis or any of that kind of stuff.
“Those are things that hold people back.”
She said doubling the number of apprenticeships in Greater Manchester would ‘be a good start’.
She added: “I think there’s also a point about giving our young people the skills they need to be work ready, different kinds of training and changing what happens in schools and colleges.
“Skills like bricklaying, plumbing, plastering, getting them into building trade, getting them work ready.”
