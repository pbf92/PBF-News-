TITLE: World Antimicrobial Resistance Awareness Week 2026: “Only together can we combat this pandemic”
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Over the course of decades, some pathogens have developed clever strategies to protect themselves from the lethal effects of antibiotics. Infections caused by such resistant bacteria are now very difficult to treat. The occurrence of antimicrobial resistance is particularly well-known in clinical settings, where hospital-acquired infections can pose a danger especially to immunocompromised patients. Katharina Schaufler, head of the department “Epidemiology and Ecology of Antimicrobial Resistance” at HIOH, therefore warns: “Antibiotic resistance is a silent pandemic: In our daily lives, we don’t notice the antibiotic resistance crisis, but the whole world is affected.” The microbiologist and veterinarian aims to educate different stakeholders on the topic: “Only together can we combat this pandemic.”
Antibiotic-resistant pathogens and resistance genes are not limited to humans; they occur practically everywhere. That is why the One Health approach is so important, according to Schaufler. “These pathogens are found in humans, animals, and the environment. They are also transmitted between these sectors. Therefore, in order to better understand and combat the pathogens, it is not enough to look at just one dimension.” For example, Katharina Schaufler and her team are investigating how clinically relevant pathogens spread. To do this, she analyzes environmental samples such as water from the Greifswald Bodden and the wild birds living there.
Schaufler is also actively involved in science communication to share knowledge about antibiotic resistance and the One Health approach with the public. In a citizen science project, she is working with high school students to investigate which antibiotic-resistant bacteria can be found in and on flies and are spread by them. In another project, Schaufler and her team are currently developing a playful educational trail on the topic of antibiotic resistance at the Greifswald Bodden.

Helmholtz Centre for Infection Research:
Scientists at the Helmholtz Centre for Infection Research (HZI) in Braunschweig and its other sites in Germany are engaged in the study of bacterial and viral infections and the body’s defense mechanisms. They have a profound expertise in natural compound research and its exploitation as a valuable source for novel anti-infectives. As member of the Helmholtz Association and the German Center for Infection Research (DZIF) the HZI performs translational research laying the ground for the development of new treatments and vaccines against infectious diseases. www.helmholtz-hzi.de/en
