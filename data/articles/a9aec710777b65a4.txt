TITLE: MNTN advertisers' site traffic rose in 15 of 26 industries after Christmas
OUTLET: PPC Land

MNTN, the New York Stock Exchange-listed company that sells streaming television advertising to small and mid-sized businesses, published research on November 23, 2026 arguing that the weeks after Christmas deserve a campaign plan of their own. Drawing on advertisers that used its platform between November 2025 and January 2026, the company reports that site traffic for the median advertiser rose in 15 of 26 industries during the period marketers call Q5, which MNTN defines as December 26 through January 31, compared with the holiday season before it. Conversions credited to MNTN campaigns rose 12% in the week after Christmas against the week before, and shoppers kept spending about as much per order as they had during the holidays.
In Short
MNTN, which sells TV ads on streaming services, says that brands advertising on its platform kept drawing website visits and sales between December 26, 2025 and January 31, 2026, with traffic up in 15 of 26 industries compared with the weeks before Christmas. That matters to any business that switches its ads off after the holidays, because the gains reached well beyond gyms, into hotels, home improvement, law offices, real estate and insurance, while gift categories such as jewelry and footwear cooled. The numbers come from one ad seller's own customers, and MNTN itself calls them correlational, so they show what moved together on its platform, not proof that January ads caused the sales.
What MNTN measured
The piece, titled "Gentler Goals and Budget-Minded Shoppers Shape Q5" and written by Cat Hausler, mixes two kinds of evidence. One is MNTN's own platform data. The other is a set of outside surveys and market figures, from Nielsen to Bank of America, that the company uses to frame its findings. The page carries no visible date; its metadata gives November 23, 2026.
A methodology note sits at the foot of the article. According to MNTN, its figures "come from advertisers on MNTN's platform between November 2025 and January 2026, using matched cohorts of advertisers active across each comparison window." In plain terms, matched cohorts means a comparison only counts advertisers that were running campaigns in both periods being compared, so that brands joining or leaving the platform in January do not distort the result. The holiday season in MNTN's analysis runs from November 21 through December 25, 2025. Q5 runs from December 26, 2025 through January 31, 2026. That is 35 days against 37 days, by PPC Land's count, which is why some of MNTN's comparisons are "adjusted for window length."
MNTN is also clear about the limits. "Because the data comes from a single platform, it does not reflect all TV advertisers, and the trends it shows are correlational," the note reads. Correlational means the figures show things moving at the same time. They do not show that the ads caused the visits or the sales.
There is a further point of context. MNTN earns its revenue from advertisers buying CTV campaigns, so research concluding that January audiences keep watching and buying supports the case for the product it sells. The article closes with a sign-up box headed "Stay Smart on All Things CTV," and its footer lists MNTN's products, including Performance TV, QuickFrame AI and Verified Visits.
Comparison windows vary from one finding to the next. "Specific comparisons vary by finding and are noted in the text," according to MNTN. The table below sets out each platform figure with the baseline it is measured against.
A January audience on the couch
MNTN starts with viewing. Total TV usage in January 2026 was the highest Nielsen had recorded in a year, about 4% above December 2025, according to Nielsen figures cited in the piece. Streaming took 47% of that viewing, "just shy of the all-time high set the month before." The monthly Nielsen Gauge splits US TV time among broadcast, cable and streaming.
Football did much of the lifting. The January 10 wild-card game between the Green Bay Packers and the Chicago Bears averaged 31.61 million viewers on Prime Video, according to Sports Media Watch, as cited by MNTN, making it the most-watched streamed NFL game on record. It beat the mark Netflix set on Christmas Day 2025 by roughly 4 million viewers. The five largest subscription streamers also carried 52% more sports programming in the first quarter of 2026 than a year earlier, according to eMarketer. Live football has pushed TV viewing around before: in October 2025, NFL games drove broadcast television to its highest share of US viewing in nearly a year.
MNTN's own numbers track the same weeks. Advertisers in its analysis saw site traffic peak in the first full week of January, the highest point of the whole Q5 window and 24% above the week before Christmas. The same advertisers reached 30% more households during the week of the NFL divisional round than in the week before Christmas. MNTN notes that the stretch also included the premiere of HBO's Game of Thrones spinoff, A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms. Total purchase value credited to MNTN campaigns grew 18% "from the beginning of January," although the piece does not say to what point.
A rise in households reached can have more than one cause. More viewers is one. Larger budgets, or cheaper inventory once holiday advertisers leave, are others, and the piece does not separate them.
The week after Christmas
Outside research suggests the shoppers who remain after December 25 are not browsing idly. US online sales in the final two weeks of December 2025 rose 9% year over year, beating growth at the start of the season, according to Salesforce data cited by MNTN. Google's research found that 87% of post-holiday shopping occasions ended in a purchase, a higher rate than at the height of the holidays, and seasonal shoppers in that research reported spending the most, across the widest mix of categories, after December 25. That Google figure is older than the rest of the piece, though. According to the chart caption in MNTN's article, Google and Ipsos measured post-holiday shopping across eight countries in the 2024 holiday season, not the 2025 season MNTN's platform data covers.
MNTN's platform data points the same way. During Q5 2025/2026, average order value for its advertisers held within 0.5% of the peak averages they recorded during the 2025 holiday season. In the week after Christmas, 74% of those advertisers saw a larger share of the site visits driven by their ads end in a conversion than in the week before. That share is a conversion rate, and it rose for roughly three in four advertisers. Conversions credited to MNTN campaigns rose 12% over the same comparison.
How long after an ad does a conversion still count? The piece does not say which attribution window applied. When MNTN linked its reporting to AppsFlyer in September, PPC Land reported that MNTN gives app installs 30 days of credit by default, against 72 hours at AppsFlyer. If a similar window applied to the website conversions in this analysis, a purchase in the week after Christmas could be credited to an ad shown during the holiday season itself. The article gives no detail either way.
Fifteen of 26 industries
The broadest finding concerns which businesses benefited. Compared with the 2025 holiday season and adjusted for window length, the median MNTN advertiser saw site traffic grow in 15 of 26 industries during Q5, according to MNTN. In most of those 15, at least 70% of brands recorded increases.
The traditional January categories still led. Fitness studios posted the largest median rise, at 74%, and the broader fitness and health category gained 56%. But the list runs well past the gym.
The categories that did not grow are just as revealing, in MNTN's reading. Fitness studios surged while exercise equipment stayed flat. Gift-focused retail such as footwear, jewelry and skincare cooled. "Those patterns suggest consumer attention in Q5 moved away from buying things and toward pursuing goals," the article states. That is an interpretation. The 11 industries without growth are not listed, and no figures are given for the ones that cooled.
Smaller resolutions
Many post-holiday shoppers are also making New Year's resolutions, and MNTN argues the nature of those resolutions has shifted. Close to 70% of adults intended to set one for 2026, up three percent from the year before and the highest level since CivicScience began tracking more than a decade ago.
Few of them are aiming for a total overhaul, though. In a survey by Headway, a learning app, respondents said they lacked the mental bandwidth to overhaul their lives (42%), to stick to rigid diet and workout plans (38%) or to chase career advancement (35%). They leaned instead toward living at a slower pace (68%), easing up on self-imposed pressure (38%) and simply enjoying life more (29%). MNTN calls the result "a resolution season built more on incremental change than 'new year, new you.'" The piece does not give Headway's sample size or field dates.
One source number sits awkwardly beside the CivicScience figure. The Stagwell page that MNTN links for its later resolution data, published on December 4, 2025, states that "42% of Americans are making 2026 New Year's resolutions." CivicScience measured intention and Stagwell measured people making resolutions, so the two need not conflict, but the gap between roughly 70% and 42% is wide, and MNTN does not address it.
Budgets on the mind, cards still swiping
Money is the theme MNTN returns to most. More than half (55%) of Americans who made 2026 resolutions committed to at least one financial goal, such as budgeting, saving, investing, paying off debt or building credit, according to the Stagwell data. Because that 55% applies to the 42% Stagwell says were making resolutions, it covers roughly 23% of all Americans, by PPC Land's calculation.
Some went further. No Spend January, a month-long pause on nonessential purchases, was taken on by 12% of Americans in 2026, and 26% have tried it at some point, according to a NerdWallet survey. US Google searches for the challenge hit a record high, up 67% year over year, outpacing searches for Dry January, which rose 47%, according to Sherwood News reporting cited by MNTN. Interest came as 44% of Americans said life felt "really expensive," a figure MNTN links to NerdWallet's studies page.
Spending did not stop. Total card spending grew 2.6% year over year in January 2026, its fastest pace since early 2024, according to the Bank of America Institute, based on the bank's internal data.
Finance advertisers on MNTN followed that interest. Finance and insurance advertisers in the analysis reached steadily more households through January: by mid-month, their weekly reach was 78% higher than just before Christmas, and by late January it had more than doubled. Median Q5 site traffic rose 5% for banking advertisers and 20% for insurance advertisers against the holiday season. "Together, these findings point to consumers who were still spending in Q5 but paying closer attention to where their money went," according to MNTN.
What MNTN draws from it
The article ends with MNTN's own conclusions about messaging. "Brands that reflect those goals, whether that's slowing down or saving more, may find a more receptive audience," according to the company. On creative: "Illustrating how a product or offering can help someone take one realistic step may go further than promising a dramatic reset." On price: "And with budgets top of mind, showing people how their money goes further could land better than a discount on its own."
Those are recommendations from an ad seller, and they connect to products MNTN sells. Its QuickFrame AI tool, which entered beta in October 2025 and builds video ads from a website address in about 12 minutes, is listed in the same page footer.
