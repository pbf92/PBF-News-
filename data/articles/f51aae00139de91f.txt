TITLE: Meta-Analysis Finds Osteoporosis Affects Nearly Fourteen Percent of Patients With Bronchiectasis
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis synthesizing observational research across international cohorts has established that osteoporosis represents a substantial comorbidity among individuals with bronchiectasis, demonstrating an overall pooled prevalence of 13.6 percent. Published by an investigative team led by Na Li, Nan Sun, Mingyang Yi, Haixia Li, and Zhiwan Wang in Frontiers in Medicine, the quantitative synthesis integrated thirty-one distinct epidemiological studies comprising 310,115 patients diagnosed with bronchiectasis, among whom 44,067 individuals were identified as having comorbid osteoporosis. Utilizing a DerSimonian-Laird random-effects statistical model to aggregate proportions, the researchers calculated a corresponding 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 11.5 percent to 16.0 percent. The analytical synthesis revealed substantial between-study heterogeneity across the literature base, characterized by an I-squared inconsistency statistic of 99.562 percent with a Cochran’s Q test p-value below 0.001. The investigators emphasized that while skeletal fragility constitutes a frequent complication for respiratory patients, marked variance across publications mandates careful interpretation of pooled estimates.
Bronchiectasis is a heterogeneous chronic airway disorder defined anatomically by irreversible bronchial dilatation visible on chest computed tomography imaging, presenting clinically with persistent cough, sputum production, recurrent bacterial respiratory infections, and acute exacerbations. Epidemiological trends document an increasing prevalence and clinical burden attributable to population aging, wider accessibility of chest computed tomography, and heightened diagnostic recognition among clinical providers. Concurrently, medical literature indicates that bronchiectasis rarely exists in isolation, instead frequently co-occurring with pulmonary and extrapulmonary comorbidities that compound functional impairment and worsen long-term prognosis. Osteoporosis, a systemic skeletal disorder defined by microarchitectural deterioration of bone tissue, diminished bone mineral density, and heightened susceptibility to fragility fractures, presents particular clinical concern within respiratory cohorts. Patients with bronchiectasis possess elevated vulnerability to progressive skeletal compromise due to overlapping clinical factors involving advanced age, impaired nutritional status, reduced body mass index, recurrent pulmonary infections, systemic hypoxemia, sedentary lifestyle behaviors, and repeated therapeutic exposures to inhaled or systemic glucocorticoids.
To establish an evidence base regarding osteoporosis occurrence in bronchiectasis, the research team structured their systematic methodology in strict alignment with the PRISMA statement and formally registered their protocol within the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews under registration number CRD420261473966. The investigational team executed standardized electronic bibliographic searches across five major biomedical databases—PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, Web of Science, and Scopus—spanning from database inception through August 8, 2026. The literature retrieval strategy deployed tailored combinations of controlled vocabulary terms and free-text keywords encompassing diverse morphological descriptors of bronchiectasis, such as cylindrical, varicose, saccular, and cystic presentations, alongside terms capturing osteoporosis, age-related bone loss, and senile skeletal degradation. Two independent researchers screened bibliographic citations, identified potentially eligible studies, extracted standardized data fields, and systematically resolved cross-screening disagreements through structured discussion or consultation with a third reviewer, verifying author information, publication dates, study regions, methodological designs, cohort sizes, and baseline population characteristics.
The screening protocol applied predetermined inclusion and exclusion criteria designed to isolate definitive epidemiological prevalence figures from observational studies, including both prospective or retrospective cohort investigations and cross-sectional designs. Eligible studies evaluated adult cohorts aged eighteen years or older with confirmed diagnoses of bronchiectasis substantiated through computed tomography imaging, validated clinical assessments, or verified diagnostic coding from established healthcare databases. Furthermore, eligible research articles specified validated diagnostic criteria for ascertainment of osteoporosis, including dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry measuring bone mineral density with standard T-score thresholds, formal clinical diagnoses within medical records, or validated healthcare database diagnostic codes. The authors excluded studies focused primarily on cystic fibrosis-related bronchiectasis, investigations failing to report separable prevalence data for bronchiectasis populations, studies reporting osteopenia or low bone mineral density without distinct osteoporosis metrics, unverified diagnostic definitions, non-English publications, and non-primary literature such as narrative reviews, conference abstracts, commentaries, case reports, letters, and methodological study protocols.
Methodological quality and risk of bias across the thirty-one included observational studies were independently evaluated by two reviewers utilizing validated design-specific assessment instruments. Cross-sectional studies were appraised using the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality scale, which assigns scores up to eleven total points, categorizing studies achieving eight to eleven points as high quality, four to seven points as moderate quality, and zero to three points as low quality. Cohort investigations were systematically appraised using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale across nine possible scoring points, designating seven to nine points as high quality, four to six points as moderate quality, and one to three points as low quality. Across the overall evidence compilation, thirteen studies met criteria for high methodological quality, while eighteen studies were classified as moderate methodological quality, with zero studies categorized as low quality. The investigators noted that quality appraisal scores characterized methodological integrity rather than functioning as quantitative numerical covariates in subsequent meta-regression models.
To perform the statistical meta-analysis, the investigators utilized Stata version 18.0, setting the primary effect measure as the proportional prevalence of diagnosed osteoporosis. Study-specific raw proportions underwent mathematical transformation using the Freeman-Tukey double-arcsine transformation to stabilize variance across disparate cohort sizes, while exact binomial 95 percent confidence intervals were derived for individual investigations. Between-study variance was calculated under the DerSimonian-Laird random-effects framework, with aggregated estimates and corresponding Wald 95 percent confidence intervals back-transformed into standard percentages via inverse transformation. Statistical heterogeneity was evaluated using Cochran’s Q test, adopting a predetermined significance threshold of alpha equal to 0.10, and quantified through the I-squared statistic. Because the calculated heterogeneity was substantial with an I-squared value of 99.562 percent, the authors carried out comprehensive pre-specified subgroup analyses and multivariable meta-regression modeling across eleven distinct study-level variables to interrogate potential sources of inconsistency, alongside leave-one-out sensitivity analyses and formal statistical assessments of publication bias.
Extensive subgroup analyses revealed marked numerical variations in reported osteoporosis frequencies across clinical subpopulations, although statistically significant differences emerged across only two examined study-level variables. Stratification by overall cohort size demonstrated statistically significant divergence, reporting a pooled prevalence of 13.1 percent across six studies enrolling fewer than 100 individuals, 16.9 percent across thirteen studies evaluating 100 to 999 patients, and 11.0 percent across twelve large investigations analyzing 1,000 or more participants, yielding a between-subgroup p-value of 0.047. Similarly, categorization by clinical population type uncovered significant variation, demonstrating a 12.5 percent prevalence in general bronchiectasis cohorts and 13.5 percent in stable outpatients, contrasted against single-study estimates of 16.1 percent during acute pulmonary exacerbations, 12.3 percent in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease overlap, 13.6 percent in coexisting asthma, and 36.7 percent in non-tuberculous mycobacterial lung disease, producing a p-value below 0.001. Conversely, subgroup stratification by study design, geographic region, national development status, patient age categories, body mass index, and gender proportions showed no significant differences.
A crucial clinical observation within the subgroup breakdowns involved the method utilized for osteoporosis ascertainment, which exhibited wide numerical variance despite not reaching statistical significance under Cochran’s Q testing. Specifically, five studies employing dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry with objective T-score criteria documented an osteoporosis prevalence of 21.3 percent with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning 10.0 percent to 35.2 percent, whereas five studies utilizing administrative claims coding recorded a pooled prevalence of 11.3 percent, and twenty-one studies relying on medical records or documented clinical diagnoses identified a prevalence of 12.6 percent, yielding a between-subgroup p-value of 0.272. In contrast, stratification by diagnostic modalities for bronchiectasis showed comparable rates: 14.2 percent for chest imaging, 12.0 percent for claims records, and 11.8 percent for clinical charts. When the researchers conducted multivariable meta-regression models assessing study design, geographic region, development status, diagnostic criteria, cohort magnitude, clinical subpopulation, body mass index, patient age, and sex distributions, none of the eleven investigated covariates achieved statistical significance.
The stability of the pooled findings was confirmed through leave-one-out sensitivity testing, which demonstrated that sequential omission of any single study produced aggregated prevalence estimates bounded between 11.8 percent and 13.6 percent. The single investigation exerting the greatest individual influence was an observational analysis authored by Tkacz in 2024, whose temporary exclusion adjusted the pooled prevalence downward to 11.8 percent with a 95 percent confidence interval of 10.1 percent to 13.5 percent, yet retained substantial overlap with the baseline result. Assessment of publication bias and small-study effects yielded contrasting diagnostic signals: while Egger’s linear regression test detected no statistically significant small-study distortion with a p-value of 0.550, Begg’s rank correlation test revealed significant funnel plot asymmetry with a p-value of 0.006. Implementation of the trim-and-fill algorithm imputed nine potentially missing studies to achieve visual funnel plot symmetry, resulting in an adjusted random-effects pooled prevalence of 10.8 percent with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 7.5 percent to 14.0 percent.
In contextualizing their findings against earlier scientific literature, the authors noted that while a previous scoping review reported an average osteoporosis prevalence of 18.6 percent across five studies, the present analysis incorporates a substantially larger evidence base and clarifies how case identification mechanisms shape reported outcomes.
