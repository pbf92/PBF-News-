TITLE: Legal Beat: Elvis Presley Estate Sues Anthropic A.I.
OUTLET: Music Connection Magazine

On October 23, 2026, the Elvis Presley Estate sued A.I. company Anthropic (owner of Claude) for 46.6 million dollars for copyright infringement. The lead Plaintiff, Authentic Brands Group, who owns Elvis’s music and likeness, claims Anthropic illegally used Presley’s songs to teach their AI models without gaining permission or providing compensation.
This is an especially bad time for Anthropic to have to defend themselves in a lawsuit considering its impending IPO (sale of shares to the public). This lawsuit is one of the first by a recording artists’ estate against an AI company.
The plaintiffs’ object to the use of 274 Elvis songs, including classics such as “Return to Sender,” “Don’t Be Cruel,” and “Can’t Help Falling in Love.” The plaintiffs claim that Anthropic deleted information, such as composer and publishing information, and copied songs without permission to teach its chatbot, Claude. A.I. platforms analyze huge amounts of recorded music to learn their structure, sounds, and expressions of songs; then they permit users to create their own A.I. generated music using what the platform has learned from the recorded music.
Anthropic developed Claude and released it in March 2023. The AI-based chatbot is also used in A.I.-assisted software development and has seen incredible sales growth, which could make it easier to defend itself against a multi-million-dollar lawsuit.
According to plaintiffs’ attorney, Richard Busch:
“The Elvis plaintiffs bring this action to enforce exclusive rights against the defendant that has built its entire business model based on copyright infringement at a scale never seen before.”
The Complaint states:
“Even Anthropic’s institution of guardrails (albeit ineffectively) to purportedly limit outputs which infringe upon music copyrights, after being caught, along with its knowledge that its guardrails were ineffective to stop creating outputs which infringed music copyrights, demonstrates that Anthropic knowingly engaged and continues to willfully engage in copyright infringement of others’ musical works.”
Musicians have complained that their music and voices are being used without their permission and they fear that AI generated “slop” could even replace human-made music soon.
The lawsuit contends that “from its inception, Anthropic’s A.I. model, Claude, has created infringing output of copyrighted material of virtually every song from every catalog available on the internet.”
Anthropic has claimed in federal court that its use of copyrighted lyrics to train Claude A.I. models is legal as it is protected by the “fair use” doctrine under U.S. copyright law. This has been a common defense in similar copyright infringement cases.
The case is important because it could set a precedent for putting limitations on A.I. companies using music catalogs. Of course, the fear of establishing a bad precedent may incentivize Anthropic to settle.
Some AI companies have recently begun impersonating artists on music streaming services. Recently, Spotify indicated that it had removed songs it considered to be “A.I. slop” from its user recommendations.
