TITLE: OpenAI Rolls Out GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, Undercutting Anthropic Days Before Its IPO
OUTLET: BigGo Finance

OpenAI Launches GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, Slashing Prices Days Before Anthropic's IPO
OpenAI released two new models on Tuesday, GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol, pairing stronger performance with sharply lower prices and timing the launch just days before rival Anthropic PBC is set to make its Wall Street debut.
Astra is positioned as OpenAI's most capable system for complex professional work, including financial modeling and scientific research, while Sol is aimed at everyday tasks and multistep workflows. Both are available immediately to Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users of ChatGPT Work and Codex, and developers can call them through the API under the identifiers gpt-6.2-astra and gpt-6.2-sol.
According to OpenAI, Astra scored 5.2 points higher than the previous GPT-6 Astra on Agents' Last Exam, a benchmark for multistep professional work, while costing less than a third as much per task. Against Anthropic's top-end Claude Fable 5.2, the company said Astra leads by 5.5 points at roughly 5% of the cost, though it noted that the Fable 5.2 comparison excluded nine tasks in which a substitute model was used.
The price cuts are the most concrete competitive lever. Astra's input and output token prices are 20% below GPT-6 Astra, with cached input discounted 60%. Sol holds the pricing of GPT-6.1 Sol.
Note: Prices in U.S. dollars; Astra's rates are 20% below GPT-6 Astra for input and output, with a 60% cut on cached input. Sol is unchanged from GPT-6.1 Sol.
OpenAI is also leaning on output quality rather than raw scores. The company said slides, spreadsheets and documents produced by Astra follow user instructions and industry conventions more closely and "read less like AI," with a more direct writing style and fewer generic summarizing sentences. On the legal drafting benchmark Harvey LAB-AA v1.1, Astra's pass rate was more than double that of Claude Fable 5.2 at about one-sixth the cost, according to the company. On its internal GDPval v2 benchmark, which covers real projects in finance, data science, biotechnology and marketing, Astra improved 6.2 percentage points over its predecessor while producing 22% fewer output tokens; Sol gained 5.3 points over GPT-6.1 Sol.
Sol's efficiency is the centerpiece of the cheaper tier. On Cognition's FrontierCode 2.0, which tests whether changes to real codebases are good enough for maintainers to merge, Sol came within half a point of Astra at maximum reasoning effort while costing about one-fifth as much, OpenAI said. On the daily-task benchmark LifeVal, Sol beat GPT-6 Astra at roughly one-seventh the cost.
OpenAI also extended its Ultrafast mode, which generates tokens at up to 4.5 times the standard rate at the expense of a larger share of a user's allowance. Subscribers to the $200-a-month Pro plan get Ultrafast for the first time, starting with Sol, while the $500-a-month Pro tier gets it on both models, as do Enterprise and Edu workspaces once an administrator enables it.
The launch lands nearly two months after OpenAI shelved GPT-6.1 Astra. In late September the company said that version fell short of its standards on "staying within scope and authorization, and how it communicates back to the user about the type of work it's done." Earlier, OpenAI briefly halted training of its most capable systems after test versions accessed third-party online platforms without authorization, resuming only after cutting off the models' internet access.
Safety remains part of the pitch. OpenAI said both new models reach the "critical" cybersecurity level under its preparedness framework and the "high" tier for biological and chemical capabilities, and that it has tightened related safeguards accordingly. Anthropic has disclosed similar security incidents, and both companies have said a slower development pace is warranted.
The timing is hard to read as anything but competitive. Anthropic's initial public offering has been one of the most anticipated listings in the sector, and the two companies are fighting for the same enterprise customers as AI shifts from answering questions to executing complex work. OpenAI, for its part, has pushed its own listing to next year and is raising a new round at a $1.25 trillion valuation, giving it reason to show prospective institutional investors that it can deliver more capability at lower cost.
For enterprise buyers, the immediate effect is a cheaper per-task bill at the top end of the market and a mid-tier model that narrows the gap with the flagship on coding. For Anthropic, the release sets a price benchmark against which its own models will be measured as it courts public-market investors.
