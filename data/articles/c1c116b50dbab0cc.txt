TITLE: Dow Jones, Nasdaq, S&P 500 preview: Consumer spending in focus after AI rout
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

Investing.com -- U.S. stocks sank Friday, capping a punishing week in which investors used Anthropic's heavily anticipated initial public offering (IPO) to lock in profits from the AI-driven rally.
The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% to 22,617.73, turning negative for the year with a loss of 2.7%. The S&P 500 dropped 3.7% to 6,910.39, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 949.73 points, or 1.93%, to 48,147.33. Both the S&P 500 and the Dow closed 10% below their record highs, putting them in correction territory.
The Nasdaq-100 tumbled 5.6% and, at its session low, was down 20% from its recent peak, a bear market on an intraday basis.
Anthropic shares fell 8.5%, surrendering a large part of the 17% gain from their Thursday debut and adding pressure to the broader AI trade. Advanced Micro Devices slid more than 13% and Nvidia about 14%. Selling in Nvidia intensified after Reuters reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission is looking into possible accounting issues at the company.
For the week, the Dow fell 8%, its worst weekly performance since March 2020. The Nasdaq lost about 10%, its steepest weekly decline in more than six years, and the S&P 500 dropped 8.6%, its worst week since April 2025, when President Donald Trump unveiled sweeping tariffs.
This week, investors will turn to consumer spending for evidence that a broadly solid economy can steady a market rattled by doubts over AI. Black Friday kicks off the holiday shopping season, and investors will watch how consumers are handling inflation, including higher energy and borrowing costs. Consumer spending makes up about two-thirds of the U.S. economy.
Falling stock prices could also weigh on holiday purchases if consumers feel less wealthy. Oxford Economics expects holiday retail sales to rise 4.2%, or 2.2% after adjusting for inflation. The estimate covers November and December and excludes autos, gasoline, bars and restaurants.
As third-quarter earnings season winds down, investors will also get results from Dell, HP and Hewlett Packard Enterprise, among others.
