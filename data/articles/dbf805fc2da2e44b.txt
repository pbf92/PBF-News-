Xbox Offers Doctor’s Note for Undefined Gaming Session Definitely Not Linked to GTA 6
They don't want you to win.
Xbox has kicked off its marketing for the game it’s not allowed to mention in its marketing. Like the weird alligator-shaped Xbox recently announced, Microsoft is doing its best to get Xbox players excited about GTA 6 without saying the game’s name.
“Are you suffering from Vitamin G deficiency?” the new marketing blast asked. “Dr. XBOX has you covered.” I don’t know much about supplements, but there’s a real lack of vitamin G, T, and A over there.
For some reason, Dr. Xbox is DJ Khalid, and he wants to get you ready for “the biggest week in gaming.” What makes it the biggest week? No one knows.
The website lets you put your name in and get a personalized doctor’s note to play games this week on your Xbox. Obviously DJ Khalid isn’t a medical professional, and there’s no gamer vitamin, so it probably won’t work on your boss, unfortunately.
“As their physician, mentor, and professional motivator, I am prescribing the patient take some time off to enjoy the biggest week in gaming,” the letter read after I put my details in. “I advise they avoid all meetings, highly stressful jobs, and unnecessary negativity.
“Symptoms may include: twitchy thumbs, controller cramps or release-date restlessness.”
There isn’t a single mention of what makes this the biggest week in gaming. That’s likely because Sony has an exclusive marketing deal with Rockstar to promote the game coming to PlayStation.
This has led to some pretty embarrassing misses on Xbox’s part. The first version of the GTA 6 TV spot failed to mention the game coming to Xbox consoles at all.
Microsoft did manage to muscle in a little more recently, with the announcement that you can play GTA 6 on the cloud. This means even people who only own an Xbox One can play GTA 6 if they stream it, which gets them access to the game at Xbox Series S graphics settings.
We still don’t know what Xbox Series S graphics settings means, however. Rockstar has said GTA 6 will run at 30fps on all platforms, and PS5 Pro gets some ray tracing upgrades, but there’s no word on what kind of compromises the studio had to make to have the game run on the Xbox Series S. We need a doctor on the case.
