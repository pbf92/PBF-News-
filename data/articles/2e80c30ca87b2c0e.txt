TITLE: How do Britons feel about key NEET proposals from the Milburn review?
OUTLET: YouGov

Majority of the public are supportive of the report’s key recommendations and expect them to be effective
Key takeaways
- Of the five key proposals polled, all had support from at least 55% of Britons, with expanding apprenticeships and college places the most popular, being backed by 94%
- Support for the policies can be found across all major voting groups
- At least 54% of Britons believe the policies will be effective at reducing the number of out of work young people
Last week, former Labour cabinet minister Alan Milburn published his long-awaited review into NEETs (young people who are not in education, employment or training), proposing widespread changes to welfare, business and education policies in order to get more under 25s into work, with an objective of more than halving the number of NEETs over the next decade.
Andy Burnham has said he wants to “move quickly” in implementing the report's recommendations, while Kemi Badenoch has said there was no need for “costly government schemes”. But what do the public think of the report's main proposals?
New YouGov polling on five key proposals finds that all are supported by a majority of the public, while none are opposed by more than 26%.
Most popular of all are the proposals to expand the number of apprenticeships and college education places, which more than nine in ten Britons (94%) are in favour of, while support is also widespread for making it compulsory for schools to offer work experience (82% support).
Just over seven in ten Britons (72%) support reducing disability and out-of-work benefits for under 25s to instead spend the money on back-to-work support, with 68% backing a cut to employers’ National Insurance for employees aged under 25.
Paying employers a cash incentive of £3,000 for taking on a young person not in education or work is the least supported policy polled, though it is still backed by 55% of the public, more than double the 26% who are opposed.
Support for these policies is cross-partisan, with backing from voters for each of the five main parties at the 2024 general election, including support for all five proposals among at least 60% of Labour voters themselves.
Reform voters are the only group where are a majority do not back all five, with less than half (47%) in favour of cash incentives for employers, though this is still greater than the 38% who are opposed to this policy. Other significant opposition to some policies can be found among Green voters, roughly a third of whom (33-35%) are opposed to reducing welfare benefits or giving employers cash incentives to hire younger workers.
Do Britons expect Milburn review proposals to be effective?
While YouGov polling last week found that most Britons (53%) said they had little to no confidence in Andy Burnham and the Labour government’s ability to reduce the number of under 25s who are out of work, tested individually, the public do mostly believe these key recommendations will be effective at reducing the number of unemployed young people if implemented.
This includes 84% of Britons believing that expanding apprenticeships and college education places will be effective at reducing the number of NEETs, with between 59-63% anticipating that the proposed changes to employers’ National Insurance, school work experience and welfare benefits will help the government achieve Milburn’s objective of cutting the rate of NEETs to 5% by 2036.
A lower 54% expect cash incentives for employers to prove effective at reducing youth unemployment, though this is still against just 25% believing it will be ineffective.
Comparing responses to the two questions shows that support for giving employers cash incentives to hire a NEET is the most in line with expectations of effectiveness.
By contrast, there is a substantial gap between the level of support for making work experience compulsory in schools, as well as redirecting NEET disability benefits to fund back-to-work schemes, and the number of people who think these will be effective.
Methodology
- Sample size: Online survey of 2,210 adults aged 18+ living in Great Britain
- Fieldwork: 18 to 19 November 2026
- Commissioned by: YouGov
