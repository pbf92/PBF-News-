TITLE: One goal per campaign: Amazon's DVA+ primer lists 15 KPIs
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon Ads today updated the Support Center page that explains how its Display, Video, and Audio campaigns are put together, setting out three campaign goals, 15 key performance indicators and a three-tier structure in which every ad group inherits its campaign's goal, budget and KPI. The page, titled "Understand DVA+ campaigns" and filed under the Support Center's "Before you begin" section, also lists five display ad types, among them a resizable image format built for Twitch and a third-party format for mobile app inventory sold through Amazon Publisher Services.
It is a primer rather than a launch. Yet it is the first Amazon page in the recent run of DVA+ documentation to lay out the full KPI menu by goal in one table, and it does so while describing the product's ancestry in two incompatible ways. The opening line says the page explains how "DVA+ campaigns (formerly known as Amazon DSP) are structured." A worked example further down has a fictional brand creating its campaigns "with Amazon Ads Agent (formerly known as Amazon DSP)." One page, one former product, two successors.
In Short
Amazon has updated its beginner's guide to DVA+, the display, video, audio and streaming TV ad buying product that replaced Amazon DSP, listing three campaign goals and 15 performance targets advertisers can pick from. It matters to brands and agencies moving campaigns into the new system, because the goal, budget and KPI are set once per campaign and passed down to every ad group, so running an awareness push and a sales push side by side means building two campaigns. The page also repeats that changing a KPI after launch resets what the campaign has learned, and it calls the former Amazon DSP by two different new names, DVA+ and Amazon Ads Agent.
Three tiers under one advertiser
The page starts above the campaign. An account can hold multiple advertisers, according to Amazon, "each representing a brand or client." The page continues: "The advertiser is the top-level entity that contains all campaigns, audiences, and ads. Each advertiser can have multiple DVA+ campaigns." Audiences, in other words, belong to the advertiser rather than to any single campaign.
Below the advertiser, campaigns are built in three parts, and the page describes each in a sentence. Campaigns "set your overall advertising goal, budget, and key performance indicator (KPI)." Ad groups "define how, where, and to whom your ads appear." Ads "are the display, video, or audio assets that customers see." The relationship between them is stated as a rule: "Every campaign contains 1 or more ad groups that inherit these top-level parameters." And each ad "links to an ad group and serves based on the targeting and bidding rules you configured."
That vocabulary is the sponsored ads vocabulary, not the programmatic one. When Amazon published its unified reporting dimensions this month, the former DSP order became a campaign, the line item an ad group and the creative an ad. The account layer above it changed earlier in the year: on July 30, 2026, existing DSP accounts were moved into a single advertiser account spanning 34 countries. Today's page describes the result of both moves without mentioning either.
What DVA+ can deliver is described broadly. According to the page, it reaches audiences "across multiple formats including display, video, audio, and Streaming TV on desktop, mobile, and connected devices," and clicks can lead to product detail pages on Amazon, a brand store, "other custom landing pages" or an advertiser's own website. A single caveat covers geography: "Some advertising options and features may vary by region."
Fifteen KPIs across three goals
Three parameters are fixed at campaign level, according to Amazon: a goal, defined as "What you want to achieve (awareness, consideration, or conversion)"; a budget; and a key performance indicator, defined as the "Metric that specifies which outcome to optimize your campaign for." The goal comes first because it gates the rest. "Your goal determines which KPI options are available," the page states.
Each goal comes with Amazon's own description of when it applies. Awareness "increases exposure for your brand or product by reaching a larger audience" and suits "top-funnel visibility, brand recall, or reaching new customers." Consideration "encourages potential customers to learn more about your product through engagement," for driving "clicks, product detail page views, or video engagement." Conversion "focuses on driving measurable actions like purchases, leads, installs, or sign-ups," and is meant for "sales or lower-funnel outcomes on or off Amazon."

By PPC Land's count, that is 2 awareness KPIs, 6 consideration KPIs and 7 conversion KPIs. Four of the 15 are defined by reference to Amazon's store: ROAS and T-ROAS count products sold on Amazon, and CPDPV and DPVR count views of detail pages on Amazon. Three of the conversion KPIs concern apps, and one of those, CPSU, is tied to subscriptions for "an Amazon Prime Video channel or streaming app."
The ROAS trio and the detail page view
The three return-on-spend options are close enough to need reading twice. ROAS covers "promoted products sold on Amazon." T-ROAS covers "all of a brand's promoted products sold on Amazon." C-ROAS widens that to sales "on and off Amazon." The difference between the first two rests on the words "all of a brand's," and the page goes no further. In Amazon reporting, total ROAS has been understood to count same-brand halo sales alongside the promoted products themselves; the definition published today does not use the word halo.
The detail page view KPI deserves a note of its own. In the guide to building DVA+ campaigns that Amazon updated on November 13, the automated flow's Traffic on Amazon objective showed cost per detail page view as a metric that was reported only and could not be set as a KPI target. Today's page lists CPDPV as a selectable KPI under the consideration goal. The two statements can both hold, since they appear to describe different build routes, but the primer does not say which route its KPI table applies to.
Frequency is marked as a beta here. Amazon's November 17 guide for streaming TV campaigns lists frequency as an awareness KPI with a target of 2 to 5 exposures per week. That streaming TV page also offers TV incremental reach, limited to the US, which the general primer leaves out.
Changing course mid-flight
The KPI is not locked. It is not free to change either. "You can change your KPI after launch, but this resets your campaign's performance learnings and may impact optimization," according to Amazon, repeating wording from the November 13 build guide. A campaign moved from a reach KPI to a completion-rate KPI partway through its flight, on that reading, starts its learning again.
What the KPI target does is described in two clauses. "The KPI target generates reports in campaign management tables under the Goal metric column and facilitates optimization when you select the right bid priority," the page reads. It does not name the bid priorities. The mechanism is older than the DVA+ name: according to an Amazon launch note dated October 25, 2023, Amazon DSP introduced goal-based bidding at unBoxed 2023, letting advertisers set a performance target such as a cost per acquisition and adjusting bids in real time to meet it, with an optimization strategy called Prioritize KPI target. The November 13 guide described bidding priority as the setting that determines how Amazon ranks ads to win impressions.
Ad groups: who, where and how much
Ad groups are where the strategy turns into delivery. "Ad groups translate your campaign strategy into execution," according to Amazon. Three things are configured in each: audience ("Who sees your ads"), placements ("Where your ads appear") and bidding ("How much you'll pay").
Several ad groups can sit under one campaign. The page's stated purpose for that is testing: multiple ad groups allow "different targeting strategies while maintaining consistent goals and budget." Inventory depends on the combination of settings. "Inventory compatibility depends on your ad group and ad settings," the page notes, pointing to a separate article on DVA+ campaign inventory by ad group.
The consequence of the inheritance rule is structural, and the page's own example spells it out. A brand that wants awareness and consideration at the same time cannot put both goals in one campaign. It builds two.
Display ads, the one tab open
The ads section is organized as four tabs: Display ads, Video ads, Audio ads and Component-based ads. Only the Display tab is expanded on the page.
Display ads, according to Amazon, "are images that appear on desktop, mobile, apps, and publisher websites," and they are pitched at a particular part of the funnel: "Use display ads for broad reach and efficient delivery across Amazon and third-party sites. They work best for awareness, retargeting, and mid-funnel performance at scale." The tab lists two first-party types and three under a heading for third-party (3P) ad experiences:
Three details stand out. Standard display carries the page's only hard creative limit, 10 image assets. Third-party serving means the creative comes from an external ad server, so the advertiser keeps its own tracking and measurement, and the page says delivery runs "through Amazon Ads Agent" rather than through DVA+. And the Twitch format sits under the third-party heading even though Twitch is Amazon's own live streaming service. Twitch display inventory has been buyable in Amazon's programmatic platform for almost four years: according to an Amazon launch note dated December 14, 2022, it arrived in Amazon DSP as open auction inventory, with 728x90 units below the Twitch video player and 728x90 and 300x250 units on Twitch browse pages. The page does not explain what AAP stands for.
Counting rules for on-Amazon display are about to change as well. Amazon has told advertisers that DVA display impressions on the Amazon Store will be counted only once the ad appears on screen from January 1, 2027, with impressions expected to fall about 63% on average. The primer does not mention it.
PureWave Home: the worked example
The page closes with an illustration. "PureWave Home's goal is to increase visibility for their new air purifier among shoppers and streaming audiences," according to Amazon. The brand is fictional, and its answer is two campaigns under one account, each with one ad group carrying three ads.
The example adds two items not listed among the three campaign parameters earlier on the page: flight dates and a delivery strategy. It also puts a streaming TV campaign under the consideration goal with a completion-rate KPI, rather than under awareness. The 15 to 30 second length is the only video specification on the page.
Two names for one former product
So what did Amazon DSP become? Today's page gives two answers. Its subtitle reads "Learn how DVA+ campaigns (formerly known as Amazon DSP) are structured and what goals they support." Its example reads "they create 2 separate campaigns with Amazon Ads Agent (formerly known as Amazon DSP)." The third-party display entry says ads deliver "through Amazon Ads Agent," and the Support Center header carries the Amazon Ads Agent brand.
Amazon retired the Amazon DSP name on September 29, 2026 and introduced DVA+ as a unified campaign type, with general availability across 35 markets expected in the first quarter of 2027. On the same day the whole advertising platform took the Amazon Ads Agent name. PPC Land's reading in October was that the buying product became DVA, with Ads Agent as the layer above it, and that Amazon DSP did not become Amazon Ads Agent.
