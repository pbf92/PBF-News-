TITLE: Parent Education Programs Boost Retinopathy Screening Adherence in Preterm Infants
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Parent-focused educational and health-literacy-informed interventions consistently improve parental knowledge and substantially increase adherence to retinopathy of prematurity screening and follow-up schedules, according to a systematic review published in BMC Pediatrics. The authors conducted the systematic review to examine and synthesise evidence regarding how parental education and health literacy initiatives influence caregiver comprehension, attendance at critical ophthalmic examinations, and clinical outcomes.
The research team developed the systematic review protocol following the PRISMA methodology and registered the project in PROSPERO under registration number CRD42024563598. The primary objective focused on systematically reviewing and synthesising available evidence concerning the effect of interventions tailored for parents or caregivers on knowledge of retinopathy of prematurity, alongside adherence to recommended screening and follow-up schedules. In addition to these primary measures, the investigators established secondary outcomes to evaluate maternal anxiety and reported clinical retinopathy of prematurity outcomes in preterm infants.
To gather relevant literature, the investigators carried out comprehensive database searches across PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, the Cochrane Library, BASE, and CINAHL. The search strategy covered studies published between January 2006 and June 2026. Eligible study designs encompassed both randomized controlled trials and non-randomized interventional studies that evaluated educational or health-literacy-informed interventions directed at parents or caregivers of preterm infants. From the literature search, a total of ten studies published between 2010 and 2023 met the criteria and were included in the systematic review.
To assess the credibility of the evidence, methodological quality was evaluated using the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklists. In parallel, risk of bias was examined using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool for randomized trials and the ROBINS-I tool for non-randomized interventional studies. Because of substantial methodological and clinical heterogeneity among the included studies, the investigators synthesised the study findings narratively rather than pooling data in a meta-analysis. Across the included body of work, most studies were assessed as having moderate methodological quality and a moderate risk of bias.
The synthesized findings revealed that educational and health-literacy-informed interventions consistently enhanced parental knowledge and awareness of retinopathy of prematurity. Adherence to recommended screening and follow-up appointments also increased substantially across the evaluated cohorts. This improvement in adherence was particularly pronounced in two quality-improvement studies reviewed by the authors, where initial baseline screening rates of 10.7 percent and 38.3 percent rose to 87.3 percent and 90.0 percent, respectively, following the introduction of structured parental interventions.
Beyond adherence and knowledge metrics, three of the included studies documented specific clinical outcomes related to retinopathy of prematurity. One study identified less severe disease progression in association with a family-centred care intervention, while another reported faster completion of retinal vascularisation. Conversely, researchers observed more advanced retinopathy of prematurity among preterm infants whose initial screening was delayed beyond nine weeks of postnatal age. Furthermore, one randomized controlled trial demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in maternal anxiety following participation in a structured educational programme based on the Health Belief Model.
In their concluding assessment, the authors determined that parent-focused educational and health-literacy-informed interventions appear effective for improving parental knowledge and adherence to screening and follow-up schedules. However, they emphasized that available evidence directly evaluating clinical and visual outcomes remains limited. The review suggests that integrating structured retinopathy of prematurity education into existing neonatal discharge and follow-up pathways represents a feasible strategy, especially in resource-limited settings, though future high-quality randomized controlled trials are required to confirm long-term clinical benefits.
