TITLE: Semrush puts a number on the visits that begin with an AI answer
OUTLET: PPC Land

Semrush today introduced a topics layer that puts Google keyword data and AI prompt data in a single report, together with a metric that estimates how many website visits a brand earns after being named in AI-generated answers. The company, owned by Adobe since April, also said it has rebuilt the click-through rate model behind its organic traffic estimates to account for AI Overviews, AI Mode and zero-click results. The topic reports start with US and UK data.
In Short
Semrush now groups Google keywords and AI chatbot prompts into topics and estimates the website visits that follow when a brand appears in AI answers on ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overviews and AI Mode. The change is aimed at search and marketing teams that have been asked to show a return on AI search work while the AI reports in Google's Search Console show no clicks. The new traffic figure comes from a predictive model, so Semrush itself describes it as a directional signal rather than a count of visits that AI caused.
What Semrush released
The announcement, published in Semrush's newsroom under the headline "Semrush's New Topics Layer Helps Marketers Measure Brand Visibility Across Google and AI Search In One View," bundles three things. The first is the Topics layer itself, which according to Semrush "brings together Semrush's expansive search intelligence of more than 29 billion keywords and over 317 million AI prompts so marketers can track how their brand performs across whole topics in Google and AI search, including ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overviews and AI Mode." The second is the AI-influenced traffic metric. The third is a revised CTR model for estimating organic traffic from Google rankings.
Semrush's argument for the topic approach is stated plainly in the release. Marketing teams, the company says, are "juggling keyword lists in one dashboard while tracking isolated AI prompts in another, which doubles the work and fragments reporting." According to Semrush, "connected visibility growth happens at the topic level, where AI assistants evaluate trusted authority to make recommendations."
The release gives one worked example. A skincare brand tracking the topic "sensitive skincare" would capture Google keywords such as "moisturizer for sensitive skin" alongside AI prompts such as "what skincare routine works for my sensitive skin?" According to Semrush, the topic view then "reveals where the brand leads in Google, how it shows up in AI answers, and where its competitor compares across both." A product video embedded in the announcement shows a summary panel with a topic count and organic traffic for a travel brand, above a table of topics such as "Luggage" and "Air Travel," some labelled as branded, each with a total search volume.
What AI-influenced traffic estimates
The metric is the least familiar part of the announcement. According to the release, it "estimates the traffic a brand gets when it's mentioned or cited prominently in a topic's AI answers, whether that traffic comes as direct visits, branded organic searches or AI referral clicks." Semrush adds: "It highlights traffic that standard analytics tools can't currently attribute: visits that started with an AI recommendation rather than a search click."
That is a broader measure than AI referral traffic, which counts only clicks on links inside AI answers and which analytics packages can already separate. The new figure tries to capture the person who reads a brand name in ChatGPT's answer, closes the chat and later types the brand's address into a browser, or searches for the brand on Google. Neither of those visits carries an AI referrer.
The metric did not appear for the first time today. A Semrush blog post by Luke Harsel, published on November 18, introduced it as available in Semrush from that day, inside Organic Rankings 2.0, next to organic traffic, mentions and citations for each topic a brand competes on. Semrush's help documentation describes how the number is built:
- A predictive model is trained on a reference dataset of aggregated web activity that links brand mentions in AI-generated answers to website visits within the following 24 hours, from any channel, including direct traffic, organic search and AI referrals.
- The training data includes cases where a visit follows a mention and cases where none does, so the model can estimate the likelihood of a later visit.
- The model is then applied to AI responses held in the Semrush AI Visibility database, taking into account how often and how consistently a brand is mentioned.
- Each topic's estimated AI search volume weights the result, so topics that more people ask about count for more.
- The output is a monthly estimate. According to the documentation, each keyword and prompt in the underlying database is refreshed at least once a month on a rolling basis.
- Coverage is limited to the four AI platforms in Semrush's prompt database: ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Mode and Google AI Overviews.
The documentation is careful about what the figure is not. It describes the number as an estimate from a predictive model rather than a direct traffic count from sources such as Google Search Console or Google Analytics, says it reflects statistical relationships rather than proof that AI caused any individual visit, and frames it as a directional signal rather than an exact count. The blog post says the same model is used for every domain and topic every month, which makes the figure suited to comparison between brands and over time rather than to reconciliation with a site's own analytics.
Two details do not line up across Semrush's own materials. The release refers to brands that are "mentioned or cited prominently," but neither the blog post nor the help page defines prominence. And while the release and blog post speak of mentions or citations, the help page describes the model in terms of brand mentions and how often and consistently they occur, without saying whether a citation without a mention counts. The provider of the aggregated web activity data used for training is not named.
How Organic Rankings 2.0 presents the data
According to Semrush's help documentation, the metric sits in one report and is reached in four steps:
- Open SEO > Organic Rankings 2.0 in the Semrush menu.
- Select the US or UK database, the only two listed.
- Enter a domain. The blog post says any domain can be entered, which allows the metric to be read for competitors as well as for a brand's own site.
- Read the domain summary, which shows a topic count, organic traffic and AI-Influenced Traffic, defined in the documentation as an estimate of visits to the brand's website, from any channel, influenced by the brand being mentioned by AI platforms across all of the domain's topics. The topics table below it carries an "AI Traffic" column for each topic. Earlier months can be selected from a date menu.
The documentation lists no plan tiers, add-ons or Enterprise-only limits for Organic Rankings 2.0, and the release does not mention pricing.
A new CTR model for Google rankings
The third change affects every organic traffic number Semrush shows, not only AI figures. According to the release, "Semrush has also updated the way organic traffic from SERP position and search volume is estimated to reflect how click-through rates on Google have changed this year." The new model "takes more features of today's SERPs into account, including search intent, branded versus non-branded keywords, SERP features, zero-click signals, and the impact of AI Overviews and AI Mode." Semrush says "the result is a more accurate estimate of the traffic a ranking position is likely to drive today." No accuracy figure accompanies that claim.
Rank trackers have long converted a position into traffic by multiplying search volume by an expected click-through rate for that position. That arithmetic has come under strain. An Ahrefs analysis of 300,000 keywords, which PPC Land reported in February, found a 58% lower click-through rate for top-ranking pages on queries with AI Overviews, with the expected rate of 0.037 falling to an actual 0.016. Seer Interactive's study of 3,119 informational queries recorded organic CTR on AI Overview queries falling from 1.76% to 0.61% between June 2024 and September 2025. And Semrush's own panel of 400 commercial keywords showed AI Overviews on 88% of them by August 2026, up from 45.75% in September 2025. A CTR curve built before that shift would overstate the traffic attached to a first-page ranking. The inclusion of zero-click search signals in the new model is an acknowledgement of exactly that.
The practical consequence for users is that numbers will move. Semrush's FAQ for Organic Rankings 2.0 says its organic traffic estimates differ from those in the legacy report. That comes one week after a separate change to a different part of the platform. On November 16, Semrush announced that its Traffic & Market toolkit had been running on a new traffic prediction algorithm since November 9, with a broader set of data sources, a machine learning model built for channel prediction, and metrics recalculated from January 2023 onward. Semrush claimed improvements of up to 40% in domain traffic estimates for large US domains and up to 30% for small and medium domains worldwide, and up to 45% and 42% in channel attribution for small and medium domains in the US and worldwide. It did not publish the method behind those figures.
Two model revisions in the same month mean that any trend line spanning early November will mix old and new estimation methods unless Semrush recalculates history, which it has said it did for Traffic & Market but has not said for the new organic CTR model.
Semantic grouping in Keyword Magic Tool 2.0
The second product to carry topics is a rebuilt keyword research tool. According to Semrush, Keyword Magic Tool 2.0 is "a redesigned keyword research tool with a new algorithm. It finds relevant keywords and prompts based on SERP and semantic similarity instead of keyword stemming, so they're grouped by what they mean, not how they're spelled."
Stemming groups words that share a root, so "moisturizer" and "moisturizing" fall together while "face cream for dry skin" might not. Grouping by SERP similarity puts queries together when Google returns overlapping results for them, and semantic similarity does the same based on meaning. Natural-language AI prompts rarely share exact phrasing with the short keywords they relate to, which is why a stemming approach would struggle to place a prompt such as "what skincare routine works for my sensitive skin?" next to the keyword "moisturizer for sensitive skin."
Organic Rankings 2.0 is described in the release as "a topic-level view of visibility across Google and AI search combined. It shows how fully a brand covers a topic compared to other industry players, and where AI systems are already citing competitors instead."
Availability
According to Semrush, "Topics will launch first in the US and UK, and then roll out to additional markets." The company did not name the next markets.
The numbers behind the layer
Semrush's database counts have been cited in several places this year, and they do not all move together. The 317 million prompt figure in today's release is the same number Semrush gave in August, when it released a native Claude connector covering 28.8 billion keywords for Semrush One and SEO Classic subscribers. The keyword count has grown since then.
The 2.18 billion figure counts observed AI responses rather than prompts. Semrush's help documentation notes that AI answers vary between runs, and the blog post describes repeated sampling of the same prompts, which would explain why responses outnumber prompts several times over.
