TITLE: Colossal Ramesses II Statues Surface
OUTLET: Biblical Archaeology Society

Colossal Ramesses II Statues Surface
Rescue teams uncover two statues and royal inscriptions
In the village of Behbeit al-Higara in Egypt’s Nile Delta, a local man reported finding an inscribed stone block on his property. Egypt’s Supreme Council of Antiquities sent an inspection team to confirm the antiquity of the block, which turned out to be pink granite—a material that was rare and valuable in ancient Egypt. Further rescue operations did not disappoint: The remains of two colossal statues were also uncovered, at least one of which depicts the famed Ramesses II, while the inscriptions on the granite block may contain two of his royal titles.
The Gharbia governate, where the village of Behbeit al-Higara is located, is in the fertile Nile Delta region of Egypt. The area is famous for the remains of a large Ptolemaic-era temple dedicated to the goddess Isis, though this temple was constructed long after the time of Ramesses II (r. 1270–1213 BCE). More closely connected to the recent discoveries are two cartouche fragments bearing Ramesses II’s name that were published in 2017. The researchers analyzing those artifacts proposed that these fragments might indicate the existence of an earlier building dating to Ramesses II. The new finds from Behbeit al-Higara strengthen that hypothesis.
According to Hisham El-Leithy of the Egyptian Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities, the first statue is a colossal head of Ramesses II, measuring about 5 feet tall (about the height of an entire ancient Egyptian!). The pharaoh dons the double crown, symbolizing the unification of Upper and Lower Egypt. The upper portion of the statue’s back bears the inscription, “Horus, the Strong Bull, Beloved of Ma’at.” The original height of the colossal statue may have reached 28 feet.
The second statue measures almost 8 feet tall, though its legs and base are missing. Its facial features are in good condition and, given the resemblance to the other statue, it also likely represents Ramesses II. The pharaoh wears the royal nemes headdress (the familiar striped royal headcloth), though its upper portion has not been preserved.
For its part, the pink granite block measures about 4 feet long and was found just a few feet away from the second statue. Its inscriptions record two royal titles: “King of Upper and Lower Egypt” and “Son of Ra.”
