TITLE: Asian stocks fail to get a lift from Anthropic's IPO pricing
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

By Marc Jones and Ankur Banerjee
LONDON/SINGAPORE, Nov 19 (Reuters) - World stocks were in danger of setting their longest losing streak of the year on Thursday as bruised bulls pinned their hopes on Anthropic's blockbuster market debut later in the day to lift the otherwise fragile mood.
Asian markets hadn't fully read the script as Taiwan and Hong Kong both fell heavily overnight, but Europe's tech stocks quickly caught the thermal in early trading while Nasdaq futures jumped nearly 1.7% ahead of Wall Street's open. [.N]
Elsewhere, gold remained stuck near a 14-month low and benchmark government bond yields [GVD/EUR] ticked up following talk of at least a further 50 basis points worth of US rate rises. But for the time being it was mostly background noise.
Claude-developer Anthropic's IPO is set to be the largest flotation of all time. Its shares were priced at a lofty $83 apiece, valuing the firm at about $1.5 trillion.
"At $83 per share, Anthropic is coming to market above its initial pricing range, but still below our $92 fair value estimate," said Malik Ahmed Khan, a senior equity analyst at Morningstar.
Khan said Anthropic would need to sustain rapid growth while managing the enormous cost of developing and running its models.
It is the first major developer to go public, so how it trades in the coming days and weeks will be heavily scrutinised to gauge the broader sentiment towards AI following the recent selloff in some parts of the market.
"The key question (for investors) is whether they are comfortable with that range of outcomes and have a sufficiently long-term horizon," Khan said.

In commodities, oil prices remained under pressure as the growing view that the oil market is turning to a surplus weighed. Brent crude futures were back under $92 a barrel, set for a third weekly fall. [O/R]
Investors were also focusing on an unprecedented three-way meeting between US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Asia.
The dollar was taking a breather after hitting a 19-month high this week. Hawkish comments from US Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller on Wednesday had bolstered expectations of another US rate hike in December.
The euro was at $1.0996, hovering near its lowest since April 2025. The Japanese yen was at 159.48 per dollar and sterling fetched $1.31515.
Waller had said on CNBC that, if the Fed wanted to get inflation down, it needed to take more "aggressive action", either through a half-point increase at the December meeting or by using the post-meeting statement to set market expectations for further hikes at the start of next year.
