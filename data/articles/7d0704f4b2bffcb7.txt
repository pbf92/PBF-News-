TITLE: GTA 6 Is Even Better When You Play It Side-By-Side With Your Wife
OUTLET: Kotaku

Needless to say, there are lots of people out there currently playing Grand Theft Auto 6, and despite what a lot of headlines and social media jokes insist, it’s not solely a boys’ club. Perhaps more than any other GTA game before it, the latest title attracts all kinds of people, and some of these people happen to be couples living in the same house—mirroring GTA 6’s protagonists just a little bit.
My partner, who has not played a Grand Theft Auto game before, and I have both been excited to lay our hands on GTA 6, and stayed up late last night for the midnight unlock time. But unlike most in our situation, we neither passed the controller back and forth nor went to different rooms to enjoy our games separately. See, because I am an insane person, I have a setup that lets us both start the game simultaneously.
Obviously, both my partner and I are game journalists, and both of us have worked in this industry for many years. When we first started living together, after she moved to San Francisco to join IGN, I foresaw a situation in which we would both have to review games at the same time. Maybe, then, it would make sense for us to have setups that allowed us to play different consoles simultaneously, maybe in different rooms or parts of the same room.
“Why not just put them next to each other?” she posited as we mentally planned out our apartment. Why not, indeed!
That birthed what visiting friends all point out is a truly odd setup, but one that occasionally pays dividends. In the setup, we have my TV on the left and her TV on the right. The consoles on the left side are mine and only hook into my TV, while the consoles on the right side do the same to her TV, with the PC in the middle duplicating the image to both. Usually one person will wear headphones if we’re both playing something.
This works out well with a lot of games. When we were playing through Elden Ring before release side-by-side, one of us would occasionally look at the other’s screen, not recognize where the other person was, and so we’d share notes on how to get there. When Tears of the Kingdom launched at midnight, an informal race to get all the shrines motivated us to solve puzzles in more interesting ways than the other person.
With Grand Theft Auto 6, though, it allowed us to see exactly what little details were changing in the game depending on our different actions and approaches.
In the game’s prologue, Lucia tries her best to make it back to a coffee shop while the police search for her, scouring the city block. Since it’s mere minutes after the opening of the game, my partner and I got there at roughly the same time. She, pragmatic as she is, chose to walk along the sidewalks until the cops eventually picked her up. I tried to cut around the building and swim, and then I got hit in the head by a police boat and was knocked unconscious. Whoops.
For me, in the cutscene that followed, Lucia was being handcuffed to a gurney while EMS and police pushed her into the back of an ambulance. For my partner, Lucia was being manhandled by police into the back of a cop car. In both situations, she pleaded about needing to go back to the coffee shop, but it was maybe a bit more fraught and scary in my case as she fought to maintain consciousness.
The scenes were similar, but different, and it was fascinating to be able to watch them side-by-side like this. That wow factor has kind of kept up throughout as we’ve continued playing. In an early mission, I watched her, playing as Jason, enter a car impound lot in a completely different way than I had. She gave me tips about when I would be able to play as Lucia, noticing that I was bumming around a lot (to which I answered, “But I have to make sure she’s happy with the house first.”)
I wonder if I’m signing up for a lifetime of doing more chores without realizing it.
We’re planning to play the bulk of the rest of the game like this; it probably won’t surprise anyone that we have a lot of TVs around the house and sometimes I move consoles around to play things in my office. But our setup is interesting, and it lets us spend time together while still partaking in hobbies that we love. Tonight we’re both going to continue playing GTA while I set up a laptop on the coffee table to watch the episode of AEW Dynamite that we missed yesterday, and that might be pushing it a little bit. Or maybe three screens is the perfect number?
