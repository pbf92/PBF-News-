TITLE: "GTA 6 is finally here": Microsoft's GitHub hyped a tool that turns PS5 games into Windows 11 apps, days after wiping 3,007 GTA 6 PC port repos, then quietly deleted the post
OUTLET: Windows Latest

Like many of you, I was caught off guard seeing GitHub’s official X account celebrate AnyPS5, an open-source tool that rewrites PlayStation 5 game executables into native Windows and Linux programs, just days after the official GTA 6 launch. The Microsoft-owned platform posted on X saying “GTA 6 is finally here” and then had the audacity to ask if changing where games run crosses “a legal line.” Realising their mistake, the post was deleted shortly after. Weirdly enough, all this happened just two days after they disabled 3,007 repositories of an unofficial GTA 6 PC port at Take-Two’s request.
AnyPS5 only went public in August, yet its progress tracker already shows 18,695 reimplemented PS5 system functions, and its pull request template asks contributors if a change was AI-assisted. If AI agents can help rebuild a console’s software layer in a few months, I can’t help but wonder how long Rockstar’s “PC version later” M.O. survives, especially with PlayStation players already eyeing Windows 11 PCs.
GitHub’s deleted post asked if changing where games run crosses “a legal line”
Here’s what the now-deleted post said:
“GTA 6 is finally here, sparking one of the biggest weeks in gaming and open source on GitHub. The repo AnyPS5 has passed 58K stars since its August launch, rewriting PS5 executables to run on Windows and Linux. Does changing where games run cross a legal line? Share your take. Happy GTA VImas!”
Strangely, there was also an Instagram video by GitHub, with an actual presenter, who shows the AnyPS5 repo card (58,000 stars) and a diagram showing how a “PS5 game file” gets “rewritten by AnyPS5” and “runs on Windows + Linux.”
She then says, “Performance seems to be the main issue for users wanting to run GTA VI,” and cuts to a November 16 AnyPS5 discussion titled “WE HAVE 3 DAYS UNTIL GTA 6 EVERYONE. WE MUST LOCK IN,” where the poster asks everyone to “GET YOUR AGENTS OR YOUR DEEVS AND WORK.” A final slide asks, “Is it legal to change where and how your games run?”
The intention might’ve been to promote Open Source, but GitHub hasn’t explained why it was removed. I honestly don’t understand how a corporate account ends a promo by asking whether the promo is legal. A highly upvoted reply on r/pcmasterrace, mentioned how shocked they were seeing GitHub would flaunt it, considering Microsoft owns it. Of course, Microsoft has quietly pulled a Copilot ad before, too.
AnyPS5 turns PS5 games into Windows apps, but a GTA 6 test didn’t even get past conversion
Emulators recreate a console’s hardware in software, and is common knowledge that it eats your performance. AnyPS5’s relinker converts a PS5 executable into a native Windows or Linux program and connects it to the project’s versions of PS5 system libraries, with “no emulation or separate runtime process.”
As the game runs as a regular program, your CPU needs the PS5’s Zen 2 instruction sets like AVX2. But the usage guide warns that converted games have no console sandbox and can access anything your account can, which is more than enough reason to avoid doing this.
Take-Two had just used GitHub to wipe out 3,007 GTA 6 PC port repos
Leonida is a native PC recompilation of the Xbox version of GTA 6. Take-Two’s DMCA notice says stolen Xbox Series files were found online on November 20, Leonida appeared the next day, and its “bring your own files” instruction was “intentionally misleading.” GitHub disabled the project’s network of 3,007 repositories on November 24.
AnyPS5 is general-purpose and says it doesn’t include or require copyrighted software, firmware or keys. Funnily enough, Sony was in the same boat before in 2000, when a US appeals court ruled that Connectix’s reverse engineering of the PlayStation BIOS was fair use for its emulator.
But Take-Two’s case against Leonida is about the timing and an obvious focus on one game.
Microsoft is on both sides of GTA 6
In case you have obscured yourself from Microsoft, XBOX sells GTA 6 too, and in August, its CTO Scott Van Vliet said Microsoft is “working closely with Take-Two and Rockstar Games“ to protect intellectual property. All it took was three months for another Microsoft account wished everyone “Happy GTA VImas.”
Ironically, the agents AnyPS5 contributors were told to get are what GitHub promoted aggressively. Meanwhile, Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick says the company is “not in the least cavalier“ about AI-assisted reverse engineering.
Rockstar still hasn’t announced a PC version of GTA 6. GTA 5 and Red Dead Redemption 2 both reached PC more than a year after their console launches. With tools like AnyPS5 improving every week, should we have to wait this long for official launches?
