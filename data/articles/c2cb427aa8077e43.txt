TITLE: Tear gas deployed against priests in the Democratic Republic of the Congo
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Clergy have spoken out after police cracked down on a protest in the Democratic Republic of Congo, reportedly tear-gassing the demonstrators while they were praying.
Authorities also banned a large outdoor mass being planned for Tata Raphaël Stadium, Kinshasa.
Cardinal Fridolin Ambongo from the Democratic Republic of the Congo described both events as a violation of the constitutional right of freedom of religion.
“A march can be dispersed, but the church cannot be dispersed. Tear gas can be fired at believers, but the truth cannot be suffocated. Pastors can be intimidated, but the word of God cannot be gagged,” he said.
The harsh action from authorities stems from a land dispute where a seminary is built.
A video posted by the Archdiocese of Lubumbashi shows members of the clergy covering their faces with cloths soaked in water.
A group or protesters can also be seen facing off with police before a canister is deployed and emits a white gas.
UK-based charity Aid to the Church in Need said they’ve been very concerned about the developments from the Central African Nation.
“They were praying the rosary when suddenly these clergy and lay people who were taking part in this peaceful prayer and demonstration were in fact disrupted and dispersed by tear gas that was unleashed as they were walking along,” spokesperson John Pontifex told Premier Christian News.
He highlighted the role of the Church as a charitable institution for a population facing armed conflict, poverty and the second largest Ebola outbreak on record. The Catholic Church provides medical aid and education in the country.
“The Church is a major provider of support that enables people to begin a pathway out of extreme poverty and towards a better world for them and their families, and the fear will inevitably be that if the Church is in any way oppressed, that it will impact these vital services.”
