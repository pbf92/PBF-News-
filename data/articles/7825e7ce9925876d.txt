TITLE: Doctor issues rapid weight gain warning after new jab study
OUTLET: Surrey Live

People stopping weight loss injections such as Wegovy and Mounjaro could regain as much as 1.5 stone within a year, according to recent research. Research publish in teh British Journal of Medicine found that 'cessation of Weight Management Medication (WMM) is followed by rapid weight regain and reversal of beneficial effects on cardiometabolic markers.'
The researchers said "Regain after WMM was faster than after behavioural weight management programmes. These findings suggest caution in short-term use of these drugs without a more comprehensive approach to weight management."
Dr Ann Nainan, GP at Medicspot, said: “The duration that people take weight loss medications for varies depending on each individual's reasons for taking the drugs. For many users, the prospect of coming off weight loss medications can be daunting due to the potential side effects.
“However, before coming off the medication, it’s crucial to speak to a healthcare professional or an expert at an online weight loss clinic to ensure you do so safely and over a period of time. Stopping weight loss medications suddenly can pose health risks and potentially reverse the results you have achieved.”
The dangers of stopping weight loss medications suddenly
- Rapid weight gain. “Recent BMJ research suggests weight often returns quickly after stopping weight-loss medication. A 2026 review in The BMJ found that people who stopped the newer weight loss injections, semaglutide (Wegovy) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro), regained an estimated 9.9 kg (about 1.5 stone) on average within a year, around two-thirds of the weight they had lost.1”
- Increased appetite and cravings. “As weight loss medications help to slow digestion and control ‘food noise’, stopping them suddenly can lead to an increased appetite returning. If this happens, the chances of food cravings and thoughts about eating intensify, which can often lead to unhealthy eating and weight gain.”
- Blood sugar spikes. “As weight loss medications are often prescribed to people with prediabetes and Type 2 diabetes, it’s crucial to know that stopping the medication suddenly can lead to blood sugar spikes and unstable glucose levels. This may result in medical treatment for the changes in blood sugar being required if left unmanaged.”
How to safely come off weight loss medications
“For any users who want to stop taking weight loss medications, it’s important you do so safely by following the points below.”
- Maintain healthy eating habits. “As weight loss medications should be supported alongside a healthy diet, it’s important to maintain this post-medication. While occasional treats are fine to enjoy, healthy foods need to form the bulk of your diet to avoid rapid weight gain.”
- Exercise regularly. “Exercising regularly is key to continuing the results secured while taking weight loss medications. Alongside helping to stabilise metabolism and energy levels, exercise helps to keep the mind focused and avoid ‘food noise’ which can lead to unhealthy habits being introduced into your daily routine.”
- Plan weekly meals. “One of the key habits to avoid to allow for a safe transition off weight loss drugs is to avoid the urge to binge eat, which is difficult when living a busy life. With that in mind, aim to plan meals and cook in bulk where possible, as this will remove the need to grab ready meals and fast food for convenience.”
“If you are considering coming off your medication, then it’s important to seek advice from a professional or organisation that can offer you dedicated weight loss coaching, as they can create a plan and guide you through the process safely and effectively. If you take medications that affect your blood sugar levels, then it could be dangerous to stop suddenly.”
