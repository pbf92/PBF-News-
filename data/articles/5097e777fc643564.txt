TITLE: Jehovah Jireh / The Lord Will Provide
OUTLET: Christianity Today

I’ve been learning a lot about loss and grief in recent days. Empty spaces. Quiet rooms. Familiar traditions that now feel different. Memories that surface without warning. A deep sorrow for moments that will never come again.
Advent meets us in the tension of hope and sorrow, of darkness and light. It does not deny sorrow, it gently reminds us that sorrow is not the story’s end. My belief that joy will triumph over sorrow is tied to one of God’s most comforting names, Jehovah Jireh—“The Lord Will Provide.”
This name first appears in Genesis 22 during a profound moment of testing in Abraham’s life. God asked Abraham to trust him completely, without understanding the path ahead. As Abraham walked up Mt. Moriah with Isaac, he carried both obedience and anguish. No father could fully understand God’s command for Abraham to sacrifice his own son. Yet Abraham trusted the character of God instead of his own reasoning.
At just the right moment, God provided a ram to be sacrificed instead of Isaac. And Abraham named that place Jehovah Jireh,“The Lord Will Provide.”
This name reveals more than God’s ability to supply material needs. It reveals the loving, attentive heart of the Father who sees the way ahead and prepares provision for his children even before they recognize their need. He is not rushed by anxiety nor delayed by our need. Jehovah Jireh means God’s provision is perfectly timed and revealed according to his perfect wisdom.
There are seasons when we wonder how things will come together. We question whether God truly sees our circumstances. Yet Jehovah Jireh reminds us that the provision we most need is rooted not in our situation but in God’s unchanging character.
In a three-year span, I gently carried the grief of losing my father, my father-in-law, and my mother-in-law. My daughter’s family moved over two thousand miles for seminary. In addition, I became an empty nester and, after two decades, left a formative women’s ministry leadership role.
Each of these experiences involved significant loss. Grief marked the value of what had been. And even while trusting the fruitfulness of what comes next, it was right to grieve—to grieve with hope (1 Thess. 4:13).
That is often how God provides: Not only in visible ways, but in the hidden places of the heart. He provides strength for weary souls, wisdom for tough decisions, comfort in sorrow, and grace for weakness. Sometimes what we need most is not a change in circumstance but assurance that God is present in the middle of it. Jehovah Jireh provides not only solutions but his sustaining peace.
Genesis 22 points us to God’s greatest provision. Just as he supplied a substitute for Isaac and Abraham, he provided salvation for us through Jesus Christ. The incarnation stands as the clearest evidence that God sees humanity’s deepest need and responds with redemption, mercy, provision, and hope.
Jehovah Jireh invites us to live with open hands and surrendered hearts because faith does not require our full understanding of God’s ways, it only requires trust in him when we cannot yet see his plan.
Wife, mother, and grandmother committed to serve individuals and families through ministry, counseling, and mentoring. Experienced as a children’s and women’s ministry pastor, rehabilitation counselor, homeschool educator, and counselor for children and families.
