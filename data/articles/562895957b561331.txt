TITLE: Meta Platforms commits up to $145B to AI as its ad machine keeps paying off
OUTLET: Crypto Briefing

Meta narrowed its 2026 capital spending guidance to between $135 billion and $145 billion, betting that AI will keep its advertising business growing
Meta Platforms is spending on artificial intelligence at a pace that would make most national budgets blush. The company has narrowed its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to a range of $135 billion to $145 billion, with the bulk aimed at AI infrastructure.
The update arrived with Meta’s third-quarter 2026 earnings report on October 21, 2026. The new range tightens an earlier forecast of $130 billion to $145 billion. The top end stayed put, but the floor rose.
Where the money is going
The spending covers the full AI stack. That means data centers, chips, networking products, and the systems needed to train Meta’s Llama AI models.
For perspective, Meta’s capital expenditures in 2025 came in at approximately $72 billion. The 2026 guidance puts the company on track to roughly double that figure in a single year.
The ad business is already feeling the lift
The company says its AI work is already improving content recommendations, ad impressions, and revenue per ad.
Q3 2026 revenue reached $64.6 billion, a 26% increase from the same quarter a year earlier. Advertising accounted for $63.06 billion of that total.

The cash flow problem
Free cash flow in Q3 2026 came in at -$2.12 billion.
Meta plans to introduce a new class of non-voting Class C stock and is looking to raise up to $95 billion through equity offerings to fund its AI ambitions.
The non-voting structure lets Meta bring in fresh capital without weakening Mark Zuckerberg’s control over the company.
Background: from dorm room to data centers
Zuckerberg founded Facebook in his Harvard dorm room in 2004. Two decades later, the company is pouring tens of billions into chips and server farms.
Zuckerberg and his team increasingly view AI as the critical lever for refining ad tools and deepening engagement on platforms like Facebook and Instagram.
