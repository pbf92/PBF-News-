TITLE: Former senior Methodist minister found guilty of sexually assaulting 5 women
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A former senior Methodist minister in Cornwall has been found guilty of eleven sexual assault and indecent assault charges involving five women.
Rev Steven Wild, 72, from St Ives, was convicted at Truro Crown Court of ten counts of sexual assault and one of indecent assault, relating to offences committed between 2004 and 2015.
Wild, who chaired the Cornwall and Isles of Scilly Methodist District from 2008 until 2022, had denied all the charges.
Prosecutors told the court that the five women had come into contact with Wild through their involvement with the Methodist Church.
The allegations first came to light in 2018 during a safeguarding audit of the Church in Cornwall, which led five women to come forward or be identified as complainants.
Opening the prosecution case, Brian Fitzherbert told jurors that Wild had been well regarded nationally and internationally within the Church, almost to the point of having "celebrity" status.
He told the court that he wasn't alleging that Wild was violent or used force, but that the church leader's senior position and reputation for being "tactile" had allowed him to behave inappropriately towards women, with complaints often dismissed as "just Steve being Steve".
The prosecutor said the alleged assaults involved non-consensual sexual touching, which often took place in public and left the women feeling uncomfortable.
One woman told the court she first met Wild in 2004 when he was conducting a service in Cornwall. She described being held and hugged for too long and kissed repeatedly on the cheek, while Wild made groaning noises and told her she was special.
When she raised concerns with another minister, she was reportedly told Wild was a wonderful man and that the Church was at war with Satan. She was warned that making a fuss would undermine that war effort.
The prosecution also described an alleged incident during a congregation trip to the Isles of Scilly, after which the woman was left crying and shaking.
Another complainant alleged that Wild pushed his groin against her thigh, while a further woman said he had touched her bottom at a Methodist conference.
He denied this. According to The Falmouth Packet, he told jurors: “I push my groin into nobody, except my lovely wife.”
The incidents were said to have taken place across Cornwall, Devon, Bristol, Southport and Derbyshire.
In a statement released on Wednesday, the Methodist Church acknowledged its "failure" in the handling of the complaints said its' prayers remain with all those affected.
Rev Dr Jonathan Hustler, Secretary of the Conference said: "We apologise unreservedly to all those who have made complaints and weren’t listened to or taken seriously, and we recognise the courage it takes to speak about experiences of abuse. Experiences like these can affect people in many different ways and the Church is committed to improving how it listens and responds to anyone who wishes to share concerns, alongside supporting them and finding a resolution."
After Wild is sentenced, the Church will resume "internal processes which had been paused while criminal proceedings took place".
While said it recognises it still has a lot of work to do to ensure its churches and communities are safe, the Church said it has already made "significant revisions" to its safeguarding work and upcoming internal work will consider if anything more needs to be done.
Rev Hustler said: "Safeguarding Officers are now overseen by one centralised management structure, not by District Chairs. In Cornwall, safeguarding officers and others are continuing to address the culture within the District, reinforcing the expectation that concerns of any kind are shared and acted upon immediately. The District is committed to ensuring it listens and responds well to those who raise concerns in the future."
He added: "We will continue to strive to ensure that everyone connected with The Methodist Church understands that safeguarding is everyone’s responsibility. "
Wild was released on bail following the verdict and is due to be sentenced on 18 December. The judge said all sentencing options remain open.
