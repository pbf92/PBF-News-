TITLE: ‘Grand Theft Auto VI’ couldn’t have come at a better time for America
OUTLET: MS NOW

Editor’s note: This article contains mild spoilers for the opening hours of “Grand Theft Auto VI.”
I’m fixing the leaking roof of my house. I’ve finished painting the bedroom, rebuilding the stairs that lead up to the house and repairing a broken railing on my balcony. It’s hard work, but it’s all worth it — I’ve got to make this house look good, after all. I’m doing all of this to prepare for the return of my partner, Lucia, who has been behind bars for an attempted robbery and might be getting out on an early release program. This is how “Grand Theft Auto VI” opens — not with a bombastic set piece, but with housework that makes the world feel real, and makes it so that I am invested in this world from the jump.
It is impossible to overstate how massive a deal “GTA 6” is. The latest title from developer Rockstar Games made its debut Thursday, 13 years after the series’ last installment. “Grand Theft Auto V’ was already one of the bestselling entertainment products of all time, and the decade-plus wait for its sequel only increased demand. The views on the reveal trailer from 2023 total 307 million and counting. Per analysts, the game sold almost 20 million units prior to release, and that number is going to climb. The game also probably couldn’t have come at a better time, especially for America.
“Grand Theft Auto” has long been a satire of American society and its most extreme elements, and it’s never been subtle about that fact. The entire plot of “Grand Theft Auto IV” is a twist on the American dream, on the idea that consumerism is the endgame of adulthood. Protagonist Niko Bellic arrives in Liberty City because he was fed stories about how easy it was to become rich and powerful. One mission in “Grand Theft Auto V” features the player sabotaging a prototype phone for a company called Lifeinvader, whose founder and CEO is a thinly veiled caricature of Mark Zuckerberg and tech CEOs in general. The mission ends with you killing him.
The early hours of “Grand Theft Auto VI” are no different. The game is set in Leonida, somewhere between a fictionalization and a caricature of Florida. At one point I drove past an anti-trans propaganda billboard which suggested that the endgame of gun reform was to force transgender operations on people. An hour later, I got out of my car near a mission, but accidentally brushed against another car. The driver of that car got out and threatened me with a baseball bat in an elevated mockery of road rage. I, wanting to get back to the mission, pulled out my gun to defuse the situation — only for a bystander to pull out their gun and shoot me multiple times, killing me. Meanwhile, the radio played a political ad stating that a female politician was using her beauty to distract people from her policies (though the game said it in far more vulgar terms).
Somehow, despite the more mature feeling of the general world and characters, the satire feels less subtle than ever. Perhaps this at least partly reflects how wild modern life is and how cartoonish politics have become in the 13 years since the last game. After all, that was two Trump presidencies ago. A lot has happened since “GTA 5” — enough that a history book would require hundreds of pages to explain the overall impact on society. It’s hard to mock something that could feasibly be seen on live TV within a week.
