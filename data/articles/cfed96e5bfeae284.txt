TITLE: When every minute counts: Real-time situational picture to improve patient care in crises
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Whether it's a natural disaster, a terrorist attack—or NATO Article 5 scenario: To ensure that a large number of injured persons receive proper care, it is essential to achieve their optimal distribution among appropriate hospitals. This requires a reliable overview of local treatment capacities, which has so far been lacking. Under the leadership of Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin, an interdisciplinary team of medical and IT experts—the REAKT consortium—aims to establish precisely such a situational picture. Funded by the Federal Ministry of Research, Technology, and Space (BMFTR), the consortium is developing a digital platform providing real-time visibility into medical resources, patient flows, and supply chains.
In the event of an acute crisis, the initial focus is on the people who need to be treated as quickly as possible due to their injuries. However, particularly if the situation persists over a longer period, the needs of the rest of the population must not be neglected. “In order to ensure the optimal distribution of injured patients, we need to know more than simply the number of available beds,” as Prof. Vera Jaecker, head of the Trauma Surgery Section and deputy director at the Center for Musculoskeletal Surgery of Charité’s Campus Virchow-Klinikum stated. “A free bed is of little use if the surgical team, the right instruments, or blood supplies are missing.”
A real-time platform instead of manual queries
What matters instead is the operational treatment capacity—that is, what a hospital can actually provide at that given moment. “So far, for example, there is a registry for intensive care beds and systems that connect emergency medical services with hospitals,” explains Vera Jaecker, who coordinates the REAKT project. “But the information is not interlinked, and there is no overview of the availability of surgical and trauma resources. At present, these can only be retrieved manually.”
To improve the management of patient care in crisis situations, the REAKT team aims to integrate existing data systems and supplement them with additional information: current operating room and bed capacities, qualified personnel trained to treat the relevant injuries, surgical instruments and sterilization capacities, blood supplies, medications, as well as inventory levels and supply chains for vital medical devices. This allows bottlenecks to be identified at an early stage, before they jeopardize patient care. Dispatch centers, emergency medical services, hospitals, and the relevant authorities should all be able to access the platform. The situational picture is complemented by a communication system that allows all stakeholders to exchange information securely.
System brings together heterogeneous data
In case IT systems or communication networks fail, resilience is a core principle of the project. “To date, there is no approach in the German healthcare system that systematically integrates the various and multifaceted data sources from hospitals, civil protection agencies, and medical logistics,” explains Vera Jaecker. “We are developing what is known as a federated system that connects existing data sources with one another, rather than replacing them with a single centralized solution. This makes it less vulnerable should individual parts fail.”
In developing the software, physicians are collaborating with researchers in the fields of communication systems, data infrastructures, and systems engineering: In addition to Charité, partners in the collaborative project include the Robert Koch Institute and the Fraunhofer Institutes FOKUS and ISST, among others. The concept is initially being developed and tested within the Berlin Trauma Network of the German Society for Trauma Surgery (DGU). This alliance of hospitals is already collaborating with regard to providing care for critically injured patients. Once the REAKT platform has been successfully tested in trial runs, the plan is to expand the concept throughout Germany.
About REAKT
The REAKT consortium is coordinated by Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin; other partners in the consortium include the Fraunhofer Institute for Open Communication Systems (FOKUS), the Fraunhofer Institute for Software and Systems Engineering (ISST), the Robert Koch Institute (RKI), the AUC – Academy of Trauma Surgery, and Capgemini Germany. The consortium is collaborating with the Senate Department for Higher Education and Research, Health and Long-Term Care; the Bundeswehr Hospital Berlin; hospitals in the Berlin Trauma Network; the German Society for Trauma Surgery as well as the companies Miele Professional and B. Braun. REAKT is receiving 4.9 million euros in funding over three years from the Federal Ministry of Research, Technology, and Space (BMFTR) within the context of the “Cross-Sectoral Measures for Resilient Supply” funding guideline.
