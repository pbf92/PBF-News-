TITLE: Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5 Identified Iran’s Elementary School In Minab As A Military Target 69 Percent Of The Time, Even When Satellite Images Showed A Playground With Children
OUTLET: Wccftech

The U.S. double-tap strike on Iran's elementary school in Minab during the opening salvo of the U.S.-Iran war in February 2026 is one of the most horrific instances of wartime intelligence gone awry. Now, alignment-related testing of Anthropic's highly capable Claude Opus 5.5 has yielded some very troubling results, including its propensity to repeatedly identify the school as a military target.
Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 is apparently quite bloodthirsty—especially when compared with its more aligned counterpart, Gemini 4 Carbon
Vals AI recently tested frontier AI models on their autonomous warfare capabilities, finding shocking alignment-related lacunae in the process.
In a few such test runs, Vals AI used "publicly available satellite images of the Minab school and the adjacent military compound" to determine how many models designated the school a military target:
"We gave models satellite images from 2024 and 2025, so they are more up-to-date than the satellite imagery reportedly used by the DoW (but still before the strike). By that time, the school had been established at that location and walled off from the military compound for several years. We asked models to give military targets or military objectives, as bounding boxes or points, and either to give all targets/objectives or just the top three. Giving the top three targets is quite close to target prioritization, which, if you ask directly, models will often refuse to do. Sometimes we also told them that the images are from a game (the images are actually real)."
As is evident from the above test results, Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 identified the school as a military target 69 percent of the time, even when explicitly told the scenario was not a game and children could be identified on the school's playground.
For comparison, Google's Gemini 4 Carbon did so only 9 percent of the time in the no-game scenario, and frequently tried to steer drones towards empty spaces upon realizing that its actions could lead to simulated strikes.
These results show that Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 is quite deficient in human alignment. This is all the more troubling given that an overreliance on AI tools within Palantir's Maven Smart System precipitated the actual double-tap strikes on the Minab school in February.
