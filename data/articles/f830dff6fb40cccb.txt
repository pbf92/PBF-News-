TITLE: Manchester United issues three-year ban after fans hurl tennis balls on pitch
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

Manchester United issues three-year ban after fans hurl tennis balls on pitch
Supporters threw tennis balls onto the pitch in protest of the club's ownership
Manchester United have reportedly banned four fans who hurled tennis balls onto the Old Trafford pitch, with one supporter receiving a three-year stadium ban.
The tennis balls were thrown in protest of the club's ownership during November’s Champions League match against Roma.
Now, at least one fan has received a three-year ban as the club's guidelines state that a 'missile throwing' offence is punishable with a three-year suspension, The Athletic reports.
It is understood that the club's view on 'missile throwing' is that it cannot be lenient and a strict line must be drawn due to a risk of harm.
The Manchester Evening News previously reported the demonstration against the Glazers and Sir Jim Ratcliffe started at 20 minutes and five seconds - a time selected to represent the year the Glazers came into situ at Old Trafford.
Hundreds of yellow and green balloons - the colours used to protest against the Glazers - were also released from the Stretford End prior to kick-off as the teams walked out of the tunnel.

Greater Manchester Police (GMP) confirmed earlier this month that three fans were arrested for items being thrown on the pitch, according to The Athletic.
The protests come amid growing frustration among supporters towards the Glazers, co-owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe, director of football Jason Wilcox, and chief executive officer Omar Berrada.
This had led to the dismissal of Michael Carrick, with Mauricio Pochettino appointed as his replacement.
