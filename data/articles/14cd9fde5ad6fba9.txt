TITLE: Silicon Valley bishop plans for nationwide AI regulation lobbying campaign
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The bishop who oversees much of Silicon Valley has called for greater guardrails on artificial intelligence, with the Catholic Church aiming to build a “groundswell” of pressure for regulation.
Bishop Oscar Cantú of San Jose, leader of the US Conference of Catholic Bishops’ AI taskforce, said AI represented an “all-hands-on-deck” moment where church leaders and congregations must lobby politicians to ensure humans remain in control of technological development.
He told Politico: “We don’t want to stifle progress, but we need progress to be safe. We need people to be safe, and we want humanity to be at the front and center and not sidelined by the technology.”
It comes after Pope Leo’s encyclical, Magnifica Humanitas, which warned that AI needs to be “disarmed” with developers working for the common good of humanity while “maintaining the human person”, rather than prioritising profit.
The Bishops’ taskforce, with eight clergy and three academic consultants, has been meeting with developers about the moral issues surrounding AI.
Bishop Oscar’s presentation at the Church plenary assembly last week came after a series of high-profile technology failures which disrupted daily activities, including the Microsoft outage and the grounding of flights across America.
He told OSV News these disruptions “set us on edge and make us wonder what’s happening”, even if AI was not to blame, because of society’s reliance on technology.
“I wouldn’t say the technologists have warned us, but they’ve mentioned their timeline. They expect this technology to be at full speed within six months to a year. And so we need to raise our voice. We need to educate ourselves quickly, calmly and raise our voice in the public square in this debate,” he added.
In his address, Bishop Oscar called for the Church to become “a school of discernment in the digital age… where people learn to verify, to slow down, and to reason together.” He also urged for developers to be held accountable for when their technologies cause harm, and for greater protections covering children and vilnerable people.
