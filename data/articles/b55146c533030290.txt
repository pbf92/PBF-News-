Hitman found guilty 18 years after being paid £20k to shoot taxi driver dead
David Harrison, 77, has been convicted of murdering father-of-four Harun Khan in Tipton, West Midlands, in 2008
A hotman who murdered a taxi driver in exchange for £20,000 back in 2008 has been convicted of the crime while already serving a life sentence for a separate contract killing.
It took jurors at Wolverhampton Crown Court under three hours to find 77-year-old David Harrison guilty of murdering father-of-four Harun Khan in Tipton, West Midlands, on 3 March 2008.
Harrison, who declined to attend his trial and was absent when the verdicts were delivered on Thursday morning, will be sentenced on Tuesday, Judge Mr Justice Wall KC informed the court.
The jury also convicted Harrison of wounding another individual in a shooting at Dudley's Thornleigh trading estate in 2007, along with two firearms offences relating to alleged possession of a 7.65mm self-loading pistol in March 2008 and a shotgun in December 2007.
The court heard that Harrison, who was imprisoned for life in 2012 with a 37-year minimum term for an unrelated murder, admitted to police in September 2014 his involvement in the shooting of Mr Khan and also Talib Hussain, who was shot in the leg in 2007.
Prosecution counsel Michael Burrows KC stated Harrison provided details "that only he as the gunman would know" and revealed he received £20,000 to carry out the fatal shooting but declined to identify who paid him.
Regarding the killing of Mr Khan, Harrison informed police he observed the victim's black Peugeot Minibus taxi arriving home, stood up as the driver exited the vehicle, and then shot him twice. During his opening speech, Mr Burrows told the jury: "He said the man fell to the ground and he pointed the gun at the man's head and pulled the trigger again before making off.
"He believed the name of the taxi driver he had killed was Khan. He would not name who had paid him for the shooting.
"But he went on to say, 'the geezer who wanted the job done was from the same community... he offered the work to somebody outside the community to deflect the blame from him. I never met him but I know who it was'."
The jury heard that a family feud had resulted in death threats being issued against 39-year-old Mr Khan, who passed away in hospital following a fatal chest wound sustained outside his Tudor Street residence in Tipton.
Mr Hussain sustained gunshot injuries to his thigh, chest and forearm when a balaclava-wearing assailant armed with a sawn-off shotgun attacked him at approximately 7pm on 14 December 2007, as he arrived at his vehicle repair workshop with his brother.
The victim's lung collapsed and he remained hospitalised for a minimum of two weeks. Harrison, formerly of Bilston, West Midlands, claimed he received £4,000 for shooting Mr Hussain.
The court was informed that in December 2012, Harrison was found guilty of the separate murder of Richard Deakin in Staffordshire during July 2010 and received a life sentence with a minimum tariff of 37 years.
Mr Justice Wall thanked the jury for their work in this "somewhat unusual" case in which the accused chose not to take part in proceedings. He stated Harrison had the option to attend court for the sentencing hearing if he wished to be, but in the event he was not, he would order his remarks be transcribed and passed to the defendant.
West Midlands Police are urging anyone with knowledge about who commissioned Harrison to conduct the shootings to step forward.
Senior Investigating Officer Jenny Birch stated: "Harrison admitted being a killer for hire and taking the life of Mr Khan, who left behind four children, in exchange for £20,000. He's clearly a very dangerous individual, having now been convicted of two murders and the shooting of a third man.
"While we are pleased that we've been able to get some answers for Mr Khan's family, the question of who paid Harrison to kill him, and to shoot a man the previous year, remains unanswered to this day. Now that nearly two decades have passed, now is the time for anyone who can help us answer those questions to come forward.
"Mr Khan's family, and the man he shot in 2007, deserve that answer. It would give them the closure that they deserve."
Anyone holding information can ring 101 citing Operation Annelid or contact Crimestoppers anonymously on 0800 555 111.
