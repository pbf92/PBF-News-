TITLE: TIMI Risk Score Predicts Early Mortality but Declines After Coronary Intervention
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

The Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction Risk Score demonstrates good discrimination for in-hospital mortality among patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention, but its predictive accuracy declines across longer follow-up intervals and remains unproven for clinical decision-making, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in BMC Cardiovascular Disorders.
Developed as a simple prognostic assessment for acute coronary syndrome, the score is widely used to evaluate risk, yet its discriminative performance following percutaneous coronary intervention varies widely across distinct patient populations, score instruments, and follow-up durations. To clarify its predictive value, investigators conducted an instrument-specific systematic evaluation of available post-intervention literature.
Researchers searched PubMed, the Cochrane Library, and ScienceDirect from their inception to June 22, 2026, targeting studies that evaluated a stand-alone version of the score in acute coronary syndrome patients treated with percutaneous coronary intervention. The systematic search identified 23 eligible studies representing 28,476 patients. Among these, 21 cohorts evaluated the STEMI instrument in ST-elevation myocardial infarction populations, two cohorts assessed the UA/NSTEMI instrument in non-ST-elevation acute coronary syndrome, and a cohort combining acute and chronic coronary syndromes was excluded from the analysis.
To synthesize discrimination across cohorts, investigators pooled logit-transformed area under the receiver operating characteristic curve values for the STEMI instrument using restricted maximum likelihood random-effects models. The statistical approach calculated 95 percent confidence intervals using the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman method along with 95 percent prediction intervals to evaluate expected performance across new clinical settings. Strata containing fewer than three studies, the UA/NSTEMI instrument, and major adverse cardiac events were evaluated descriptively. Methodological quality was appraised using the PROBAST tool for risk of bias, while certainty was evaluated through the GRADE framework adapted for prediction models.
For patients evaluated with the STEMI instrument, the pooled area under the curve reached 0.83, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.76 to 0.88 and a 95 percent prediction interval from 0.61 to 0.94, indicating good discrimination for in-hospital mortality. Discrimination remained acceptable at short-term and intermediate milestones, showing a pooled area under the curve of 0.79 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.68 to 0.86 and a prediction interval of 0.47 to 0.94 at 30 days, and 0.78 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.71 to 0.83 and a prediction interval of 0.57 to 0.90 at 1 year. However, discrimination decreased beyond 2 years to a pooled estimate of 0.68, with a confidence interval of 0.55 to 0.79 and a wide prediction interval of 0.30 to 0.91, alongside substantial unexplained heterogeneity reflected by I-squared values between 85 and 93 percent.
Predictive discrimination for major adverse cardiac events proved lower and notably inconsistent, with area under the curve values ranging between 0.50 and 0.78 and a median of 0.68 across seven included studies. In an exploratory pooled analysis of the only comparable composite outcome, which combined all-cause death, myocardial infarction, and stroke across three studies, the pooled area under the curve was 0.67 with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 0.54 to 0.77. For the UA/NSTEMI instrument, reported area under the curve values ranged from 0.55 to 0.68 across only two studies, which precluded meta-analytic pooling and left evidence for that specific instrument insufficient.
Overall study quality and evidentiary certainty revealed significant shortcomings across the synthesized literature. None of the 23 reviewed studies met the criteria for a low overall risk of bias under PROBAST assessment, and evidentiary certainty graded under the prediction-model framework remained low or very low across all evaluated outcomes. The authors concluded that wide prediction intervals, severe heterogeneity, and low certainty substantially restrict the transportability of these predictive estimates to other healthcare settings. Because neither model calibration nor practical clinical utility has been formally established in this population, the findings do not support a defined clinical role for the score after percutaneous coronary intervention.
