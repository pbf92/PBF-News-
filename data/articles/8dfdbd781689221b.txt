TITLE: More than 1,500 church leaders urge government to drop 'conversion therapy' ban
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

More than 1,500 church leaders from across denominations have urged the government to drop its proposed ‘conversion therapy’ ban, warning it could “criminalise” Christian teaching.
A further 2,000 supporters have also put their name to an open letter to equalities minister Bridget Phillipson, stating the draft Conversion Practices Bill would also threaten “normal, responsible Christian parenting”.
The government states its plans target abusive behaviour which puts pressure on someone to change their sexuality or gender identity. Breaking the law could be punishable with a jail term of up to five years.
Signatories of the letter co-ordinated by the Greater Love Declaration believe it would create “an ill-defined and wide-ranging criminal offence” that could include honest pastoral conversations, such as a parent talking to a child about transgender beliefs, could fall foul of the law.
The letter tells the minister: “None of our churches would ever condone abusive practices of the kind which advocates for this bill have implied take place in Christian churches, and which are thankfully already illegal. Nevertheless, as drafted this bill would criminalise normal, healthy Christian ministry, along with normal, responsible Christian parenting."
It explains how signatories follow the Christian teaching of “goodness of marriage (the sexually-faithful union of one man and one woman for life), its importance for family life, and the moral duty to keep sexual intimacy for marriage,” alongside the belief that “sex cannot be changed”.
“The Bill creates a speech crime, and those who teach mainstream, historic Christian doctrine would inevitably be judged guilty of it," the letter adds. "Needless to say, we will not cease to fulfil our duty to God to teach his ways faithfully, even if an unjust law should make us criminals for doing so.”
It questions the “appropriating” of the Christian concept of conversion, of “turning, in obedience to Jesus Christ and by the power of the Holy Spirit, from self, sin and pride to humble trust in God.” The letter states it is “not only grossly offensive to Christians but will inevitably catch genuine, faithful, historic Christian ministry and Christian parenting in its scope.”
A committee of MPs and Peers has been appointed by the government to scrutinise its draft legislation. At a hearing earlier this month, Christian campaigner and Stop Conversion Therapy Coalition founder Jayne Ozanne outlined her support for the measures.
She said: “Prayer goes to the heart of this. I speak as a Christian; I speak as someone with a deep love of prayer. But not all prayer is healthy or good. In my case, it caused me, sadly, after 20 years of seeking out prayer, to end up in hospital fighting for my life because my body couldn’t take the strain of the constant messaging that who I was was un`acceptable.”
The committee’s public call for evidence, which asks questions about religion and belief, closes at 5pm on Monday. It will report back in January, before any bill is laid before parliament.
