TITLE: YouTube adds 15 firms to its Creator Partnerships API
OUTLET: PPC Land

YouTube today welcomed 15 companies as new partners of its Creator Partnerships API, the interface through which outside influencer software, agencies and commerce platforms draw on YouTube's data about creators and their audiences. The list, published on the YouTube for Business LinkedIn page on November 23, 2026, runs from influencer platforms Traackr, GRIN and Social Native to StreamElements, the Korean e-commerce platform Cafe24, Taiwan's iKala and firms the post itself places in Indonesia, Hong Kong and Berlin. "We're excited to welcome new partners to the #YouTubeCreatorPartnershipsAPI ecosystem this November," the post reads. It names the companies but says nothing about what each one built, in which markets, or with which parts of the API.
In Short
YouTube named 15 more companies that connect their tools to the data behind its creator marketplace, the system brands use to find, hire and measure YouTube creators. It matters to brands and agencies that run creator campaigns through outside software, because those tools can now draw on YouTube's own audience figures instead of estimates. The post gives no detail on what each company integrated, and at least two of the "new" names had already been linked to the API earlier in 2026.
What the post says
The announcement is short. After the opening line, YouTube for Business describes the partners' role in one sentence: "By integrating directly with YouTube's data and insights, these partners are making it easier than ever for marketers to find the right voices, streamline workflows, and scale creator partnerships globally." It then lists the companies under the line "Check out the latest," each name tagged to a LinkedIn company page.
That framing is YouTube's own. The post offers no figures - no count of creators reached through the new partners, no campaign results, no date by which integrations went live. Three of the 15 entries carry place names: Fair Influence is followed by "(Indonesia)," TECDO appears as "TECDO HONG KONG LIMITED," and Joli is written as "Joli Berlin." Two entries combine names. "Skill Metaverse (Sandbox Network)" links to two separate company pages, and "SAMY Isotype" tags only SAMY's page, with "Isotype" left as plain text.
The 15 November entries
Two names that are not new
The word "new" sits awkwardly against the public record for two of the 15. StreamElements already appeared among 17 companies in Google's "YouTube Creator Partnerships API Directory" on June 2, 2026, according to Net Influencer, which listed the directory's entries as CreatorIQ, TRIBE, Fabulate, #paid, Alfan Group, Brand Ripplr, Companion, impact.com, Influencer, Meltwater, NiceHouse, Open Influence, Sprout Social, StreamElements, Superfiliate, The Outloud Group and Viral Nation. Cafe24, for its part, was described on August 10, 2026 as the first company in Asia to integrate the API, according to Net Influencer, which cited an August 6 report by Seoul Economic Daily. In that report, Cafe24 said customers could use the API directly inside its creator marketing service.
So what, exactly, is new in November? The post does not explain the overlap. A listing could reflect a second integration, broader access, or simply a formal welcome into a monthly cohort for work completed earlier. None of these readings is confirmed by YouTube.
The cohort format itself is not new. On September 4, 2026, indiantelevision.com reported an "August Partner Integration cohort" of six companies, according to YouTube for Business: Tomato Interactive, Unfy, Precisify, Global OneClick, Later and Creator Ads. Counting the June directory, the August cohort and today's post, 37 distinct companies have been publicly tied to the API, a PPC Land count in which StreamElements appears twice. That is not a full census: Net Influencer described the June directory as not comprehensive.
What the API is
YouTube unveiled the current shape of its creator business at its NewFront presentation in New York. In a YouTube blog post dated March 23, 2026, Melissa Hsieh Nikolic, Director of Product Management for YouTube Ads, introduced YouTube Creator Partnerships as the new name for BrandConnect, with tools inside YouTube Studio for creators and inside Google Ads and Display & Video 360 for advertisers. The same post said that through an expanded Creator Partnerships API, select partners could bring those capabilities into their own tools, and that the creator pool spanned more than 3 million members of the YouTube Partner Program. A day later PPC Land reported that YouTube Creator Partnerships replaced BrandConnect in seven markets: the US, India, Indonesia, the UK, Brazil, Australia and Canada.
Access was restricted from the start. Net Influencer reported on March 30, 2026 that the API was open only to a select group of outside influencer marketing platforms, that access was invite-only, and that there was no self-serve route for individual developers or for most brands and agencies. CreatorIQ, TRIBE and Fabulate were among the first to receive it, according to that report. Australian outlet Mediaweek reported on March 27 that Fabulate had been selected as a launch partner, and impact.com described itself in an April 21 release as an early adopter.
Google's directory page presents the listed companies as agencies and platforms that use the API to help advertisers reach their audiences. Its listings carry no dates.
What partners say the API provides
Neither YouTube's March post nor today's post lists the API's data fields. The partners' own material fills part of that gap.
The common thread is first-party data. Without platform-supplied figures, influencer tools rely on public profile data or on creators connecting their own analytics accounts. Under the API, YouTube supplies the audience breakdown itself for creators who have opted in, according to CreatorIQ. That shifts the basis of creator selection from a vendor's estimate to a platform's own figures - and makes YouTube, rather than the vendor, the source of the numbers on which money is allocated.
How a campaign is linked, according to partner documentation
The mechanics of tying a sponsored video to a campaign are described most fully in Sprout Social's help pages, which present the company as one of the API's launch partners. According to Sprout Social, the process has four steps:
- The brand creates a "Brand Deal" in the partner's software, which produces a campaign code.
- The creator uploads the video, marks it as paid promotion, selects the brand under "Brands" and enters the campaign code.
- As an alternative route, the brand sends a "Create a Video Collaboration" request, which the creator accepts in YouTube Studio.
- Once the video is linked, the campaign summary shows YouTube metrics, with audience demographics and Total, Paid and Organic views.
According to CreatorIQ, the campaign code also applies the paid partnership label to the video and supports the permission a brand needs to put paid media behind it. That paid step is creator partnerships boost, which YouTube's March post described as the product formerly known as partnership ads. Sprout Social's page does not state a Partner Program requirement or an opt-in condition for the linking itself.
The geography of the November list
At least six of the 15 entries are tied to Asia. The post itself places Fair Influence in Indonesia and TECDO in Hong Kong. Cafe24 is a South Korean e-commerce platform, according to Net Influencer; TAGby and Featuring are also Korean influencer marketing businesses, and iKala is based in Taiwan. Joli is the only entry the post ties to a European city.
The spread matters because Creator Partnerships itself is no longer confined to the seven launch markets. PPC Land reported on July 5, 2026 that YouTube added the UK, Germany, Japan and Singapore on the creator side, with a help page listing 24 countries and regions including Hong Kong, Indonesia and South Korea. Google Ads Help now states that, starting in June 2026, core Creator Partnerships features are available in all locations where the YouTube Partner Program is active, while describing the product as in open beta.
Why this matters for marketers
Creator budgets are now large enough for measurement plumbing to carry commercial weight. PPC Land reported in July that US creator ad spend was projected at $43.9 billion for 2026, up from $37 billion in 2025, according to IAB. In November, an IAB survey of 53 industry leaders found that 66% wanted a common definition of a "view", with 67% naming measurement as the top problem in the creator economy. An API that hands outside tools YouTube's own audience and performance numbers addresses part of that complaint, but it also concentrates the definition of performance in the platform selling the ads.
YouTube's playbooks have treated outside tools as one of three doors into the same system. The creator marketing playbook distributed in May said brands could match with creators directly in Google Ads, through Display & Video 360 or through select API partners. In April, PPC Land covered how creator partnerships boost works inside Google Ads, including unified organic and paid reporting. The headline figures YouTube attaches to that system have moved: PPC Land reported on October 15 that a YouTube for Business LinkedIn post raised the creator boost lift claim to 30% from 20% on the same data.
The linking layer is also changing. Google said on November 13 that Display & Video 360 will let creators link videos to advertisers by code, with no launch date. In September, YouTube said it would label brand deals that creators fail to disclose, which affects the same paid promotion flag that partner tools rely on to connect videos to campaigns.
TikTok is building a parallel structure. On November 10, TikTok added a Premier Creative Partner tier to its Marketing Partners Program and tied qualification to TikTok One, through first-party access or API integrations, with SaaS providers as one of four creator marketing segments. Earlier in the year, TikTok One added Creator AI Search and revamped its partner tools. For influencer software vendors, platform API access has become a credential to display. For brands, the number of tools that can read YouTube's creator data grows, while the data underneath stays in one place.
What the post does not say
The post gives no criteria for admission, no statement on whether access remains invite-only, and no description of which API functions each company uses. It does not explain why StreamElements and Cafe24 appear as new, or what "Isotype" refers to next to SAMY. It does not give a total partner count.
