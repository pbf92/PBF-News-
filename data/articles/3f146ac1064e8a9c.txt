TITLE: The huge risk with $2 trillion AI opportunity
OUTLET: The Nightly

Anthropic tipped to pop at open, future of AI winners unclear
Anthropic’s $2 trillion IPO offers investors a chance to cash in on the AI revolution, but its valuation, mounting costs and intensifying competition raise the risk of a costly bet.
Investors are being offered a rare opportunity to own a slice of one of the world’s most influential AI companies, but whether Anthropic’s blockbuster stock market debut represents a chance to profit from the next tech revolution, or a dangerous bet on an AI bubble prone to pop is uncertain, analysts say.
The maker of AI assistant Claude is seeking to raise up to $US90.4 billion ($132 billion) at a valuation that could reach $US1.4 trillion ($2 trillion), putting it among the world’s most valuable tech businesses before it has established a record of profitability.
Damien Klassen the Chief Investment Officer of Nucleus Wealth Management said the six-year-old company has a brilliant, if short, history, but that doesn’t necessarily mean it’s a wise investment.

“If the AI boom continues and they maintain their position at the front then Anthropic will be worth a lot more in five years than it is today,” he said.
“Its past financials are actually pretty good. But it’s a double-edged sword. as the issue is they’ve contracted for all this forward compute and if they don’t get the demand that becomes a problem.”
Anthropic shares are being offered to local investors through eight popular brokers including CommSec, Stake, Bell Potter and Sharesies.
The company has emerged as a leader players in selling AI assistants to corporates.
It counted more than 11,900 customers paying subscription fees of $US100,000 per year or more over the trailing twelve months to September 30.
It also revealed 1,900 customers paying $US1 million per year, or more, and about 250 paying at least $10 million annually.
However, it faces competition from the likes of OpenAI, Google, Meta, and a growing stable of cheaper Chinese AI models.
Revenues soared nearly 12-fold to nearly $US17 billon over the three months to September 30.
While its operating loss for the quarter reached $US567 million, although most analysts expect it to turn profitable on the assumption its blockbuster growth rates continue.
“If you think AI’s a bubble and will unravel then you’d avoid it,” says Klassen. “If you think AI has legs and Anthropic is the early leader then you’d like the look of it. And the trajectory Anthropic is on is way better than anyone else, but it’s early days so they could be overtaken by competition.”
First day pop?
For investors hoping to make a quick profit, the immediate question is whether Anthropic shares will surge when they begin trading on November 19.
In June, shares in Elon Musk’s space and AI infrastructure business SpaceX rose about 10 per cent on their market debut, delivering an early gain for IPO investors.
Stock broker Pepperstone says its pre-IPO market in Anthropic shares is trading at $US103.88 – around 25 per cent above the high side of a theoretical IPO price of $78.
This real-time gauge of investor expectations suggests the shares may surge on listing, although this is far from certain and just one measure of demand for the stock.
“We’re not buying Anthropic yet,” says Klassen “But would I prefer it to SpaceX? Absolutely.”
According to Rob Talevski, the chief executive of Webull Securities Australia, both SpaceX and Anthropic only offered limited amounts of new shares to investors, compared to the total number of shares on issue.
“This [Anthropic] is another enormous company opening a very small door for new investors,” said Tavlevski. “At the top of the proposed price range, the shares on offer are worth around $US64 billion – less than 5 per cent of a company valued at up to $US1.4 trillion.”
Tavlevski said the chances of Anthropic popping on its share market debut on November 19 are offset by poor sentiment around AI that’s linked to Nvidia’s decision to force the resignation of its former chief executive Jensen Huang.
“AI sentiment has cooled and as we know Firmus pulled its ASX float last month after demand fell away,” he said.
“Anthropic is still loss-making and that may matter more once its first earnings report as a listed company lands. But on day one, once again, it’ll come down to how little stock is available and the fear and greed in the bellies of traders.”
As the Tuesday deadline for applications arrives, brokers are typically talking up the float. CommSec has reportedly flagged strong demand in the second mega AI listing of 2026.
