Man City charges case identifies huge problem that could cost billions
Manchester City were found guilty of breaching the Premier League's financial rules earlier this year, with officials currently working through a lengthy appeals process
The Premier League has asked its clubs to provide legal protection to Richard Masters and other board directors in the event Manchester City's backers seek to sue them individually.
The club were found guilty of breaches of the English top flight's financial rules earlier this year by "artificially inflating" their finances through "sham" sponsorship deals over a nine-year period between 2009 and 2018. Man City are currently appealing the verdict.
A statement in September read: "The independent Commission found that Manchester City arranged ‘sham’ commercial deals with a number of its sponsors during the period, which were part of a disguised funding scheme, whereby those companies were only required to pay a portion of the relevant sponsorship fees."
Chief executive of the League, Masters, meanwhile, said: "The core decision establishes the facts of what happened at Manchester City during this period. It details how the club systematically broke Premier League Rules for nearly a decade.
“It also vindicates the Premier League’s decision to pursue this case against Manchester City. While the process to date has been both long and difficult, the League has remained determined that the facts be established independently.

“It is a key responsibility of the Premier League to ensure that the Rules, approved by the clubs themselves, are upheld to protect the integrity of the competition. It is paramount that the League remains competitive and fair for all clubs and for the fans. We take that role extremely seriously.
“This disciplinary case, and this decision, are the most significant in Premier League history. There are elements of the case that remain to be decided, including, importantly, what sanction must follow for these breaches. Now we have the Commission’s decision, we are committed to moving swiftly through the remainder of the process, to provide certainty for the League, our clubs and fans.”

City have always protested their innocence throughout the saga and have launched a formal appeal against the findings of the case, which were presented in late September by an independent panel. Within 24 hours of the verdict being delivered, Etihad Airways released a statement that it was considering legal action against the Premier League.
It complained that the judgement had suggested that the company had been "involved in improper commercial arrangements," which it vehemently rejects. The airline also noted that the English top flight had not contacted them as part of its hearing against City, while citing "damaging implications."
A statement issued on Monday, meanwhile, explained how Etisalat - another City sponsor - is also considering legal avenues against the Premier League. It read: "Emirates Telecommunications Group Company PJSC [Etisalat] views the published findings of the English Premier League, as they relate to Etisalat, as entirely false and defamatory."
"Etisalat is actively considering all options relating to its rights and associated legal remedies in this matter," it added, in a worrying turn of events. It is estimated that a lawsuit could cost billions of pounds if a corporate defamation case is brought to the League.
According to The Times, Masters, EPL chairwoman Alison Brittain and non-executive directors Mai Fyfield, Dharmash Mistry and Matthew Ryder KC do not have indemnity against legal action by third parties, such as club sponsors.
The League will seek to change that when it meets with its 20 clubs in London on Thursday, November 19, aiming to reach an agreement for clubs to collectively pay legal expenses and damages arising from sponsors' actions.
Sponsors could be aggrieved by the Premier League's official statement using the word "sham" to describe deals made with City. As a result, the 20 clubs of the League have been sent a two-page proposal to consider before voting on matters.
At least 14 clubs must accept it for the proposal to pass. The document makes no mention of City, its sponsors or the ongoing charges case for the proposed protection.
It is understood that Etihad and Etisalat are awaiting the League's final judgement before they decide on their next steps. The club's appeals hearing is set to start in December, and a final decision is not expected to land until early 2026.
