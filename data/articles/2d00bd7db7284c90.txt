TITLE: Is Nvidia (NASDAQ:NVDA) Still Attractive After a 30% Rebound on Huang's Return, With a USD 130 Billion Quarter Guided?
OUTLET: kalkine.com

Key Highlights
- Nvidia (NASDAQ:NVDA) shares were indicated about 30% higher near USD 165 before the November 23 open after Jensen Huang was reinstated.
- At that price the stock trades near 20 times trailing adjusted Earnings of USD 8.39 per share, up from about 15 times at Friday's close.
- The company has guided fiscal fourth-quarter Revenue of USD 130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, with gross Margin of 71% to 72%.
- A USD 0.25 quarterly Dividend has a Record Date of December 2 and is payable on December 23.
Valuation After the Rebound
For investors in Nvidia (NASDAQ:NVDA), Monday's rebound changes the valuation picture as much as the Leadership picture. At Friday's close of USD 125.53, the shares traded at about 15 times trailing adjusted earnings of USD 8.39 per share, the lowest multiple in roughly a decade and far below the median of about 45 times since 2014. The premarket price near USD 165 lifts that multiple to roughly 20 times. Even after the jump, the stock sits about 32% below its October 6 record of USD 243.37. On that basis the shares remain cheaper than they were for most of the past two years, despite earnings that are still growing quickly. Fiscal third-quarter revenue was USD 115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier, and adjusted Earnings Per Share were USD 2.68. Management has said it expects revenue growth of about 75% in fiscal 2028 and estimated the cloud industry Backlog at USD 2.5 trillion. The rebound suggests investors are again pricing in that growth path now that the founder who set it is back in charge. Whether the multiple expands further will depend on how the remaining legal and regulatory questions resolve.
The Next Earnings Test
The next major operating checkpoint is the fiscal fourth-quarter report, which Nvidia typically issues in late February. Guidance calls for revenue of USD 130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, in a quarter that includes an extra week and excludes any China Data Center compute revenue. Gross margin is guided at 71% to 72%, down from 74.9% in the third quarter, as memory costs rise and new platforms ramp. Management described that range as the bottom for margins, with fiscal 2028 expected at 72% to 73%. Investors will look for confirmation that the Vera Rubin ramp, which produced nearly USD 20 billion in its first quarter of shipments, continued at pace through the leadership disruption. They will also look for any comment on whether customers delayed orders during the eleven days without Huang. A clean quarter would support the view that the crisis was a governance event rather than a Business one.
Capital Return and Key Risks
Shareholder returns remain substantial. Nvidia returned a record USD 28.1 billion in the third quarter and has USD 227.3 billion left on its repurchase authorisation, which it plans to complete by the end of fiscal 2028. The USD 0.25 quarterly dividend has a record date of December 2 and a payment date of December 23. The risks are now more legal than operational. Huang faces a closed-door House interview on December 1, a reported civil inquiry by securities regulators is unresolved, and Shareholder suits are pending in federal court in California. An independent board committee is also still investigating whether Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. Long-term holders may view the lower multiple as compensation for those risks, while short-term traders face elevated Volatility around each new headline. Position sizing and a clear view of the legal calendar are likely to matter more than usual over the next few months.
Where Things Stand
After Huang's return, Nvidia (NASDAQ:NVDA) trades near 20 times trailing earnings with a USD 130.0 billion quarter guided and large Buybacks under way. The main open risks are the congressional, regulatory and legal processes still ahead.
