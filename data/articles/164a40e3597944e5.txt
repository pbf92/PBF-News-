TITLE: ChatGPT links to websites in nearly half of chats, up from 10%, Peec AI finds
OUTLET: PPC Land

ChatGPT is putting clickable links to outside websites into more than four times as many conversations as it did at the start of October, according to data published today on LinkedIn by David Konitzny, a GEO researcher at Berlin-based Peec AI. The share of conversations in Peec AI's data with at least one such link stood at around 10% until early October, jumped to 41% on October 9 and peaked at 49.5% on October 14. A chart released with the post puts the figure at 47.8% on November 14 and marks October 7 as the day GPT-6 became ChatGPT's default model. The links have also multiplied within conversations, and most linked URLs are not among the sources ChatGPT cites in the same chat.
In Short
ChatGPT has started placing clickable website links directly in far more of its answers: in Peec AI's measurements, the share of conversations with at least one such link went from about 10% before October to 41% on October 9 and 47.8% by November 14. Brands and publishers that track how they appear in AI assistants are affected, because conversations with links now carry about four of them on average instead of two, and 63% of the linked URLs do not appear among the cited sources of the same chat. Being linked and being cited have become two separate outcomes, although the post itself says more links do not automatically mean more clicks or referral traffic.
What the post says
Konitzny opened with a single sentence: "ChatGPT is linking to more websites than ever." The second paragraph framed the period. "The way ChatGPT includes external links has changed significantly over the past few weeks. And the data shows some interesting developments," Konitzny wrote.
The definition matters for everything that follows. "By external links, I mean clickable links to external websites directly within ChatGPT's answers, not citations or links that open a sidebar," the post reads. The measurement therefore counts only links embedded in the body of an answer - the kind a reader can click without opening the list of sources.
The first finding concerns frequency: "Until early October, only around 10% of conversations contained at least one external link. On October 9, that number suddenly jumped to 41%, reaching a peak of 49.5% on October 14."
The second concerns density. "Before October, conversations with external links most commonly contained just one link, accounting for around 43% of those conversations," according to the post. That share has dropped to 18%. Conversations with five or more external links, the post states, "account for almost 39% of all conversations containing links, making this the largest group when combined." The average number of links rose "from roughly 2 to 4."
"So we're not just seeing more conversations with external links. We're also seeing a shift from predominantly single links towards multiple links within the same conversation," Konitzny concluded from those two measures.
The third concerns overlap with citations. "63% of external URLs don't overlap with cited source URLs in the same conversation," and "47% of linked domains don't overlap with cited source domains," according to the post.
The post closed with a caveat of its own. "Of course, more external links don't automatically mean more clicks or referral traffic. But the data highlights a significant change in how ChatGPT connects its answers to external websites."
The figures
Every figure below comes from the post or from the chart published with it, except where marked as a PPC Land calculation or reading.
How Peec AI counts a link
The post and its chart describe the measurement in enough detail to reconstruct its structure, though not its sample. According to Peec AI, the method has five parts.
- The unit is the conversation, which the chart calls a "chat." Each daily figure is a share of conversations, not of individual answers or prompts.
- An external link is a clickable link to an outside website placed directly within ChatGPT's answer. Citations and links that open a sidebar are excluded.
- The first chart, titled "Share of chats with at least one external link," plots that share daily from August 27 to November 14, 2026.
- The second chart, titled "Chats with links, by number of external links," covers only conversations that contain at least one link and splits them into seven bands: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and "7 or more" links per chat.
- A separate comparison checks each linked URL and each linked domain against the cited sources of the same chat. The results are expressed as the share of linked URLs, and of linked domains, that "do not appear in the sources of the same chat."
The GPT-6 marker
The written post never names a model. The chart does. Its headline reads "Since GPT-6, ChatGPT links out more often, and with more links," its subtitle describes "External links in ChatGPT answers, daily, 27 August to 14 November 2026," and a dashed vertical line on both panels is labelled "7 Oct, GPT-6 default."
There is a gap between the marker and the jump. On the chart, the share of conversations with a link sits between roughly 10% and 12% on October 7 and 8 and rises steeply only afterwards, which matches the October 9 date in the text. A staggered rollout would produce exactly that pattern; so would a separate change shipped two days later. The post offers no OpenAI statement either way.
GPT-6 brought at least one other change in how ChatGPT handled outbound links. PPC Land reported on November 1 that, according to Search Engine Roundtable on October 14, the utm_source=chatgpt.com parameter had disappeared from ChatGPT links with the release of GPT-6 about a week earlier, before returning on October 15.
The line also did not hold its peak. By PPC Land's reading of the chart, the share fell back into the high 30s around October 20 and to roughly a third of conversations at the turn of the month, before climbing above 45% in the first half of November and ending at the labelled 47.8%. The second panel suggests the shift toward multiple links came in two steps: the single-link band narrowed sharply after October 9, widened slightly in late October, and narrowed again in the first days of November, while the "7 or more" band thickened at both points. The post does not explain either movement.
Linked is not the same as cited
The overlap figures may prove the more consequential part of the post. ChatGPT's cited sources are tied to the pages its web search retrieves to support an answer, the grounding step. The in-answer links, by Peec AI's count, often point somewhere else. "This means that websites can appear as direct links in ChatGPT without being included in its cited sources," Konitzny wrote.
The two figures differ in an instructive way. If 63% of linked URLs fall outside the cited URLs but only 47% of linked domains fall outside the cited domains, some links must go to a different page on a site that is cited elsewhere in the same chat - an article cited as evidence, say, and the same company's homepage linked in the text. The post does not break that down, and the denominators (URLs and domains) are not the same, so the gap is an indication rather than a measurement.
The comments under the post supplied a hypothesis. Olaf Kopp, whose LinkedIn headline lists SEO and agentic commerce optimization, wrote: "I guess links from websites are not cited as a source are mostly brand links for example made for navigating to the entities website."
Konitzny agreed. "That's my assumption as well! This would also explain why most external links point either to a homepage or a specific product page. So, as you said, they seem to serve a primarily navigational purpose. Which makes sense, right?" came the reply.
Justin Berg, founder of Rock The Rankings, pressed on the same point. "The 63% non-overlap is the part I'd want to dig into. Are those direct links more likely to point to product, pricing, or comparison pages, while citations point to supporting articles? If so, a brand could be part of the buyer's next step without appearing in the cited sources at all. Have you looked at that breakdown?" Konitzny's answer was brief: "those direct links usually point to the homepage or a product page." No share was given for either page type.
Business Hub timing
Kevin Indig, a growth advisor, connected the data to OpenAI's new tools for businesses. "Interesting timing with business hub focusing on site references for mentions," Indig commented. "That's exactly what I was thinking too. That's why I took another look at the data," Konitzny replied.
PPC Land reported on November 6 that ChatGPT was building mention and click charts for verified businesses, and on November 10 OpenAI opened the program as a beta. In that launch, covered by PPC Land on November 11, Business Hub gives claimed businesses counts of mentions and website clicks, along with "discovery topics," which OpenAI defined as "common themes across conversations where their website is cited."
Mathias Decourt, co-founder of Unveil SEO, offered a commercial reading in a reply to Konitzny: "Good point, they want to satisfy both their users and cited websites (by showing ChatGPT impact in their Business Hub)." That remains a commenter's interpretation. The post does not link its link counts to Business Hub's click metric, and nothing in it shows whether a click on an in-answer link and a click on a citation are counted the same way in OpenAI's dashboard.
A reversal of the 5.3 pattern
The direction of travel is notable because ChatGPT had moved the other way earlier in the year. PPC Land reported in April that ChatGPT 5.3, launched on March 3, 2026, cut outbound links, citing Resoneo data shared by Lily Ray that showed the average number of unique domains per response falling from about 19.8 to about 15.9. That measure counted domains across a response, not clickable links in the text, so the two series cannot be compared directly. Still, the contrast is plain: one model release narrowed the set of sites shown; another, by Peec AI's count, multiplied the links.
Links in ChatGPT have long been a moving target. In March 2024, OpenAI made links more prominent in ChatGPT Browse for paying users. In June 2025, ChatGPT added UTM parameters to more links, making its traffic easier to separate in analytics tools. Two months later, Profound reported that ChatGPT referral traffic fell 52% from July 21, 2025 as citations shifted toward Reddit and Wikipedia.
Peec AI has studied the retrieval side before. Its analysis of 5 million fan-out queries from ChatGPT, Perplexity and Grok, published on May 5, 2026, found that ChatGPT averaged 2.1 fan-out searches per query. Separate research covered by PPC Land in August, based on 60 conversations, found that only 110 of 3,554 pages ChatGPT retrieved were cited, or 3.1%, and that brands named in ChatGPT's own search query reached the answer far more often than those retrieved but never named.
Google's parallel moves
Decourt's first comment drew a comparison with Google. "It looks like they got inspired by Google Ai Overview. They might have figured that adding links to their answers are beneficial to their users. Links are great," Decourt wrote.
Google has made a series of moves in that direction. On May 6, 2026, it added five outbound link features to AI Mode and AI Overviews, among them inline links and hover previews on desktop. On October 15, eight days after the GPT-6 marker on Peec AI's chart, Google pushed links higher in its AI answers and said use of its Preferred Sources feature had grown more than 7x since the start of the year.
Why it matters for marketers
For practitioners of GEO, the post adds a category to the scorecard. Mentions and citations have been the standard units of AI visibility; a direct link in the answer text is a third, and the data suggests it does not travel with the other two as reliably as many might assume.
