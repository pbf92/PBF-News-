TITLE: Bishop of Leicester backs stronger government action on youth employment crisis
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A high-profile Church of England bishop has welcomed a government review into youth inactivity, saying its findings should prompt a “serious” response from ministers.
Rt Rev Martyn Snow said the report highlighted the need for urgent action to help more young people into work, education or training.
The review, led by former Labour minister Alan Milburn, recommends an overhaul of the benefits system for under-25s.
It says the UK should aim to reduce the proportion of young people not in education, employment or training – known as NEET – from 13 per cent to around five per cent by 2036.
Milburn says young people with anxiety or neurodivergence should be both “expected and enabled” to work.
He also recommends financial incentives for businesses that take on 16 to 24-year-olds.
Reacting to the report, Bishop Martyn said: “We encourage the Government to respond with the urgency this challenge demands and to work with schools, employers, charities, churches and local communities to ensure that no young person is left behind.”
Bishop Martyn says the Church of England is committed to playing a part in a national response.
“We welcome the review's focus on expanding opportunity, while recognising the importance of ensuring that those facing the greatest challenges receive the support they need to flourish,” he said.
According to the Office for National Statistics, the number of NEETs between 16 and 24 is 13 per cent.
Secretary of State for Work and Pensions Pat McFadden welcomed the report in Parliament today, saying the government will publish a White Paper next year addressing the issue, to be followed by legislation.
