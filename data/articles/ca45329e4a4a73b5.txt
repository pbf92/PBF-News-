TITLE: HubSpot won't charge extra when Claude or ChatGPT works its CRM
OUTLET: PPC Land

HubSpot will not charge its customers more for routine work done inside the software from Claude or ChatGPT, and AI agents that plug into its existing APIs will not draw down HubSpot Credits, according to a post the company published on its newsroom on November 17, 2026. Credits enter at a third level: a governed environment for agents that HubSpot says it is still building, where work "at scale" will be paid for with its usage currency.
The post, titled "HubSpot is headless. Here's how we'll price it," is written by Duncan Lennox. It sets out the commercial terms behind a strategy that HubSpot has been building for more than a year - letting customers operate its CRM from within third-party AI models rather than through HubSpot's own screens. It arrived five days after co-founder Brian Halligan returned as chief executive to lead what the company calls a refounding, and thirteen days after HubSpot told investors its revenue growth would slow to 12% in the fourth quarter.
In Short
HubSpot said on November 17 that everyday use of its software from Claude or ChatGPT is included in the seats customers already pay for, and that outside AI agents using its regular APIs count against each plan's API allowance rather than its credits. Credits apply when heavy users go past what their seat includes and when agents run inside a governed environment that HubSpot is building, which comes with permissions, logs and extra tools. The cost of AI work in HubSpot will now depend on which of the three routes an agent takes, and the post gives no rates, limits or start dates for the new environment.
Three routes in, three ways to pay
The post divides AI access to HubSpot into three routes: connectors and an MCP server that let people work in HubSpot from AI assistants, the existing APIs for agents that customers build or buy, and a governed environment for agents that HubSpot has yet to release. Each carries a different billing rule. The post opens with a three-line summary. "Every HubSpot seat comes with enough headless access to complete your everyday work from Claude or ChatGPT, at no additional cost," the first line reads. The second: "Customers can connect third-party agents to HubSpot's existing APIs. That usage counts toward the API capacity already included with their plan, and does not consume credits." The third: "For teams that want to give their third-party agents strong governance, full traceability, and purpose-built tools to deliver better outcomes, that usage runs on HubSpot Credits."
The structure is easy to state. Its details are not. The post does not say how much connector use counts as "everyday," what the "capacity included with their seat" amounts to, what the governed environment is called, when it will be available or how many credits its actions will cost.
How the three routes work, according to HubSpot
The post describes each route in turn. Read together, the arrangement has six parts.
- Connector access. A HubSpot user works from an AI tool through "HubSpot's connectors for Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot, plus our MCP server and Agent CLI." HubSpot's examples are a sales rep summarizing open deals, a customer success manager preparing for a renewal call and a marketer launching a campaign "without opening another tab."
- Seat entitlement. That connector use "comes included with a HubSpot seat." The post does not define a ceiling for it.
- Overflow to credits. "For power users who lean on AI tools more than most, HubSpot Credits will give them the flexibility to expand the capacity included with their seat without buying an add-on or additional seat type." The sentence is in the future tense, and the post gives no date.
- Customer-built or bought agents on existing APIs. An agent a customer has built or bought connects "with the same APIs that power thousands of integrations today." Its calls count against the plan's API capacity and do not consume credits. "For everyone already using those APIs to connect their existing tech stack to HubSpot, nothing changes," Lennox writes.
- Extra API capacity. "HubSpot continues to include generous API capacity in our plans. For teams that push the limits of what is already included, additional capacity can be added at any time."
- The governed environment. "HubSpot is building a governed environment designed specifically for agents," the post states. Agents there get "purpose-built agentic APIs that provide deeper business context and the ability to complete work across HubSpot at scale." Customers get "control over what each agent can access and do, a record of every action, and clear visibility into which agent did what." Work at scale in that environment, such as enriching records and personalizing outreach, is paid for with credits.
That sixth part is where the money is. Steps one to five largely describe what already exists. The environment in step six is the new product, and the post's own framing positions it as the route for agents that need to be trusted "especially when no one is watching."
What the included API capacity amounts to
The post calls HubSpot's API allowance "generous" without giving numbers. HubSpot's Product & Services Catalog does give them. According to the catalog's Technical Limits page, which carries no date, the included daily call limits and the effect of buying capacity packs stood as follows on November 18, 2026.
Each pack adds 1 million calls a day, and the catalog caps purchases at two packs. Burst limits are set per 10 seconds: 100 requests for Free and Starter, and 190 for Professional and Enterprise. The catalog is not consistent on what the packs do to that figure. Its table shows 350 and 600 requests per 10 seconds for Free and Starter with one and two packs, and 440 and 690 for Professional and Enterprise, while a sentence on the same page says the packs raise the rate limit to 250 requests per 10 seconds.
That matters for the second route. An agent that works through a customer's records one call at a time shares those limits with every other integration on the account, from form tools to data warehouses. "Additional capacity can be added at any time," in the post's words, means in the catalog's terms up to two packs at $500 a month each. Beyond that, the catalog lists no further option.
What HubSpot Credits cost
Credits are HubSpot's single usage currency, and the post makes them the meter for both overflow connector use and the governed environment. Current pricing is in the same catalog and on HubSpot's credits product page.
According to the catalog, included credits are not added together across products. An account receives the highest allotment that its highest subscription qualifies for, unused credits do not roll over into the next month, and usage pauses once the included credits are gone unless more are bought. The credits page adds that a default account-level limit can be set at up to 50% beyond an account's monthly credits. Customers on HubSpot's flexible seats-and-credits pricing are subject to different credit rates under a separate supplement, the catalog states.
The catalog also prices HubSpot's own agents in credits. A Customer Agent resolving one conversation on a text channel costs 50 credits. The Prospecting Agent recommending outreach for one lead costs 100. One AI action in a workflow costs 10, a Data Agent prompt on one record costs 10, and the Content Agent, in beta, consumes 1,000 credits per piece of content. Custom agents, also in beta, are priced at one credit per action unit. At the pay-as-you-go rate, a resolved conversation therefore costs $0.50 and a prospecting recommendation $1.00, by PPC Land's calculation.
None of these entries mentions third-party agents, MCP or the governed environment. The credit rates for outside agents that enrich records or personalize outreach in the new environment are not published.
HubSpot has been adding controls to this meter through the autumn. On October 15, 2026, it introduced AI spend controls including feature-level credit caps and the ability to move spend from seats to credits mid-contract, according to CMSWire.
Where headless came from
"Headless" in HubSpot's usage means the CRM working without its own interface in front of it: data and actions reachable by whatever model or agent a customer chooses. The company has been building towards that for more than a year. It launched a deep research connector for ChatGPT in June 2025, available to paid ChatGPT users, and a connector for Anthropic's Claude in July 2025, open to customers on all HubSpot tiers with a paid Claude subscription. These connectors are the first route in the November 17 post.
Lennox set out the architecture on May 4, 2026. In that post, PPC Land reported, HubSpot laid out a plan to let any agent run its CRM through open APIs, an open MCP server, a data layer covering contacts, companies, deals, conversations, tickets and activity, and an intelligence layer that was not yet open to outside systems. Lennox was chief product and technology officer at the time. Full API parity was a stated goal without a timeline, and the only pricing commitment was that bringing data into HubSpot would remain free.
The November 17 post fills in what May left out. It confirms that outside agents on the existing APIs will not be charged in credits, and it identifies the governed environment, with its "purpose-built agentic APIs," as the place where HubSpot intends to charge. Whether that environment is the intelligence layer described in May, the post does not say.
HubSpot's adoption numbers come from its third-quarter results. When the company forecast fourth-quarter revenue below the third quarter's on November 4, then chief executive Yamini Rangan said customers created more than 22 million CRM objects through connectors in the quarter, up 157% on the second quarter, and described HubSpot as the most popular CRM connector on ChatGPT and Claude. The November 17 post repeats the claim as "the #1 CRM connector in both Claude and ChatGPT." Neither the call nor the post explains how the ranking is measured.
The quarterly report filed alongside those results was more cautious about the same strategy. Its risk factors warned that customers authorizing third-party AI platforms and agents to reach HubSpot through APIs and MCP may reduce direct engagement with HubSpot's platform, limit cross-selling and make third-party AI access harder to monetize. The November 17 post is, in effect, HubSpot's answer to that risk: leave the assistant route uncharged and build a paid route next to it.
A credits model already in pilot
The post lands in the middle of a broader pricing change. HubSpot is running a seats-and-credits pilot for new customers that began in the Nordics in April 2026 and in Benelux in June, and was extended to all new customers in Europe, the Middle East and Africa in the fourth quarter. It replaces separate Hub subscriptions with a single "go-to-market seat" and moves every usage-based feature into credits. On the November 4 call, Rangan listed what that covers: email sent, tickets resolved, data enriched and APIs.
That creates a question the post does not answer. Under the pilot, as Rangan described it, API usage is in credits. Under the November 17 post, API calls from third-party agents "will not consume credits." The post does not say whether the statement applies to pilot customers, or whether the API capacity rule replaces what was described on the call. HubSpot is scheduled to report what it learned in EMEA in February 2027.
Credits have also been used as a sales tool. In September, when OpenAI let advertisers run ChatGPT ads from HubSpot, HubSpot offered an AI Growth Bundle with HubSpot Starter and HubSpot Credits at up to 65% off for a year.
