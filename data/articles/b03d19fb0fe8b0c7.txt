TITLE: What Manchester United players said to Senne Lammens in dressing room after mistakes vs Sporting
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

What Manchester United players said to Senne Lammens in dressing room after mistakes vs Sporting
Man Utd drew 2-2 with Sporting CP in Lisbon but victory was snatched from their grasp after two Senne Lammens mistakes and some poor refereeing.
Joshua Zirkzee backed Manchester United goalkeeper Senne Lammens to bounce back from his Lisbon nightmare after the Belgian made two mistakes that cost his side a Champions League victory.
Lammens' evening had started with a penalty save, but 75 seconds later he made a rash decision to race off his line and conceded a second penalty, which Luis Suarez scored.
He then dropped a cross at the feet of Suarez with 10 minutes to play, giving the Colombian a simple task of hooking home an equaliser to deny Mauricio Pochettino a first win in charge of United.
The gaffes continued Lammens' uncertain second season in Manchester, but his mentality has always been a strength and Zirkzee revealed his teammates had given him space in the Jose Alvalade dressing rooms after his howlers.
"In this moment, we just give him space," said Zirkzee. "Not sure if he wants to hear anything at all. But Senne’s a great kid. He's got a good head on him.
"And we all know that next game he is going to be at the top of his game. And we didn't draw because of him. I'm also someone who likes to look at myself and I didn't score."

Asked how important it was to show support to Lammens, Zirkzee added: "It's everything. This team is a family, so there's no one who will suffer by themselves. Mistakes happen. Let's go on to the next one. Senne’s a great goalkeeper."
United turned in a much better performance in Lisbon and led 2-1 after a quickfire Bruno Fernandes double at the start of the second half.
But for the second game in a row they had reason to be furious with the officiating. After Leny Yoro was wrongly sent off eight minutes into Pochettino's first game in charge at Anfield, this time Polish referee Szymon Marciniak controversially disallowed two United goals.
The decision to blow for a free-kick before Amad scored just before half-time was a particularly poor decision, but a late winner was also chalked off after VAR adjudged Patrick Dorgu had fouled the goalkeeper.
It has left United with a burning sense of injustice and Zirkzee admitted it feels like the world is against his team at the moment.
"Yeah, we're in a place where it can feel like that pretty quick," he said. "But as I said, we've got a locker room that's built on supporting each other and we will get out of it eventually. And if it's us against the world, so be it."
Zirkzee was reluctant to blame the referee entirely and did feel United's performance gave them a platform to build on ahead of a return to Premier League action against Brentford.
"For the team that we are, we shouldn't really be looking at what the referee decides, but you cannot deny that it was some key decisions that weren't in our favour, to put it like that," he said.
"But, as I said, we shouldn't really be looking at the referee. All we have to do is look at ourselves. In the end, I think we lost because of small details, little mistakes.
"But we definitely have something to build on, mainly the way we started the second half. I think that's something we can build on and move on with."
