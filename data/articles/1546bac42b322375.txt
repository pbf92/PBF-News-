TITLE: Pathstone’s venture bet put clients in Anthropic at a $14B valuation
OUTLET: InvestmentNews

Clients at the RIA and multi-family office Pathstone gained exposure to Anthropic when it was valued at $14 billion, a representative for Pathstone told InvestmentNews. On Thursday, the Claude maker began trading on Nasdaq after an IPO that valued it at $1.5 trillion – more than 100 times the valuation at which Pathstone clients gained exposure.
Anthropic priced its shares at $83 on Wednesday in an $81 billion offering, the largest IPO on record by capital raised.
“If you don’t own these companies while they’re in the private markets, you’re missing a lot of the run up,” Pathstone CEO Matthew Fleissig told InvestmentNews. "I'd say in general, our clients are probably around a third in alternatives, so we've been able to have quite a bit of exposure on behalf of our clients."
The multi-family office reports about $185 billion in aggregate assets, including affiliate firms. That includes about $110 billion in regulatory assets under management at Pathstone Family Office LLC, according to its March 2026 Form ADV. It serves about 900 families, with an average client size of over $100 million.
Fleissig said clients gained Anthropic exposure through the firm’s venture funds and co-investment opportunities, the same route it has used for SpaceX. Pathstone's Form ADV lists 175 private funds. Pooled investment vehicles such as private funds made up 30% of the separately managed account assets it reported at year-end 2025.
“Of your private equity portfolio, we’re going as high as 30 to 35 percent in venture,” Fleissig said. "If you don't own, go down the list — Stripe, Databricks, Snowflake, SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI — before they go public, you're just missing out on a lot of value creation. You're also missing out on holdings that would be significant names inside of the S&P 500."
For Pathstone clients, Anthropic's IPO itself matters less. Lockups range from a small portion that can be sold soon to more than a year – one of the factors advisors should look out for in Anthropic’s mega IPO. Pathstone has been advising clients on whether to take fund distributions as shares in-kind or in cash.
“I think tomorrow is more about a mark and an exit on your performance report than it is people getting physical cash,” Fleissig said Wednesday, the day before Anthropic's IPO trading began.
The listing also creates a wave of employee wealth. Anthropic’s 5,300 employees hold an estimated $232 billion combined, according to Fortune, and at least 400 early hires will be worth $100 million or more, according to CNBC.
Fleissig called Thursday's IPO "one of the nation’s greatest liquidity events that we have ever seen.” In July 2026, Choreo CEO Jason Van de Loo told InvestmentNews that the Chicago-based RIA was expanding its pre-IPO strategy to target employees of OpenAI and Anthropic as wealth management clients ahead of their employers’ public offerings.
TechCrunch reported that Anthropic’s IPO could deliver $156 billion to charity. Fleissig described a "culture" of giving within the company and he expects philanthropic efforts from Anthropic's new employee millionaires will favor donor-advised funds over foundations.
"You always have the battle between the foundations and donor-advised funds. The donor advised funds, it's just really efficient. It's a lot less paperwork. There's much less costs, so I think you're going to continue to see a lot of work with donor advised funds going in here," said Fleissig.
For advisors, the bigger disruption from Anthropic may be its product rather than its stock. OpenAI previewed a personal finance experience for US ChatGPT Pro subscribers in May, and Anthropic followed earlier this month with Personal Finance in Claude, a read-only beta for linked bank and card accounts.
Anthropic's entry into personal finance follows its Claude for Financial Advisors rollout with Schwab and several other key wealth management vendors.
“I’ll say that Claude and ChatGPT are the largest investment advisors in the world right now," said Fleissig. “If you want to run a cash flow simulation, or you want to run your retirement planning, or you want to run a sufficiency analysis, you can do that in Claude, and you don’t need Pathstone to do that.”
Consumers’ growing reliance on AI for money questions comes as a study published in September 2026 by AI firm Saturn found that major AI models answered financial questions incorrectly or incompletely 57% of the time.
Beyond investments, Pathstone offers reporting, accounting, bill pay, personal CFO services, private aviation management, payroll, and property and casualty insurance — which are the sort of offerings Fleissig says that advisors will have to inreasingly lean into to differentiate from a market that could rely on AI chatbots for financial advice.
"I think it's going to change what you have to offer as a wealth management firm, or you can use Claude and ChatGPT," said Fleissig. "It's a very fascinating part of how the evolution of wealth management firms will go forward, as everyone now has access to information, and what you're really looking for is judgment, experience, and a trusted partner."
