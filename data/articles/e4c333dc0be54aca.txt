OpenAI calls for industry-wide AI brake tied to perplexity
OpenAI OPENAI is calling for frontier AI developers to adopt common limits on model development tied partly to perplexity, arguing that improvements in the metric can foreshadow the emergence of powerful and unpredictable new capabilities.
In a post Sunday, OpenAI Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki proposed an industry standard that would establish measurable thresholds developers agree not to cross until corresponding advances are made in understanding and monitoring AI behavior AIQ ARTY THNQ BOTZ CHAT.
Perplexity measures how well a model predicts the next token, with lower readings representing better performance. OpenAI generally tracks its logarithm, also known as cross-entropy loss, and Pachocki argued that the measure has remained unusually useful for tracking the underlying intelligence created during pretraining even as downstream AI benchmarks rapidly become obsolete.
OpenAI said GPT-3.5 registered log-perplexity of about 0.99 on its main internal evaluation dataset, while GPT-4 reached about 0.91. As losses moved below 0.8, the company began seeing transformative capabilities in areas including cybersecurity and mathematics. A model at roughly 0.76 began producing what Pachocki described as groundbreaking mathematical results.
Looking ahead, Pachocki said OpenAI expects further transformative capabilities as models move into roughly the 0.68-0.70 range, although the company does not know what those capabilities will be.
That uncertainty underpins the proposed development limits. Pachocki said frontier labs should agree on shared evaluation and red-teaming standards and establish a first threshold they would not cross until monitoring technology reached a defined milestone, such as a successor to current chain-of-thought monitoring systems.
The proposal comes as OpenAI sees AI beginning to contribute to its own development. Pachocki said an acceleration in pretraining research could emerge as soon as 2027 if AI systems begin generating fundamental algorithmic advances, potentially speeding progress faster than alignment and monitoring techniques can keep pace.
OpenAI acknowledged that perplexity is an imperfect common standard. Results can be difficult to compare between laboratories because developers use different datasets and training mixtures, and perplexity does not capture all capability gains, including those produced by chain-of-thought reasoning.
