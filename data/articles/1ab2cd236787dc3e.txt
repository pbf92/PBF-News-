TITLE: 7 underrated GTA 6 changes no one is talking about
OUTLET: GAMINGbible

GTA VI is leaps and bounds ahead of previous Grand Theft Auto titles; so much so there are likely dozens of new features you’ve probably missed so far.
I’ve logged well over 100 hours by now and I’m still learning something new every day I play. With that in mind, here are seven features the game doesn’t explicitly tell you about, but are good to be aware of.
Losing your Wanted Level becomes much easier with the right car tires
While the vehicle customisation feels like it’s lacking in some areas, it makes up for cosmetic omissions with practicality.
Depending on which auto-shop you go to, you can equip your favourite vehicles with different tyres to improve their handling on-and-off-road.
Advert
Having a getaway car capable of driving across grass and mud is such a huge advantage when you’re fleeing from the police, so it’s an option to look into.
Stealth is much deeper than silent takedowns
When robbing a bank or jewellery store the goal should always be getting in and out without getting caught, and there’s much more to this than discreetly knocking out guards.
Most robbery locations have a backdoor that can be picked open from Chapter 3 onwards, and more often than not there’s an alarm and CCTV footage that can be disabled too. Recently I robbed three stores back-to-back and escaped without causing a chase thanks to these new stealth options.
Clothing customisation in GTA 6 far exceeds GTA 5
GTA VI leans heavily into roleplay. Jason and Lucia are your characters and you can customise them as you see fit, right down to the style of clothing they wear.
However, what I love most about this is how you can wear the same piece of clothing in multiple ways. A shirt for instance can be buttoned up, unbuttoned, tucked into your pants, etc. It’s something I’ve wanted ever since playing GTA Online for the first time, so when you buy yourself a fresh fit be sure to stop by the mirror or changing room to see what styling options it has
Doomscrolling GTA 6’s Snapmatic app can make you rich, fast
Rockstar Games created its own brand of social media network for GTA 6 called Snapmatic, which seems to be a light-hearted jab at Instagram.
It’ll show you what some of Leonida’s residents are getting up to out in the world, but if you see someone flaunting wealth, flash cars or other valuables see if their post comes with a location attached.
If it does, you can drive there and steal whatever they’ve got for a profit.
GTA 6 lets you watch full TV series
Prior to GTA VI’s launch Rockstar Games was pushing a new character called Macca The Gator.
The mascot could be seen on merchandise and in-game advertisements, and even a TV series you can watch in your hideouts.
There are other TV shows as well, with actual episodes rather than the nonsensical short-clips of GTA V.
How to push and shove vehicles in GTA 6
If your car gets flipped you can’t just magically roll over like in GTA V, but if the car lands on its side you might be able to salvage it by walking into it to initiate a push. If it’s still not budging you can press square or X to give it a shove.
This is handy for cars, but it’ll also work for boats you’ve accidentally marooned, and the other I used the mechanic to push a beached jet ski into the water for a getaway.
Leave your car’s engine running if you’re expecting a quick getaway
Something I’ve seen loads of other players do in GTA VI is park their car nearby a robbery location and switch the engine off.
First of all, you should park your car a brisk jog away so it can’t be ID’d by witnesses and CCTV, and second if you double-tap triangle or the Y button you’ll get out with the engine still running. Once you’ve got your ill-gotten gains you can jump back in your car and speed off much quicker, as every second counts in those heists.
We’re just scratching the service when it comes to neat additions in GTA VI. Rockstar Games literally thought of everything when crafting the world of Leonida and we’ll probably be discovering new details and secrets for many months to come.
