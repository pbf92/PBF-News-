TITLE: Andrews on facing Pochettino's Manchester United
OUTLET: Brentford FC

Brentford head coach Keith Andrews has discussed the challenge of taking on Manchester United in Mauricio Pochettino's first home game in charge of the club.
The Bees travel to Old Trafford to face the Red Devils in the Premier League on Saturday afternoon (3pm kick-off GMT).
United fell to a 4-0 defeat against rivals Liverpool at Anfield last weekend, before being held to a 2-2 Champions League draw at Sporting in midweek.
On coming up against Pochettino this weekend, Andrews said: “He's someone that we know. I studied his teams quite a lot in my coaching journey and had a lot of admiration for him. Even before his Tottenham team, it was his Southampton team.
“You can see traits of what he is as a manager and what he would like to push into his new United team. Of course, you've got to balance that with where they are currently and what they have been doing.
“I think you can see the little transition, even though it's been seven or eight minutes against Liverpool [before Leny Yoro was sent off] and then the game against Sporting Lisbon.
“We'll expect a very well-organised Man United side, even though they've been restricted in terms of what they can do on the grass.”
“I studied his teams quite a lot in my coaching journey and had a lot of admiration for him”
United go into the game sat second-bottom of the table and are winless in the Premier League since late August, while Monday night's 4-0 victory against Everton moved the Bees up to sixth.
“It's still Manchester United at Old Trafford and the new manager's first game at home, which will create an energy in the stadium that we've all felt over the years with what that stadium is capable of,” Andrews continued.
“We've seen it in recent weeks when they've gone behind, how they've managed to get back into it and the part that the crowd can play in that.
“Exactly where they are is not really my concern. My only concern is how we try and win on Saturday.”
Saturday's game is the first of three exciting clashes inside a week, with reigning champions Arsenal and fellow title contenders Manchester City visiting Gtech Community Stadium on Wednesday and Saturday night, respectively.
“At the start of last season, if I'd had that schedule, I would have been a little bit daunted by it, but I'm not in the slightest,” the west Londoners' head coach added.
“It's a massive week for us. I think it's exactly what this club has strived towards, being where we are and pitting our wits against the top teams in this league. It's a week we should absolutely embrace the challenge of.”
