TITLE: Wall Street calls Anthropic ‘a steal’ and ‘generational opportunity’ as stock slips below its IPO price
OUTLET: Fortune

Wall Street analysts don’t seem worried about Anthropic’s volatile performance since its highly anticipated public debut last week, even as the AI darling fell nearly 7% Tuesday and slipped below its issuance price. In fact, some even told Fortune the stock’s move lower is a “steal” and a “generational opportunity” for investors looking to get in.
Anthropic shares now sit about 1.3% below their $83 IPO price and roughly 26.5% below the $111.50 they opened at on the Nasdaq on Nov. 19, but many on Wall Street still call the stock a buy. Experts argue that a slide in a newly public company doesn’t mean it’s doomed and the Claude maker’s fundamentals look remarkable as AI spending continues to pick up.
“I think this is a generational opportunity,” said Scott Berg, a senior equity research analyst at Needham. “To get this underneath the issue price, I think is ultimately going to be reflected as a steal for someone that wants to get involved.” Needham rates the stock a buy with a $150 price target, which implies roughly 83% upside from Tuesday’s close of $81.92.
Berg said demand is climbing as enterprises rush to adopt Anthropic’s AI coding agents like Claude Code. Goldman Sachs forecasts that revenue will jump from roughly $52 billion in 2026 to $136 billion in 2027, an increase of more than 161%. Needham’s own model is for growth of 156%. “The history of the world’s never seen growth like this before at this scale,” Berg told Fortune, calling Anthropic the “leader in frontier intelligence.”
Berg isn’t alone in his view. On Monday, Wedbush also issued a buy call with a $120 price target. Analysts called the stock in a note to clients “the clearest public way to own frontier AI at the start of a very large adoption curve.” The firm added that “the addressable market is vast, spanning a meaningful share of global knowledge work, and enterprise adoption is still early.”
Canaccord Genuity initiated coverage Tuesday with a buy rating and a $135 target, pointing to Anthropic’s potential to become “one of the most important enterprise technology franchises of the AI era.”
With all of this bullish commentary, why is the stock falling? The backdrop for AI-linked stocks has soured as investors question how long the trade can really last. Nvidia’s Monday rally, sparked by Jensen Huang’s return as CEO, faded on Tuesday as the Nasdaq Composite closed down nearly 1.5%.
“I don’t think that short term volatility is unexpected given what we’ve seen,” Evan Schlossman, a principal at investment firm Neostellar Capital, told Fortune.
Some experts predict Anthropic could pan out like Facebook’s 2012 IPO. Shares of the company, now known as Meta, dipped below the $38 offer price on the second day of trading and kept sliding, bottoming at $17.55 in September 2012. That was roughly 54% below the IPO price. The stock didn’t climb back above $38 until the summer of 2013, after the company proved it could make money from mobile ads. Meta is now one of the world’s largest companies by market value.
“[Meta] stumbled their first quarter out of the gate, and then they got their revenue on their advertising model right,” Berg said. “We saw growth at massive scale for several years in stock, which underperformed and traded below its IPO price then went on to a massive three to four year run.”
Not everyone on Wall Street says to buy Anthropic. Ken Mahoney, president and CEO of Mahoney Asset Management, warned investors against jumping in, arguing that too much about the company remains unknown.
The math behind the buildout worries him most. He cited a Financial Times column last week estimating that Anthropic would need over $600 billion in annual revenue to earn an adequate return on its nearly $1 trillion in infrastructure commitments. That is about seven times its current run rate. To Mahoney, the spending is “an ambitious gambit predicated on sustained exponential revenue growth.”
“Our best advice is to give this more time to trade and get a feel for it, and at least an earnings report or two out of the way to see how it reacts as well,” Mahoney said in a statement to Fortune.
