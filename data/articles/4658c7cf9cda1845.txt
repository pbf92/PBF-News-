TITLE: Pochettino leads Man Utd fury at Yoro red card ruling
OUTLET: BBC Sport

- Published
Manchester United head coach Mauricio Pochettino has questioned the competence of video assistant referee officials after defender Leny Yoro had his red card overturned by the Football Association.
Yoro was sent off eight minutes into United's 4-0 defeat at Liverpool on Sunday after bringing down Aleksander Isak just outside the United penalty area.
United sources say they have conclusive proof it was impossible for Isak to reach the ball after he took a heavy touch, that it would have run on to goalkeeper Senne Lammens and Yoro should not have been punished for denying a goalscoring opportunity.
Pochettino said he had some sympathy with referee Sam Barrott but he is livid at how the situation played out.
"I am very disappointed," the Argentine said. "This type of thing can never happen.
"We can accept the referee can make a mistake. But if we have VAR, what were they doing? What were they doing? Are we going to repeat the game? Are we going to have the possibility to play 11 by 11? No.
"We have an unbelievable car, but we need to be at the level to drive [it]. We accept that the technology is there but what are they doing?"
United are furious at both the nature of the audio of the incident, and referees' chief Howard Webb defending referee Barrott's decision and the VAR John Brooks' reinforcing it on the Mic'd Up programme, which was shown on Sky Sports.
They intend to take the matter up with both the Premier League and ProRef.
It is the second time this season United been on the wrong end of a major VAR error.
Webb apologised to United after Erling Haaland's winner in the Manchester derby at Old Trafford on 13 September was allowed to stand even though a clearly offside Enzo Fernandez was interfering with play.
There has been no apology from Webb on this occasion.
Instead, he has doubled down on the call, saying: "I think, by and large, most people would see that as a correct decision, with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball."
United sources claim this interpretation is wrong.
The club are unhappy at the speed the on-field decision was confirmed. It is thought United used data around Isak's top speed and his chances of reaching the ball in their defence of Yoro to the FA. They claim this could have been done "within minutes" of the incident occurring.
This is not the only bone of contention at Old Trafford around the incident.
Pochettino also pointed out Liverpool forward Bradley Barcola was too close to the United wall from the resulting free-kick and Dominik Szoboszlai's opening goal should have been disallowed.
"No-one talked about [the fact] that the goal wasn't legal," said Pochettino. "Barcola was touching our wall. It must be disallowed.
"We need to defend my players and my club, and that is to denounce it and to say, the decision wasn't good. And now, the three points, where are the three points?"
Webb defends Yoro red card
What referee's chief Webb said about the incident on the Mic'd Up programme.
"There's a question mark, I guess, around whether or not he would get onto that ball before Lammens. Now, in the moment, the referee looked at it and felt he would.
"That was the referee's call made on the field, and then the VAR checks that to see whether that decision made on the field was clearly and obviously incorrect, and formed the opinion that it wasn't.
"That if there was any doubt, then it was doubt.
"It wasn't going to be something that was clearly and obviously wrong and therefore allowed that decisive on-field decision to stand. and
"I think, by and large, most people would see that as a correct decision, with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball.
"The alternative view is that Lammens might get there first, but I think it's a little bit debatable. Certainly not clearly wrong."
