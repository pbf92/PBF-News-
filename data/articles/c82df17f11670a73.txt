TITLE: Hip Arthroscopy Outperforms Personalized Therapy for Femoroacetabular Impingement at Two Years
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Hip arthroscopy delivers superior functional recovery and quality of life compared with personalized hip therapy in patients with femoroacetabular impingement syndrome at 12 to 24 months. According to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in the Journal of Orthopaedic Surgery and Research by Zi-hao Deng, Xin-yang Huang, Hao-xuan Li, Zhe Xue, and Jin Zhang, surgical intervention consistently outperformed conservative therapy across clinical and radiological parameters. The study evaluated mid-term outcomes between both interventions to offer evidence-based guidance for clinical decision-making.
The authors conducted their systematic review in accordance with PRISMA guidelines and prospectively registered the research protocol in PROSPERO under identifier CRD420251066546. The investigators searched major biomedical databases, including PubMed and the Cochrane Library, covering publications released from June 2015 to June 2025. Across this ten-year search interval, the team identified five randomized controlled trials meeting their selection criteria, providing a pooled sample size of 846 patients diagnosed with femoroacetabular impingement syndrome.
Researchers examined both patient-reported clinical measures and objective radiological metrics across the included randomized cohorts. Primary functional assessments included the international Hip Outcome Tool (iHOT-33), the Hip Outcome Score for activities of daily living (HOS-ADL), and the Hip Outcome Score sports subscale (HOS-sports). The review also evaluated general health-related quality of life using the EQ-5D index and the EQ-5D visual analogue scale. In addition, investigators examined anatomical parameters, focusing specifically on the maximum alpha angle and the lateral center-edge angle.
To synthesize trial data, the authors applied a random-effects model for meta-analysis and utilized narrative synthesis when data were insufficient for quantitative pooling. At 12 months, hip arthroscopy demonstrated statistically superior functional outcomes over personalized hip therapy. The surgical group achieved a weighted mean difference of 14.47 points on the iHOT-33 (95% CI 7.45 to 21.50; I² = 81%). Daily living and athletic scores also favored surgery, with a weighted mean difference of 12.40 on the HOS-ADL (95% CI 8.56 to 16.24; I² = 31%) and 13.62 on the HOS-sports scale (95% CI 7.73 to 19.52; I² = 48%). All observed functional advantages persisted at 24-month follow-up.
Health-related quality of life metrics similarly demonstrated significant benefits favoring surgical intervention at 12 months. The EQ-5D index yielded a weighted mean difference of 0.08 (95% CI 0.01 to 0.14; I² = 84%), while the EQ-5D visual analogue scale revealed a weighted mean difference of 4.36 (95% CI 0.91 to 7.82; I² = 52%). Furthermore, hip arthroscopy significantly reduced the maximum alpha angle by 8.26 degrees (95% CI -10.71 to -5.81) at 12 months, showing no statistical heterogeneity across trials (I² = 0%).
Assessment of evidence certainty revealed notable differences across the reported clinical outcomes. The certainty of evidence was graded as high for reductions in the maximum alpha angle, moderate for 24-month iHOT-33 and 12-month HOS-ADL measures, and low to very low for the remaining endpoints. The researchers concluded that hip arthroscopy shows consistently better outcomes than personalized hip therapy across function, quality of life, and radiology at 12 to 24 months, supporting surgery while emphasizing that individual patient circumstances should guide clinical decisions.
