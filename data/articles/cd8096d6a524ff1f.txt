'What happens in GTA 6 stays in GTA 6': Police across US warn fans about taking the game to their real lives
Police departments are reminding Americans that street racing, auto theft and gun violence are still crimes in real life, as excitement over Grand Theft Auto 6, a game following the lives of career criminals, reaches a fever pitch in the US.
"Play the game. Don't play yourself," Atlanta police warned in a social media post, while Denver authorities said that "running from police is still illegal".
Over 20 million units of the game have been purchased worldwide since its release this week, making GTA 6 likely the biggest entertainment launch in history.
The game, known for its freedom of play, ranging from buying a cheeseburger to firing a grenade launcher, is set in a Florida-inspired state.
"Assaulting someone for extra points, it's not worth it," an officer from the San Antonio Police Department, in Texas, said in a video.
Since Rockstar Games' 1997 inaugural release in the Grand Theft Auto series, the game has been both celebrated and condemned for its depictions of violence and its invitation to players to virtually participate.
It allows players to do everything from selling drugs to soliciting prostitutes and shooting law enforcement officers.
GTA 6's release in the US on Thursday ended a 13-year break in the series, unleashing a surge of fan enthusiasm and also concern among police departments.
"We need to talk," the San Juan Police Department, also in Texas, said in a video published on Instagram the same day the game launched in the US.
"We know you're excited and we know you've been waiting a long time, but what happens in GTA 6 stays in GTA 6."
In Arizona, the Pima County Sheriff's Office told GTA 6 fans in a Facebook post: "Real-life laws don't come with cheat codes, respawns, or mission restarts."
Law enforcement officials weren't the only ones worried, with one pundit on Fox News wondering if the game might "destigmatise crime". Rosanna Scotto also raised the question of whether it made young people "more inclined to use guns at an earlier age now because of these kind of video games".
Research on the relationship between video game violence and its effects on players is mixed.
"Attributing violence to violent video gaming is not scientifically sound and draws attention away from other factors," the APA said.
But the APA also said research showed an association between violent video games and increased aggressive behaviour and decreased moral judgement.
Despite concerns, the release of GTA 6 has had a meteoric landing, topping $2.36bn (£1.8bn) in first-day sales, and generating news headlines over the surge in people skipping school and work in order to play.
