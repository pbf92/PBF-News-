TITLE: Mauricio Pochettino’s first Manchester United practice sneakily recorded by drone
OUTLET: New York Post

The soccer world got a glimpse — an unauthorized one into Mauricio Pochettino’s first training session with Manchester United after a drone operator captured and posted video footage of it on YouTube.
The person, who posts on the account “CC OVERVIEW,” flew the drone near United’s Carrington training ground on Thursday, recording 12 minutes of footage that shows Pochettino speaking with players and working with them on the practice pitch just three days out from Sunday’s match against Liverpool.
Manchester United has not publicly commented on the incident, but considering how closely soccer clubs guard their tactics in training, this is a major security breach ahead of the former U.S. men’s national team head coach’s debut as the Premier League club’s new manager.
Signs placed around the area near the training ground prohibit the use of drones and threaten “prosecution” and being “subject to civil action” if anyone is “found operating a drone over this location,” Sky News reported.
The user who posted the video claimed everything that he did was legal.
“It is unrestricted airspace, meaning anyone can fly over it, the drone remained in line of sight at all times, it didn’t fly over anyone or even near anyone so was a perfectly legal flight going by the [Civil Aviation Authority] rules that regulates drone usage,” the individual wrote in a comment on the original video. “The drone is correctly labelled and I hold all the relevant licences, public liability and insurance required.
“As for leaking team news/ tactics, it was half an hour into the manager’s first training session, there are many media there so certainly won’t be the only footage of training released.”
Manchester United does use drones to record its training sessions, but Sky News reported that Thursday’s training had been closed to the media.
The Civil Aviation Authority’s guidelines do state that anyone who uses “these devices where people can expect privacy, such as inside their home or garden, you’re likely to be breaking data protection laws.”
Man United spent a pretty penny getting Pochettino to the Premier League club, which included paying U.S. Soccer the $10 million buy clause that the now-ex-coach had in his contract with the national team.
Pochettino called it an “opportunity and it’s a privilege” to take charge at Manchester United, in an interview with the club’s internal media, and that there was no time for Man U to “waste time.”
“We need to be ready to compete Sunday and now it’s not to think to rest, it’s not to think to sleep, it’s not to think, it’s to be ready and to prepare everything to be sure that we are going to compete in the best condition at Anfield,” he said.
