TITLE: Can Pochettino resuscitate a dysfunctional Manchester United?
OUTLET: The Hindu

To shape a lasting legacy is one of the greatest allures in elite sport. Ambitious men and women, from time immemorial, have jumped in head first at this opportunity despite the scrutiny it attracts and the sacrifice it demands.
From Carlo Ancelotti’s quest to restore five-time World champion Brazil’s glory days to Xavi Hernandez’s desire to return Dutch football to the top of the totem pole, there are examples galore.
The latest to join the bandwagon is Mauricio Pochettino, who has been entrusted with the responsibility of resuscitating a dysfunctional Manchester United. Since the legendary Alex Ferguson departed in May 2013 after winning his 13th Premier League title in 26 full seasons at the helm, Pochettino is the Red Devils’ eighth regular head coach.
In this period, United has barely competed for the top prize at the domestic level and has only the 2016-17 Europa League title to show for continental success. The challenge for Pochettino, in the aftermath of the dismissal of former player and club darling Michael Carrick, will not just be about arresting the seemingly terminal decline but also making United the feared outfit it once was.
“It’s a historic club with amazing years competing and being one of the best clubs in the world,” the Argentine said in his inaugural press conference after becoming the latest man to be lured by United’s grandeur and history. “The job is to try to find a way to get the results that the club needs, and it’s because Manchester United needs to be in a different place than we are now.”
