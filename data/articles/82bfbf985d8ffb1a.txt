TITLE: Anthropic IPO turns thousands of tech workers into overnight millionaires, reports say
OUTLET: Fox Business

A wave of wealth is washing over Silicon Valley as AI giant Anthropic hits the public markets, reportedly turning thousands of tech workers into millionaires overnight.
The company behind the Claude AI chatbot made its debut on the Nasdaq Thursday under the ticker "ANT."
After pricing its initial public offering at $83 a share Wednesday, Anthropic's stock surged out of the gate, opening at $111.50 and giving it a fully diluted valuation of roughly $1.5 trillion.
Based on share counts in SEC filings, Anthropic’s roughly 5,300 employees are sitting on a combined fortune of around $232 billion at the $83 IPO price, which averages out to roughly $44 million per worker, according to CNBC.
Although estimates vary, the expected wealth creation is massive.
Private-share marketplace Hill.com estimates that a majority of Anthropic's staff are now millionaires, including roughly 2,800 workers holding stakes worth at least $10 million, according to The Wall Street Journal.
At least 400 early employees are projected to hold stakes worth $100 million or more, at the $83 share price, with several potentially becoming billionaires, CNBC reported, citing investors.
Anthropic’s seven co-founders, including CEO Dario Amodei and President Daniela Amodei, each hold between 230 million and 240 million shares, putting their individual net worths at roughly $20 billion apiece at the $83 share price, according to CNBC.
Unlike traditional IPOs that restrict insider selling for about six months, Anthropic is letting some shareholders tap into their fortunes immediately.
According to the company's SEC filing, non-founder executives, directors and current and former employees are permitted to sell up to 10% of their locked-up holdings on Day 1 — making as many as 315 million insider shares eligible to hit the market right away.
Founders and major stakeholders face staggered lockup restrictions lasting up to a year.
FOX Business reached out to Anthropic for comment.
