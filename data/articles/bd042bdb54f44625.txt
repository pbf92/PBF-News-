Gold extends gain after Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia helm sparks rally
Gold prices increased for the second day after Jensen Huang's return to the helm of Nvidia recharging bets on AI and spurring rallies across markets.
Bullion rose 1% nearing $3,670 an ounce after it gained 2.7% in the prior session. The rebound partially retraced the near 9% drop last week which took the metal to its lowest level in over a year.
Nvidia reinstated Huang as the president and CEO of the firm less than two weeks after he resigned under a cloud of scandal.
The announcement led to Wall Street witnessing its best day ever since April 9, 2025, and helped a spur rebound in gold two weeks after it had dropped below $4,000 an ounce support level.
An earlier rout in technology stocks prompted investors to sell the bullion to cover losses, adding to the pressure from stubborn inflation and hawkish commentary from US Fed officials.
Investors are still estimating the monetary policy to continue being tight in the coming months.
On Monday, the Fed New York President John Williams said the central bank has yet "work to do" to tame sticky inflation and the market is pricing in another interest hike rate at the US Fed's December meeting.
Elsewhere, the metal received some support from Chinese gold imports, which rose to a record $209 billion this year, so far, in an unprecedented buying spree that is helping keep a lid on its trade surplus with major partners such as the European Union.
Spot gold increased 0.6% to $3,656.5 an ounce as of 9.08 am in Singapore. Silver was up 1.1% at $43.57 an ounce, after rising almost 5%on Monday. Platinum and palladium advanced as well.
