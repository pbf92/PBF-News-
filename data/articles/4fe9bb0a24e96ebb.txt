TITLE: I Feared GTA 6 Was a Rockstar Retread, Until Its Love Story Stole My Heart
OUTLET: PCMag

I love Grand Theft Auto VI, but it's not perfect. I’ll forever praise the world that the developers at Rockstar managed to bring to life. Spending 100 hours in Vice City and beyond took me from the stinkiest swamps to the most dazzling penthouses. Alongside the activities to partake in and details to marvel at, GTA 6 gave me the tools to be the criminal that I wanted to be. But rich open worlds are GTA's bread and butter, so you'd hope a new entry would bring more to the table after so long. And what good is a stunning world without caring about the people in it? GTA's juvenile cynicism may make you laugh at its characters but it never makes you love them.
However, despite retaining some disappointing flaws that have plagued the series since the start, from stagnant structure to questionable tone, GTA 6 still surpassed my expectations and is one of my favorite games of the year. Why? GTA 6’s biggest and boldest change negates the pitfalls of its predecessor. Grand Theft Auto VI is sincere enough to actually make me care about what really matters: Jason and Lucia’s love story. (WARNING: Major spoilers ahead.)
Worn-Out Places, Worn-Out Faces
Grand Theft Auto VI’s romance is the secret soulful weapon the game needed, because while I did enjoy my experience a lot, it’s not perfect. Amid all the hype over the years, there were plenty of valid reasons to be skeptical about GTA 6. After playing the entire game, I think some of that skepticism was warranted. My biggest overall problem is that this isn’t some grand reimagining of GTA; it’s just another GTA.
Whenever I stopped going on flights of fancy in the open world focused on progressing story sequences, I was hit with a sense of déjà vu as someone who has played every major GTA game over the past two decades. Missions flowed similarly, with obvious tells when a seemingly solid plan would explode into a fracas. Once again, I found myself going from rags to riches, from the petty crime of poor people to the legal crime of corporate America. I collected a cohort of colorful quest givers with their own little storylines I knew would play out over a handful of levels.
GTA 6 is by no means a lazy game. Being popular isn’t the same as being hack, and even at its most annoying, GTA has always been exceptionally intentional and well-crafted. So much of GTA 6’s effort and refinement can be seen in the vast array of micro elements spread out across a world with such scale and scope. But the larger gameplay structure the world leans on is not that dissimilar from the classic open-world crime games we’ve been playing since 2001. It's a borderline retro game, an accidental throwback.
The sense of datedness crops up in other places, too. GTA 6 features several main Black characters that feel authentically written and joyfully performed, with female rap duo Real Dimez receiving one of the best subplots. But there are lingering traces of racial weirdness. Certain Black communities are presented as especially dangerous, as if the game doesn’t start out with you delivering drugs to white hillbilly mud huts. In the context of the game, that danger is framed as something exciting, not something to avoid. But it’s one thing for the Houser Brothers (GTA’s creators) to genuinely love hip-hop music for its transgressive quality. It’s another thing to view the very presence of Black people as something transgressive, and GTA has yet to fully figure that out.
These stale issues might be easier to overlook if we still got new Grand Theft Auto games every other year or so like back in the 2000s. But it’s been more than a decade since GTA 5, and all we’ve gotten since from the franchise is the increasingly all-consuming GTA Online, which isn’t even available at launch with GTA 6. Obama was president when the last GTA came out. The entire Destiny saga came and went since then. GTA 5 was a PS3 game, and now we’re on the cusp of the PlayStation 6. After such a long wait, the stakes are too high, so you get why Rockstar had to play it safe. GTA 6 also needs to last the next decade or more, and there’s a point where we should recognize some expectations are impossible to live up to.
However, every new creative work is a chance to try something different; to be experimental. So in that sense, GTA 6 is a bit of a missed opportunity, which stings because who knows when we’ll see the series again. GTA 6 is like an old movie star who hired the best plastic surgeon money can buy. They look genuinely great, and you get why they became a star in the first place. But you can still tell it’s them.
Once I clocked the formula, I feared the game would be stunning yet shallow. But then, before I even realized it, GTA 6 found a new way to be novel—by opening my heart.
The Better Half
I look at Jason Duval (Dylan Rourke) and Lucia Caminos (Manni L. Perez) and I see plenty of pieces of past Rockstar protagonists. Lucia has a tolerance for chaos that’s very Tommy Vercetti, while Jason is lowkey and folksy like Arthur Morgan transported to modern day. Like many of their predecessors, both of them spent a lot of this game as glorified lowlife henchmen to bigger, more extravagant criminals. Rosencrantz and Guildenstern are dead, and Jason and Lucia took their place. Even being able to swap between multiple playable characters was GTA 5’s big twist. However, Jason and Lucia aren’t just partners in crime; they’re romantic partners. That crucial element, which GTA 6 pulls off so successfully, makes all the difference.
For starters, this setup gives GTA 6 a whole new pool of pop culture references to draw from. Synthesizing gangster flick inspirations has always been the franchise's strong suit. Jason and Lucia are charming bank robbers in the style of Bonnie and Clyde. Lucia’s prison life feels very Orange Is the New Black. By going from trivial theft to death-defying car stunts, they’re Dom and Letty from Fast and Furious. They’re slick operators smart enough to erase security footage and tough enough to win underground cage fights. They find themselves wrapped up in escalating crime conspiracies while following a suave mastermind, just like in Money Heist. And every time they put on new outfits to seduce a target, while still keeping their own connection strong, I thought of Philip and Elizabeth from The Americans.
Maintaining Jason and Lucia’s relationship is also a major gameplay mechanic unto itself. Of course, GTA 6 didn’t invent this. Dating sims have been around for years, A Boy and His Blob has a dedicated hug button, and recent Fire Emblem games have shown that making your characters kiss can be as compelling as sending them into battle. But GTA 6, maximalist project that it is, triples down by providing so many different interactive avenues for showing affection.
Squeeze the trigger not just to shoot your gun but to make Jason and Lucia hold hands. Before Lucia even returns from prison, Jason must clean up around the house to make it more welcoming. Choose when to go to bed together, and when to actually sleep instead of just scrolling on your phone. I spent real moments of my real life driving to a fake store to buy fake groceries so my sick video game boyfriend would feel better. My real wife was enthralled.
The relationship impacts larger moments, too. An early major mission is a hospital escape sequence (something that felt right out of Phantom Pain) that shows the strength of the couple’s teamwork, even under extreme duress. However, during later missions, it’s actually possible to abandon your partner during a fight if things get too intense. Not only is this a tactical error—you essentially turn a co-op shooter into a solo one without dropping the difficulty—but it hurts your home life, too. It lowers the relationship meter and affects how Jason and Lucia interact. You can repair the damage, but it takes time and effort. You actually have to play as the guilty partner and do some loving gestures as penance.

GTA 6 tracks everything from going on cute dates at various restaurants to shopping for clothes together, and even playing parodies of pretentious indie games (which is maybe punching down, coming from a behemoth like Rockstar). Throwaway mini-games like this are another GTA staple, a way to fill up the world with small details. But they gain added resonance here as GTA 6 reframes these small details into small acts of love that make up a larger whole. It does wonders for the game just as an engine for player-driven role-playing and storytelling. When I drove down to pick up Lucia from prison and celebrate her freedom, I had Jason listening to Spanish music on the radio because I thought he thought she might like it. It was so loud you could still hear it even as I got out of the car and walked over to the gate.
You’re invested in Jason and Lucia as a couple because you influence them with your choices, and that means potentially taking them in the opposite direction. You can perform emotional honesty or stay cold and keep secrets. You can have a wandering eye or stay faithful. I personally didn’t enjoy playing in a way that added tension, but I appreciated that option was always there. If I was the person who liked to make my dolls kiss, I imagined the person who likes to trap Sims with full bladders in rooms with no exits. Both are valid play experiences. The possibility of failure is also just a constant and accurate reminder that a relationship, even when it’s worth it, is something you have to work for.
Hot Together
Grand Theft Auto VI deeply cares about the emotional integrity of its central relationship. That alone is satisfying, but it also affects the game in greater ways. GTA 6 has perhaps the best, most mature, and most well-calibrated tone yet for a Grand Theft Auto game. It’s not pure parody like the PS2 classics, the brooding overcorrection of 4, or nearly as obnoxious as 5’s descent into nihilism. If anything, it’s a thematic sequel to Red Dead Redemption 2, arguably Rockstar’s most celebrated narrative achievement.
That’s not to say GTA 6 is some overly serious affair. Dumb comedy is simply baked into the franchise’s DNA. You frequently use a Venmo-like mobile app to handle various payments, and that app is called “Buck Me.” Radio stations are filled with commercials for gamer vibrators and shady BBL surgeons. Missions have you uncovering vape scams, spying on evil bodybuilders, and infiltrating sex mansions and nudist resorts. One of the biggest tests of your relationship is how you choose to behave when undercover on a porno set. Again, for better or worse, GTA 6 is still GTA.
But for me, all of that stupidity goes down easier because there’s now something much more legitimate at the core. Jason and Lucia themselves aren’t a joke. Their affectionate banter is authentic and genuine. You understand why they are attracted to each other, why Lucia values Jason’s military reliability while he craves her crafty spontaneity. They make you want to cause trouble in the open world just because you’re curious how they’ll react. You and me against the world, babe. Their connection to each other keeps everything grounded and adds weight, even as the scripted action and synth score shift into gritty, neon '80s crime-flick cheese. The sex puns aren’t just tolerable, but a charming respite from the harsh reality of their situation.
Speaking of sex, for an adult relationship to have chemistry, it can’t shy away from the physical side. When the relationship meter is at maximum level, it says Jason and Lucia are “Hot Together.” During their reunion, the camera demonstrates why Lucia is a woman Jason would wait for, showing her in the shower wearing nothing but an ankle monitor. And throughout the game, Jason is arguably more objectified, particularly during a very Magic Mike-coded dance sequence.
