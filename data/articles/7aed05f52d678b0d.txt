First Tick: Top global cues to watch in today's trade
Indian benchmark indices are likely to see a muted start on November 17, tracking GIFT Nifty, which is trading unchanged at 25,296.50in early trade.

Indian equity markets started the week on a strong note on November 16, with the Nifty closing near the 25,300 mark, as buying in FMCG and banking stocks offset selling pressure in pharma, auto, metal and realty shares. Sustained foreign fund inflows and falling crude oil prices also supported market sentiment.
At close, the Sensex was up 510.40 points or 0.63 percent at 82,102.85, and the Nifty was up 146.80 points or 0.58 percent at 25,291.10.
The broader markets underperformed the benchmark indices, with the Nifty Midcap index falling 0.5% and the Nifty Smallcap index declining 1.1%.
Here is how financial markets across the globe fared overnight:
GIFT Nifty (Flat)
GIFT Nifty was trading flat at around 25,296.50 in early trade, indicating a muted start for the domestic equity markets.
Asian Markets (Down)
Asian shares followed Wall Street lower on Tuesday, weighed down by weakness in technology companies after the US stepped up efforts to curb China's access to advanced AI chips, escalating the race for technological dominance.
South Korea's tech-heavy KOSPI slid 2% shortly after the open, while Japan's Nikkei fell 1%.
MSCI's broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan eased 0.63%.
US Equities (Slip)
A broad selloff dragged US stocks lower on Monday as fallout from Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO soured enthusiasm for AI stocks, raising the stakes for the chipmaker's earnings report on Tuesday and Anthropic's initial public offering later in the week.
The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 739.38 points, or 1.41%, to 51,608.17, the S&P 500 lost 145.08 points, or 1.92%, to 7,416.59 and the Nasdaq Composite lost 626.51 points, or 2.49%, to 24,509.76.
Dollar Index (Flat)
The US dollar was perched near its highest point since April 2025 on Tuesday, helped by growing bets on more aggressive Federal Reserve rate hikes as inflationary pressures in the world's largest economy persist.
Against a basket of currencies, the dollar was a touch higher at 103.61.
US Bond Yield (Up)
The yield on 10-year Treasuries was little changed at 5.14%, while 2-year Treasury bond were up 2 basis points at 4.86%.
Asian currencies (Mixed)
Asian currencies traded mixed against the US dollar in early trade on November 17. The Indonesian rupiah declined 0.34%, while the South Korean won slipped 0.25%. The Singapore dollar and Japanese yen also weakened marginally by 0.08% and 0.04%, respectively.
On the other hand, the Malaysian ringgit rose 0.04%, while the Taiwan dollar and Thai baht gained 0.05% and 0.04%, respectively. The Philippine peso edged up 0.01%, while the Chinese renminbi was marginally lower by 0.02%.
Crude (Slips)
Oil prices fell on Tuesday as expectations of a supply surplus, fuelled by recovering Middle East exports, overshadowed concerns about continued attacks on tankers in the region.
Brent crude futures were down 44 cents, or 0.5%, at $92.00 a barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) futures fell 44 cents, or 0.5%, to $85.28.
Gold (Flat)
Gold steadied after its lowest close since September 2025, under pressure from stubborn US inflation and losses on Wall Street.
Bullion was near $3,860 an ounce, after slipping 0.7% in the previous session. Traders have raised bets that the Federal Reserve will tighten monetary policy further in 2027.
Fund Flow Action
Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) bought equities worth Rs 4,864 crore on November 16, extending their buying streak to the fifth consecutive session. Meanwhile, domestic institutional investors (DIIs) continued their selling for the 12th straight session, offloading equities worth Rs 3,519 crore.
