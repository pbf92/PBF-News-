TITLE: Psychosocial Interventions Support Substance Use Disorder Treatment in Africa
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive mixed-methods systematic review demonstrates the overall effectiveness of psychosocial interventions for treating substance use disorders across African settings. Researchers evaluated the active components and behavior change techniques deployed throughout the continent, where structured syntheses of these therapeutic practices had previously been lacking. The resulting synthesis established that structured psychosocial programs deliver measurable improvements in substance use outcomes while also benefiting broader mental and physical health symptoms.
Conducted in accordance with Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines under PROSPERO registration CRD42024542926, the investigation systematically searched five major scientific databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, The Cochrane Library, Web of Science, CINAHL, and Academic Search Complete. From an initial identification of 4,168 articles, 121 individual studies met all formal inclusion criteria. Among these included publications, 27 studies contributed qualitative findings alongside quantitative outcome assessments.
The majority of included investigations evaluated interventions administered to adult participant groups. Across the identified literature, the most frequently utilized clinical models comprised brief intervention approaches, motivational interviewing, and cognitive behaviour therapy. To identify the specific active ingredients operating within these treatments, researchers systematically coded behavior change techniques utilizing the established Behavior Change Technique Taxonomy version 1 framework.
Most examined studies reported at least one active behavior change technique, with intervention protocols incorporating an overall range of 1 to 14 distinct techniques. The analytical coding demonstrated that the most heavily utilized technique categories focused on goals and planning, feedback and monitoring, social support, and shaping knowledge alongside natural consequences. Conversely, techniques addressing associations, scheduled consequences, self-belief, and personal identity emerged as the least frequently utilized categories across the programs.
The qualitative data synthesis identified four prominent thematic domains: perceived benefits, satisfaction and acceptability of the intervention, perceived challenges or barriers, and actionable recommendations for future programming. The authors noted that substantial heterogeneity across study designs, sample sizes, and diverse outcome measures inevitably limited direct comparability across the literature. Nevertheless, characterizing these active techniques clarifies the foundational components of regional care and highlights clear opportunities to optimize future psychosocial substance use disorder treatments across Africa.
