TITLE: Dynamic reconstruction enables self-adaptive mass transfer toward industrial alkaline water electrolysis
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

While improving the intrinsic activity of electrocatalysts remains a major research focus, the overall efficiency of OER is severely constrained by mass transfer kinetics at the catalyst/electrolyte interface. Under industrially relevant conditions, bubble kinetics strongly influence mass transfer and catalytic performance, while dynamic reconstruction is widely regarded as a key bridge linking precursors to actual catalytic active phases. However, the synergistic interplay among reconstruction, bubble release behavior, and mass transfer remains poorly understood.
To elucidate the intrinsic relationship between reconstruction and mass transfer at high current densities, a research team from Jilin University employed an oxidant-induced corrosion strategy to successfully prepare a sub-square-meter-scale NiFeOxSy/NM (0.5 m × 0.5 m) pre-catalyst. Published in the journal Science Bulletin, this work directly links dynamic reconstruction with self-adaptive mass transfer behavior at industrial-scale current densities and reveals the impact of self-reconstruction on bubble release behavior. Under high current densities, rapid in-situ electrochemical desulfurization drives the spontaneous conversion of NiFeOxSy/NM into highly active γ-Ni(Fe)OOH species, inducing high-frequency bubble release and intense interfacial micro-convection. This process reshapes the local solvation environment, and promotes timely OH− replenishment, thereby establishes a self-optimizing mass transfer mechanism that effectively maintains high interfacial alkalinity and suppresses structural corrosion caused by the local acidic micro-environment.
Benefiting from exceptional mass transfer kinetics and intrinsic catalytic activity, the catalyst enables stable operation for 1000 h at 1.0 A/cm2 in 30 wt% KOH at both 25 °C and 80 °C. Even after undergoing 100 startup/shutdown cycles (1200 h) under accelerated durability test conditions, no significant performance degradation. Simultaneously, γ-Ni(Fe)OOH-based electrolyzer achieved an industry-level current density of 1.0 A/cm2 at 1.85 V (60 °C) for ≥ 1600 h. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of the self-enhanced mass transfer effects in improving the overall efficiency of OER.
