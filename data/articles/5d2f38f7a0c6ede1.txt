TITLE: GTA 6 Finally Fixes The Biggest Problem The Cars Of Grand Theft Auto Games Have Always Had
OUTLET: The Autopian

Tomorrow, the sequel to the single most profitable entertainment product of all time will be bestowed on the world. Grand Theft Auto VI, or just GTA 6, has been in the works for so long that there’s a whole meme about everything we got before it. I fully expect global productivity to shatter tomorrow, and as I’m getting excited, too, I learned of a new feature that GTA VI will have that no other GTA game has had before it. Finally, the cars in a Grand Theft Auto game will need to fill up with fuel.
2013’s Grand Theft Auto V was a massively profitable product. Rockstar sold around 235 million copies of the game, which generated over $3 billion alone. Then, GTA V‘s microtransaction system kept the money flowing. Reportedly, the world’s gamers have spent some $8.9 billion on GTA V. Such a runaway success hasn’t been matched by anything, even the biggest James Cameron films.
Rockstar captured lightning in a bottle with GTA V, and it didn’t wait long before starting work on a sequel. Development work on GTA VI began around 2014 while the money from GTA V was still rolling in by the truckload. Following up such a success is not easy, and Rockstar has spent the past twelve years building a game that has the potential to blow even GTA V out of the water.
From the looks of things, GTA VI blends the thrills of GTA V with the gritty realism of GTA IV (2008). The latter had such quirks as an entire game-based Internet, unrivaled crash physics, detailed ballistics, the Euphoria physics engine, and realistic car handling. GTA V, meanwhile, took GTA back towards an arcade-like feel, with cars that handled like they were on rails and could take massive hits with little more than scratches in their bumpers. Now, it seems like Rockstar is planning on topping even GTA IV‘s realism.
Cars Didn’t Run Out Of Gas In Other GTAs
Adding a fuel gauge seems like such a silly detail, but it’s actually a big deal. I’ve played every 3D entry of Grand Theft Auto, including the entries for the PlayStation Portable. Fuel gauges have appeared as plot devices before. For example, in the Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas mission “Supply Lines,” the player pilots an RC plane, and the fuel gauge functions like a mission timer. In GTA V, every car has unlimited fuel until its fuel tank takes too many bullets. Then, the car would sputter before coming to a stop.
But outside of these events, the cars in GTA never run out of fuel. At least to me, this has always been pretty weird since cars are a huge part of these games. Likewise, Rockstar Games hasn’t been afraid to put gas stations in GTA. In GTA V, gas stations even had enterable interiors and cash registers that could be robbed. Yet, in vanilla (stock) versions of GTA, the cars themselves never actually needed to visit them. It’s a similar deal with electric cars and aircraft, which will easily run forever on their full tanks of unicorn farts.
To be fair to Grand Theft Auto, this is pretty much the norm with sandbox games. I mean, players never run out of fuel in Forza Horizon or in Midnight Club. I don’t ever remember running out of fuel in Driver, Saints Row, or Watch Dogs. However, one standout sandbox video game that did have a working fuel gauge was Mafia. In that game, cars gradually ran out of fuel as you drove them. The fuel would also run out faster if the car got banged up or shot. Admittedly, I’ve always been a car nerd first and a gamer second, third, fifth, tenth, or whatever, so I often collect as many cars in these games as I do in real life. Then, often, I just go out on gentle virtual cruises, breaking no laws. As a kid, I simulated getting gas, even though the games never required it from me. As an adult, I’d mod the PC versions of GTA games to give myself a fuel gauge. So, to me, Rockstar Games adding a fuel gauge mechanic is wild!
How Running Out Of Gas Or Battery Works In GTA VI
In GTA VI, just driving around in any vehicle will cause it to consume fuel or battery power. When your fuel tank or battery is full, or at least not in need of topping up, there’s no indication to be found on the in-game head-up display. But when you begin running low, an alert will show in the HUD. Then, you’ll have to visit a fuel station or a charging station before your car runs out and stops. Refueling a car is activated by pressing RT or R2, depending on the console. This triggers a cutscene, and you’ll be back on the road again within 10 seconds.
The folks at Kotaku tested the fuel economy of some of the cars in the game. A pickup truck lasted 29 minutes and 8 seconds before running out of gas. A Banshee (the GTA version of the Dodge Viper) took 18 minutes and 31 seconds to run out of gas.
So that’s a confirmation that the cars in the game do have different driving ranges, just like in real life. Kotaku reports that running out of gas in the game is actually pretty rare. Remember, a big part of GTA is stealing cars, anyway, and it’s unlikely you’re going to be driving around in the same car for even 20 minutes straight, unless you’re cruising as I do. Reportedly, it takes just 15 minutes to cross the entire GTA VI map.
The addition of a gas mechanic could be fascinating in situations like police chases. Just like in real life, you could sputter out with all of Vice City’s police force on your tail! EV charging times are not yet confirmed, but we do know that aircraft, boats, and motorcycles will need fuel, too. Honestly, I’m looking forward to that aviation one. Maybe I can simulate my own Gimli Glider.
The fuel gauge joins a trove of other realism improvements to GTA 6. The non-playable characters are said to be more lifelike; there will be podcasts that you can listen to in your in-game car or on your in-game phone, improved weather, imperfect road conditions, greater density in people, traffic, and surroundings, and a game intelligence system that reacts to what you do. Rockstar even brought back the eating and exercising mechanics from GTA: SA, then introduced new things like Flock cameras, an ever-present police force, and more character personalization.
Get Pumped
Reportedly, earlier builds of GTA VI were so realistic that Rockstar decided to cut some game mechanics. Apparently, Rockstar baked a feature into the game where you had to go grocery shopping to fill the refrigerator to feed your character. You will still need to eat, but Rockstar got rid of the whole grocery shopping for groceries thing. So, by all accounts, this should be the most realistic Grand Theft Auto game yet.
If you’re one of those who have a PlayStation 5 or an Xbox Series X/S, Grand Theft Auto VI drops tomorrow for $79.99 for the Standard Edition and $99.99 for the Ultimate Edition. Sorry, PC gamers, we’re going to have to wait until sometime in 2027 to enjoy GTA VI. If you’re a console gamer, you’ll need up to 225.34 GB to install this monster.
Since I am now more of a PC gamer nowadays, I will be missing out on the launch. As it is, my newest console is an Xbox One Day One Edition, so too vintage to play GTA VI, anyway.
