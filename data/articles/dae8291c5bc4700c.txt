TITLE: ChatGPT ads start counting people as SHEIN pays $100m and fake TVs cost $135m
OUTLET: PPC Land

Wednesday, November 25, 2026, the eve of Thanksgiving in the United States, was a day of counting. OpenAI told ChatGPT advertisers that their reports can now estimate how many people saw an ad, not just how many times it appeared, and quietly added more than 2,000 internet addresses for the agent that fetches pages during conversations. The Wall Street Journal turned bots away by default, while a study found most big US retailers now welcome AI agents. Pixalate put a $135 million price on a streaming TV fraud built on fake televisions that vanish at midnight, LG opened its home screen ads to DoubleVerify, and X won a London judgment against two users it accused of farming its creator payouts. The FTC settled with SHEIN for $100 million over late parcels and unsupported claims, Vermont finalised rules that ban feeds built on children's data, and Germany named its enforcer for the EU Cyber Resilience Act. Amazon announced its car marketplace for the UK and pushed out a stack of changes for advertisers. YouTube, Disney, ESPN and Taboola adjusted how audiences watch and buy, and brands prepared for a Thanksgiving table reshaped by GLP-1 drugs and a 100th Macy's parade.

ChatGPT learns to count people
Until this week, ChatGPT's advertising reports counted impressions and clicks. Now they try to count people. PPC Land detailed how ChatGPT ad reports add 9 metrics built on how many people see the ads. In a "ChatGPT Ads Product Update" emailed to advertisers on November 25, OpenAI said Ads Manager and the Ads API now offer Reach, which estimates the unique people who saw an ad at least once, and Frequency, defined as impressions divided by Reach for the same period. Seven more were named but not defined: Click Reach, Click Frequency, Total Reach, Total Frequency, Unique CTR, Unique CPC and Cost per 1,000 Reached. The email does not say how OpenAI identifies a unique person, and its help pages had not listed the new metrics on the same day.
Search Engine Roundtable's Barry Schwartz, who published the email first, set out the other changes in ChatGPT ads new call-to-actions, business agents beyond the US and more audience metrics. Single image ads now show a "Learn more" link under the description by default, pointing to the landing page already set in Ads Manager, "so no additional setup is required," OpenAI wrote. Product feed advertisers can hide sale prices once a promotion ends, and rejected ads now get more detail on which policy applies. "The guidelines for approving ads haven't changed," OpenAI added. The scale behind these tweaks is no longer small: Ads Manager opened to all US businesses on May 5, 2026, and by October 21 ads were running in 89 countries, with more than 100,000 advertisers since launch.
OpenAI is also visiting more of the web. PPC Land found that OpenAI adds over 2,000 internet addresses ChatGPT uses to visit websites. The company expanded, without announcement, the public list of IP blocks for ChatGPT-User, the agent that fetches pages while people chat. Search consultancy Merj flagged the change, and PPC Land's own comparison counted 130 new blocks, or 2,080 addresses, with no removals; the file grew from 237 entries to 367. "I think something big is about to happen with OpenAI/ChatGPT," wrote Ryan Siddle, Merj's managing director, who compared it with Apple's expansion of Applebot addresses in August. Some commenters tied the timing to Black Friday, which OpenAI has not confirmed. Because OpenAI says robots.txt may not apply to ChatGPT-User, checking these addresses is one of the few ways a site can verify that a visit really comes from OpenAI.
Publishers are drawing their own lines. PPC Land reported that the Wall Street Journal tells all bots to keep out, except more than 60. Citing a Nieman Lab report, the article explained that Dow Jones changed its robots.txt files in May so that bots are refused by default across its consumer news sites, with only an approved list allowed in. The Journal's list runs to more than 60 bots, including search crawlers and three OpenAI scrapers; OpenAI's licensing deal with News Corp is reported at more than $250 million. "We're seeing astronomical growth in bot traffic while human referrals and traffic to news sites are being curtailed," said Nolly Evans, Dow Jones chief development officer. Bot analytics firm Known Agents found that only about 2% of 1,000 leading English-language publishers refuse all bots by default, and its index puts bots at about 34% of pageviews across more than 5,000 sites. Robots.txt is a request, not a lock, and Slate told Nieman Lab that some bots ignore it.
Retailers are moving the other way. PPC Land examined a study in which Amazon blocks 20 of 24 AI agents by name, the most of 79 top US retailers. Thrad, a company that sells ads inside AI chatbot conversations, reviewed the robots.txt files of 79 large US retailers. In 2026, 16 of them named at least one AI agent and granted it access, up from one in 2024, and named invitations rose from one to 63. Ulta Beauty led with 11, followed by Gap with eight. Counting retailers that leave access open by default, such as Walmart, Costco, Target and Home Depot, 72 of the 79 either invited agents or did not restrict them. Amazon stands apart. "They're responding by making their websites and product information more accessible to AI agents," said Thrad's chief executive Andrea Tortella of the open majority. The study does not name the 24 agents or measure whether any of them actually buy anything.
What those agents read when they answer shopping questions is changing too. PPC Land covered research showing that best-of lists climb to 65% of AI Black Friday toy links, iPullRank finds. iPullRank tracked 416 shopping prompts daily for two years using Profound data. In Black Friday week 2025, across ChatGPT and Google's three AI products, best-of lists rose from 46% to 65% of links in toy answers and from 62% to 78% for superstores. Brand websites gained nothing, and their share of toy links fell from 6% to 4%. ChatGPT is running about two weeks ahead this year: best-of lists were already 66% of its Black Friday links from November 1 to 10, against 38% a year earlier. "The data shows what appeared in AI answers. It can't tell us why each AI model made those choices," the study cautions. Mike King, iPullRank's founder, summed the result up as "earned media composed by a machine."
Fraud on the big screen
The cheapest way to steal from television advertisers is to invent the television. PPC Land reported that Pixalate estimates fake TVs cost advertisers $135 million in six months. Pixalate calls the scheme Mayfly, because its fake devices were invented each day and discarded at midnight UTC. It flagged 5.46 billion impressions from May to October and priced them at an assumed $25 CPM. Daily volume climbed from between 12 and 21 million impressions in the summer to 76 million in October, when the estimated cost reached $1.9 million a day. The scheme impersonated 3,277 apps, including Hulu, SYFY and The Roku Channel, across six TV platforms. Among impressions with supply chain data, 77% came from sellers not listed in the app's app-ads.txt file. In a test on one platform, only 0.7% of Mayfly device IDs survived past midnight, against 70.8% of genuine Fire TV IDs. "Genuine TVs update their software. The versions Mayfly reports stay fixed," the report notes. Pixalate names no exchange or operator, and the $25 price comes from a benchmark rather than actual buyer costs.
Television makers are inviting the auditors in. PPC Land reported that LG lets DoubleVerify check its TV home screen ads for fraud. LG Ad Solutions said on November 24 that advertisers using DoubleVerify can apply its fraud and invalid traffic checks to campaigns on LG smart TV home screens, including Menu ads, and claimed to be the first TV maker to offer this. "Advertisers are looking for more than campaign metrics. They want confidence in what's working," said Kelly McMahon of LG Ad Solutions. The release discloses no pricing, method, launch advertisers or home screen fraud rates, and it is unclear whether the checks cover inventory sold by LG's resellers. DoubleVerify itself reported a 140% rise in connected TV fraud schemes in the first quarter of 2026 and is being bought by Nielsen for about $2.15 billion.
A publisher-side vendor argues that even the best checks before an auction miss something. PPC Land covered how MonetizeMore says 10.8% of the traffic it checked was bots posing as people. Kean Graham, its founder, argued on LinkedIn that the fraud filters in AWS's new RTB Fabric modules see only bid request details, which suit simple invalid traffic. His company's tool measured that simple kind at 0.4% over 12 months, but sophisticated invalid traffic, meaning real browsers routed through residential proxies, at 10.8%. "A module in the bid path only sees the bid request," he wrote. "So as pre-bid filtering becomes table stakes, verification must move to the page to catch what slips through." The data is unaudited and comes from a company selling an on-page tool.
Supply path arguments come with their own vendor data. PPC Land reported that The Trade Desk says a direct connection lifted DAZN Media+ auction revenue 41%. DAZN Media+, which sells ads for Kayo Sports, BINGE and Foxtel in Australia, connected its streaming inventory directly to The Trade Desk through OpenPath and adopted UID2. Over roughly three and a half months after launch, compared with the same span before, biddable revenue rose 41%, fill rates were 16 times higher and win rates 2.9 times higher than through other paths. "That growth was not solely the result of spend shifting between supply paths," the case study says, without publishing the split. It arrives as The Trade Desk reported its first annual revenue decline since its 2016 listing.
Fraud also runs through creator payouts. PPC Land reported that X wins a UK judgment against two users it says took GBP207,384 in creator pay. The High Court in London entered a default judgment for X against Vivek Kumar Sen and Zamyang Sherpa after neither defended the claim. X alleges the pair ran six linked accounts in its Creator Revenue Sharing Programme between August 2023 and February 2026, manufacturing engagement through near-identical posts and mutual likes and reposts, and puts its own investigation costs at no less than GBP75,000. "We do not tolerate fraudulent behavior on X," said James Burnham of X. Nikita Bier, its head of product, praised the speed: "From a user flagging it to a judgement in 3 months." Damages await a later hearing.
Amazon's long list
Amazon sells almost everything online, and from next year it will help sell British cars. PPC Land reported that Amazon Autos will list Hyundai cars in the UK from early 2027, its first market outside the US. Shoppers will browse, compare prices and reserve cars on Amazon.co.uk and finish the purchase at a dealership. Hyundai is the launch partner with four models, and Vertu Motors will list used cars. The UK service will show upfront prices, an indicative part-exchange valuation and finance details. In the US, Amazon Autos has grown from 48 cities at launch in December 2024 to more than 150, with 35 brands. "Now we are taking the partnership beyond the U.S. for the first time," said Hyundai's chief executive José Muñoz, who said 78% of Amazon-influenced sales come from customers new to the brand. Neither that figure nor Amazon's own claims carry a published method, and no dealer fee was disclosed.
