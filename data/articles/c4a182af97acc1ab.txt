TITLE: 'The protection of human dignity is crucial': European bishops warn over human rights in Rwanda migrant plan
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

European bishops have voiced their concerns over human rights after European interior ministers announced plans to send migrants to Rwanda.
Interior ministers, the eqiuivalent of the home secretary in the UK government, from Germany, Greece, Austria, the Netherlands and Denmark announced an aim to open a "return hub" in the East African country to start operating next year.
Migrants whose asylum applications have failed, but whom cannot return to their country of origin, would be sent to Rwanda, which is where the former Conservative government also earmarked as a destination to send migrants.
However Rt Rev Bishop Stefan Ackermann, Bishop of Trier, has warned that he believes basic human rights will not be guaranteed for migrants expelled to Rwanda.
The German Bishops’ Conference (DBK) representative for Migration and Refugees also raised questions over whether “sending people to a country with which they have no connection whatsoever” was wise.
“[G]iven the challenging human rights situation and the poor conditions for refugees in Rwanda, a humane reception there is virtually impossible,” Bishop Stefan said in a statement published by the DBK last week.
Human Rights Watch reported that last year, the Rwandan government led by President Paul Kagame, repressed political opposition and linked the M23 militia to serious abuses.
It reported the example of the leader of unregistered opposition party Development and Liberty for All (DALFA-Umurinzi), Victoire Ingabire, who was arrested and charged with forming a criminal group.
The international watchdog organisation also reported in 2024 that religious grouops were subject to strict legal requirements to gain official status, and that smaller churches are systematically taregted.
Archbishop Marino Crociata, president of the Commission of Bishops’ Conferences in the European Union, echoed Bishop Stefan's warning. "The decision to outsource the accommodation of migrants to third countries leaves a worrying impression and appears to be a form of deportation," he added.
Rt Rev Gerard de Korte, Bishop of Hertogenbosch, based in the Netherlands, was less direct. “For our Church, the protection of human dignity is crucial,” he told Katholiek Nieuwsblad. “The entire project remains an experiment and must therefore be closely monitored.”
From 2022 to 2024, Boris Johnson, Liz Truss and Rishi Sunak's Conservative governments attempted to launch a policy of deporting migrants to Rwanda, which re-elected Presaident Paul Kagame with 99% of the vote in 2024 in an election not deemed free or fair.
The so-called "Rwanda Plan" was unanimously ruled unlawful by the UK Supreme Court in 2023 over concerns of human rights violations by Rwanda.
