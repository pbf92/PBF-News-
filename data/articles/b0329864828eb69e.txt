E-Scooter Brain Injuries Last Months for Many Riders
A UW Medicine study of Harborview patients links e-scooter crashes to long-lasting brain injury symptoms and lost work.
Updated on Nov. 24, 2026 in Health

UW Medicine researchers reviewed 305 patients treated for brain injury symptoms after e-scooter crashes at Harborview Medical Center, surveying 64 of them by phone. Of those surveyed, 84% reported at least one brain injury symptom during recovery, and the study was published November 17 in JAMA Network Open.
Why it matters
The findings suggest e-scooter crashes can cause injuries whose effects extend well beyond the emergency visit, affecting work and daily life for months. For Seattle, where the study was conducted, the results add evidence to the case for helmet use and safer riding habits.
The study reviewed 305 patients from July 2020 through December 2024, and 64 were surveyed by phone. Respondents with CT-visible injuries reported symptoms lasting a median of 11 months, while 17% reported an injury-related job change or loss.
The players
Harborview Medical Center
Harborview Medical Center is a Level I trauma center in Seattle operated by King County and affiliated with the University of Washington.
UW Medicine
UW Medicine is the academic health system of the University of Washington, which includes hospitals, clinics and a major medical research enterprise.
JAMA Network Open
JAMA Network Open is an open-access, peer-reviewed medical journal published by the American Medical Association.
The details
Researchers at the University of Washington School of Medicine reviewed hospital records of patients treated at Harborview Medical Center and conducted telephone surveys with patients, most more than a year after their crashes. Nearly 70% of respondents said they had been drinking alcohol before crashing, and only five of the 64 surveyed were wearing a helmet.
Timeline
- July 2020: Case review period begins at Harborview Medical Center.
- December 2024: Case review period ends.
- November 17, 2026: Study published in JAMA Network Open.
Health Landscape
The growth of shared e-scooter programs in U.S. cities since around 2017 has brought a new category of head injuries into emergency departments. This study adds longer-term recovery data to the early wave of trauma reports from those crashes.
Riders who experience headaches, dizziness or memory changes after a crash may have symptoms that last for months, even without a visible scan injury. Wearing a helmet and avoiding alcohol before riding are the practical precautions the study's findings point toward.
The takeaway
Symptoms from e-scooter brain injuries can persist far longer than many riders expect, so any head impact warrants medical evaluation. Helmet use and sober riding remain the clearest steps to lower risk.
