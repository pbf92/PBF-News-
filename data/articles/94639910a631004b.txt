TITLE: Anthropic IPO: Claude Maker Opens at $111.50 in Record Nasdaq Debut
OUTLET: TechRepublic

Anthropic’s first Nasdaq trade put the maker of Claude well above its IPO price. The gain didn’t hold at that level for long.
The AI company began trading Thursday, Nov. 19, under the ticker ANT. Its Class A shares opened at $111.50, about 34% above the $83 offering price, in what The Associated Press called the largest public stock offering on record. By around 1:20 p.m. ET, shares had slipped to $94.60, still about 14% above the offering price, according to Business Insider’s live coverage. That was an intraday reading, not the closing price.
For businesses already using Claude, the debut also puts a new set of public disclosures behind a vendor they may depend on.
The numbers behind Anthropic’s debut
The $83 IPO price was set before the shares began trading. The $111.50 opening trade was the price at which shares first changed hands on Nasdaq. Neither figure, on its own, tells you how much cash the company expects to receive.
According to Anthropic’s final prospectus, the company is selling about 956.4 million of those shares. Existing stockholders are selling the remaining 23.6 million shares, and Anthropic won’t receive the proceeds from their sale.
A planned $26 billion private placement is separate from the public offering. The filing names seven buyers, including Google, Nvidia, AMD, and Microsoft. Those companies are slated to buy a different class of stock at the same $83 per-share price, subject to closing conditions.
Anthropic estimates it will receive approximately $104.8 billion in combined net proceeds from the two transactions, after underwriting discounts, commissions, and estimated offering expenses, assuming underwriters don’t exercise their option to buy more shares.
The pricing announcement said the offering was expected to close Nov. 20, subject to customary conditions. That settlement date is separate from both the first day of trading and Thursday’s closing stock price.
What the filing tells Claude customers
The prospectus puts some scale behind the business that sells Claude models and tools.
Anthropic reported $17 billion in revenue for the three months ended Sept. 30, up from $1.4 billion in the same quarter a year earlier. The company also says it derives all of its revenue substantially from a limited number of products and applications, primarily its Claude models and the apps built on its Claude Platform.
Those disclosures offer a different way to evaluate a vendor than looking at model benchmarks alone. If your team is comparing Claude with GPT-6, you can now track the business behind the model alongside its performance on your own workloads.
The first trade doesn’t tell an IT team whether its cloud provider will support a particular model, how much usage will cost, or what support is available if a service goes down. Those answers still come from product documentation and contracts. That distinction matters most when Claude is already embedded in a process, such as reviewing formulas in Excel, where vendor performance and human review are separate questions.
The next milestone for the market is the regular-session close.
For IT buyers, it’s the next filing: Look for changes in spending, customer concentration, and the risks Anthropic says could affect its ability to deliver. The stock’s first day tells you how investors responded. The disclosures give you something you can use in your next vendor review.
