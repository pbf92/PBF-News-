What's Going on with MercadoLibre Shares on Friday?
MercadoLibre, Inc. MELI shares are trading higher on Friday, even as a tariff- and inflation-driven sell-off hit the broader market, led by tech and AI names. The Nasdaq fell sharply.
Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva appointed Bradesco Chairman Luiz Carlos Trabuco as finance minister, effective January 2027. The move could ease investor concerns about relations between the government and financial markets ahead of Lula’s fourth term.
Trabuco, who leads Brazil’s second-largest private lender, agreed to take over the finance ministry when Lula begins his new term next year, Reuters reported.

Debt and Fiscal Challenges
Trabuco will need to reassure investors that Lula’s spending commitments can be balanced with efforts to contain rising public debt, which has reached nearly 83% of GDP, an increase of approximately 11 percentage points since 2023.
Lula also wants to create conditions for the central bank to lower its benchmark interest rate, currently at 13.75%.
On Nov. 11, the government announced fiscal measures targeting savings of approximately 0.5% of GDP in 2027 while maintaining social program spending.
Economic Outlook
Analysts say additional fiscal measures are needed to stabilize public debt and reduce borrowing costs.
Economists expect Brazil’s GDP to grow just 1% in 2027, while inflation is forecast at 5%, above the government’s official 3% target, according to Reuters.
Recent Earnings Snapshot
This month, MercadoLibre reported mixed third-quarter results, as higher shipping costs and marketing spending pressured margins.
Revenue rose to $10.75 billion, beating Wall Street’s estimate of $10.66 billion. However, earnings of $8.83 per share missed analysts’ estimate of $9.27.
Brazil’s gross merchandise volume rose 36%, while items per buyer increased 16%. Lower free-shipping thresholds helped boost shopping frequency.
