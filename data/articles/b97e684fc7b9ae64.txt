TITLE: Anthropic Stock Crashes 36% From Record High as Market Cap Falls Below $1 Trillion
OUTLET: Coinpaper

Key takeaways
- Anthropic stock falls 36% from its debut-day high
- ANT briefly loses its trillion-dollar market capitalization
- Infrastructure commitments and upcoming share unlocks remain risks
Anthropic (NASDAQ: ANT) shares plunged to an intraday low of $73.85 on Wednesday, November 25, extending their decline from last week's record high of $115.10 to approximately 36%.
The selloff briefly pushed the Claude developer's market capitalization below $1 trillion, less than a week after its historic Nasdaq debut. At its session low, ANT traded 11% below its $83 initial public offering price.
The latest decline coincided with renewed weakness across AI stocks, with semiconductor manufacturers and cloud infrastructure providers facing mounting selling pressure.
Anthropic Stock Erases Its Entire IPO Premium
According to Anthropic's official announcement, its initial public offering generated approximately $93.5 billion in gross proceeds, including shares purchased through the underwriters' additional allocation. Approximately $91.6 billion went to the company before offering expenses.
Anthropic opened at $111.50 on November 19 before reaching $115.10, representing a premium of nearly 39% over its offering price.
However, shares have struggled to retain those gains. ANT closed its first trading session at $97.17, followed by $88.91 on Friday, $87.64 on Monday and $81.91 on Tuesday.
Wednesday's intraday low represents a loss of approximately $41 per share for investors who purchased ANT at its debut-day peak.
Unlike the capital generated through Anthropic's IPO, the decline in its market capitalization does not directly reduce the cash available to the company.
Nvidia, Google and Microsoft Investments Fall Below Entry Price
Anthropic's public offering was accompanied by a separate $26 billion private placement involving seven major technology companies: Nvidia, Google, Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, Micron and TSMC.
The companies collectively purchased approximately 313.3 million Class N shares at $83 each, matching the public offering price.
At Wednesday's intraday low, those shares had an implied value of approximately $23.14 billion using ANT's publicly traded share price, representing a theoretical decline of $2.87 billion.
However, this does not constitute a realized investment loss, and the privately placed shares are subject to different trading restrictions.
Anthropic's underperformance has become particularly noticeable since Monday, when the broader technology sector staged a substantial recovery that failed to lift ANT.
Anthropic Faces $901 Billion in Spending Commitments
Beyond immediate market volatility, investors must assess the financial implications of Anthropic's long-term computing obligations.
The company's final SEC prospectus disclosed approximately $900.9 billion in spending commitments, primarily related to computing infrastructure, extending through 2045.
These commitments are not equivalent to outstanding debt or immediately payable expenses. Nevertheless, they illustrate the scale of revenue growth required to support Anthropic's infrastructure expansion.
Another potential catalyst is the December 15 share unlock, when up to 303 million shares could become eligible for public sale.
Meanwhile, the broader AI sector correction continues to challenge valuations across the industry's largest companies.
