I have read several letters that chastise BAR for not supporting literal biblical interpretation. This is a misunderstanding of archaeology and biblical archaeology in particular. Archaeology is a social science that uses the techniques of exact sciences and historical disciplines to uncover facts and provide an independent view of the artifacts and peoples found in the Bible. This independence provides an additional source of information concerning biblical peoples, structures, and culture.
People develop an understanding of religion through faith and the text of the Bible, as well as secular writings and artifacts. It is logically dangerous to assume a strict view of the Bible as a simple revealed literal truth. The Bible uses many literary devices, including history, biography, metaphor, parable, health guidance, heroic stories and legends, and spiritual precepts.
Thus, it is not necessary, nor is it ultimately the best way of understanding the biblical message, to assume complete agreement among differing approaches to that understanding.
Allen R. Stata
Marion, Alabama

Biblical Ataroth
The article “Sacred Center: The Iron Age Temple at Biblical Ataroth,” by Chang-Ho Ji and Aaron Schade, details the Ataruz temple complex. Both the aerial photo and the artist’s reconstruction of the site show the complex surrounded by bare hills, with the Dead Sea in the distance. The article also says that no houses or domestic features were found. So, where is Ataroth? Did King Mesha obliterate it?
Kennedy Gammage
San Diego, California
Chang-ho Ji And Aaron Schade respond:
Given the substantial archaeological evidence from the site, Ataruz is likely biblical Ataroth, which King Mesha claims to have destroyed. When we stated that “no houses or domestic features have been found,” we meant that all buildings excavated thus far appear to have been part of the temple complex. In other words, we have not yet identified any buildings or architectural features from this period that can be clearly interpreted as only residential or domestic structures.
Editor’s Note: The article “Sacred Center” described the Ataruz temple as facing southeast, “toward the rising sun on the summer solstice.” As noted by several readers, this is not the direction of the rising sun on the summer solstice, when the sun rises in the east-northeast. The authors acknowledge the error, noting further that the solar and seasonal alignments of the temple are still under study.
Remembering Gabby
I was saddened by the passing of archaeologist Gabriel Barkay (Strata, “Milestones”). I got to know Gabby by watching him in Simcha Jacobovici’s Naked Archaeologist series. The insight Gabby shared was invaluable to learning and understanding biblical archaeology, especially in Jerusalem. When Simcha visited him at the Temple Mount Sifting Project, Gabby told him that all the material the project was sifting was archaeologically valuable; his perseverance proved him right. He will be missed.
Frank Nocco
Riverview, Florida
Why So Many Churches?
In his column, author Jacob Ashkenazi asks “Why Did Early Christians Build So Many Churches?” I can think of two reasons. First, large churches that served entire communities were very expensive to build back then; they didn’t have steel and the cost of building arches to support expansive roofs was prohibitive. Second is proximity. Since there was no public transportation, churches had to be accessible by foot, which meant that each town needed several churches.
Levente Joseph Orth
Greenwood, Missouri
Was Mary a Second Wife?
The column “Was Mary a Second Wife?” by Helen Bond was intriguing and thought provoking.
Something Jesus said from the cross may be an indication that Joseph had children from a previous marriage: “He said to His mother, ‘Woman, behold your son!’ Then He said to the disciple, ‘Behold your mother!’ And from that hour that disciple took her to his own home” (John 19:25–27, NKJV). Did Jesus say this because he preferred that John (rather than one of his own brothers) take care of her, or because he was the only son of Mary and didn’t want her to be left alone? I’m beginning to think it was the latter.
