TITLE: Meta faces legal action over fake casino ads targeting French consumers

Fraudulent adverts promoting illegal online casinos are at the centre of a legal dispute in France, with gambling operator FDJ United accusing Meta of failing to prevent its platforms from being used to mislead consumers.
The company, which operates national lotteries in France and Ireland, claims its branding has been copied in social media promotions designed to direct users towards unauthorised gambling websites.
Some advertisements allegedly imitate FDJ retail outlets or use images resembling the front pages of established newspapers, creating the impression that the gambling services being promoted are legitimate.
The allegations have prompted FDJ to launch summary legal proceedings against Meta, the parent company of Facebook and Instagram, seeking action against the offending advertisements.
The dispute is particularly significant in France, where online casino gambling is prohibited, although licensed operators can legally offer certain other forms of online gambling.
FDJ claims the advertisements expose consumers to unregulated gambling websites without the protections required of authorised operators.
FDJ raises concerns over fraudulent casino advertising
The company said it had repeatedly raised concerns with Meta but had not received adequate assurances that the advertisements would be identified and removed.
Between 18 and 21 May 2026, FDJ reported 226 new instances of allegedly illegal content appearing across Meta's platforms. Some closely resembled advertisements the company had already brought to Meta's attention.
The scale of the problem was outlined in reporting by iGaming Business, which detailed FDJ's decision to pursue legal action following unsuccessful discussions with the technology company.
FDJ chairwoman and chief executive Stéphane Pallez also addressed the issue in an opinion article published by French newspaper Les Echos earlier this week.
Pallez warned that fraudulent gambling promotions could expose users to risks beyond losing money through gambling, including the possibility of withheld winnings, compromised bank accounts and stolen personal information.
She also raised concerns about the potential exposure of minors to illegal gambling advertisements on social media, where unauthorised operators do not face the same regulatory requirements as licensed casinos.
The FDJ chief argued that the consequences extend beyond individual consumers, with illegal gambling also creating wider social and security concerns.
Pressure grows on Meta over illegal gambling promotions
The French legal challenge comes amid growing scrutiny of how Meta handles gambling advertising elsewhere in Europe.
In the Netherlands, gambling industry association VNLOK has also pursued legal action against the company, alleging that unauthorised gambling operators have been able to advertise extensively on its platforms.
The Dutch association previously claimed that Meta removed just 11% of the illegal gambling advertisements it reported in May 2026, with the figure increasing to 15% in June.
The separate disputes raise questions about the effectiveness of Meta's advertising controls and the responsibilities of social media companies when potentially unlawful promotions are brought to their attention.
They also highlight the difficulties facing licensed gambling operators and national regulators when attempting to tackle advertising campaigns that can be distributed across multiple countries.
Illegal gambling poses wider challenge across Europe
The dispute comes against a backdrop of concern about the size of Europe's illegal online gambling market.
Pallez estimates that more than 80 million people across Europe encounter offers from unauthorised gambling operators each year.
She also cited estimates suggesting that illegal gambling deprived European governments of more than €20 billion in tax revenue in 2025.
FDJ has contrasted those figures with the economic contribution of regulated gambling businesses, reporting that it contributed €5.1 billion to French public finances in 2025 and supported 57,200 jobs.
The company believes tackling illegal gambling requires cooperation beyond the gambling industry itself.
It has called for technology companies, financial institutions, payment providers, sports organisations and groups working to protect young people to coordinate efforts against unauthorised operators.
FDJ has also announced a public awareness campaign intended to help consumers recognise the importance of using legal gambling services.
Its legal challenge against Meta will now test whether the company can secure stronger measures to prevent fraudulent advertisements from appearing on its social media platforms.
The outcome could have implications beyond France as gambling businesses and regulators across Europe seek more effective ways to restrict the online promotion of illegal casino websites.
