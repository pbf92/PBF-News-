TITLE: Microsoft jabs at OpenAI and Anthropic, touting cheaper, customizable AI models of its own
OUTLET: GeekWire

Microsoft is a partner and investor in both OpenAI and Anthropic, and it sells their AI models to its own customers. But another role was on full display during the company’s Ignite keynote Tuesday: competitor.
“Simply stated, anything you can do in Claude or ChatGPT, you can do better with Copilot and for less,” said Judson Althoff, CEO of Microsoft’s commercial business, during the opening keynote at the company’s annual conference for IT professionals and business customers in San Francisco.
Jacob Andreou, Microsoft’s executive vice president of Copilot, went further. “We’ve all seen Anthropic and OpenAI embrace documents, slides, and spreadsheets, but their simplified viewers and editors force compromise on power and collaboration,” he said.
The full versions of Word, Excel and PowerPoint inside Copilot are included in the subscription. “That means that Copilot is the only way to draft a PowerPoint presentation without spinning a meter,” Andreou said.
Microsoft’s cost comparison: Andreou backed up the pitch with a chart of estimated monthly costs per business user, including subscription and usage fees. It put Copilot at $30 for an “everyday” AI user, compared with $81 for ChatGPT and $205 for Claude.
For “advanced” users of each company’s agent tools, it showed $96 for Copilot, $149 for ChatGPT and $299 for Claude. Most of the Claude figure is estimated usage charges on top of Anthropic’s $20 enterprise seat.
The numbers come from research Microsoft commissioned and paid for from GigaOm, based on testing Microsoft ran in October on modeled workloads. They reflect enterprise pricing, which adds usage charges to a per-seat fee, not the flat monthly subscriptions that individuals pay for ChatGPT and Claude.
Partners and rivals: The pitch comes as Anthropic expands into Microsoft’s core business. Last week it introduced new Claude-based tools for spreadsheets and whiteboards that compete more directly with Office. The company is expected to make its IPO this week, putting new scrutiny on its financials and long-term growth prospects.
The companies’ products are also intertwined. Anthropic’s Claude for Microsoft 365, for example, lets people use Claude inside Office apps for tasks such as sorting email and creating slide decks.
The product has less than a tenth as many weekly users as the paid version of Copilot in Office, The Information reported Monday, citing internal Microsoft data. Andreou is one of the executives who see that as a sign Microsoft can keep customers from switching, the report said.
New MAI models: Microsoft AI also released a new batch of its own MAI models, built to compete with OpenAI and Anthropic for a fraction of the price. It’s a notable stance, given that Microsoft still sells their models, too. On the same stage, CEO Satya Nadella announced that OpenAI’s GPT-6.2, released Tuesday morning, is now available in Microsoft Foundry.
The lineup includes MAI Code 2, available in GitHub Copilot starting Tuesday. Microsoft says it delivers 96% of the coding performance of Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5 at 5% of the price, on benchmarks Microsoft chose.
Microsoft also introduced MAI Cyber 2 Flash, a security model it says matches OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra on one benchmark at less than 2% of the cost per task. Other new models cover image editing, live speech and transcription.
The other new business: MAI Licensed Weights. Companies can take a Microsoft AI model, customize it with their own data and run it in their own Azure environment, with help from Microsoft’s Frontier Company consulting arm. AT&T used an earlier version of the program, Custom Models, to build a MAI model for 5G network analytics.
It’s the latest step in Microsoft’s push for what Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman has called “long term self-sufficiency,” which started with seven homegrown models at Build in June.
Keeping costs in check: The cost theme ran through the keynote. In a demo, Althoff showed Copilot building a keynote outline, a slide deck and a customer tracker for a retail conference, plus an agent he named Amelia to keep the work going.
The cost of his two-hour Sunday session: about 10,000 credits, or roughly $100. Althoff presented that as a bargain compared with the staff time it would have taken: “my team hits the ground running for NRF without me bothering them,” he said.
Microsoft is also selling tools to keep that bill in check. A new Agent 365 Business Analytics feature shows business leaders what’s driving their AI spending and what they’re getting for it, and a companion Cost Management tool lets IT set spending policies and get alerts. Microsoft cited a McKinsey survey in which 93% of respondents reported exceeding their AI budgets.
In another notable announcement, Microsoft and Salesforce unveiled “Copilotforce,” bringing Salesforce customer data and actions into Copilot, Teams and Fabric. The Copilot plugin is available in preview.
Earlier, Nadella opened the keynote by addressing last week’s global Outlook outage.
