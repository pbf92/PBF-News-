TITLE: Goldman Sachs warns US stock selloff could worsen next week
OUTLET: TradingView

Goldman Sachs warns US stock selloff could worsen next week
Goldman Sachs says the pain in US stocks may not be finished. The bank warned that the selloff could deepen next week, after its proprietary Panic Index climbed to 9 out of 10 on November 20, 2026.
Crypto-linked equities are absorbing some of the damage. Goldman's bitcoin-sensitive stock basket has dropped 11%, outpacing several of the bank's other thematic trackers.
The Nasdaq 100 fell 5.6%, its worst weekly showing since March 2020.
Semiconductors fared even worse. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 11%, matching the drop in Goldman's bitcoin-sensitive basket.
Nvidia has slid nearly 50% from its early-October peak.
Goldman's other thematic baskets tell a similar story. Its retail-favorites index declined 8.9%, while its cyclical-versus-defensive basket slipped 3.2%.
The core of Goldman's warning centers on commodity trading advisors, or CTAs. These are systematic funds that follow price trends: when markets go up, they buy, and when markets go down, they sell.
Over the past month, CTAs sold $57 billion in US equities. That selling flipped them to a $19 billion net short position, meaning they are now positioned to profit if stocks fall further.
Goldman's models suggest these funds could unload another $28.6 billion to $80.6 billion, depending on where markets head.
Goldman also pointed to momentum traders as a primary driver of the recent decline. Both institutional and retail investors have been shedding risk assets.
Two threads run through the current downturn. The first is growing skepticism about whether the enormous sums being poured into AI infrastructure will produce returns that justify the spending.
The second thread is the lack of a fresh catalyst. The much-anticipated Anthropic IPO did not deliver the risk-on boost some investors may have hoped for.
For crypto investors, the 11% drop in Goldman's bitcoin-sensitive basket is the clearest signal. These are publicly traded companies whose fortunes tend to move with Bitcoin, and they are falling alongside the broader tech complex.
The weakness in the retail-favorites basket adds to that picture. Retail-preferred assets and crypto-related equities often share an investor base, and both are being sold as risk appetite fades.
What to watch next week comes down to a few markers. Whether the Nasdaq 100 can find a floor after its worst week since March 2020 will shape how much additional selling CTAs carry out.
Nvidia's trajectory is another key indicator. As the most prominent AI stock, its near-50% slide from the October peak has become shorthand for broader doubts about the sector.
With the Anthropic IPO failing to spark a rally, the market currently lacks an obvious reason to reverse course, and Goldman's models suggest that, absent one, systematic sellers could keep pressing.
