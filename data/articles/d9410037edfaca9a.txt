🟢 Overview
The Volume Clock Trend is a trend-following indicator that measures market time in traded volume rather than in bars, so its clock line moves further on heavily traded bars and barely moves on quiet ones. Trend changes are confirmed only after enough volume has traded with price held beyond an ATR-based acceptance band around the line, which lets traders follow trends backed by participation while low-volume drifts and thinly traded breakouts carry less weight in both the line and the signals.
🟢 How It Works
The indicator is built around a volume clock, which lets the amount traded, not the number of bars, set how fast time passes. The idea goes back to Peter Clark, who proposed in Econometrica in 1973 that the clock driving price changes is cumulative trading volume, and Easley, López de Prado, and O'Hara later characterized high-frequency trading in The Journal of Portfolio Management in 2012 by strategic decisions made on a volume clock. On a chart, this means a session open where many times the usual volume changes hands counts for more time than a quiet overnight bar, while a standard moving average treats both the same. The calculation runs in three stages: the volume baseline and clock speed, the clock line, and the state engine that confirms trend changes.
First, the baseline and each bar's clock speed are set. The baseline is an exponentially weighted average of volume over the Volume Baseline span, built from the bars before the current one and skipping bars that trade no volume, so each bar is measured against the participation that preceded it. A bar's clock speed is its volume divided by that baseline, capped at the Participation Cap so isolated spikes such as session opens or liquidation bursts cannot dominate. On symbols without volume data, such as many indices, every bar runs at a speed of one, so the indicator still works there as a time-based trend follower without the volume weighting, and participation pulses do not appear.
Next, the clock line is built. It is an exponential average of the source whose step on each bar depends on that bar's clock speed, with Clock Length setting its time constant in bars of baseline volume. Because the decay runs in volume time, a bar that trades twice the baseline moves the line as far as two average bars, a bar at half the baseline moves it half as far, and a bar that trades no volume leaves it in place. Heavy participation therefore pulls the line toward price quickly, while quiet drifts barely move it.
Finally, the state engine confirms trend changes. An acceptance band is set at a multiple of the 14-bar Average True Range on each side of the clock line. While consecutive bars close with the source beyond the same edge, their clock speeds add up, so the count measures how much volume, in bars of baseline volume, changed hands while price stayed away from the line, and a close back inside the band or beyond the opposite edge restarts it. A bullish trend is confirmed once the count above the band reaches the Acceptance Volume, and a bearish trend once the count below it does, so a breakout on heavy volume can confirm within a single bar while a slow drift on thin volume needs many more. The first confirmation in either direction ends the neutral warm-up state. Trend changes are evaluated only on closed bars, and each one stays in place once its bar closes.
🟢 Signal Interpretation
▶ Bullish Trend (Clock Line in Bullish Color): When a bullish trend is confirmed, the clock line, glow, and acceptance band take on the bullish color, and a trend change point marks the bar where the line switches, showing that buyers held price above the band while enough participation built up for the push to count as accepted rather than thin. It suggests a long bias that holds through pullbacks until enough volume trades with price held below the lower edge to confirm a bearish trend.
▶ Bearish Trend (Clock Line in Bearish Color): When a bearish trend is confirmed, the line, glow, and band turn to the bearish color, with a trend change point at the switch, indicating that sellers have kept price below the band while enough volume changed hands to establish acceptance at lower prices. It suggests a short bias that holds through bounces until participation builds above the upper edge enough to confirm a bullish trend.
▶ Participation Pulses and Glow: Candles whose volume reaches the Pulse Threshold, measured as a multiple of the baseline before the Participation Cap applies, are colored as participation pulses, in the Pulse Up Color when they close at or above their open and the Pulse Down Color when they close below it, while the glow beneath the line brightens on bars that advance the clock quickly and dims on quiet ones. Pulses near a trend change show that heavy bars carried the confirmation, while a change confirmed without them came from more moderate participation, and on the live bar a pulse colors the candle as soon as the volume traded so far reaches the threshold, with its color following the candle's direction until the bar closes.
🟢 Features
▶ Preconfigured Presets: Three parameter sets for different trading styles and timeframes. Default is balanced for swing trading on 1H to daily charts and uses the Clock Length, Volume Baseline, Acceptance Band, and Acceptance Volume set in the inputs, so each can be adjusted freely. Fast Response suits intraday charts from 5 minutes to 1 hour, with a shorter clock and a lower acceptance volume, so the line follows price more closely and trends confirm on lighter volume. Smooth Trend suits position trading on daily and weekly charts, with a longer clock and baseline, a narrower band, and a higher acceptance volume, so trends confirm only after sustained participation beyond the line. The Participation Cap applies under every preset.
▶ Built-in Alerts: Three alert conditions support hands-off monitoring. Bullish Trend Signal fires on the closed bar that confirms a bullish trend. Bearish Trend Signal fires when a closed bar confirms a bearish trend. Any Trend Change fires on either. Alert messages include exchange, ticker, and timeframe, and the Any alert() function call option also sends the volume accepted beyond the band, the level of the clock line, and the band edge that price closed beyond.
▶ Visual Customization: Six color presets (Classic, Aqua, Cosmic, Cyber, Neon, and Custom) apply coordinated bullish and bearish colors to the clock line, glow, acceptance band, trend change points, and optional bar and background tints, with a neutral color during warm-up, while participation pulses keep their own up and down colors under every preset so they stay visible even when bar coloring is on. Every color adapts automatically to light and dark chart backgrounds, and the custom bullish, bearish, and neutral pickers apply only when Custom is selected. The glow, acceptance band, pulses, trend change points, and bar and background coloring can each be toggled, with adjustable transparency for the bar and background tints.
