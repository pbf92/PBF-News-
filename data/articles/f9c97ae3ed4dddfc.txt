TITLE: Report: Manchester United ownership rift deepens over club’s future
OUTLET: Sports Business Journal

Manchester United’s owners have seen “rifts” stemming from “competing egos, poor commercial decisions, a failed recruitment strategy -- on and off the pitch,” according to sources cited by Turner & Hellier of BLOOMBERG NEWS. The club has not made a profit since the 2018-19 season, “casting doubt on its characterization” by co-Chair Avram Glazer of it as a commercial unit. Revenue from broadcast, sponsorship and matchday income has “continued to grow but crucially not at the same rate as United’s main rivals in England and Europe.” On Thursday, shares in the club dropped more than 7% after publication of “underwhelming first quarter results that revealed more net losses.” Sources added that the Glazer family, “split over whether to keep or sell Manchester United, is once again at loggerheads over the future of their ownership.” Sources mentioned that the unexpected summer purchase of a stake in rival Liverpool by a Jeff Bezos-led consortium “caught several members of the Glazer family off guard, leaving some members believing they had missed an opportunity to cash out.” Even the appointment of the new coach “generated media reports of tension” between the Glazers and minority owner Jim Ratcliffe. Avram and Joel Glazer recommended coach Mauricio Pochettino. The club said that the hiring “was a unanimous decision among its owners.” Sources added that the intervention of the Glazer brothers in club recruitment “is unusual, given Ratcliffe’s official control of the football side of the business,” and “comes at a time when some of the Glazer siblings have been pushing each other to sell more of the club” they value at about $6B (BLOOMBERG NEWS, 11/20).
