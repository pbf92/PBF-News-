Anthropic Cuts Vulnerability Disclosure Window From 90 Days to 30 as AI-Accelerated Discovery Undermines Old Assumptions
Anthropic announced on November 16 that it has revised its coordinated vulnerability disclosure (CVD) policy, sharply shortening the grace period before disclosure. Vulnerabilities classified as "high" or "critical" severity will move from the previous 90 days to 30 days, while all others will be set at 45 days. The policy applies to cases reported to vendors or open-source software (OSS) maintainers on or after January 1, 2027, while reports already filed and under active remediation will keep the existing deadlines.
Anthropic cites the rapid improvement in AI's ability to find vulnerabilities as the rationale for the change. Traditional disclosure rules were designed on the assumption that remediation work proceeds at human speed and that the same flaw is unlikely to be rediscovered through separate paths. According to the company, neither assumption holds today. Models can now assist in writing fix code, and flaws found by one model are detected with high probability by another.
The revision includes several changes beyond the shortened deadlines. The threshold for deciding to hand a case to an external vulnerability coordinator when a maintainer does not respond to the initial report moves up from 30 days to 15 days. The waiting period before full technical details are shared after a patch is released is also compressed from 45 days to 30 days.
The company also clarified how existing cases will be handled. For "high" and "critical" cases that remain undisclosed even after 90 days, maintainers will be given an additional 30-day grace period, after which the cases will be published on the dashboard under the new policy starting January 1, 2027.
Note: Applies to cases reported on or after January 1, 2027. Reports already in progress keep the previous deadlines.
Exceptions were retained and expanded. The policy of disclosing vulnerabilities with confirmed real-world exploitation within 7 days stays in place. For software that is difficult to fix quickly, such as critical infrastructure, or for defects that require specification or RFC changes, deadlines will be adjusted individually with vendors. In addition, AI-discovered results that have not undergone human review (such as those from OSS Scanner) are excluded from the new rules, avoiding the risk that false positives lead directly to publication.
Measures to ease the burden on maintainers were also included. Each report will come with a remediation guide, and patches or mitigations will be prepared where possible. The company also signaled a willingness to discuss a sustainable pace with maintainers before sending a large volume of reports to a single project.
On the support side, Anthropic is offering "OSS Scanner," a free service that regularly scans code with the company's most powerful models and delivers the results to maintainers of important open-source projects. Eligible maintainers and contributors can apply for the "Claude for Open Source Program," which provides six months of free access to Claude Ultra. For security professionals, including OSS maintainers, the company is also establishing a "Cyber Verification Program" that adapts Claude's safety measures for defensive security work.
The announcements confront the industry with the reality that AI is taking the lead in security research. As the time from discovery to exploitation shrinks, a 90-day grace period is too long for defenders, while compressing it to 30 days places a heavy burden on the maintenance setups of small open-source projects. As reporting becomes more automated, there are concerns that projects with fewer hands will struggle to keep up, and that more cases will be published without a fix. Anthropic's combination of free tools and human support appears aimed at balancing faster disclosure with the sustainability of the ecosystem. For corporate development teams, the need is growing to shift vulnerability handling for dependent libraries from a quarterly cadence to a monthly operation.
