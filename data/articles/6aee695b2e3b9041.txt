TITLE: Wall St futures fall after S&P 500, Nasdaq end lower; inflation data eyed
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

Investing.com-- U.S. stock futures inched lower on Tuesday evening after the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite slipped during the regular session on tech losses, while investors awaited key inflation data for clues on the Federal Reserve's interest-rate outlook.
S&P 500 Futures inched 0.3% lower to 7,193.50 points, while Nasdaq 100 Futures fell 0.4% to 26,168.75 points by 19:58 ET (00:58 GMT). Dow Jones Futures traded 0.3% lower at 49,930.0 points.
The weakness in futures reflected continued caution around richly valued technology and artificial intelligence-related stocks, which have been a major driver of Wall Street's gains but have also become increasingly vulnerable to profit-taking amid concerns over elevated valuations and the sustainability of AI-related spending.
The pullback followed Monday's spectacular rally, which had been fuelled by Nvidia's (NASDAQ:NVDA) reinstatement of Jensen Huang as chief executive. Nvidia had surged 31.5% on Monday, adding nearly $1 trillion to its market capitalisation, but reversed course sharply on Tuesday as investors locked in profits.
The NASDAQ Composite dropped 1.5%, and the S&P 500 fell 0.5%. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, however, advanced 0.9%, highlighting a rotation away from technology stocks towards more traditional and defensive sectors.
Nvidia shares fell nearly 6%, while memory-chip maker Micron Technology and Advanced Micro Devices also suffered substantial losses.
Newly listed artificial intelligence company Anthropic slid below its $83 initial public offering price, adding to concerns about investor appetite for high-valued AI businesses. The reversal came after the technology-heavy Nasdaq had rallied nearly 7% on Monday, its strongest daily gain in several years.
Attention now turns to Wednesday's personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index, the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge and the last major inflation reading before its December policy meeting.
The data will be accompanied by preliminary third-quarter gross domestic product figures, weekly jobless claims, durable goods orders and personal income and spending figures.
