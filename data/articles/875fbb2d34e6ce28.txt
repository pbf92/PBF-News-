TITLE: Did Arm CEO Rene Haas Dodge a Bullet With Jensen Huang’s Reinstatement at Nvidia?
OUTLET: 24/7 Wall St.

Did Arm CEO Rene Haas Dodge a Bullet With Jensen Huang’s Reinstatement at Nvidia?

Which looks stronger for a retirement-focused investor right now: Arm Holdings (NASDAQ:ARM | ARM Price Prediction) or NVIDIA (NASDAQ:NVDA)? The question got sharper this month. Arm CEO Rene Haas was reportedly considered for NVIDIA’s top job after Jensen Huang’s November 12 resignation. Bloomberg reported that Haas spoke with NVIDIA directors but expressed reservations, and Huang was reinstated on November 23. That same day, Haas pledged to stay in his current role through 2033.
So far, the market has paid back Arm. Arm shares are up 47.75% year to date, while NVIDIA is down 19.76%. Over the past month, both names have fallen hard: Arm is off 27.69% and NVIDIA 30.88%.
Valuation: NVIDIA Trades at a Steep Discount to Arm
Arm trades at 191 times trailing earnings and 176.52 times free cash flow, for a free cash flow yield of just 0.57%. NVIDIA trades at 30 times earnings and 37.29 times free cash flow, with a 2.68% free cash flow yield.
The gap gets wider on forward numbers. At $149.30, NVIDIA trades at about 9x the consensus estimate of $16.6053 in EPS for the fiscal year ending January 2028. That estimate stood at $12.9992 90 days ago. Arm trades at about 52x its fiscal 2028 consensus of $3.0869. Winner: NVIDIA.
Growth Trajectory: NVIDIA Is Growing Faster From a Far Larger Base
Arm’s growth is real. Fiscal Q2 2027 revenue rose 27.1% to $1.443 billion[1], its fifth straight quarter of growth above 20%[2]. License revenue climbed 50% to $773 million[1], data center royalties doubled year over year[2], and management sees up to $2 billion of Arm AGI CPU revenue across fiscal 2027 and 2028. On the other side of the ledger, royalty revenue grew only 8%[1], and GAAP EPS of $0.12 missed the $0.48 consensus. The new chip business also carries lower margins. Management expects the first AGI CPU to deliver gross margins in the high 30s to low 40%[2] range, compared with Arm’s current 92.48%.
NVIDIA’s fiscal Q3 2027 revenue jumped 103.21% to $115.84 billion[3], beating consensus. Data Center revenue reached $107.6 billion[3], and management guided Q4 to $130 billion plus or minus 3%. It also raised its fiscal 2028 growth outlook to approximately 75%[4]. Winner: NVIDIA.
Leadership and Risk: Arm Has the Stability Edge
NVIDIA faces ongoing governance scrutiny. On November 17, Chairman Harvey Jones said the board “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.”[4] The board reversed course within a week. Huang’s return has not ended the independent board investigation or congressional scrutiny, and S&P placed NVIDIA’s credit rating on downgrade watch on November 25 over concerns about board independence. On Thursday, Barron’s also reported that NVIDIA halted server shipments to Southeast Asia.
Arm has its own risks. SoftBank is its controlling shareholder, and R&D spending rose 24% to $857 million[1]. Higher memory prices are weighing on smartphone royalties. Selling its own CPUs also puts Arm in competition with licensees. Haas himself named NVIDIA among the “four guys inside the data center that command the majority of royalties.”[2] Still, Arm’s CEO is locked in through 2033, and NVIDIA’s leadership is still being sized up. Winner: Arm.
Verdict: NVIDIA Holds the Edge for Retirement-Focused Investors
Haas stayed at Arm, but NVIDIA offers the stronger financial profile. For retirement-focused investors, NVIDIA wins. It makes a 55.60% net margin and a 101.49% return on equity, and it carries debt-to-equity of just 0.073. It pays a $0.25[3] quarterly dividend and has $227.3 billion of buyback authorization left. Arm pays no dividend. NVIDIA is doubling revenue at about 9 times forward earnings despite the governance noise.
Arm fits a different investor: an aggressive grower with a decade-long horizon who believes the AGI CPU will turn a licensing company into a second silicon chipmaker and can stomach a 52x forward multiple. Arm has the steadier CEO, while NVIDIA has the stronger financials at a lower price. For income-minded portfolios heading into 2027, NVIDIA screens as the stronger fit.
