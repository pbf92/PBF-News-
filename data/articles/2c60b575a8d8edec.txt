TITLE: Schroth Exercises Rank Highest for Correcting Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis Curvature
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive systematic review and network meta-analysis has determined that Schroth exercises yield the most pronounced reductions in spinal curvature and axial trunk rotation among targeted physical training programs evaluated for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis. Published in the Journal of Orthopaedic Surgery and Research, the investigation systematically examined and compared the relative therapeutic value of multiple exercise-based regimens against conventional therapies and non-exercise control groups. The analytical model synthesized evidence across dozens of clinical investigations to clarify which specific movement strategies offer measurable structural improvements for young patients managing the condition.
The investigators focused specifically on trials contrasting four primary active exercise categories: Schroth exercises, defined as a dedicated subgroup of physiotherapy scoliosis-specific exercises; general physiotherapy scoliosis-specific exercises excluding Schroth methods; core stabilization exercises; and conventional exercise therapy. These active treatment protocols were assessed either in head-to-head comparisons against one another or against inactive non-exercise control conditions to capture relative performance benchmarks across standard clinical outcomes.
The quantitative evaluation focused on three primary outcome parameters critical to the management and assessment of adolescent idiopathic scoliosis: the Cobb angle, which quantifies lateral spinal curvature; axial trunk rotation, measuring rotational deviation of the torso; and health-related quality of life, evaluated through scores from the Scoliosis Research Society-22 questionnaire. The analytical protocol was prospectively documented on the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews under the registration code CRD420251049820 to ensure systematic transparency, adherence to predefined criteria, and methodological rigor throughout data collection and subsequent quantitative syntheses.
To assemble the clinical trial dataset, reviewers executed broad systematic searches across Web of Science, the Cochrane Library, Embase, PubMed, and Scopus from their earliest inception dates up to July 20, 2025. This search strategy was supplemented by the manual examination of reference lists alongside dedicated gray literature screening to capture relevant unpublished or non-indexed datasets. Screening for study eligibility, extraction of clinical parameters, and risk of bias evaluations using the revised Cochrane RoB 2.0 tool were carried out independently by pairs of reviewers, with discrepancies resolved through consensus or consultation with a third independent reviewer.
Across the screened literature, thirty-six randomized controlled trials encompassing a total pooled cohort of 1,911 participants met the formal inclusion criteria for synthesis. The resulting frequentist network meta-analysis demonstrated that Schroth exercises achieved the most substantial clinical improvements relative to conventional exercise therapy. Specifically, participants undertaking Schroth programs achieved significant reductions in Cobb angle, reflecting a standardized mean difference of minus 1.19 with a ninety-five percent confidence interval ranging from minus 1.94 to minus 0.43, and axial trunk rotation, reflecting a standardized mean difference of minus 1.37 with a ninety-five percent confidence interval from minus 2.37 to minus 0.37.
Schroth exercises also demonstrated statistically significant structural advantages when contrasted directly against non-exercise control groups. In these comparisons, the modality produced a standardized mean difference of minus 0.89 for Cobb angle reduction, with a confidence interval between minus 1.57 and minus 0.22, alongside a standardized mean difference of minus 1.29 for axial trunk rotation, bounded by a confidence interval from minus 2.15 to minus 0.44. Surface under the cumulative ranking curve analysis reinforced these quantitative findings, ranking Schroth exercises highest overall for curvature correction at ninety-four percent, followed in descending therapeutic order by core stabilization exercises at seventy-four percent and other physiotherapy scoliosis-specific exercises at seventy-one percent.
While Schroth programs registered the highest cumulative rankings, both core stabilization exercises and non-Schroth physiotherapy scoliosis-specific exercises also significantly surpassed conventional exercise therapy, each demonstrating a Cobb angle standardized mean difference improvement of approximately minus 0.90 without differing significantly from Schroth exercises. In contrast to these structural alterations, no single exercise modality exhibited a statistically significant advantage regarding health-related quality of life assessed by the Scoliosis Research Society-22 survey. Furthermore, although qualitative reports from multiple included trials suggested improvements in physical appearance, the authors emphasized that such aesthetic outcomes were derived primarily from qualitative observations rather than standardized measurements, underscoring the necessity for further high-quality trials to confirm broader non-radiological benefits.
