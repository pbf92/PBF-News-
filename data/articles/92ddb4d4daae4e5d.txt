TITLE: The Wildlife in GTA 6 Is Unreal, and You Need to See It Up Close
OUTLET: PCMag

“Leave the gun, take the binoculars.” Alright, that’s not quite the line from The Godfather, but it’s great advice for trying something that isn’t likely to get you into trouble with the law in GTA 6. I’m speaking, of course, of watching the wildlife. Leonida’s virtual world is rife with critters, to the point where GTA 6 is as much of a birdwatching, underwater exploration, and hunting game as it is a Bonnie and Clyde-style crime simulator.
This isn’t a big surprise to me. During the early blitz of reports on GTA 6, I kept hearing one argument brought up over and over again: GTA 6 is more of a sequel to Red Dead Redemption 2 than it is to GTA 5. And, as someone who’s played RDR2 a few times and earned a Zoologist badge on both Xbox and Steam, I immediately thought of Arthur Morgan’s quest to study, kill, and skin every animal in the game in order to get the best-looking sketches in his journal.
What I was prepared for was just how good the animals in GTA 6 look, and how much they act like their real-world counterparts. Birds fly away if you get too close (or if you look at them for too long), unless they're suburban seagulls who have literally no fear of humanity. Rattlesnakes will give you a loud warning if you tread too close, and bite you if you don’t back off. Wild boars will charge you. Alligators will mostly ignore you, unless they decide they’re hungry. GTA has come a long way since the days of shooting at pigeons and taking Chop for a walk.
GTA 6 isn’t the first game to include realistic animals and wildlife observation, but I can’t think of one with such a rich array of fauna, or such realistic graphics and behaviors. I spend a decent amount of my downtime out at my local wildlife preserve or watching birds at my backyard smart feeder with camera in hand, and let me tell you, watching wildlife in GTA 6 isn’t that far off from the real thing. It makes the animals in RDR2 look one-dimensional in comparison.
What Kind of Animals are in GTA 6?
There are a total of 255 animal species that you can find and study in GTA 6, and while I don’t know if that’s an homage to Link’s maximum rupee storage in the original Legend of Zelda, I’d like to think it is. The species are divided into six categories: Hunting, Fishing, Mammals, Cats & Dogs, Reptiles & Amphibians, Birds, Aquatic, and Other.
Most of the categories are self-explanatory, but a few aren’t. Hunting encompasses a hodgepodge of animals hunted for sport or meat in the real world, including reptiles, mammals, and birds. Likewise, Fishing is where you’ll find the fish that you can catch with your rod and reel in-game. Other, well, I’m curious about that myself. I’ve been playing for nearly a week and trying to find as many animals as I can, but I haven’t opened up any in this category. Knowing Rockstar, though, I expect some weird entries among the 23 species in this section.
I don’t want to hit you with spoilers; I’ll mention some specific species in this story, but nothing that you can’t see in your neighborhood or during a trip to an actual Florida wildlife preserve, and I’ll give you some tips on where to look for animals. But if you’re curious to find out what’s there and can’t wait to discover them via gameplay, you can see the full list of GTA 6 animals in a guide on PCMag’s sister site, IGN.
How Do You Study Animals in GTA 6?
The game starts you off with a pair of binoculars, which are the easiest way to bring animals into close focus. To get to your binocs, open your weapon wheel, switch over to the Equipment tab (at the 5 o’clock position of the circle), and scroll to the binoculars. Use the left trigger to bring them up to your eyes, and the up arrow on the D-pad to zoom in.
Once you have a new animal in view, you’ll see an icon prompting you to “Study” it at the bottom right corner of the screen, along with a general description of what type of animal it is (bird, mammal, dog, and so on). Hold down R1 (PS5) or RB (Xbox), and the wheel around the button prompt will start to fill in. Once it’s done, the animal is added to your Progress page. Animals is the fourth entry in this menu.
You’ll see that one of the blank squares about that species is now filled in with a picture, along with a little bit of information about the species. I’ve already learned a few new facts from this, including that flamingos were once thought to be extinct in America. Additionally, when you see the species again, its name will appear in the bottom-right corner of your screen as you spot it through your binoculars. You can also use a scoped rifle to study animals.
That’s for above ground. Underwater is a little trickier, to the point where it took me some effort to figure out how to study aquatic life. You can’t use your binoculars when swimming, so you’ll need to get decently close to a fish or other underwater animal and hold the left trigger to lock on. That’ll open up the Study option, and from there the steps are the same.
There’s one more way to study animals—kill them. If you shoot an animal and pick up its carcass, it’s added to your list, just as if you’d studied it. I prefer to be nonviolent to wildlife, both in life and in game, but hey, you do you.
Where’s the Best Place to Find Wildlife?
Finding wildlife is where experienced birders and naturalists have a leg up. GTA 6’s animals hang out and behave just like the real thing. If you want to catch views of herons and other wading birds, head to the wetlands or the beach. If you want to find a dolphin, hop on your boat and dive underwater. Squirrels and songbirds just hang out in your neighborhood, and you’ll quickly discover that seagulls are everywhere. Leonida is based on Florida, after all.
My best advice on finding new animals in-game is the same one I give to photographers who want to try out the birding and wildlife genres—keep your eyes and ears peeled. Just keep looking and pay attention for movement out of the corner of your eye, and try to figure out where you’re hearing birdsong or other noises in the wild. If you’re out in the wild and hear a weird grunting noise or the telltale sign of a rattlesnake, take a look around; you’ll probably end up finding an animal nearby. Animals are everywhere in the game. You just have to look for them.
You’re going to have the best luck studying animals with binoculars or a long scope. Be aware that if the animal is too far away, you won’t be able to study it at all. This can make catching some species tricky. It takes a little effort to get close enough to flying songbirds and keep them in view for study. Gulls, on the other hand, are basically fearless around people. If you act weird around them, they’ll fly away, but you can practically walk up to suburban herring gulls and pet them. (I wouldn’t recommend it; after catching tuberculosis in Rockstar’s last game, I worry that avian flu may end up being the death of your character if you go around petting birds.)
You’ll need to be a little careful when trudging around the wilds of Leonida. I was bitten by a snake during one wildlife outing, and had an alligator come at me during another. It’s dangerous out there, so yeah, I know I made the joke about putting your gun down earlier, but it’s wise to carry some protection if you’re exploring the wilds. How else are you going to protect yourself from 30 to 50 feral hogs?
As for aquatic life, hop in your boat (or steal one), head out to the open water and jump in. You can make short dives right when you start playing, but if you progress a bit into Chapter 1, you’ll be gifted some scuba gear. It’s really key for diving to the bottom of the bay and spending some real time checking out the sea creatures. You also have fishing poles on your boat, and on the dock, when you catch a fish, it’ll be added to your Progress list automatically.
Is There an Achievement for Studying Animals?
GTA 6 doesn’t have its own achievement for tracking down all the species, and I’ll admit that after snagging the rare Zoologist trophy in RDR2, I was a little disappointed that it’s not included. But it does count toward your goal if you are trying to get 100% completion in the game. You don’t have to study every species to tick that box off your checklist, just 100 of them. I don’t think that’s going to be hard to hit if you put in the effort; I’m still in Chapter 1 and have studied 82 species so far. I plan on getting them all; for me, finding new in-game animals is just as fun as crimes and car chases.
What Other Games Should I Try?
If you’re like me and you love the animal-studying aspect of the game, I’d wholeheartedly recommend RDR2 for anyone who finds it relaxing and fun to trudge through the swamps in GTA 6. It also has a great story, mixed with the mission design and action you’re used to from a Rockstar game, just in an early 20th-century western setting.
Aside from that, Beyond Good and Evil is a classic, and while I haven’t played it since it debuted on the original Xbox (yes, I’m that old), it was remastered in recent years for modern consoles and Windows systems. In the game, you’re an investigative reporter who takes a job cataloging wildlife on an alien planet. It got rave reviews back in the day, and while I’m twice as many years old as when I last played it, I remember it being a great one. I’m definitely going to check out the remaster when I have the time.
There are some others that I haven’t played, but they look like they scratch the same itch. If you consider Pokémon to be wildlife, you can check out New Pokémon Snap for the Nintendo Switch; it’s all about taking pictures of everyone’s favorite pocket monsters. Flock Around, available for PC and Macs, is a game that’s all about bird watching. Its cartoon graphics can’t match GTA, but it looks like a good way to spend a cozy evening.
And of course you can take up birding or wildlife observation in real life, too. I get a kick out of spying on my local songbirds and squirrels, and still get a bit of a thrill when I spot a great blue heron or bald eagle at my local wildlife trail. You’ll want to pick up a pair of binoculars, or a good camera with a telephoto lens if you get really into it. If you have enough fun in GTA spotting critters, check out my recommendations on the best tech for birdwatching—all of my recommendations apply to spotting mammals, reptiles, and other fauna from a distance too.
