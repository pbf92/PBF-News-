TITLE: Fiona built her small business on Instagram. Then Meta locked her out without warning
OUTLET: Nine.com.au

Australian small business owner Fiona Ellis Jones is devastated after her business social media accounts were abruptly closed by Meta.
Ellis Jones received a message on Instagram last week informing her that her two business accounts, Richmond Botanical and Barrenjoey Farm, had been disabled.
She toldToday she initially thought it was “some kind of spam”, but when she checked her accounts she found she had lost access.
“They’re really valuable accounts for us. We have across our Meta platforms about 20,000 followers, it’s 10 years of work, so to just lose it, and to have no explanation from Meta, is just devastating,” she said.
Looking into the situation, Ellis Jones discovered more small businesses that had also had their accounts deactivated with no explanation from the tech giant.
“The timing’s not great. We’ve got Black Friday this week, we’re coming into Christmas,” she said.
Ellis Jones told Today she suspects her account closures are the result of a “bot trawl” and said her attempts to get help from Meta has resulted in hours of interactions with “chatbots” rather than human intervention.
This is despite both her accounts both having earned “verified” status.
She was informed by the bots there was no avenue for human editorial review.

Former Facebook CEO for Australia and New Zealand, Stephen Scheeler, described the situation as “frustrating.”
He said the issue for small business owners is the lack of human assistance and the data that is lost as a result.
“I think the reason it happens is the AI systems are making a decision, they’re looking for patterns, but they don’t understand the context.”
Scheeler said this issue has been “going on for years at big tech platforms” and warns it is going to become more challenging “with other AI platforms coming into our lives, and AI being used by all the products and services we use.”
“We want our AI to make our lives better, but we have to remember that there’s a human who is the customer.”
Scheeler said while this may be a “small” problem for Meta, it is a serious issue for small business owners who may lose their livelihoods.
Ellis Jones has lost hope of retrieving her accounts and can’t understand what the issue could have been.
“Our accounts are super wholesome. We share photos of our life in Tasmania, our beautiful farm, our highland community, our cows. We also run our own fragrance business, we share pictures of our shops,” she said.
They were also recently gaining “getting significant traction.”
She can’t imagine any of her content being in breach of Meta standards.
Ellis Jones said their entire business model is run on social media, and it is their main avenue of communication with their customers, including the majority of their sales, not to mention running Meta advertising campaigns.
Scheeler said unfortunately there’s no easier answer for small business owners seeking a resolution, aside from Ellis Jones’ efforts so far and reaching out to media.
“I imagine Meta will reply now,” he said, adding there is an Australian Small Business Ombudsman who “does have some power to contact Meta” on the behalf of small business owners.
“But the reality is there a hole in the regulation and legislation today that these platforms don’t have a legal obligation to provide her with an answer that she seeks, and that is why she is in this limbo.”
** Both of Ellis Jones’ accounts have since been reinstated.
