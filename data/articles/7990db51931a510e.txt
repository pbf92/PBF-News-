TITLE: Google Maps starts taking questions in 30-plus European countries
OUTLET: PPC Land

Google today began extending Ask Maps, the Gemini-powered conversational search feature inside Google Maps, to what it describes as "30+ countries across Europe," eight months after the feature first went live in the United States and India.
The announcement, published on Google's Keyword blog under its Maps category, carries no byline and runs to 166 words. It names no country. It gives no language, no platform and no date beyond the word "Now." What it does contain is a superlative, two large numbers and three examples - and a pair of links to other Google posts that describe features the European text does not say are included.
In Short
Google today started rolling out Ask Maps, a feature that lets people type everyday questions into Google Maps and get conversational answers, to more than 30 European countries, after launching it in the US and India in March. The answers are built from Google's data on more than 300 million places and reviews from over 500 million contributors, which means the Business Profiles and reviews of European shops, restaurants and service providers now feed an AI answer layer rather than only a list of map pins. Google has named only Spain, Germany and the UK, has listed no languages or devices for Europe, and its European and launch posts say nothing about advertising inside Ask Maps.
A short post with a large claim
The European post opens with a sentence that sets the scale of what Google wants the feature to be seen as. "Ask Maps is the biggest update to Google Maps in over a decade," according to Google. It continues: "Powered by the world's freshest map and Gemini models, it answers complex, real-world questions a map couldn't before."
That superlative has a history. In March, Google used almost the same wording for something else. The launch post that introduced Ask Maps described the driving side of the same release, Immersive Navigation, as "our biggest navigation upgrade in over a decade," and stated: "We're giving the driving experience in Maps its biggest update in over a decade with Immersive Navigation." The two claims are not strictly contradictory, since one refers to navigation and the other to Maps as a whole. But the "decade" label has now migrated from the driving product to the conversational one, and the European post does not mention Immersive Navigation at all.
The core of the European text is a single sentence of numbers. "With information about over 300 million places, including reviews from more than 500 million contributors," the post says, before describing Ask Maps as personalised and able to "turn plans into action." Both figures are identical to the ones Google gave in March.
Three examples follow, each a bolded label with a sample query:
- Asking anything about anywhere, illustrated with "where can I charge my phone ASAP" and "find me a public tennis court with lights for playing tonight."
- An on-the-go companion, described as the ability to "Create a trip itinerary, complete with 'Know Before You Go' insider tips and real-time info about the weather and parking for each stop."
- Searching saved lists, introduced with "Skip scrolling through hundreds of saved places" and the sample query "Which spots saved in my lists have availability for 5 in 30 minutes?"
The post closes by pointing readers to two other Google posts for more detail. One covers food ordering inside Ask Maps, the August update that PPC Land reported added ordering through Square and Toast in the US. The other is a holiday-travel post published the same day. According to that post, itineraries and saved-list search work wherever Ask Maps is offered, while route tips and appointment finding launch in the US first. The European post itself does not say which of the linked features apply in Europe, and food ordering was US-only when it launched in August.
Eight months from launch to Europe
Ask Maps has now gone through three distinct rollout phases. The first two were documented in Google's own posts and in PPC Land's earlier reporting; the third is today's.
The gap between the first and third phases is 249 days, a PPC Land calculation from the two post dates. For the European phase, the European post offers no list at all. Google's companion holiday post, published the same day and linked from the European announcement, gives slightly more: according to Google, Ask Maps is starting to expand to 30 countries across Europe, including Spain, Germany and the UK, which takes it to more than 200 countries and territories worldwide. That count of 30 differs from the "30+" in the European post, and neither post names the other 27. TechCrunch reported the same three countries.
The March post was precise about scope. "Ask Maps starts rolling out now in the U.S. and India on Android and iOS, with desktop coming soon," according to Google. The European post contains no comparable sentence. Whether European users get Ask Maps on desktop, and whether the feature works in languages other than English, are questions the text leaves open.
The August phase matters for reading today's announcement. When PPC Land covered that update, the expansion reached more than 150 countries and territories, yet no European country was among those named. The agentic AI features added at the same time - food ordering, hotel and event discovery - were limited to the US. Gmail-linked personalisation and conversation memory were described as reaching every market where Ask Maps was available at the time. Today's post does not say whether any of those August additions apply in Europe.
What the March launch set out
The March 12 post, signed by Miriam Daniel, VP and GM of Google Maps, is the fuller of the two documents and remains the only detailed public description of how Ask Maps is meant to work. It opened with a broad statement: "Today, Google Maps is fundamentally changing what a map can do."
Google described Ask Maps in that post as "a new conversational experience that answers complex, real-world questions a map could never answer before." One sample query was "Is there a public tennis court with lights on that I can play at tonight?" A longer example involved a road trip taking in the Grand Canyon and Horseshoe Bend, with the post stating that to build an itinerary, "Maps analyzes information from over 300 million places, including reviews from our community of more than 500 million contributors."
Personalisation was central. According to the March post, results draw on places a user has searched for or saved in Maps. The example given was a request for a table for four at 7pm with friends travelling from Midtown East, which returned convenient midway spots with vegan options because Ask Maps already knew the user liked vegan restaurants. Once a place was chosen, the post said users could "book restaurant reservations, save places to a list or share them with friends," and get directions in a few taps.
The second half of the March post dealt with Immersive Navigation. According to Google, the feature renders a 3D view of buildings, overpasses and terrain, and highlights lanes, crosswalks, traffic lights and stop signs when needed. Gemini models, the post said, "analyze fresh, real world imagery from Street View and aerial photos" to show landmarks and medians along a route. Smart zooms and transparent buildings give drivers an earlier view of turns. Voice guidance was rewritten to sound more natural, with Google's example: "Go past this exit and take the next one for Illinois 43 South."
The post also gave two operational figures. "Every second, Maps incorporates over 5 million updates to traffic around the world," according to Google, and the community of drivers provides "more than 10 million of these contributions every single day" on disruptions such as road construction and crashes. Arrival guidance includes Street View previews of the destination, parking recommendations, the building's entrance and which side of the street to be on.
Immersive Navigation had a separate, slower schedule. "Immersive Navigation starts rolling out today across the U.S. and availability will expand over the coming months to eligible iOS and Android devices, CarPlay, Android Auto and cars with Google built-in," according to the March post. Today's European post is silent on it.
How Ask Maps works, according to Google
Taken together, the two posts describe the Ask Maps flow in six stages. This is a description of what Google's documentation says, not an evaluation of how the feature performs.
- Entry point. According to the March post, users tap an "Ask Maps" button inside Google Maps, which launched on Android and iOS.
- Query. The user types a question in natural language. Google's examples range from single needs (a phone charger, a lit tennis court) to multi-stop trip plans.
- Answer. Ask Maps replies conversationally and, according to the March post, displays a customised map of the options.
- Personalisation. Results are tailored using places the user has searched for or saved. A PPC Land interview analysis from April quoted Google's search chief Nick Fox as saying Ask Maps draws on a user's lists, previously visited places and current location.
- Itineraries and lists. According to today's post, Ask Maps can create a trip itinerary with "Know Before You Go" tips plus real-time weather and parking information for each stop, and can search a user's saved lists by availability. Google's same-day holiday post says the route tips launch in the US first; the European post lists them without that limit.
- Action. According to the March post, users can book restaurant reservations, save or share places, and start navigation from the answer.

Business Profiles become source material
For local businesses, the operative phrase in both posts is "over 300 million places." Many of them are Google Business Profile listings, with hours, attributes, photos and reviews maintained by the businesses themselves and by Maps users. When Ask Maps answers a question about a lit tennis court or a table for five in 30 minutes, it is reading that data and choosing what to surface.
PPC Land examined this shift in April, when Google's April 9 Business Profile newsletter introduced Ask Maps to merchants. That analysis described Google Business Profile becoming a data layer that feeds AI, Maps and Search, and argued that profiles with missing structured data are effectively invisible to conversational queries. A query such as "availability for 5 in 30 minutes" depends on reservation data; "a public tennis court with lights" depends on attribute data that a listing either carries or lacks.
Reviews are the other input. Google's "500 million contributors" are, in practice, a body of user-generated content that Google polices at scale, and not without dispute. In April, Google said it had blocked or removed 292 million policy-violating reviews in 2025 and was using Gemini to moderate edits. Maps has also been asking users at business locations whether the business offered rewards in exchange for reviews, a prompt that reporting cited by PPC Land in June traced to around September 2025 and that practitioners linked to large waves of review deletions. Once those reviews feed a generated answer rather than a star rating on a listing, moderation decisions shape which businesses get named in a sentence and which do not.
None of this is new in principle. Google already feeds Maps data into other AI products; in December 2025, Gemini began serving local results from Google Maps in a visual format, initially in English only. What changes today is the geography. Business owners in European markets who manage their listings through Google now have those listings read and summarised by a conversational system inside Google Maps itself.
