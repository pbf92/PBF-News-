TITLE: Tea, rings and bed sheets: the Indian goods in Walmart's holiday range
OUTLET: PPC Land

Walmart on November 25, 2026 published a newsroom feature naming Indian brands and manufacturers whose products sit in its holiday range for gifting, home and lifestyle, from tea and lab-grown diamond rings to bed sheets, towels and decorative metalware. The post, datelined New Delhi and Bentonville, Arkansas, names 16 companies by PPC Land's count and describes two separate routes into Walmart: Indian brands that sell their own products to shoppers on Walmart.com, and Indian factories that make goods sold under Walmart's and Sam's Club's own labels. It appeared on the same day the Office of the US Trade Representative proposed tariffs of up to 25% on Indian goods, a development the post does not mention.
In Short
Walmart published a post on November 25, 2026 listing Indian companies that supply holiday gifts, home decor and household textiles for its customers in the US, Mexico and Canada. It matters for brands and online sellers because it shows the two ways Indian businesses get onto Walmart: listing their own products on Walmart.com, or manufacturing Walmart's store-brand goods. The post gives no sales figures, no seller count and no word on tariffs, so how much these products are worth to Walmart, and what new US duties on India would do to their prices, remains unknown.
What Walmart published
The post is short. Walmart lists it as a five-minute read under its International news section, and it carries no figures about sales, volumes or the number of Indian sellers involved. What it offers instead is a set of named companies, the cities they operate from, the products they sell and the channel each one uses.
Its framing is seasonal. According to Walmart, "As customers across the United States, Mexico, and Canada prepare for the holiday season, Indian brands and manufacturers are contributing to Walmart's broad assortment of products for gifting, entertainment, and everyday celebrations." The products, the post says, reach customers through Walmart.com, Walmart stores and Sam's Club.
Avaneesh Gupta, Senior Vice President for General Merchandise and Fashion Sourcing at Walmart and Sam's Club, gave the company's view of India's role. "India's manufacturing capabilities, and innovations are increasingly important to Walmart's global supply chain," Gupta said in the post. "We see these strengths across categories, from home and apparel to food, wellness, and general merchandise. During the holiday season, they're helping us deliver the quality, value, and assortment customers expect."
"Sourcing" in this sense simply means buying goods from suppliers to stock stores and websites. The word "value" in Gupta's statement is not incidental. Walmart's own 2026 Holiday Trend Report found that 43.8% of its surveyed shoppers named affording the holidays as their top stressor, and 70.2% said lower prices would make holiday shopping easier.
Gifts sold by Indian brands on Walmart.com
The first half of the post covers Indian brands selling through Walmart Marketplace. This is the part of Walmart.com where outside companies list and ship their own products, and Walmart charges a fee on each item sold. It is a different arrangement from a direct-to-consumer model, in which a brand sells only through its own website or shops. On a marketplace, the brand keeps control of its product and pricing but borrows the retailer's shoppers.
From the perspective of an Indian company, a shopper in Ohio or Ontario is an international customer. The post uses that language throughout, describing these brands as "reaching international customers across a range of gifting and lifestyle categories."
Eight gifting brands are named:
The post groups Majestic Pure and Botanic Hearth together and does not say which of the two sells which product. Jewelry takes up three of the eight slots. Rosec Jewels sells lab-grown diamond wedding and engagement rings, bridal sets and gift sets, according to Walmart, while LeCalla sells earrings in 925 sterling silver, which is 92.5% pure silver.
VAHDAM is not new to Walmart's seasonal features. Walmart named the New Delhi tea brand in an October 31, 2024 newsroom post on Indian food brands for Diwali, alongside Britannia, Bikano and Jayanti Spices, the last of which supplied spices sold under Walmart's Great Value label.
Two of the brands sell fragrance and personal care. That category has carried tighter rules on Walmart.com since July 2025, when Walmart limited beauty and personal care listings to brand owners and authorized distributors, requiring other sellers to produce invoices or brand authorization letters.
Ashin Mathew, India Head of Cross-Border Trade at Walmart Marketplace, described the pitch to Indian sellers. "Indian sellers bring a compelling combination of product diversity, craftsmanship, and the ability to respond to evolving customer preferences," Mathew said. "Walmart Marketplace enables them to bring their brands and expertise to international customers at scale. During the holiday season, that reach helps them connect with shoppers across a wider range of gifting and lifestyle needs."
Household goods: factories and marketplace sellers
The second half of the post turns to the home. Here Walmart describes a different relationship. According to the post, Indian manufacturers contribute "through long-standing sourcing relationships and private-label programs across Walmart and Sam's Club."
Private label means products sold under a store's own brand name but made by another company. Walmart's $35 holiday meal basket, published on November 17, leaned on two such labels, Marketside and Great Value. The India post names no Walmart or Sam's Club label for the textiles it describes.
Shree Krishna Impex, a handicrafts supplier in Moradabad, sells decorative metalware and home decor, "including products developed for Walmart's seasonal assortment," according to the post. Walmart lists Welspun, Trident and Indo Count as "based in Gujarat, Budhni, and Kolhapur, respectively," mixing a state with two towns: Budhni is in Madhya Pradesh and Kolhapur in Maharashtra. All three supply products "including bed sheets and towels" through private-label programs at Walmart, Sam's Club and Walmart.com.
The four marketplace sellers are described as a group. According to Walmart, they offer "bedsheets, bath towels, pillows, table linens, and kitchen textiles," without saying which seller offers what. The post suggests these items can double as gifts "when families visit one another during the festive period."
How Indian companies reach Walmart customers
According to the post, there are two routes, and they work differently:
- As a marketplace seller. The Indian company lists its own branded products on Walmart.com and sells to the shopper directly. Walmart provides the storefront and the traffic. All eight gifting brands and four of the home textile companies in the post use this route.
- As a supplier. The Indian company makes goods that Walmart or Sam's Club buys and sells, often under a Walmart-owned label. The shopper sees the store brand, not the factory. Four companies in the post, Shree Krishna Impex, Welspun, Trident and Indo Count, are described this way.
Walmart's separate "Sell from India" page for marketplace sellers sets out the requirements for the first route. According to that page:
- Sellers register for the US marketplace. Walmart says that once US registration is complete, sellers can "sell globally from India" on its platform, though the page names only the US as a market.
- Sole proprietors need a GSTIN, the registration number under India's goods and services tax. Partnerships and companies need a GSTIN plus a company registration document (the page calls it a COI certificate) or articles of association. Hindu Undivided Family businesses cannot register.
- A US return address is required.
- Payments run through Payoneer or PingPong, with payouts in Indian rupees.
- There is no monthly or setup fee. Walmart charges a referral fee on each item sold, and Walmart Fulfillment Services, which stores and ships goods for sellers, is optional.
Walmart first opened its US marketplace to select Indian sellers in January 2022, and in October 2022 invited Indian businesses to apply to Walmart Canada's marketplace. A Global Seller Summit Walmart held in Delhi on September 27, 2022 drew more than 500 Indian manufacturers, brands and sellers, according to the company.
The $10 billion promise behind the post
The holiday feature is one small piece of a much larger commitment. On December 10, 2020, Walmart said it would triple its exports of goods from India to $10 billion a year by 2027. At the time, according to Walmart, its annual exports from India were worth about $3 billion. The categories named then included food, pharmaceuticals, health and wellness, general merchandise, apparel and homeware.
Walmart has repeated the target since, but it has not published a recent annual figure.
The May 2026 figure came from John Furner, Walmart's President and CEO, at the second Walmart Growth Summit India in New Delhi, according to IBTimes India. Walmart Vriddhi is the company's training program for India's micro, small and medium enterprises. Both cumulative totals are stated as minimums ("over") and cover different, partly unstated periods, so the gap between them cannot be read as a single year's sourcing.

Section 301 is the US trade law that lets the Trade Representative respond to foreign trade practices it judges unfair. Under the November 25 proposal, according to Business Standard, the rate would be calculated net of existing Section 301 duties. Indian goods currently face a 10% tariff on top of regular customs duties, the paper reported, with separate duties on steel, aluminium, cars, patented medicines and semiconductors.
Smaller shipments face a separate change. The US ended its de minimis exemption, the $800 threshold below which imported parcels entered duty-free, for all countries on August 29, 2025, under a July 30, 2025 executive order. And from January 5, 2027, a US Customs and Border Protection rule will require many foreign companies that import goods into the US to file through a validated customs broker.
None of this appears in the Walmart post. US tariffs are paid by whoever imports the goods, which can be the retailer, the supplier or the marketplace seller, depending on the deal. The post does not say who imports the products it names.
Why this matters for marketers
Walmart's marketplace is growing faster than the rest of its US online business. In the quarter to October 31, 2026, net sales from Walmart US marketplace and fulfillment services rose 49%, the third straight quarter above 45%. Every new outside seller is also a potential advertiser. Marketplace sellers buy sponsored listings through Walmart Connect, the company's retail media network, the advertising business Walmart runs on top of its store and website. A post that names Indian brands and explains how they reach US shoppers also reads as a pitch to Indian sellers not yet on the platform.
Walmart has been building the logistics for that kind of seller for some time. At its 2024 seller summit, the company introduced a Cross Border service shipping full containers from Asian ports to its US fulfillment centers. Walmart's 2025 seller playbook described sellers managing the US, Canada, Mexico and Chile from one account.
Amazon is chasing the same exporters. In June 2026, as part of a $48 billion India investment plan, Amazon said it had enabled more than $20 billion in cumulative ecommerce exports from India and set a target of $80 billion by 2030.
