TITLE: 'One of the greatest civilisational challenges of our time': Human Rights Commission hears of global Christian persecution
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A panel of experts have shared details about the persecution of Christians around the world to the Tom Lantos Human Rights Commission in the US.
The hearing was held on 18 November was particularly to focus on “emerging threats and efforts by governments and civil society to protect vulnerable Christian communities”.
David Trimble from the Religious Freedom Institute highlighted the case of Christians in Nigeria.
“The physical violence perpetrated toward Christians is dehumanising," he said. "Christians face mass killings, sexual violence, torture, kidnapping for ransom, destruction of property, forced displacement. Churches, schools and religious leaders have been targeted.”
He said authorities in the West African nation have downplayed the violence, failed to govern with accountability and left Christian communities vulnerable.
He also highlighted the plight of Syriac Christians, saying the faith is “on the verge of [extinction]” in Syria and Iraq, and urged the United States government to prioritise religious freedom training in the conflict zones.
Authoritarianism is the top cause of religious restrictions
The three main drivers of limited religious freedom across the world are authoritarianism, armed conflict and violent extremists, according to Timothy Shah from the University of Dallas.
“Self-organising religious communities are the single greatest check on state power,” he said. "We will not turn this around without a new global wave of democratisation.”
Religious refugees facing barriers to entry to the US
Matthew Soerens, from the humanitarian organisation World Relief, told the panel the flow of religious refugees to the US has almost completely stopped. The commision heard from the panel that from the 50 countries where Christians face the most severe persecution, not a single refugee was resettled.
“…through an abrupt reversal of past commitments, refugees fleeing persecution have been completely shut out from resettlement in the United States,” he said.
The US government has, however, allowed Christian refugees from South Africa a legal path to settle in America.
Soerens asked the commission to "encourage" the president to reopen the refugee resettlement program to those persecuted on the basis of their religious beliefs.
Jeremy Tedesco, from the Christian legal group Alliance Defending Freedom, warned of growing censorship in Europe. Among others, he pointed to the cases of Isabel Vaughn-Spruce and Adam Smith-Connor, who were both charged after praying near an abortion centre.
“A government which can police your prayers will have little hesitation policing your speech,” he said.
