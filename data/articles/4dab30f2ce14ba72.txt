Why Is the Stock Market Down Today? The Short Answer
The stock market is down today because new US curbs on AI chip exports and a sudden leadership change at Nvidia have hit technology shares. Those shares drive the main indices. Expectations of a December Fed rate hike and doubts over AI valuations add to the pressure.
On Monday 16 November the S&P 500 posted its third straight loss, AP reported (AP via The Manila Times). The global stock selloff spread on Tuesday, as stocks in Asia bore the brunt of the new chip measures (Bloomberg).
- The US Commerce Department issued a rule on 16 November that tightens access to advanced AI chips for data centres in five countries.
- Nvidia reports results after the US close today, five days after its chief executive stepped down.
- Markets price a Fed rate hike in December, and the 10-year Treasury yield is close to 5.1%.
- Investors are questioning whether heavy AI spending will pay off, with a large AI listing due this week.

The VIX is often called Wall Street's fear gauge. It uses S&P 500 option prices to show how much movement traders expect over the next 30 days. Readings in the mid-teens are typical of calm markets. Readings above 20 suggest traders are paying more to protect their portfolios, so Monday's close of 22.91 points to rising nerves.
Measured from the record closes of 6 October 2026, the S&P 500 is about 5.1% lower and the Nasdaq Composite about 11.2% lower (Portfolio Terminal). A fall of 10% from a record is usually called a correction. The Nasdaq is in that zone, while the S&P 500 is still above it.
For a longer view, the S&P 500 rose in four of the last five full calendar years and fell 18.11% in 2022. The Nasdaq Composite showed the same pattern, with a fall of 33.10% in 2022.

Reason 1: New US Curbs on AI Chip Exports to China
On Monday 16 November, the US Commerce Department issued an interim rule on access to advanced AI chips (Fortune). Licences are now needed to ship these chips to data centres in five named countries, among them Singapore and Malaysia. A licence is also needed when large data-centre operators anywhere give China-headquartered companies access to big chip clusters (TECHi).
The rule takes effect on publication in the Federal Register, scheduled for 19 November. Customers outside the US, Taiwan and China made up about 1.3% of Nvidia's revenue in the quarter to 26 July 2026, TECHi reported. It added that real end-use exposure may be larger (TECHi).
Markets also reacted beyond Nvidia. Bloomberg reported that Asian stocks bore the brunt of the new measures (Bloomberg).
Reason 2: Nvidia's Leadership Change Before Earnings
Nvidia led Monday's tech losses, and its shares fell 4.1% at the close on 16 November (AP).
On 12 November, Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive with immediate effect. He also left the board, where he was chairman. Chief financial officer Colette Kress became interim CEO and keeps her finance role (Paperjam). A US Senate committee has asked Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify at a public hearing on 3 December (Al Jazeera).
Third-quarter results are due after the US close today, with the release expected around 16:20 ET (TECHi). Analysts expect earnings per share of $2.47 on revenue of $108.85bn, according to Investing.com (Investing.com). Forecasts are not a reliable indicator of future performance. Options pricing implied a move of about 7.8% after the report, UBS said, as cited by Reuters (Reuters via Investing.com).
Reason 3: A December Fed Hike, High Yields and a Strong Dollar
The Fed's target range stands at 3.75% to 4.00% after a hike in September. After a hot October inflation report, CME FedWatch showed a 100% chance of another hike at the meeting of 8 to 9 December, as of 10 November (MPA).
Rate expectations have lifted Treasury yields. The 10-year yield was 5.10% at 09:18 London time on 17 November (Bloomberg).
High yields lower the present value of profits expected far in the future, which weighs on growth shares. The dollar index stood at 103.61 during Asian trading hours on 17 November, near its highest since April 2025 (Reuters via Investing.com). A strong dollar can trim the overseas earnings of large US companies.
The selloff started in chip shares. Higher yields and a firm dollar now weigh on the wider market as well.
Our review of the October Fed meeting and the preview of Kevin Warsh's speech on 16 November give more background. Our earlier note on how inflation data and Treasury yields move the Nasdaq 100 shows how rates feed into tech.
Reason 4: Doubts Over AI Valuations
Worries about an AI bubble were already building, AP reported. Monday's losses hit stocks tied to AI spending hardest, with AMD down 5.6% and GE Vernova down 4.9% at the close on 16 November (AP). Investors also want to know whether debt-fuelled AI spending can continue into 2027, Reuters reported (Reuters via Investing.com).
The Anthropic IPO is seen as a test of demand. It is set to price on 18 November at $70 to $78 a share and start trading on 19 November. The company is seeking to raise up to $64.4bn (Newsquawk).
Company profits offer a counterweight. S&P 500 earnings per share are on track to be nearly 32% higher than a year earlier, AP reported, citing FactSet (AP). Our analysis of the AI capex stock selloff looks at the spending debate in more detail.
Is the Selloff Spreading Beyond Tech?
It has reached other regions. The MSCI Asia Pacific Index was down 1.5% at 09:18 London time on 17 November, with South Korea and Taiwan leading losses (Bloomberg). Around 05:00 CET, Saxo put Taiwan's Taiex down about 2.5% and South Korea's Kospi down 2.6% (Saxo).
In Europe, the Stoxx 600 was down 0.8% at 09:18 London time (Bloomberg). Shortly after the European open at 08:00 London time, Germany's DAX was down 0.9% and chip equipment maker ASML was 1.2% lower (Investing.com).
In the US, Monday's losses went beyond chip makers, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1.41% (AP). Some shares rose. Tyson Foods gained 8.7% on 16 November after a profit beat (AP).
