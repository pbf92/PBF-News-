TITLE: 'They left her in a pool of her own blood': Report alleges brutal persecution of Christians in North Korea
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A UK-based human rights organisation alleges that Christian persecution is embedded into everyday life by the North Korean state in schools, workplaces and neighbourhoods.
Korea Future's Enemies of the State: Understanding anti-Christian persecution in the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea report pulls together nearly 600 interviews and material obtained within the authoritarian dictatorship.
The report places the state's opposition and persecution of the Christian faith in relation to the state ideology of Juche, or "Kimilsungism-Kimiljongism".
Korea Future say that Christianity can be seen as an obstacle to "supreme loyalty to the Kim leadership" and that from schools, the idea of Christian persecution is embedded.
The account of Sumin demonstrates the educational indoctrination of Christianity as per the report.
She learnt to hide her faith and her prayer, being told by her mother to pretend to play outside or cough if she was approached during prayer.
She was later detained in South Hamgyong Province as an adult, and gravely assaulted when she closed her eyes to pray.
“Officials recognised what she was doing, entered the cell, and beat her. They left her on the floor in a pool of her own blood, with two broken ribs, a broken collarbone, missing teeth, and a fractured jaw,” the report stated.
Political education materials also reportedly contain anti-Christian propaganda, with the Bible "understood as an instrument of infiltration, a religious gathering as a potential hostile organisation, and Christian assistance as an attempt to secure political allegiance". Meanwhile, Christian missionaries are portrayed as enemies in textbooks, as per Korea Future's account.
Persecution impacts adults too – Minseo, one of nine individuals the report focuses on, attended church in China, but when she was returned to North Korea, she was questioned by authorities for her faith.
“The officers wanted to know how many times she had gone, where the church was, who had been there, and what they had said," the report reads.
She is said to have been beaten at a provincial state-security office to force her to admit her church attendance.
The group has urged the UK and European governments to coordinate and target sanctions against Pyongyang's officials.
Christian persecution watchdog Open Doors ranks North Korea as the worst country for persecution on their World Watch List.
Open Doors said Christians could be killed instantly if they are identified, with owning a Bible a serious crime.
