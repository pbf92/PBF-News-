XP shares skyrocket, what you need to know
What happened?
Shares of XP jumped 5.8% in the afternoon session after third-quarter 2026 U.S. economic growth accelerated, October 2026 personal income and consumer spending increased and weekly unemployment claims declined.
The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis reported in its second estimate that real gross domestic product (GDP) increased at an annual rate of 4.3% in the third quarter of 2026, up from 2.2% in the second quarter. The agency revised real GDP up by 0.9 percentage point from its advance estimate, primarily reflecting upward revisions to investment and consumer spending.
In a separate release, the Bureau of Economic Analysis reported that personal income increased $201.8 billion, or 0.7% at a monthly rate, in October 2026, while personal consumption expenditures rose $63.8 billion, or 0.3%. According to the release, the income gain primarily reflected increases in compensation, government social benefits and personal income receipts on assets.
Separately, the U.S. Department of Labor reported that seasonally adjusted initial unemployment claims fell by 13,000 to 189,000 in the week ending November 21, 2026, from the previous week's revised level of 202,000. The release also showed that the 4-week moving average decreased by 1,500 to 196,000.
Stronger economic growth, higher personal income and lower unemployment claims can support consumer spending, borrowing and transaction activity across diversified financials. Share prices in the sector often rise when economic data points to steadier demand for financial services.
What is the market telling us
XP’s shares are extremely volatile and have had 30 moves greater than 5% over the last year. In that context, today’s move indicates the market considers this news meaningful but not something that would fundamentally change its perception of the business.
The previous big move we wrote about was 9 days ago when the stock dropped 4.7% on the news that Federal Reserve Governor Michael S. Barr warned of leverage and circular-financing risks in artificial intelligence as the U.S. Commerce Department cracked down on overseas AI compute leasing. In remarks published by the Federal Reserve, Barr said on-balance-sheet debt across the seven largest AI firms is expected to exceed $800 billion by year-end, while nominal off-balance-sheet leases and collateral guarantees surpass $3 trillion and circular equity stakes top $100 billion. He cautioned that high hedge-fund leverage via dealer prime brokerage could fuel "self-reinforcing fire sales" during a repricing, alongside algorithmic conflicts steering clients toward proprietary products or higher transaction fees.
Separately, CNBC reported Commerce’s Bureau of Industry and Security will require licenses for advanced chips bound for Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, and Brazil to block Chinese firms from leasing Nvidia compute abroad. That policy shift could complicate Wall Street’s AI deal pipeline.
XP is down 10.9% since the beginning of the year, and at $14.41 per share, it is trading 55.8% below its 52-week high of $32.60 from October 2026. Investors who bought $1,000 worth of XP’s shares 5 years ago would now be looking at only $487.55.
