TITLE: Gemini 4 Carbon Is on Google AI Pro, and Only in Antigravity
OUTLET: OrcaRouter

On 23 November 2026, a $19.99 Google AI Pro subscription started including Gemini 4 Carbon inside Google Antigravity — the same model that needed the $99.99 AI Ultra tier in the same IDE thirteen days earlier. Google described the change as "upgrading models across tiers": Carbon moved down to Pro, Gemini 4 Hydrogen opened to everyone, and Gemini quotas were reset for every Antigravity user. Nothing about the model changed. What changed is who is allowed to type into it, and on what clock.
The second half of that sentence is the part the announcement buries. The grant is scoped to Antigravity. In the Gemini app, Carbon has sat behind AI Ultra since it went public on 10 November, and nothing in the 23 November post moves it. If you read "Carbon is now available on Google AI Pro" as a statement about the Gemini app, AI Mode or AI Studio, you will spend an evening looking for a model picker that does not exist.
What changed on 23 November
Four things shipped together, and they are easy to conflate because Google announced them in one breath.
• Model access — Gemini 4 Carbon is now selectable in Antigravity for paid Google AI Pro subscribers at $19.99 a month, not just AI Ultra at $99.99.
• Hydrogen — Gemini 4 Hydrogen is available to all Antigravity users, which Google says was previously promised. It is the baseline row now, not a paid upgrade.
• Quota — Google reset Gemini quotas for everyone in Antigravity, not only for the people who gained Carbon.
• Interface — the Orchestrator and Executor model pickers moved into a dedicated panel under Settings, Models, Orchestrator Settings, so the model that plans and the model that executes are chosen in one place.
The quota reset is doing more work here than the model grant, and the week's reporting makes that plain. A reset the preceding Saturday accompanied caching improvements that Google said increased the amount of work a quota buys by 22%. TheBlockBeats reported that Antigravity head Varun Mohan tied the 23 November reset to the product's one-year anniversary and said Carbon's first-token latency had improved by roughly 1.5×. That is three bulk resets in a month — Gemini and Argon on 7 November, Gemini for all users on 14 November, all models on 23 November. Google is not adding capacity one model at a time; it is adding it one announcement at a time.
The quota is a five-hour clock, not a bank
The Antigravity plans documentation is unusually blunt about what a Pro subscription actually is. Paid Pro gets "a high, generous quota, refreshed every five hours until the weekly limit is reached", with a higher weekly rate limit that Google does not put a number on. Carbon sits inside that shared Gemini quota rather than carrying a separate allowance.
That structure explains the complaint that followed the reset, and it is a fair one. A reset is a grant, not a credit. It refills a window that was already going to refill; it cannot be saved, stacked or spent later. If your week is front-loaded — a Monday migration, a Thursday demo — three resets across a month do not help you on the Thursday. They help you on whichever day Google chose. The people asking for banked resets are asking for the quota to behave like a balance instead of a tap, and Google has published nothing suggesting it will.
There is a second, less philosophical problem. In the Google AI Developers Forum, paid Pro subscribers reported Gemini 4 models showing as "Unavailable" in Antigravity after renewing through Family Sharing or moving between tiers — an entitlement desync rather than a quota exhaustion. The suggested fixes are the usual ones: confirm the plan at one.google.com, refresh the model list under Settings, start a new conversation, sign out and back in. If Carbon does not appear for you this week, that is the first thing to check, not your remaining quota.
What Carbon does to a five-hour window
Access is the easy half. The harder question is what a Pro-tier quota looks like when the model inside it reasons in long form.
Artificial Analysis measures Gemini 4 Carbon at its high effort setting at 60 on Intelligence Index v5.0.1, at $7.76 per completed index task and 33.3 minutes per task, with 69.13 seconds to the first answer token. Those are paid-API economics, not subscription economics, but the shape carries over: Carbon spends a lot of tokens before it answers, and quota is denominated in tokens.
The most concrete evidence comes from DataCamp's hands-on test, run through OpenCode with identical prompts for both models in its comparison. At high effort, one Carbon attempt emitted 40,881 reasoning tokens and then hit the model's 65,536-token single-response ceiling while still writing the file, so nothing was saved. Another lost its provider connection after 18 minutes. At medium effort, two of three pages completed, in 34 and 12.5 minutes. Five attempts produced two working pages.
Two operational facts fall out of that. The 65,536-token maximum output is a real ceiling for a model asked to produce a whole artefact in one turn, and an agent that reasons for 40,000 tokens before it starts writing has very little room left. And a verbose model burns a five-hour quota faster than a terse one doing the same job — which matters more on a Pro plan than on a metered API, because on Pro the failure mode is waiting, not paying.
An entitlement is not an API key
Nothing about the 23 November change reaches the API. A Google AI Pro subscription is an entitlement to the Gemini app, Antigravity and the other consumer surfaces; it does not issue an API key and does not make gemini-4-carbon callable from your own code. The API model has been paid-tier-only since 10 November, with a 1,048,576-token context window and three published effort levels — low, medium and high — and no free row.
The API price is also the reason this week matters more than the tier change. Carbon is $2.00 per million input tokens and $10.00 per million output tokens through 30 November. On 1 December those become $4.00 and $20.00. Anything you benchmark this week is being measured at half the rate it will run at for the rest of its life.
That repricing is where a router earns its place. Gemini 4 Carbon is routed on OrcaRouter as google/gemini-4-carbon, at the provider's list price with 0% markup — so the December step arrives on the same day Google makes it, with no separate negotiation and no second contract. The same key also reaches the GPT-6.2 and Claude rows, which is what makes a cost comparison against a rival a matter of changing one string rather than opening an account.
What leaves Antigravity on 30 November
The same announcement carried a removal date, and it is the one Pro subscribers should diarise. Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 3.8 Flash leave Antigravity on 30 November. Google did not frame this as a downgrade, because for Pro users it is not one: Carbon replaces Argon as the frontier row, and Hydrogen covers the cheap end.
But a model swap inside an IDE is not free if you have built around it. Antigravity sessions that pin Argon, prompts tuned to its refusal behaviour, and any saved configuration that names it will need to move. The Orchestrator Settings panel is the place to check, because the model that plans and the model that executes are now chosen separately — it is possible to switch the executor to Carbon and leave a deprecated orchestrator in place without noticing until a session behaves oddly.
Three dates that decide the next fortnight
Strip the announcements down and the calendar is short.
• 30 November — Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 3.8 Flash leave Antigravity. Re-point anything that names them.
• 1 December — Carbon's API rate doubles, from $2.00 and $10.00 to $4.00 and $20.00 per million tokens. The introductory window closes; the subscription tier is unaffected.
• Ongoing — entitlement desync. If Pro shows Carbon as unavailable, the problem is account state before it is quota.
The subscription question is settled: $19.99 now buys the frontier Gemini model inside Antigravity, which is a genuine widening of the tier and the most consequential thing Google has done with Carbon since it became callable. The billing question is not settled, and it resolves in seven days. Anyone planning to put Carbon into a real pipeline should price it at $4.00 and $20.00, then decide whether the Pro seat is a testing environment or the beginning of a production habit.
