TITLE: Edwin van der Sar proven right with Manchester United warning to Michael Carrick
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

When Michael Carrick left last season behind and went on his summer holidays, the former Manchester United boss will have no doubt felt pretty good.
Having replaced Ruben Amorim in January as caretaker manager, Carrick led United into the Champions League with an excellent second half of the season and was appointed on a permanent basis. But Edwin van der Sar, the United great and ex-CEO at Dutch giants Ajax refused to get caught up in hailing that the good times and trophies would soon be heading back to the red side of Manchester.
Van der Sar made clear his delight for Carrick. He told talkSPORT: "I think what Michael brought was stability, clarity, got some players in, played a lot of times the same team and formation that was recognisable for the fans.

"I think the performances that he put on, the results that he got, the board and directors took their time to look at options. They saw how things developed, saw what the players looked like, and probably had some contact with Bruno and some established players to make up their mind.
"And, yeah, I'm very happy for Michael that he got the opportunity to do it on a permanent basis."
But he made clear that there were big challenges that awaited Carrick over the summer and beyond, with one particular “headache” at the centre of his thinking.
“The big thing is Casemiro leaving, which will leave them a big gap in midfield," added the Dutchman. "I think the last year and a half, the appreciation for him and his career has been immense, so that will be a headache.
"Goal scoring-wise, with the addition of Mbeumo, Cunha, and Sesko, they have a little more grit, a little bit more stability, a little bit older players and in the past, they went for some young players.
"I think they will do that again this summer, they will look at the Premier League to see which players are available to fill the gaps."

With Casemiro gone, United did indeed seek to overhaul their midfield.
Youri Tielemans’ form has been patchy since arriving from Aston Villa for £35m, Andrey Santos joined for £50m from Chelsea and has looked a squad player at best - with the feeling being that United greatly overpaid for the Brazilian - while Carlos Baleba eventually joined for £70m towards the end of August but has had his own injury issues since arriving from Brighton.
The “headache” of which Van der Sar warned really has come to fruition and while United have continued to score goals - 14 in their opening 10 games, the same number as fourth place Liverpool - a Casemiro-less midfield has struggled to protect its defence; thus, United have conceded more goals than anyone else, except bottom of the pile Crystal Palace.
With only one win in their opening 10 games, Carrick was dismissed on Thursday, just over five months after being made permanent boss.
His former team-mate had warned things wouldn’t be easy this season. How right he has been.
