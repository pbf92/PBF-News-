Prefrontal Cortex Activation May Predict Cognitive Outcomes in Children With Sleep-Disordered Breathing: JAMA
A cross-sectional study found that task-related prefrontal cortex (PFC) activation is associated with the severity of sleep-disordered breathing (SDB) and influences its relationship with cognitive performance in children. Functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) may help identify children whose cognitive outcomes differ from what conventional SDB severity measures predict, enabling more individualized assessment of cognitive risk. The study was published in JAMA Otolaryngology–Head & Neck Surgery by Nithya N. and colleagues.
To evaluate if prefrontal cortex activation in executive function tests correlated with SDB severity and mediated the cognitive differences, a prospective cross-sectional study was carried out with the recruitment of 284 children aged 5 to 11 years old referred for SDB screening in two academic pediatric otolaryngology clinics between January 2024 and March 2026. Exclusion criteria included neuromuscular disorders, previous airway surgery, continuous positive airway pressure treatment, sickle cell disease, stimulant drugs, and developmental delay at baseline.
Severity of SDB was measured with the Pediatric Sleep-Related Breathing Disorders Questionnaire, nocturnal Apnea-Hypopnea Index (AHI), and SpO2 nadir. Pre-frontal cortex hemodynamics were assessed with the use of functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) while the subjects performed a computerized go/no-go test assessing attention and impulse control. Cognitive variables measured by parents' report on the Behavior Rating Inventory of Executive Function and objective cognitive tests from the NIH Toolbox Cognition Battery.
Key findings:
- Evaluations on 284 children (age 7.9 ± 1.8 years; 130 female [45.8%]) demonstrated a significant correlation between SDB severity, PFC recruitment, and cognitive integrity.
- Out of all the participants, 237 (83.5%) were at high risk of developing SDB, having a mean AHI of 11.7 ± 14.5 events per hour and a mean nocturnal SpO2 nadir of 86.4% ± 8.2%.
- There was a significant correlation between higher AHI and increased left dorsolateral PFC activation (oxyhemoglobin: β = 0.08; 95% CI, 0.03 to 0.12; deoxyhemoglobin: β = -0.07; 95% CI, -0.11 to -0.03).
- There was a significant correlation between lower SpO2 nadir and increased left dorsolateral PFC activation (β = 0.14; 95% CI, 0.06 to 0.23).
- Right dorsolateral PFC activation was correlated with worse executive function as reported by parents after FDR correction.
- No polysomnographic severity measure was significantly correlated with objective NIH Toolbox performance after FDR correction.
- PFC activation moderated the relationship between SDB severity and cognition, such that those children who showed increased PFC activation had similar cognitive performance regardless of SDB severity.
In summary, results from a cross-sectional multi-center trial indicate that task-related activation of the prefrontal cortex is associated with the severity of pediatric SDB and acts as a mediator between nocturnal respiratory events and cognitive performance. Conclusions drawn from the study highlight the importance of functional near-infrared spectroscopy in identifying children whose cognitive abilities fall outside the scope of polysomnographic parameters.
Reference:
Navarathna N, Novi S, Nusraty S, Bortfeld H, Mitchell RB, Isaiah A. Prefrontal Activation and Cognitive Heterogeneity in Pediatric Sleep-Disordered Breathing. JAMA Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg. Published online November 05, 2026. doi:10.1001/jamaoto.2026.3461
