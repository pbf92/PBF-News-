TITLE: Walmart's global ad growth drops to 25% as Flipkart's sale slips a quarter
OUTLET: PPC Land

Walmart today reported that its global advertising business grew 25% in the third quarter of fiscal 2027, down from 38% three months earlier, after the timing of Flipkart's Big Billion Days sale in India pushed international advertising into a 15% decline.
The US picture was steadier. Advertising in the Walmart U.S. segment, which now includes the VIZIO television business and the self-serve platform Vibe.co, grew 37%, against 38% in the second quarter. Walmart Connect on its own, excluding VIZIO and Vibe, rose 36%. Total revenues for the three months to October 31, 2026 reached $188.8 billion, up 5.2%, or 4.5% in constant currency, and Walmart raised its full-year outlook for sales, operating income and earnings per share for the second time this fiscal year, after an increase in August.
In Short
Walmart's advertising business grew 25% worldwide in the quarter to October 31, slower than the 38% it posted in the previous quarter, mainly because a big Indian shopping event moved into the next quarter. In the US, where Walmart Connect, VIZIO TVs and Vibe sit, advertising still grew 37%, and the finance chief said the US online business would be close to break-even without any ad income. For anyone buying retail media, that means ads now supply most of the profit in Walmart's US online business, and the company expects the delayed Indian sale to lift its fourth quarter.

The gap between a 16.0% rise in reported operating income and a 19.5% fall in net income comes from below the operating line. Walmart booked a $341 million loss on equity and other investments in the quarter, against a $2.081 billion gain a year earlier, and the presentation attributes the latest loss primarily to "a decrease in the underlying stock price of our investment in Symbotic." The year-earlier quarter also carried a $722 million non-cash share-based compensation charge at PhonePe, the Indian payments company, which flattered this year's reported operating growth. Stripping out those items, adjusted operating income rose 5.5% in constant currency, ahead of the 4.4% growth in net sales.
Walmart said enterprise net sales growth of 4.4% in constant currency exceeded the top end of its own guidance range of 3.0% to 3.75% for the quarter. When it reported second-quarter results in August, Walmart had guided to that range, along with adjusted EPS of $0.62 to $0.64. Analysts had expected EPS of $0.64 and revenue of $187.27 billion, according to Investing.com.

Does the slowdown reach the US? Only partly. The drop in the global figure sits mostly outside the country. Walmart International's advertising business fell 15%, and its eCommerce sales slipped 2%, which the release attributes to the timing of Big Billion Days. The company expects the effect to reverse: "eCommerce sales and advertising expected to grow similarly in the back half compared to first half of the year," the release states. Chief Financial Officer John David Rainey said on the call that the shift was worth about 100 basis points of headwind to enterprise sales growth in the third quarter and should add about 100 basis points in the fourth.
Inside the US, the two lines tell slightly different stories. Total Walmart U.S. advertising growth barely moved, from 38% to 37%. Walmart Connect, the company's retail media network, decelerated more, from 43% to 36%. The difference is VIZIO and Vibe, both of which are counted in the 37% but not in the 36%. Vibe was not part of the second-quarter figure at all: Walmart agreed to buy the company on June 23 and, as PPC Land reported in November, completed the deal on August 4, after the second quarter closed.
Rainey linked the advertising growth to the marketplace. "Strength in marketplace has also been a driver of advertising growth. As more sellers join the marketplace, they see sales increase by utilizing Walmart Connect advertising capabilities," he said, according to the Investing.com transcript of the call. "U.S. advertising, including VIZIO and Vibe.co, grew 37%, led by strength in Walmart Connect, which was up 36%."
How Walmart defines its advertising metric
The advertising figures are not a line in the income statement. Walmart's "Earnings key terms" document defines the metric in these words: "Advertising business is an operational metric used to gauge advertising activities provided by Walmart as we leverage our assets for B2B opportunities, the value of which is recorded in either net sales or as a reduction to cost of sales depending on the nature of the advertising arrangement. Amounts included in this metric are incremental to our retail merchandise operations and are conducted by our specifically designated advertising groups within Walmart."
That accounting split matters. Part of what brands pay Walmart for media never appears as revenue; it reduces cost of sales and so shows up inside gross margin. PPC Land examined in March how such rules create gaps in reported retail media revenue. The same definitions document says eCommerce net sales "also includes net sales from other business offerings that are part of our ecosystem, such as fulfillment services, data insights and certain advertising arrangements," so a portion of advertising is also inside the 23% US eCommerce growth figure.
The effect is visible in the margin. Walmart U.S. gross profit rate rose 50 basis points to 28.1%, which the presentation says "reflects improved business mix primarily from growth of digital advertising," alongside lower pharmacy sales.
VIZIO screens and Vibe attribution
Seth Dallaire, Walmart's chief growth officer, spent most of his answer to a Bank of America analyst on the television hardware. "The TVs that are powered by VIZIO OS have been the number one sellers in the U.S., and our unit volume for those TVs is up nearly 150% year over year," he said. "So that unit volume, the installation base as that continues to grow, allows advertisers more opportunity to speak with the owners of those TVs, our customers and members."
Walmart agreed to buy VIZIO for about $2.3 billion in February 2024, and in October VIZIO said time spent on its free WatchFree+ service had risen 58% in a year. Dallaire said the contribution of VIZIO to advertising revenue growth was visible in the third quarter and would continue into the fourth.
On Vibe, the CTV platform Walmart agreed to acquire in June, which then served more than 10,000 advertisers, Dallaire gave a timeline. "The Vibe.co contribution is encouraging as well, because that platform was just integrated into VIZIO and our connected TV offering a few weeks ago," he said. "So we're now able to deliver capability for sales attribution through those VIZIO television sets in ways that we've never been able to do before." He did not quantify Vibe's revenue, the number of advertisers using the Walmart audiences, or how sales attribution is measured. Rainey repeated that costs related to the acquisition and integration of Vibe are expected to be a roughly 20 basis point headwind to full-year operating income growth, the same figure Walmart gave in August.
US e-commerce, with and without the ads
The most consequential remark for media buyers came in response to Simeon Gutman of Morgan Stanley. Rainey said the Walmart U.S. eCommerce business delivered "over 12% incremental margins in the quarter," an acceleration, and that the company should "generally" expect that level "in the low double-digit range going forward." He then separated the advertising out.
Excluding advertising, Rainey said, "we are actually getting to a point where we are pretty close to break-even without advertising, which was pretty remarkable," according to the Investing.com transcript.
Taken at face value, the remark implies that advertising income supplies most of the profit Walmart's US online business now generates. Rainey credited network densification, supply chain investment and the delivery network for the improvement. When JPMorgan's Chris Horvers asked whether marketplace scale explained it, Rainey pointed to speed, supply chain investment and delivery density, describing eCommerce "ex advertising" as "getting to a point that is more or less break-even."
There is a caveat that Walmart itself attaches. Its definitions document describes eCommerce profitability as "a managerial accounting view of operating results associated with our eCommerce net sales," adding that the measures "reflect certain assumptions and allocations of expense to our eCommerce business offerings, and as such, may not be comparable to eCommerce profitability measures reported by other companies." No profit figure for the eCommerce business was published.
The underlying volume figures were strong. Walmart U.S. eCommerce sales grew 23%, the 11th consecutive quarter above 20%, and contributed about 530 basis points to the segment's 2.9% comparable sales growth. Store-fulfilled delivery grew more than 40%, and expedited deliveries, those arriving in under three hours, made up more than 37% of store-fulfilled orders. Rainey said stores were the last-mile fulfillment nodes for more than 80% of US eCommerce orders. Dave Guggina, president of Walmart U.S., said the company can reach 96% of US households in three hours or less and 60% in 30 minutes or less. "And mind you, that assumes a 15-minute pick time in the store," he said, adding that deliveries in 30 minutes or less increased almost three times in the quarter.
Globally, eCommerce net sales grew 17% and represented 24% of net sales. On the quarter's $186.8 billion of net sales, that share equals about $44.8 billion of eCommerce net sales, by PPC Land's calculation. Rainey said roughly 25% of Walmart's overall business is now digital and that this share could rise "maybe another 10 to 15 percentage points" over the next five to seven years, a shift that weighs on operating expenses because, in his words, digital orders hit selling, general and administrative costs "more than an in-store transaction."
Marketplace: 49% growth and a GMV caveat
Walmart U.S. marketplace and fulfillment services net sales grew 49%, the third consecutive quarter above 45%. In August, PPC Land reported second-quarter marketplace growth of 52%. Rainey said nearly 50% of marketplace gross merchandise value flows through Walmart Fulfillment Services, with units delivered same day and next day up more than 60%. Dallaire added that units promised next day or faster rose 81% year over year.
Rainey also explained why the comparable sales figure understates the marketplace. "It's important to note that within our comp sales disclosure, we include the commission and services fee portion of an e-commerce marketplace transaction, but not the overall value of that transaction, which is defined as gross merchandise value, or GMV," he said. Because the marketplace leans toward general merchandise, the gap is visible in that category: Walmart U.S. general merchandise GMV grew mid-single digits in the quarter while reported general merchandise comparable sales grew low single digits.
