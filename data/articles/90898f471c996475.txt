TITLE: Rupert Lowe attacks ‘woke’ Church of England over motion on Restore Britain
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The leader of Restore Britain has hit out at the Church of England, labelling the Archbishop of Canterbury and the Church as “woke”.
Rupert Lowe was responding to a Private Members Motion proposing that the Church distance itself from his far-right political party.
The motion proposes that the House of Bishops declare “the policies, objectives, activities and public statements of Restore Britain are incompatible with the Church of England’s teaching” on equality.
The motion also proposes that the General Synod affirm that all people are created in the image of God and that racism is a sin.
A Church of England spokesperson told Premier Christian News that the motion does not represent current Church policy, adding: “The submission of a Private Member’s Motion does not constitute a decision of the General Synod or a position of the Church of England.”
The motion needs signatures from at least 100 Synod members before being eligible for debate at the General Synod in February.
Lowe threatened legal action if the motion is accepted and leads to clergy who support the party being suspended. “We will fight them in the court,” he wrote on X. “I am not tolerating it.”
In a video, he added: "This is just palpable rubbish from a Church, a woke Church of England that's lost its way, that now describes itself of defender of faiths, not defender of the faith, which is what any established Church should be doing."
The motion was submitted by lay member Sam Wilson. He told Premier he had brought the motion because he saw value in having it debated by the 478 Synod members.
“Every person, wherever they're from, is made in the image of God … and I don't think that the way that Restore has used language and the way that Restore has campaigned has that in mind,” he said.
Wilson said he believed the motion would reach the requisite number of signatures. “It's not about me," he added. "It's not about my personal interpretation. It's about what the Synod think,” he said.
Leaders of Restore Britain have criticised the proposal and the Church on social media. “It’s a sad day for the Church of England itself really,” Lowe said. “I think it really needs to be restored itself.”
Wilson said that, following the posts, he had faced fierce criticism online. “When it tips over into intimidation, I think that is a sad thing about our society today,” he told Premier.
“And I just hope that we can have this debate in our society today in a grown-up way and in a more loving way than we currently are.”
