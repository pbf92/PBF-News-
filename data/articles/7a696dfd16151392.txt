TITLE: Mauricio Pochettino believes USMNT experience makes him a 'complete coach' ahead of Man United job
OUTLET: www.sportingnews.com

Mauricio Pochettino said leaving the United States men's national team to make his Premier League return with Manchester United was the "hardest decision of his career."
Nevertheless, the Argentine will coach his final game with the USMNT on Tuesday against Haiti before linking up with the Red Devils to ready them for his debut, a Sunday showdown against rivals Liverpool.
With United sitting 19th in the Premier League table, Pochettino must hit the ground running to have the team ready for a fight against relegation and rise up the standings to a place more befitting of the club's stature.
According to the man himself, who has previous coached at Tottenham, Chelsea, and Paris Saint-Germain, it was his time in international football with the USMNT that has seen him turn a corner as a coach, along with his assistants Jesus Perez, Toni Jimenez, Sebastiano Pochettino, Miguel D'Agostino, and Silvia Tuya.

Mauricio Pochettino believes USMNT made him a 'complete coach'
New Manchester United manager Mauricio Pochettino has plenty of experience coaching in the Premier League. But he believes he needed to go outside Europe — and out of club football management completely — in order to better himself ahead of a return to the English top flight.
"I think now we are a complete coaching staff after that experience [with the United States]," Pochettino said during his pre-match press conference on Monday ahead of the second leg against Haiti.
Pochettino's argument is that because international football forces coaches to implement game plans and tactical styles with just a few training sessions every few months, they have to get more efficient. It's that efficiency that he believes makes him a better club coach, especially joining Man United midseason on a quick turnaround.
"Because before it's easy, no? From Monday to Saturday to prepare for the game. You control everything, you have control and access to everything and you can prepare. But when you have a few days to do training but you need to prepare for the way that you want to play and the way that the opponent plays the manager has to be very clinical and to find different ways to approach it with the players."
Pochettino will have to make use of these skills right away, as he is coaching his final game for the United States against Haiti on Tuesday, November 17 before flying out to England straight away and preparing Man United for their huge rivalry match on Sunday against Liverpool.
"In the last two years we have learned a lot about how to approach and create an impact in one meeting, in one exercise on the field, in one message. That's why I think now we are a very complete coaching staff. We don't say that we are going to win all the games. But I think we are much better, we are more prepared to go for the project."
How does Mauricio Pochettino fit Manchester United?
Mauricio Pochettino must prove quickly that he can join Man United and be a fixer, because the needs of this current situation seem to call for someone with a different reputation than his.
The Argentine is not known as a manager who has come into a job midseason and patched up problems quickly. Rather, his reputation is that of a project lead who can implement a long-term plan that comes to fruition in a few years.
That is not what is required at Man United right now. With the Red Devils sitting 19th in the Premier League table, he must rescue them quickly to save them from relegation. While there is still over half the season still remaining, he must reverse their course quickly to save them from continuing to spiral out of control. If Pochettino's effect takes some time to sink in, and the negative results continue for the next month, the environment will get increasingly toxic and the job could become even tougher.
Additionally, Mauricio Pochettino's press will need significant tweaking to fit the Man United squad, which not only doesn't have the personnel to play Pochettino's style, but also struggles to press in any way under Carrick. In fact, the outgoing Man United coach even criticized his team's inability to press after Bruno Fernandes's inflammatory comments last month.
"Whatever system you play, whatever tactics you play, if you don't run, and you don't run forward and you don't run back, you are not going to win," Carrick said in response to his captain's critique that they do not press man-to-man.
Pochettino, in short, has a lot of work to do. It remains to be proven whether he's actually the man for the job.
