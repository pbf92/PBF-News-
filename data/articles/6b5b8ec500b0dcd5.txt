TITLE: Why Mauricio Pochettino arrival could spell the end for Jason Wilcox at Man Utd
OUTLET: The Sun

MANCHESTER UNITED have turned to Mauricio Pochettino to rescue their disastrous season following the sacking of Michael Carrick.
The Red Devils sit 18th in the Premier League table after a dismal start to the campaign that saw standards slip at Old Trafford.
And SunSport’s Manchester United correspondent Samuel Luckhurst believes the Argentine will immediately lay down the law to an under-performing squad — though a power struggle with the hierarchy could be brewing.
Speaking on The Luckhurst View, he said: “He is someone who is going to demand a hell of a work ethic from the players.
“They’ve not worked hard enough this season. And that wasn’t entirely down to them.
“I know that a fellow journalist said recently that, ‘sacking Michael Carrick, it’d be tantamount to shooting Bambi.’

“Well, it wasn’t. It wasn’t like that whatsoever. He should have been put out of his misery a few weeks beforehand. And also he was responsible for this very lax culture that he’d allowed to creep in.
“And it’s bizarre that someone who was so successful at the club as a player allowed that to happen. And you look at the running stats, you look how low down you are. I think they rank 20th out of 20 for the athleticism of squads in the Premier League.”
Our United man thinks Carrick ultimately paid the price for his own attitude as a player.
He added: “We’ve seen it from the very start of the post-Ferguson era when David Moyes came in and said, ‘I’m going to make you run more.’ And United players, among them Michael Carrick, bristling at that constructive criticism.
“It seems like every manager since then has had an issue with them not being fit enough or not running enough.
“So for that alone, a lot of United supporters will want to get on board with Pochettino cracking the whip with them.”
While Pochettino is expected to whip the dressing room into shape, his arrival raises major questions about how he will coexist with technical director Jason Wilcox.
United have handed the former Tottenham boss the title of head coach rather than manager — a structure that has previously caused friction.
Luckhurst added: “Given his background at Tottenham, he has to be the main man. He has to be the boss.
“That’s why he only lasted a year at Chelsea with their complicated structure.
“Whereas at Tottenham, I think the sporting director or the technical director was gone a year after Pochettino left. And then they didn’t really bring anyone else in while he was there.
“So that’s going to be a fascinating dynamic to see how that plays out with Jason Wilcox, who of course is the director of football.
“I think from United fans perspective, it’s a polarising appointment because he has been in gradual decline since he didn’t get the job in 2018.
“And he didn’t really leave his imprint on PSG where he was only there for 18 months.
“The fact that he was in club football exile with the United States felt peculiar given his age going into that job at that time, even though it had the lure of taking charge of the States in a home World Cup and then of course extending his contract as well.
“So there are a lot of misgivings about him, but also clearly given the work that he has done and some of the non-negotiable aspects that he’ll impose on this team, there are elements about his coaching style that United do need, not that that’s particularly difficult given where they are in the table.”
Luckhurst thinks Pochettino and Wilcox may end up in a power struggle that only one man emerges from.
He explained: “I don’t think Pochettino is going to back down over that.
“That’s one reason why he lasted only a season at Chelsea. There were too many issues with the hierarchy. And I think they had two sporting directors at the time.
“And that’s just not what he was used to from his time at Tottenham. And he did have relative success at Tottenham. It will be a fascinating dynamic.
“And he’s there on a two-and-a-half-year contract. I would be amazed if in two and a half years time, Pochettino and Jason Wilcox are still at United.
“It already feels like something would have to give there. And most United supporters would not be sorry to see the back of Jason Wilcox, clearly.”
Despite sitting in the relegation zone ahead of a daunting trip to Liverpool, Luckhurst insists Pochettino must aim far higher than mere survival.
With 28 games remaining and the gap to the Champions League places standing at just 10 points, United still have time to salvage their campaign.
Luckhurst said: “I don’t think it’s difficult for Pochettino to just get them out of the relegation zone, but they’ve still got to be looking at trying to finish in the top four.
“They’ve got what, 28 games left. They’re 10 points off the top four.
“Given how other teams are so inconsistent, erratic. Liverpool are quite streaky, Chelsea are erratic.
“City, I think we’re all expecting them to be deducted points at some point. Well, it’s got to happen sooner rather than later, given the integrity of the league is at stake there.
“Brighton have been brilliant, but you still wonder whether they’re going to be able to keep it up for the next six months.
“So, and also I look at that United squad and as dismal as the defence is in, Carrick did not help things by playing square pegs and round holes.
“It’s not going to be difficult, I think, for Pochettino to merely get them out of the relegation zone.
“But the fact that we’re even talking about, you know, will Manchester United finish in the top 10 or where should they be aiming for is a problem in itself.
“But I maintain that in terms of the firepower on that squad and what they’ve got up top, they should still be looking to at least challenge for Champions League qualification.”
