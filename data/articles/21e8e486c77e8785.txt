TITLE: Vietnam authorities demolish historic church
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A historic church building in Vietnam has been destroyed by the authorities, adding to concerns over religious freedom in the country.
The L’Accueil building – which belongs to the Redemptorist Catholic missional community in the central city of Huế – has been the source of a long-running land dispute with the authorities.
Clergy and parishioners gathered to pray as heavy machinery surrounded the church and demolished it.
“Justice cannot be established through force, and the truth cannot be erased along with the structures that have been demolished,” said the church in a statement.
The church acquired the land in 1930 and it has since been developed into a multipurpose complex, including the hosting of educational and cultural activities.
According to historical records cited by the church, the local authorities formally recognised the congregation's ownership in 1971. While they say that some assets have been transferred to the state, ownership of the land remains unchanged.
Despite Vietnamese law stating that land is collectively owned by the people and governed by the state, the church has asked the local authorities to review historical records and disclose the written reasons for their action.
The authorities have defended the move, saying the project serves public interests. In October, a notice appeared on the property announcing plans for a public park and car park.
In its 2026 annual report, the US Commission on International Religious Freedom (USCIRF) recommended the designation of Vietnam as a Country of Particular Concern (CPC), citing systematic violations of religious freedom.
