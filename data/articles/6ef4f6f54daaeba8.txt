TITLE: Funding deadline looms for outback rescue helicopter
OUTLET: ABC News (Australia)

In short:
Jack Martin was learning to walk when he fell into a fire pit at a remote campsite.
LifeFlight South West, the service that flew him to hospital, has less than six months until its funding contract expires.
What's next?
As the only LifeFlight branch not supported by state funding, the service is calling for certainty.
Jack Martin needs surgery every year to ensure the scarred skin on his arms can grow with him.
This will be part of the six-year-old's life until he stops growing.
His mother, Olivia, said the annual procedure was also another reminder of the traumatising accident.
"He was burnt to the bone," she said.
The grazier said her son was a toddler when he fell into a campfire that appeared to have been long-extinguished while on a family camping trip at Queensland's remote Carnarvon Gorge.
"Underneath the white ash was a full fire pit of coals and it completely degloved Jack from both elbows to the tips of his fingers," Ms Martin said.
While fires are commonly banned in the national park, several campgrounds have individual fire pits.
Ms Martin said she had relived the day of Jack's accident repeatedly in her head, and she could not imagine where her family would be without the outback rescue service that flew Jack to hospital.
The family called Triple Zero (000) and held Jack's arms in two bottles of water as they waited for emergency services to arrive at the campground, a three-hour drive from the nearest hospital.
"That's not something that time can just wait for; Jack was burnt so badly," Ms Martin said.
"There was no airstrip nearby, so the Royal Flying Doctor couldn't come and get us. We needed a helicopter."
LifeFlight flew Jack to the Queensland Children's Hospital burns unit in Brisbane, where he had surgery every three days for six weeks.
"It was terrible that he was so young, but I'm also so grateful because he can't remember the pain," Ms Martin said.
The Roma-based LifeFlight South West is funded by a collection of resource companies and is now without a funding agreement beyond April next year.
Oil and gas companies started the service 15 years ago after seeing the need to reach remote workers, but they are calling for the government to help fund operations.
Living on a property north-east of Roma, Ms Martin said it was hard to fathom rural life without knowing a service like LifeFlight was there.
"I'd hate to think that based on the fact that LifeFlight might not be available, that Jack mightn't have the full use of his left hand like he does today," she said.
The LifeFlight South West branch covers an area larger than Victoria, and includes Charleville and Augathella to the west, Carnarvon Gorge to the north, and over the New South Wales border, Moree to the south.
LifeFlight South West chair Kate Scott said her branch was the only Queensland base not supported by the state government.
"I cannot imagine that amount of area and all those people and all those communities being left high and dry when you ring Triple Zero," she said.
She praised the work of paramedics and the Royal Flying Doctor Service, but added they had trouble reaching the accident without a helicopter.
In a statement, Toowoomba and Surat Basin Enterprise chief executive Jo Sheppard said the oil and gas companies were calling for government funding for the service.
She said the gas companies' reliance on the service was decreasing, but the community need was increasing.
"If the Queensland government funds the rest of the network, it is reasonable that the Queensland government also supports the service for this region," she said.
"Should this service be lost, more pressure will be placed on the [LifeFlight] service that is based out of Toowoomba, on the road ambulance network, and the communities across the region will be left in a vulnerable position."
Ms Sheppard said several oil and gas companies would consider continuing to contribute to the service at a reduced amount if the government offered funding.
A spokesperson for Minister for Health Tim Nicholls said the Queensland Ambulance Service maintained a comprehensive response capability in the region.
"Including 24/7 fixed-wing retrieval services and road ambulance operations," the statement read.
They said they were considering funding arrangements for the future of the Roma base, but that it was essential that industry funding was part of the solution.
