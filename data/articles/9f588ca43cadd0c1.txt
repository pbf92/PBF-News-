Economic calendar for crypto: bitcoin swings a fifth more on CPI days
Fed rate decisions, US inflation, the jobs report and options expiry: these dates in the economic calendar move the bitcoin price. We measured how strongly bitcoin swung on these days in 2026 and list every date through January 2027.
An economic calendar lists the dates on which central banks make decisions and statistics offices publish new data. For crypto, four of them matter most: the rate decision of the US Federal Reserve, the US inflation data (CPI), the US jobs report and the monthly options expiry. We measured how bitcoin reacted on these days in 2026: on the ten CPI dates the median daily range was 3.5 percent, against 2.9 percent on all other days of the year. The inflation data therefore bring roughly a fifth more movement into the price. The Fed days themselves, by contrast, were surprisingly calm.
This guide explains how to read an economic calendar, which dates are coming up through January 2027 and what the 2026 figures reveal about how bitcoin behaves. It deliberately gives no buy signals: a date tells you when the price is more likely to move, but not in which direction.
Economic calendar explained: date, time, forecast and actual figure
An economic calendar is a table of fixed release dates for economic data and central bank decisions. Each row usually contains five items: the date, the time, the country, the indicator and three values. These three values are the previous figure (the last release), the forecast (the consensus of the economists surveyed) and the actual figure, which appears at the stated minute.
For the price, the actual figure almost never counts on its own; what counts is how far it deviates from the forecast. Professionals call this the surprise. If inflation lands exactly on expectations, it is already priced in, and often little happens. If it deviates by one or two tenths of a point, traders shift their expectations for the next rate moves within seconds. It is precisely this rate path that decides how much money flows into risky assets such as stocks and cryptocurrencies.
Many calendars mark the importance of a date with one to three symbols. For crypto, a rough filter is enough: only high-importance US dates and the rate decisions of the Fed and the ECB are worth an entry in your own calendar. Individual sentiment indices or regional industrial data rarely move bitcoin measurably. Why crypto reacts to US data in the economic calendar
Bitcoin has no interest rate and no earnings that a central bank could influence directly. Even so, the price depends on interest rates. The reason is liquidity: when rates rise, safe assets such as government bonds and overnight deposits pay more, and interest in assets without ongoing income falls. When rates fall, the search for yield intensifies. And since exchange-traded bitcoin ETFs in the US began managing billions, the same fund managers who watch bonds and tech stocks also trade bitcoin.
The Fed matters more than the ECB here, because bitcoin is traded in dollars worldwide and most trading volume follows US market hours. For you as an investor in the euro area, there is a second layer: you count in euros. If a US release moves the dollar, the euro value of your holdings changes even when the dollar price of bitcoin stays the same.
Fed rate decision in the economic calendar: eight FOMC meetings a year, the next on December 9
The Fed's Federal Open Market Committee, the FOMC, sets the target range for the policy rate eight times a year. Each meeting lasts two days. On the second day, the statement is released at 2:00 p.m. Washington time, which in winter is 8:00 p.m. CET. Half an hour later the Fed chair's press conference begins, and it often moves the price more than the decision itself.
The dates are published well in advance in the Fed's meeting calendar. For 2026 they are January 27/28, March 17/18, April 28/29, June 16/17, July 28/29, September 15/16, October 27/28 and December 8/9. At the March, June, September and December meetings, the Fed also publishes its members' projections, including the so-called dot plot. It shows where each member sees the policy rate in the coming years and is therefore regarded as particularly market-moving.
At its last meeting on October 28, the Fed left the target range for the policy rate at 3.75 to 4.00 percent, with two dissents against ten votes in favor. How a possible rate move affects leveraged positions is something we worked through in our guide on Fed rate hikes and crypto leverage. For 2027, the Fed lists January 26/27 and March 16/17 as its first meetings.
Fed days in the 2026 economic calendar: a calm decision day, a restless day after
For the following figures we analyzed the daily candles of bitcoin against the US dollar on the Kraken exchange, each running from midnight to midnight in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). The daily range is the gap between the daily high and the daily low, divided by the opening price. It shows how far the price swings within a day, regardless of where it closes in the evening. For comparison: across all 318 days from January 1 to November 14, 2026, the median daily range was 2.85 percent and the median daily change from open to close was 1.15 percent.
The result is surprising: on the decision day itself, the median range was only 2.25 percent, below the average for the year. Only the March meeting, one of the four with projections, produced a markedly more restless day. Many traders attribute this to timing: the statement comes out in the early evening European time, and most of the reaction falls into the night and therefore into the next daily candle. Twice the following day brought a sharp decline, by 5.2 percent on January 29 and by 4.3 percent on October 29. Seven dates are a small sample, however. No rule that bitcoin falls on the day after the Fed can be derived from them.
If you hold a leveraged position, you should therefore keep an eye on the night and the following day as well as the decision day. How far the price may fall before an exchange closes a leveraged position is explained in our guide to the liquidation price in crypto.
US inflation (CPI) in the economic calendar: about a fifth more daily range for bitcoin
The Consumer Price Index (CPI) measures how much more expensive a fixed basket of goods becomes for US households. The US Department of Labor publishes it through its statistics agency, the BLS, once a month at 8:30 a.m. New York time, which in winter is 2:30 p.m. CET. Two figures get most of the attention: the headline rate and the core rate, which excludes volatile energy and food prices. The core rate is seen as a better indication of how persistent inflation is, and therefore of the Fed's next move.
On the ten CPI dates from January to November 2026, bitcoin swung more than on an ordinary day:
The median daily range on CPI days was 3.48 percent and the median daily change 1.73 percent. Both figures are clearly above the yearly averages of 2.85 and 1.15 percent. Three times bitcoin rose by more than four percent on a CPI day, in January, February and July. The direction was mixed, however: six times the price closed higher, four times lower. The CPI date is therefore a day of heightened volatility, though without a reliable direction.
The next dates according to the BLS release calendar: the November data come out on December 10, 2026, and the December data on January 13, 2027.
Jobs report in the economic calendar: what employment data change in the price
The US jobs report (Employment Situation, usually called non-farm payrolls or NFP in trading) generally comes out on the first Friday of a month, also at 2:30 p.m. CET in winter. It gives the number of new jobs created outside agriculture, the unemployment rate and the trend in hourly wages.
For the Fed, the labor market is the second pillar alongside inflation: its mandate is stable prices and maximum employment. Many new jobs and strongly rising wages argue against rate cuts, weak figures argue for them. That is why the price reacts to the jobs report by the same logic as to the CPI. Also watch for revisions: the BLS corrects the figures for the two previous months, and a large correction can overshadow the current month's actual figure.
The BLS has scheduled the November report for early December. Because the details differ between the agency's calendars, you should check the exact date in the BLS release schedule shortly beforehand.
ECB rate decision in the economic calendar: for euro investors the exchange rate counts too
The European Central Bank meets less often than the Fed, and its decision matters less for bitcoin. For you as a euro investor it still counts, because it moves the exchange rate between the euro and the dollar. The ECB Governing Council announces its decision on the second day of the meeting at 2:15 p.m. CET, and the press conference follows at 2:45 p.m. CET. According to the ECB's meeting calendar, the next monetary policy meetings are on December 16 and 17, 2026, and on February 3 and 4, 2027.
A worked example shows the effect: if the euro rises two percent against the dollar while bitcoin stays unchanged in dollars, your holdings fall by about two percent measured in euros. Deribit options expiry in the economic calendar: the last Friday of the month at 9 a.m. CET
Alongside the economic data, there is one date that matters only in the crypto market: the options expiry. An option gives its buyer the right to buy (call) or sell (put) bitcoin at a fixed price. On expiry day that right ends, and open contracts are settled. The largest exchange for crypto options, Deribit, lets its monthly options expire on the last Friday of the month at 8:00 a.m. UTC, which in winter is 9:00 a.m. CET. On the last Friday of March, June, September and December, the quarterly options also expire, and their volume is usually much larger.
Around expiry, traders watch the so-called max pain price. That is the price at which option buyers as a whole would suffer the largest loss. A widespread theory holds that the price is pulled toward this level before expiry. This has not been proven, and large traders also hedge their positions in other ways.
Our measurement for the ten monthly expiries from January to October 2026: the median daily range was 4.14 percent, even higher than on CPI days. Strikingly, bitcoin closed lower on nine of these ten Fridays. That is no basis for a trading rule, because the sample is small and the price was falling for long stretches of 2026 anyway. One example of such a date is described in our article on the options expiry on October 30.
The next monthly expiries fall on November 27, 2026, and January 29, 2027. By the calendar, the December quarterly expiry falls on December 25, which is Christmas Day. Whether Deribit moves the date because of the holiday is shown in the exchange's expiry list.
