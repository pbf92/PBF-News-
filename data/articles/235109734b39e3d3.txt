TITLE: GTA 6 review: a near-perfect masterpiece with one frustrating flaw
OUTLET: Ynetnews

This review and rating are based on nearly 50 hours of gameplay on Xbox Series X, after completing two and a half of the game's five story chapters. The review contains no major spoilers.
Wow, where do I even begin? After nearly 50 hours of playing GTA 6 (45 hours, to be precise), I've managed to complete only two of its five main chapters and reach the halfway point of the third. Not because the game is particularly difficult, and certainly not because the story doesn't interest me. It's simply incredibly difficult to get from point A to point B without discovering at least three other things I'd rather do first. Sometimes it's a particularly bizarre side mission, sometimes it's a gorgeous car I simply have to steal and sell to Moses, and sometimes I just find myself wandering around, exploring the game world and marveling at what Rockstar has spent 13 years building.
After more than a decade of waiting and expectations that seemed almost impossible to meet, it was difficult to believe Rockstar could actually deliver a game that lived up to all the hype. Yet even as someone who has been gaming for years and reviews video games and gaming hardware professionally, I wasn't prepared for what I experienced here. As far as I'm concerned, this is the game of the decade.
For those who haven't followed the frenzy surrounding its launch, the latest installment in the Grand Theft Auto series takes us back to Vice City, the fictional version of Miami, and the state of Leonida, modeled on Florida. At the center of the story are Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, a criminal couple who become entangled in a major criminal affair and are forced to rely on each other. But as always with GTA games, the main story is only a small fraction of everything the game has to offer.
Who on earth thought of all this?
Let's start with the world itself and the extraordinary attention to detail that makes it so special. When Jason or Lucia drinks from a water bottle, for example, you can actually see the water level drop. If you repaint your car at one of the familiar Pay 'n' Spray garages from previous games, the paint's finish will differ from what you'd get at a professional vehicle customization shop. Even the fabric of your character's shirt moves naturally with their body, and if you spend enough time running around in a tank top, you'll see actual sweat stains appear. Now try to imagine how many details like these are scattered throughout GTA 6's enormous world, and how much work went into them. After all, none of us would have complained if the water level inside Jason's bottle remained unchanged while he drank from it. Most players probably won't even notice.
Anyone who played Red Dead Redemption 2 is already familiar with Rockstar's obsession with detail. But even compared with that 2018 game, which still looks better than quite a few recent releases, GTA 6 represents an enormous leap forward. Lighting, reflections, material textures, facial expressions, body movements and even the characters' hair look remarkably natural. It's the closest thing to reality I've ever experienced in a virtual world.
Non-player characters (NPCs) also behave more convincingly than ever. They have conversations with one another, go about their business and react to what's happening around them. They're no longer simply extras placed there to fill the sidewalks. Try robbing them, for example, and each will react differently, at least to a certain extent. Ultimately, there are still recurring behavioral patterns. You can stand on a street corner, do absolutely nothing and simply watch everything happening around you. GTA 6's world doesn't need you to make it interesting. And the more you explore, the more references, secrets and internet jokes you'll discover scattered throughout it.
Miami on steroids
Twenty-four years have passed since the release of GTA: Vice City, one of the series' most iconic and beloved installments. For those who grew up playing it, returning to the legendary city is an experience in itself. The new Vice City is essentially Miami on steroids, and despite the enormous differences between the two games, Rockstar has managed to preserve the atmosphere of the original and make you feel as though you're returning somewhere you've already visited. The beaches, palm trees, neon signs, nightlife, luxury cars and ostentatious wealth existing alongside poverty and crime make Vice City's identity unmistakable, even now that it is no longer stuck in the 1980s. There's even one side mission that will transport longtime fans of the series straight back to the original game. I won't spoil anything beyond that, but it's definitely worth finding.
Outside the city, you'll discover swamplands and small towns populated by Southern American rednecks, those stereotypical rural, gun-loving characters who have long been caricatured in American popular culture. There are also marinas and country roads, each offering an atmosphere entirely different from that of the big city.
As in previous installments, Rockstar isn't afraid to mock the more ridiculous aspects of American society. In the years since the previous game was released in 2013, reality has provided the developers with plenty of new material, from influencer culture and social media obsession to all manner of bizarre internet phenomena. Just open Jason or Lucia's smartphone and browse Snapmatic, the game's satirical version of Instagram, to understand how enthusiastically Rockstar takes aim at the influencer culture that has become such an integral part of our lives. Some of the content is genuinely hilarious.
One of my frustrations with many open-world games released in recent years is that their maps keep getting bigger, but after just a few hours, you already know what awaits you at virtually every marked location: the same missions, the same rewards and the same enemies with only cosmetic variations. Anyone familiar with the recent Assassin's Creed games knows exactly what I'm talking about. In GTA 6, however, it's genuinely worth venturing off the beaten path and exploring your surroundings, because the amount of content Rockstar has scattered throughout Leonida is simply staggering. Alongside hilarious and utterly bizarre side missions, there are countless luxury vehicles to acquire and customize, activities, secrets, bonuses and tributes to cultural icons that you won't want to miss. Much of this content will go completely unnoticed if you focus exclusively on the main story missions.
There are several discoveries I'm desperate to share in this review, but a large part of the enjoyment comes from finding them yourself, and I'd rather leave that experience to you. I'd also recommend watching as few YouTube and Instagram videos as possible to avoid spoilers. After waiting so many years for this game, why rush to finish it?
GTA has grown up, just like us
One criticism that has emerged since GTA 6's release concerns its story and the relatively slow pace of its opening chapters. I understand where that criticism comes from, but at least as far as I've progressed, I consider it the most mature and believable story in the series' history. Anyone expecting GTA 6 to be another version of GTA 5 with better graphics may be disappointed, but I'm glad Rockstar didn't take that approach. Many of us were teenagers when the previous game came out in 2013. Today, we're building careers, getting married and, in some cases, raising children. We've grown up, and Rockstar seems to have decided it was time for GTA to grow up with us.
Much of GTA 5's charm came from the chaotic dynamic between Michael, Franklin and Trevor, three completely different individuals forced to work together. This time, Rockstar has built its story around a couple who must confront the consequences of their criminal lifestyle while trying to maintain a normal relationship, or at least as normal a relationship as anyone can have in the world of GTA. Jason and Lucia, as well as the characters surrounding them, have depth, personality and clearly defined motivations. You can understand why they behave the way they do, even when it's painfully obvious they're making terrible decisions. The game is still crude, violent and outrageous, exactly as you'd expect from GTA, but this time I genuinely care about what happens to the relationship between its two criminals.
As a longtime female fan of the series, I'm also delighted that we finally have a woman as a main protagonist in one of its 3D installments. More importantly, Lucia is far more than a box-ticking exercise. She has her own personality, ambitions, vulnerabilities and a central role in the story, and she's every bit as interesting as Jason.
One innovation I particularly enjoyed is the ability to shape the relationship between the two protagonists. They can spend time together, go on dates, have conversations and decide how much effort to invest in their relationship. The relationship system has four different levels, with the highest called Hot Together. At least from my experience so far, it's fairly clear that this is also the relationship level Rockstar wants players to achieve, as it influences interactions between the two characters, unlocks special scenes and affects how the story subsequently unfolds. So yes, in a game where we've spent years primarily stealing cars, engaging in police chases and settling scores, we suddenly have to work on our relationship too. Who would have thought?
Learning to drive again
Anyone who spent considerable time playing GTA 5 will notice the gameplay improvements the moment they get behind the wheel. The driving model falls somewhere between GTA 4's heavier physics and GTA 5's lighter, more forgiving handling. Vehicles have a much more convincing sense of weight, and the differences between driving a sports car, an old pickup truck and an SUV are immediately apparent. The improved physics are also evident in traffic accidents, vehicle damage and the way characters interact with their surroundings. Collisions between objects in the game world feel much less like predetermined animations.
The shooting mechanics, which have not always been Rockstar's strongest feature, are also considerably better this time. Character movement during combat, aiming and shooting animations feel more natural, while weapons have a more convincing sense of weight than before. The police are also much less predictable. For those unfamiliar with the series, the number of stars displayed during a police chase indicates how wanted the player is and the intensity of the police response. In GTA 6, police behavior is more dynamic, making pursuits considerably more believable and challenging than in previous installments. I'll admit there was one point when the chase became so intense that I simply gave up rather than keep trying to escape.
All this realism comes at a price, of course. Rockstar has packed so many possibilities into the game that sometimes it feels as though there are more actions to perform than buttons on the controller. Some everyday activities require more effort than before, and the elaborate animations occasionally come at the expense of how quickly you can perform them. Players who enjoyed GTA 5's fast-paced, arcade-style gameplay, which prioritized action and accessibility over precision and realism, may find some of these changes irritating. Personally, I like the direction Rockstar has taken, although I can certainly understand those who prefer the previous approach.
Don't forget to turn on the radio
One of the things I've always loved about GTA games is their radio stations. As a teenager, they introduced me to songs and bands I might never otherwise have discovered. Rockstar has outdone itself in this department as well. The musical selections and collaborations with various bands and artists are exceptional, giving you plenty of reasons to extend a drive simply to finish listening to whatever song is playing.
