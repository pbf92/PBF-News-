TITLE: I played GTA 6 on a launch day PS5 and one crash almost bricked my console
OUTLET: GAMINGbible

Now that GTA VI is finally out, many are wondering whether you need a PlayStation 5 Pro to get the most out of it. After reviewing the game on the launch-day PlayStation 5 console, I’ve got the definitive answer.
GTA VI targets 30fps on all platforms. From the Xbox Series S to PlayStation 5 Pro, 30fps is the absolute best you’re going to get, and Rockstar Games has said it doesn’t plan on boosting it any further.
After sinking over 70 hours into my review playthrough, I can understand why. Leonida is so densely packed with content that 60fps would easily be too much for most hardware. Maybe the PC port will have the edge consoles don’t.
Rest assured though, your base PlayStation 5 will be more than enough. Throughout the review period, there were only a handful of times where I noticed the framerate dropping below 30fps, and even then I don’t think it dipped below 25.

The dips were noticeable for sure, but they were so infrequent they barely made up 1% of my playthrough.
Obviously, this will differ from console to console. I regularly clean my PlayStation 5 due to how much dust can build up inside, so while it’s not in pristine condition, it’s at least well-maintained.
However, even that didn’t prevent the game from crashing at least once. What’s more scary, though, is how it almost took my console with it…

The PS5 crash that nearly cancelled my GTA 6 review
When the crash occurred, I was around 40 hours in. I also wasn’t doing anything in the game. I was just jogging around Vice City as Lucia – checking out the shops and waiting for one of the next story missions to unlock.
Jason had some side-missions to complete though, so I thought I’d switch over to him and wrap those up before continuing the main story.
As the camera panned up and slid over to Jason’s current whereabouts, however, the game closed, and before I could register what was happening, my screen went black.
The PlayStation 5 was still switched on, though. My controller stopped working, and my attempts to perform a hard reset with the console’s power button were failing as well.
In the end, I had to manually unplug the console as it just refused to power down, and after leaving it for five minutes and plugging it back in, my anxiety turned to panic when it wouldn’t turn back on.
By this point, it was too late in the evening to start troubleshooting, so I instead chose to ignore the problem entirely, go to bed, and hope it’d resolve itself by the morning. Thankfully, when I woke up the next day and tried booting up the console for a second time, it worked. It had to repair the internal storage, but there was no lasting damage to its data.
As scary as this was, it’s never happened again. I’ve also got no idea how it happened in the first place.
It’s probably been patched since, but just in case, I’d advise all players to save often – especially if they don’t trust the game’s autosave.
Aside from this major hiccup and a few minor ones, my time with GTA VI has been everything I’d dreamed it would be, even on a base PlayStation 5.
While the PlayStation 5 Pro will still be the better console in terms of ray-tracing and loading times, no one is encouraged to upgrade their console. Your base PlayStation 5 will do just fine
If you’re dead-set on upgrading, though, now’s the right time with all the Black Friday deals available now.
