TITLE: 'Bordering on blasphemous': Brand accuser responds to call for prayer behind bars
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Russell Brand’s comments protesting his innocence from behind bars is an insult to “real Christians”, according to a woman who has waived her right to anonymity to allege she was the victim of his sex crimes.
Mia Latter, now 37, claims she was raped by the former comedian when she was 16 and he was 30. He denies the allegation.
Brand is serving a ten and a half year prison sentence, having been found guilty of rape and three counts of sexual assaults after a trial which ended earlier this month.
In a message read by his wife on their YouTube channel on Sunday, Brand said: “Beloved brothers and sisters in Christ, God bless you for your support. As you know, I am not guilty for the offences I have been jailed for, so something strange is happening. The good news is that Christ is with us when we choose him, and I do.”
Speaking on Sky News, Latter said: “His version of Christianity is an insult to real Christians.”
“I was shocked that he was, during the trial and even from prison, has been making these messages with religious overtones and Christianity being the message, whilst in the middle of that doing ad breaks for gambling sites. It all seems to just be a giant hypocrisy, and bordering on blasphemous.”
Sky's Sarah-Jane Mee asks Latter: “When a convicted rapist is platformed and prayed for, what do survivors hear?”
Latter replied: “It’s an insult to the victims. It takes so much to get to trial. It takes months and months and months, and it requires so much of your personal life being ransacked. Your family members, your friends, colleagues, people you haven’t spoken to for years and years, being interviewed. You, in some cases, have to give over medical records. Then to get a jury to listen to you and a jury to believe you, it’s an insult to the jury, an insult to the justice system, an insult to the victims.
“Him saying that he wants his family to be prayed for, well, all of these women in this case, they’re someone’s child, they’re someone’s mother, they’re someone’s sister. They’re the ones that deserve prayers, not him,” she added.
Number Ten has called the letter “clearly appalling” and an “urgent investigation” has been launched by prison authorities in response.
A Downing Street spokesman said: “We expect action to be taken if he’s found to have broken the rules. Prisoners are strictly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims and speaking to the media is only permitted in exceptional circumstances. Prisoners cannot get around these rules by having someone else publish their comments.”
