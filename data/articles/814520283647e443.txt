TITLE: Thousands regain water supply after major outage
OUTLET: BBC News

- Published
Most residents in Stourbridge, in the West Midlands, should start to see their water supply return to normal after a major outage, according to Severn Trent.
The firm said it expected customers to be back on supply during Wednesday evening.
"We have had a huge number of specialist teams working hard in the area, who have been moving water through the network and using a fleet of over 30 tankers to restore supplies," the firm said.
It comes after thousands of properties were without water on Wednesday morning as teams worked to locate a burst pipe.
"We're thankful to the local community who have been supportive, unbelievably understanding and patient given the circumstances," a Severn Trent spokesperson added.
"Our priority will now be making sure the last few remaining customers are also back on as quickly as possible."
Ridgewood High School and Ashwood Park Primary School remained closed on Wednesday, and residents said they were unable to cook, wash or flush their toilets.
Emergency bottled water stations were operating since 08:00 GMT, although supplies temporarily ran out at one site before a fresh delivery was arranged.
On Wednesday morning, it was reported that 6,000 homes and businesses were without water, but by lunchtime that had risen to 8,500 as engineers attempted to find the source of the leak.
Earlier, Howard Perry, head of water quality at Severn Trent had warned some residents might be without water for four days.
"We are very hopeful we can get customers back on supply before that," he said.
One resident said she had a three-week-old baby and was concerned when supplies of bottled water temporarily ran out.
She said she used a two-and-a-half-litre bottle just to clean and sterilise baby bottles.
"They've delivered water to the vulnerable, and they've been pinched, people are taking it off the doorstep," she added.
Emergency bottled water stations were set up at Sainsbury's car park on Sandringham Way in Amblecote and at King George V Park on Lawnswood Road in Wordsley.
The scale of the disruption was clear, as residents queued for bottled water.
Customers were allowed to take up to 24 litres of water each, but the limit was later reduced to 12 litres per person as demand increased.
There was a constant stream of residents arriving throughout Wednesday morning.
Motorists faced lengthy delays just to reach the distribution point.
One resident said it took about 40 minutes to get close enough to turn into the Sainsbury's car park, leading some drivers to abandon their cars on nearby residential streets and continue on foot.
Another resident, caring for young children, said the lack of updates from the water supplier were concerning.
"We've had no updates from Severn Trent at all about what's going on," she said.
"I've got two children and we've got no water today at the moment at all.
"You turn your taps on, nothing comes out."
At the water station, residents expressed mixed views about the response to the water supply problems.
Some praised Severn Trent staff, saying crews worked hard in difficult conditions to restore supplies and support affected communities.
Others questioned whether the amount of water being distributed would be sufficient, particularly if repairs continued into a second day. There were also concerns about how long supplies at the distribution point would last.
Keith Hunter said the situation had left him unable to "cook dinner or flush my toilet".
"These companies charge more and more money every year, but the service doesn't get better."
Resident Chris Clinton doubted the six two-litre bottles of water would last until the issue was resolved.
"This is horrendous because there's no water at all, so you can't wash. You can't put anything on or do anything, it's just terrible," he said.
"For the amount of people that it affects, that's just not going to be good enough.
"They need tankers, people need to have a water supply."
Marion Thompson said the closed schools and queuing for water made her think of the early Covid-19 pandemic.
"The school by us is shut again so kids can't go to school, so it's like everything's happening at the same time."
