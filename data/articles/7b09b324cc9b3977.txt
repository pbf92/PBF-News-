TITLE: Nvidia Reportedly Uninterested in Bringing Jensen Huang Back as CEO
OUTLET: VGC / IGN

Nvidia is reportedly not entertaining the idea of bringing Jensen Huang back as CEO following rumors that his allies had established a "war room" to push for his reinstatement.
The latest turn in the tech giant's whirlwind week arrived yesterday, when the Financial Times reported that the company's ex-boss had supported efforts from his friends in Silicon Valley to see him brought back into the fold. One source said these allies had assembled a "war room" to lay the foundation for his return, but it now seems those efforts may have been for naught.
The Nvidia board of directors is not considering reinstating Huang as CEO after he resigned from his position Thursday, November 12, one source told CNBC. Instead, company leadership is focused on the ongoing investigation launched after Huang confirmed he had an affair with a woman whom members of the U.S. government believed to be a Chinese spy on November 8.
The report states Nvidia hired law firm Wachtell Lipton to look into his relationship with the individual and how it may have compromised company secrets and/or national security. A message from the board added it had "no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised" when announcing the investigation last week.
One of the supposed goals of Huang's secret war room of Silicon Valley supporters was to gain White House support for the reinstatement – and it's a plan that seems to have panned out. U.S. President Donald Trump told reporters today (via Bloomberg) that he "would not mind" seeing the embattled tech leader return.
"I would not mind. I would not mind that," he said. "I threw out the name of Tim Cook, but I would say that Jensen's reinstatement… I think he learned a big lesson. I guess maybe one lesson's more important than another, but I would certainly not mind seeing that."
Huang and Nvidia are now trying to pick up the pieces after the investigation began last week. The story so far has seen tech billionaire Elon Musk publicly back Huang, with some Nvidia employees saying they'd like him reinstated, too. Additional reports have said the former CEO had additional affairs with not one but two of his subordinates.
