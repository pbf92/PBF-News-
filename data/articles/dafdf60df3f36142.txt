TITLE: Cyber Frauds through Google and Facebook Ads: How Online Advertisements are becoming a Tool for Scammers
OUTLET: Cybersecurity Insiders

Online advertisements have become an integral part of our digital lives, helping businesses promote products, services, and investment opportunities to millions of users. However, cyber-criminals are increasingly exploiting advertising platforms such as Google and Facebook to deceive unsuspecting users and carry out financial fraud. What appears to be a genuine advertisement from a reputed company or financial institution may sometimes be a carefully designed trap to steal money, personal information, and banking credentials.
One of the growing concerns is the misuse of sponsored advertisements to promote fake investment schemes, fraudulent shopping websites, online lending applications, and phishing links. Scammers create advertisements that closely resemble legitimate brands, banks, government schemes, and financial service providers. By using attractive offers, unbelievable discounts, and promises of exceptionally high investment returns, they encourage users to click on malicious links and share sensitive information.
Fake Investment Schemes and Financial Frauds
Investment-related frauds are among the common techniques used by cybercriminals. Fraudsters publish advertisements featuring photographs and videos of well-known personalities, business leaders, and celebrities to make their schemes appear trustworthy. In some cases, artificial intelligence and deepfake technology are used to create convincing promotional videos that falsely suggest celebrity or government endorsement.
Once users click on these advertisements, they are redirected to fraudulent websites or WhatsApp groups where they are persuaded to invest money. Initially, victims may even receive small returns to build confidence. However, when they invest larger amounts, scammers disappear with their money.
Malicious Applications and Phishing Advertisements
Another growing threat involves advertisements promoting fake mobile applications. Users may be encouraged to download applications promising entertainment, attractive deals, quick loans, or additional income. These applications can contain malware capable of accessing sensitive information stored on smartphones.
Similarly, phishing advertisements redirect users to websites that imitate the login pages of banks, payment platforms, and popular online services. Once victims enter their passwords, OTPs, card details, or other confidential information, hackers can misuse those credentials to gain unauthorized access to financial accounts.
US authorities have also raised concerns about malicious applications promoted through Facebook and Instagram advertisements. Reports have highlighted cases involving applications designed to steal banking credentials and facilitate unauthorized financial transactions.
Why are these Frauds difficult to Identify?
The biggest challenge is that fraudulent advertisements can appear alongside genuine sponsored content. Users often assume that advertisements displayed on popular platforms have been thoroughly verified. However, Google’s advertising policies prohibit phishing and deceptive practices, and Meta also maintains policies against fraudulent advertisements. Despite these safeguards, malicious campaigns can sometimes evade detection and reach users before being removed.
How can Users Protect Themselves?
Users should avoid clicking on advertisements promising guaranteed investment returns, unrealistic discounts, or instant loans without proper verification. Always visit the official website of a company instead of relying on advertising links. Never share OTPs, banking passwords, UPI PINs, or confidential financial information with unknown individuals.
Additionally, avoid downloading APK files from unfamiliar websites and carefully review application permissions before installation. Suspicious advertisements should be reported to the respective platforms.
If someone becomes a victim of financial cyber fraud in USA, they should immediately contact the national cybercrime helpline or report the incident through the official Internet Crime Complaint Centre
As digital advertising continues to expand, cyber-criminals are finding new ways to exploit online platforms. Therefore, users must understand that an advertisement appearing on Google or Facebook is not automatically a guarantee of authenticity. Digital awareness, careful verification, and responsible online behavior remain essential to preventing cyber fraud.
