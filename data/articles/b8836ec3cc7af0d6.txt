TITLE: State approves $50 million for behavioral health expansion
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

The California Department of Health Care Services (DHCS) has approved a $50 million Behavioral Health Continuum Infrastructure Program (BHCIP) award to Palomar UC San Diego Health and UC San Diego Health to expand access to mental health and substance use disorder treatment across the region.
The funding supports three complementary projects across the two health systems designed to create a sustainable continuum of behavioral health services and increase access to care for residents in surrounding communities.
The award reflects a collaborative effort among Palomar UC San Diego Health, UC San Diego Health, San Diego County Behavioral Health Services and DHCS to address growing behavioral health needs throughout the region.
“This investment represents an important opportunity to expand behavioral health care throughout the region,” said Patty Maysent, CEO of UC San Diego Health. “Our collaboration allows us to grow services across multiple care settings, connect more patients to the care they need and strengthen the behavioral health infrastructure our communities will rely on for years to come.”
The approach is designed to help more patients receive services closer to home, reduce barriers to treatment and improve coordination between crisis, inpatient and outpatient care settings.
“This funding supports a sustainable strategy for expanding behavioral health care resources,” said Diane Hansen, CEO of Palomar UC San Diego Health. “The investments will strengthen access to crucial services and help create a more connected network of care for patients and families.”
The $50 million award supports three projects that will expand services while broadening a coordinated network that connects patients to a full spectrum of robust mental health and substance use services:
- Palomar UC San Diego Health Escondido: Development of a new comprehensive behavioral health outpatient pavilion, which will include a 16-bed crisis stabilization unit for immediate intervention; 20 partial hospitalization and 20 intensive outpatient spaces to treat substance use disorder; 180 community mental health center slots for therapy and psychiatric care, and advanced interventional treatments, including transcranial magnetic stimulation and ketamine therapy. The site will serve a wide age range, from children to older adults.
- Palomar UC San Diego Health Poway: A repurposed 12-bed inpatient psychiatric unit for patients age 15 and older. In addition, separate investments from the BHCIP grant will support a newly established eating disorders inpatient unit, the first of its kind in the region.
- East Campus Medical Center at UC San Diego Health: Twenty new inpatient beds for ages 18 and older, complementing planned partial hospitalization programming, interventional psychiatry services and behavioral health workforce development initiatives.
The initiative will launch with Palomar UC San Diego Health Escondido, while the Palomar UC San Diego Health Poway and East Campus Medical Center projects are expected to be funded by early 2027. New behavioral health services are expected to come online in phases across the three locations between 2027 and 2029.
Distributing care across three projects allows patients to move through a more robust continuum of care, according to Steve Koh, MD, MPH, MBA, psychiatrist-in-chief and associate chief medical officer for behavioral health at UC San Diego Health.
“Behavioral health treatment isn’t at a single touchpoint, but rather a series of services that helps patients when and where needed. Patients need to be able to move through a full spectrum of care that is available across the region,” Koh said. “Crisis stabilization, inpatient care, step-down programming, outpatient treatment — when all of those resources live across a connected network, patients get matched to the right level of care at the right location, which helps prevent a crisis situation requiring emergent care. That’s a meaningful step forward for patients, families and communities throughout the region.”
The projects will also support workforce development and clinical training opportunities in behavioral health, helping address longstanding provider shortages while expanding access to specialized expertise throughout the region.
About UC San Diego Health
UC San Diego Health, the region's only academic health system, is dedicated to delivering outstanding patient care through commitment to community, groundbreaking research and inspired teaching. For 2026-27, U.S. News & World Report ranked UC San Diego Health as the best hospital system in San Diego and among the nation's best in eight adult medical and surgical specialties, including cancer, cardiology, geriatrics, neurology/neurosurgery and pulmonology. It is the first health system in San Diego County to make the national Honor Roll (top 20) for outstanding patient care. The 1,011-bed health system includes three hospitals — Hillcrest Medical Center, Jacobs Medical Center and East Campus Medical Center — as well as Sulpizio Cardiovascular Center, Moores Cancer Center, Shiley Eye Institute, Koman Family Outpatient Pavilion, McGrath Outpatient Pavilion, and Altman Clinical and Translational Research Institute. The health system also includes primary care and same-day services at clinics throughout Southern California.
About Palomar UC San Diego Health
Palomar UC San Diego Health is a new regional health system dedicated to providing exceptional, patient-centered care for the communities of North San Diego County and the surrounding regions. Formed through a Joint Powers Authority between Palomar Health and UC San Diego Health on July 1, 2026, the health system brings together the strengths of a public community health system and a nationally ranked academic health system to expand access to specialized care, innovative treatments and leading physicians. The health system is committed to delivering compassionate, high-quality care while investing in the future health of the region.
