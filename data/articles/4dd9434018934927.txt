TITLE: Guernsey Police warn parents of GTA 6 impact on children
OUTLET: BBC

Police warn parents of GTA 6 impact on children
Parents who want to play Grand Theft Auto 6 are being urged by police in Guernsey to consider the impact of the game's content on their children.
Rockstar's action-adventure game releases on Thursday, marking 13 years since the last edition in 2013, which went on to sell nearly 230m copies.
The game, which allows players to explore vast open worlds, has been controversial in the past around its depictions of graphic sexual content, violence and drug use.
Guernsey Police said adults who wanted to play the game with their children should be careful of being "blinded by nostalgia", and should know the new game was "so much bigger and so much more realistic".
It is set in Leonida, a fictional US state heavily based on Florida, and the game's map is reportedly twice the size of Grand Theft Auto V.
But Laura Simpson, digital safety lead for Guernsey Police, said the way the game presented themes such as relationships could be "damaging".
Simpson said: "There is a lot of blood and gore, but also things such as decapitations and unhealthy relationships.
"With stuff like that people go 'oh yeah', but it's some of those depictions that can be the most damaging.
"If they are learning [from GTA 6] what a relationship could look like then some of these ideas can be more damaging to their future partnerships."
The game is rated 18-plus, but Simpson said Guernsey Police was "realistic" about children seeing the game played via online streaming or their parents.
"If people are making that choice, don't be blinded by nostalgia," Simpson said.
"Your memory of how you felt playing it is not necessarily how your kid is going to feel playing this new iteration, because it is so, so much bigger and so much more realistic."
She said people were "triggered, and hurt, and upset by different things".
"Something that could be a paper-cut kind of harm to one kid could turn into a lifelong trauma for another, and we don't know.
"The people who know these things are likely to be parents, and they know their kids better than anyone."
