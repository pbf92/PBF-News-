TITLE: Manchester United make crucial transfer decision ahead of January window
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

Manchester United prioritise January striker as Mauricio Pochettino seeks attacking reinforcements
Manchester United will make signing a striker their January transfer priority as Mauricio Pochettino attempts to lift the club out of the Premier League relegation zone.
According to Charlotte Duncker's report for The Times, the new head coach and United's board agree that reinforcements are required after a start that has delivered one victory from 11 league matches.
Sunday's 4-0 defeat at Liverpool exposed familiar defensive weaknesses. However, with Benjamin Sesko unavailable until January at the earliest, United also face a pressing problem at the other end of the pitch.
Photo: IMAGO
Sesko absence leaves Pochettino short of options
Sesko's shin injury has restricted him to two starts this season, denying United regular access to the player recruited to lead their attack.
The 23-year-old has scored 14 goals in 38 appearances since his £73.7 million arrival from RB Leipzig. His strong finish to last season offered encouragement, although Michael Carrick frequently preferred Bryan Mbeumo or Matheus Cunha through the middle.
Pochettino must now find a reliable approach while waiting for Sesko to recover.
Joshua Zirkzee provides another option, but The Times describes him as better suited to playing behind a striker than leading the line. His return of 11 goals in 86 United appearances also illustrates why the club are considering additional firepower.
The issue is therefore about both availability and suitability. United have attacking players, but fewer convincing options for the specific role Pochettino needs to fill.
Balogun connection offers potential route
Folarin Balogun is among the possibilities outlined in the report.
The Monaco forward worked with Pochettino during the Argentine's spell as United States head coach and, according to The Times, would welcome a reunion.
Balogun has also made his desire to move clear after coming close to joining Everton during the summer.
"I'm sure in the upcoming transfer windows, I'll look to leave again," he said.
"I think my point of view was very clear."
Those comments establish his willingness to consider an exit. They do not confirm that United have submitted an offer or that Monaco have agreed to negotiate a January departure.
The existing relationship with Pochettino could nevertheless make him an interesting option. The manager already knows his game, while the player understands the demands of working under him.
United have several weeks to assess whether that familiarity can translate into a feasible deal.
Left-back recruitment remains possible
A striker may be the priority, but United could also look to strengthen at left-back.
Pochettino admires Luke Shaw, whom he coached at Southampton. The defender's availability remains a concern, however, after missing United's past eight matches.
The Times reports that Alejandro Balde wants to leave Barcelona during the winter window after failing to start a game this season. United considered him in the summer, with Barcelona seeking £25 million for the 23-year-old.
That makes him another situation to monitor rather than a confirmed signing.
United's recruitment choices will also reflect how the hierarchy assesses the existing defence. Speaking to Sky Sports before the Liverpool defeat, chief executive Omar Berrada defended the depth available.
"There's a lot of discussion about the back line but we did come into it thinking we had depth, we have nine players that can play in the back line," he said.
Having numbers available and finding a settled, effective combination remain different challenges.
Immediate fixtures leave no room to wait
January offers an opportunity to improve the squad, but Pochettino needs results before the window opens.
United face Sporting Lisbon in the Champions League before hosting Brentford. The report also states that they have notified the FA of their intention to appeal against Leny Yoro's red card at Anfield.
If the suspension stands, Yoro will miss the Brentford fixture, adding another selection complication.
Changes are also taking place behind the scenes. Strength and conditioning coach Silvia Tuya is due to join Pochettino's staff once she receives a work permit, while former Arsenal fitness coach Sam Wilson has been appointed.
Those additions form part of the wider work required to improve performance and availability. Recruitment alone will not solve United's problems.
For Pochettino, the immediate task is to make better use of the players he has. The January striker search reflects an acknowledgement that, in one important position, the club believe he also needs more help.
