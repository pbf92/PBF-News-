Bolton shop licence under review after police allege ‘sustained pattern of criminal conduct’
Police said the Tias Wines premises in Bolton was suspected of being associated with 'counterfeit tobacco, non-compliant vapes, nitrous oxide, suspected medicines offences and potential proxy sales to children'
An-off licence subject to a closure notice is to have its licence reviewed after police alleged an ‘ongoing pattern of criminality’. Greater Manchester Police has requested the council revokes the licence of Tias Wines on Blackburn Road, Astley Bridge, Bolton.
Town hall documents published ahead of the review state the premises is the subject of a closure notice until December 3, which forbids anyone from entering the shop apart from the licence holder, officers from the council and police.

A report to be put in front of a licensing panel considering the application reads: “On September 4, the premises was made subject to a closure order due to illicit activity taking place at the premises.
“The order was granted by the court following consideration of evidence submitted by both the police neighbourhood team and trading standards.”
The shop currently has a licence to sell alcohol from Monday – Sunday from 8.30am– 2.30am.
PC Ben Brookfield has requested the review on behalf of the Chief Constable. His written submission to the panel reads: “The evidence demonstrates a persistent and ongoing pattern of criminality associated with the premises known as Tia Wines.”
His statement said that in February, 2025, a test purchase was made at the premises when a buyer successfully obtained two packets of 20 Lambert & Butler cigarettes for £12.
PC Brookfield said examination of the products confirmed them to be counterfeit.
He said a subsequent joint visit by Bolton trading standards and GMP on March 10, 2025 identified further concerns.
Officers discovered 20 packets of illicit tobacco, nine non-compliant vapes, nine counterfeit earphones and two large canisters of nitrous oxide concealed in bags beneath a plastic bread tray in the rear ginnel area directly associated with the premises, the PC added.
PC Brookfield said further intelligence received in June 2025 alleged that individuals associated with the premises were involved in the sale of counterfeit cigarettes and nitrous oxide, with vehicles in the vicinity reportedly being used to store and supply illegal products.
He said this intelligence was consistent with the findings made during enforcement activity.
A further visit on in January, 2026 resulted in the seizure of 26 non-compliant disposable vapes, six non-compliant NUSUK branded deodorants and three suspected counterfeit cigarette sticks located beneath the till.
The police evidence states that recorded intelligence from February 2026 states that staff at Tia Wines were selling illegal cigarettes and nitrous oxide.
PC Brookfield said the information suggested that illicit products were being stored within vehicles parked nearby and retrieved by staff when customers requested them.
He added that further intelligence was received in June which alleged that the premises was selling individual cigarettes to children aged 14 years, supplying illicit tobacco and nicotine products, and storing significant quantities of nitrous oxide at the rear of the premises.
On July 23, GMP said a further test purchase was conducted.
Once again, two packets of 20 Lambert & Butler cigarettes were purchased for £12. Examination confirmed the products were counterfeit.
The evidence as a whole was used to successfully apply for the closure order currently in effect.
PC Brookfield said: “Taken collectively, the evidence establishes a sustained pattern of criminal conduct involving counterfeit tobacco, illicit tobacco products, non-compliant vaping products, nitrous oxide, suspected medicines offences and potential proxy sales to children.

“Repeated enforcement action, intelligence development, product seizures and changes in management have failed to bring about any meaningful improvement in compliance.
“It is therefore the opinion of GMP that there is compelling evidence that the premises has been, and continues to be, associated with serious criminality.”
The panel will also consider evidence from a neighbour of the shop.
In a statement published by Bolton council, they said: “People regularly come outside the premises late at night and sound their car horns and play loud music.
“Groups of people gather outside, and vehicles are constantly coming and going. “On occasions, people have sounded their horns to attract attention, after which what appeared to be nitrous oxide (laughing gas) was brought out from the premises and taken to vehicles.”
Bolton trading standards have also backed the application to revoke the shop’s licence.
They said: “Given the enforcement history of the premises and its association with the availability of illicit and illegal products, there is a material concern that customers may return expecting such products to remain available when the premises reopens.”
The licence review is set to take place on Tuesday, November 24.
