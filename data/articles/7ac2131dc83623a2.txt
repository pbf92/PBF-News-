TITLE: Instagram’s Amazon play could reshape creator commerce as Meta eyes a bigger share of advertising budgets
OUTLET: Storyboard18

Instagram’s latest Amazon affiliate integration could be about much more than helping creators earn commissions. By allowing creators to embed shoppable product links directly into their content without taking a share of their earnings, the Meta-owned platform is positioning itself to capture a larger role in the consumer purchase journey, potentially strengthening its appeal to advertisers while challenging parts of the existing affiliate marketing ecosystem.
The integration allows creators to connect their Amazon Influencer accounts and add affiliate product links to Reels, feed posts and carousels, enabling them to earn commissions on qualifying purchases.
While Amazon continues to handle transactions, Instagram is making a play for the stage that precedes them: product discovery, recommendations and purchase intent.
Industry executives believe the move reflects a broader strategy to bring creator monetisation and commerce closer to Instagram's advertising ecosystem, rather than build a standalone transaction business.
"Instagram doesn't necessarily need to own the transaction, it wants to own product discovery. Amazon can handle checkout, while Instagram becomes the place where consumers discover what they eventually buy," said Anirudh Sridharan, Co-founder of HashFame, a brand-creator network app.
He added that making monetisation easier without taking a cut also gives creators another reason to build their businesses on Instagram, potentially allowing the platform to capture a larger share of the attention and advertising budgets associated with commerce.
Why Instagram can afford to skip affiliate commissions
Unlike affiliate platforms that earn from facilitating transactions, Instagram's established advertising business gives it a different commercial incentive.
Saurabh Sankpal, Co-founder and CCO at Re. Awakening Collective, believes the integration could help Meta strengthen the connection between creator content and advertising outcomes.
"Instagram's money is in ads, so it can skip the commission. Every purchase traced back to a Reel tells Meta which content, which creator and which audience led to a sale. That data makes its ad targeting stronger, and better targeting is what pulls brand budgets," Sankpal said.
The strategic opportunity, according to executives, lies in understanding which creators, content formats and audiences influence shopping decisions.
Kalyan Kumar, Co-founder and CEO of KlugKlug, said Instagram's decision not to charge commissions should not be mistaken for an absence of commercial value.
"Instagram saying it doesn't take a commission is interesting, but it doesn't mean there is no commercial value for the platform. It gets a more seamless creator experience, greater product interaction, stronger creator monetisation and potentially valuable signals about consumer interests and purchase intent," Kumar said.
However, the extent to which these signals translate into more effective advertising and measurable incremental sales remains to be established.
Could Instagram threaten affiliate platforms and agencies?
The integration also raises questions about the future of intermediaries that facilitate creator-led shopping through affiliate links, automated messages and tracking infrastructure.
In India, shopping-related comments such as "link please" have become a common feature of fashion and beauty Reels, giving rise to businesses that connect interested consumers with product listings.
Sankpal pointed to Wishlink as an example of how platforms have commercialised this behaviour through automated direct messages containing shopping links.
"For Amazon links, yes. Today a creator needs a third-party tool to turn a comment into a DM with a link. If the link sits on the Reel itself, that step disappears, and so does part of what the middleman sells," he said.
However, he cautioned that the immediate impact remains limited because the integration currently covers Amazon.
Platforms such as Wishlink and EarnKaro continue to offer access to multiple retailers, including marketplaces popular among India's fashion and beauty shoppers.
Sridharan similarly argued that while platforms focused primarily on affiliate links and tracking could face pressure, services such as creator discovery, campaign management and analytics would remain relevant.
Kumar said agencies would increasingly need to demonstrate commercial intelligence beyond managing affiliate links.
"The winners will be those who bring intelligence and accountability to creator commerce, rather than simply provide another layer of links and dashboards," he said.
Will creators increasingly be paid for sales rather than reach?
Instagram's integration also comes amid growing pressure on influencer marketing to demonstrate measurable business outcomes.
Executives say brands are experimenting with hybrid compensation structures that combine fixed creator fees with commissions or performance incentives.
"Pure commission-only deals are still rare. The common structure now is a smaller fixed fee plus a commission or a bonus tied to sales. Brands have started asking what the post sold, and reach alone doesn't answer that," Sankpal said.
He cited Trendweave data analysing more than four million orders, which found that influencer-driven sales in India grew over 28% year-on-year in the first half of 2026, while creator earnings from product sales increased more than 35%.
However, industry experts warned against treating affiliate conversions as a complete measure of influencer effectiveness.
Kumar estimated that conventional tracking through affiliate links, UTM parameters and coupon codes captures only around 7–12% of the actual influence generated by creators.
A consumer might discover a product through an Instagram Reel, research it elsewhere and eventually purchase it through another marketplace or offline retailer, leaving the original creator without attribution.
"Pay for the influence, reward the conversion. Don't pretend they are the same thing," Kumar said, advocating a hybrid model that compensates creators for content and credibility while rewarding measurable sales.
Sridharan also cautioned that affiliate campaigns may be easier to measure without necessarily delivering better returns than traditional influencer marketing, which often builds awareness and consideration before a transaction occurs.
The bigger battle: Product discovery, purchase intent and advertising budgets
For Instagram, the larger opportunity could lie in competing for commerce-related marketing expenditure rather than commissions on individual transactions.
Sankpal believes the integration puts Instagram in more direct competition with YouTube Shopping for creator-commerce budgets, particularly as brands seek clearer evidence that content drives purchases.
Yet whether Instagram can demonstrate this value beyond clicks remains an open question.
Kumar highlighted Instagram's claim that directly embedded product links generate approximately twice as many clicks as links in creator bios, but cautioned that clicks should not be confused with conversions.
Questions around creator participation, category-wise conversion rates, content formats and independently verified sales outcomes will ultimately determine the integration's commercial effectiveness.
For advertisers, the critical question is whether Instagram can demonstrate that its creator ecosystem generates incremental demand, rather than merely capture the final click on purchases consumers might have made anyway.
As platforms compete to connect content, commerce and advertising, Instagram's Amazon partnership could mark another step towards making product discovery a more measurable part of the marketing funnel. Whether that translates into a sustained shift in brand budgets will depend on the results.
