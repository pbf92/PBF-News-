TITLE: Digital media behind 98 per cent of ad violations, says ASCI
OUTLET: Indian Television Dot Com

MUMBAI: Ninety-eight per cent. That is the share of advertising violations flagged by the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) between April and September 2026 that sat in digital media, and one platform, Meta, accounted for 73.4 per cent of all violations of the ASCI Code. The finding comes from ASCI’s Half-Yearly Complaints Report 2026-27, released in Mumbai on 25th November 2026, and it shows how consumer protection is being tested in an advertising ecosystem increasingly dominated by digital platforms. The good news is that the watchdog is getting faster, sharper and more trusted.
During the six months, ASCI reviewed 3,951 complaints pertaining to 2,308 advertisements. Of the advertisements processed, 94 per cent required modifications, 72 per cent were found to mislead consumers through dishonest claims and 30 per cent promoted harmful products or unsafe situations. Crucially, 85 per cent of violations were identified through ASCI’s proactive monitoring, which shows the growing value of surveillance in catching advertising risks before consumers report them.
Advertisers, for their part, are listening. Voluntary compliance following ASCI’s intervention has held strong, a noteworthy validation for self-regulation. It rose from 80 per cent in FY2023-24 to 83 per cent in FY2024-25 and 86 per cent in FY2025-26, and stayed steady at 86 per cent during April to September 2026.
The public is also more engaged. Complaints from consumers rose 20 per cent, from 1,124 in April to September FY2024-25 to 1,347 in the corresponding period of FY2026-27. Of the complaints received in the current reporting period, 84 per cent came through ASCI’s TARA platform, an indication of greater public awareness about the role of ASCI as well as their own rights.
Meta is where the numbers get big. Between April and September, 1,563 ads on Meta platforms were found to have violated the ASCI Code. Of these, 90 per cent were revenue-generating sponsored advertisements rather than organic brand content, and 41 per cent of these were for categories where advertising is prohibited. A significant proportion related to offshore and illegal betting and was flagged to the regulators as well as Meta for action. The findings are significant because the ads persist even after the introduction of the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025, which prohibits any online money games.
Overall, 633 cases relating to restricted or prohibited advertising were referred to the government. These included:
- 590 illegal betting and gambling advertisements
- 28 cases under the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act
- 6 relating to alcohol
- 4 related to National Medical Council provisions
- 5 related to other matters
Under a memorandum of understanding (MoU) with Telangana RERA, 333 real estate ads were referred to TGRERA for violations of the RERA Act. A further 136 advertisements were referred to the relevant regulatory authorities for continued non-compliance.
In betting, 99 per cent of the 590 cases were detected through proactive surveillance, and sponsored advertisements accounted for 98 per cent of the cases. ASCI reported these advertisements to I4C and the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting for appropriate action.
After offshore and illegal betting, the highest number of violations were recorded in:
- Personal care: 456
- Realty: 360
- Food and beverages: 312
- Electronics and consumer durables: 95
- Healthcare: 82
- Education: 68
These seven categories accounted for 90 per cent of advertising violations during the period.
Influencers remain a pressure point. Of 659 advertisements processed for influencer-related violations, 95 per cent required modification. This was most pronounced in personal care, which recorded 263 influencer-related violations. The category also saw concerns around exaggerated and unsubstantiated skin and hair transformation claims, misleading safety and ingredient claims, and unsubstantiated environmental assertions.
Of the 312 food and beverages violations, 74 per cent related to nutraceutical products. ASCI observed claims around conditions such as PCOD, anaemia, cancer risk and heart health, as well as rapid weight-loss claims without adequate substantiation. Influencer disclosure remained a concern in this category too, with 181 violations. In electronics and consumer durables, violations were mainly around the launch of mobile phones.
Once violations are identified, though, advertisers move quickly. The report points to high responsiveness: 83.5 per cent of influencer advertisements were not contested, and such advertisements were modified within an average of six business days.
Manisha Kapoor, chief executive and secretary general of ASCI, said: “Advertising is increasingly shaped by the speed and scale of digital platforms, making it important to ensure that consumer protection keeps pace with the changing environment. Our findings show that misleading claims, inadequate disclosures and advertisements for products prohibited by law continue to pose concerns for consumers. The rate of voluntary compliance has been steadily rising over the last four years reflecting greater responsiveness among advertisers. However, the scale of violations also highlights the need for sustained vigilance and accountability across the ecosystem.”
Alongside stronger proactive monitoring, ASCI continued to improve the speed of complaint resolution. The average turnaround time for formally investigated complaints fell to 15 days during April to September 2026, compared with 30 days in FY2023-24. The proportion of cases not contested by advertisers has also risen steadily, from 49 per cent in FY2023-24 to 71 per cent during April to September 2026. Such cases have an average closure time of eight days.
