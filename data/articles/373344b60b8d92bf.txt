TITLE: Tufts University professors named top researchers in the world
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Two Tufts University faculty members have been named to a ranking of the world’s most highly cited researchers. The researchers in the Clarivate 2026 list have a significant impact on the research community as judged by the rate their work is cited by their peers, according to Clarivate, an information and analytics firm focused on research.
The highly cited papers rank in the top 1% by citations for a field or fields and publication year, and only about 1 in 1,000 researchers worldwide qualify.
The Tufts researchers are Renata Micha and Dariush Mozaffarian.
For this year’s analysis, the papers surveyed were the most recent available—those published and cited from 2015 to 2025.
“Tufts researchers are actively seeking solutions to many pressing issues in science, and this recognition shows the impact that they are making,” said Bernard Arulanandam, vice president for research and innovation at Tufts. “All of us at Tufts are proud of their accomplishments, and everyone who supports their work.”
“We congratulate Dr. Mozaffarian and Dr. Micha for their consistent contributions in nutrition epidemiology and public health nutrition through a body of seminal research that has advanced our understanding of diet-disease relationships and informed nutrition recommendations around the world,” said Christina D. Economos, dean, professor, and New Balance Chair in Childhood Nutrition at the Gerald J. and Dorothy R. Friedman School of Nutrition Science and Policy.
Renata Micha is an adjunct associate professor at the Gerald J. and Dorothy R. Friedman School of Nutrition Science and Policy and an associate professor of human nutrition at the University of Thessaly, Greece. Her research focuses on nutritional epidemiology, global dietary assessment, and the relationship between diet and chronic disease. She develops and applies innovative methods to assess dietary patterns worldwide, quantify their impact on cardiometabolic health, and evaluate nutrition policies and interventions to improve population health.
Her work has contributed to understanding diet-related disease burdens across countries and population groups, providing evidence to inform public health priorities and nutrition policy. She has authored more than 160 scientific publications, with research published in leading journals including the New England Journal of Medicine, The Lancet, Circulation, and PLOS Medicine.
Dariush Mozaffarian is a cardiologist, public health scientist, director of the Food is Medicine Institute at the Friedman School, and University Professor. A globally recognized expert in nutrition science and policy, Mozaffarian has helped position Food is Medicine as a transformative movement in U.S. health care. Under his leadership, the Institute has advanced research and policy initiatives demonstrating that key Food is Medicine programs such as medically tailored meals and produce prescriptions can improve health outcomes, reduce hospitalizations, and lower healthcare spending.
He has authored nearly 700 scientific publications on nutritional priorities for cardiometabolic health and on evidence-based policy approaches and innovations to advance nutrition security, reduce diet-related diseases, improve health equity, and reduce health care costs in the U.S. and globally. He is also the author of the recently published Food Is Medicine: Healing Our Bodies, Nourishing Our Minds, and Transforming Our Food System.
