TITLE: Digital platforms account for 98% of ad violations, Meta alone contributes 73.4%: ASCI
OUTLET: MediaNews4U

Mumbai: Digital platforms accounted for 98% of advertising violations identified by the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) between April and September 2026, with Meta alone contributing 73.4% of the violations of the ASCI Code, according to the organisation’s Half-Yearly Complaints Report 2026-27 released on Tuesday.
During the six-month period, ASCI reviewed 3,951 complaints pertaining to 2,308 advertisements for potential violations. Of the advertisements processed, 94% required modification. The report found that 72% of the advertisements misled consumers through dishonest claims, while 30% promoted harmful products or unsafe situations.
ASCI identified 85% of the violations through proactive monitoring, underlining the role of surveillance in detecting potentially misleading and non-compliant advertisements before they are reported by consumers.
Voluntary compliance by advertisers following ASCI’s intervention remained at 86% during April-September 2026. Compliance levels have risen from 80% in FY 2023-24 to 83% in FY 2024-25 and 86% in FY 2025-26.
Public participation in advertising self-regulation also increased during the period. Complaints from consumers rose 20%, from 1,124 in the corresponding period of FY 2024-25 to 1,347 in April-September 2026. Of the complaints received during the current reporting period, 84% came through ASCI’s TARA platform.
Among digital platforms, 1,563 advertisements on Meta were found to have violated the ASCI Code. Of these, 90% were revenue-generating sponsored advertisements rather than organic brand content, while 41% belonged to categories where advertising is prohibited. A significant share involved offshore and illegal betting advertisements, which were flagged to regulators and Meta for action.
The findings come despite the introduction of the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025, which prohibits online money games.
Overall, ASCI referred 633 cases involving restricted or prohibited advertising to the government. These included 590 illegal betting and gambling advertisements, 28 cases under the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, six relating to alcohol, four concerning National Medical Council provisions and five involving other matters.
Under a memorandum of understanding with Telangana RERA, ASCI also referred 333 real estate advertisements to the Telangana Real Estate Regulatory Authority for violations of the RERA Act. Another 136 advertisements were referred to relevant regulatory authorities for continued non-compliance.
In the betting category, 99% of the 590 cases were detected through proactive surveillance, while sponsored advertisements accounted for 98% of the cases. ASCI reported these advertisements to the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) and the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting for appropriate action.
Beyond betting, personal care, real estate and food and beverages recorded the highest number of advertising violations, with 456, 360 and 312 cases, respectively. Electronics and consumer durables accounted for 95 violations, followed by healthcare with 82 and education with 68. Together, these seven categories accounted for 90% of the advertising violations during the period.
Influencer-related advertising also remained an area of concern. Of the 659 advertisements processed for influencer-related violations, 95% required modification. The personal care category accounted for 263 such violations, with concerns involving exaggerated or unsubstantiated skin and hair transformation claims, misleading safety and ingredient claims, and unsupported environmental assertions.
In food and beverages, 74% of the 312 violations related to nutraceutical products. ASCI observed claims concerning PCOD, anaemia, cancer risk and heart health, as well as rapid weight-loss claims without adequate substantiation. Influencer disclosure was another concern in the category, accounting for 181 violations.
The electronics and consumer durables category recorded 75 violations, primarily related to mobile phone launches.
The report also highlighted advertiser responsiveness after violations were identified. A total of 83.5% of influencer advertisements were not contested, and these advertisements were modified within an average of six business days.
Manisha Kapoor, CEO and Secretary General, ASCI, said, “Advertising is increasingly shaped by the speed and scale of digital platforms, making it important to ensure that consumer protection keeps pace with the changing environment. Our findings show that misleading claims, inadequate disclosures and advertisements for products prohibited by law continue to pose concerns for consumers. The rate of voluntary compliance has been steadily rising over the last four years reflecting greater responsiveness among advertisers, However the scale of violations also highlights the need for sustained vigilance and accountability across the ecosystem.”
ASCI also reported improvements in complaint resolution timelines. The average turnaround time for formally investigated complaints fell to 15 days during April-September 2026, compared with 30 days in FY 2023-24.
