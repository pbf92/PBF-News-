TITLE: SODI announces major workforce development investments from SB Energy, OpenAI
OUTLET: The Telegram News

The Southern Ohio Diversification Initiative (SODI) announced $6.4 million in workforce development investments through SB Energy and OpenAI’s $80-million Community Benefit Fund for the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus. The funding announced is the first step in a broader workforce development effort to help residents gain the skills and training needed for jobs at the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus.
The first three investments include $1 million for the Electrical Training Academy, $5 million for Shawnee State University and $400,000 for the USW Local 689 ACE Training Center. These training programs will help prepare residents for construction and operating jobs associated with the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus. Jobs at the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus include skilled trades and construction, technical operations and facilities management.
“SODI’s work to build a new economic future for the PORTS site goes hand in hand with preparing local people to take part in it,” said Steve Shepherd, Executive Director of SODI. “SB Energy and OpenAI’s initial investment in workforce development strengthens our local education and training partners that know our region and can help our residents build bright careers here.”
The investments reflect priorities identified through open houses, listening sessions and meetings with educators, labor organizations, employers and residents. Additional workforce investments are planned as that work continues.
The Electrical Training Academy in Portsmouth builds on local electrical apprenticeship tradition with the International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers (IBEW) Local 575 and National Electrical Contractors Association (NECA) contractors. SB Energy and OpenAI’s $1-million investment will support equipment, training laboratory improvements and expanded pre-apprenticeship programs with regional Career and Technical Centers, including Pike County CTC, Scioto County CTC and Buckeye Hills CTC.
These expanded programs will help participants prepare for registered apprenticeships, strengthening the local union workforce needed to build projects like the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus. Funding will also help reach more adult learners and home-school students alongside traditional high school participants.
“Union apprenticeship gave me the opportunity to learn a skilled trade, earn a living, and build a career,” said Matt Louanglath, Director of the Electrical Training Academy. “SB Energy and OpenAI’s investment will help us introduce more people to that same opportunity. Working with our labor partners, contractors and local schools, we can prepare more residents for the electrical work ahead and careers that can support their families for years to come.”
SB Energy and OpenAI will work with Shawnee State University to support renovating and equipping laboratories for electromechanical systems, advanced computing and other applied technical fields, providing modern space to teach the skills those pathways require. The goal is to help students have a variety of options between school, hands-on training and college that will lead to employment opportunities to operate, maintain and support the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus’s power and computing facilities.
The investment in Shawnee State University will also support ShawneeWORKS, a recently announced and first-of-its-kind partnership between SSU and five regional career technical centers and laboratory improvements for hands-on technical education.
“Southern Ohio’s career technical centers are a valuable source of skilled talent, and ShawneeWORKS brings us together to give their students more opportunities to continue their education and build careers here,” said Eric Braun, President of Shawnee State University. “Working with SB Energy and OpenAI, we can connect our region’s schools with the new jobs coming to the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus, so local students and adults will be prepared for those opportunities.”
The Appalachian Career Empowerment (ACE) Training Program is operated by Piketon-based United Steelworkers Local 689. The investment will support the ACE Training Center and the program’s goal of training 720 people over three years. Training in industrial safety, equipment operation and manufacturing processes will build relevant skills to support construction, maintenance and facility operations at the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus.
USW Local 689’s decades of experience representing workers at the DOE site provide a strong foundation for preparing residents for this work.
“Our members know what it takes to work safely and reliably in a complex industrial facility,” said Andria Tipton, President of USW Local 689. “Through ACE, we can share that experience with the next generation ready to build careers in Pike County and Southern Ohio.”
“Our focus is on helping local residents gain the skills and qualifications for the thousands of jobs our project is bringing to the area,” said Chandler Grooms, Community Development Manager, SB Energy. “By working directly with education and training partners, we can help shape programs around the work ahead and give residents clear steps toward employment.”
“We want the communities that host our compute campuses to be better off because we’re there. That’s why we’re investing in apprenticeships and hands-on training in Southern Ohio now, so students and workers here can build the skills to help construct and operate the PORTS-Pike campus and use those skills throughout their careers,” said Gloria Blauvelt, Community Engagement Lead, OpenAI.
Each investment supports one or more priorities in SB Energy and OpenAI’s broader workforce approach, from career exploration and technical education to training access and modern equipment. Future investments will also consider the support residents need to complete training and find work, along with opportunities for local businesses to grow.
To learn more about how community input is guiding SB Energy and OpenAI’s workforce investments, SODI encourages residents to read Helping Local People Prepare for Local Careers on the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus website. These investments complement SODI’s work to bring new employers and productive uses to the former Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant site. Connecting that development with local training, education and business opportunities is central to building a lasting economic future for the site and surrounding communities.
For more information about SODI’s redevelopment work and regional partnerships, visit www.sodidevelopment.org.
