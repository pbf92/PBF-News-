TITLE: 9 Ways 'GTA 6' Actually Got Worse After 13 Years (Rockstar Needs To Fix #3 ASAP)
OUTLET: MovieWeb

Grand Theft Auto 6 delivers fantastic gameplay after an extended 13-year wait. There are some standout elements, like the mission that transitions players from Chapter 1 to Chapter 2, which features exceptional storytelling and is a blast to play through. However, as much as I'm enjoying my time with GTA 6, like many others, I find some changes confounding.
The game had a lot of expectations to live up to, and it ultimately succeeded as an action-adventure game with some RPG-like elements. But that's also where it may have gone too far in some respects, leaning into what worked for Red Dead Redemption 2. While RDR2 is a masterpiece in its own right, it works at a slower pace, as the Western genre often does. GTA is a fast-paced mayhem simulator.
GTA 6 is still a massive open world where your actions have consequences that affect everything around you. But the rampage-style arcade missions you took on as Trevor in GTA V would feel out of place in this world. Still, the changes and the way you approach gameplay go much deeper, and it's not always for the best, slowing the pace and leaning into a life simulator over the action-packed mayhem most expected.
The Need to Eat, Lift Weights, and Do Chores
Some aspects of GTA 6 feel unwarranted, like needing to lift weights. Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas featured a similar gameplay element, but it wasn't hailed as a highlight, and no one begged for it to come back. The game also tells you to eat or face consequences, which hinders gameplay. You could be about to kick in the door of a drug den to rob it blind, only to be told you're hungry and need to eat, so your health won't regenerate. If you're lucky, there may be a bag of chips to pick up and eat, but with cops potentially on the way, it cuts into your time.
Numerous missions pertain to doing chores. Whether it is fixing up the house or buying groceries, you will spend time doing the very tasks you sat down to escape. It's not lost on me that this endears the player to the characters and allows for an emotional bond, which is critical in the early stages of the story. Still, it can seriously hinder pacing and escapism. Plus, you are playing as a couple, so your actions can affect the relationship in positive and negative ways, which makes doing the menial tasks feel important when most would rather skip past them.
The Weapons Wheel & Personal Inventory
Players have grown pretty accustomed to using the GTA Weapons Wheel to navigate their personal inventory. It lets you carry an impossible number of guns, which you can tuck away at any time to hide. GTA 6 leans into the realistic RPG elements with its changes to the Weapons Wheel. You have what you own, with weapons stored in Ammu-Nation gun safes and personal vehicles, and clothes stored in closets, plus additional outfits you can place in your car.
When you're out and about in the massive open world, you have what you're carrying, and you can retrieve items from your personal vehicle. However, weapons such as rifles and shotguns remain visible, making you stand out like a sore thumb. You can drop the weapons or stash them in a vehicle, but you can't simply tuck them away into the depths of your Weapon Wheel. This adds realism to the story and the world it creates, but it also hinders a core GTA experience. I feel like this will need to change in some way for GTA 6's online mode when it finally releases, but that remains to be seen.
You Have to Stop for Gas
I cannot tell you how shocked I was when my car ran out of gas and left me stranded in the middle of the road on my way to a mission. When I tried to escape the hassles of real life, I wasn't expecting to be stranded and in need of help. Now, before pulling off a heist, you'd better make sure you have a full tank of gas, or risk running dry while attempting to outmaneuver the cops. This is one feature I wish they would remove in Rockstar's next patch.
The need to refuel your vehicle adds another level of realism, but it doesn't feel like the mayhem simulator Grand Theft Auto needed. It's not like your horse would die if you didn't feed it in RDR2, and the only mayhem this fuel change is causing is my personal frustration. I appreciate that you can pay to fix your car at gas pumps, especially with the added destructibility, but you should never run out of gas. It's a gameplay feature nobody asked for, and it prioritizes realism over the player's experience.
You Can't Flip Your Upside-Down Car Back Over
I assume this is another aspect of GTA 6 that was changed to capture realism, since you can't simply roll your car back onto its wheels when it flips upside down. In GTA V, if you end up on your roof, you can turn the wheel and right the car. That feature is gone, since even when your vehicle is airborne, you can't maneuver it the same way. When you end up on your roof, you have to get out of the car and attempt to physically roll it over.
Your character needs to push up against the vehicle, and it gives you a button prompt to push the vehicle back onto its wheels. There's no guarantee this works, as the vehicle could be too damaged to drive, or it may be stuck somewhere that you can't roll it back onto its wheels. There's an irony in watching your character display Hulk-like strength by pushing tons of metal over as if it weighed nothing, when that's somehow more realistic than turning the wheel and rolling it over. Still, if you're stuck with your personal vehicle, you can call to get it back, but it will cost you.
You Can't Outright Steal Any Car
In another nod to realistic gameplay, you aren't a master car thief, so you can't just take any car off the street. As you progress through the story, you get tools to help you out, like a slim jim to open some locked cars and a key cloner for certain high-end vehicles, but you have to earn them. Past GTA games may have had specific vehicles you couldn't outright take, but now it's a built-in feature that feels like the antithesis of the game's very promise.
Lucia and Jason are already criminals when the game starts, giving the developers an easy way to justify an excellent ability to steal cars without hindering selection. Yet they don't capitalize on that, forcing an education in carjacking that works well for the story but keeps players from immediately turning the world into their mayhem-laden oyster. One of the best aspects of playing GTA is finding unique cars, boats, and planes far beyond your financial reach to purloin at your leisure, and now it feels like you have to grind just to steal basic cars.
Travel Times Are Too Long
GTA 6's map is massive. While it takes players back to Vice City, it also opens up all of Leonida, which is modeled after Florida. The map includes versions of the Florida Keys, the Everglades, and the small towns in between. While the world is beautifully detailed and offers untold avenues of exploration, it can take a long time to travel between areas. Going from the bottom of the Keys to the top of the map feels like a real, arduous drive. Even driving to missions can feel excessively long, with character dialogue often ending long before you arrive.
However, it's not just the distance. The roads are narrower than in GTA V, and there is a lot of traffic, making it hard to pass other vehicles or navigate populated areas, which increases travel times. Even boat trips can take excessively long, as you cross open water for minutes at a time. The map is impressive, but the further away something is, the more likely you are to throw it in auto-drive and take a bathroom break rather than make the 8-minute trek. You could also use the ride-share app and skip the trip, but that costs money.
You Can't Replay Story Missions
One of the best features in GTA V was being able to replay missions. Having the option to go back and replay The Paleto Score or Trevor's wild Minor Turbulence mission after completing them was a great feature. Even Red Dead Redemption 2 featured this ability with its sweeping story and massive world. GTA 6 doesn't have this built in. It's the type of game people will sink hundreds of hours into simply exploring, but now they won't be able to experience some of their favorite elements without a full new playthrough.
GTA 6's story structure can include branching narrative elements based on your actions and criminal profile. While that may hinder straightforward mission replay, if any company could figure it out, it would be Rockstar. There are also numerous locations you can rob that are then devoid of valuables, but they could be fun to revisit with a different approach. You could create your own workaround with multiple save files, but that feels tedious. Maybe Rockstar will add something in a future update, but with some stellar missions already rushing by, I wish there were an easy way to replay them now.
The Garage System Limits Your Personal Vehicles
Early on in GTA 6, you only have one vehicle for Jason. This expands in later chapters, as you unlock the ability to register stolen vehicles in your name, turning them into new personal vehicles. You can store your personal vehicles in garages, and the game lets you find and buy new garages with more parking spaces. However, there is a maximum of 18 cars if you own all the garages. That's not a wild change from GTA V's story mode, where the characters faced similar limitations. Still, there was the expectation of an upgrade.
GTA 6 is in the hands of players who spent 13 years in GTA Online, paying for garage after garage and saving dozens upon dozens of personal cars. I assume GTA 6 Online will offer a similar level of storage for personal vehicles, but stepping back into the story's limited storage space was a shock. Plus, with Jason and Lucia trying to save money, adding more ways to spend it only encourages players to stick around longer. It could even play into the couple's relationship woes. The game also doesn't currently appear to feature any of GTA V's crazier vehicle types, like submarines, which feels like a waste with all that water around Leonida, but could be an online feature down the road.
Cheats Are Locked Until You Beat the Game
Using cheat codes in GTA is a time-honored tradition, underscored by their continued inclusion in the latest game. Yet, this time around, you can't use them until you beat the game. I understand why Rockstar would lock the cheats in GTA 6, since having infinite money and access to every weapon early on would ruin the sanctity of the story. Still, it always opened the world up to new avenues of mayhem, giving you unfettered access to weapons you didn't even know the game had.
While I prefer to play the story without cheat codes, having a second save where I could go wild with them would be a fantastic, stress-relieving gaming experience. Unfortunately, to get that experience, you'll first have to play through the game's story, which even low estimates put at around 40 hours.
