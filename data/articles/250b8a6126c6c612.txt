TITLE: 7 'Grand Theft Auto VI' Changes That Make the Game Harder Than Any Previous GTA (#2 Is Massive)
OUTLET: MovieWeb

Grand Theft Auto VI is the latest entry in the video game series known for letting players wreak havoc in a virtual world. But the newest installment has a few significant changes that make it harder to do so, especially if you haven't played other games like Red Dead Redemption 2. Some updates drastically change the way players approach missions, while requiring us to make sure Jason and Lucia get enough calories and hit the gym.
Gameplay updates are a fun way to create new challenges, and that's true in Grand Theft Auto VI. Some changes can be frustrating at first, but even a small spike in difficulty can make the game world feel more immersive and rewarding. However, if you haven't played a Grand Theft Auto game in a while, consider these changes before diving into Vice City.
The Wanted System Is More Than Six Stars
One of the hardest adjustments in Grand Theft Auto VI is the updated Wanted System and the new Criminal Profile. In older games, committing a crime immediately attracted police attention, with more severe crimes resulting in higher wanted levels. As players continued to engage in illegal activity, their star level would rise, eventually prompting a heavily armed force to hunt them down.
This is still the case in Grand Theft Auto VI, but in a much more nuanced way. A wanted level triggers only when an NPC sees you commit a crime, someone reports it, or an alarm is triggered. The police then build a profile that includes a player's clothing, vehicle, and weapons. They will also recognize that player's face if they weren't wearing a mask while breaking the law.
Players also cannot escape the police by just outrunning them. They'll be wanted within a defined area around the crime scene, which is highlighted in red on the in-game map. They'll need to change their outfit and repaint their vehicle if they want to stick around the area without attracting the police.
Grand Theft Auto VI's new Criminal Profile system is shaped by players' actions. Finishing missions without killing anyone establishes a player as a professional criminal, while causing mayhem and killing enemies negatively impact their profile. Causing too much damage can get them labeled a Psychopath, and they can even hit an Irredeemable level, meaning they can no longer improve their standing.
Civilians Will Fight Back
Civilians and NPCs react to the main characters in previous games, but they take it to a new level in Grand Theft Auto VI. Vice City feels like a living, breathing environment, where residents have a sense of self-preservation and aren't just punching bags.
Players can still take them out and cause mayhem, but they don't make it easy. NPCs respond to actions, similar to how they react in Red Dead Redemption 2. There's an option to greet or antagonize almost everyone, resulting in a peaceful encounter or a full-on fistfight.
Attempting to steal a car might prompt an NPC to yell in a player's face or fight back instead of simply letting them take their property. Civilians will also comment on your appearance, making remarks about Jason being ripped or teasing him if he's put on some weight. Carrying a long gun will also draw comments, making it harder to carry more firepower without attracting attention.
The Bottomless Weapon Wheel Is Gone
One of the biggest challenges in Grand Theft Auto VI is the limited number of weapons players can carry on their person. Previously, the game let you carry an unlimited number of weapons and access them at any time, which was fun but not realistic. Now, players are limited to two concealed handguns and two long guns, with one long gun slung on their backs and the other carried in their hands.
Players can adjust their loadout from their personal vehicle or from an Ammu-Nation store, but they'll need to decide what weapons they want in their inventory as they explore and complete missions. Anyone expecting an unlimited arsenal stored in their pocket, like in Grand Theft Auto V, is in for a rude awakening.
Vehicles Are More Realistic
Driving a car in Grand Theft Auto VI feels heavier and less predictable than in the previous game. Holding down the gas can send the car into a fishtail, and keeping control of a vehicle going 100 miles per hour is a challenge. The type of car or truck players are driving also impacts its handling, with older vehicles feeling heavier and slower.
Cars also require maintenance in Grand Theft Auto VI. You can (and will) run out of gas if you don't stop for fuel regularly. Electric vehicles also need to be charged at charging stations. Hitting other cars or objects can also cause significant damage, making driving feel riskier. Older players familiar with Grand Theft Auto IV might be surprised at how similar this game feels to driving around Liberty City.
Other vehicles, like airplanes and helicopters, are also tough to drive at first. Don't be surprised if you crash a few times while adjusting to the new mechanics.
Money Is Tight, So Spend Wisely
Grand Theft Auto VI has a surprisingly robust money system that is tough to master. Starting out, Jason is strapped for cash and living from job to job. Players need to complete missions, commit robberies, or engage in other crimes to earn cash. And once they have the money, they need to store it at home or risk losing it when they die.
They'll also be prompted to open a bank account at a certain point in the story. This gives them another way to stash cash, as they can deposit money at ATMs. Certain items, like larger tattoos, also require a bank account for payment. Stolen goods also don't instantly turn into usable currency. Players must visit certain locations to sell items or trade in marked bills.
Players Can’t Steal Every Car
Grand Theft Auto is named after the criminal act of stealing a vehicle. However, Grand Theft Auto VI makes it harder than ever to steal a car, especially if it's a newer one.
Players can steal any car that is currently being driven by an NPC, but as we've established, they'll give you a bit of trouble. Taking them out still results in a new ride, though.
Parked cars come with an entirely different set of challenges. Older cars can be stolen by breaking a window or using a slim jim to quietly unlock them. But newer cars require a key cloner or another tool. Expensive cars also have trackers that alert the police when stolen, which must be removed if players want to keep or sell the cars. They'll also need to register cars in their name if they want to keep them.
Fitness Levels Impact Stats
Skimping on protein and skipping leg day aren't options in Grand Theft Auto VI if you want the best stats possible. Players' in-game stamina, strength, focus, and body fat are tied to workout mini-games. Using kettlebells, doing squats, and doing pull-ups directly increase strength. Hitting a heavy bag increases cardio, and shooting a basketball or fishing increases focus.
Players' stats are also boosted by what they eat or drink. Having a protein shake can keep your character fed and in a healthy calorie range, while eating fast food will fill them up but negatively impact body-fat levels. All of these stats can be tracked in the fitness app on your phone, which is worth checking between missions.
A watered-down version of this system was previously featured in San Andreas and Grand Theft Auto V, but Grand Theft Auto VI raises it to a new level. A player's appearance also affects how other characters interact with them, so completing these mini-games is worth the time.
