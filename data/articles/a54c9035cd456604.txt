Nasdaq Rebounds as AI Stocks Rally: What Changed After Last Week’s Sell-Off?
The Nasdaq is staging a sharp comeback as AI stocks rally following last week’s brutal sell-off, with Nvidia surging nearly 30% and AMD gaining around 10%. Semiconductor stocks are leading the recovery, while investors are returning to technology shares that suffered heavy losses during the recent market rout.
The rebound marks a significant reversal in Wall Street sentiment, but today’s gains raises a bigger question: what changed, and is the AI trade recovering or simply experiencing a relief rally?
Nvidia Leads the AI Stock Rally With a $1 Trillion Market Cap-Jump
Nvidia shares surged nearly 30% after reports of a meeting between CEO Jensen Huang and President Donald Trump triggered a sharp reversal in investor sentiment. The rally reportedly added nearly $1 trillion to Nvidia’s market capitalization within minutes, making the chipmaker the biggest catalyst behind Monday’s AI-stock recovery.
Amidst the NVDA price rally, the company added nearly $1 trillion market cap in just 10 minutes, highlighting the intense move. The sharp move also lifted semiconductor peers, with AMD gaining around 10%, as investors returned to beaten-down AI stocks following last week’s sell-off.

AI infrastructure stocks are participating as well, with data-center and cloud-computing companies recovering from recent declines. The broad-based move suggests investors are buying across the AI ecosystem rather than concentrating solely on Nvidia.
What Changed After Last Week’s Sell-Off?
Last week’s sell-off was driven by mounting concerns over AI valuations, the enormous capital expenditure commitments made by technology companies and uncertainty about how quickly those investments will translate into profits.
The pressure intensified as investors questioned whether AI infrastructure spending could remain sustainable at current levels. Nvidia’s leadership uncertainty and concerns surrounding the broader AI investment cycle added to the selling pressure, pushing technology stocks lower.
Monday’s reversal suggests some of those concerns may have been overshadowed by renewed confidence and aggressive dip-buying. Investors are also responding to the magnitude of last week’s declines. After valuations and share prices dropped sharply, some traders appear to view beaten-down AI stocks as attractive opportunities, particularly companies with strong earnings growth and established demand.
Is the AI Rally Back?
Despite Monday’s powerful rebound, the broader investment debate surrounding artificial intelligence remains unresolved. Technology companies continue to commit billions of dollars toward AI infrastructure, but investors increasingly want evidence that the spending will generate sustainable revenue, earnings and cash flow.
That question remains central to valuations across semiconductor companies, cloud providers and data-center operators. The rebound could therefore represent a technical recovery following an aggressive sell-off rather than a definitive reversal of the broader trend.
Upcoming economic data, corporate earnings and developments surrounding AI spending will likely determine whether the rally extends beyond Monday. Investors will also watch whether Nvidia and other semiconductor stocks can retain their gains as the market reassesses the sector’s outlook.
Monday’s rally has delivered a powerful rebound to the AI trade, but Wall Street’s focus remains on whether the industry’s growth can justify its enormous investment cycle. For now, investors are buying the dip, but the sustainability of the recovery remains the bigger test.
