Study Finds Gaps in Maternal-Fetal Surgery Coverage
A Penn Nursing analysis of Medicaid and commercial insurer policies finds coverage for fetal surgery lags behind medical advances.
Updated on Nov. 24, 2026 in Healthcare

A study led by Penn Nursing and published in JAMA Network Open found that insurance coverage for maternal-fetal surgery has not kept pace with advances in the field. Researchers analyzed coverage policies from March through August 2025 across all 50 state Medicaid programs and 30 large commercial insurers.
Why it matters
The study finds inconsistent recognition of procedure codes and opaque commercial policies that could delay prenatal care. Researchers project that coverage gaps may disproportionately affect lower-income and Medicaid-insured families.
The study evaluated 19 procedure codes, including 15 CPT codes and four HCPCS S-codes, across insurers representing roughly 70% of U.S. enrollment. Researchers did not report how these coverage gaps have affected patient outcomes.
The players
Penn Nursing
The University of Pennsylvania School of Nursing led the study examining insurance coverage for maternal-fetal surgery.
JAMA Network Open
An open-access peer-reviewed medical journal published by the American Medical Association that published the study.
The details
Researchers evaluated 19 procedure codes across Medicaid programs and commercial insurers, finding that comprehensive fetal surgery codes are covered significantly less often than individual procedure codes. Tennessee provided no publicly verifiable coverage information for maternal-fetal surgery.
Timeline
- March through August 2025: Researchers analyzed insurance coverage policies.
- November 24, 2026: Study findings were published in JAMA Network Open and reported.
Healthcare Landscape
The findings highlight uneven coverage under current CPT and HCPCS coding standards for fetal procedures across Medicaid and commercial plans. The gaps affect how fast advances in maternal-fetal surgery reach patients in the United States.
Families relying on Medicaid may face uncertainty about whether fetal procedures will be covered before a pregnancy reaches a critical point. Patients with commercial plans may need to check plan documents directly, since many insurers did not publish usable coverage details.
The takeaway
Patients facing a fetal diagnosis should ask their insurer or Medicaid office directly whether specific fetal procedure codes are covered. Clear answers before care is needed can help avoid delays in prenatal treatment.
