TITLE: Buyang Huanwu Decoction Linked to Lower Oxidative Markers in Stroke Meta-Analysis
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and meta-analysis published in Frontiers in Neurology reports that combining the traditional herbal prescription Buyang Huanwu Decoction with conventional Western medical treatments is associated with measurable improvements in circulating oxidative stress markers and clinical functional scores among patients recovering from ischemic stroke. The quantitative synthesis, conducted by an interdisciplinary research team based at Gansu University of Chinese Medicine, Northwest Minzu University, and Gansu Provincial Maternity and Child Care Hospital, pooled data from ten randomized controlled trials published between 2018 and 2026. Across a combined cohort of 1,076 participants, patients assigned to receive the adjunctive herbal therapy exhibited statistically significant decreases in malondialdehyde concentrations alongside significant increases in the protective antioxidant enzymes superoxide dismutase and glutathione peroxidase relative to those managed exclusively with conventional control regimens. The pooled analyses also demonstrated superior overall clinical effective rates and greater reductions on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale, indicating a reduction in post-stroke neurological deficits. However, the study authors emphasize that because the assessed certainty of evidence across reported endpoints was graded as low or very low, these therapeutic associations must be interpreted cautiously pending further rigorous trial verification.
Ischemic stroke constitutes a primary global contributor to sustained disability and adult mortality, presenting a complex pathophysiological progression characterized by immediate arterial occlusion followed by secondary brain tissue destruction. As documented in the experimental background of the systematic review, the interruption of steady cerebral blood flow deprives brain parenchyma of indispensable glucose and oxygen, causing acute mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation breakdown and rapid intracellular adenosine triphosphate exhaustion. When blood flow is reintroduced during clinical reperfusion, re-oxygenation interacts with functionally impaired electron transport chains within damaged mitochondria, triggering an explosive burst of reactive oxygen species. This surge in free radicals overwhelms endogenous antioxidant defenses, inducing cascade events that include structural protein oxidation, nucleic acid damage, and extensive lipid peroxidation across vulnerable neuronal membranes. The resulting molecular degradation exacerbates secondary neurovascular damage, neuronal necrosis, and neurological impairment. Within this biochemical framework, oxidative stress represents a central mediator connecting the original ischemic insult to ongoing cerebral injury, providing a biological rationale for evaluating therapies capable of modulating free radical formation or bolstering endogenous enzymatic neutralization pathways.
To investigate the clinical evidence surrounding this intervention, the investigators conducted a comprehensive literature search across eight biomedical databases, encompassing PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, Web of Science, the China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Wanfang, VIP Information, and the Chinese Biomedical Literature Database from their respective inception dates through July 1, 2026. The systematic review adhered to the established Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines and maintained prospective registration in the PROSPERO database under the registration identifier CRD420261460080. The research team applied predefined eligibility criteria that restricted inclusion to published randomized controlled trials comparing adjunctive Buyang Huanwu Decoction alongside conventional Western medical therapies against conventional interventions without herbal co-administration in patients diagnosed with ischemic stroke. Non-randomized experimental cohorts, animal models, duplicate publication records, conference papers, dissertations, and investigations examining non-conforming intervention protocols were systematically excluded during multi-stage screening. Initial database queries yielded 301 distinct citations, which were independently screened, collated, and methodologically appraised by two assessors, resolving differences via consensus discussion and third-party arbitration to establish the final ten-study qualitative and quantitative synthesis corpus.
The ten analyzed trials encompassed an exact balance of treatment assignments, dividing 1,076 total participants equally into 538 experimental group participants and 538 control group participants. Across the included study designs, eight investigations compared conventional Western therapies combined with oral Buyang Huanwu Decoction directly against identical conventional Western management alone. In one trial conducted by Chen and colleagues, both study arms participated in family-based continuity of care protocols, with the active intervention group additionally receiving Buyang Huanwu Decoction. In another trial conducted by Zhong, both treatment arms underwent concurrent clinical acupuncture protocols, while the experimental cohort received the herbal formulation as an added therapy. Intervention delivery consistently followed a schedule of one prepared decoction dose per day across active arms, though overall intervention durations ranged from two weeks up to three months across trials. Baseline clinical characteristics documented across the trials represented acute phase patients treated within hours of symptom onset, recovery stage individuals treated weeks after presentation, and chronic sequelae populations, illustrating substantial clinical variation in patient duration and therapeutic timing across individual study designs.
Analysis of circulating malondialdehyde, a primary end-product and surrogate marker of cellular lipid peroxidation, was extracted from nine trials comprising 926 stroke patients. Because reporting laboratories quantified malondialdehyde concentrations using equivalent unit expressions of nanomoles per milliliter and micromoles per liter, data were synthesized as mean differences. Pooling under a random-effects model revealed that patients receiving adjunctive Buyang Huanwu Decoction exhibited a statistically significant reduction in malondialdehyde levels relative to control patients, yielding a weighted mean difference of minus 1.76 units with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning from minus 2.17 to minus 1.35, supported by a Z statistic of 8.43 and a significance value of p less than 0.00001. However, substantial between-study statistical heterogeneity was observed, characterized by an I-squared value of 83 percent and a tau-squared of 0.31. Methodological appraisal showed that two trials utilized thiobarbituric acid reactive substance assays, five trials employed enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kits, and two trials omitted specific analytical detection protocols. Subgroup comparisons examining acute stroke intervals versus post-acute intervals demonstrated consistent marker reductions without statistically significant inter-group difference (p equals 0.46), while treatment duration stratifications spanning two weeks, four weeks, or extended timelines similarly demonstrated non-significant subgroup variance (p equals 0.81).
Enzymatic antioxidant activity was evaluated through pooled syntheses of superoxide dismutase and glutathione peroxidase. Superoxide dismutase was reported across all ten included trials, accounting for 1,076 patients. Because individual trial laboratories utilized disparate assay platforms—including xanthine oxidase methods, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays, pyrogallol auto-oxidation colorimetry, and thiobarbituric acid colorimetric procedures—yielding non-comparable baseline units such as units per milliliter, international units per milliliter, and kilounits per liter, investigators computed standardized mean differences. Under a random-effects framework, the herbal decoction group achieved a statistically significant elevation in superoxide dismutase relative to control cohorts, demonstrating a pooled standardized mean difference of 1.30 with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.90 to 1.70 (Z equals 6.35, p less than 0.00001), despite considerable statistical heterogeneity reflected in an I-squared of 89 percent. Subgroup stratification by treatment duration demonstrated a statistically significant difference between duration categories (p equals 0.03), showing the largest standardized elevation in trials applying treatment durations extending beyond four weeks (standardized mean difference of 2.01). Furthermore, pooled data from three trials evaluating glutathione peroxidase across 280 participants showed an increase favoring the herbal decoction arm, with a standardized mean difference of 1.73 and a 95 percent confidence interval from 1.28 to 2.18 (Z equals 7.53, p less than 0.00001).
Functional and clinical response indicators were assessed via standardized neurological impairment measures and overall treatment efficacy rates. Eight trials involving 798 participants evaluated the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale, revealing lower residual deficit scores among participants allocated to adjunctive Buyang Huanwu Decoction compared with control participants. The random-effects meta-analysis demonstrated a pooled mean difference of minus 2.84 scale points, with a 95 percent confidence interval from minus 4.18 to minus 1.51 (Z equals 4.17, p less than 0.0001), though inter-trial heterogeneity remained elevated at an I-squared of 97 percent. Subgroup stratification by intervention length indicated greater neurological score reduction in trials administering therapy for more than two weeks and up to four weeks, yielding a mean difference of minus 3.85 points. In parallel, analysis of overall clinical effective rates across seven trials comprising 816 patients demonstrated an odds ratio of 4.20 favoring adjunctive decoction therapy, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 2.62 to 6.72 under a fixed-effect model (Z equals 5.97, p less than 0.00001), accompanied by an I-squared heterogeneity of zero percent. Absolute risk modeling within the certainty appraisal indicated 154 more clinically improved patients per 1,000 individuals treated.
Short-term safety and adverse event frequencies were extracted from four contributing trials encompassing 434 participants. Five recorded adverse occurrences were documented among 217 patients in the Buyang Huanwu Decoction arms (representing 2.3 percent), compared with twelve occurrences among 217 individuals within the control arms (representing 5.5 percent). Fixed-effect synthesis yielded an odds ratio of 0.39 with a 95 percent confidence interval extending from 0.14 to 1.15, corresponding to an absolute estimate of 33 fewer adverse events per 1,000 patients, ranging from 47 fewer to 8 more events. Because this statistical comparison yielded a p-value of 0.09 and crossed the line of null effect, the difference between cohorts did not achieve statistical significance. The authors emphasize that these limited observational figures cannot be interpreted as definitive proof of clinical safety or as an absence of adverse risk. Given that only four trials tracked safety metrics and clinical follow-up intervals remained confined to short intervention phases, comprehensive long-term safety profiles, drug interactions, and extended systemic risks associated with ongoing decoction administration remain quantitatively uncertain.
Evaluation of herbal composition across the trial corpus highlighted notable variability in formula construction and ingredient dosing, introducing marked pharmacological heterogeneity into the pooled evidence base.
