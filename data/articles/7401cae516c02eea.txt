Stock of the Day: Will Robinhood Rally from Here?
Robinhood Markets, Inc. HOOD shares are lower on Wednesday. They have lost 50% of their value in less than two months.
But the downtrend may be ending; the shares have reached a support level. This is why Robinhood is the Stock of the Day.
As you can see on the chart, Robinhood found support around $63 in late March. When the stock rallied off this level, some people who sold at it regretted their decision to do so.
• Robinhood Markets shares are sliding. Why is HOOD stock dropping?
Some also decided that if and when they could eventually do so, they would buy their shares back at the same price they were sold. So, when Robinhood dropped back to $63, they placed buy orders and these orders created support.
Sometimes stocks rally after they drop to support levels. This occurs when some of the people who created the support with their buy orders become anxious and impatient.
They increase their bid prices. Other anxious and impatient buyers see this and do the same thing. It can result in a snowball effect that can force the shares higher.
Some traders use moving average prices to determine when to enter or exit a position. If a short-term moving average crosses above a long-term moving average, it is a buy signal. If it crosses below it, it is a sell signal.
The blue line on the chart is the 10-day moving average price. The red line is the 20-day moving average. As you can see, using these moving average crossovers has been an effective strategy for Robinhood.
