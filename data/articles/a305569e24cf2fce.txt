TITLE: GTA 6 Becomes Biggest Entertainment Launch in History
OUTLET: Hungarian Conservative

Grand Theft Auto 6 has broken livestreaming records and generated an estimated $1.65 billion in preorder sales, despite having been available for less than a day, as anticipation surrounding the release drives unprecedented interest across the gaming industry.
According to data from StreamCharts, the game attracted 6.38 million concurrent viewers across Twitch, YouTube and Kick by 12:30 UTC following its release in New Zealand. That figure is nearly four times the previous record, set by Valorant during its closed beta.
GTA 6 also surpassed Valorant’s Twitch record of 1.7 million concurrent viewers, established in 2020. Within its first few hours, the game had accumulated tens of millions of hours watched on Twitch, equivalent to more than 1,500 years of continuous viewing.
Popular streamer Rubius drew 311,000 live viewers within an hour of starting his GTA 6 broadcast. At one point early on launch day, the game’s Twitch category attracted more than 3.3 million concurrent viewers, while overall Twitch viewership was up 111 per cent compared with the same time a week earlier.
‘Within its first few hours, the game had accumulated tens of millions of hours watched on Twitch, equivalent to more than 1,500 years of continuous viewing’
While we talk about the New Zealand records, it is important to mention that according to internet traffic data, New Zealand’s population rose by approximately 8000 per cent yesterday. The explanation is straightforward: the game was released at midnight, according to one’s own time zone. So most players and streamers either used a VPN or, in the case of Xbox users, changed the date and time settings on their consoles to have access to the game about 12 hours before their intended launch window.
The number of copies sold has not yet been disclosed, but market intelligence firm Sensor Tower estimates that GTA 6 has already generated more than $1.65 billion in preorder revenue. Around 75 per cent of those sales were reportedly made for Sony’s PlayStation 5, with the remainder coming from Microsoft’s Xbox Series S and Series X consoles.
The release comes 13 years after Take-Two Interactive and Rockstar Games launched Grand Theft Auto V in September 2013 for the PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360. Despite its age, GTA V has continued to rank among the 20 best-selling games every month.
Take-Two reported in its November earnings release that GTA V had sold approximately 235 million copies. The company has also said the game reached $1 billion in retail sales faster than any other entertainment release in history.
GTA 6 has also received strong critical reviews. IGN awarded it a perfect score of 10, while the game is tied with The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time for the highest rating on Metacritic. It has also received nominations in eight categories at The Game Awards, including Game of the Year.
However, players hoping to buy a new console to play the title may face significantly higher costs than in previous generations. Prices have risen amid tariffs and shortages affecting memory and storage components.
‘New Zealand’s population rose by approximately 8000 per cent yesterday’
The main constraint on the game’s potential sales was the number of PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series consoles in circulation. The limiting factor, especially, was the availability of PlayStation 5 Pro consoles. These machines have better hardware than their counterparts, making them the ideal platform to play GTA 6 on.
Sony’s PlayStation 5 Digital Edition, which launched at $399 in November 2020, now costs $599 in the United States. The disc-drive version has risen from its original $499 price to $649, while the PS5 Pro costs well over $1000 (if you can find one for sale).
Microsoft’s Xbox Series S has increased from $299 to $499, while the Series X has climbed by $250 to $749.
When GTA V was released in 2013, the PlayStation 3 cost around $250 and the Xbox 360 approximately $230. Adjusted for inflation, those prices would amount to roughly $350 today.
Even after accounting for inflation, current-generation consoles are therefore around $300 more expensive than the systems available when the previous instalment launched.
