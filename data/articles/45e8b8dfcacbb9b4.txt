TITLE: Mauricio Pochettino's USMNT tenure ends with one final win and a promising future for those left behind
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

CINCINNATI — Around 9 o'clock Tuesday, on a crisp night when emotion outweighed performance and the outcome barely mattered, the referee's final whistle signaled the end of Mauricio Pochettino's two-year tenure and the start of the U.S. coaching search.
The 54-year-old Argentine oversaw his 37th and final match, a 2-0 victory over Haiti that secured passage to the CONCACAF Nations League semifinals on an 8-0 aggregate over two legs.
He hugged his staff, embraced his players and maybe shed some tears. "Country Roads" — the U.S. World Cup anthem — played over the sound system as he waved and applauded the supporters. He posed for a staff photo.
He then headed down the tunnel and to the locker room before preparing for the overnight flight to England and his new job in charge of Manchester United.
His U.S. exit transpired so quickly, over the course of a nine-day international window. Pochettino arrived at the national training center near Atlanta three months into a four-year contract, aiming to continue integrating young players and sustaining the momentum of post-World Cup results.
But when Premier League royalty offered him his dream job, the course of this camp and the USMNT's long-term plans were abruptly altered.
On Tuesday, Pochettino was in a Major League Soccer stadium in southwest Ohio. On Sunday, he will lead struggling United into Anfield, Liverpool's storied grounds, for England's most famous rivalry.
Pochettino's accomplishments went beyond the 23-12-2 record, the first-place finish in the World Cup group stage and Round-of-16 berth. He gave playing time to 78 players and first caps to 31. He called up players in 2025 that became fixtures in 2026 and showed faith in a bundle of teenagers who are now poised to propel the program for the foreseeable future.
Just as importantly, Pochettino also reset the team culture and elevated the pride in representing the country and the U.S. crest.
In U.S. circles, a duality emerged: Pochettino bailed on the program but left it in a very good place as the 2030 World Cup cycle begins to accelerate next year. The next coach will inherit a team with depth, confidence and chemistry.
Any concerns about the U.S. mindset following Pochettino's announcement Friday were put to rest a day later with a 6-0 romp over the Haitians in Miami. Then on Tuesday, before a TQL Stadium audience mixed with U.S. and Haitian supporters, the Americans buried their overwhelmed foes with two exquisite goals before halftime.
Christian Pulisic's third goal in two matches came on a spectacular bicycle kick — perhaps the best of his 36 in a U.S. uniform — and Julian Hall used instincts and touch beyond his 18 years for his fifth goal in six matches to start his international career. Weston McKennie assisted on both with wonderful passes.
With two victories and a wide goal differential, the U.S. will be the No. 1 seed in the Nations League semifinals in late March at SoFi Stadium outside Los Angeles. Canada and Panama have also advanced, while Mexico and Jamaica compete for the last slot later Tuesday.
Before that, under-23 coach Steve Cherundolo will oversee next month's MLS-heavy senior camp and Dec. 13 friendly vs. Jamaica in Fort Lauderdale, Florida.
Cherundolo might be among the domestic candidates to replace Pochettino, though the U.S. Soccer Federation is expecting to conduct a global search. Officials are aiming to hire someone before the Nations League semifinals. The next major tournament, the CONCACAF Gold Cup, is next summer. 2030 World Cup qualifying is still at least a year away.
In his final match, Pochettino retained the same starting lineup that ran wild in Miami. Though the U.S. was all but certain to advance, Pochettino and the players said in the buildup to the second leg that they would treat the match as if the aggregate was 0-0.
They needed 25 minutes to go ahead, but the quality of Pulisic's goal was well worth the wait.
McKennie chipped a cross from the end line to the back side. Eight yards out, Pulisic turned away from the net, lowered his back, went airborne and stung a right-footed overhead kick that slipped past three defenders and into the far side-netting.
The lead swelled in the 43rd minute. In a synchronized moment, McKennie lifted the ball to Hall making a central run behind the defense. The Red Bull New York star touched the ball with his right foot, settled it with his chest and, in an instinctive flash, used the outside of his right foot to beat goalkeeper Alexandre Pierre to the far corner.
Earlier this fall, Hall scored in each of his first three caps and, after failing to score in his fourth, he posted goals in each Haiti match.
Pochettino's one halftime change came with special meaning as 19-year-old center back Neil Pierre, the son of Haitian immigrants, replaced Chris Richards.
After Auston Trusty's header appeared to cross the goal line before being cleared and Haiti threatened twice, Pochettino turned back to his bench in the 65th minute. Pulisic gave way to 17-year-old Cavan Sullivan and Malik Tillman yielded to Folarin Balogun.
