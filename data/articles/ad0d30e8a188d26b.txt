TITLE: Prime Video sets December 5 for 14-episode Korean epic Sacred Jewel
OUTLET: PPC Land

Prime Video released a trailer on Thursday, November 19, 2026, for Sacred Jewel, a 14-episode Korean historical drama set in 1258, and confirmed that the series will premiere on Saturday, December 5. It will stream "exclusively on Prime Video on December 5 in Thailand and more than 240 countries and territories worldwide," according to a press release datelined Singapore and published on Amazon MGM Studios' press site. Ahn Bo-hyun, Lee Sung-min and Claudia Kim lead the cast. SLL and Celltrion Entertainment produced the series, with Jung Dae-yoon directing from scripts by Jung Hyun-min. The release names a single market, Thailand, and is silent on how many episodes arrive on launch day, which languages are offered and whether South Korea is part of the worldwide release.
In Short
Amazon has put out a new trailer for Sacred Jewel, a Korean period drama about a young warrior sent to recover a jewel that could save a kingdom at war with the Mongols, and it starts streaming on Prime Video on December 5, 2026. The show reaches more than 240 countries and territories, but in Korea itself it is reported to air on the broadcaster JTBC and the streaming service Coupang Play, and Thailand, the one market Amazon singles out, is not among the countries where Prime Video sells ads. For advertisers, that means the Asian audience Amazon is courting with this title is mostly a subscription audience, not ad inventory, at least on the list Amazon has published.
A convoy, a princess and an ultimatum
The release carries a long headline: "One Relentless Quest Against Impossible Odds: Prime Video Unveils New Exclusive Trailer for Korean Historical Epic Sacred Jewel." Its subhead describes "a young warrior" who "leads a desperate mission to save a kingdom in this sweeping tale of action, romance and hope." Below a still of Ahn Bo-hyun in costume, a line reading "Watch Prime Video Exclusive Trailer HERE" links to a video on YouTube.
According to Amazon, the trailer takes viewers into the "war-torn Goryeo Dynasty," where the young warrior Baek Gyul "is made commander of a small convoy of the kingdom's finest men and sent on a dangerous mission that could change the course of the war." It shows his relationship with Princess Wang Hee and "the Emperor's harsh order that Baek Gyul must never return if the mission fails." The release says the trailer ends with "thrilling action as the convoy of hope faces the dangers of the battlefield."
The longer synopsis fixes the setting. "Set in 1258 during the Goryeo Dynasty, after nearly three decades of relentless warfare against the Mongols, the kingdom stands on the brink of collapse," the release says. The Mongol campaigns against Goryeo began in 1231, which fits that count. With defeat close, an expedition led by Baek Gyul sets out "to recover a sacred jewel believed to possess miraculous powers capable of protecting Goryeo." The group, according to Amazon, "fights its way through enemy territory and ventures beyond the mountains toward the eastern sea," while Princess Wang Hee "races to their aid."
That is the whole of the creative description. There are no episode titles, no running times and no age rating.
A note on the name
One line in the release is aimed at journalists rather than viewers. Set in italics and wrapped in asterisks between the trailer link and the dateline, it reads: "NOTE: Please refer to our streaming service as Prime Video, not Amazon Prime Video." The service, in other words, is to be named without its parent, even in a release that ends with an "About Amazon" section.
Cast and crew
The release lists five actors, each with three earlier credits, and three names behind the camera. The table records them as Amazon wrote them.
Two overlaps stand out. Reborn Rich appears in the credits of both Lee Sung-min and the director, Jung Dae-yoon, so Sacred Jewel reunites an actor and director from that series. And Spring Fever, the first credit listed for Ahn Bo-hyun, also appears in the release's list of titles already on Prime Video. The release does not say who plays Baek Gyul or Princess Wang Hee. A still of Ahn Bo-hyun heads the release, and headlines on Soompi and t2ONLINE describe him as a Goryeo-era warrior and special commander, but Amazon's text never links actors to characters.
The writer's credits are both historical dramas. The Nokdu Flower is set during the Donghak peasant uprising of 1894, and Jeong DoJeon follows the founding of the Joseon dynasty, which replaced Goryeo in 1392. Sacred Jewel moves the writer back more than a century, into the 13th century.
How Korea watches it
Amazon's release does not mention South Korea as a market. Earlier coverage does. In an April 2026 report on the series' festival debut, Señal News said Sacred Jewel would air on JTBC and Coupang Play in Korea, with international audiences reached through Prime Video. A Soompi report from April 14, 2026, described it as an upcoming JTBC drama due in the second half of the year. A later Soompi interview piece, published November 13, gave the premiere as December 5 at 10:40 p.m. Korea time, without naming a channel.
If both accounts hold, the arrangement is a familiar one in Korean television: a domestic broadcaster and a domestic streamer carry the show at home, while a global platform takes the rest of the world. It would also explain the careful wording. Amazon's exclusivity applies to "Thailand and more than 240 countries and territories," not to every country. The release neither confirms nor rules out a Korean exception, and it does not say whether Prime Video episodes go live at the same moment as the Korean broadcast.
The series has had a long run-up. According to Señal News, Sacred Jewel screened on April 24, 2026, at the ninth Canneseries festival in Cannes, in the Rendez-Vous section as part of a Korea Fiction program, before more than 300 industry professionals and fans. Soompi published the first teaser on August 11, 2026. The November 19 trailer is the latest step, and Amazon calls it "exclusive."
Promotion in Southeast Asia continues past launch day. PhilSTAR L!fe reported on November 19 that Ahn Bo-hyun and Claudia Kim are scheduled to attend a premiere event at Shangri-La Red Carpet Cinemas in Manila on December 5, with details still to be announced.
The catalogue around it
A full paragraph of the release is about other titles. Sacred Jewel, Amazon says, "joins thousands of other shows and films available on the Prime Video catalogue." The list that follows has three groups. Korean series come first: Final Table, Love in Disguise, A Love Other Than Yours, See You at Work Tomorrow!, Spring Fever, Surely Tomorrow, Head Over Heels and GOOD BOY. Anime follows, with Nippon Sangoku The Three Nations of the Crimson Sun and Mobile Suit Gundam GQuuuuuuX. The last group is labeled "award-winning and critically acclaimed Amazon Originals": Elle, Off Campus, Project Hail Mary, Masters of the Universe, Fallout, The Summer I Turned Pretty, Heads of State, The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power, Reacher and The Boys.
The grouping matters. Sacred Jewel itself is not called an Amazon Original anywhere in the text, and the Korean titles are kept apart from the Originals. Amazon MGM Studios does file the series under "original series" on its press site, where the release links to a "Sacred Jewel | Season 1" page. The release does not say whether any of the listed Korean shows are licensed or commissioned, which is the line that usually separates a platform's back catalogue from its own productions.
The practical points come last. "Prime Video members are able to watch films and shows anywhere and anytime on thousands of compatible devices," according to Amazon. "In the Prime Video app, members can also download episodes on their mobile devices and tablets and watch anywhere offline at no additional cost."
Where the ads run, and where they do not
For the advertising market, the most useful fact about Sacred Jewel may be the one market Amazon chose to name. Thailand does not appear on Amazon's list of countries with Prime Video ads.
The most recent list on Amazon Ads' site is in a news item dated March 17, 2026, which announced launches in four further countries. It names 16 markets where Prime Video ads already ran and four scheduled for 2026. The same page cites "average monthly ad-supported reach of more than 315 million" customers worldwide, based on Amazon internal data for September 2024 to August 2025. PPC Land reported that figure when Amazon's ad revenue reached $21.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up from the 200 million Amazon disclosed in April 2024.
In the markets on that list, ads are the default and viewers pay extra to remove them. In the United States, Amazon replaced its $2.99 ad-free add-on with Prime Video Ultra at $4.99 a month from April 10, 2026, a 67% increase. In Thailand, Singapore, the Philippines and South Korea, none of that applies, at least on the published list. A viewer in Bangkok who watches Sacred Jewel on Prime Video is a subscriber, not an impression. Amazon may have added markets since; the page, which still carries the March date, lists none beyond these 20.
That gap is worth setting against what Korean viewers themselves tolerate. A Magnite survey of 905 adults, published in October, found that 67% of South Korean streamers use ad-supported services and accept about 7 minutes of ads an hour, on self-reported answers to a study commissioned by a company that sells that inventory. In Korea, though, any advertising sold around Sacred Jewel would go through its Korean carriers, if the reported arrangement holds, not through Amazon.
A crowded month for Korean drama
The competition for the same audience is visible on the calendar. Disney+ launched The Remarried Empress on November 3, and Disney said the series had the most-viewed premiere of any local original on Disney+ and Hulu worldwide, counting three days of views, and ranked as the top Korean title premiere in more than 50 markets. PPC Land noted that Disney gave no view count behind the claim for The Remarried Empress. Its finale is scheduled for November 25, ten days before Sacred Jewel begins. Disney described its launch in rankings rather than audience numbers. Amazon's release offers neither for its Korean catalogue.
"Large and profitable," without a number
The boilerplate at the foot of the release contains one financial sentence: "In April 2026, Amazon shared Prime Video is a large and profitable business in its own right." PPC Land noted the line in its coverage of the release for all eight episodes of The Terminal List season two. It gives no revenue, no operating income and no margin.
Andy Jassy's annual shareholder letter, published on April 9, 2026, was hardly more specific. PPC Land found that it gave Amazon's advertising business a single sentence and grouped Prime Video with Pharmacy and Grocery as businesses improving their economics. Advertising itself is reported: Amazon's ad revenue reached $19.64 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 10.9%, its growth slowed by Prime Day moving to June. Prime Video's share of that figure is not broken out.
Disney is moving in the same direction from the other end. After a fiscal year in which its streaming unit earned $2.57 billion at an 11.5% margin, Disney said it would stop reporting streaming operating income from February 2027. Amazon describes Prime Video as profitable without publishing the figure; Disney published the figure and is about to stop. For media buyers trying to judge how much the platforms will spend on titles such as Sacred Jewel, the public data is getting thinner on both sides.
