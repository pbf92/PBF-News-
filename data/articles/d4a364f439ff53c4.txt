TITLE: 95% of influencer ads scrutinised by ASCI required modification; personal care leads violations
OUTLET: Storyboard18

Nearly 95% of the 659 advertisements processed for influencer-related violations by the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) between April and September 2026 required modification, highlighting persistent compliance challenges in India's rapidly expanding influencer marketing ecosystem.
According to ASCI’s Half-Yearly Complaints Report 2026-27, released on November 25, personal care emerged as a major concern, recording 263 influencer-related violations, followed by food and beverages, which recorded 181 influencer disclosure violations.
The findings come at a time when advertisers are increasingly integrating influencer-led content into their marketing strategies across beauty, personal care, nutrition and consumer electronics. Personal care, nutraceuticals emerge as areas of concern The personal care category recorded 456 advertising violations overall during the six-month period. Concerns included exaggerated and unsubstantiated skin and hair transformation claims, misleading safety and ingredient claims, and unsupported environmental assertions.
Meanwhile, of the 312 violations recorded in the food and beverages sector, 74% pertained to nutraceutical products.
ASCI observed advertisements making claims related to conditions such as PCOD, anaemia, cancer risk and heart health, alongside rapid weight-loss claims without adequate substantiation.
Influencer disclosures also remained a significant concern in the category, with 181 violations recorded during the reporting period.
However, the report pointed to relatively high responsiveness among influencers once violations were identified. Around 83.5% of influencer advertisements were not contested, with such advertisements modified within an average of six business days. Meta accounts for 73.4% of advertising violations Digital media accounted for 98% of advertising violations identified by ASCI during April-September 2026, with Meta alone accounting for 73.4% of violations of the ASCI Code.
As many as 1,563 advertisements on Meta platforms were found to have violated the code. Of these, 90% were revenue-generating sponsored advertisements rather than organic brand content, while 41% belonged to categories where advertising is prohibited.
A significant proportion of these advertisements related to offshore and illegal betting, which were flagged to regulators as well as Meta for action.
The findings come despite the introduction of the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025, which prohibits online money games.
ASCI reviewed 3,951 complaints pertaining to 2,308 advertisements during the six-month period. Of the advertisements processed, 94% required modification, 72% were found to mislead consumers through dishonest claims, and 30% promoted harmful products or unsafe situations.
Around 85% of violations were identified through ASCI’s proactive monitoring. Illegal betting leads advertising violations Offshore and illegal betting emerged as the largest category of advertising violations, with 590 cases, followed by personal care (456), realty (360), food and beverages (312), electronics and consumer durables (95), healthcare (82) and education (68).
Together, these seven categories accounted for 90% of advertising violations during the reporting period.
ASCI referred 633 cases involving restricted or prohibited advertising to the government, including 590 illegal betting and gambling advertisements, 28 cases under the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act and six alcohol-related cases.
Of the 590 betting-related violations, 99% were detected through proactive surveillance, while sponsored advertisements accounted for 98% of the cases.
These advertisements were reported to the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) and the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting for appropriate action.
Separately, 333 real estate advertisements were referred to the Telangana Real Estate Regulatory Authority, while another 136 advertisements were referred to relevant regulatory authorities for continued non-compliance. Voluntary compliance remains steady at 86% Despite the scale of violations, voluntary compliance among advertisers following ASCI’s intervention remained steady at 86% during April-September 2026, compared with 80% in FY2023-24 and 83% in FY2024-25.
“Advertising is increasingly shaped by the speed and scale of digital platforms, making it important to ensure that consumer protection keeps pace with the changing environment. Our findings show that misleading claims, inadequate disclosures and advertisements for products prohibited by law continue to pose concerns for consumers,” said Manisha Kapoor, CEO and Secretary General, ASCI.
“The rate of voluntary compliance has been steadily rising over the last four years reflecting greater responsiveness among advertisers. However, the scale of violations also highlights the need for sustained vigilance and accountability across the ecosystem,” she added.
The average turnaround time for formally investigated complaints also declined to 15 days during April-September 2026, from 30 days in FY2023-24.
Meanwhile, the proportion of cases not contested by advertisers increased from 49% in FY2023-24 to 71% during the current reporting period.
