TITLE: The women who built Anthropic: Meet the female force behind Claude
OUTLET: The Times of India

It hasn’t been long since Anthropic made history with its record-breaking IPO. And as the AI giant grabbed headlines with that remarkable achievement, most people discussed Anthropic’s wild ride to the stock market as all about staggering numbers: trillions in valuation, billionaire founders, Wall Street sharks, and the breakneck race to rule AI. But honestly, if you manage to zoom in a little, there’s a whole other story worth telling. The company that just made history with that jaw-dropping IPO? It isn’t just a shrine to the bravado of male founders. So much of what’s made Anthropic so influential, from what powers Claude to their famed AI assistant, comes from the women whose fingerprints are everywhere: in ideas, decisions, investment, and the company’s DNA.
And it’s not just inspirational sloganeering. Look closely, and you’ll find seven women whose work, from founding, funding, defining culture, steering products, to setting ethical boundaries and even shaping Claude’s ‘personality’, helped build Anthropic into what it is. The company’s massive debut on the Nasdaq on November 19, 2026, raising $81.3 billion in the single largest IPO ever, grabbed headlines. But the real story, in many ways, is how these women helped make all that happen.
Meet Anthropic’s other co-founder (President and Board Chair): Daniela Amodei, sister to Dario Amodei. People tend to name-check her brother, Dario, as the ‘face’ of Anthropic, which is fair, but kind of misses the point. Daniela Amodei walked away from OpenAI in 2021 to co-found Anthropic, and her mark on the company runs deep. She’d been OpenAI’s VP of Safety and Policy, and before that? It was Stripe, for running risk operations, which isn’t exactly the classic ‘techie’ path. An English literature grad from the University of California, Santa Cruz, she’s proof that the best leaders don’t always show up with a CS degree.
As President and Board Chair, Daniela balances research, engineering, governance, and the nuts-and-bolts of getting products shipped. She’s also one of the company’s seven original founders who’ve pledged to give away 80% of their Anthropic windfall to charity, which is a move that instantly made her one of the richest women in tech at IPO.
For the unversed, Yasmin Razavi is the venture capitalist with nerves of steel (Spark Capital General Partner, Anthropic Board Member). Every runaway success story has that one investor who saw the future a little sooner than everyone else. For Anthropic, that person is Yasmin Razavi of Spark Capital, who stepped in with a $75 million bet way back when the company was valued at “just” $5 billion. By 2026, Spark’s investment had ballooned to over $3 billion, before even counting the IPO surge.
Razavi isn’t just in it for the returns; she’s the sole external investor on Anthropic’s board and shapes the company’s strategy from the inside. A Harvard Business School alum with stints at McKinsey and Index Ventures, she’s a classic example of how conviction and timing in venture capital can change everything.
Meet the philosopher, ethicist, and Claude’s conscience: Amanda Askell, who’s the lead researcher in philosophy and alignment. What separates Claude from other chatbots isn’t speed or scale; it's the intent behind every answer. Amanda Askell has shaped the very substance of Claude’s “thinking.” Before joining Anthropic, she researched at OpenAI, focusing on ethics and decision theory, and came to Anthropic ready to tackle the hardest challenges in AI: honesty, helpfulness, and the role of AI in society.
She’s the key author behind Claude’s Constitution, which is a set of guiding values for the AI’s behavior. With degrees from Oxford and NYU, Askell brought a philosopher’s eye for nuance into the heart of an emerging tech industry that desperately needs it. AI ethics isn’t just a buzzword for her; it’s her job to make sure Claude reflects real values, not just slick engineering.
Talk about turning technical breakthroughs into products people use, and Ami Vora is the Chief Product Officer you reach out to. It’s one thing to invent powerful tech. Turning it into something people actually like and use? That’s a much trickier art. That’s where Ami Vora comes in. Her resume reads like a tour of Big Tech royalty: over 15 years at Meta across WhatsApp, Facebook, and Instagram, a stint as CPO at Faire, and an early career at Microsoft. She knows a thing or two about making products that stick.
Vora oversees all of Anthropic’s consumer and business products, translating deep tech into experiences real people want to come back to. She’s the bridge from research lab to the messy, demanding real world.
Dianne Penn is the Head of Product, AI Research and Labs, and the one guiding Claude’s evolution. Penn joined Anthropic before it was cool, when the product team was just five engineers and the big dreams were still on the whiteboard. She cut her teeth as a technical product manager, steering every generation of Claude from 2 onwards and launching products like Claude Code and the Model Context Protocol.
Penn’s background swerves from trading high-yield bonds at JPMorgan to building at Amazon’s Alexa AI. That mix of finance grit and tech focus is exactly what you need to steer breakthrough research ideas into scalable, working products that actually earn their keep.
When it’s about building trust in an industry obsessed with trust, Sasha de Marigny, Chief Brand and Communications Officer, is Anthropic’s gem. If you believe all tech PR is fluff, try launching a high-risk AI company and see how it feels to be misunderstood by the world. Sasha de Marigny was Anthropic’s first communications hire, arriving in 2023, and rose quickly to Chief Communications Officer. She built the team from scratch, pulling together editorial, communications, and branding into a cohesive global force.
De Marigny previously helped take Stripe’s brand international and worked at Thrive Capital. Her focus at Anthropic is about more than getting the word out. She’s been central in shaping how Anthropic, and by extension, Claude, earns trust, manages global perception, and avoids the kinds of PR disasters that plague less thoughtful competitors.
Meet the one powering the investment machine: Effie Epstein, the Managing Partner, Sound Ventures. She’s not in the daily trenches at Anthropic, but she’s crucial to the story. Sound Ventures, which she helps run (with Ashton Kutcher and Guy Oseary), backed Anthropic and watched its stake soar to $7.5 billion by IPO time. Epstein has built a reputation for picking winners in tech, and her belief in Anthropic speaks volumes.
