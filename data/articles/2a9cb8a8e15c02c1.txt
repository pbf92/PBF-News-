TITLE: Search interest rises 18% when brands return to TV after a year off, VAB says
OUTLET: PPC Land

Brands that came back to television after at least a full calendar year away recorded higher search interest, more website visitors and higher revenue in their first year back, according to a custom analysis the Video Advertising Bureau released today. The 34-page report, "The Comeback Effect," follows 40 brands across 20 categories that had no measured TV spending for one or more calendar years between 2016 and 2025 and then returned. Branded search interest rose 18% on average across 29 of them. Monthly unique website visitors rose 57% across 11 others. Annual revenue grew 27% on average across six with figures in US securities filings.
VAB is the US trade body for TV networks and streaming companies, and the report is part of its case for TV advertising. Its opening page does not hedge: "'Going dark' on TV has historically had significant consequences for advertisers as a shift from brand-building media weakens consumer relationships, reduces brand visibility and erodes market share." The evidence for the comeback, though, comes from comparing each brand with itself - the year before it returned against the year after - with no group of brands that stayed off TV for comparison. And the brands did not merely come back. On average they spent $4.9 million on TV in their first year back, 2.1 times the $2.3 million they had spent in the year before their break.
In Short
The VAB, a trade group that promotes TV advertising, studied 40 brands that stopped buying TV ads for at least a year and then came back, and found that their Google search interest, website visits and sales were higher in the year they returned. Each of those results comes from a different, smaller set of the brands - 29 for search, 11 for website visits and six for revenue - and the brands came back with more than double their earlier TV budgets. Because nothing in the report compares them with brands that stayed off TV, it shows what happened alongside a return to TV, not how much of it the TV ads caused.
What VAB measured
The report sits in VAB's "Impact in Action" series, which the cover describes as "Practical guidance from real-world marketing examples." It was prepared by Jason Wiese, executive vice president and director of strategic insights, and Reed Kiely, vice president of data insights and trends. Like most VAB research, it is gated: every page carries the line "This information is exclusively provided to VAB members and qualified marketers," and VAB's announcement directs marketers and agencies to create an account. The report pages carry no publication date.
"This custom analysis was built with 10 years of TV ad investment data and outcomes for a diverse group of brands after they returned to TV following at least one full year away," VAB said in its announcement. The announcement opens with the premise: "When a brand steps away from brand-building media, its relationship with consumers weakens, its visibility drops and its market share erodes. Returning to TV can help reverse that and strengthen overall brand performance."
Four data sources sit underneath the findings. TV spending comes from Nielsen Ad Intel for January 2016 to December 2025, covering broadcast TV, Spanish-language broadcast TV, national cable, national Spanish-language cable, spot TV, syndication and streaming TV, with promos and public service announcements excluded. Streaming is included only from August 2022. Search interest comes from Google Trends for the United States. Website traffic comes from Comscore's Media Metrix multi-platform data for people aged 18 and over, January 2023 to September 2026. Revenue comes from annual 10-K and 20-F filings with the Securities and Exchange Commission, 2017 to 2025.
How the analysis was built
According to the report's methodology pages, the analysis was assembled in six steps.
- Brand selection. VAB identified brands with at least one full calendar year without measured TV spending in Nielsen Ad Intel between 2016 and 2025 that then returned to TV. The result was 40 advertisers across 20 categories.
- Defining the two years. "Year 'Off' TV" is the 12 months before a brand's return and "Year 'Back On' TV" the 12 months that follow it. A footnote repeated through the report reads: "Year = is equivalent to 12 months and does not necessarily align with the calendar year."
- Spending comparison. Each brand's TV spending in its first year back was compared with its spending in the year before it went dark.
- Search. "Using Google Trends we compared the relative search volume (based on search index) of brands for the 12 months before they returned to TV vs. the 12 months after they returned to TV."
- Website traffic. "We analyzed website traffic data between Jan 2023 - Sep 2026 through Comscore to compare monthly unique visitors for the 12 months before they returned to TV vs. the 12 months after they returned to TV." Only brands with measured traffic on their own websites between January 2023 and December 2025 qualified.
- Revenue. For brands whose parent companies trade on major US exchanges, VAB "analyzed the brand level U.S. sales revenue between 2017 - 2025 for the calendar year before they returned to TV vs. the calendar year after they returned to TV." The results pages define the second year differently, as the "calendar year they returned to TV."
The 40 brands were then split. Twenty-nine went into the search analysis and 11 into the website analysis, with no brand counted in both. The six revenue brands - Avis, Cognizant, Remitly, Salesforce, Wise and Zynga - all come from the search group.
The 40 brands
The brands range from household names to start-ups. VAB groups them into 20 categories.
Most hiatuses were short. Thirty-four of the 40 brands, or 85%, were off TV for one year before returning. Four were off for two years, one for five and one for six. By calendar year, the dark periods cluster in the second half of the decade.
The counts add up to 53 because six brands were dark for several consecutive years. By brand age, 12 brands (30%) are under 15 years old, 16 (40%) are between 16 and 49 years old, and 12 (30%) are 50 or older.
The calendar matters for one reason the report does not discuss. Nielsen's streaming spend enters VAB's dataset only in August 2022. Of the 53 brand-years counted as dark, 27 - just over half - fall in 2017 to 2021, before any streaming purchase would have registered, according to a PPC Land calculation from the table above. A brand recorded as off TV in those years was off measured linear TV; it may still have bought streaming ads.
Back at twice the budget
VAB presents the spending figure as a strength of the returning brands. "Brands made a stronger commitment when they 'returned to TV' by more than doubling their annual investment vs. the year before their hiatus," the report states. The 40-brand average went from $2.3 million in the year before the break to $4.9 million in the first year back, an increase of $2.6 million.
Those are modest national budgets. They sit closer to the long tail of VAB's Welcome to TV report on 160 first-time national advertisers, published on November 9, than to the Super Bowl-scale buys that dominate TV coverage.
The doubling also complicates the reading of every result that follows. The comparison is not between being on TV and being off it at the same level of spending; it is between a dark year and a year with more than twice the earlier TV budget. The decision to return was the brands' own, too. A company that doubles its TV spend may be launching products, entering new markets or raising its other marketing at the same time, and the report does not track any of that. Statisticians call this problem endogeneity: when spending rises because of expected demand, the measured effect of the spending absorbs the effect of whatever caused it.
Search: 29 brands
The search analysis covers January 2016 to August 2026. Across 29 brands, the average monthly Google Trends index rose from 60.6 in the year off TV to 71.8 in the year back, which VAB reports as an 18% increase. Twenty-two of the 29, or 76%, reached their highest search interest - an index of 100 - during the year they were back on TV.
Google Trends does not count searches. The report gives Google's definition: the index "represents search interest relative to the highest point on the chart for the given region and time period, a value of 100 is the peak popularity for the term, a value of 50 means that the term is half as popular."
That definition bears on the 76% figure. In each of the six example charts VAB publishes, the window covers exactly 24 months - the year off and the year back. Within such a window, a brand's peak of 100 has to land in one of the two years. The finding therefore compares 22 brands that peaked in the year back with seven that peaked in the year off. Because the comparison pairs the same 12 calendar months in consecutive years, seasonal swings such as holiday shopping fall into both periods.

Website visitors: 11 brands
The second group - Adore Me, Columbia, Dodge, Huckberry, New Era, Omega, Qualcomm, Ruggable, The Economist, Varo and Vinted - is made up of brands that returned recently enough to fall inside Comscore's January 2023 to September 2026 window. Their average monthly unique visitors rose from 777,000 in the year off to 1.221 million in the year back, up 57%, or 444,000 visitors a month. VAB's headline puts it this way: "Brands returning to TV saw nearly 60% more unique website visitors on average, driving an influx of new customers to their digital storefront." The data counts visitors, not customers.

The average leans heavily on those five. Assuming the 777,000 and 1.221 million figures are simple averages across the 11 brands, the group's combined monthly visitors rose by about 4.88 million, according to a PPC Land calculation. The five published examples account for about 3.99 million of that, or 82%, and Vinted alone for about 31%. The six brands VAB does not show - Adore Me, New Era, Omega, Qualcomm, Ruggable and Varo - rose from about 3.86 million to 4.75 million combined, an increase of roughly 23%. That is still a rise, but well below the 57% headline.
Revenue: six brands
The sales section is the smallest. "Across the six brands we analyzed with sales data, annual revenues increased by 27% on average in the year they returned to TV," the report states, with an average increase of $729 million. VAB presents its tables as "Annual U.S. Sales Revenue," but the source notes show that three of the six figures measure something else.
The 27% is a simple average of six percentage changes, so Remitly's $135 million counts as much as Cognizant's $799 million. Added together, the six brands' revenue rose from $26.76 billion to $31.14 billion, an increase of 16.4%, according to a PPC Land calculation. Salesforce and Avis supply about 69% of the combined dollar increase, which is why the $729 million average is so much larger than four of the six individual changes.
Timing matters here as well. Zynga's year back, 2020, was the first year of the Covid-19 pandemic. Avis, Remitly and Cognizant compare 2021 with 2022, years shaped by the recovery from the pandemic and by high inflation.
