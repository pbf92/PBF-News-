TITLE: Google Ads surcharges hit 3% in France and Italy from January 2027
OUTLET: PPC Land

Google will raise the surcharge it adds to advertising bills for ads served in France and Italy to 3% on January 1, 2027, and will cut the equivalent charge for ads served in Turkey by half, from 4.5% to 2.25%, according to the jurisdiction-specific surcharges page of the Google Ads Help Center. France moves up a full percentage point from 2%. Italy moves up half a point from 2.5%. The help page carries no publication or update date. Google Payments notices on the French and Italian increases reached advertisers on November 11, 2026, according to Search Engine Journal.
In Short
Google will charge 3% on top of ad costs for ads shown in France and Italy from January 1, 2027, up from 2% and 2.5%, and will cut its Turkey charge from 4.5% to 2.25%. The fee follows where an ad is served, not where the advertiser is based, so any account reaching people in those countries pays it, and Google says it will not refund charges from traffic that matched campaign settings. The surcharge sits outside the ad price itself and appears as a separate line per country on invoices and in the account's Transactions section.
What changes on January 1
Three entries on the same Google Ads help page change on the same date. Each is labelled a Regulatory Operating Cost, and each applies to ads served in the named country rather than to advertisers registered there.
Google's Display & Video 360 page on country-specific fees lists the same three changes with the same January 1, 2027 date, so the new rates are not limited to Google Ads accounts. The help page for Google Ads also covers YouTube placements purchased on a reservation basis, which carry the surcharges under the same monthly schedule.
Three entries, two templates
The French and Italian entries are built from one template. The France text reads in full: "As of January 1, 2027, a 3% France Regulatory Operating Cost will be added to your next invoice or statement for ads served in France. Prior to January 1, 2027, the France Regulatory Operating Cost was 2%. The Regulatory Operating Costs are being added to cover part of the cost of complying with Digital Services Tax legislation in France."
Italy's entry is identical apart from the country name and the old rate: "As of January 1, 2027, a 3% Italy Regulatory Operating Cost will be added to your next invoice or statement for ads served in Italy. Prior to January 1, 2027, the Italy Regulatory Operating Cost was 2.5%." Both entries describe the prior rate in the past tense, although the date on which it stops applying has not yet arrived.
Turkey follows a different pattern. "As of January 1, 2027, the Turkey Regulatory Operating Cost has been reduced from 4.5% to 2.25%," the page states. "The Regulatory Operating Costs are being added due to significant increases in complexity and cost of complying with regulations in Turkey." The justification for a charge that is being halved still refers to "significant increases," and, unlike the French and Italian entries, it makes no reference to a digital services tax. Why does one text name the tax and the other not? The page offers no explanation.
The labels matter too. Google uses two names for these charges. Austria and the United Kingdom carry a "DST Fee," a direct reference to their digital services taxes. France, Italy, Spain and Turkey carry a "Regulatory Operating Cost." The help page treats both identically for billing and refunds and does not define a difference between them beyond the reasons stated in each country entry.
Turkey's cut follows a cut in Turkey's own tax
The halving of the Turkish surcharge coincides with a change in Turkish law. Turkey cut its digital services tax rate from 7.5% to 5% as of January 1, 2026, and to 2.5% as of January 1, 2027, under Presidential Decision No. 10767, published in the Official Gazette on December 25, 2025, according to EY.
The arithmetic lines up closely. Google's current 4.5% Turkish surcharge equals 90% of the 5% tax rate in force during 2026, and the coming 2.25% equals 90% of the 2.5% rate that applies from January 2027, a PPC Land calculation. Google's page does not describe the surcharge as a share of the national tax, so the ratio is an observation rather than a stated formula.
The Turkish charge has moved several times. When Google first passed digital tax costs to advertisers, Turkey received a 5% regulatory operating cost from November 2020, alongside 5% in Austria and 2% in the United Kingdom. The rate rose from 5% to 7% on July 1, 2024, according to PPC News Feed, which recorded the same round of changes as lifting Italy from 2% to 2.5% and Spain from 2% to 3%. Google's page now gives 4.5% as the Turkish rate that applies until the end of 2026.
How Google bills and reports the surcharge
According to the help page, the surcharge moves from ad delivery to the invoice in six stages. The page frames these as billing mechanics, and the wording below follows its description.
- Calculation. "Surcharges are levied based on the number of ad impressions or clicks that are served in a specific jurisdiction." An account advertising in several countries is charged only for the jurisdictions where a surcharge applies. Google's example: an advertiser running ads in Germany, Austria and Poland is "levied only the surcharges that correspond to the jurisdictions where the surcharges are applicable."
- Postpay and monthly invoicing. The surcharges are added to Google Ads costs "one time at the end of every month, to be paid the next time you're charged." They are also "added on top of your account budget." The page's example: a €100 account budget that accrues €5 in Austria DST fees is billed €105, plus any VAT.
- Taxes on top. The surcharges are themselves subject to sales tax, VAT, GST or QST where those apply, and those taxes are charged in addition to the surcharge.
- Prepayments. For prepay accounts, or prepayments made on a postpay account, the surcharges "may be charged after your payment has been fully spent." That can leave an open balance, deducted automatically from the next prepayment. The page's example uses Turkey: €7 of regulatory operating costs against a €100 payment leaves €93 of credit for ads.
- YouTube reservation placements. For YouTube placements bought on a reservation basis, the surcharges are added once at the end of each month and paid with the next charge. They also sit on top of any account budget.
- Where the charge appears. The surcharge shows as "a separate line item per jurisdiction" on the monthly invoice or statement, and in the "Transactions" section of the account. The page gives one navigation step: Transactions, within the Billing menu.
The account budget point deserves attention. An account budget does not cap the surcharge. It is added above the budget, so the invoiced amount for an account with ads served in France will exceed its budget by up to 3% from January, before VAT.
Served, not based
The page states the principle in one sentence. The surcharge "applies every time an ad is served in specific jurisdictions, regardless of where your actual business is located." From January, a Berlin retailer, a New York software company and a Paris agency will all pay the same 3% on ads served to people in France.
That principle interacts with location targeting in a way the page addresses directly. Campaigns can reach people in countries they do not explicitly target. Google's example: ads may show "to users in the United Kingdom because your business is in a location of interest for them." The page then places the burden on the account holder: "it's the advertiser's responsibility to ensure that your campaign target settings are correct and the ads are served in the intended jurisdictions."
On refunds, the wording is unambiguous. "Google will not refund the Regulatory Operating Costs or DST Fees that were levied as a result of legitimate clicks and impressions served according to your campaign target settings."
According to Google, a custom report showing which jurisdictions an account's ads reached takes five steps:
- Go to Report editor within the Insights and reports tab of the Campaigns menu.
- Select Distance from the template.
- Create a predefined "Distance" report.
- Remove all default rows.
- Add a "Country/Territory (user location)" row.
The page adds a recommendation of its own: "If you find that your ads are served in an unintended jurisdiction, it's recommended that you explicitly exclude the jurisdiction from your target setting."
The surcharge map from January
With the three changes, the help page lists six active jurisdictions and two that no longer carry a charge. France, Italy and Spain will share a single 3% rate. Austria stays the highest at 5%.
The Display & Video 360 version of the page gives different start dates for some of the older charges, listing July 1, 2023 for Austria, the United Kingdom and India, which suggests those surcharges reached Display & Video 360 later than Google Ads.
Canada shows how quickly a surcharge can appear and disappear. In August 2024, Google introduced a 2.5% surcharge for ads served in Canada starting October 1, 2024. Less than a year later, Canada rescinded its digital services tax on June 29, 2025, to advance trade talks with the United States, and Google's page now states that ads served in Canada are no longer subject to the fee as of July 1, 2025.
Google is not alone in passing these costs through. Amazon charges its own regulatory advertising fees in France, Italy, Spain, the United Kingdom, Austria and Turkey, and extended a 3% fee to Canadian advertisers from August 15, 2024. Meta began charging location fees on July 1, 2026, including 3% in France, Italy and Spain, according to Search Engine Journal. From January, Google and Meta will therefore apply the same 3% rate in all three countries.

The effect on efficiency metrics is small per campaign and cumulative across a portfolio. An account generating €40,000 in revenue from €10,000 of French media cost has a return on ad spend of 4.0 on media cost alone. Measured against media cost plus the surcharge, the figure is about 3.92 at the current rate and about 3.88 from January, a PPC Land calculation. For Italian traffic the gap widens by half as much. For Turkish traffic it narrows.
The reconciliation challenge is not new. Google added a line-by-line billing report to Google Ads in January 2026, with account, campaign, usage and payment details, and noted that final taxes and fees are calculated at the end of the month. According to the help page, the surcharges are added at that same month-end point.
What the page does not say
The help page carries no date, does not state what share of the national tax the surcharge covers beyond the words "part of the cost," and does not explain why the Turkish entry still cites rising compliance costs for a halved charge. It does not say whether the surcharge is visible to Smart Bidding or included in campaign cost columns, and it does not specify which time zone governs the January 1 switch for accounts billed outside Europe.
