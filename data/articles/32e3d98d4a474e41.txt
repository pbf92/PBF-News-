TITLE: ASCI report: Digital media accounts for 98% of ad violations, Meta leads
OUTLET: BusinessLine

Digital media accounted for nearly 98 per cent of advertising violations identified by the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) between April and September 2026. Meta platforms alone accounted for the largest share at 73.4 per cent of violations , with 1,563 ads found violate the ASCI Code; significant proportion related to offshore and illegal betting. Overall, ASCI reviewed 3,951 complaints during these six months, pertaining to 2,308 ads for potential violations.
Of the advertisements processed, 94 per cent required modification, 72 per cent were found to mislead consumers through dishonest claims, and 30 per cent promoted harmful products or unsafe situations, according to ASCI’s Half-Yearly Complaints Report 2026-27.
The self-regulatory body said 1,563 ads on Meta platforms violated the ASCI Code during the April-September period. “Of these, 90 per cent were revenue-generating sponsored advertisements rather than organic brand content, and 41 per cent of these were for categories where advertising is prohibited,” it added. It said a significant proportion related to offshore and illegal betting, and that these were flagged to the regulators and Meta for action.
“The findings are significant because the ads persist even after the introduction of the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025, which prohibits any online money games,” it added.
After offshore and illegal betting, the highest number of violations was recorded in personal care (456), followed by real estate, food and beverages, electronics and consumer durables, healthcare and education. “These seven categories accounted for 90% of advertising violations during the period,” it added.
Manisha Kapoor, CEO and Secretary General, ASCI, said: “Advertising is increasingly shaped by the speed and scale of digital platforms, making it important to ensure that consumer protection keeps pace with the changing environment. Our findings show that misleading claims, inadequate disclosures and advertisements for products prohibited by law continue to pose concerns for consumers. The rate of voluntary compliance has been steadily rising over the last four years, reflecting greater responsiveness among advertisers. However, the scale of violations also highlights the need for sustained vigilance and accountability across the ecosystem.”
ASCI said it referred nearly 633 cases related to restricted or prohibited advertising to the government. These included nearly 590 illegal betting and gambling advertisements, 28 cases under the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, 6 relating to alcohol and four related to National Media Council Provisions.
Of 659 advertisements processed for influencer-related violations, 95 per cent required modification. Nearly 263 influencer-related violations were in the personal care category, including exaggerated and unsubstantiated skin and hair transformation claims, misleading safety and ingredient claims, and unsubstantiated environmental assertions.
