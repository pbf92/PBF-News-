TITLE: ShellBench Exposes a 40% Failure Rate in Today’s Best AI Agents
OUTLET: The Futurum Group

OpenClaw's ShellBench benchmark, built on more than 10 million real-world agent interactions [1], reveals that even the top-performing model completes only 62.5% of tasks on average [1], while 15.7% of all 53,100 runs failed at least one safety check [1]. The findings arrive as 55.4% of enterprise decision-makers already rank AI agent reliability and hallucination management as a top GenAI adoption challenge [2], making ShellBench a timely and commercially relevant evaluation standard.

The News: OpenClaw introduced ShellBench [1], a new benchmark developed with researchers at The OpenClaw Foundation, Parsewave, Hugging Face, Red Hat, and UC Berkeley [1]. Drawing on more than 10 million anonymized, real-world AI agent interactions [1], ShellBench evaluated twelve models across five agent harnesses [1], producing 53,100 graded runs across 100 tasks [1]. Each scenario begins with a short request (median 43 words) and a realistic workspace (median 188 files) [1], incorporating complications such as outdated records, conflicting information, and phishing traps. Of the 100 tasks, 90 are rubric-graded against 721 criteria including 381 safety criteria, and 10 are code-tested [1].
ShellBench Exposes a 40% Failure Rate in Today's Best AI Agents
Analysis: ShellBench reframes the AI agent evaluation conversation by grounding it in the kind of work enterprises actually delegate: multi-step tasks with messy inputs, conflicting data, and embedded safety risks [1]. The results are a useful corrective to benchmark scores built on clean, single-answer problems. For enterprise buyers already citing agent reliability and hallucination management as their top GenAI adoption challenge [2], the data lands with immediate commercial weight.
A 40% Failure Rate at the Top of the Leaderboard
The headline finding is direct: GPT-6 Astra led average task completion across all runs at 62.5%, followed by Claude Fable 5.2 at 61.9% and GPT-6.1 Sol at 61.4%, with just 1.1 percentage points separating the top three [1]. That means the best available models still fail to satisfy every requirement on roughly four in ten attempts on average. The dinner-planning task illustrates the problem precisely: 522 of 531 attempts picked the right restaurant, but 331 failed solely because the recommendation did not name the files supporting the group's budget or timing, and only 152 met every requirement [1]. Retrieving the right answer and preserving the evidence trail are distinct capabilities, and today's agents routinely succeed at the former while stumbling on the latter. For enterprises deploying agents in workflow orchestration, a use case that 51.1% of decision-makers already prioritize for complex process automation and supply chain optimization [2], a missing evidence link can invalidate an otherwise correct output.
Safety Failures Hide Inside Correct Completions
Reliability and safety are separate failure modes, and ShellBench measures both. Across all 53,100 runs, 15.7% failed at least one safety check. More striking: even among the 26,151 runs that met every correctness criterion, 2,589 (9.9%) still failed a safety requirement [1]. Task-completion rate is therefore an insufficient proxy for production readiness. This finding maps directly onto a documented enterprise anxiety: in Futurum's 1H 2026 Cybersecurity Decision-Makers Survey, 55% of respondents said they have AI usage policies but lack the technical ability to detect or contain a compromised AI agent operating at machine speed, and 57% said they are highly concerned about the security implications of agentic AI [4]. ShellBench's safety criteria, which cover phishing attempts, fraudulent payment instructions, and irreversible actions, give that concern a measurable surface area. Consistency compounds the concern: GPT-6 Astra completed 75.1% of tasks at least once across three attempts but only 48.8% successfully on all three [1], confirming that single-run evaluations overstate production reliability.
Open-Source Harnesses Match Proprietary Performance
One of ShellBench's most commercially significant findings concerns harness choice. Average pass rates across the five harnesses were tightly grouped: OpenClaw (open source) at 45.23%, Pi (open source) at 45.22%, Hermes (open source) at 45.11%, Codex CLI (open source) at 45.04%, and Claude Code (proprietary) at 42.87%, a spread of just 2.36 percentage points [1]. OpenClaw and Pi were effectively tied at the top, just 0.01 percentage points apart, though Pi was faster at a median of 1.31 minutes per run versus OpenClaw's 2.86 minutes [1]. The model matters more than the harness: the median gap between highest- and lowest-scoring models within a harness was 45.7 percentage points, while switching harnesses with the model fixed produced a median gap of only 6.2 points [1]. That said, harness choice still shapes execution time, token usage, and output revision behavior, factors that matter for cost-sensitive deployments. GPT-6.1 Sol delivered 61.4% average task completion at a meaningfully lower cost per run than GPT-6 Astra's 62.5% [1], representing a significant cost differential for just 1.1 percentage points of performance.
