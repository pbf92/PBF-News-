TITLE: Digital Game Interventions Show Limited Attention Gains in Children With ADHD
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Adaptive game-based digital cognitive interventions produce a small improvement in objective attention among children with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in Child Psychiatry & Human Development. The systematic evaluation examined whether computerized gaming tasks that adjust dynamically to a player’s performance offer measurable cognitive and behavioral benefits for pediatric patients. Across eleven identified randomized and quasi-randomized trials representing 905 participants aged 6 to 13 years, the overall findings indicated that while targeted attention tasks yielded measurable progress, broader cognitive and behavioral improvements remained constrained.
To evaluate the existing evidence base, researchers conducted comprehensive searches across PubMed, the Cochrane Library, Scopus, and Web of Science through July 2026. The authors selected trials that tested adaptive digital tools against control conditions in pediatric ADHD populations. Investigators assessed methodological quality using the revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials and evaluated the overall certainty of the evidence using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework. Statistical analyses synthesized effect sizes using random-effects models with Hedges’ g, restricted maximum likelihood estimation, and Hartung–Knapp adjustments.
The meta-analysis evaluated two pre-specified primary cognitive outcomes: objective attention and working memory. Across seven trials reporting objective attention metrics, adaptive game-based interventions produced a statistically significant benefit, yielding a Hedges’ g of 0.29 with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 0.08 to 0.51 and an I-squared heterogeneity value of 19.1 percent. In contrast, working memory outcomes evaluated across six trials showed an effect size of 0.31 with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning 0.00 to 0.62 and an I-squared value of 46.3 percent, demonstrating a borderline outcome that did not achieve statistical significance.
Secondary behavioral evaluations focused on ADHD symptom domains reported by different observers. Interventions showed statistically significant reductions in caregiver-reported symptoms, with parent- or guardian-rated inattention yielding a Hedges’ g of minus 0.47 across eight studies, with a 95 percent confidence interval from minus 0.74 to minus 0.21 and an I-squared value of 6.0 percent. Similarly, parent-rated hyperactivity-impulsivity across eight studies demonstrated a Hedges’ g of minus 0.40, with a 95 percent confidence interval between minus 0.68 and minus 0.12 and an I-squared heterogeneity of 17.2 percent, favoring the digital tools.
Despite positive caregiver impressions, symptom reductions were not corroborated across other observational settings. The meta-analysis found no statistically significant improvements in symptom ratings provided by classroom teachers or by clinicians and independent investigators. Furthermore, analysis of total ADHD symptom severity revealed no significant overall effect. Broader cognitive domains assessed across the included literature, including inhibitory control, cognitive flexibility, reaction time, and reaction-time variability, also failed to demonstrate statistically significant differences compared to control conditions.
The certainty of the synthesized evidence varied substantially across domains under the GRADE framework. The investigators determined that the certainty of evidence was moderate for working memory findings, low for objective attention and parent-rated hyperactivity-impulsivity, and very low for total ADHD symptom severity. Because cognitive benefits were largely restricted to small gains in objective attention and behavioral improvements were confined to parent ratings, the authors concluded that adaptive digital games may serve as targeted adjuncts, but current evidence remains insufficient to establish broad clinical benefit.
