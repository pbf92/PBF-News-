Anthropic shares fall as investors question AI valuations
Trading at roughly 40 times trailing sales, the newly listed company faces scrutiny over whether years of rapid growth are already reflected in its price.
Anthropic's post-listing decline deepened the gloom surrounding the AI sector on Friday, underscoring how even one of the most eagerly anticipated stock market debuts in history has struggled to revive investor enthusiasm.
Widely seen as a proxy for the AI trade, Anthropic's listing had been viewed as a potential catalyst for an industry struggling to regain momentum after a turbulent stretch.
But the stock was last trading at $88.87, about 9% below its closing price following Thursday's smooth debut. At that price, Anthropic had a fully diluted valuation of approximately $1.6 trillion and a market capitalization of about $1.2 trillion, based on shares outstanding.
The stock could find support later this year. London-based index-rebalance analytics firm Intropic estimates that index-tracking funds could account for purchases of up to 30% of Anthropic's shares by year-end.
Near-term volatility, however, could persist as the initial enthusiasm surrounding the listing fades and investors assess whether a valuation of about 40 times trailing 12-month sales can be sustained.
The debut has also done little to ease broader concerns about the health of the AI trade. Wall Street's main indexes ended sharply lower on Thursday, while futures pointed to further declines on Friday.
Among the Magnificent Seven stocks, Nvidia and Meta Platforms fell more than 2% each in premarket trading, while Amazon and Microsoft dropped more than 1%.
Chip stocks also slid, with AMD and Sandisk down about 7% each and Intel losing more than 5%.
The focus now shifts to a potential OpenAI listing, which would provide another major test of investors' appetite for AI stocks. OpenAI has indicated that it may go public next year.
Anthropic's IPO has drawn comparisons with Elon Musk's SpaceX, which went public in June in the second-largest IPO on record.
Although the companies have different business models, some investors have pointed to the sharp swings in SpaceX shares in the weeks following its debut as a reason for caution about buying Anthropic immediately after its listing.
Retail investors traded approximately $772 million worth of Anthropic shares on Thursday, the highest volume for an IPO debut since Vanda Research began tracking the data in 2012.
The stock's sharp swings have left investors weighing whether to bet on a rebound or brace for further losses. Options trading, which is due to begin next week, could add to the volatility.
Competition could also weigh on investor sentiment. Apple is developing an AI agent that can perform tasks on behalf of users, entering a market already being pursued by OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta, Bloomberg News reported on Friday.
