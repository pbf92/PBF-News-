Woodward earnings: What to look for
Aerospace and defense company Woodward WWD will be reporting results this Wednesday after market hours. Here’s what to look for.
Woodward met analysts’ revenue expectations last quarter, reporting revenues of $1.11 billion, up 21.2% year on year. It was a solid quarter for the company, with a solid beat of analysts’ EBITDA estimates and a beat of analysts’ EPS estimates.
Is Woodward a buy or sell going into earnings? Read our full analysis here, it’s free for active Edge members.
This quarter, the market is expecting Woodward’s revenue to grow 14.5% year on year, slowing from the 16.5% increase it recorded in the same quarter last year.
Analysts covering the company have generally reconfirmed their estimates over the last 30 days, suggesting they anticipate the business will stay the course heading into earnings. Woodward rarely misses Wall Street’s revenue estimates.
Looking at Woodward’s peers in the aerospace segment, some have already reported their Q3 results, giving us a hint as to what we can expect. Rocket Lab delivered year-on-year revenue growth of 72.7%, beating analysts’ expectations by 3.8%, and ATI reported revenues up 26.5%, topping estimates by 7.9%. Rocket Lab traded up 6.5% following the results while ATI’s stock price was unchanged.

Investors in the aerospace segment have had steady hands going into earnings, with share prices flat over the last month. Woodward is down 5.7% during the same time and is heading into earnings with an average analyst price target of $420.50 (compared to the current share price of $310.48).
