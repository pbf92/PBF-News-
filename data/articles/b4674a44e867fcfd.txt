DJ Khaled’s Dr. Xbox Doctor’s Note Prescribes a Day Off for GTA 6
DJ Khaled has a new job title for the week: Dr. Xbox. In a post on Xbox’s official blog on 16 November, the music star diagnosed gamers worldwide with “Vitamin G deficiency” and pointed them to DrXbox.com, a site that generates a personalised doctor’s note prescribing a day off for “the biggest week in gaming”.
Neither the post nor the note mentions Grand Theft Auto 6, but the calendar fills in the blank. Rockstar Games’ GTA 6 launches on Thursday 19 November on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S at US$80, and Xbox One owners can stream it through Xbox Cloud Gaming.
What the Dr. Xbox Note Prescribes
The diagnosis comes with a full list of symptoms. Khaled’s post names twitchy thumbs, controller cramps, late-night gaming dreams and “release-date restlessness”, all identified, by his own account, after “extensive research (and a whole lot of gaming)”. His treatment plan fits in four words: “Take. The. Day. Off.”
Getting the note is simple. You enter your name, and DrXbox.com produces a note signed by Dr. Xbox from “the sunny shores of tropical Miami”, in which he describes himself as the patient’s “physician, mentor, and professional motivator”. The prescription tells you to avoid all meetings, highly stressful jobs and unnecessary negativity, which on a Thursday rules out most of the working population. Khaled suggests sticking the finished note on your fridge, showing it to your boss, or sending it to the group chat.
Dr. Xbox’s Credentials and the Fine Print
Khaled is upfront about his qualifications. His degree comes from XBOX University, which he admits may be unofficial, and he calls his own medical credentials “questionable”. Xbox’s footnote is less playful: “Doctor’s note is not real. XBOX is not responsible if your employer doesn’t have a sense of humor.”
So the note works as something to screenshot, not something to hand to HR. Anyone who genuinely needs Thursday free is better off filing a leave request this week and keeping Dr. Xbox for the group chat.
Why Dr. Xbox Never Says GTA 6
Khaled only ever calls it “a BIG week in gaming” and never says what makes it big. There’s a reason for that: Sony reportedly holds a marketing deal for GTA 6, which leaves Xbox promoting a game it sells without being able to say the name. The result is a sick note for an illness with one obvious cause and no official name.
The workaround hasn’t always gone smoothly. The first version of GTA 6’s TV spot left Xbox consoles out entirely. Xbox hardly needs to spell it out now, though, since Take-Two Interactive, Rockstar’s parent company, has already described the game’s presales as “unprecedented”. The stunt also plays on predictions from media industry analysts that a large number of players will take Thursday off anyway.
GTA 6 on Xbox: Platforms, Cloud Play, and Performance
Xbox did get one concrete win into the launch. Because GTA 6 is available through Xbox Cloud Gaming, Xbox One owners, who aren’t getting a native version, can stream the game at Xbox Series S graphics settings.
What those settings actually look like is still unclear. Rockstar has said GTA 6 runs at 30fps on every platform, with ray tracing upgrades on PS5 Pro, but the studio hasn’t explained what the Series S version gives up to hit that target. That’s one diagnosis even Dr. Xbox hasn’t attempted.
