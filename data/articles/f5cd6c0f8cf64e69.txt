TITLE: Digital platforms account for 98% of advertising violations:ASCI Report
OUTLET: ET BrandEquity

Digital platforms account for 98% of advertising violations:ASCI Report
                    In the first half of 2026, digital media became the primary source of advertising violations in India, accounting for an overwhelming 98 per cent of all reported cases. A large share of these infractions stemmed from Meta, where 94 per cent necessitated content revisions, and 72 per cent  contained deceptive claims. This surge in public grievances, coupled with ongoing hurdles in influencer marketing, underscores a critical need for enhanced consumer protection vigilance.
                                    
Highlights
- Digital media accounted for 98 per cent of advertising violations.
- Meta was responsible for 73.4 per cent of these violations.
- 85 per cent of violations were identified through proactive monitoring.
                    
                    
                    
                                        Digital media accounted for 98 per cent of advertising violations identified by the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) between April and September 2026, with Meta alone accounting for 73.4 per cent of violations under the ASCI Code, according to ASCI’s half-yearly complaints report for 2026-27.
During the six-month period, ASCI reviewed 3,951 complaints relating to 2,308 advertisements. Of these, 94 per cent required modification, while 72 per cent were found to contain misleading claims and 30 per cent promoted harmful products or unsafe situations.
ASCI found that 85 per cent of violations were identified through its proactive monitoring rather than consumer complaints. Advertiser compliance following ASCI’s intervention stood at 86 per cent during the reporting period, unchanged from FY 2025-26. Compliance has risen from 80 per cent in FY 2023-24 to 83 per cent in FY 2024-25 and 86 per cent in FY 2025-26.
Public complaints also increased during the period, rising 20 per cent from 1,124 in April-September FY 2024-25 to 1,347 in the corresponding period of FY 2026-27. Of the complaints received in the latest reporting period, 84 per cent were submitted through ASCI’s TARA platform.
Between April and September, 1,563 advertisements on Meta platforms were found to have violated the ASCI Code. Of these, 90 per cent were sponsored advertisements generating revenue, rather than organic brand content, while 41 per cent promoted products or services in categories where advertising is prohibited.
A significant number of these cases involved offshore and illegal betting advertisements, which were flagged to regulators and Meta for action. The findings come despite the introduction of the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025, which prohibits online money games.
Separately, 333 real estate advertisements were referred to the Telangana Real Estate Regulatory Authority (TGRERA) under an MoU with the regulator for violations of the RERA Act. Another 136 advertisements were referred to relevant regulatory authorities for continued non-compliance.
Betting advertisements accounted for a significant share of the cases referred to the government. ASCI found that 99 per cent of the 590 cases were identified through proactive surveillance, while sponsored advertisements accounted for 98 per cent of the cases. The advertisements were reported to the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) and the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting.
Beyond betting, personal care recorded 456 violations, followed by real estate with 360, food and beverages with 312, electronics and consumer durables with 95, healthcare with 82 and education with 68. Together with betting, these categories accounted for 90 per cent of advertising violations during the period.
Influencer advertising also remained an area of concern. Of the 659 advertisements processed for influencer-related violations, 95 per cent required modification. Personal care recorded 263 such violations, with concerns including exaggerated skin and hair transformation claims, unsubstantiated product claims, misleading safety and ingredient claims, and unsupported environmental assertions.
In food and beverages, 74 per cent of the 312 violations related to nutraceutical products. ASCI flagged claims concerning conditions such as PCOD, anaemia, cancer risk and heart health, alongside rapid weight-loss claims that lacked adequate substantiation. Influencer disclosure was another concern, accounting for 181 violations in the category.
The electronics and consumer durables category recorded 75 violations, largely linked to mobile phone launches.
ASCI reported that 83.5 per cent of influencer advertisements were not contested by advertisers and were modified within an average of six business days.
Manisha Kapoor, CEO and secretary general, ASCI, said: “Advertising is increasingly shaped by the speed and scale of digital platforms, making it important to ensure that consumer protection keeps pace with the changing environment. The rate of voluntary compliance has been steadily rising over the last four years reflecting greater responsiveness among advertisers,
However the scale of violations also highlights the need for sustained vigilance and accountability across the ecosystem.”
ASCI also reported a reduction in the time taken to resolve complaints. The average turnaround time for formally investigated complaints fell to 15 days during April-September 2026, compared with 30 days in FY 2023-24.
The proportion of cases not contested by advertisers increased from 49 per cent in FY 2023-24 to 71 per cent during April-September 2026. These cases took an average of eight days to close.
