TITLE: No invite for China at Anthropic IPO: After SpaceX, Claude parent too shuts Chinese investors out
OUTLET: The Times of India

Everyone wants a slice of the AI bigfoot, but where you come from could decide whether you get one!
Earlier billionaire Elon Musk's AI and aerospace giant 
SpaceX
 barred investors from Hong Kong and mainland China from buying its shares. Now, Anthropic followed a similar route. While the IPO was open only to investors from selected countries, the repeated exclusion of Chinese investors by major US technology companies raises questions.
The artificial intelligence company behind the Claude chatbot reportedly turned away investment from Chinese and Hong Kong firms ahead of its public listing, in what went on to record as the biggest initial public offering (IPO) in history.
Three people familiar with the decision said Anthropic had excluded investors from the two regions, New York Times reported.
The move comes amid growing US efforts to restrict China's access to advanced artificial intelligence technology. As Washington and Beijing compete for supremacy in AI, technology and investment are increasingly becoming flashpoints in their already strained relationship.
Why keep Chinese investors at arm's length?
The exclusion comes against the backdrop of a growing technology rivalry between the US and China. 
The Trump administration has been taking steps to restrict China's access to advanced AI technology, while both countries are drawing red lines around their homegrown technologies.
Anthropic's decision appears in line with a wider trend among major American technology companies. SpaceX and its bankers had taken a similar step ahead of the company's blockbuster IPO in July, shutting Chinese investors out of the offering.
OpenAI, another leading AI company, is also expected to bar Chinese investors from its eventual public listing. It has already prevented investors from China from participating in its private fund-raising rounds.
There is another common thread connecting all three companies: Anthropic, SpaceX and OpenAI have all counted the US government among their clients.
Trump and Xi may be talking, but technology remains a sticking point
The decision also comes at an interesting moment in US-China relations. US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping met on Thursday at the APEC meeting in Shenzhen, China, with both leaders looking to bring some stability and goodwill into an increasingly rocky economic relationship.
During the meeting, Xi called on members to build an "open ecosystem" for technology. Yet, while the two leaders are attempting to improve ties, their governments continue to build barriers around trade, technology and capital.
The latest developments around AI chips underline just how difficult that balancing act is. Hours before Trump arrived in Asia for meetings with regional leaders, his administration moved to restrict China's access to advanced artificial intelligence chips.
Beijing was quick to warn that it could respond with countermeasures if Washington continued with the restrictions.
"If the US persists, China will take countermeasures," China warned.
A spokesman for China's Ministry of Commerce also expressed strong opposition to the latest US measures on Thursday.
"China is deeply concerned about — and firmly opposes — a series of recent economic and trade restrictive measures taken by the US against China," the spokesman told NYT.
The latest restrictions on artificial intelligence are adding to a series of escalating moves by both countries. China experts have warned that the new AI rule could make it harder for Trump and Xi to reach any meaningful agreement to ease tensions.
