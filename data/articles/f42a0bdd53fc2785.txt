Back to the future! Nvidia earnings/forecast lifts Nasdaq futures 1.3%
Summary
- Nasdaq futures rose about 1.3% after Nvidia beat forecasts, guided Q4 revenue to $130bn and forecast about 75% growth next year. The board ruled out Huang’s return, while a reported SEC probe and a delayed 10-Q keep governance risks in view.
The bounce in Nasdaq futures should spread to Asia’s chip supply chain, with Taiwan, South Korea and Japan’s semiconductor names the most direct beneficiaries of Nvidia’s comments on pricing and supply deals. The bigger test is whether the gains survive the US cash open, since after-hours rallies on single-stock news can fade once wider liquidity returns. The open governance issues are timed risks: Treasury Secretary Bessent’s 10-day deadline, the 10-Q filing due by early December and any update on the CEO search could each move the stock and, through it, the index. For now, traders have taken the demand story at face value.

Nasdaq futures rose around 1.3% on Globex after Nvidia’s results and earnings call, a reminder of how much weight a single company’s numbers can still carry for the wider market. The rebound follows a bruising November for AI stocks, with Nvidia’s own shares down about a quarter this month before the report.
The numbers that turned sentiment
Nvidia beat forecasts across the board. Third-quarter revenue rose 103% from a year earlier to around $116 billion, against expectations of about $109 billion, and adjusted earnings came in at $2.68 a share. Fourth-quarter revenue is guided to $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, above estimates of about $125 billion.
The call did more to lift the mood. Interim chief executive Colette Kress said she expects revenue to grow about 75% in fiscal 2028 and pointed to a cloud-industry backlog of $2.5 trillion, up from $2 trillion three months earlier. Older chips are commanding higher prices, with Hopper contracts renewing at up to double their original level. Nvidia also said it intends to return all excess free cash flow to shareholders.
The governance overhang remains
Chairman Harvey Jones said the board does not intend to reinstate Jensen Huang, who resigned last week, and has hired Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent chief executive. He declined to discuss Huang’s departure or the board’s investigation. Shares fell after hours at first, then recovered to trade nearly 3% higher during the call.
Other questions remain open. The Wall Street Journal reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia, including whether its disclosures misled investors, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wants answers from the board within 10 days. Nvidia has also not yet filed its quarterly 10-Q alongside its results, breaking with its usual practice, although it has until early December.
The balance sheet story
Nvidia’s role as a financier of the AI industry is also drawing attention. The Financial Times reported total commitments of about $518 billion, including $390 billion to lock in supply, mainly of memory chips. Receivables rose almost a third in the quarter to about $81 billion.
What comes next
The futures rally shows the market wanted proof that AI demand is intact, and Nvidia supplied it. Whether the gains hold through the US cash session is another matter. Demand is no longer the main question for Nvidia. Leadership, regulators and the 10-Q are, and any of them could test the recovery in the days ahead.
