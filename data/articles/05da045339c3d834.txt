TITLE: Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says Claude helped prove the Riemann hypothesis, verification pending
OUTLET: Crypto Briefing

Levent Alpöge's claim arrived one day after Anthropic's IPO, but the Clay Mathematics Institute still lists the problem as unsolved
One day after Anthropic went public, one of its mathematicians announced that the company’s AI may have done something humans have failed to do since 1859.
On November 20, 2026, Levent Alpöge said he worked with Anthropic’s Claude model to produce a claimed proof of the Riemann hypothesis. That is a 167-year-old problem and one of the most famous open questions in mathematics. Independent checks are still underway, so for now this is a claim, not a coronation.
What Alpöge is claiming
Alpöge is a Turkish-American mathematician at Anthropic. He described the effort as a collaboration with Claude rather than a solo human achievement or a fully autonomous machine result.
The work reportedly ran for about six weeks. It started in early October and wrapped on November 16, 2026, four days before the announcement.
The output is large. The proof was formalized in Lean, a proof assistant: software designed to check every logical step so that nothing rests on a mathematician’s say-so.
The Lean archive contains approximately 175,000 source files and totals 239 MB. It comes paired with a four-page note that presumably serves as the human-readable summary.
Reports suggest the total machine-generated text could exceed 250,000 pages.

The Clay Institute isn’t popping champagne yet
The Riemann hypothesis is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems. Each carries a $1 million award from the Clay Mathematics Institute.
As of now, the institute continues to list the hypothesis as unsolved. That is not a snub. It is how the process works.
To be eligible for the prize, a solution needs a peer-reviewed publication. It then faces a two-year scrutiny period. So even in the best case, the check is not getting cut anytime soon.
Not Alpöge’s first headline this year
This is not the first time Alpöge has made noise in 2026. Earlier this year, he was credited with breakthroughs that included counterexamples to the Jacobian and Carathéodory conjectures.
A counterexample shows a conjecture is false by finding a case where it breaks. Proving a conjecture true across every case is a different and usually harder task. The Riemann claim falls in that second, more demanding category.
The timing question
Anthropic priced its IPO on November 19, 2026, at $83 per share. The Riemann announcement landed the very next day.
The source material does not link the two events, and there is no indication the timing was coordinated. Still, the sequence is hard to ignore.
What to watch
What to watch: the progress of independent verification of the Lean archive, whether a peer-reviewed paper appears, and whether the Clay Institute changes the problem’s status. The two-year scrutiny clock does not start until publication, so patience is mandatory.
For now, the most accurate summary is the boring one. A credible mathematician says Claude helped him prove one of math’s hardest problems. The machine checked its own homework, and the humans are now checking the machine.
