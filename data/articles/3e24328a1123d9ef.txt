Wall Street ends a chaotic week on high as AI remains in focus
Wall Street ended a turbulent week with stocks higher as a renewed rally in artificial-intelligence shares gained momentum, easing demand for Treasuries and safe-haven currencies.
The AI rebound lifted the Nasdaq 100 by 2.6%, following a decline that brought it close to bear-market territory. The S&P 500 recorded its strongest weekly performance since April 2025. In the post-Thanksgiving session, stock and bond markets closed early.
Investors will be monitoring whether the steepest losses have passed after weeks of concern over returns on substantial AI investments. Questions about higher borrowing costs, regulation, and technology-related risks remain unresolved.
The latest market move trimmed a recent rally in which traders reduced expectations for Federal Reserve rate hikes over the coming year, despite persistent inflation. Even so, money markets continued to price in a strong likelihood of a December increase.
Two Chinese companies installed Nvidia Corp.’s advanced Blackwell semiconductors at a Shanghai data centre associated with Alice Huang, the woman central to a controversy involving the US chipmaker.
US technology stocks led the market lower as concerns over inflation and a hawkish Federal Reserve weighed on sentiment. Investors also evaluated how tariffs could affect global supply chains and economic growth. Treasury prices climbed as investors sought safe-haven assets.
MSCI’s global equity index declined 0.60%. Nvidia fell more than 8% at one point before ending the session down 3.91%.
Anthropic PBC shares rose back above their initial public offering price of $83.
US Treasury yields fell on Wednesday, though they recovered from earlier lows. The yield on the benchmark 10-year Treasury note dropped 6.2 basis points to 4.84%, putting it on track for its seventh daily decline in eight sessions.
Nvidia surrendered some of its gains from earlier in the week, leaving its shares down about 10% since Monday, when CEO Jensen Huang’s return triggered a 31.5% rally. S&P Global Ratings said the company faced the risk of a rating downgrade.
Data showed that US consumer confidence rose more than expected in November, reaching its highest level in nearly two years. Separately, figures indicated that the US economy grew considerably faster than first estimated in the third quarter, while inflation rose further in October.
