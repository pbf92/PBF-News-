Psychological Stress-Based EHR Alerts May Help Reduce Unnecessary Preoperative Tests Before Cataract Surgery: Study
A randomized clinical trial evaluating electronic health record (EHR)-based nudges found no statistically significant reduction in overall preoperative testing for cataract surgery, the primary study outcome. Secondary findings suggest that incorporating psychological stress-related nudges alongside behavioural interventions, workflow modifications, and/or financial incentives may improve effectiveness. The clinical Implication of this is that EHR nudges alone may be insufficient to change preoperative testing practices for cataract surgery; multifaceted interventions warrant further investigation. The study was published in JAMA Ophthalmology by Catherine A. and colleagues.
To investigate whether behaviorally-based interruptive EHR reminders would help to decrease low-value pre-operative testing, researchers performed a pragmatic, randomized controlled clinical study at the UCLA Health system in Los Angeles, CA. Physicians performing pre-operative testing were randomly assigned either to receive an interruptive reminder alert or to be in the group that receives the usual care without the reminder. Patients' visits within the timeframe of 30 days prior to undergoing cataract surgery were silently included in the study from June 24, 2021, to April 11, 2023, with the data analysis being performed during June 2020-April 2024.
All three EHR reminders were based on the guidelines provided by professional societies and indicated no benefit in terms of patient safety, financial implications, or increased patient stress. The primary study goal was the change in proportions of visits with pre-operative testing orders before and after the intervention. The secondary goals include cancellation of tests due to the alert reminder, efficacy of the reminder in individual cases, and test rates in certain diagnostic categories.
Key findings:
- The evaluation in terms of 229 doctors participating in enrollment procedures (from a total number of 776 randomized doctors) managing 1,045 preoperative consultations (among 967 patients, on average aged 72.1 ± 9.1 years; 595 [56.9%] women) was characterized by minor numerical changes without primary statistical significance.
- The mean rate of signed preoperative tests dropped from 83% (± 29%) to 79% (± 31%) in case of intervention versus a drop from 84% (± 30%) to 82% (± 31%) in the control group.
- With the use of the mixed-effects model, there is no statistically significant association of the exposure to the EHR nudge with the decrease in overall testing (adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 0.68; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.34–1.34).
- Secondary process evaluation found that 16% of the signed test orders were immediately cancelled by the clinician after being exposed to interruptive EHR nudge.
- The alert highlighting the patient psychological stress led to targeted efficacy with the reduction of test orders for blood cell counts (aOR, 0.37; 95% CI, 0.18–0.75; P = 0.01) and electrocardiograms (aOR, 0.42; 95% CI, 0.21–0.83; P = 0.01) in comparison with the control.
In summary, the results of randomized trials indicate that the use of behavioral nudges embedded in an EHR does not significantly decrease preoperative cataract tests. It is noteworthy that although specific messaging on psychological stress was able to lower the number of ordered tests, it is clear that changing low-value care behaviors necessitates behavioral nudges together with workflow and payment changes.
Reference:
Sarkisian CA, Villaflores CW, Vangala SS, et al. Effect of Electronic Behavioral Nudges on Low-Value Preoperative Testing: A Pragmatic Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Ophthalmol. Published online November 05, 2026. doi:10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2026.4950
