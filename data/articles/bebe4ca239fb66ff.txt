TITLE: 'Grand Theft Auto VI' releases deluxe edition of soundtrack after staging music festival in Miami
OUTLET: Yahoo Tech

Grand Theft Auto VI is the biggest entertainment launch in history — and music is once again a huge part of it.
On Friday, Atlantic Records and Rockstar Games released Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album, a 34-track double LP featuring some of the world's biggest artists, including Dua Lipa, Cardi B, Lana Del Rey, Gunna, Yung Lean, Travis Scott, Morgan Wallen, Keith Richards and two tracks by Justice: one featuring Tame Impala, SZA and King Gizzard and the Lizard Wizard (which doubles as the theme song), the other a collaboration with yacht rock legend Michael McDonald.
Then on Monday, Atlantic and Rockstar announced the surprise release of a deluxe edition of the soundtrack with an additional 23 songs by artists including Slayyyter, 21 Savage, Offset, Denzel Curry and Overmono, a Welsh electronic music duo who contributed five tracks to the project.

The game itself features two dozen virtual radio stations (with names like Back Country Radio, Cocoteo FM and the Chamber 106.6) hosted and curated by artists including Bad Bunny, Lana Del Rey, Morgan Wallen, Robyn and Burna Boy, which gamers can listen to while exploring Leonida, GTA's sun-drenched, neon-hued fictional Florida-like state.
Justice's 'first and last' video game score
For GTA 6, Justice also composed a three-hour, 50-track original score, which will be released on Dec. 11. The French duo of Gaspard Augé and Xavier de Rosnay recently said that it will "probably be the first and last" video game score they'll ever do.
Not that they weren't appreciative. The dance titans said that they consider the score a "real" Justice album, even though each track was tailored for a particular scene.
"GTA 6 gave us the excuse and direction to do it," they said. "The music always had to be listenable on its own."
The 'Vice City' blueprint
When it comes to video game soundtracks, the Grand Theft Auto franchise is widely credited with pioneering the approach. In 2002, the soundtrack to Grand Theft Auto: Vice City was broken up into a seven-album box set that featured a literal who's who of '80s hitmakers: Hall and Oates, Michael Jackson, INXS, Tears for Fears, Kim Wilde, Blondie, A Flock of Seagulls, the Human League, Foreigner, Mr. Mister, Roxy Music, Squeeze, REO Speedwagon, Wang Chung, Bryan Adams, Lionel Richie, Toto, Mötley Crüe, Megadeth, Ozzy Osbourne, Anthrax, Iron Maiden, Slayer, David Lee Roth, Quiet Riot, Loverboy, Herbie Hancock, Run-D.M.C. and Grandmaster Flash and the Furious Five.
"The Grand Theft Auto: Vice City soundtrack albums will arguably represent the most diverse, extraordinary, and innovative collection of music ever assembled for any piece of entertainment — whether it be video games, television, or feature films," Terry Donovan, Rockstar's co-founder, said at the time. "Every track was lovingly and laboriously chosen to reflect the vast array of '80s musical styles and create the perfect audio landscape for Grand Theft Auto: Vice City."
The 'GTA' festival
On Saturday, more than 10,000 GTA fans descended on Miami's Mana Wynwood Convention Center for "Grand Theft Auto VI: The Festival," an event featuring live musical performances from some of the soundtrack's artists, including Cardi B, Travis Scott, Don Toliver and Kodak Black, as well as a DJ set by Justice.
The neighborhood itself was already dotted with billboards and graffiti promoting the game's fictional "Vice City." And there were plenty of unofficial afterparties from Wynwood to South Beach.
As Osvaldo Espino wrote in the Miami New Times, "the city's music scene is bringing the game to life, one party at a time."
