TITLE: Scouts fear Andy Burnham's tourist tax could price kids out of camping trips
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

Scouts fear Andy Burnham's tourist tax could price kids out of camping trips
A Manchester Scout leader dubbed the levy the 'tent tax'
Scout groups fear Andy Burnham's tourist tax could price some kids out of camping trips. The organisation warned they could face a £1 million annual bill as a result of the levy, which one Manchester troupe leader dubbed the 'tent tax'.
Ministers have said local areas will be able to decide on exemptions but the Scout Association has called for an explicit England-wide commitment to spare youth groups from the charge. The Visitor Levy Bill will give mayors and local leaders the ability to introduce a fee on overnight stays.
Labour regional mayors have indicated they will cap the levy at 5% but there is no maximum limit in the legislation. The Scout Association warned the tax they would face could cost the charity £1 million across the country, based on a 5% charge on the 1,423,344 annual overnight stays across the organisation's Squirrels, Beavers, Cubs, Scouts, Explorers and volunteers.

Chief Scout Dwayne Fields said: "We strongly support investment being made available to strengthen local communities. Well-crafted legislation can unlock that opportunity.
"But we are firmly opposed to any proposal that would see local mayors tax Scouts and other voluntary youth organisations for camping trips that equip young people with skills for life, while burdening volunteers with the role of unpaid government tax collectors."
MPs challenged Communities Secretary Angela Rayner about the issue when the legislation was first debated in the Commons.
She told the Commons that local leaders 'will be able to make exemptions' and the Government expects them 'to factor various needs into the decision-making process, including those of medical visitors, disabled visitors and children, as well as the need for simplicity for business'. Devolution minister Sally Jameson said strategic authorities would be able to exempt 'lower-cost accommodation that is commonly used for educational trips and groups such as Scouts and Squirrels'.
But the Scout Association said access to affordable activities should not depend on where they take place and there should be a nationwide exemption. The organisation highlighted the Linnet Clough site in Greater Manchester, which has 25,000 visitors a year.
Greater Manchester East Scouts estimates that without an exemption, the proposed levy would add £11,000 a year to the costs faced by Scout groups using the site, while collecting the tax would cost more than £6,700 a year.
Chris Taylor, lead volunteer for Greater Manchester East Scouts, said: "This new tent tax is madness."

Labour MP Gareth Snell, who has already challenged ministers in the Commons, said: "Scouts provide some of the most memorable experiences for young people up and down the country. None more so than those made on camps, roasting marshmallows, building dens and learning new skills.
"For young people in the Scouting movement, these might be the only time they spend nights away from home. It is inconceivable that, when designing this new levy, the Government would have proactively considered including Scouts, Cubs or Squirrels camping with their troops at designated Scout camp sites.
"While mayors may choose to take these venues and activities out of any levy they may raise, the easiest and fairest way to ensure that those camps are excluded from any levy is to write it into the Bill."
