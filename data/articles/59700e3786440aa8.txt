TITLE: Global Meta-Analysis Reveals High MRSA Burden and Clonal Convergence Across Continents
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis examining the molecular epidemiology of methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus across six continents has found that approximately one out of every three clinical and community isolates globally exhibits resistance to methicillin. Synthesizing data published between 1998 and 2025 according to the PRISMA 2020 guidelines, the investigation calculated a global pooled MRSA prevalence of 31 percent, with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning from 19 to 47 percent under a restricted maximum likelihood random-effects model. The analysis captured an extensive spectrum of epidemiological settings, highlighting marked global disparities in pathogen distribution alongside an alarming degree of between-study variability that underscores how healthcare infrastructure, infection control programs, and antibiotic management practices shape the ongoing spread of resistant bacteria worldwide.
The study protocol relied on broad database searches of PubMed, Scopus, and the Cochrane Library, supplemented by Google Scholar and manual screening of cross-referenced literature and surveillance records to track resistance across diverse human populations. Following the rigorous screening of 1,543 initial records and the evaluation of 363 full-text articles against pre-specified criteria requiring human clinical or carriage data, clear numerical denominators, and molecular or phenotypic confirmation, a total of 100 studies were retained for qualitative synthesis. Among these, 22 observational studies contributed explicit numerator and denominator data representing 1,936 MRSA cases extracted from 5,399 total evaluated isolates or individuals. Study-level proportions fluctuated across a wide margin from a low of 2.9 percent in a Gabonese cohort to a peak of 94.9 percent in a comprehensive multicenter surveillance initiative across Saudi Arabia, reflecting deep geographical and institutional divergence.
Geographic subgroup analyses revealed that the highest concentrations of MRSA burden occur within the Middle East and Africa, contrasting sharply with rates documented in other parts of the world. In the regional analysis, the Middle East exhibited the highest pooled prevalence at roughly 50 percent, bounded by a 95 percent confidence interval of 9 to 91 percent, closely followed by Africa with a pooled estimate of 45 percent and a confidence interval spanning 12 to 84 percent. Conversely, Asian cohorts demonstrated intermediate distributions, while European investigations documented the lowest regional prevalence at approximately 12 percent, within a 95 percent confidence interval of 3 to 34 percent. Across the evaluated literature, European and North American environments have experienced progressive declines or stabilized reservoirs driven by targeted hygiene campaigns and stewardship protocols, whereas developing settings face persistent transmission hurdles.
The quantitative synthesis revealed extraordinary statistical heterogeneity across the global literature, recording an overall inconsistency metric of 98.3 percent with an estimated between-study variance parameter of 2.137. Subgroup partitions displayed similarly high levels of internal dispersion, including 99.2 percent in the Middle East and 96.6 percent in Africa, demonstrating that substantial operational variability persists even among geographically clustered nations. Tests evaluating regional differences yielded a borderline statistically significant result across groups, while supplementary sensitivity analyses, including iterative leave-one-out testing, confirmed that no individual dataset artificially dictated the overall conclusions. Omitting single investigations shifted the overall pooled point estimates within a stable margin between 28 percent and 34 percent, and verifying the findings through the Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation produced a congruent pooled prevalence of 33 percent, validating the internal mathematical stability of the model.
To formally investigate potential contributors to this elevated heterogeneity, the researcher conducted an exploratory random-effects meta-regression incorporating geographic region and publication year as potential moderating variables. When evaluating the subset of studies supplying full prevalence data, incorporating geographic region accounted for roughly 36 percent of the overall heterogeneity metric, with regional point estimates aligning directionally with subgroup findings, showing the Middle East with a positive arcsine coefficient of 0.259 relative to the African baseline and Europe showing a negative coefficient of 0.241. A separate regression tracking publication years across the eleven studies that explicitly reported sampling dates suggested a modest upward temporal progression of 0.029 arcsine units per year, though this trend did not achieve conventional statistical significance. Substantial residual variance remained unexplained across both models, demonstrating that geographic and temporal factors alone cannot fully account for the broad differences in clinical study populations, sampling frames, and laboratory identification techniques.
Molecular characterization of the isolates documented a worldwide evolutionary transition marked by the replacement of traditional hospital-associated strains with flexible, community-associated clones possessing enhanced environmental fitness. Historically dominant hospital lineages, exemplified by the multi-resistant ST239 carrying staphylococcal cassette chromosome mec type III elements, have been progressively displaced across multiple continents by lineages such as ST22 carrying SCCmec type IV and ST5 variants. In Asian and Latin American settings, longitudinal monitoring identified recurring shifts where older sequence types were supplanted by ST59 and ST398 backgrounds exhibiting improved cellular growth capacity and distinct toxin repertoires. Furthermore, mobile cassette exchange involving SCCmec types IV and V was consistently observed across hospital wards and outpatient clinics, facilitating the steady diffusion of virulent community lineages into acute healthcare environments.
The synthesis distinctly identified the Middle East as a critical dynamic intersection where international MRSA lineages converge, intermingle, and co-circulate. Molecular data from Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Oman, and the United Arab Emirates highlighted the ongoing coexistence of European epidemic clones like ST22-IV, Asian lineages like ST59-IV and ST239-III, and American lineages like ST8-IV alongside locally predominant strains, including ST6-IV carrying spa type t304 and ST80-IV. Widespread carriage of virulence factors such as Panton-Valentine leukocidin was extensively recorded across both carriage and invasive isolates, often in conjunction with toxic shock syndrome toxin genes. Multiple surveillance datasets from the Arabian Gulf further noted the appearance of hybrid hospital-community strains and plasmid-driven resistance mechanisms, a development accelerated by intense transnational travel, regional labor migration, and religious pilgrimages that bring diverse populations into frequent contact.
Antimicrobial resistance profiling compiled across primary studies established that beta-lactam resistance remains almost universally mediated by the mecA gene, which encodes penicillin-binding protein 2a with reduced binding affinity for beta-lactams, while also highlighting the emergence of divergent mecC mechanisms in select cohorts. Widespread co-resistance to non-beta-lactam therapeutic classes was pervasive, with erythromycin resistance reported across all monitored regions and high frequencies of clindamycin and fluoroquinolone resistance concentrated in the Middle East, Asia, and the Americas. Multidrug-resistant phenotypes predominated in Middle Eastern and African clinical isolates, where extensive co-resistance to aminoglycosides, macrolides, and tetracyclines was routinely observed. Despite these escalating multi-resistance trends, first-line glycopeptide and oxazolidinone therapies, notably vancomycin, teicoplanin, and linezolid, maintained near-universal in vitro activity across nearly every primary investigation, with linezolid resistance documented in only 2.1 percent of isolates in a single Iranian investigation.
Methodological quality assessments performed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for observational designs scored twenty-eight primary studies, identifying twenty investigations as good quality with scores ranging from 7 to 9, while eight were classified as fair quality with scores between 5 and 6, resulting in an average score of 7.1. All fair-quality studies received reduced ratings in the comparability domain due to minimal analytical control for confounding variables, though none fell into the poor quality tier. Visual inspection of funnel plots demonstrated an even dispersion of primary investigations around the pooled effect size, and an Egger’s regression test returned a p-value of 0.87, confirming the absence of substantial small-study effects or systematic publication bias across the global dataset, even though regional funnel plots revealed slight asymmetry attributed to smaller studies reporting elevated prevalence in high-burden zones.
The study also clearly documented several critical methodological limitations inherent to synthesizing international resistance literature, most notably that screening, study selection, and data extraction were executed by a single author rather than through dual independent reviewers as recommended by Cochrane standards. The underlying evidence base exhibited substantial operational heterogeneity, as primary studies relied on varied diagnostic approaches spanning cefoxitin disc diffusion, oxacillin minimum inhibitory concentration testing, and polymerase chain reaction detection of resistance genes, while pooling diverse clinical matrices that mingled carriage surveillance with invasive bloodstream infections. Furthermore, because several primary datasets did not provide isolate-level stratification between nosocomial and community origins, separate subgroup analyses could not be performed, indicating that the overall pooled rate functions as an aggregate descriptive summary rather than an exact prevalence metric for any individual healthcare facility.
