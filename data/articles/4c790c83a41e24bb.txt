TITLE: Finance apps now pay for 1.83 installs for every free one, Adjust finds
OUTLET: PPC Land

On November 24, 2026, Adjust released The finance app insights report: 2026 edition, a 27-page benchmark which finds that finance apps now get 1.83 installs from paid advertising for every install that arrives without an ad, up from 1.21 in 2025, even as people open those apps more often and stay in them longer. The Berlin-based company, which measures app marketing for advertisers and is owned by AppLovin, built the report on anonymized data from the apps it tracks between January 2024 and September 2026.
In Short
Finance apps are being used more and for longer, but a bigger share of their new users now comes from paid ads: 1.83 paid installs for every free one in 2026, against 1.21 a year earlier. That matters for banks, payment firms, lenders and trading apps that buy app ads, because fewer new users came back the next day (11.5%, down from 12.5%) and the price of 1,000 ad views rose 13%. The download count now says less about a campaign than what happens afterwards, which is where the report finds the biggest gaps between types of apps, countries and ad partners.
How Adjust built the benchmarks
The report sets out its method on a single page. According to Adjust, the benchmarks rest on six choices:
- Data. "A mix of Adjust's top 5,000 apps and the total dataset of all apps measured by Adjust." The figures come from two sources, one covering a list of 45 countries and one covering roughly 250 countries and territories based on the ISO 3166-1 standard.
- Caveat. In the report's words: "Data is based on aggregated, anonymized data from apps tracked by Adjust and may not reflect the entire global app market." Unless a third party is named, every figure is Adjust's own.
- Types of app. Eight groups, which the report calls subverticals: finance, banking, buy now pay later, credit and loans, crypto, insurance, payment and stock trading.
- Places. Five regions (Asia-Pacific, Europe, Latin America, the Middle East and North Africa, and North America) plus a global total, and 21 countries: Brazil, Germany, Austria and Switzerland (grouped as DACH), France, India, Indonesia, Ireland, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, the Philippines, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Korea, Thailand, Türkiye, the United Arab Emirates, the U.K., the U.S. and Vietnam.
- Period. January 2024 to September 2026. "2026" in the charts means January to September 2026. Growth figures for regions and app types compare the third quarter of 2026 with the third quarter of 2025.
- Ratios and money. All amounts are in U.S. dollars. The paid-to-organic ratio is written as X:1. According to the report, "a value of 3 (3:1) means that for every 100 organic installs, there are 300 paid installs."
A few terms recur throughout. An install is a download followed by a first opening of the app. A paid install is one that Adjust traces to an ad campaign; an organic install is one it cannot trace to any ad, such as a download after a search in the app store or a tip from a friend. A session is a single visit to the app. Day 1 retention is the share of new users who open the app again on the day after installing it. Adjust, a mobile measurement partner, sits between apps and the ad networks they buy from and decides which ad, if any, gets credit for each install.
Headline figures, global
Visits grow faster than downloads
The report's central claim is simple. People are using finance apps more, and the growth is coming from existing users rather than new downloads. Global finance app sessions rose 21% in 2025, according to Adjust. In 2026 they started at 33% above the previous year in January and peaked at 36% in March. By the third quarter, sessions were 18% higher than a year earlier, while installs grew 5%.
Read side by side, those figures show the pace of growth roughly halving between the first quarter and the third, although visits were still rising more than three times as fast as downloads. The report does not comment on the slowdown. It does say that session growth has outpaced install growth since late 2025, and puts the cause down to "everyday money tasks, like checking balances, sending payments, and tracking investments" moving into apps.
Sensor Tower, the app data firm, offers a wider market view in the same document. It expects almost 8.9 billion finance app downloads in 2026, up nearly 6%, with sessions growing faster, at 10%, according to the report. The two sets of numbers measure different pools of apps, which helps explain why Adjust's session growth figure is higher.
Where growth came from
Latin America led every region in the third quarter. Installs there rose 54% and sessions 48% against the same quarter of 2025, according to Adjust. Mexico's finance app sessions grew 50%, almost all of it from payment apps; Brazil's grew 45%, driven mainly by banking apps. Most of the region's install growth came from buy now, pay later and payment apps.
The Middle East and North Africa posted 23% install growth and 9% session growth. Saudi Arabia stood out, with sessions up 48%, mostly from payment apps. The report cites figures showing electronic payments made up 85% of all retail payments in Saudi Arabia in 2025, up from 79% in 2024.
Asia-Pacific grew installs 10% and sessions 14%. Japan (13%) and Vietnam (15%) accounted for most of the region's session growth, mainly through banking and stock trading apps. Thailand's sessions rose 34%, mostly from credit and loan apps. India's grew 9%. In Europe, France's sessions rose 41%.
Banking and payment apps carry the traffic
Between January and September 2026, banking apps accounted for 23% of finance app installs but 54% of sessions, according to Adjust. Payment apps made up 25% of installs and 35% of sessions. Together, the two groups generated almost nine in every ten finance app visits.
The report's interpretation is direct: "For marketers, a banking install is worth more in engagement than its share of volume suggests." Payment apps follow the same pattern at a smaller scale, with about 1.4 times as many sessions as their share of installs would suggest.
The mirror image is credit and loan apps. They took 30% of all finance installs, the largest share of any group, but only 3% of sessions. On PPC Land's reading of the chart, that is the widest gap between installs and visits of any group with a printed figure. The report does not discuss it. Crypto apps had 8% of installs and 2% of sessions; stock trading apps 8% and 4%.
Growth in the third quarter came from smaller groups. Buy now, pay later apps grew sessions 52%, the fastest of any group, and installs 19%. Payment apps grew installs 17% and sessions 25%. Banking sessions rose 23%; the report leaves the banking install figure blank.
Usage by type of app
Nearly two paid installs for every free one
The most consequential number for ad buyers is the paid-to-organic ratio. Globally, it rose from 1.21 in 2025 to 1.83 in the first nine months of 2026, according to Adjust. Put another way, PPC Land calculates that about 65% of finance app installs now come from ads, up from about 55% a year earlier.
Lenders lean on advertising the most. Credit and loan apps recorded 3.92 paid installs for every organic one, up from 3.30, which means roughly four in five of their new users arrive through an ad. The report's explanation: "Lenders typically compete for borrowers within a short decision-making timeframe, and paid channels are one of the most direct ways to capture intent." Stock trading apps rose from 1.06 to 1.29, banking apps from 0.51 to 0.66, and payment and insurance apps from 0.46 to 0.56 each. Crypto stayed flat at 0.72.
Buy now, pay later apps went the other way. Their ratio fell for a second year in a row, from 0.67 in 2024 to 0.50 in 2025 and 0.47 in 2026, a 6% decline in the last year. It was the only group to rely less on paid installs, which the report attributes to more users finding these services on their own.
By country, the ratio tracks the weight of lending apps in each market. India rose from 3.63 to 6.50, meaning about 87% of its finance installs now come from ads, on PPC Land's calculation. Thailand stood at 5.06, Indonesia at 4.27, the Philippines at 4.04 and Malaysia at 3.99; Vietnam was at 3.33. Latin America rose from 2.76 to 3.52, with Mexico at 3.25 and Brazil at 1.13.
The organic-led markets are a short list. In the U.S. the ratio fell from 0.58 to 0.53, so roughly one install in three comes from an ad. The U.K. and Ireland were at 0.43, Germany, Austria and Switzerland at 0.46, Japan at 0.55 and Europe as a whole at 0.65. In the Middle East and North Africa the ratio was 0.94, with the UAE above 1 at 1.15.
The report draws a practical conclusion for paid-heavy markets: there, cost per install "can be similar across partners while the users they acquire behave very differently," and comparing ad partners on events after the install, "like a completed loan application, shows which ones are worth the budget." It also credits app store optimization and referral programs with protecting the organic base. That last point lands in a year when app stores made more room for paid placements: Apple added more ad positions to App Store search results from March 3, 2026.
Longer visits, fewer returns
The average finance app session lasted 8.09 minutes in 2026, up from 7.26 in 2025, the second annual increase in a row, according to Adjust. Banking apps grew the most: their average session rose 27%, from 6.99 to 8.88 minutes. With banking sessions also up 23% in the third quarter, the report concludes that banking apps are "gaining on both counts."
Not every group followed. Insurance sessions shrank from 6.58 to 4.54 minutes, a 31% fall on PPC Land's calculation, and buy now, pay later sessions from 6.71 to 5.59 minutes. Stock trading (13.72 minutes) and crypto (12.97 minutes) remain the apps people spend longest in per visit.
India has the longest finance sessions of any market tracked, at 14.60 minutes, followed by South Korea at 12.38 and Brazil at 11.60. South Korea posted the biggest increase, from 10.97 minutes, driven by stock trading apps. The report cites the Korea Financial Investment Association, which counted a record 110.8 million active trading accounts in early 2026. France had the shortest sessions, at 4.71 minutes; the U.S. averaged 5.31.
Day of download and the next day
New users opened a finance app 1.47 times on average on the day they installed it, according to Adjust, with banking apps highest at 1.60. Stock trading was the only group to improve on install-day use, edging from 1.47 to 1.49, the second year in a row it rose even as it took on more paid installs. The report reads that as a sign stock trading apps are "adding paid volume without lowering the quality of the users they bring in." Japan led the markets at 1.55 visits on download day; the U.S. and Brazil were lowest at 1.35.
The next day tells a less flattering story. Day 1 retention for all finance apps fell from 12.5% to 11.5%, which the report puts down to "a more competitive market": "With more apps to choose from and accounts quicker to open, users can try several apps before committing."
Banking apps kept the most new users, at 20.3%, roughly one in five, though that was down from 20.8%. Crypto (15.9%) and payment apps (15.0%) followed. Stock trading improved from 13.8% to 14.4%, buy now, pay later held at 12.5%, and credit and loan apps slipped to 9.8%. Insurance was lowest at 9.4%.
Japan had the best next-day retention of any market, at 17.8%, followed by the U.K. and Ireland (16.6%), Germany, Austria and Switzerland (16.3%) and France (15.1%). In each of the strongest markets, organic installs outnumber paid ones, the report says. The weakest were India (8.7%) and Thailand (8.8%). PPC Land notes that the four markets with the highest paid-to-organic ratios - India, Thailand, Indonesia and the Philippines - all had next-day retention of 10% or less.
