TITLE: Man charged with arson after 'substantial damage' at historic York church
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A man has appeared in court charged with arson to a historic church altarpiece in York.
Multiple fires are believed to have been started deliberately in Holy Trinity Church on Micklegate at around 12pm on Wednesday.
A statement from North Yorkshire Police said “substantial damage has been caused to a large and listed historical altarpiece within the church.”
Michael Robert Peter Evans, aged 42 of Lowther Terrace in York, appeared at York Magistrates’ Court on Friday accused of arson to the altar, which is valued at £30,000.
He was not asked to enter a plea and was released on bail, ahead of appearing at York Crown Court on 29 December.
A statement on the Holy Trinity Church website said the fire was spotted by a visitor and put out by a young man from a nearby shop within minutes.
“Their quick thinking and courage almost certainly saved this ancient building from serious harm. Our thanks also go to the crews of North Yorkshire Fire & Rescue Service, who attended so promptly.
“Holy Trinity has stood on Micklegate for more than nine hundred years and has come through fire before. It remains a place of prayer for everyone. We hold in our prayers all who were caught up in today's events, including the person responsible,” the statement added.
