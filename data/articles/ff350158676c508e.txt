TITLE: This Dietary Supplement Could Have Potential Brain Benefits, Study Finds
OUTLET: SciTechDaily

A supplement containing a substance naturally made by gut bacteria increased blood flow to a memory-related brain region in adults with obesity, raising new questions about its potential benefits.
When the brain works harder, it needs more blood to supply oxygen and nutrients. For people with obesity, changes in blood vessel function can affect circulation, raising questions about how well the brain responds to these demands. A new clinical trial suggests a dietary supplement could help increase blood flow during mental activity, although researchers have yet to show that it improves memory.
Researchers at the University of Oklahoma investigated urolithin A, a substance produced when certain gut bacteria break down compounds found in foods such as strawberries, pomegranates, and walnuts. The amount people naturally produce varies, and existing research suggests that production declines with age and increasing body mass index. Urolithin A is also available as a dietary supplement.
Brain blood flow increases during memory tests
In the randomized clinical trial, published in JAMA Network Open, 50 adults with obesity, ages 40 to 64, received either 1,000 milligrams of urolithin A daily or a placebo for four weeks.
Participants taking the supplement showed a greater increase in blood flow to the brain while completing a challenging memory task. The difference was especially noticeable in the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, an area involved in holding and using information over short periods. However, researchers found no significant improvements in blood vessel function in the arm or performance on memory tests.
“The brain needs an adequate supply of blood to meet the demands of mental activity,” said first author Ana Clara da Costa Pinaffi Langley, Ph.D., an assistant professor in the Department of Nutritional Sciences at the OU College of Allied Health on the OU Harold Hamm Health Campus. “We found that urolithin A increased blood flow to the brain during a memory task, suggesting the supplement may help the brain meet its increased need for oxygen and nutrients when it is working harder. We plan to conduct more research to understand what this finding means for brain health.”
Whether improved circulation benefits memory remains unclear
According to the researchers, this is the first human study to report an effect of urolithin A on blood vessel function in the brain. The supplement was well-tolerated, although the trial involved relatively few participants at a single research site. Longer studies with larger groups will be needed to determine whether the changes in blood flow lead to lasting health benefits.
“These findings offer an interesting starting point for understanding how urolithin A affects the brain,” said study co-author Andriy Yabluchanskiy, M.D., Ph.D., an associate professor in the Department of Neurosurgery at the OU College of Medicine. “Although we saw increased blood flow to the brain during mental activity, participants did not demonstrate measurable improvements in memory or attention. Future studies will need to determine whether taking the supplement for a longer period leads to meaningful improvements in cognitive function or other health benefits.”
The researchers ultimately hope to find ways to help people maintain their cognitive health as they age.
“The idea is to mitigate cognitive decline,” Langley said. “We want to expand the time that people are living healthier lives.”
