TITLE: Motorway ad seller i-media wins exclusive rights at Gridserve EV sites
OUTLET: PPC Land

Motorway advertising company i-media today signed an exclusive partnership with Gridserve, the electric vehicle charging operator, covering advertising at Gridserve's Electric Forecourts, a national network of screens built into its chargers, and the experiential rights at its sites. According to i-media, the agreement takes its network of screens aimed at electric vehicle drivers to more than 1,000. Neither company disclosed what the deal is worth, how long it runs, when the first campaigns can go live or how many of those screens sit at Gridserve locations.
In Short
i-media, which sells advertising on screens at UK motorway service stations, has signed an exclusive deal to sell advertising and on-site brand activity across Gridserve's electric car charging sites. The deal matters to brands that want to reach electric car drivers, who i-media says spend an average of 27 minutes at a charging location, and the companies are pitching that audience to finance, technology, travel, luxury and consumer goods brands as well as carmakers. Advertisers can now buy motorway and charging-site screens from one seller, but the release gives no price, contract length or Gridserve screen count, and it does not explain how i-media's number-plate camera data produces the home-location and spending profiles it advertises.
What the two companies agreed
The release, issued in London and dated November 17, 2026, calls the deal an "exclusive multi-media partnership" and lists three parts. The first is Gridserve's Electric Forecourts, which the companies describe as "purpose-built destinations that have redefined the EV charging experience in the UK." The second is what the release calls Gridserve's "national network of charger screens". The third is "full experiential rights", a phrase the release does not define; in outdoor media it usually refers to live brand activity at a site, such as product sampling or vehicle displays, rather than time on a screen.
Together, according to the companies, these create "the most comprehensive commercial advertising proposition across the UK's fast-growing EV charging estate." The subheading puts the result in one line: "i-media EV Network set to total over 1,000 screens." The body text is firmer, saying the deal "takes i-media's EV network to over 1,000 screens targeting EV drivers specifically on the move." How many screens i-media's EV network had before today, and how many Gridserve adds, is not stated.
Scale on the Gridserve side comes in two figures. According to the release, Gridserve "operates more than 1,600 chargers across 200 locations", a count the release's notes attribute to Zapmap data from March 2026. The release describes Gridserve as the operator of the "UK's most used public EV charging network", a claim it does not support with a comparison. Gridserve's own website lists five Electric Forecourts - at Braintree, Norwich, Gatwick, Stevenage and Markham Vale - and the company described Markham Vale, with 39 ultra-rapid bays, as its fifth flagship forecourt in a first-half update published on July 23, 2026. The same update said Gridserve added 285 charging bays across 24 new or expanded Electric Super Hub locations in the first six months of 2026.
On the i-media side, the release says its "wider estate already covers approximately 98% of UK motorway service areas." The company's boilerplate describes it as "the UK's leading motorway DOOH network, with an audience of +355m visitors and 1,000+ full-motion screens across 134 motorway service areas nationwide," and as "the only commercial touchpoint along UK motorways, with exclusive advertising and sponsorship rights." Both are i-media's own descriptions.
What the executives said
Sam Tester, Chief Revenue Officer at i-media, put the case in terms of targeting. "i-media is giving advertisers verified, ANPR-led targeting at exactly the right moment in the journey," Tester said. "EV drivers are a high-value, fast-growing audience, and until now there's been no reliable way to reach them with precision while they charge. This partnership closes that gap."
Phil Diamond, Chief Portfolio Officer at i-media, focused on the brand. "GRIDSERVE is the standout name in premium EV charging in the UK - the brand that defined what a truly purposeful charging destination looks like," Diamond said. "Bringing their Electric Forecourts, charger screen network and experiential rights into our portfolio gives brands something genuinely new: a connected, data-informed pathway through the EV journey, from the motorway to the destination forecourt. We have been building the case for the EV audience for some time. This partnership means we can deliver it at a scale and quality that no other media owner can match."
For Gridserve, Kirsty Tarbuck, Head of Customer Experience, framed the deal around customer data. "Understanding our customers and what they need from every part of their journey is at the heart of what we do," Tarbuck said. "Partnering with i-media gives us an exciting opportunity to build on that understanding, using data and insight to create experiences that are more relevant, engaging and valuable." She added: "By combining GRIDSERVE's understanding of the EV driver with i-media's expertise in digital out-of-home media and audience insight, we can create a more connected customer experience while giving brands meaningful opportunities to engage with audiences in the right place and at the right time."
Tarbuck's reference to "using data and insight" is the only point in the release where Gridserve's own customer information comes up. Whether Gridserve charging data will be combined with i-media's audience data, and on what terms, is not addressed.
The figures behind the pitch
How i-media says its number-plate targeting works
The release leans on ANPR, short for automatic number plate recognition, and on a product i-media calls ANPR Audiences, which it describes as "the company's real-time audience delivery solution." Taken together with what i-media said in June, when its motorway screens were connected to VIOOH's marketplace, the company's description of the system covers five stages.
- Capture. Cameras at each location read the number plates of arriving vehicles. In June, i-media said its proprietary system reads vehicle type, make, model and fuel type in real time, without identifying drivers or collecting personal data.
- Aggregation. According to the June description, the readings are turned into aggregate, fleet-level audience segments rather than individual profiles.
- Activation. The segments decide what plays on screen. In June, i-media named EV targeting, premium-make targeting and fuel-retailer targeting as uses, with campaigns able to adapt in real time.
- Profiling. Today's release goes further and presents ANPR Audiences as a deterministic targeting product, one based on identified vehicles rather than modelled estimates. According to its boilerplate, the product delivers "deterministic audience targeting at scale, with an understanding of where its audience lives, their demographic profile, spending habits and travel behaviour - never previously available in out-of-home."
- Buying. Since June 9, 2026, i-media's motorway screens have been available to programmatic buyers through VIOOH and the demand-side platforms connected to it, including The Trade Desk and Google's Display and Video 360. The release does not say whether the Gridserve screens will be sold through the same route or only directly.
The fourth stage sits awkwardly beside the first two. A system that reads plates without identifying drivers and produces fleet-level segments is not obviously the same thing as one that understands "where its audience lives" and its "spending habits". The release does not name the data sources used to connect a plate reading to a home area or to spending, nor whether vehicle registration records, postcode-level statistics or third-party panels are involved. The release's notes attribute the ANPR and EV audience data to "i-media internal research, 2025," without a sample size or method.
Number-plate cameras have drawn attention in other settings this year. In October, PPC Land reported that a Proton map of Flock Safety's records showed 214,073 devices in service across the United States, against the roughly 120,000 cameras the company had cited publicly. Flock's records are searched by police rather than used for advertising, and nothing in the i-media release suggests any comparable use. The comparison is a reminder, though, that plate data is sensitive enough for buyers to ask how it is processed before they rely on it.
The electric vehicle audience case
The release argues that the audience is growing fast enough to justify the deal now. According to figures from the Society of Motor Manufacturers and Traders (SMMT), more than one in four new cars sold in the UK in July 2026 was battery electric.
Source: SMMT, published August 5, 2026. The release cites the share and growth figures correctly. SMMT itself noted, however, that the July 2025 base was depressed because some buyers delayed purchases until it was confirmed which models qualified for the government's Electric Car Grant, so part of the 44.5% rise reflects a weak comparison month.
Gridserve, according to the release, "delivered three million charging sessions on its Electric Highway in 2025 alone, with over 87GWh of energy delivered." From those figures the companies conclude that "the audience at these sites will grow substantially in scale, dwell and commercial value." No forecast of sessions or screen impressions accompanies that sentence.
The second pillar is time. According to i-media, the "27-minute average dwell at an i-media or GRIDSERVE charging location compares favourably with any comparable premium DOOH environment." The release gives no figure for any comparable environment. In June, i-media gave a different number for its motorway estate as a whole: an average visit of 22 minutes at its service areas. The two figures measure different things - a charging stop and a general service area visit - and both come from i-media.
Beyond carmakers
Car brands are the obvious buyers, and the release says so. Its larger claim is that the audience suits other categories. According to the companies, the EV driver is "affluent, mobile, time-rich during the charge, and skewed towards premium consumption," a profile that "aligns with the target profile for financial services, technology, travel, luxury retail and FMCG."
That is, in effect, a contextual advertising argument made about a place rather than a web page: the setting is meant to say something about the person standing in it. The release puts it in plain terms, describing these drivers as "consumers who have already made a significant financial and lifestyle commitment to new technology; they are, by definition, an early adopter audience receptive to premium brand messaging." No survey, purchase data or campaign result is offered in support of that description.
For brands outside the car sector, the companies say the combination of i-media's motorway service areas and the Gridserve network "now creates the first truly national, joined-up EV audience proposition." According to the release, "A brand can follow the same consumer from the motorway to their destination charge point, with consistent messaging in a high-quality environment unencumbered by ad fatigue or digital clutter." Following "the same consumer" across two networks implies some way of recognising that consumer at both, which brings the question back to the plate data described above. Does the matching happen at vehicle level, or at segment level? The release does not say.
