TITLE: Non-Antiarrhythmic Upstream Therapies Lack Consistent Evidence After Atrial Fibrillation Ablation
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive systematic review and network meta-analysis has determined that currently available randomized trial evidence is insufficient to identify any non-antiarrhythmic upstream drug therapy with a consistent and reproducible benefit for preventing recurrence following catheter ablation in patients treated for atrial fibrillation. Although radiofrequency catheter ablation combined with pulmonary vein isolation represents an established and effective treatment strategy for atrial fibrillation, post-procedural arrhythmia recurrence remains a substantial clinical challenge for patients and electrophysiologists alike. To counteract this vulnerability, investigators have increasingly evaluated non-antiarrhythmic medications, including sacubitril and valsartan combinations, sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers, statins, colchicine, and corticosteroids, which have been proposed to mitigate recurrence by targeting underlying tissue inflammation, cellular oxidative stress, and structural cardiac remodeling.
Because clinical findings across individual trials have remained inconsistent and direct comparative data between these distinct drug classes are limited, researchers conducted a Bayesian network meta-analysis to simultaneously synthesize and contrast the comparative effects of these interventions on post-ablation recurrence and related cardiac outcomes. The authors performed an extensive literature search spanning major medical repositories, including PubMed, EMBASE, the Cochrane Library, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov, and the World Health Organization International Clinical Trials Registry Platform for eligible articles published before December 31, 2025, without applying language restrictions. The systematic review restricted its eligibility to randomized controlled trials that evaluated sacubitril and valsartan, SGLT2 inhibitors, ACE inhibitors or ARBs, statins, colchicine, or corticosteroids against placebo or standard post-ablation care, which included routine anticoagulation and pharmacological rate control, among adult patients undergoing initial radiofrequency catheter ablation procedures.
Across the synthesized evidence base, the research team identified and pooled data from twenty-three randomized controlled trials comprising a collective cohort of 2,874 participants. The investigators conducted Bayesian network meta-analyses to model odds ratios for binary clinical endpoints, including late recurrence, early post-ablation recurrence, and composite endpoint events, while continuous structural changes in left atrial diameter were assessed using mean differences. In the primary unstratified analysis, sacubitril and valsartan therapy was associated with significantly lower odds of late arrhythmia recurrence when compared directly against placebo, yielding an odds ratio of 0.28 with a 95 percent credible interval ranging from 0.12 to 0.65. Similarly, sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors demonstrated lower odds of late recurrence relative to standard post-ablation care, displaying an odds ratio of 0.36 with a 95 percent credible interval between 0.16 and 0.89.
However, when sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors were compared directly against placebo controls, the estimated treatment effect on late recurrence failed to achieve statistical significance, exhibiting an odds ratio of 0.38 alongside a broad 95 percent credible interval spanning from 0.12 to 1.36. Crucially, neither the protective association observed for sacubitril and valsartan nor that seen for SGLT2 inhibitors was reproduced when the investigators conducted stratified subgroup analyses categorized by the clinical classification of atrial fibrillation. While both associations persisted when the researchers conducted a sensitivity analysis that removed four trials available solely as conference abstracts, the inability to replicate findings across distinct atrial fibrillation subtypes demonstrates that the primary outcomes are not robust to variations in the clinical composition of the experimental network.
The network meta-analysis also analyzed early post-procedural recurrence, generating extremely small point estimates for sacubitril and valsartan, with an odds ratio of 0.04 and a 95 percent credible interval of 0.00 to 0.33, and for SGLT2 inhibitors, with an odds ratio of 0.02 and a 95 percent credible interval of 0.00 to 0.35. The study authors explicitly noted that these remarkably dramatic effect sizes are clinically implausible and most likely represent analytical instability originating from sparse event data rather than genuine biological treatment benefits, concluding that these early recurrence figures must be viewed as purely exploratory. In structural evaluations, sacubitril and valsartan significantly reduced left atrial diameter compared with placebo, showing a mean difference of negative 1.09 millimeters with a 95 percent credible interval from negative 2.02 to negative 0.22 millimeters, although whether this structural change confers meaningful clinical protection against recurrence remains completely uncertain.
Importantly, the network meta-analysis revealed no statistically significant differences between any of the evaluated non-antiarrhythmic upstream therapies, standard post-ablation medical care, or placebo when assessing composite clinical endpoint events. These composite outcomes encompassed repeat catheter reablation, electrical cardioversion procedures, emergency outpatient visits, and hospital admissions driven directly by post-ablation arrhythmia recurrence. The authors highlighted that this null result across the specific composite endpoints that govern overall healthcare utilization and acute clinical management represents the single most consistent finding of the entire investigation, indicating that upstream pharmacological strategies have not produced demonstrable reductions in the downstream therapeutic interventions and acute care visits typically required after catheter ablation procedures.
Given the totality of these findings, the study authors concluded that the currently available randomized evidence base remains insufficient to support any non-antiarrhythmic upstream pharmacotherapy as having a consistent or reproducible beneficial effect on post-procedural recurrence after catheter ablation for atrial fibrillation. The synthesized data can neither definitively confirm nor reliably exclude a clinically relevant patient benefit from these adjunctive medications. Consequently, the routine clinical administration of upstream non-antiarrhythmic agents for the targeted prevention of post-ablation recurrence cannot be supported by current data, and the authors emphasize that resolving this clinical question will require adequately powered, multicentre randomized trials incorporating continuous cardiac rhythm monitoring protocols.
