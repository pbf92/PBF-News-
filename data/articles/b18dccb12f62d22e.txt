TITLE: Pochettino’s Manchester United rebuild: Key problems awaiting the former USMNT coach
OUTLET: Diario AS

Having been unveiled as the new head coach of Manchester United, Mauricio Pochettino says he’s “excited” by the “privilege” of taking over at Old Trafford. United are an “amazing club”, the Argentine told the Red Devils’ in-house TV channel on Thursday.
However, as he prepares to visit Liverpool in his first game in charge, Pochettino must get to work on a team whose current predicament certainly does not merit such gushing vocab.
As things stand, after all, United find themselves in a relegation battle. With just eight points to their name after 10 Premier League games this term, the 20-time English champions sit 18th, just one point better off than bottom team Crystal Palace. They have made their worst start to a top-flight season in 40 years.
“Needs two defenders in January”
Now, let me offer you a startling piece of soccer insight: If a team concedes more goals than it scores, winning games may be a struggle. And United’s lowly Premier League position owes much to the side’s dreadful defending.
Plugging United’s leaky backline right away is easier said than done for Pochettino, given he’s inherited a squad so poorly built that a right-back of rather questionable quality, Diogo Dalot, has of late been toiling away on the left of defense. Nevertheless, shipping 19 goals in 10 league games is truly sub-optimal stuff - and must be corrected.
Indeed, United’s goals-against average of nearly two per game is far, far worse than the rate they managed in their last relegation season, more than 50 years ago. In 1973/74, Tommy Docherty’s team let in a comparably miserly 48 in 42 games: 1.14 per match.
Gary Neville, the legendary United defender-turned-pundit, says Pochettino must push hard for mid-season reinforcements at the back. “He needs two defenders in January,” Neville told the Overlap podcast this week. “He’s got to insist that he gets a minimum budget in the transfer window so he can turn this around.”
In an appearance on the Stick to United podcast, meanwhile, Neville ventured that it may take Pochettino three years - if he gets that much time - to reshape the roster as a whole. “The earliest Mauricio Pochettino can unravel this squad is two seasons if he does well,” the former right-back said. “It could take three to get this squad unravelled.”
United aren’t running hard enough
“One of the biggest problems,” Neville also told Stick to United, “is the team hasn’t been running as far as they should.” The stats bear out that assessment: per data compiled by the soccer stats specialists FotMob, United sit 19th in the Premier League’s distance table, with a team average of 112.5 kilometers (69.9 miles) per match.
A coach known for dynamic, hard-running teams, Pochettino has, in part, been brought in to knock United into physical shape. The club expects the 54-year-old to be “demanding on fitness”, writes United expert Andy Mitten in The Athletic.
Not only was Old Trafford’s front office attracted by a coach with the “charisma, appetite and gravitas” to lead United, Mitten says, but Pochettino “brings a tactical understanding and plays a type of high-intensity, aggressive football that the fans (hopefully) will like”.
Fernandes isn’t firing
In Bruno Fernandes, easily the club’s best signing of the post-Ferguson era, United boast last season’s Football Writers’ Association Footballer of the Year. In 2025/26, the Portuguese racked up 30 goal involvements in 35 Premier League games, including 17 in 17 in United’s late-season charge to a third-placed finish under Pochettino’s predecessor, Michael Carrick.
This season, though, Fernandes’ form has nosedived. Yes, he registered a hat-trick and an assist in August’s 5-2 win over Ipswich; but in his seven league appearances since then, he has offered up just two more goal involvements - and plenty of pouty, toys-out-of-the-pram body language.
What’s more, Fernandes compounded his poor form by running his mouth off after a humbling Champions League defeat last month. In the wake of United’s 4-1 thumping at Como, he appeared to question Carrick’s tactics in a postgame interview, suggesting the team’s approach had helped the Italians to “get confidence”.
The captain was dropped for United’s next game, a 3-0 home loss to Bournemouth - albeit Carrick insisted Fernandes had been benched for “pure football” reasons.
A cultural reset is needed
Fernandes’ controversial post-Como comments came a week after another European loss, to Atlético Madrid, had seen his teammate Lisandro Martínez aim a public broadside at United’s leadership.
It’s hard to disagree with Martínez that United’s summer recruitment, overseen by the under-fire director of football Jason Wilcox, was deficient. But, like Fernandes’ choice words days later, the defender’s outburst was hardly indicative of a locker room brimming with the requisite discipline. Nor did it speak of a top-to-bottom togetherness within the club.
Speaking on Thursday, Pochettino spoke of the importance of fostering the right “culture” at United. “To create the culture is the most important thing,” he said. “To create the habits, the principles.” He added: “We need to create chemistry between us and between the staff, between the fans and the players and the staff, the directors, the owners and everything. I think we need to be united.”
