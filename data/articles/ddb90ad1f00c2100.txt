TITLE: Surgical Simulation Enhances Microvascular Anastomosis Skills Despite Marked Evidence Heterogeneity
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Simulation-based training substantially enhances procedural knowledge, technical skill scores, and operative speed for surgeons learning microvascular anastomosis, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in BMC Medical Education. The comprehensive evaluation synthesized evidence across randomized controlled trials, non-randomized comparative investigations, and pre-post intervention studies to determine how bench-top and virtual practice models influence surgical performance. While trainee scores and execution times exhibited marked improvements across multiple simulation modalities, the authors underscored that substantial statistical heterogeneity and an unstable estimate for vessel patency mean that current results should primarily be viewed as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive proof of optimal instructional design.
Microvascular anastomosis represents a technically demanding cornerstone of reconstructive surgery, frequently utilized in oral and maxillofacial, plastic, and head and neck surgical procedures. The technique enables complex anatomical restoration through free flap tissue transfer, which demands exceptional manual dexterity and precise hand-eye coordination under high microscopic magnification. While simulation models have increasingly emerged as valuable instructional adjuncts to conventional apprenticeship training, comparative evidence systematically synthesizing the relative efficacy of synthetic benches, biological tissue preparations, and digital platforms had remained limited.
To evaluate these educational interventions, investigators conducted an exhaustive literature search across PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science from each database’s inception through April 12, 2026. The search protocol, registered in the PROSPERO database under registration number CRD42025634721, identified experimental and observational studies assessing simulation models for microvascular anastomosis training in surgical education. Eligible designs encompassed randomized controlled trials, non-randomized comparative cohorts, case-control studies, and single-group pre-post designs. Methodological quality was evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool for randomized trials and the Medical Education Research Study Quality Instrument for non-randomized investigations, after which random-effects meta-analyses and exploratory meta-regressions were conducted.
The systematic search initially retrieved 1149 bibliographic records, yielding a final analytical sample of 26 studies encompassing 589 individual surgical trainees. Among these publications, 23 investigations supplied sufficient extractable data to contribute quantitatively to one or more pooled meta-analytic comparisons. Specifically, 16 separate studies reported quantitative measurements on procedural knowledge and technical skill scores, 15 investigations assessed operative speed, and 6 studies evaluated anastomotic patency outcomes. The remaining 3 eligible studies lacked complete or extractable numerical metrics and were consequently examined exclusively within the authors’ qualitative narrative synthesis rather than statistical pooling.
In the primary pooled analyses, simulation-based training produced significant advantages across all measured surgical outcomes. Procedural knowledge and technical skill scores demonstrated a large positive effect, yielding a standardized mean difference of 2.30 with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 1.58 to 3.02 and a significance value of P less than 0.001. Operative completion speed also improved significantly among simulation-trained participants, reflecting a standardized mean difference of -1.75, a 95 percent confidence interval between -2.55 and -0.95, and a P value below 0.001. Anastomotic patency showed a higher likelihood of success following training, with an odds ratio of 7.54, a 95 percent confidence interval of 2.22 to 25.58, and a P value of 0.001.
Despite these favorable summary estimates, statistical heterogeneity was exceptionally high across the primary outcomes, reaching an inconsistency index of 89 percent for technical skill scores, 89 percent for operative speed, and 78 percent for vessel patency. To explore potential sources of this variability, the researchers conducted prespecified subgroup analyses categorizing interventions by simulator fidelity and participant trainee background. Across simulator categories, low-to-moderate-fidelity models and high-fidelity biological tissue models demonstrated larger improvements in procedural knowledge and technical skill scores than digital platforms, although effect magnitudes and consistency varied noticeably between modalities.
Detailed subgroup comparisons revealed that low-to-moderate-fidelity synthetic simulators produced the highest numerical point estimate for knowledge and technical skill acquisition, generating an effect size of 3.33 with a 95 percent confidence interval from 2.10 to 4.56, accompanied by substantial heterogeneity at an inconsistency index of 94 percent. High-fidelity biological models demonstrated a more stable pooled effect size of 2.32, coupled with an inconsistency value of 76.5 percent. In contrast, digital and robotic simulation platforms yielded a more modest but completely consistent effect size of 0.82, displaying an inconsistency index of zero percent across evaluated cohorts.
Sensitivity analyses affirmed the overall numerical stability of the pooled estimates for procedural knowledge acquisition and operative speed reduction, but revealed meaningful instability in the anastomotic patency metric. The observed patency benefit was disproportionately sensitive to the inclusion of individual high-effect studies, indicating that vascular patency estimates must be interpreted with caution. The authors concluded that while progressive curricula moving from low-fidelity synthetic trainers for novices to high-fidelity biological tissues for advanced residents present a logical instructional framework, the current fragmented evidence base cannot definitively establish an optimal training sequence.
