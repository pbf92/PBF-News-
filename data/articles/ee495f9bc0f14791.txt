TITLE: Cochrane review: Turmeric makes little or no difference to knee osteoarthritis
OUTLET: Doctors.net.uk

19 November 2026
By Olivia Bowthorpe
Turmeric supplements are unlikely to bring clinically meaningful improvements in pain, physical function or quality of life for people with knee osteoarthritis, according to a Cochrane review.
The supplements are a popular option for people with the condition, which has limited treatment options, researchers said.
But “popularity and biological plausibility do not necessarily translate into clinically meaningful benefits,” said lead researcher, Dr Ivan Shirinsky, head of the Laboratory for Multimorbidity in Rheumatic Diseases in Novosibirsk, Russia.
The review included nine randomised controlled trials involving 1,245 people with knee osteoarthritis.
Participants’ were mostly aged around 52 to 62 years, and 67% were women. All the trials lasted no longer than 90 days and tested different oral turmeric or curcuminoid preparations.
In four trials comparing turmeric with placebo, involving 349 participants, supplements reduced pain by less than one point more than placebo on a zero to ten scale. This was below the 1.5-point difference the researchers consider clinically important.
