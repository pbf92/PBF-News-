Nasdaq Tumbles 2.5% as AI Rout Deepens Before Nvidia's Earnings Test
Wall Street's artificial-intelligence trade came under renewed pressure on Monday, with the Nasdaq Composite bearing the brunt as investors trimmed exposure to chipmakers and AI infrastructure names on the eve of Nvidia's quarterly results. The selling pushed the tech-heavy index into correction territory, more than 10% below its record close in October.
The Nasdaq fell 626.51 points, or 2.49%, to 24,509.76. The S&P 500 shed 145.08 points, or 1.92%, to 7,416.59, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 739.38 points, or 1.41%, to 51,608.17. Technology led the decline among S&P 500 sectors, sliding about 3%, while consumer staples and utilities were the only groups to finish higher.
Nvidia, the center of the AI investment thesis, fell 4.1% to $156.14 after posting its longest streak of weekly declines since October 2012. The company is due to report results on Tuesday. Uncertainty over its leadership has compounded the selling: founder Jensen Huang stepped down as chief executive and board member after disclosures about extramarital relationships, including one with a businesswoman whom U.S. officials suspect of spying for Beijing. The Financial Times reported Monday that Huang is weighing a return to the top job, and President Donald Trump said he would not object to a reinstatement. A petition urging his return has drawn more than 178,000 signatures, among them prominent Silicon Valley figures.
The pressure extended across the semiconductor complex. Intel fell 7%, Micron lost 7.6%, Broadcom dropped 3.9% and Advanced Micro Devices declined 5.6%. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index tumbled 5.3%. AI cloud providers CoreWeave and Nebius slid 6.1% and 7.6%, respectively, while server makers Dell and HP fell 7% and 2.9% and Super Micro Computer lost 6.9%.
The pullback was global. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 2.42% to 59,662.02, slipping back below the 60,000 mark, and South Korea's Kospi dropped 3.6%. Both benchmarks carry heavy weights in semiconductor and AI supply-chain shares.
"It's going to be super important if they downplay the forward guidance for the next quarter or first quarter next year," Nadia El Bilassy, senior market analyst at Equiti Group, told Reuters.
Sentiment also took a hit from new interim U.S. rules curbing China's access to advanced AI chips from suppliers such as Nvidia. The measures were announced as Trump departed for talks with President Xi Jinping.
Investors are bracing for a second catalyst this week: Anthropic's stock-market debut, a listing expected to test appetite for AI exposure and potentially amplify swings across the sector. The company plans to price its shares on Wednesday and begin trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker ANT.
"The success of the IPO, along with Nvidia's earnings, will definitely be the barometer for whether the correction will continue or not," El Bilassy said.
Background: The AI trade has drawn sharper scrutiny in recent weeks as borrowing costs climb, growth concerns mount following the Democratic sweep in the midterm elections, and questions linger over Nvidia's management. The benchmark 10-year Treasury yield hovered near 5.13% on Monday after touching 5.15% on Nov. 13, a headwind for valuations that depend on profits discounted far into the future. The New York Fed's November manufacturing survey added to the mixed picture, with its general business conditions index falling 18 points to 7.8 from 25.5 in October; employment growth accelerated to its fastest pace in more than four years while input and selling prices continued to rise, though more slowly.
Note: Closing levels for Monday, Nov. 16.
Attention now turns to the Federal Reserve. Chair Kevin Warsh was scheduled to speak in San Francisco on Monday at an AI and productivity conference, and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack and other policymakers are due to appear during the week. Minutes of the latest Federal Open Market Committee meeting, due Wednesday, may sharpen expectations ahead of next month's rate decision. Markets fully price an increase in December, with the odds of a half-point move at nearly 9% after last week's hotter-than-expected inflation data, according to the CME FedWatch tool.
Note: Closing prices for Monday, Nov. 16. Peaks are the highest closing levels since early September.
Away from technology, Tyson Foods jumped 8.7% after fiscal fourth-quarter profit beat estimates, even as the meat producer projected a steeper-than-expected decline in annual revenue. Decliners outnumbered advancers 3.45 to 1 on the New York Stock Exchange, and 3,697 Nasdaq stocks fell against 1,131 that rose. The Nasdaq recorded 489 new 52-week lows against 51 new highs. Trading volume across U.S. exchanges reached 17.91 billion shares, compared with a 20-session average of 18.66 billion.
Impact: The coming days present a concentrated test for the AI trade. Nvidia previously guided to roughly $108 billion in fiscal third-quarter revenue, following a record $96.2 billion in the prior period, and analysts expect about $109.1 billion with earnings per share up 90.4% from a year earlier. The report will show whether demand for AI computing can withstand mounting questions about infrastructure financing, margins and management stability. A strong print, paired with a well-received Anthropic listing, could arrest the pullback; disappointment on either front risks extending a correction that has already pulled Nvidia to its lowest levels since last year, where it trades at about 20 times trailing earnings and roughly 10 times forward estimates, well below the S&P 500's forward multiple of about 19. For portfolio managers, the selloff is forcing a re-examination of concentration risk in semiconductors and AI-linked cloud names, while elevated Treasury yields and a Fed leaning toward tightening leave little room for valuation errors.
