TITLE: Live event property
OUTLET: PPC Land

What the field is
Live event property is a reporting dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that labels each row of live event data with the commercial property the ad ran against. Amazon defines it as "the rights package, league, or content property associated with the live event inventory," according to the unified reporting dimensions reference updated on November 11, 2026, as quoted in PPC Land's count of the 156 unified report dimensions. In practice the values are names such as a league, a subscription sports package or a branded event series. Amazon's own 2024 example values were "NBA League Pass" and "ONE Championship".
The field exists because live sport is bought and priced by property. An advertiser paying a premium to appear around a basketball playoff game, or a football broadcast, wants to see what that money bought, separately from cheaper on-demand streaming and from other sports. Generic content fields such as content genre say "sports". Live event property says which sports product.
Where it sits in the reporting structure
Unified reporting groups its 156 attributes into 11 categories. Live event property is one of nine live event attributes, all placed in the Supply category alongside supply source, supply type, deal and publisher fields. The other eight are Live event genre, Live event content provider, Live event ID, Live event name, Live event inventory type, Live event ad break order, Live event ad slot order and Live event ad slot type. PPC Land's analysis found that six of the nine carry a former name from Amazon's older Live Event report, although it did not list those earlier names.
The Supply category holds 35 attributes, 34 of which carry the DVA+ product marker, against two for Sponsored Products and one each for Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display. DVA+ is the AI-assisted campaign type that merges the buying of what used to be the Amazon DSP (demand-side platform), Sponsored Display and streaming TV ads. PPC Land lists "live event ad slots" among the DVA+ capabilities in the reference. The published material does not state product markers field by field, so the safest reading is that Live event property applies to DVA+ and programmatic video buying, not to sponsored ads. Amazon's general rule covers the rest: "When a dimension does not apply to an ad product, its value appears as null."
A report built with Live event property plus impressions, cost, reach and video completions returns one row per property. Adding Live event name or ID drills down to individual games.
How the value gets there
The property label is metadata that travels with the inventory, not something the advertiser sets. Live event supply reaches Amazon's buying platform through two routes.
The first is Amazon-owned inventory, mainly Prime Video. Amazon's US live sports page lists 17 exclusive Thursday Night Football games, more than 60 exclusive NBA regular season games plus the Play-In Tournament and playoffs coverage, 30 WNBA regular season games, five NASCAR Cup Series races, 25 National Women's Soccer League games and the NBA and WNBA League Pass channels. Outside the US, rights include the UEFA Champions League, the NHL and Wimbledon, varying by market. Each of these is a natural property value.
The second is open-internet live supply from other publishers, sold through supply-side platforms (SSPs). When Amazon announced its Live Events Optimizer, it said publishers declare deal metadata covering "sport, league, broadcaster, geography, distributor" for certified live deals. That metadata is how a league or rights package name from a third-party stream can surface in an Amazon report. According to Amazon, by July 21, 2026 more than 375 certified deals across 28 sports had been built since December 2025 with five certified SSPs.
On the wider industry side, there is still no OpenRTB field for "property". The bid request carries content signals instead. The OpenRTB 2.6-202606 release, published on June 11, 2026, added realtime and firstbroadcast to the Content object to separate a live match from a replay. Identifying the event itself relies on content.id or an extended content ID, as set out in IAB Tech Lab's Live Event Ad Playbook implementation guide. League and rights package names therefore come from deal metadata and the seller's own catalogue, not from a standard bidstream field.
A worked example
Consider a sportswear brand running a DVA+ video campaign across one autumn weekend, with spend spread over Thursday Night Football, an NBA regular season game on Prime Video and live college games bought through a certified SSP deal. A report with Live event property, impressions, cost and completed views might return three property rows plus a large null bucket for non-live streaming impressions. If, hypothetically, the football row showed a cost per mille (CPM, cost per thousand impressions) of US$45 and the basketball row US$30, the buyer could weigh that gap against reach and completion rate. Without the field, both would sit inside one streaming TV line.
Origin and evolution
The dimension predates unified reporting. On July 9, 2024, Amazon opened live event open auction supply to self-service DSP advertisers in the United States, Canada and Mexico, having previously sold it only through its managed service. The same announcement said "property-level reporting (e.g. NBA League Pass, ONE Championship)" was available in the Amazon DSP Report Center under a "Live event" dimension.
Inventory grew quickly. Prime Video introduced ads in late January 2024, and the 11-year NBA agreement, worth about US$1.8 billion a year, began with the 2025-26 season. PPC Land reported that Prime Video took the NBA Finals to more than 17 countries in June 2026. Amazon's ad sales chief Alan Moss said the company had become a "full year-round sports network", with 80 net new advertisers in the NFL and 30 new advertisers in the NBA's first year.
Amazon's Live Events Optimizer, a streaming TV line item strategy for live events across Amazon-owned and open-internet supply in 21 markets, followed. Its announcement page carries a heading date of December 1, 2025 while its page metadata says November 18, 2025.
Unified reporting then absorbed the older dimension. It opened in beta on November 11, 2025 and reached general availability in June 2026. The November 11, 2026 reference formalised the nine live event attributes under their current names. Legacy report pages enter read-only mode on December 17, 2026 and shut down on January 31, 2027, after which property-level live reporting exists only in unified reporting.
Why it matters for marketers
Live sport is among the most sought-after streaming inventory. Digiday reported in May 2026 that Amazon offered combined direct and programmatic buying across its sports portfolio, and that some NBA and WNBA inventory was sold out. Amazon's own figures put the 2025 Thursday Night Football average at 15.3 million viewers according to Nielsen, the highest in the package's 20-year history, with a Wild Card game at 31.6 million.
Blending properties hides the real cost of reach. The field also lets advertisers check whether automated tools are concentrating spend. Amazon's automatic deal selection for streaming TV, launched in April 2026, and the Live Events Optimizer both choose inventory with limited buyer input. Property-level reporting is one of the few ways to see where those systems sent money.
The same need is visible across the market. Magnite said that more than 5,800 advertisers bought live sports inventory through its platform that they had not bought the year before, with live sports spend up 56%. Buyers spreading money across those routes need a consistent property label to compare them.
Limitations and disputes
The field depends on seller metadata, and there is no shared taxonomy. One publisher may label a game "NBA", another "NBA League Pass" and a third the name of a regional broadcaster. Amazon's definition, which accepts a rights package, a league or a content property, allows all three. Values are therefore not guaranteed to roll up neatly across Amazon-owned and third-party supply.
Amazon has not published a complete value list, a field ID for the application programming interface (API) or the former name of this specific field. Third-party connector references reviewed for this article did not list live event fields at all.
Reporting granularity is also uneven. Property tells an advertiser which package, not which moment. Questions about whether an ad ran in a decisive final quarter or a blowout depend on the ad break and ad slot order fields, and on concurrency data that the IAB Tech Lab's guide, open for comment until November 23, 2026, is still standardising. Finally, Amazon reports on inventory it also sells. Viewing and attention claims for its own sports, such as TVision attention rankings or EDO search lift cited by Amazon, come from Amazon's marketing material rather than independent audits.
How it differs from related fields
Live event genre. Genre is the sport or event category, such as basketball or football. Property is the commercial product within it. Two properties can share one genre.
Live event content provider. The provider is the company supplying the stream or rights, such as a broadcaster or platform. A single provider can carry several properties.
Live event name and ID. These identify a single event, such as one game. Property groups many events under one league or package.
Content genre and content title. These general content fields cover all streaming and video, live or not. Live event property is populated only for live event inventory and is null elsewhere.
Recent developments
Amazon's four-step DVA+ campaign builder remains in a US closed beta, with general availability across 35 markets planned for the first quarter of 2027. On the standards side, IAB Tech Lab's LEAP guide, released for comment on October 28, 2026, covers forecasting and concurrent stream counts. Amazon's Pramod Jagtap said it "shortens integrations and lets partners line up quickly when timing matters most."
