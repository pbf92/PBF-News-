TITLE: The Stock Market Is Primed for an Explosive Week. Nvidia and the Federal Reserve May Decide What Comes Next.
OUTLET: The Motley Fool

Year to date, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -1.03%) and Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -0.74%) have added 8% and 5%, respectively. But the stock market faces two important tests this week that could determine whether the recent sell-off continues or the major indexes stage a recovery.
First, artificial intelligence giant Nvidia (NVDA -0.44%) will announce third-quarter earnings after the market closes on Tuesday, Nov. 17. Second, the Federal Reserve will release minutes from its October policy meeting on Wednesday, Nov. 18.
Here's what investors should know.
Image source: Getty Images.
Nvidia's earnings will test the strength of the AI boom
Nvidia is one of the largest companies in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite, accounting for about 6% of the former and 12% of the latter. So, its quarterly financial results can have an outsized impact on both indexes. Furthermore, Nvidia dominates the market for artificial intelligence accelerators, which means its earnings offer valuable insight into the health of the broader AI industry.
The Wall Street consensus says Nvidia's revenue will increase 91% to $109 billion, while non-GAAP earnings increase 91% to $2.48 per diluted share. But investors may be more interested in management's guidance, given growing concerns about the sustainability of AI spending.
Importantly, Nvidia beat Wall Street's consensus earnings estimates in seven of the last eight quarters, according to LSEG. But the market has often been unimpressed by strong results. Nvidia shares fell by an average of 0.9% on the day after those eight reports, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite fell by an average of 0.2% and 0.3%, respectively.
So what? Strong financial results alone may not satisfy the market. Wall Street will be looking for evidence that those strong results are sustainable, and that means proof that massive investments in AI infrastructure are yielding sufficient returns. Also, this will be Nvidia's first quarterly report without former CEO Jensen Huang, and his resignation will likely be a popular topic on the earnings call.
Importantly, while several Wall Street analysts have expressed concern about the situation, given that Huang's relationships with suppliers and customers was considered one of the company's competitive advantages, most analysts still think Nvidia is undervalued. The stock has a median target price of $275 per share, implying 76% upside from the current share price of $156.
Fed meeting minutes could reveal clues about future interest rate hikes
The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark interest rate by 25 basis points in September, the first rate increase in three years. Since 2000, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq have dropped by an average of 11% and 17%, respectively, within three months of the first hike in a new cycle. The current drawdown has so far been less severe than average, but the Nasdaq has entered correction territory and the three-month window is not yet over.
Stubborn inflation was the driver behind the Fed's pivot to tighter monetary policy. Tariffs have contributed to the problem, but they are not the entire impetus. Research from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis found that tariffs added about 0.5 percentage points to inflation in June, while other factors (particularly higher energy prices tied to the Iran war) contributed about 1.2 percentage points.
Minutes from the Fed's October meeting will be released on Wednesday, Nov. 18, and they could reveal how central bank officials are weighing those inflationary pressures against the risk of slowing economic growth. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has promised to restore price stability, and with inflation running about 1.6 percentage points above the 2% target, the odds are good that the Fed will approve another quarter-point rate hike in December.
Indeed, CME Group's FedWatch tool currently indicates a 96% probability of a quarter-point rate hike when the Fed meets next month. That assumption may blunt the impact of the October minutes, but investors could react strongly if the Fed is considering more aggressive action, such as a 0.5 basis point rate hike.
Higher interest rates can hurt stocks by raising borrowing costs, which slows consumer and business spending, ultimately pressuring corporate profits. Higher interest rates also make bonds look more attractive, which can motivate investors to pull money out of the stock market. That explains why the first hike in a new tightening cycle is often accompanied by a stock market correction.
