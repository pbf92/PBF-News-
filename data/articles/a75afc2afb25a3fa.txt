TITLE: Shopify urges entrepreneurs to stick with their big idea
OUTLET: Campaign US

Lots of people have ideas for a new business. But many hesitate to take the leap. To those dreamers, Canadian e-commerce giant Shopify has a simple answer: Go for it!
That’s the message behind “Just an Idea,” a seven-country brand campaign with strategy and creative from Mother Los Angeles. It encourages people to give their business ideas a shot, with help from Shopify and its AI startup tool, Sidekick.
The campaign is anchored by a fast-paced, two-minute hero which will debut during college football games in the U.S. on Nov. 14.
The spot presents four stories of would-be entrepreneurs who, like many people, have an idea for a business. There is an office worker who wants to stop pigeons from flying into windows; a couple with an idea for doggie couches; a group of high schoolers who’ve developed personalized pimple patches, and an aspiring fashion designer hoping to turn his sketchbook drawings into an apparel brand.
Unlike many people, however, these protagonists find the courage to actually share their idea. Momentum builds as they start to believe they can turn their idea into reality, but then reality stops them in their tracks.
The many practical challenges of the next steps curdle into self-doubt and soon the heroes are back to feeling like it was “just an idea” after all. Their stories, and the spot, almost grind to a halt, until they discover Sidekick. The tool helps the office worker develop a storefront strategy and identify markets, for example, while giving the dog owners professional product photos, website copy and a launch strategy.
The campaign targets people who’ve never considered themselves entrepreneurs but may have an idea they’ve been afraid to pursue, said Neal Hicks, Shopify’s director of creative. According to Shopify’s “Entrepreneur Effect” report, 57% of entrepreneurs who waited before opening their business say they wish they had started sooner.
“For years, a lot of people with great ideas talked themselves out of them because they didn’t have the money, the time or the experience,” Hicks said. “With AI, a lot of those reasons just aren’t true anymore.”
The goal of the campaign is to get people with big ideas to reconsider what’s possible, he said. “If someone who’s been sitting on an idea watches the film and thinks, ‘You know what? Maybe now I can,’ it worked.”
The film’s quick pace is designed to capture the excitement and uncertainty of turning an idea into a business. “It’s just on the verge of overwhelming,” said Tobias Lindborg, creative director at Mother. “It set the direction for everything else.”
Marcus Ibanez directed the spot for production company Iconoclast. It was shot on film and set to Yung Lean’s song “Forever Young.” This is the first time Shopify has worked with Mother L.A.
The campaign is running in seven countries, including Canada, with ads on OTT and broadcast TV, and paid social media.
It comes at a time when technology, particularly artificial intelligence, is making it easier to launch a business.
Shopify said the number of first-time sellers on its platform increased sevenfold from 2018 to 2025, with businesses selling more than US$1 billion daily on Shopify last year.
Built into Shopify, Sidekick is designed to help users work through questions and tasks they might otherwise need experience or outside expertise to tackle. Hicks describes it as a tool that “shows up the way a great partner does: It’s there when you’re stuck, it knows the things you don’t yet, and it helps you take the next step.”
But he said the campaign is less about promoting a single new feature than showing people how much the barriers to starting a business have changed.
“We believe we’re at the start of a new era of entrepreneurship, with more people doing work they love and far more of the world’s ideas actually getting made,” Hicks said. “We want to help define what that looks like.”
