Staff at Grand Theft Auto VI developer vote against union recognition
Staff at the developer behind the Grand Theft Auto game franchise have voted against recognising a workplace union.
More than 1,500 workers at Rockstar, which has an office in Edinburgh, took part in the two-week ballot over formal recognition by the Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain (IWGB).
The vote took place during an employment tribunal concerning a group of Rockstar staff who claim they were dismissed for trade union activities last year. Rockstar has denied this and alleged staff were sacked for discussing confidential information online.
About 75% of staff balloted voted against union recognition proposals, with 24.7% backing them.
It is thought to be one of the largest entertainment launches in history, attracting billions of pounds in its first seven days of sales.
IWGB said union recognition would have established a "formal framework" for staff to negotiate collectively with management over issues including pay, overtime compensation and bonuses, leave and pensions.
The union said it would also have allowed staff a right to consultation around some workplace changes, such as redundancies and the introduction of artificial intelligence.
It said the result of the ballot did not prevent workers from continuing to "push for workplace improvements" through the IWGB or joining the union.
"For anyone at Rockstar who believes in a fairer workplace, a fairer sector and a fairer society - this isn't a sign to retreat," staff represented by the union said.
"It's a sign to double down on what we stand for.
"Hundreds of our co-workers voted in favour of our union at Rockstar, yet sadly we recognise we have more work to do to build understanding and win the trust of more of our colleagues. We're ready to put in the effort to make that happen."
About 34 people are believed to have been dismissed from Rockstar North in October 2025 across the UK and Canada over what the company described as "the sharing of confidential company information".
Jennifer Kolbe, head of publishing at Rockstar Games, said: "This result reflects our team's strong support for continuing the direct relationships, collaboration and open dialogue that have helped make Rockstar Games so innovative and strong.
"As we look ahead, we're excited to build on all we've accomplished together."
