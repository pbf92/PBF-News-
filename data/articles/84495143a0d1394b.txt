TITLE: Is GTA 6 Actually Worth Playing On The Xbox Series S?
OUTLET: BGR

Is GTA 6 Actually Worth Playing On The Xbox Series S?
"Grand Theft Auto 6" officially releases on November 19th, and depending on the time zone you're in, you may already have access to it. Some eager players are switching the time zone on their console to play it early, a process dubbed 'the New Zealand trick.' While Rockstar has released a statement that outlines why "Grand Theft Auto 6" will look best on PS5 Pro, prices for Sony's console have gone absolutely wild.
Xbox Series X|S owners are also able to play the console-exclusive title immediately upon release, but because the hardware in Microsoft's console doesn't quite stack up to the PS5 Pro, there have been some concerns about its in-game performance. Based on testing and benchmark results we've seen from game reviewers who were granted early access to the title, we can say that "GTA 6" is absolutely worth playing on Xbox Series S — with some compromises.
The game itself is locked to 30 FPS on console, so you shouldn't expect buttery smooth gameplay in the first place. But the Xbox Series S comes with some limitations of its own. The PS5, PS5 Pro, and Xbox Series X run the game at a native 1440p resolution with upscaling to 4K through PSSR or Rockstar's built-in TAA. The Xbox Series S, however, runs the game at just 720p with upscaling to 1080p via FSR 4. Even at a lower resolution, Xbox Series S owners should be prepared for dropped frames.
Xbox Series S in-game performance
As you can see from the screenshot above from Digital Foundry's "GTA 6" review (via YouTube), the difference in visual fidelity between the Xbox Series X and Series S is fairly small. Graphical compromises come in the form of messier or altogether omitted shadows, a shorter render distance for game objects, creating a pop-in effect, shallower depth-of-field, and lower-resolution textures, such as in the volumetric clouds, moving water, and characters' facial hair.
With that said, there is a tradeoff between the Xbox Series X and Series S in how the game runs. The Series X stays close to the consistent 30 FPS target set by Rockstar, but some dips into the mid-20s should be expected during resource-intensive scenes. The Series S, however, doesn't quite stack up. You can expect closer to a consistent mid-to-high 20s frame rate during typical gameplay like driving a car at high speeds, with combat scenes hitting closer to low-to-mid 20s. Cutscenes and walking around Leonida will usually reach the 30 FPS target, though resource-intensive moments like multiple car explosions may see your FPS dip down to around 20 or lower.
IGN (via YouTube) released early gameplay footage on the Xbox Series S in a video titled "GTA 6 Series S Gameplay – It's Shockingly Good" that shows how the game actually runs smoothly a majority of the time, though there are obviously noticeable frame drops during certain scenes and transitions. Several users in the comments said that they had a great overall experience playing "GTA 6" on the Series S, which is impressive given the hardware limitations Rockstar had to work around with a six-year-old console.
