Parents Wary of AI Therapy for Children
A Harvard-linked study finds most parents are uncomfortable with AI-led behavioral therapy for their children.
Updated on Nov. 25, 2026 in Children’s Health
Live Poll
Would you be comfortable with your child receiving AI-led mental health therapy?
A study published in JAMA Pediatrics on November 2, 2026, by researchers from Harvard Medical School, Boston Children's Hospital and RAND Corporation found that 76% of parents are uncomfortable with AI-led behavioral therapy for children. The survey covered 572 parents with at least one child aged 5 to 17, conducted in November 2025.
Why it matters
The findings signal that parental trust is a significant hurdle for AI tools aimed at children's mental health care, at a time when young people are already turning to chatbots for advice. Diagnostic accuracy and empathy emerged as the central concerns shaping whether families would accept such therapy.
The peer-reviewed survey of 572 parents found 58.5% cited diagnostic accuracy as an important factor and 49.4% flagged chatbot empathy as a concern. A separate 2025 national survey found 19.2% of young people aged 12 to 21 used AI chatbots for mental health advice, but the study does not report how parental comfort would shift with clinical outcomes.
The players
Harvard Medical School
Harvard Medical School is the medical school of Harvard University, based in Boston, Massachusetts, and one of the most prominent medical research institutions in the United States.
Boston Children's Hospital
Boston Children's Hospital is a pediatric hospital in Boston affiliated with Harvard Medical School and widely regarded as one of the leading children's hospitals in the country.
RAND Corporation
RAND Corporation is a nonprofit research organization that conducts policy analysis and public health research for government and private clients.
The details
Parents rated factors including diagnostic accuracy, empathy, medical professional recommendation and confidentiality. Diagnostic accuracy was the biggest concern, and 46.8% of parents wanted a medical professional's recommendation before using such therapy.
Timeline
- November 2025: Survey of 572 parents conducted.
- 2025: National survey finds 19.2% of young people used AI chatbots for mental health advice.
- November 2, 2026: Study published in JAMA Pediatrics.
Health Landscape
The study, produced with RAND Corporation, adds to a growing body of research on public acceptance of digital mental health tools. Its findings suggest that adoption of AI in pediatric care will depend on parental trust as much as on the technology itself.
Parents weighing AI-led therapy for a child will want to ask about diagnostic accuracy and whether a medical professional is involved in recommending it. The findings suggest that families are likely to expect clinician oversight before accepting such tools.
The takeaway
Parents are far more likely to accept AI in children's therapy when accuracy and clinical oversight are clearly established. Trust, not just technology, will determine how these tools reach families.
