TITLE: Ex-Man United star shocked by Mauricio Pochettino's surprise post-match training plan
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

Mauricio Pochettino had his non-playing Manchester United stars complete a weights session after their UEFA Champions League draw with Sporting Lisbon on Wednesday.
Much has been deliberated about the squad's physical condition and how it will be tested by the demands Pochettino brings. The Argentine has long been renowned for his high-intensity style, which requires his players to maintain a certain level of fitness.
For years, United have consistently ranked among the Premier League sides who cover the least ground, a statistic that has been repeatedly used against them. However, Pochettino had several of his players undertaking additional sessions once the final whistle blew in Portugal.
While it would be standard practice for players to complete a series of sprints or some form of cool-down, enforcing a weights session is extremely uncommon.
Former United midfielder Owen Hargreaves was present in Lisbon and was left stunned by the activity, insisting it was something he had never witnessed before. "I have never seen a weight session away at the Champions League," he said, according to The Mirror.
Pochettino has been at the helm for just one week, having departed the U.S. Men's National Team to take charge in Manchester, and his methods have already been identified as a significant cultural shift for the players, who have not worked under a manager in recent years who places such emphasis on their running statistics.
Jamie Carragher highlighted, following the weekend defeat to Liverpool, how United's grueling fixture calendar would give Pochettino minimal opportunity to work with his squad in training sessions to guarantee they reach his required fitness standards, which may explain Wednesday's workout.

"When you think about Pochettino coming in, and we're saying the team don't look fit," said Carragher. "This was a big thing when Jürgen Klopp first came in, he was all about high energy and getting the team fit.
"But you've got no time to train, and in the odd week you might not have a midweek game. They're out of the Carabao Cup, so when that comes around that will be a really heavy-loaded week. But I remember the injuries that Liverpool got under Klopp in that first season, and Klopp wasn't saying, 'I'm getting this wrong'; no, they weren't fit enough. I'd imagine something similar will happen with Pochettino because they don't look physically right."
The Sky Sports analyst argued that the new boss would need to accept the gamble of losing players to injury in order to get them prepared, and unavoidably, he would sacrifice one or two during the process.
