Jonathan Krinsky Sees More Market Downside as Apple Holds Up
Apple shares have held up as Wall Street sells tech stocks and the S&P 500 enters a correction. The index is down roughly 12% since its October 6 record, while Apple has gained around 3%, according to BeInCrypto. Investors have treated Apple as a safe haven during the AI stock selloff; its capital spending is around 2% of revenue, according to Bernstein, and investors rotated away from AI hardware stocks earlier this month. BTIG chief market technician Jonathan Krinsky said Apple’s resilience could signal further declines for the broader market, telling CNBC it would be historically unprecedented for the S&P 500 to reach a final bottom while Apple remains near its current level. In an analysis of nine major S&P 500 bottoms since 2016, BeInCrypto found Apple’s median decline was about 24%; the stock is currently down just 4% from its high. Apple’s immediate support is around $340, followed by $325 and $317. Krinsky sees a possible short-term market rebound but said weak market breadth points to further downside. A sustained break below $340 could indicate the selloff is reaching Apple, though historical patterns do not guarantee another decline.
