TITLE: Only 2 of Amazon's 42 engagement metrics cover Sponsored Products
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon Ads has written down how it counts a started video, a completed audio ad, a five-second view and a muted spot, in a Support Center page updated on November 17, 2026 that defines 42 engagement metrics and gives, for most of them, the name each one carried before. The page belongs to the documentation for unified reporting, the single reporting surface Amazon has been building across its ad products.
The definitions are precise. The coverage is lopsided. By PPC Land's count of the page's three tables, 34 of the 42 metrics carry the marker for DVA+, the campaign type that replaced the Amazon DSP name, and 23 carry the Sponsored Brands marker. Sponsored Products appears against two metrics: five-second views and five-second view rate. Sponsored Display and Streaming TV Smart mode/Sponsored TV, both of which appear on the companion dimensions page, are marked against none.
Everything about audio stays on the programmatic side. All 16 audio metrics apply to DVA+ alone, while the six peer benchmarks on the page, for video completion rate and cost per completed view, apply only to Sponsored Brands.
In Short
Amazon has published the definitions of 42 metrics that measure how people interact with video, audio and interactive ads, along with the older names each metric used in DSP and sponsored ads reports. This matters to anyone moving reports into Amazon's unified system before the old report pages close, because the names change and coverage differs sharply by ad product: audio metrics exist only for DVA+, and Sponsored Products gets two video measures. What changes in practice is the vocabulary: a "Video start" becomes "Starts (video ad)", an "Audio view" becomes "Plays (audio ad)" and the eCPVC becomes "Cost per completed view (video ad)", with formulas stated on the page.
Three tables, one vocabulary
The page sits under "Measure results" in the Support Center, which now carries the Amazon Ads Agent name in its header. Its summary line reads: "Find engagement metrics for reporting, including interaction, attention (video ad), and attention (audio ad) metrics." Engagement metrics, the page explains, measure how shoppers interact with ad content itself, such as watching videos, listening to audio ads or engaging with interactive elements, "before taking any shopping events."
The note above the tables repeats, word for word, the principle Amazon set out on its dimensions page: "Unified reporting uses standardized metric and dimension names across all ad products. The Metric column shows the name used in unified reporting. The Former columns show the corresponding names used in other reporting surfaces." Each table carries five columns: Metric, Definition, Ad product, Former DVA+ name and Former sponsored ads name. The Ad product column holds icons only. The page has no change log, so there is no record of what the November 17 update altered.
PPC Land count of the Amazon Ads Support Center page "Engagement metrics", updated November 17, 2026. Sponsored Display and Streaming TV Smart mode/Sponsored TV carry no marker on any row.
Two patterns run through the count. Every metric marked for DVA+ has a former DVA+ name, so each programmatic engagement measure has a traceable predecessor. The sponsored ads side is thinner. Of the 23 video metrics marked for Sponsored Brands, only seven show a former sponsored ads name: first quartile, midpoint, third quartile, complete views, unmutes, five-second views and five-second view rate. Starts, completion rate, cost per completed view, pauses, resumes, mutes, replays, both skip metrics, video impressions and all six benchmarks carry the Sponsored Brands marker with an empty sponsored ads column. The page does not say whether a blank cell means the metric is new to Sponsored Brands reporting or simply had no mapped equivalent.
How the video and audio counts are built
The definitions describe a sequence, from the moment an ad is delivered to the moment it is benchmarked against other brands. According to the page, the chain runs in seven steps, with audio following video at most of them.
- Impression. "Impressions (video ad)" is "The number of times a video ad was displayed." For audio, "Impressions (audio ad)" is "The number of times an audio ad was served."
- Start. "Starts (video ad)" is "The number of times a video ad was played." The audio equivalent uses the same wording for an audio ad.
- Short-play thresholds. Video uses a five-second mark: "5-second views (video ad)" counts "The number of times a user watched at least 5 seconds of a video ad or the entire ad if it was shorter than 5 seconds." Audio uses two seconds: "Plays (audio ad)" counts "The number of times an audio ad was played for 2 seconds or longer."
- Quartiles. First quartile, midpoint and third quartile count plays reaching at least 25%, 50% and 75% of the ad. For video only, each definition adds: "If the ad was rewound, we use the total percentage of unduplicated video that was viewed to calculate the completion rate."
- Completion. "Complete views (video ad)" is "The number of times a video ad was played till the end." "Complete listens (audio ad)" uses the same words for audio. Audio also has a publisher-defined checkpoint: "Progress (audio ad)" is "The number of times an audio ad was played until the time marker assigned by the publisher."
- Rates and costs. "Completion rate (video) = complete views (video) / starts (video)" and "Cost per completed view (video) = total cost / complete views (video)." The audio formulas mirror them: "Completion rate (audio) = complete listens (audio) / starts (audio)" and "Cost per completed listen (audio) = total cost / complete listens (audio)".
- Benchmarks. For Sponsored Brands, completion rate and cost per completed view each come with 25th, 50th and 75th percentile values drawn from peer brands. Cost benchmarks "are displayed in the currency selected for conversion."
Two denominators, and what counts as seen
The formulas carry a distinction that matters when figures are compared across reports. Amazon's completion rate divides complete views by starts, not by impressions. It therefore measures how many plays that began went on to finish, and an ad that was displayed but never started does not pull the figure down. Five-second view rate takes the opposite approach. It is defined as "The percentage of impressions where a user watched at least 5 seconds," so impressions form its base. Two rates from the same campaign can rest on different populations.
Impressions themselves are not defined identically across formats. A video impression is a display event; an audio impression is a serve event. Neither definition refers to whether the ad was in view. The page sends that question elsewhere: "Viewability metrics for video ads, such as viewable distribution and Viewable CTR (vCTR), are listed with other viewability metrics in Traffic metrics." The counting of an impression has become a live issue at Amazon. In early November, Amazon told advertisers that DVA display ads on Amazon store placements will count an impression only once the ad appears on screen from January 1, 2027, with counts expected to fall about 63% on average. The engagement page does not mention that change, and its video impression definition does not refer to viewability.
The rewind rule on the video quartiles raises a quieter question. Each quartile and the complete-view count says Amazon uses "the total percentage of unduplicated video that was viewed to calculate the completion rate" when an ad is rewound. Read literally, a viewer who watches the first 40% twice has seen 40% of the ad, not 80%, and would not reach the midpoint. The audio quartile definitions carry no such rule, even though "Rewinds (audio ad)" exists as a separate count.
The thresholds differ from those used elsewhere. Amazon's two-second audio play is twice the threshold in the audio glossary IAB Australia published in October, which counts a play after one second of listening; that glossary keeps a two-second mark for its audible ad impression. On video, Amazon's five-second view is shorter than either ThruPlay duration at Meta, where a six-second option was reported alongside the 15-second default on November 16. None of these figures are interchangeable, and a "view" on one platform is not a "view" on another.
Sponsored Brands gets the video toolkit
The video table is the only one that spans ad products, and it splits three ways. Fifteen metrics apply to both DVA+ and Sponsored Brands: starts, the three quartiles, complete views, completion rate, cost per completed view, and the seven interaction counts of pauses, resumes, mutes, unmutes, replays, skips back and skips forward, plus video impressions. Six apply to Sponsored Brands alone, all of them benchmarks. Two apply to Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Products together: five-second views and five-second view rate. DVA+ carries neither of the five-second metrics.
The Sponsored Products marker is narrow, and it matches how Amazon presented the format. When Amazon introduced Sponsored Products video at unBoxed on November 11, 2025, allowing one to five product feature videos per product in the United States, it pointed to click-through rates eight times higher among the 20% of shoppers who watched for longer than five seconds. The five-second mark Amazon used to promote the format is the only engagement measure the page assigns to Sponsored Products.
Sponsored Display is absent from every row, although it appears in the key of the dimensions page. Amazon retired the Amazon DSP name on September 29, 2026 and describes DVA+ as bringing together campaigns previously created through Amazon DSP, Sponsored Display and Sponsored TV, which may explain why the DVA+ marker stands in for display video here. The page does not say so. Streaming TV Smart mode/Sponsored TV, which the dimensions page described as not yet supported while still marking it against many fields, is not marked at all.
A percentile puzzle in the cost benchmarks
The six benchmark rows are where the page is least consistent. For completion rate, the three percentile definitions follow the standard reading. The 25th percentile is "the value where 25% of peer brands fall at or below", the 50th is "the value where half of peer brands fall above and half fall below" and the 75th is "the value where 75% of peer brands fall at or below."
For cost per completed view, the wording changes. The 25th percentile is again "the value where 25% of peer brands fall at or below." The 50th percentile, though, is described as "representing the value where 75% of peer brands fall at or below," and the 75th as "representing the value where 25% of peer brands fall at or below." A median cannot have 75% of brands at or below it, and the 25th and 75th percentiles cannot share the same definition. At least one of the three lines is wrong.
Amazon's own benchmark documentation helps narrow it down. A separate Support Center page, updated on November 11, describes cost percentiles in plain terms: for cost metrics including cost per completed view, the top 25% marks the higher-cost level and the bottom 25% the lower-cost level, and that page says the benchmarks rest on ad-spend weighted calculations. On that reading, the p75 cost value marks the expensive end, with most peer brands paying less, which is the opposite of what the engagement page's p75 line says. The same benchmark page named Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Streaming TV and DVA+ as the products with benchmark columns in unified reporting. On the engagement page, the two video benchmarks are marked for Sponsored Brands only.
Benchmarks have moved quickly. Amazon introduced benchmark report types in November 2025, and benchmarks reached general availability in 18 markets on May 18, 2026, covering eight metrics. Completion rate (video ad) and cost per completed view (video ad) are two of them.
