TITLE: Review Identifies HE4 and ROMA as Most Reliable Biomarkers for Ovarian Masses
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive evidence synthesis of 67 systematic reviews and meta-analyses has evaluated the diagnostic accuracy of single and combined biomarkers for ovarian cancer across distinct clinical scenarios. The research, published in the Journal of Ovarian Research by Xiaohong Xian, Ming Yan, Jiayi Lu, Jinhui Tian, and colleagues, demonstrates that no single global ranking captures biomarker utility. Instead, clinical performance depends heavily on the specific medical objective, showing that diagnostic accuracy shifts markedly between the preoperative evaluation of adnexal masses, population screening, and post-treatment recurrence monitoring.
To clarify the diagnostic performance of available tests, the researchers conducted systematic literature searches across PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science. They retrieved meta-analyses and systematic reviews focusing on diagnostic markers, subsequently appraising their methodological rigor with A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews, version 2 (AMSTAR-2). Furthermore, the certainty of the accumulated evidence was graded using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) framework, supported by pairwise indirect comparisons and multi-dimensional subgroup analyses designed to capture real-world clinical variation.
For the differential diagnosis and preoperative triage of adnexal masses, indirect descriptive comparisons indicated that human epididymis protein 4 (HE4) and the Risk of Ovarian Malignancy Algorithm (ROMA) offer the most reliable evidentiary support. HE4 attained the highest numerical specificity at 0.89, with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning 0.88 to 0.91. ROMA demonstrated the most balanced diagnostic profile, achieving an estimated sensitivity of 0.86 alongside a specificity of 0.82. Both HE4 and ROMA were supported by moderate-certainty evidence under GRADE assessments, establishing them as dependable adjuncts for surgical triage.
In the same adnexal mass setting, lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) ranked highest in descriptive indirect comparisons for numerical sensitivity, reaching 0.92 with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.88 to 0.96, while also exhibiting the highest diagnostic odds ratio. Despite these strong numerical parameters, the researchers emphasized that LPA remains promising but unvalidated for routine adoption. Consequently, current clinical decisions prior to surgery should prioritize established evidence-backed tools like HE4 and ROMA over unvalidated alternatives, aligning testing strategies directly with the diagnostic demands of pelvic mass evaluation.
Evaluating strategies for early detection and population-level screening revealed clear constraints among current blood tests. The review established that no standalone single biomarker achieves the sensitivity threshold necessary for independent screening programs. While individual blood markers proved inadequate in isolation, numerical accuracy improved considerably when utilizing multi-marker panels, cell-free DNA (cfDNA) methylation profiling, and artificial intelligence models. Specifically, artificial intelligence-based algorithms achieved a pooled area under the curve of 0.95, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.92 to 0.96, though prospective validation remains essential.
For cancer recurrence monitoring, cancer antigen 125 (CA125) demonstrated high specificity but only moderate sensitivity, while published evidence for alternative surveillance markers remains scarce. Across all diagnostic applications, subgroup investigations demonstrated that performance metrics are not static properties of the biomarkers. Diagnostic accuracy was consistently elevated in advanced-stage malignancies compared to early-stage disease, and proved higher in epithelial tumours than in tumours with mixed histologies. These stage-related differences reached statistical significance for ROMA and CA125, but not for HE4, LPA, or osteopontin (OPN).
Patient demographic and clinical characteristics also introduced substantial diagnostic variability across the evaluated studies. Menopausal status produced statistically significant variations in the diagnostic performance of ROMA and CA125, whereas HE4, LPA, and OPN demonstrated no significant divergence by menopausal state. Additionally, the clinical performance of both HE4 and CA125 dropped when control cohorts consisted of patients with benign gynaecological conditions rather than healthy individuals. Because these subgroup analyses are exploratory and hypothesis-generating, the authors concluded that future research must prioritize prospectively validated, multi-marker strategies.
