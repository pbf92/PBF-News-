TITLE: Cochrane library seeks $233,000 increase, warns of closures under draft budget
OUTLET: CochraneNow

A delegation from the Cochrane Municipal Library asked town council on Nov. 16 for an 18 per cent funding increase in the 2027 budget, warning the library would likely close at least two days a week under the draft budget.
Library board chair Chuck Love and trustee Tom Hovland made the $233,000 request as council neared the end of budget deliberations. Library director Jessie Pepin also attended and provided details when called upon.
The draft budget includes a three per cent increase of about $38,000. The board estimates it will cost $52,000 to comply with new provincial library requirements taking effect Jan. 1.
The delegation also outlined two lower funding options. A 16 per cent increase, or $206,000, would maintain current hours and services and cover compliance costs. A 14 per cent increase, or $181,000, would mean a 13 per cent cut in operating hours. Under either option, the board would shelve its recently completed needs assessment and put capital fundraising on hold.
Draft budget could mean two days of closures each week
“What will happen if we only get three per cent? What will happen is we will probably have to close at least two days a week,” Love said. “There will be staff reductions and possible layoffs.”
Closing two weekdays would cut 20 hours of public access each week. Love said the cuts would also mean fewer programs and less outreach, putting community partnerships the library has built over the years at risk.
Under the 14 per cent option, Love said the library could close at 6 p.m. instead of 8 p.m., cutting into the time many families use the library after work and school.
“I know when I show up at around seven o’clock at night there are at least four kids that are getting tutored while there,” he said.
Another option would be to close on Mondays. Love said that would mean 52 fewer days of public access a year, affecting more than 19,000 visits and eliminating 144 programs.
What the full request would fund
Coun. Tara McFadden asked for the minimum increase needed to maintain current hours and services while meeting the new provincial requirements. The delegation put that figure at $206,000.
“That’s the number that I just need to take clearly into our budget deliberations,” McFadden said. She said council has to weigh maintaining services and planning for the future against residents’ concerns about the cost of living.
The additional $27,000 in the full request would allow the library to test recommendations from its needs assessment and begin raising money for a future facility. The board wants to gauge demand for ideas such as arts spaces and programming with community agencies before committing to the ongoing costs of offering them in a new building.
What Bill 28 compliance involves
The library is already identifying materials in its collection that could be subject to restrictions, the delegation told council. Starting Jan. 1, staff will have to manage access to restricted materials, review new purchases and regularly check shelves for restricted items that may have been returned to general circulation.
The delegation said Cochrane is the largest user of the Marigold Library System because its own collection is limited. Under the new requirements, staff will have to screen incoming holds from other libraries and flag restricted items Cochrane sends to other libraries in the system.
Staff will also have to verify the ages of new cardholders, maintain authorization records for existing cardholders, manage access to public computers and monitor computer use by children aged 12 to 15, the delegation said.
The full request would bring staffing from 19.2 to 21.2 full-time equivalents. One position would help with compliance, a responsibility existing staff would also share. The second would take on marketing and communications and help launch capital fundraising, giving librarians more time to deliver programs and services.
Current building needs repairs
Coun. Patrick Wilson asked what accounted for the gap between the estimated $52,000 in compliance costs and the full $233,000 request. The delegation pointed to the cost of delivering existing services and maintaining the building, including replacing the original, 20-year-old carpet and repainting the walls. Patrons have repeatedly said in surveys that the library looks run down, the delegation said.
“We understand all of this costs money and we also understand that with all of this you have a lot of difficult decisions to make,” Love told council.
Council accepted the report as information.
Budget deliberations.continue today.
