TITLE: Michael Owen brands Manchester United 'classless' after Michael Carrick firing
OUTLET: Yahoo

Michael Owen branded Manchester United's handling of Michael Carrick's firing as 'classless' ahead of their weekend clash with Liverpool.
Sunday will see the Reds welcome Man United to Anfield, in what will be Mauricio Pochettino's first game in charge since opting to leave his post with the USMNT to replace Carrick. United's handling of Carrick's firing was a PR disaster, with reports emerging that the club intended to approach Pochettino to replace the former, a club legend, before even dismissing him.
It was later revealed United had reached an agreement with the Argentine to become the club's next head coach while Carrick was still in post. Carrick was fired on Nov 12, with Pochettino then announced as the new United boss on Nov. 13.
In Owen's eyes, the handling of Carrick's firing lacked class, especially given his status as a club legend who helped United win the 2008 Champions League final and multiple Premier League titles.
"I've had the immense privilege of leading Manchester United, the football club that I love," said Carrick in a statement following his firing. "There are very few who get to live that and feel it, and for that I'm incredibly grateful."
He added, "I want to say a big thank you to the supporters. I must say, you are a special, rare breed."
The former Liverpool forward reckons much of United's current issues stem from the leadership at the top, adding that public criticism of the club by current players Lisandro Martinez and Bruno Fernandes paints a picture of chaos.

"It all filters through from the top, and you look at leading into that, the next biggest thing in the lead-up was two players coming out and being critical of tactics.
"In a well-run club, it just doesn't happen. Things like that never happen."
Owen also believes that Carrick handled the situation with class, something which was not afforded to him by United, adding his dignified exit probably did the club a favor.
"Thankfully, he [Carrick] held his head up high with a lot of integrity like you would expect from Michael Carrick, probably did Manchester United a favor," said Owen.
"Nothing that the club does in recent times has smacked of class, has it? It's just not the club it once was, and I'm sure it will return, but it might take a bit more change before it does."
