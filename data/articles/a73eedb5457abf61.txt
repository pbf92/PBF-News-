TITLE: Christian political prisoner released in Cuba, but brother remains detained
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A Cuban political prisoner who is a Baptist Christian has been freed after five years in prison, although his brother remains detained.
Nadir Martín Perdomo and Jorge Martín Perdomo were arrested in July 2021 after taking part in peaceful protests on the island. Courts handed Nadir a six year sentence and Jorge eight years, for assault, contempt and public disorder.
The UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention concluded the brothers were denied their liberty without due process and a legal basis, so were being held against international law. It called for them to be paid reparations.
Their mother, Marta, has repeatedly warned the brothers have been denied medical care, a particular issue for Nadir as he suffers from epilepsy, asthma, gastritis, food malabsorption syndrome, a hiatal hernia and renal colic. She told Christian Solidarity Worldwide (CSW) his release may have been influenced by pressure put on authorities about his health.
However, the family believes Jorge has not been released in retaliation for complaints published on social media. This has not been confirmed by judicial sources.
Scot Bower, chief executive officer at CSW, said the religious freedom watchdog welcomes the release of Nadir.
“However, it cannot be presented as a humanitarian gesture, or as a response by the Cuban authorities to the UN, as it essentially marks the completion of a prison sentence he should never have had to serve.
“We continue to call for the immediate and unconditional release of Mr Martín Perdomo’s brother Jorge and all other political prisoners, and insist that the Cuban authorities must now ensure not only that Mr Martín Perdomo is able to enjoy his freedom in peace, but that adequate reparations are made to his family as per the recommendations of the UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention,” he added.
Earlier this month, CSW condemned a wave of Christians being arrested by Cuban authorities. Recent cases have included a pastor being detained for “disobedience” despite cancelling a public prayer event; and 21 year-old influencer Anna Bensi, who was motivated by her faith to post about hardship on the island, being sentenced to house arrest.
