TITLE: Sizeable chunk of voters still have doubts about election count integrity, SC’s Winthrop U. poll says
OUTLET: Post and Courier

ROCK HILL — There’s a widespread taboo against discussing politics at the Thanksgiving table.
But for those intent on skirting that etiquette, Winthrop University’s latest polling provides food for thought on this month’s midterm elections.
Across the country, the contests showed Democrats winning big, including in Republican strongholds such as South Carolina. But notable numbers in the polling cast doubt on the integrity of the vote tabulations, according to the Winthrop University Poll-YouGov Survey, which contacted 1,500 participants in the U.S. between Nov. 11-16.
About 30 percent didn’t believe their states accurately counted votes in the midterms. That amount was comprised of 40 percent of Republicans surveyed and 11 percent of Democrats.
President Donald Trump himself had cast suspicion of fraud on the Nov. 3 election, pondering why some polls didn’t match the final result.
“When it comes to believing in fraud, not all Republicans are created equal,” said Scott Huffmon, director of the Winthrop Poll.
“More than half of the Republicans who said the MAGA label best described them believed there was fraud in this election,” he added. “That number drops to 45 percent among those who feel the label ‘America First’ best describes them. Perhaps the most interesting finding is that fewer than a quarter of ‘Traditional’ Republicans believe the results of this election were tainted by fraud."
Partisanship was a defining characteristic for belief in fraud across the presidential elections of 2020 and 2024.
”While the size of the swing between elections is larger for Republicans when it comes to believing in fraud, Democrats are not immune to attitudes that move with the electoral fortunes of candidates of their party," Huffmon said.
The Winthrop Poll results using all respondents have a 2.84 percent margin of error. Margins of error for partisan subgroups are larger.
Other topics polled included Trump’s performance and the direction of the country. About 63 percent of respondents said they feel the country is on the wrong track, and the same percentage disapproved of Trump’s leadership.
