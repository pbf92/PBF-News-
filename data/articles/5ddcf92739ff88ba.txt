TITLE: Gallup index places Morocco at the global average for safety
OUTLET: Yabiladi.com

Morocco scored 80 out of 100 on the 2025 Law and Order Index, in line with the global average, according to the 2026 Global Safety Report published by US polling organization Gallup.
Morocco’s score matched those of the United Kingdom, Russia, Romania and Bulgaria, each of which received 80 points. It was slightly ahead of Turkey and Greece, which scored 79 and 75, respectively.
Of the five North African countries compared, Algeria ranked first with 85 points, followed by Libya with 83 and Tunisia with 81. Morocco’s 80 points placed it ahead of Mauritania, which scored 66.
Elsewhere in the Arab world, the United Arab Emirates scored 95, Bahrain 93, and Saudi Arabia and Kuwait 92 each. Jordan scored 88, while Egypt and Iraq each received 86. Algeria scored 85, Libya 83 and Tunisia 81. At the lower end of the table, Lebanon scored 75, Palestine 73 and the Comoros 72.
Four measures of perceived safety
The Law and Order Index does not measure officially recorded crime or assess security institutions solely by legal and administrative standards. Instead, it is based on survey responses to four questions: whether people feel safe walking alone at night where they live; whether they trust their local police; whether they or a family member have had property stolen in the previous 12 months; and whether they have been assaulted or mugged.
Gallup combines the responses into a score from zero to 100. A higher score reflects a stronger sense of safety, greater trust in the police and less reported exposure to theft and assault.
The global average was 80 points, down three points. Scores ranged from 96 in Tajikistan, which topped the ranking, to 55 in both Liberia and Sierra Leone. Worldwide, 72% of adults said they had confidence in their local police. Meanwhile, 14% said they or a family member had had property stolen during the year, and 9% reported being assaulted or mugged.
The report was based on representative surveys of people aged 15 and older in 140 countries and territories in 2025. In most countries, the surveys included around 1,000 respondents or more. According to Gallup’s methodology, their margins of error ranged from 2.2 to 5.5 percentage points.
Gallup notes that Law and Order Index scores are associated with several development measures, including national income, health, food security and homicide rates. More prosperous countries tend to score higher, while higher homicide rates are generally linked to lower scores.
