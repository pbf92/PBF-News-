Tesla stock trades up, here is why
What happened?
Shares of electric vehicle pioneer Tesla TSLA jumped 11.9% in the afternoon session after CNBC reported that U.S. equities surged following Nvidia's reinstatement of Jensen Huang as CEO, reviving the artificial intelligence trade after the previous week's sell-off.
According to the CNBC report, the Nasdaq Composite soared 6.9% and the S&P 500 soared 5.1%. Nvidia shares soared 31%, while "Magnificent Seven" members Alphabet and Meta Platforms surged 6% and 5%, respectively, and Microsoft rose 4%, the report said. Separately, TipRanks reported that Tesla began releasing version 15 of its Full Self-Driving (FSD) software over the weekend. According to the TipRanks report, Tesla claimed the update improves how the system handles dangerous driving situations by 25 times.
What is the market telling us
Tesla’s shares are quite volatile and have had 16 moves greater than 5% over the last year. But moves this big are rare even for Tesla and indicate this news significantly impacted the market’s perception of the business.
The previous big move we wrote about was 14 days ago when the stock gained 5.3% on the news that Chief Executive Officer Elon Musk confirmed plans to wind down Cybertruck assembly at Gigafactory Texas to accelerate mass production of the autonomous Cybercab robotaxi.
In statements carried by Reuters, Musk posted on social platform X that Tesla will refund customer deposits because orders far outrun remaining production capacity before the Texas line is dismantled in approximately eight months. The pivot follows Musk's announcement that the opportunity cost of the pickup's manufacturing complexity is vastly outweighed by incremental Cybercab volume, CNBC reported.
The advance coincided with a broader market rebound across growth and electric vehicle shares, with Tesla recovering after a 6.0% slide over the preceding week. The strategic reallocation removes an operational drag, with Musk adding in a social media post that "the opportunity cost of the complexity of Cybertruck is vastly outweighed by the value of incremental Cybercab production.".
Tesla is down 11.6% since the beginning of the year, and at $387.42 per share, it is trading 20.9% below its 52-week high of $489.88 from December 2025. Despite the year-to-date decline, investors who bought $1,000 worth of Tesla’s shares 5 years ago would now be looking at an investment worth $1,048.
