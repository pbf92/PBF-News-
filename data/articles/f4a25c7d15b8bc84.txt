TITLE: Long-term international migration, provisional: year ending June 2026
OUTLET: Office for National Statistics

Long-term immigration is where an individual moves to the UK for 12 months or more; long-term emigration is where an individual leaves the UK for 12 months or more. Long- term net migration is long-term immigration minus long-term emigration.
Long-term net migration
Our provisional estimates show long-term international net migration stood at 77,000 in year ending (YE) June 2026 and has more than halved compared with YE June 2025 (updated to 204,000). Excluding the coronavirus (COVID-19) pandemic, when international travel restrictions were in place, this is the lowest recorded net migration estimate since 1997 (when we used surveys to measure migration rather than administrative data).
While people arriving from outside the EU continued to add to the population, non-EU+ provisional net migration fell to 258,000 in YE June 2026, down from 379,000 a year earlier.
The fall in the net migration of non-EU+ nationals was the main reason for the latest decrease in overall net migration. It was driven by non-EU+ immigration falling by more than 100,000, while emigration remained broadly stable. Compared with YE June 2025, non-EU+ arrivals for work-related reasons fell by 45,000, asylum-related reasons fell by 19,000 and study-related reasons fell by 33,000.
Unlike non-EU+ nationals, more British and EU+ nationals left the UK long term than arrived. Provisional net migration for British nationals remained broadly stable at negative 137,000, while EU+ net migration was negative 45,000 and has been negative since YE June 2022.
In our latest figures, because updated data was unavailable, British estimates are based on an extended 15-month projection, which is longer than we would usually use to produce these estimates. This means they are subject to greater uncertainty and revision. More information is available in Section 6: Updates to estimates.
Long-term immigration
- The provisional estimate for total long-term immigration in YE June 2026 is 738,000, a decrease of 17% from the updated YE June 2025 estimate of 893,000; this continues a downward trend in our long-term immigration estimates from the peak at 1,469,000 in YE March 2023.
- Most people immigrating into the UK in YE June 2026 were non-EU+ nationals, provisionally estimated at 550,000, a decline from 671,000 in YE June 2025.
- When looking at the reasons for immigration of non-EU+ nationals in YE June 2026, 47% came for study-related reasons, 25% for work-related reasons, and 13% were for asylum-related reasons.
- The immigration of EU+ and British nationals in YE June 2026 has remained broadly stable, provisionally estimated at 79,000 and 109,000, respectively.
Long-term emigration
- The provisional estimate for total long-term emigration in the most recent period is 661,000.
- Long-term emigration has been increasing since 2022 but was broadly stable with our updated YE June 2025 estimate of 689,000.
- In YE June 2026, 292,000 non-EU+ nationals left the UK, remaining broadly stable compared with the previous year; around 53% of these originally arrived with study-related visas.
- Emigration of EU+ nationals declined to 124,000, down by 15% from the updated YE June 2025 estimate of 145,000.
- In YE June 2026, an estimated 245,000 British nationals left the UK, remaining broadly stable in the last five years.
The latest Registration and Population Interaction Database (RAPID) data were unavailable for this release. As a result, British national estimates are subject to greater uncertainty than usual. Please see Section 6: Updates to estimates.

Updates to estimates
For this release, we have more available and updated Home Office data for EU+ and non-EU+ nationals. This means we get a more complete picture of travel behaviour, which we incorporate into our estimates.
Alongside this, we have also improved how we categorise non-EU+ nationals with dependant joiner visas, allowing them to be more accurately classified as either work or study dependants. This change affects the breakdown by reason for migration but not overall totals.
We are working with the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) to obtain updated Registration and Population Interaction Database (RAPID) data for estimating British national migration. In the meantime, we have projected YE December 2025 estimates from our May 2026 release forward to YE June 2026 using a model informed by timelier EU+ migration data. We will update the estimates in our next release.
The way we measure uncertainty has changed for this release and will apply to all releases from now on, as we have implemented revision-based intervals for non-EU+, EU+ and total estimates. This method illustrates how modelling revisions can provide information to users on the stability of current long-term international migration (LTIM) estimates, through predicting a likely revision range based on the trends and patterns of previous revisions. British national uncertainty remains simulation-based for this release, as they are the most recent group to undergo methodology changes.
More information on these updates is available in Section 6: Updates to estimates.

With these statistics, you can:
- explore our new provisional estimates of net migration, immigration and emigration for the latest two time periods: year ending (YE) March 2026 and YE June 2026
- download the time series for YE June 2012 to June 2026, including standard revisions for YE March 2025 to YE December 2025
- compare historic time series for EU+ and non-EU+ nationals with caution because of changes to methods and updated data from YE June 2021
- use the uncertainty estimates in our accompanying dataset to understand the likely range within which LTIM estimates could be revised
With these statistics, you cannot:
- treat British national migration estimates as directly comparable across all periods; estimates from YE June 2021 are not comparable with those for earlier periods because of substantial methodological changes; estimates for YE March 2026 and YE June 2026 are modelled projections subject to greater uncertainty, so comparisons with earlier periods should be interpreted with caution
- add the most recent estimates to a time series from previous releases; as a result of revisions, the full time series associated with this release should be used
- compare revision-based intervals with previously published uncertainty intervals, as the new revision-based intervals capture more sources of uncertainty beyond the adjustments made to LTIM estimates
- use revision-based intervals to assess the accuracy of LTIM estimates, as they are provided to help users understand the stability of current estimates
- use non-EU family migration estimates alone to inform decisions, because of some ongoing data investigations in this breakdown
The latest figures are provisional and typically remain so for a year, when updated data allow us to replace them with more complete estimates.
More information on the complexity of measuring migration and why we revise provisional data is available in our article, What is international migration and how do we estimate it?
Our estimates have been rounded to the nearest thousand. Most calculations (including percentages) are done on unrounded numbers, so totals may not equal the sum of individual parts.

Net migration is the difference between long-term immigration (people coming to the UK) and emigration (people leaving the UK).
In this statistical article, long-term migration refers to people moving to or from the UK for 12 months or more, in line with the United Nations (UN) definition.

Net migration by nationality
Net migration was provisionally estimated at 77,000 in YE June 2026.
The fall over the year was mainly driven by lower net migration among non-EU+ nationals, which has continued to fall since 2023, when total net migration peaked at 944,000.
Net migration remained negative for British and EU+ nationals, meaning that, in each group, more people left the UK long term than arrived (Figure 3).
The provisional estimates for YE June 2026 show that:
- non-EU+ nationals' net migration was provisionally estimated at 258,000, a decline of 121,000 from the updated YE June 2025 (379,000)
- British nationals' net migration was provisionally estimated at negative 137,000, a fall of 12,000 from the updated YE June 2025 (negative 125,000)
- EU+ nationals' net migration was provisionally estimated at negative 45,000, an increase of 6,000 from the updated YE June 2025 (negative 51,000)
Definitions of British, EU+ and non-EU+ are in Section 7: Definitions.
