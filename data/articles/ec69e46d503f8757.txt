TITLE: LG lets DoubleVerify check its TV home screen ads for fraud
OUTLET: PPC Land

LG Ad Solutions on November 24, 2026 made DoubleVerify's verification available for campaigns that run on the Home Screen of LG smart TVs, the menu that appears when a set is switched on, with independent checks for fraud included. The service is available globally, according to the announcement, and covers Menu ads and "other native IAB standard ad formats" on that screen.
The announcement was distributed through Business Wire at 10:03 AM Eastern Standard Time under a Mountain View, California dateline. Its subtitle carries the headline claim: that LG Ad Solutions is the first TV maker to offer DoubleVerify verification for Home Screen inventory. No prices, start dates for individual campaigns, launch advertisers or fraud rates were published alongside it.
In Short
Advertisers who already pay DoubleVerify to check that their ads reach real people can now use the same checks on ads shown on the LG TV home screen, the menu that appears when the TV is switched on. This matters to brands buying TV advertising because, according to LG, the home screen has until now been outside the independent checks that many of them require for the rest of their media. What changes is that home screen ads can sit inside the same verification reports as the rest of a TV campaign, although the release does not say what it costs, which measurements beyond fraud are included, or how it applies in the markets where Teads sells LG's home screen.
What the two companies announced
The core of the release is one sentence about fraud and invalid traffic, the industry's term for ad impressions that were not seen by a genuine person: views generated by bots, by software pretending to be a TV or by servers in data centers. According to LG Ad Solutions, it "announced the availability of DoubleVerify (DV) verification for campaigns running on its Home Screen inventory, including independent fraud and invalid traffic (IVT) verification."
Verification firms such as DoubleVerify sit between buyer and seller, measure impressions independently and report how much of what was bought was real.
LG Ad Solutions framed the change as closing a gap in home screen advertising. "For many advertisers, independent verification is a standard part of the media buying process," the release states. "Until now, brands could not apply those same verification standards to Home Screen inventory." The integration between the two companies, according to LG Ad Solutions, "removes that barrier, making it easier for advertisers to include Home Screen inventory as part of broader connected TV campaigns."
The "first" claim is narrow. LG Ad Solutions says it is the first OEM - original equipment manufacturer, the trade name for the company that builds the device - to offer DoubleVerify verification for Home Screen inventory. That is a statement about one verification firm on one type of inventory. It is not a claim that no other TV maker offers any outside measurement on its home screen, and PPC Land has not independently tested it.
What the release covers
How the arrangement works, according to the release
The release does not include a technical description or a setup guide. It does describe the arrangement in four parts.
- Who it is for. The service is aimed at "advertisers using DoubleVerify," according to the announcement. These are brands and agencies that already have a contract with DoubleVerify for other media.
- What is applied. Those advertisers can "apply the same verification standards to Home Screen inventory that they already use across their media investments." The checks named in the release are fraud and invalid traffic.
- Which units are covered. The scope is LG's Home Screen inventory, "including Menu and other native IAB standard ad formats."
- Where it runs. The capability is "Available globally," according to LG Ad Solutions. No country list is given.
Missing from that description is the mechanism. The release does not say whether DoubleVerify's code runs inside LG's webOS operating system, whether measurement is triggered through a software kit embedded in the TV, or whether the checks happen before an ad is bought or after it has run.
What the executives said
Kelly McMahon, EVP of Global Operations at LG Ad Solutions, tied the move to outcome measurement. "Advertisers are looking for more than campaign metrics. They want confidence in what's working," McMahon said. "Helping advertisers measure outcomes begins with giving them confidence in every part of their media investment. Expanding independent verification to Home Screen inventory is another step toward making that possible."
Todd Randak, GM of CTV at DoubleVerify, described it as an extension of what buyers already do elsewhere. "As Home Screen advertising continues to grow, advertisers expect the same trusted verification capabilities they use across the rest of their other campaigns," Randak said. "Bringing DoubleVerify media quality measurement to Home Screen inventory helps brands apply consistent verification practices across more of their media buys."
Randak's phrase "media quality measurement" is broader than the release's own list, which names only fraud and invalid traffic. DoubleVerify's media quality products elsewhere include viewability and brand suitability. Whether either applies to LG's Home Screen is not stated.
Why a menu screen needed new standards
The Home Screen is not a commercial break. It is the interface itself: rows of apps, banners and tiles that a viewer scrolls past before choosing what to watch. That makes home screen advertising different from a 30-second spot inside a streaming app, where verification tools have been running for years.
Industry standards have only recently caught up with the format. In December 2025, IAB Tech Lab released CTV ad format standards for public comment covering six formats, among them menu ads, defined as headline banners or tiles that sit within the navigation menus of smart TV home screens. In July 2026, IAB Tech Lab finalized the signals that tell a buying system which format is on offer, and menu and home screen units were assigned to the native object of an OpenRTB bid request rather than the video object used for pause, overlay and screensaver ads. That is the background to the release's reference to "Menu and other native IAB standard ad formats."
Measurement followed two weeks before LG's announcement. On November 10, 2026, IAB Tech Lab extended its Open Measurement SDK to Pause, Menu, Screensaver and Overlay Ads, noting that Menu ads are "also known as Homescreen" ads. The Open Measurement SDK is a shared piece of code that lets verification firms measure impressions without each building its own integration. Randak was quoted in that release too, calling the extension to emerging formats "an important step toward giving advertisers greater transparency into media quality."
LG Ad Solutions' release does not mention the Open Measurement SDK. Whether the DoubleVerify integration relies on it, or on a separate connection built between the two companies, is not disclosed. The timing is close, but PPC Land has no confirmation that one led to the other.
The fraud backdrop
Why would buyers want fraud checks on a screen that LG itself controls? DoubleVerify's own data on connected TV gives part of the answer. In May 2026, DoubleVerify reported that CTV fraud schemes and variants rose 140% in the first quarter of 2026 compared with the same quarter of 2025. The same report put the fraud and invalid traffic violation rate for campaigns with its protection at below 1% in 2025, against 9% for unprotected campaigns. Those are a vendor's figures about the value of its own service, and they cover connected TV broadly, not the LG Home Screen.
Smart TV platforms have had their own problems. In August 2026, Samsung banned residential proxy software from apps on its Tizen TV platform after researchers found such code in a large share of TV apps. The same coverage reported that more than 42% of LG apps in one scan carried proxy code and that LG made a comparable commitment for webOS in the week of July 21, 2026. Proxy software of that kind lets outside traffic appear to come from an ordinary home connection.
There is an important difference, however. Those cases concerned third-party apps installed on TVs. The Home Screen is LG's own interface, and the ads on it are served by LG Ad Solutions. The release gives no figures on how much invalid traffic, if any, has been found on Home Screen placements, so the scale of the problem the service addresses on this specific inventory is unknown.
One screen, many doors
LG's Home Screen has been sold through a growing number of routes in 2026, and each one raises the question of whether the new checks follow the inventory.
The Teads arrangement is the one that sits awkwardly next to the word "globally." When LG and Teads renewed their deal in April, Teads was described as the sole point of access to LG's HomeScreen inventory in markets including France, Germany, Italy, Poland, India, Japan and Singapore. LG Ad Solutions' release does not say whether DoubleVerify checks apply to Home Screen campaigns bought through Teads in those 26 markets, or only to inventory LG Ad Solutions sells itself. Neither the Best Buy nor the Magnite announcements mentioned fraud verification; neither did the release on November 24 say whether those routes are covered.
Teads' attention product is a different kind of measurement. It predicts how much attention a placement receives; it does not count fraudulent impressions. That distinction matters to the "first" claim: outside measurement was already present on some home screen placements before November 24, but not, as far as the announcements show, DoubleVerify's fraud checks.
Where DoubleVerify stands
DoubleVerify is announcing new CTV coverage while its own ownership changes. In August 2026, Nielsen agreed to acquire DoubleVerify for $13.60 a share in cash, an enterprise value of about $2.15 billion, with closing expected in the first quarter of 2027. In its third-quarter results on October 30, DoubleVerify reported revenue of $205.6 million and stopped holding earnings calls until the sale closes; the volume of media transactions it measured on connected TV rose 22% in the quarter, faster than the 11% rise across all measured transactions.
New supply such as the LG Home Screen feeds that CTV volume. It also sits next to a long-running effort by the company to set terms for the streaming market: in December 2025, DoubleVerify urged the streaming industry to adopt transparency standards, including support for the Open Measurement SDK on CTV.
Accreditation is a separate matter. In April 2024, DoubleVerify earned accreditation for CTV viewability from the Media Rating Council, the US body that audits measurement services, covering video viewable impressions in connected TV. Nothing in the November 24 release says that measurement of Home Screen Menu units, which are not video, falls under any accreditation.
Competitors are active in the same space. In April 2026, IAS launched Total TV with show-level data and third-party checks on device type, viewability and invalid traffic, starting with Disney, NBCUniversal, Paramount and Prime Video. That product was aimed at streaming publishers rather than TV makers' own menus.
Samsung and the other TV makers
LG is not the only manufacturer selling its home screen to more buyers. In June 2026, Samsung opened its Smart TV home screens to programmatic buying through The Trade Desk and DV360. PPC Land noted at the time that it was not yet clear whether comparable transparency tools would be available for home screen placements. LG's announcement answers that question for its own sets and for one vendor.
