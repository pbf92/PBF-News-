TITLE: Boolean Method: Search Strategy Guide & Rules
OUTLET: CASRAI

On this page
The Boolean method is an information retrieval framework that uses logical operators—primarily AND, OR, and NOT—to combine keywords and controlled vocabulary into reproducible search strategies for academic literature reviews and evidence syntheses. In scholarly manuscripts, the Boolean method section documents how research questions are deconstructed into conceptual blocks, how synonyms are expanded using OR, how distinct concepts are intersected using AND, and how database-specific syntax is applied across bibliographic platforms to ensure complete methodological transparency and reproducibility.
Purpose of the section
The methodology section of any systematic review, scoping review, meta-analysis, or evidence synthesis manuscript must provide a transparent, auditable account of how studies were identified. Documenting the Boolean search method fulfills the core scientific requirement of reproducibility: an independent researcher, librarian, or peer reviewer should be able to execute the exact search strings in the identical bibliographic databases and retrieve the same record set as of the specified search date.
Formal reporting guidelines—including the PRISMA 2020 statement (Page et al., 2021) and the PRISMA-S literature search extension (Rethlefsen et al., 2021)—mandate that authors present the full, unedited search strategy for at least one major database in the main text or supplementary material. The Boolean method section serves as the narrative bridge between the high-level research question and the mechanical execution lines of database querying. It explains the conceptual logic governing why particular terms were combined, how index terms interact with free-text keywords, and why specific exclusions or limits were enforced.
In addition to reproducibility, this section demonstrates methodological rigor by detailing the deliberate trade-off between search sensitivity and search precision. In evidence syntheses conducted under Cochrane Handbook standards (Lefebvre et al., Chapter 4) or JBI guidance (Aromataris and Munn, 2020), search sensitivity—the probability of identifying all relevant studies—takes priority over precision. Describing the Boolean architecture assures reviewers that the authors did not arbitrarily truncate candidate literature or introduce selection bias at the retrieval stage. Understanding the mechanics of Boolean search operators for literature searching allows researchers to articulate these choices systematically in their final manuscript.
Structure
Structuring the Boolean method section in a scholarly paper requires a logical progression from conceptual formulation to platform-specific syntax execution. Authors should follow a standardized six-step ordered workflow when drafting this section of the manuscript:
- Conceptual deconstruction of the research question: The section must explicitly state the structural framework used to break down the investigation into distinct thematic facets. Authors typically employ established models such as PICO (Population, Intervention, Comparator, Outcome) for clinical inquiries, PEO (Population, Exposure, Outcome) for observational studies, or SPIDER (Sample, Phenomenon of Interest, Design, Evaluation, Research type) for qualitative syntheses. The text should clarify which elements of the framework were translated into search concepts and which were deliberately omitted from the Boolean string to protect search sensitivity (for example, omitting generic outcome terms that are poorly indexed in electronic abstracts).
- Vocabulary harvesting and term expansion: For each retained conceptual facet, the manuscript must detail the dual-track identification of candidate search terms: controlled vocabulary (such as Medical Subject Headings [MeSH] in MEDLINE/PubMed, Emtree terms in Embase, or Thesaurus terms in PsycINFO) and free-text keywords (title, abstract, and author-assigned keywords). Authors should document the tools and sources consulted to assemble these terms, including subject specialist consultation, seminal seed studies, and database indexing trees.
- Intra-block disjunction using the OR operator: Within each separate conceptual block, all identified synonyms, spelling variants, acronyms, lexical derivatives, and controlled vocabulary descriptors must be linked using the Boolean OR operator. This creates a unified conceptual set where the presence of any single term satisfies that conceptual requirement. The manuscript should explain how wildcards, truncations, and proximity delimiters were deployed within this block to capture linguistic variation without retrieving unmanageable noise.
- Inter-block conjunction using the AND operator: Once each conceptual block is fully assembled as a distinct set, the manuscript must show how the separate blocks are intersected using the Boolean AND operator. The resulting query requires retrieved citations to satisfy all linked conceptual dimensions simultaneously. Authors must document the rationale for the number of intersecting sets; while two- or three-set intersections (such as Population AND Intervention) represent standard practice, four- or five-set intersections frequently over-constrain the query and lead to omissions of eligible trials.
- Controlled handling and documentation of the NOT operator: Methodological standards from both Cochrane and JBI strictly advise against the routine application of the NOT operator (and its platform variants, such as AND NOT). If the authors utilized negation—for instance, to remove animal studies or specific publication formats—the Boolean method section must explicitly define the validated filter employed (such as the Cochrane human-studies filter) and justify why negation was safe to execute without inadvertently discarding multi-topic comparative articles.
- Nesting, operator precedence, and database syntax translation: The final structural component documents how parentheses were applied to enforce order of operations, and how the master strategy was adapted across differing database search engines. Because platforms evaluate unparenthesized Boolean operators under conflicting precedence rules, the text must outline the explicit grouping used and reference the platform-specific syntax files deposited in the paper supplementary files. When syntheses incorporate specialized tools, such as the CADIMA evidence synthesis tool, authors should note how Boolean outputs were exported and imported across screening pipelines. The Boolean search method across conceptual blocks
To demonstrate the practical application of this six-step structure, consider the conceptual block structure. Each block acts as an independent logical container. In a review assessing cognitive behavioral therapy for adolescent depression, Block 1 (Population) aggregates all pediatric and adolescent terms linked by OR; Block 2 (Condition) aggregates all depressive disorder terms linked by OR; and Block 3 (Intervention) aggregates psychotherapy terms linked by OR. The master strategy is achieved through the conjunction: (Block 1) AND (Block 2) AND (Block 3). Documenting this block architecture enables readers to evaluate whether missing synonyms compromised retrieval within any individual conceptual strand.

We designed and executed a comprehensive literature search across five electronic bibliographic databases: PubMed (NLM), Embase (Elsevier), Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (Wiley CENTRAL), Web of Science Core Collection (Clarivate), and Scopus (Elsevier). Searches were initially executed on March 12, 2026, and updated on October 15, 2026, with no date or language restrictions applied. The search strategy was developed by the research team in collaboration with an experienced medical information specialist, following the Peer Review of Electronic Search Strategies (PRESS) 2015 Guideline Statement (McGowan et al., 2016).
The research question was conceptualized into three core thematic blocks using the PICO framework: (1) target population (adolescents and young adults), (2) clinical condition (major depressive disorder and persistent depressive disorders), and (3) intervention modality (digital cognitive behavioral therapy). For each concept, we combined platform-specific controlled vocabulary terms with free-text keyword variants identified through pilot searches and examination of relevant landmark trials. Within each conceptual block, all synonyms, truncation stems, and indexed subject headings were joined using the Boolean OR operator. The three individual concept sets were subsequently intersected using the Boolean AND operator. The NOT operator was not employed in any search string to prevent the accidental omission of multi-faceted trials.

Common mistakes
Audits of published systematic reviews frequently uncover critical errors in Boolean search construction that compromise study findings. When drafting the Boolean method section, authors should review and eliminate the following common failure modes:
- Missing parentheses and operator precedence collisions: Bibliographic databases do not process Boolean operators in a uniform sequence. For example, in Clarivate Web of Science, the AND operator takes precedence over OR (evaluating A OR B AND C as A OR (B AND C)), whereas in Elsevier Scopus, OR takes precedence over AND (evaluating A OR B AND C as (A OR B) AND C). Failing to enclose multi-term synonym blocks in explicit parentheses causes the search engine to execute unintended logic, either severely over-constraining the query or flooding the results with millions of irrelevant records. Learning to search Web of Science effectively requires mastering these platform-specific operator hierarchies.
- Misuse of the Boolean NOT operator: Authors often attempt to narrow overly broad results by applying NOT to exclude irrelevant populations or publication types (such as NOT animals, NOT editorial, or NOT review). However, bibliographic records frequently address multiple topics. An article titled “Systematic review of pediatric depression: comparison with animal models” would be entirely eliminated by a simple NOT animals or NOT review exclusion, despite containing vital human trial data. Cochrane guidelines recommend avoiding NOT entirely, or restricting its use to rigorously validated, multi-line methodological exclusion filters.
