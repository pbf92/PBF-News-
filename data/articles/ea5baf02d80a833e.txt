TITLE: Grupo Barak brings Spanish worship to UK as Latin American Christian community grows
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A Christian worship band has said Latin American communities are helping to bring new life to churches across Europe, after selling 1,400 tickets for its first-ever UK concert.
Barak, a Spanish-language worship group from the Dominican Republic, performed at the Royal Regency London on 13 November as part of its La Iglesia 2026 European tour. The event was supported by La Escala Europa and Latitud UK.
The group has more than 2.2 million followers on Instagram and has been nominated twice for a Latin Grammy, in 2017 and 2023, in the Best Christian Album in Spanish category.
Speaking to Premier Gospel's The Latin Selection after the London concert, Barak said it had been particularly encouraged by the number of young people who came to worship.
"We were blown away by the number of people who attended – so many young Latinos with such a deep desire to worship and bless God," the group said.
It also performed in Rotterdam, Genoa and Palermo as part of the European tour.
Barak said they believed the growth of Latin American communities across Europe was helping to strengthen churches.
"We know that Latinos are full of fire – they know how to worship God with such intensity," it said.
"God is using this entire Latino community to come into these cities, plant a seed from God, and keep the fire of God burning throughout Europe."
The comments come as the Latin American population in the UK continues to grow.
The 2021 Census recorded more than 278,000 people born in Latin American countries living in England and Wales, although the wider community is thought to be considerably larger.
A study by Queen Mary University of London, commissioned by the Latin American Women's Rights Service and Trust for London, found that almost 90% of Latin Americans surveyed identified with a religion.
Of those, 63% identified as Roman Catholic and 17% as Evangelical Christians.
The research, published in 2011, also found that almost 70% attended church services, with more than a third going every week.
Barak said it had also seen churches from different denominations coming together during the tour as one Church.
Asked which song best introduces the group, it chose 'Todo va a estar bien' – meaning 'Everything will be alright'. The band said the song could encourage people facing difficult circumstances and waiting for answers to prayer.
Another favourite, 'La tierra canta' ('The earth sings'), explores what happens through worship.
The group added: "When heaven comes down to earth, miracles occur, and extraordinary things take place."
For Barak, however, the impact of its music goes beyond the songs themselves.
The group said seeing Latin Americans carry their faith into new countries was one of the most encouraging parts of the tour.
"They strive to carry the fire they had in their home countries to every new place," it said. "And that brings us great joy, because it is a way of spreading the gospel."
Barak also asked believers to pray for them as they continue their music ministry, that they would remain faithful and find inspiration for new songs.
