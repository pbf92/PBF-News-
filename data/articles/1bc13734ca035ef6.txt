TITLE: 'His fate matters to me': Vilnius priest ready to forgive British tourist who 'smashed Jesus statue'
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A priest from Lithuania believes his congregation is ready to forgive a British tourist who allegedly stole and destroyed its statue of Jesus Christ.
CCTV footage from Vilnius Old Town showed the man staggering while holding the statue up, while another man attempts to film on his phone. The man holding the statue shouts “praise the Lord”, then as he struggles to remain upright, the statue's arms fall off. After it falls and shatters, the pair flee the scene.
The statue was taken from the inner courtyard of the 15th-century Shrine of Divine Mercy Church on the evening of 11 November.
Father Elijas Anatolijus Markauskas told LRT: “Looking at his face, I see he is not an angry person. That man’s fate matters to me. I cannot accept that this will now wreck his life.”
“I would be very open to communication, and if he offered to come to us at the Mercy Sanctuary, we would not hold a trial; we would give him a chance to rehabilitate himself. Not much is needed for rehabilitation – to show initiative, that the person understands he behaved badly, and simply to say “Forgive me” – not even to us, but to the people,”
“I would like no sanctions to be applied to him. Perhaps it sounds strange, but I think he has understood everything,” he added.
Police in Vilnius have served the man a “notice of suspicion”, making him an official suspect and confiscating his passport. Officers have declined to confirm his name.
The British tourist is being investigated for "desecration of a grave or other place of public reverence”, which carries a maximum sentence of three years in prison.
