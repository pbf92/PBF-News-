TITLE: New report: Churches have untapped potential to support NHS health reforms
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A new report from the Good Faith Foundation has said churches have a unique opportunity to offer support to the NHS's new Neighbourhood Health programme.
The Accelerate Wellbeing report is based on the results of a three-year programme supporting five church-led projects focused on improving wellbeing through collaboration with the NHS.
These took a variety of forms, from chaplaincy and a church-run community hub to support services for men and young people.
In March, the Government published a policy paper outlining its framework for Neighbourhood Health. It set out the need for Neighbourhood Health Centres (NHCs) to shift healthcare towards community-based services.
According to the report, these changes provide a strong case for collaboration between the health sector and local churches.
“There’s so much potential within the church to make a really, really big difference in the long run,” Esther Platt, a consultant from the Good Faith Foundation, told Premier Christian News.
“I want churches and Christian organisations to see themselves as a really big player in the health and wellbeing of our country.”
Dr Louise Vaughn from the Whole Person Health Trust told Premier she had seen an increase in demand for chaplaincy through GP referrals.
“The idea of whole person health is that you can’t be fully well unless you’re also spiritually well, just like you can’t be fully well if you’re not psychologically well.”
She said churches can provide a sense of community for those in need.
“For the local church, to be able to recognise they have a role in that, and they are able to provide that for individuals – where that is the right thing for them – it is super important.”
But to have an impact, Platt said there are several factors Christian organisations and churches need to contend with, from a rapidly changing healthcare sector to financing difficulties.
“Particularly over the last four or five years, due to the cost of living, it has been harder for lots of charities and smaller projects to get the support they need because there hasn’t been as much available.”
The Good Faith Foundation supported the projects with mentoring, audits and networking.
