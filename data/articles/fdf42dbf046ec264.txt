TITLE: Jehovah Rapha / The Lord Who Heals
OUTLET: Christianity Today

Imagine you have dropped into the crowded street depicted in Luke 8 and the healing of the hemorrhaging woman. What would your conversation be like with the “One who heals”?
Me: Hey, Rapha. Got a minute?
Jehovah Rapha: Always. What do you need?
Me: This healing thing—it always wrecks me. I just watched you heal the woman who had been bleeding for 12 years. The crowd, the noise, the immediate and physical cessation of pain. And then the word that took my breath away: “daughter.”
Not unclean. Not unwanted. Not outcast. You wanted everyone to hear: “You are mine!”
Jehovah Rapha: I feel like there’s a “but” coming.
Me: It probably is a “but.” I wish it was an “and.”
Jehovah Rapha: And what?
Me: I’ve got questions. I don’t get the waiting thing. Why 12 years of suffering? And all the money spent—resources depleted, cast out from her community. If I’m honest, it really makes me mad. It just doesn’t make any sense. Why did you allow such a long, unending barrage of futility?
Jehovah Rapha: Take a breath … a deep one … now tell me more.
Me: There is just so much pain and suffering around me and in me. I deeply resonate with the futility of those 12 years—she begged for answers, she poured out her resources, only to hit a dead end. Nada! How did that accomplish any good? I don’t understand how you can just sit by for that long? Get up and get in there sooner!
Jehovah Rapha: I hear a lot of ache there. A lot of pain.
Me: Yeah. It is hard to handle it all. I can barely sit still. I just want to get away from it—numb out or distract myself. It feels like this tension will end me.
I don’t get all the waiting, the slowness, and the suffering, and really struggle to also hold the wonder and mystery of healing. I don’t know how to hold the ache and the awe. And to be honest—the ache seems more like a clanging alarm going off in my head.
Jehovah Rapha: You know that ache? That’s me. In you.
That’s you longing and aching for what I long and ache and wait for: wholeness. Restoration. The knitting back together of that which was torn asunder. The desire to see all that is fragmented and broken be made whole again.
It’s such a good part of you that aches and laments and grieves and wrestles and wants something different for this world and for all those who are precious to you. It is actually knitting something back together in you. Thank you for bringing all of your heart to me.
Maybe we should talk some more.
Me: Yeah. There is so much here. Maybe we should.
Jehovah Rapha: You can bring everything, and I do mean everything—you don’t have to edit your language for me.
Me: You knew I was doing that?
Jehovah Rapha: Yeah—most people do.
Me: Where do you want to meet?
Jehovah Rapha: Let’s go back to that place where the ache started. I’ll meet you there … I am already there.
