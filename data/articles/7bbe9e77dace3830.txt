TITLE: Just a THIRD of Britons like 'God Save the King' as bombshell poll reveals apathy to national anthem
OUTLET: GB News

Just a third of Britons have a positive view of God Save the King, according to a bombshell poll which found the British national anthem was the least popular at home among eight nations surveyed.
Only 34 per cent of British citizens like the song, while a further 28 per cent dislike it, the YouGov poll of nearly 4,000 people showed.
People in all seven other countries were more likely to say they enjoyed their own anthem.
In France, 59 per cent are fond of La Marseillaise, while 58 per cent of Americans have an affinity for The Star-Spangled Banner.
Even Spain's Marcha Real, which has no official lyrics, enjoys more support than God Save the King
Some 46 per cent of Spaniards rate their anthem, the second-lowest figure among the nation-states surveyed, while just 16 per cent disregard it.
Royal commentator Richard Fitzwilliams told GB News that judging the anthem on its musical merits missed part of its appeal.
“This ignores the emotions those of us who are patriotic naturally feel when we sing it,” he said.
Mr Fitzwilliams praised “the emotional lilt of La Marseillaise” but argued the British anthem had a power of its own.
“The world knows God Save the King or Queen and what it symbolises,” he said.
“So, despite not competing as music with some other national anthems, it raises our spirits, as no other tune could.”
Graham Smith, of the anti-monarchy group Republic, called God Save the King “a royal anthem that fails to mention the country or the people who live here”.
He told Britain's News Channel he wanted someone to write a new anthem for the UK.
“It can’t be difficult to imagine a UK anthem that we could all sing with pride and gusto. It’s time we found someone to write it,” he said.
LATEST DEVELOPMENTS
Within Britain, dislike of the anthem was strongest in Scotland, where 47 per cent said they disapproved of the song and just 23 per cent said they approved it.
In Wales, 41 per cent said they dismissed the anthem, compared with 27 per cent who appreciated it.
God Save the King fared better in England, where 36 per cent endorsed it and 26 per cent opposed it.
There was also a divide between generations, with 38 per cent of those aged 18 to 24 hating the anthem compared to 25 per cent who cherish it.
Among those aged 65 and over, 41 per cent gave God Save the thumbs up and 22 per cent the thumbs down.
YouGov found 56 per cent of adults in England favoured an official English anthem, including nine per cent who picked God Save the King.
Jerusalem and Land of Hope and Glory were joint favourites on 15 per cent each, while a further 27 per cent said England had no need for a separate anthem.
There were clearer favourites in Scotland and Wales as 51 per cent of Scots chose Flower of Scotland, while 70 per cent of Welsh adults backed Land of My Fathers.
Labour MP Toby Perkins, who brought a bill before the Commons in 2016 calling for England to have its own sporting anthem, said he was sad to hear the poll results.
The Chesterfield MP told the People's Channel he wanted to keep God Save the King as “a unifying song for the four nations” at events such as the Olympics and Davis Cup.
“I want us to be able to celebrate both our Englishness and our Britishness, but also recognise these as separate entities,” he said.
Mr Perkins suggested the public could choose an English anthem from a shortlist in a TV vote, with his own choice being Jerusalem.
“Just imagine the power of 80,000 England fans at Wembley singing Jerusalem together before a big game, helping fire up our players whilst also celebrating English identity and pride,” he said.
YouGov surveyed 3,987 adults in Great Britain online between September 10 and November 11.
