TITLE: Nvidia Stock Looks Like a Screaming Buy Below $160. Here's Why.
OUTLET: The Motley Fool

By Patrick Sanders –
Key Points
- Nvidia stock jumped 31% when the board brought back CEO Jensen Huang, and at least four firms upgraded the shares, with price targets of as much as $270.
- Third-quarter revenue rose 103% to $115.8 billion, gross margin expanded to 74.8%.
- Huang's return doesn't mean all of Nvidia's problems are behind it.
Well, that was quick.
Less than two weeks after Nvidia (NVDA -4.92%) parted ways with Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang, the board of directors brought him back on Nov. 23. Nvidia stock jumped 31% in a single day as investors welcomed the return of the man who built Nvidia into the dominant artificial intelligence (AI) chip company.
If you missed Monday's gains, I wouldn't be too discouraged. Nvidia is trading a little above $150 at this writing, and several Wall Street analysts think there's plenty of money left to be made. That's why I believe that Nvidia is a screaming buy at less than $160.
President Donald Trump lobbied for Nvidia to reinstate Jensen Huang, left. Image source: Getty Images.
Wall Street is warming to Nvidia again
At least four firms upgraded Nvidia stock on Nov. 23. And even the most conservative price target among them represents a healthy gain over Nvidia's closing price of $165.05 that day.
StoneX has a "buy" rating with a $270 price target, while Oppenheimer is close behind with a $250 price target. Melius Research put its target at $220, and Bernstein has an "outperform" rating on Nvidia stock with a $200 target.
Meanwhile, longtime tech bull Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives increased his price target from $225 to $250 but said the bull case for Nvidia is closer to $300.
I wrote last week about Ives' assessment of the tech stock sell-off that's hammered the Nasdaq this month. Ives identified Huang's departure as one of seven reasons for the losses, but the most important part of his argument was that he doesn't see the projected $4 trillion in AI infrastructure spending going away.
That's why I found Nvidia's earnings report last week so encouraging. Even without Huang in the building, management raised its projection for fiscal 2028 revenue growth from 70% to 75%.
Huang's return doesn't mean all of Nvidia's problems are behind it. The board is continuing its investigation, and the company says it will cooperate with government investigators. But investors should remember how much Nvidia's business has improved even as its stock price has fallen.
Nvidia's growth is outstanding
Nvidia generated $115.8 billion in sales in the third quarter of fiscal 2027 (which ended Oct. 25), up 103% from a year ago. Data center revenue increased by 110% to $107.6 billion.
For all the talk about competition from Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -1.97%) and the custom processors that cloud computing companies are developing, Nvidia can still command excellent prices for its graphics processing units (GPUs). Its gross margin was 74.8% in the third quarter, up from 73.4% a year ago.
Nvidia also began shipping its next-generation Vera Rubin computing platform during the quarter. The company expects fourth-quarter revenue of $130 billion -- and that's without factoring in any data center computing revenue from China.
Nvidia won't double its revenue next year, but I have a hard time finding another company of Nvidia's size growing anywhere near this fast.
Nvidia stock is a buy
Even after Monday's whopping gain, Nvidia is still trading more than 37% below its all-time high.
And think about how much Nvidia's earnings have increased. The company generated earnings per share of $2.70 in the third quarter, compared to $1.30 a year ago. At $170 per share, you'd be paying less than 16 times Nvidia's annualized third-quarter earnings.
That's not particularly expensive for a company that's projecting 75% revenue growth next fiscal year.
I don't know how long it will take for investors to regain confidence in Nvidia's board of directors, and there's a real possibility that the investigations will yield more bad news. But I don't think Nvidia needs to resolve all of its regulatory problems to justify a substantially higher stock price based on its earnings growth.
I'd happily buy more Nvidia stock below $160.
