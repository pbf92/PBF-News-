TITLE: CPI | OpenAI Names Cravath Partner to Lead Defense in Musk Antitrust Trial
OUTLET: PYMNTS.com

OpenAI has selected Gary Bornstein, a senior litigation partner at Cravath, Swaine & Moore, to oversee its defense against an antitrust lawsuit brought by companies controlled by Elon Musk, according to Reuters.
The proceedings, scheduled for March 2027 in Fort Worth, Texas, will put competition in the rapidly expanding artificial intelligence industry under renewed legal scrutiny.
Musk’s X Corp. and SpaceX Super Intelligence allege that OpenAI and Apple engaged in anticompetitive practices intended to establish dominance in the generative AI chatbot market. Apple reached a settlement with the plaintiffs in September, though the financial and contractual terms remain confidential, Reuters reported.
The litigation represents another chapter in the increasingly contentious relationship between Musk and OpenAI Chief Executive Officer Sam Altman.
The two entrepreneurs helped establish OpenAI in 2015. Musk resigned from its board in 2018, a year before the organization established a commercial business structure.
The Texas proceedings follow a separate legal setback for Musk in May, when a California federal jury rejected his lawsuit concerning OpenAI’s departure from its original founding objectives, according to Reuters.
Musk’s companies have appointed John Hughes, a partner at Bartlit Beck, to lead their courtroom strategy. Hughes previously represented plaintiffs in litigation challenging Harvard University’s admissions policies that ultimately led to a Supreme Court decision ending race-conscious college admissions.
Bornstein, meanwhile, brings experience in technology-sector competition disputes, having represented Epic Games in its legal battle over Apple’s App Store policies.
OpenAI’s legal team also includes attorneys from Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, while Musk’s companies are receiving additional representation from Axinn Veltrop and Stone Hilton, Reuters reported.
US District Judge Mark Pittman will preside over the Texas trial.
Reuters said OpenAI, Bornstein and Musk’s companies did not immediately respond to its requests for comment.
The case is X Corp. and SpaceXAI v. OpenAI, US District Court for the Northern District of Texas, No. 4:25-cv-00914-P.
