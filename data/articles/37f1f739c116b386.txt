TITLE: Referee being 'drunk' claims are 'entirely unfounded'
OUTLET: BBC Sport

- Published
Claims that a referee was drunk during an Asian Football Confederation (AFC) Champions League Two match are "entirely unfounded", officials say.
Syrian referee Hanna Hattab was replaced at half-time of the match between Iran's Gol Gohar and Al Jazira of the United Arab Emirates.
Reports claimed Gol Gohar staff accused the referee of being drunk following what has been described as some erratic decision-making in the first half.
Iranian news agency ISNA quoted one club official as saying: "The referee was not in a normal state," and suggesting they would not have played on had the referee not been removed.
Accusations of drunkenness were also widely shared on social media.
But Hattab, who is part of the AFC's elite refereeing group, has released a statement denying the allegation, saying he has been defamed and threatening legal action.
The fourth official took over for the second half, with Al Jazira winning the match.
Hattab said he will be "issuing an official statement that clarifies all the details and facts related to what happened".
Omani sports journalist Issa Said, who covers Asian competitions, claimed on social media that what happened to Hattab was "due to a drop in his blood sugar, which caused him to lose concentration and focus".
"He underwent a medical examination after the match and was not intoxicated, contrary to what has been circulating."
In their statement on the match, which was played in Asia's second tier continental competition, the Syrian Football Association backed Hattab.
"Following an assessment of his health, it has been confirmed - contrary to various claims circulating on social media - that the reports regarding his situation are entirely unfounded," they wrote.
"The Committee reiterates its unwavering support for him and is committed to thoroughly investigating all details and circumstances surrounding the incident."
