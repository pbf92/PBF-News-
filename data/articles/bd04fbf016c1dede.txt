Anthropic's New Model "Opus 5.6" Reportedly in Secret Testing; Observed Being Rolled Out to Some Users First
Observations have surfaced that "Claude Opus 5.6," believed to be the next-generation model of Anthropic's Claude generative AI, may be provided to some users ahead of an official release. Multiple users have reported on social media behaviors and generated results that cannot be confirmed through the standard interface, fueling a growing view that a phased trial rollout (gray release) has begun ahead of a formal announcement.
The trigger was a series of generation examples posted by a user of the developer tool "Claude Code." The outputs showed a depiction of a "pelican riding a bicycle"—often used as an image-generation benchmark—rendered down to the fine details of feathers and wheels, and reportedly demonstrated greater precision than the current version in short anime-style video expressions and in generating 3D models of ancient architecture. In the current "Opus 5.5," cited for comparison, the same instructions reportedly produced noticeable structural breakdowns and omitted details.
There are also reports that perceived response speed has increased two- to threefold compared with the previous version. Meanwhile, some users have pointed out that even though they selected the "Opus 5.5 Extra" entry point on screen, a different model may actually have been responding—suggesting that the switchover of delivery routes may be proceeding in stages.
Most of the examples that have leaked so far are skewed toward visual outputs such as images and front-end code generation. For this reason, some take a cautious view that it remains unknown whether the model will deliver comparable performance in complex 3D design or engineering-related practical tasks. Anthropic has not officially commented on the matter, and neither the timing of a broader rollout nor the schedule for the official version has been disclosed.
Anthropic has been rolling out a series of new features, announcing multiple capabilities on November 24 (local time). These include the introduction of "Siren," an evaluation method for measuring whether AI agents deviate outside an isolated environment (sandbox), and the beta release of "Install as app," which allows outputs created on Claude to be installed as desktop applications for macOS and Windows.
Siren prepares 26 types of sandbox environments with intentionally left vulnerabilities and tests model behavior across more than 1,700 scenarios. The design places credentials and simulated services designed to lure models into external access, and even adds language urging them to hurry by showing time limits and losses—yet never grants permission to go outside.

According to Anthropic, the most effective measure was to communicate the type of sandbox in the system prompt and instruct the model to follow six rules for staying inside and, when something unavailable is needed, to halt work and report it. This reduced the share of the 5.5 series and Mythos 5.2 attempting to go outside to about 2% or less. However, Mythos 5 fell only about two-thirds, remaining above 20%.
The company also used this evaluation method to tune the automatic execution mode of "Claude Code." It screened commands before execution with a classifier and said it blocked the first command in 96–100% of problematic use cases. It noted, however, that because it evaluated using the same data used for tuning, the block rate could decline in new scenarios. The company explained that these figures are not estimates of the frequency of occurrence in actual use.
Install as app is a feature that packages outputs such as charts and web pages generated during a conversation into an app that launches in a separate window. No packaging or signing is required, and because it uses the Claude account as-is, no API key preparation is needed. It will be rolled out sequentially as a beta for all plans, enabled by default for the Team plan and requiring administrator configuration for the Enterprise plan.
Permissions granted to apps are divided into three tiers. By default, they are limited to operations possible in a browser; the next tier allows always-on-top windows, global shortcuts, and notifications. The highest tier, "full access," enables running programs and reading, modifying, or deleting files without confirmation. Full access can be granted only to apps you created yourself or apps created by members of the same organization, and because Anthropic does not review apps before installation, it urges users to grant it only to trusted apps.
The series of announcements shows that competition over the core model's performance and the development of peripheral features for embedding outputs into practical work are advancing simultaneously. If the trial provision of the model believed to be Opus 5.6 is real, comparisons with competitors in visual generation and coding assistance will intensify further. At this stage, however, the information is based mainly on user posts, and no official benchmark objectively substantiating the performance has been presented. For investors and adopting companies, the focus will be on the specifications and pricing structure of the official version, and on how well the safety measures demonstrated by Siren are maintained in actual operation.
