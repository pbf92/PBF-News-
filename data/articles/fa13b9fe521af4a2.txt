Just days after its release, Grand Theft Auto 6 has become a vehicle for political misinformation. Viral footage falsely claims the game features a mission recreating Charlie Kirk’s assassination, while pro-Russian accounts are sharing fake screenshots containing anti-Ukrainian messages. Meanwhile, Russia’s own Defence Ministry has exploited the game's popularity to promote military recruitment. Vedika Bahl explains how GTA 6 is being used to push real political agendas in Truth or Fake.
After years of anticipation, Grand Theft Auto 6 (GTA 6) finally hit the shelves on November 19. Almost immediately, social media users began sharing footage of supposedly controversial scenes and missions incorporated into the new video game.
Known for its violent gameplay, satire of American society and deliberately provocative content, the franchise has become fertile ground for political misinformation.
Fake Charlie Kirk assassination mission goes viral
On the day of the game's release, a video began circulating on X claiming that developer Rockstar Games had included a mission recreating the assassination of American conservative activist Charlie Kirk, who was killed in September 2025.
One post amassed nearly a million views, with several social media users expressing outrage over the developers' alleged decision to incorporate Kirk's killing into the storyline.
However, the footage has nothing to do with GTA 6. Fact-checkers at NewsGuard traced the video back to GTA Online, the multiplayer component of the franchise's previous instalment, GTA 5, where players can design and publish their own missions.
Rockstar Games did not create the mission. In fact, according to reporting by entertainment magazine Variety earlier this year, the company removed user-created missions recreating Kirk's assassination and added his name to a list of prohibited terms.
While the user-made footage is genuine gameplay, it has been shared out of context to capitalise on the popularity of the new release.
Fabricated screenshots push anti-Ukrainian narratives
Beyond American politics, GTA 6's release is also being exploited by pro-Russian accounts to spread anti-Ukrainian misinformation.
Several Telegram channels have circulated an image supposedly showing graffiti depicting Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky alongside the word "WANTED". Users claimed the graffiti appeared specifically in the Polish-language version of GTA 6.
While the game supports Polish, changing the language settings does not alter its virtual environment. Verified gameplay footage of the same location shows the "WANTED" graffiti without Zelensky's face, indicating that the image was digitally manipulated.
Another fabricated screenshot supposedly shows an in-game television news broadcast featuring a map depicting Ukraine as part of Russian territory.
As reported by Ukrainian news agency Ukrinform, the image contains SynthID, an invisible digital watermark embedded in content generated or edited using Google's artificial intelligence tools.
These types of fabrications appear aimed at exploiting the game's global popularity to imply that anti-Ukrainian narratives are culturally accepted in the mainstream.
Russia uses GTA 6 imagery to recruit soldiers
Meanwhile, Russia's own Defence Ministry has capitalised on the excitement surrounding the game's release to promote military recruitment.
On November 19, the day of the game's release, the ministry published a recruitment poster on Telegram imitating GTA 6's distinctive visual style, encouraging Russians to sign up for military service to fight in Ukraine.
Accompanied by the slogan "Choose your own mission", the advertisement borrows from the language and aesthetics of the video game.
Unlike the fabricated gameplay screenshots circulating online, the recruitment poster was genuinely published by the Russian Defence Ministry, although the artwork itself appears to have been AI-generated.
