TITLE: 'Failing' Rochdale high school where children 'do not feel safe' put into special measures
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

A secondary school in Rochdale where 'harmful behaviours' like racism, sexism and homophobia 'have become normalised' has been put into special measures by Ofsted.
The school inspector found that St Cuthbert's RC High School, on Shaw Road, had failed to adequately safeguard its pupils, who said they "do not feel safe".
Ofsted concluded that there was a culture in the school, which is part of the St Teresa of Calcutta Catholic Academy Trust, "that is not sufficiently safe or respectful and where pupils are not consistently protected from harm".

All six areas of the inspection were marked as requiring 'urgent improvement': achievement, attendance and behaviour, curriculum and teaching, inclusion, leadership and governance, and personal development and wellbeing.
The school leadership has now written to parents, insisting that it is "committed to addressing" the concerns.
The Ofsted report states: "His Majesty’s Chief Inspector is of the opinion that this school requires special measures because it is failing to provide an acceptable standard of education, and the persons responsible for leading, managing or governing the school are not demonstrating the capacity to secure the necessary improvement in the school."
It adds: "Leaders have not established a culture in which staff consistently challenge discrimination, including racism, sexism and homophobia. As a result, these harmful behaviours have become normalised."
The report says: "Pupils experience discriminatory language in daily interactions, and this goes unaddressed by staff. Pupils report feeling unsafe because prejudicial language and conduct are not routinely challenged. They lack confidence that staff will protect them."
It goes on to say: "Pupils do not feel safe in school. They are not proud to attend. Pupils do not feel a sense of belonging to the school community."
Pupils' behaviour on the corridors was "unruly and chaotic", with fights at break times putting them 'at risk of harm'.
The children "do not consistently treat others with respect. Derogatory and discriminatory language, including racist and sexist terminology, is often not challenged by staff," the report states. "Pupils are used to being treated disrespectfully by their peers. Staff do not feel equipped or empowered to tackle this behaviour. Bullying is not dealt with effectively. Pupils lack confidence that staff will protect them or respond appropriately when issues arise."
The inspector says that while school has recently made it easier and clearer to report when students hurt each other, "child-on-child incidents occur too often. Staff, parents and carers are concerned about the regularity and visibility of these behaviours."
The report also found that students arrived at the school struggling with basic reading, writing, and math, but leaders lacked a clear plan to help them catch up, which slowed down their overall learning. This also made it harder for students with special educational needs or disabilities to make steady progress.
Attendance was deemed consistently low, with little sign of improvement over time. "Teachers lack the expertise to deliver the curriculum appropriately," the report states.
School staff went on strike for a significant period last year, due to the frequent changes in leadership and not having a permanent headteacher. Many parents and carers also reported feeling unhappy.
The school currently has 1,023 pupils on the register.
In a letter to parents and carers, dated November 18, John McAuley – chair of the board of directors – and Chris Foley – Catholic senior executive leader – said the result of the recent inspection was "disappointing".
It says: "We acknowledge and understand that you will be deeply concerned or frustrated by this outcome and want to reassure you that our dedication to the children and school community is unwavering.
"The report identifies areas where the school needs to urgently improve, including behaviour, attendance, safeguarding, inclusion, curriculum, teaching, leadership and wellbeing.
"We want to be clear that we accept the findings and are committed to addressing them. While the Trust had already recognised many of these challenges and had begun implementing improvement measures before the inspection, progress has not been fast enough, nor had the impact that children deserve.

"Over the coming months the school will receive regular monitoring inspections by Ofsted, which we will share with you at the point of publication"
The letter to parents adds that 'the focus is now is on delivering the rapid and sustained change that pupils need'.
The percentage of St Cutherbt's pupils who achieved grade 5 or above in English and maths GCSE in 2024/25 was 29% - below the national average of 45.4%.
The percentage of pupils missing 10% or more of their possible mornings and afternoons was 32.7% in 2024/25 - above the national average of 23.4%.
