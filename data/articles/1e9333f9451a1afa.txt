IVIG Improves Outcomes in Newly Diagnosed Inflammatory Myopathies: Study
Netherlands: A new study found that adding intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) to prednisone significantly improved disease activity in patients with newly diagnosed idiopathic inflammatory myopathies, including dermatomyositis, compared with prednisone alone. The double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial, conducted in the Netherlands among 42 patients, supports IVIG as a potential adjunct to corticosteroid therapy. However, the increased risk of thromboembolic events warrants careful consideration.
- At 12 weeks, the mean TIS was 60.0 in the IVIG group compared with 42.5 among those receiving placebo, indicating significantly greater overall improvement with adjunctive IVIG.
- Moderate improvement was observed in 91% of patients receiving IVIG compared with 53% in the placebo group.
- Major improvement occurred in 70% of patients in the IVIG group versus 26% receiving placebo.
- Patients treated with IVIG reached a moderate response sooner, with a median time of 4 weeks compared with 12 weeks for placebo.
- One asymptomatic case of deep venous thrombosis was detected among patients receiving IVIG.
