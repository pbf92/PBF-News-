TITLE: Christian social worker wins Court of Appeal case over withdrawn job offer
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A Christian social worker has won a major Court of Appeal case against a mental health charity that withdrew his job offer over his views on same-sex marriage.
A majority of judges ruled that Touchstone Leeds directly discriminated against Felix Ngole because of his religious beliefs.
Mr Ngole was offered the role of mental health support worker in 2022, subject to references. But Touchstone, which describes itself as an ally of the LGBT+ community and says it's committed to diversity and inclusion, withdrew the offer after finding news coverage of his earlier case against Sheffield University and after a second interview. That coverage reported Facebook posts in which he said homosexuality was a sin.
The charity said it had concerns over the safeguarding of its service users if Mr Ngole were to take up the role.
The Christian Legal Centre, which supported the 48-year-old, says Touchstone's director of people told an employment tribunal that his views put the lives of staff and service users at risk.
The Court ruled in favour of Mr Ngole on all grounds in a majority decision, finding that Touchstone Leeds committed unlawful direct discrimination by refusing to employ him over his publicly expressed Christian beliefs. The Court also refused the charity permission to appeal to the Supreme Court.
Speaking to Premier News, Mr Ngole said: "I've worked as a social worker. I've worked as a teacher. I've worked as a housing officer. I've never had a single complaint against me. I've worked with trans people, gay people. An employer is saying that because of your belief, people are going to die. That's quite a scary thing to hear."
It is his second Court of Appeal win. In 2019, the Court of Appeal ruled in favour of Mr Ngole against Sheffield University and that it acted unlawfully, breaching his right to freedom of expression when it expelled him from his social work course over Facebook comments expressing traditional Christian views on marriage and sexuality.
In total, he has spent nearly a decade in and out of the courts: "It's been tough. My grandchildren were born and I didn't see them. I would have loved to be there, because I had to work far away from home. I couldn't get any job."
He also said the experience had led to direct threats against him: "We had a time when we were threatened. But, I think if you were to ask me again, I would say it's worth it. Justice is worth it."
Andrea Williams, chief executive of the Christian Legal Centre, which has supported him in both cases, said: "This is an absolutely huge victory. I don't think we can overstate how important this legal victory is for Christians in this country and indeed across the world, when it comes to the freedom of Christians to express their faith."
