TITLE: Labour's Rosebank rebellion inspires clergy protest
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Three members of the clergy have painted an oil rig and the words "Stop Rosebank" on the pavement outside the Treasury building in Westminster in protest at a giant oilfield in the North Sea.
Church of England priests Rev Hilary Bond and Rev Jonathan Herbert, alongside retired Baptist minister Rev James Grote, were inspired by a private letter written by at least 100 MPs to Prime Minister Andy Burnham urging him to oppose the oilfield off the north coast of Scotland.
Rosebank's projected lifetime emissions make it incompatible with the UK's legally binding climate commitments.
"Rosebank will do nothing to reduce our energy bills or improve our energy security. But one thing it will do – is crash through UK climate goals and increase human suffering on our planet," Rev Bond said.
"As a Christian minister, called to care for people, I have to speak out. The primary role of government is to keep people safe and the country secure... People have lost homes, livelihood and even their lives. The burning of oil from Rosebank will only add to this emergency," Rev Grote added.
Christian Climate Action sent a letter of its own to Andy Burnham, saying: "This opposition exists not least because of the scientific evidence that dictates that we must stop burning new oil and gas, but also because of the care that we are called to show for others, particularly when the alternative renewables-based pathway is so clearly available to us."
A government decision on Rosebank is expected in the coming weeks.
