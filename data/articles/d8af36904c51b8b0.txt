TITLE: King Charles says Christian faith is the compass guiding his reign
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

King Charles has spoken of how his Christian faith has guided him throughout his life and reign, describing it as a source of strength and direction as he officially inaugurated the Church of England’s new General Synod.
Addressing members at Church House in London on Tuesday, the King said his faith had “developed and deepened” over the decades, providing “the sustenance which nurtures and the compass which guides my every act as Sovereign”.
He added: “I was baptised and confirmed into this faith which has developed and deepened across the decades of my life, to this day.”
It' is the first time the King has presided over a General Synod inauguration. He took the opportunity to pay tribute to his late mother, Queen Elizabeth II, who attended ten openings of previous General Synods.
He said he was following in her footsteps, adding that he shared her commitment to the life of the Church.
"As her successor, I share with all my heart and all my strength a humble commitment to the life of the Church, rooted in a deep and abiding Christian faith, following the teaching of Our Lord Jesus Christ," he added.
Turning to Christians facing persecution, violence and displacement around the world, the King urged Synod members to remember them in their prayers.
He paid particular tribute to ancient Christian communities in the Middle East and Holy Land, whose faith has endured despite centuries of challenges.
Their perseverance, he said, reminds us of “the enduring power of faith, hope and love”.
“Our brothers and sisters in Christ are not, and never shall be, far from our hearts or our minds,” he added.
The King also called for stronger relationships between Christian traditions, reflecting on his recent meetings with Pope Leo XIV in Rome and Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew in Scotland.
He said closer ties between the Anglican, Roman Catholic and Orthodox traditions could help bring Christ's message of salvation to the world.
“I trust that you will continue to work to deepen relations across all Christian communities, both in England and around the world,” he said.
The address marked the start of a new five-year term for the General Synod, the first presided over by Archbishop of Canterbury, Most Rev Sarah Mullally.
The King encouraged members to approach their discussions with empathy and a spirit of fellowship, particularly when faced with disagreement.
He said he hoped their deliberations would be “imbued with the love of God” and that they would be able to put themselves in the shoes of those with whom they disagreed.
Over the next five years, the church's legislative body will debate some of the key issues facing the Church, including safeguarding, church finances, clergy wellbeing and the challenges of maintaining its historic buildings.
