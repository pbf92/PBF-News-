TITLE: Converted product category
OUTLET: PPC Land

Converted product category is a dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that labels each conversion row with the catalogue category of the product the shopper actually bought, rather than the product the advertiser promoted. The distinction exists because Amazon credits advertising with more than the advertised item. A shopper who clicks an ad for a coffee grinder and buys a bag of beans from the same brand generates a sale that Amazon attributes to the grinder campaign. Advertised product category describes the grinder. Converted product category describes the beans. Together, the two fields show where advertising-driven demand lands across a catalogue.
Where the field sits in the unified report
Amazon's unified reporting dimensions page, a Support Center reference updated on November 11, 2026, lists 156 attributes grouped into 11 categories. PPC Land's count of that page found that the Product category holds 18 attributes, of which DVA+ carries 17, Sponsored Products 18, Sponsored Brands 9 and Sponsored Display 17. DVA+ is Amazon's display, video and audio buying, sold as Amazon DSP (demand-side platform) until September 29, 2026.
The Product category splits into two families. Nine Advertised product fields describe what was promoted: ID, name, parent ID, brand, category, subcategory, product group, marketplace and SKU (stock keeping unit). Eight Converted product fields describe what was purchased: ID, name, parent ID, brand, category, subcategory, product group and marketplace. A final field, Product relevance, connects them.
The two families do not cover the same ad products. According to PPC Land's reading of the reference, the advertised product fields apply to DVA+, Sponsored Display and Sponsored Products, but not to Sponsored Brands or Sponsored TV. The converted product fields apply to all five. Converted product category is therefore one of the few product descriptors available on Sponsored Brands rows, where the creative often promotes a store or a collection rather than a single item. Where a dimension does not apply, Amazon's documentation says its value "appears as null."
How the category gets onto a row
Converted product category is a lookup, not a measurement. The sequence runs roughly as follows.
Step one is the ad event. A shopper sees or clicks an ad. For sponsored ads the credited interaction is normally a click. For DVA+ it can be an impression or a click, under a last-touch model that Amazon's DSP reporting guide, dated August 2022, described as having a "14-day lookback window."
Step two is the purchase. Within the attribution window the shopper buys one or more items. Each purchased item has an ASIN (Amazon Standard Identification Number), the identifier Amazon assigns to every catalogue product. That ASIN becomes the Converted product ID. PPC Land notes that no unified field name contains the word "ASIN"; Converted product ID replaces what DVA+ reports called "ASIN" and Sponsored Products reports called "Purchased ASIN."
Step three is the catalogue join. Amazon reads the purchased ASIN's catalogue attributes and writes them into the other converted fields: item name, parent ASIN (the family that groups size and colour variations), brand, product group, category and subcategory. PPC Land reports that the converted fields "mostly inherit DVA+ names such as 'Item name,' 'Parent ASIN' and 'Brand name.'" The legacy DSP product report exposed Product group, Product category and Product subcategory as separate columns, according to data connector Funnel's field list, which suggests those columns are the direct predecessors. Amazon's reference gives no former name for Converted product category itself.
Step four is classification. Product relevance then records whether the converted product was "directly promoted in the campaign or attributed based on relevance to promoted products (halo)." In DVA+ reports this was called ASIN conversion type, with values Promoted and Brand halo.
A worked example shows the output. A kitchenware brand runs a Sponsored Products campaign advertising a stand mixer. In a week the campaign is credited with 40 orders. Grouped by Converted product category, 28 orders sit in the mixer's own category, while 12 fall in an adjacent category for baking tins sold by the same brand. Advertised product category reads the same on all 40 rows; the converted field is what reveals the 30% of orders that left the mixer's category.
Category, subcategory and product group
Amazon's catalogue does not use a single tree. The browse tree of numbered nodes that shoppers navigate supplies the Benchmark browse category ID and Benchmark browse category level fields in the same report. Product group, category and subcategory come from a separate internal classification attached to each ASIN.
As of November 2026, Amazon has not published how many categories exist in this classification, how they map to browse nodes, or whether the labels are the same in every marketplace. As a result, Converted product category and Benchmark browse category path can describe the same product with different labels.
Origin and evolution
Reporting on purchases other than the advertised product began separately on each side of Amazon Ads.
On the DSP side, brand halo attribution has been part of Amazon DSP reporting for years. The 2022 reporting guide stated that "brand halo expands attribution to all ASINs that have the same brand name(s) as the ASINs tracked by the order," with brand defined as "the name in the byline." A Product dimension in custom reports listed each purchased ASIN with its brand, parent ASIN and conversion type.
On the sponsored ads side, the purchased product report for Sponsored Products lists "non-advertised ASIN purchased by the customer attributable to your campaign," with halo sales counted within seven days of a click. Amazon renamed the columns "Sales (halo)" and "Units sold (halo)," replacing "7 day other SKU sales." Sponsored Brands gained an Attributed Purchases report giving ASIN-level detail, which Amazon extended to book publishers on June 6, 2023, noting that "book products don't have brand halo expansion."
Category-level views followed. On April 14, 2026, Amazon announced that Omnichannel Metrics (OCM), its US measurement product for Amazon DSP, would show sales across "the most purchased product categories" at study and campaign level.
Unified reporting brought these threads into one schema. Amazon opened unified reporting in beta on November 11, 2025 and took it to general availability on June 8, 2026, when unified reporting exited beta. The template gallery at launch included a Converted Product template. The November 11, 2026 reference fixed the eight converted fields in their current form, with all five ad product markers.
Why it matters
For advertisers, the field answers a question that ASIN-level halo reports answered only slowly: which parts of the catalogue are advertising actually selling? Grouped by category, the same data shows whether a campaign for a flagship product is lifting accessories, consumables or unrelated lines.
It also bears on budget and margin. Categories carry different referral fees and margins, so a dollar of halo sales in a low-margin category is worth less than one in the promoted category. Return on ad spend (ROAS) calculated on total attributed sales hides that mix. Splitting sales by converted category makes it visible.
For agencies, the timing is set by Amazon. Legacy report pages become read-only on December 17, 2026, and unmigrated reports will be lost on January 31, 2027. Scripts built on the DSP Product category column or on the Sponsored Products purchased product report have to be remapped to the unified names before then.
Limitations and disputes
The definition is thin. Amazon's reference does not publish the category list, the source of the classification or the date at which an ASIN's category is read. If a product is recategorised, it is not documented whether historical rows change.
Halo inflates the picture. Converted products include sales Amazon attributes on brand relevance, not on the ad itself. Amazon's own Sponsored Products documentation warns that the purchased product report "only shows conversion metrics... for non-advertised products, so the conversion metrics' totals won't match the campaign totals." Category totals built from converted rows can therefore be read as incremental demand when they partly reflect purchases that would have happened anyway.
Windows differ by product. Sponsored Products halo columns use seven days from a click. The DSP guide describes a 14-day window including views. A single converted category report mixing ad products blends two attribution rules.
Sponsored TV is unaddressed. PPC Land's count gives converted fields five markers, yet the reference also states that Sponsored TV Smart mode is not supported yet.
Commonly confused terms
Converted product category vs Advertised product category. Advertised product category describes the item promoted; the converted field describes the item bought. They match on promoted conversions and can differ on halo conversions.
Converted product category vs Benchmark browse category. The benchmark fields name the browse node that sets a campaign's peer group for benchmarks. They describe the comparison group, not any purchased product.
Converted product category vs Converted product group and subcategory. Product group is a broader catalogue grouping and subcategory a narrower one. The three are separate columns describing the same purchased ASIN at different depths.
Converted product vs converted currency. Converted currency, in the same reference, names the currency in which money metrics are displayed. The word "converted" there refers to exchange rates, not purchases.
Recent developments
Amazon updated the dimensions reference on November 11, 2026, the page behind PPC Land's count of 156 unified report dimensions, which placed the converted product fields on all five ad products and renamed ASIN conversion type as Product relevance. On November 17, 2026, Amazon revised its data availability page, and PPC Land reported that daily unified reports are capped at 120 days per pull from 24 months of history, with monthly data reaching back 72 months.
