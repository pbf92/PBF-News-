TITLE: Ad platforms get their own line in Google Analytics budget plans
OUTLET: PPC Land

Google Analytics on November 19, 2026, said its cross-channel budgeting tool now supports source platform dimensions, so projection and scenario plans can set individual ad platforms side by side instead of folding spend into broad channel groups. The tool has been in beta since January, still changes nothing in connected ad accounts, and remains unavailable to some properties, while its Meridian-based model is limited to an alpha for select Analytics 360 customers.
In Short
Google Analytics' budget planner can now show spend, conversions and recommended shifts for each ad platform, not only for broad groups such as paid search or paid social. That matters to advertisers who import cost data from several platforms and want one model to suggest where money should move between them, and the model doing the suggesting belongs to Google, one of the platforms being compared. Nothing changes automatically: the plans are estimates, budgets still have to be moved inside each ad account, and the version built on Google's attribution data needs at least a year of cost history from two or more channels.
What Google Analytics posted
The change surfaced in a post on the Google Analytics LinkedIn page, which had 599,310 followers, on the afternoon of November 19 in Europe. "You asked for deeper ad spend visibility, so we expanded the view," the post opened. "Cross-channel budgeting in Google Analytics now supports source platform dimensions."
Three claims followed. According to Google Analytics, the update lets advertisers "Move past broad channel groups to evaluate individual ad platforms side by side," "Track pacing and shift budget to your highest-return platforms in real time," and "Model future scenarios with response curves backed by data-driven attribution." The image in the post carried a "What's new" label and the name Source platform budgeting, with the line "Evaluate and optimize spend across individual ad platforms."
A pinned comment from the same account pointed to the Help Center article "About cross-channel budgeting." That page is undated. As of November 20, Google's "What's new in Google Analytics" page had no entry for the change; its most recent item, dated November 6, covers Data Manager.
The phrase "in real time" sits awkwardly next to the documentation. The help page describes pacing as a weekly exercise: one listed benefit is to "Track the pacing of your spend and conversions weekly to make quick, in-quarter adjustments based on overperformance or underperformance." Shifting budget is also not something the tool does. "These tools are for planning purposes only and won't affect budgets or spend in your connected accounts," according to the page.
From channel groups to platforms
Until now, the budgeting reports worked at the level of channels. The help page gives the new granularity a single sentence, placed in the section on scenario plan recommendations: "In order to improve the ease of use and actionability of your budgeting plans, we are launching new source grouping dimensions, source platform dimensions, and (alpha-only) source platform x campaign type dimensions." The page continues: "With these changes, you will be able to better analyze and optimize performance and standardize reporting for both Google and other platforms."
The groundwork was laid in June. Google added a Source Group field on June 11, 2026, and reclassified the existing Source Platform values so that variants such as "facebook", "fb" and "Meta-facebook" collapse into one value. Source Group is a separate dimension from Source Platform: the same traffic can appear as "Meta Ads" at the platform level and "Instagram" at the group level. Google said at the time that the update would improve budget analysis and make cross-channel reporting in the Advertising section simpler.
In the interface Google Analytics showed alongside the post, a scenario view plots a response curve against ads cost, marked "Plan budget: $118K", and a table breaks results out by "Source platform", with the platform names obscured. The curve flattens as spend rises, the diminishing-returns shape that mix models commonly describe with Hill curves. Three headline metrics sit above the chart: "Ads cost", "All conversions (by conv. time)" and "Cost per all conversions (by conv. time)". The figures are illustrative, and rows beneath the total split each one into "Optimized" and "Projected" values. The total of $117,846.62, in line with the plan budget, compares with $84,231.67; conversions rise 17.36%, from 3,984.81 to 4,676.69, while cost per conversion rises 19.21%, from $21.14 to $25.20. In the illustration, more spend buys more conversions at a higher cost per conversion, which is consistent with a target to "drive the most number of conversions possible with my defined budget" rather than to minimize cost per conversion.
Two plans, two jobs
Cross-channel budgeting splits into two plan types, both reached through the Budgeting menu of the Advertising section, which according to Google "is only available on the desktop version of Google Analytics."
Projection plans, according to the help page, answer questions such as "Am I on track to spend my plan budget?" and "Which channels are overperforming or underperforming?" Recommendations at the top of the chart highlight "the most and least efficient channels based on expected performance." Wide confidence intervals carry a meaning of their own: when performance shifts in ways "that aren't explained by seasonality or holiday effects," the bands may widen, which "indicates that performance patterns have changed, and the model may need additional data to adjust."
Scenario plans take the opposite direction in time. Google describes them as a way to "understand return on investment (ROI) at different budget levels and create future media plans that drive the highest return based on your defined budget." The response curve can be explored interactively, and a projected performance view shows "how your plan will perform if no optimizations are made." Offline data enters the model when offline imports have been set up.
How the help page describes setup
According to Google, viewing the reports first requires the Advertising section to be configured, "including linking to Google Ads and setting up conversions." The route to the reports has three steps: sign in, select Advertising from the left menu, and click a report name in the Budgeting drop-down menu.
Projection plan, eight steps
- Sign in to Google Analytics.
- Select Advertising from the left menu.
- Under "Budgeting", click Projection.
- Click Create plan.
- Enter a plan name, for example "My Q4 plan".
- Enter a planning period, for example "Q4'25".
- Under target KPI, select Budget ("To spend the entire budget during the plan period"), Conversions or Revenue.
- Enter a target value for the selected KPI.
Scenario plan, nine steps
- Sign in to Google Analytics.
- Select Advertising from the left menu.
- Under "Budgeting", click Scenarios.
- Click Create plan.
- Enter a plan name, for example "My Q4 plan".
- Enter a planning period, for example "Q4'25".
- Under target KPI, select Conversions ("To drive the most number of conversions possible with my defined budget") or Revenue.
- Select a budget plan estimate.
- Select a conversion, from a list limited by "the amount of historical data available."
The sample planning period in both procedures is still "Q4'25", a quarter that ended almost eleven months before the November update.
Rules written into the documentation
Several sentences on the page define what the plans can and cannot do. On freezing: "If you check your plan during or after its date range, the response curve will remain the same as when it was first created. The curve reflects market conditions at the time of creation and doesn't update automatically unless you actively modify any of the plan settings such as budget, date range, target metrics." The next paragraph appears to narrow that: "To check the updated response curve and projections, create a new plan." Recommendations are described as "static estimates tied to the plan parameters at the time of creation."
On data scope, a note states: "The budgeting models consider all click-through-conversions, YouTube engaged view conversions and all cost data. Include impressions in your data import when available to prepare for future updates." Impressions, in other words, are not yet an input. On guarantees, both plan types carry the same caveat: projection outputs "shouldn't be treated as guarantees of performance," and scenario outputs are "estimates based on our model, and not guarantees of performance."
A year, two channels, or two years
One reply under the post asked the practical question. "is a full year of imported ad spend data still a prerequisite for this functionality?" wrote Ross (Uroš) Djunisijevic, an analytics specialist at Littledata.
Google's model documentation answers it, and the answer depends on the model. The help page says plans are powered by "either a DDA-based model or Google's open-source Meridian model," depending on whether a property runs standard Google Analytics or Analytics 360.
The table also qualifies the third claim in the LinkedIn post. Response curves "backed by data-driven attribution" describe the beta DDA-based model. Google's Meridian page says that model does not use attribution as an input. Meridian is a marketing mix model, which works from aggregated spend and outcome data. According to Google's DDA-based budgeting page, app conversions are not yet supported and key events are not supported. A September 29, 2026 entry on Google's What's new page put it more broadly: cross-channel budgeting features support web conversions only.
Where the non-Google data comes from
The two-channel threshold makes imported cost data the gate. The Budgeting overview page, according to the help page, shows recently created or modified plans, "Product links" to Google products such as Google Ads and Display & Video 360, and a "Cost import" panel listing "Usage, date of last upload, and file match rate," described as the way to "upload campaign data for non-Google campaigns and channels." A fourth panel, on manual third-party integrations, currently shows only static text. "In the future, this section will dynamically show which platforms have manual integrations available for budgeting models," the page says.
Google has spent more than a year widening that pipe. It added nine data sources for cost data import in June 2025, including BigQuery, Snowflake and Amazon S3, then shipped native connectors for Pinterest and Meta and TikTok in autumn 2025, each attempting to backfill at least 24 months of history. In November 2025 the feature was renamed campaign data import. According to Google's What's new page, a currency field became mandatory for cost uploads on July 28, 2026, and a validation report flagging campaigns without cost, clicks or impressions followed on August 10.
The worked examples
The help page includes two worked examples. In the projection example, an advertiser running a 12-week initiative against a $100,000 revenue goal finds itself under-pacing on spend and revenue after five weeks. The plan shows "Channel A is the most efficient with 75 ROAS, while Channel B drives the lowest ROAS," and the advertiser shifts $5,000 from Paid Social to Paid Search. The example does not say which channel is which, nor in what unit the ROAS figure is expressed.
The scenario example is tidier. A $50,000 budget for the first quarter, $1,500 more than the fourth quarter, is reallocated by adding $1,000 to Search and $800 to Video while cutting $300 from Display, for "3% more conversions." By PPC Land's calculation, the three moves net to exactly the $1,500 increase, and the implied fourth-quarter budget is $48,500.
