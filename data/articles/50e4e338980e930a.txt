TITLE: UK condemns attacks on Orthodox Christian communities in Ethiopia
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The government has condemned violence against civilians and attacks on places of worship in Ethiopia, following reports of assaults on Orthodox Christian communities in the country's Oromia region.
Foreign Office minister Kirsty McNeill made the comments in response to Conservative MP Desmond Swayne, who asked what representations had been made to the Ethiopian government over reported attacks on churches and Christian communities.
In a written parliamentary answer, McNeill said: "We are concerned by reports of violence affecting communities in Ethiopia's Oromia region, including in Arsi Zone. The UK condemns all violence against civilians, including attacks against places of worship and people because of their religion."
The minister also outlined the UK's support for peacebuilding and human rights work in the region.
She said this included backing local peacemakers, women's groups and the Inter-Religious Council of Ethiopia, as well as supporting the Ethiopian Human Rights Commission to monitor and investigate abuses.
The response comes amid renewed violence against Orthodox Christians in Oromia.
Last month, at least 60 Orthodox Christians were killed in two attacks in the Arsi Zone, according to the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. More than 20 people were seriously injured, and around 20 homes were burned.
The World Council of Churches has condemned what it describes as a pattern of violence against Orthodox communities in the area.
It says the attacks have affected both Amhara and Oromo Christians, with earlier violence in May and June also resulting in deaths, damaged churches and hundreds of people being displaced.
