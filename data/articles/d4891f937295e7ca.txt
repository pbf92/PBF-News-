TITLE: FDJ United sues Meta over illegal casino ads using its branding
OUTLET: Focus Gaming News

The case adds to growing pressure on digital platforms to address gambling advertisements from unlicensed operators.
Key takeaways
- FDJ United has launched legal proceedings against Meta over advertisements for unlicensed online casinos targeting French consumers.
- The operator alleges some advertisements unlawfully reproduced FDJ brands, logos, retail outlets and other recognisable assets.
- Online casino products remain prohibited in France, and French law also bans the promotion of such services.
- FDJ estimates more than 80 million Europeans are exposed to illegal gambling offers each year.
- Similar legal action is being pursued against Meta in the Netherlands by gambling trade association VNLOK.
FDJ United has initiated legal action against Meta, alleging the company failed to prevent advertisements for illegal online casinos appearing on its social media platforms and using FDJ branding. The dispute comes as French authorities continue efforts to combat unauthorised gambling activity and amid broader European scrutiny of how digital platforms moderate advertising content.
France.- Meta is facing another lawsuit in Europe over its publication of adverts for unlicensed gambling. The French gambling operator FDJ United, which also runs France’s national lottery, has launched legal proceedings against the owner of Facebook and Instagram over its failure to remove adverts for unlicensed casino products that use its own branding.
According to French newspapers Le Figaro and Les Echos, FDJ United is seeking interim relief against the technology company, arguing that it has failed to prevent illegal gambling advertisements that imitate its logos and other assets. Online casino products, which remain unregulated in France, and articles 56 and 57 of the Gambling Act 2010 prohibit both the operation and promotion of games in the vertical.
FDJ United chief executive Stéphane Pallez is quoted as saying that the company is seeking the removal of the advertisements in question. She said: “FDJ United has initiated legal proceedings against Meta through an application for interim relief to secure the removal of advertisements promoting illegal activities by identically reproducing the group’s brands and distinctive signs to promote online casinos, which are strictly prohibited in France.
“Several unlawful posts went as far as to reproduce FDJ partner retail outlets, as well as the front pages of well-known newspapers, to reinforce their misleading nature.”
FDJ estimates that more than 80 million Europeans are exposed to illegal gambling offers every year and argues that social media and other digital platforms provide significant visibility for unlicensed operators. The operator also warned that the use of familiar branding and deceptive promotions can make unauthorised gambling sites appear legitimate. It’s calling for a coordinated national effort involving licensed gambling companies, digital platforms, financial institutions, industry associations and sporting organisations.
Meta has been facing pressure over gambling advertising in various jurisdictions. Both the Dutch gambling regulator Kansspelautoriteit and the British Gambling Commission have criticised the company’s record on taking action against illegal offerings. In the Netherlands, the gambling trade association VNLOK is pursuing legal action against Meta over allegations that it has failed to stop advertising for illegal gambling operators reaching Dutch consumers.
VNLOK submitted a complaint to the European Commission in June and a lawsuit in Amsterdam in September. It argues that repeated reports about illegal gambling advertisements have not been met with an adequate response.
This month, the European Commission unveiled proposals for a Digital Fairness Act, which includes measures aimed at tackling manipulative marketing practices. However, questions surrounding liability for illegal gambling advertising will continue to depend on existing laws and the decisions of courts and regulators.
