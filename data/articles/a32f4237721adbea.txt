AI Rout Turns Gold Into Source Of Cash: What Comes Next?
Gold fell to a 14-month low on Friday as a rout in technology shares prompted investors to sell bullion to cover losses elsewhere. The metal slid alongside falling Treasury yields and the US dollar.
Spot gold fell to $3,502 an ounce- the lowest level since September 2025. In total, bullion lost 8.9% for the week, while the Invesco QQQ ETF QQQ sank 9.31% and State Street SPDR S&P 500 ETF (NYSE:SPY) fell by 7.98%, entering correction territory
Rate Hike Bets Set the Stage
Minutes from the Federal Reserve’s October meeting showed that nearly all policymakers anticipated further rate increases. According to the CME FedWatch, traders currently price in an 88.6% chance of a quarter-point increase in December.
“The ongoing AI-related equity selloff is prompting some forced liquidations of gold positions to cover losses,” David Meger, director of metals trading at High Ridge Futures, told Reuters.
Ole Hansen, head of commodity strategy at Saxo Bank, wrote that bullion “has become a source of cash for investors facing mounting losses elsewhere,” Mining.com reported.
In a market commentary, former Bridgewater economist Bob Elliott said pro-growth pricing “remains quite elevated” and warned the selloff could become “more self-reinforcing to the downside.”
Once downturns start, he wrote, they typically require “both much cheaper pricing and some sort of policy support to get to the bottom, neither of which we are at yet.”
Interestingly, there was little sign of a broad exit from gold funds. Holdings in gold ETFs tracked by Bloomberg were little changed over the preceding month despite a roughly 10% drop in bullion, Hansen said.
Mining shares were less resilient, since their leverage to gold price works both ways. The VanEck Gold Miners ETF (NYSE:GDX) closed the week 14.80% below the previous Friday’s close. The largest names in the sector, Newmont NEM, Barrick Mining B and Agnico Eagle Mines AEM lost 15.03%, 15.19% and 13.64%, respectively.
Technical Outlook
Gold’s daily chart shows the magnitude of the post-summer carnage. After peaking in August and briefly moving above the 200-day moving average, bullion reversed course and fell by over 25%.
Gold, daily chart, Source: TradingView
Zooming out, the next key support area is the Q2-Q3 2025 price range. For much of that time, price remained in a tight accumulation between $3,200 and $3,450.
Thus, price could easily fall further, test that range, and wait for the next catalyst to move it either way.
