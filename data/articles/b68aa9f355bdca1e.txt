TITLE: LUKE ALFRED | Manchester United’s sinking feeling
OUTLET: Business Day

Wikipedia’s list of Manchester United managers post-Sir Alex Ferguson is almost as long as Wikipedia’s page on Manchester United itself, although not as long as the list of 18 clubs currently above them in the English Premier League (EPL).
Mauricio Pochettino, who was announced as United manager earlier this month, is the 13th since Ferguson retired in May 2013. The list includes caretaker spells by Michael Carrick (twice), Ryan Giggs, Darren Fletcher, Ruud van Nistelrooy and Ralf Rangnick.
Looking back, one sees middling to big names: David Moyes had the unenviable fate of being the first to take over in the wake of Ferguson’s departure at the end of the 2012/13 season. And we might easily forget Louis van Gaal’s spell at the club between May 2014 and May 2016.
Come to think of it, José Mourinho also had a United spell, didn’t he? He was another two-year man. We also need to factor in Erik ten Hag and Rúbin Amorim, the Portuguese coach, there more recently. Unfortunately for him, Amorim was unable to hit the two-year high bar.
For some coaches, the route after United was not always down. Rangnick, not a name you would associate with spectacular Manchester United success, passed on a job as AC Milan’s technical director later. He now coaches the Austrian national side.
Had Rangnick taken the technical director job at AC Milan, he would have teamed up with Amorim, now head coach there. Milan are currently in fourth place in Serie A, five points behind top-placed Lazio, three behind Roma and two behind arch-rivals Inter, whom they beat 3-1 at the San Siro at the end of October.
It’s a result that would have given Amorim welcome breathing space because last season Inter won the Serie A title, with AC Milan finishing fifth, so missing out on a Champions League place. In finishing fifth, Milan scored the fewest number of goals of the top six sides in the league. Amorim addressed this by spending in excess of €70m (about R1.3bn) on Paris Saint-Germain’s Portuguese striker, Gonçalo Ramos shortly after he arrived at the club. Let’s see what transpires.
Scoring goals is a widespread problem, and applies not only at AC Milan but Manchester United. United’s top-scorers last season were Bryan Mbeumo and Benjamin Ŝeŝko, with 11 each. By way of comparison, Manchester City’s Erling Haaland scored 27; Brentford’s Igor Thiago 22. Having joint top scorers on 11 goals apiece won’t make Europe shudder.
One has to go back to the season in which Ferguson left to find a United striker who scored over 20 EPL goals. In 2012/13, Robin van Persie scored 26 EPL goals and no-one at United has matched that since.
Many reasons — club culture, poor buying, lack of youth structure follow-through — have been proffered for United’s fall from grace but here’s a tangible problem: lack of goals. United can’t score them and can’t create the conditions that help scoring be holistically achieved. Simple.
Mourinho, now at Real Madrid, suffers from no such issues. This is because Kylian Mbappé has kryptonite in both feet. Mbappe scored 25 La Liga goals last season (42 in all competitions) and has already scored 13 La Liga goals this season and it’s a month before Christmas.
It’s perhaps no coincidence that Van Persie’s 26 goals helped United to the 2012/13 league title, their last. They won it by 11 points over Manchester City, and the clubs have headed in different directions since.
After United’s 4-0 defeat by Liverpool on Sunday (and their home game against Brentford tomorrow), it’s difficult to see how Pochettino can arrest the slide without a top-notch striker. Mbeumo has scored just four league goals this season and Ŝeŝko is injured. Rumours are that he’ll be moved on in the January transfer window; but who to buy?
I’ve often found football fans, no matter where in the world, are only a sentence or two away from a cheeky Manchester United theory. There are many: the problem owners, the coach, the players, the strength of the canteen tea. But let me say this: scoring is everything, on the pitch, as in life.
