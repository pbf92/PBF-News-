TITLE: Converted product group
OUTLET: PPC Land

Converted product group is a dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that labels each conversion row with the high-level catalogue group of the product that was actually bought, rather than the product that appeared in the advertisement. It exists because the two are often different. A shopper who clicks an ad for a coffee machine may buy descaling tablets from the same brand, and Amazon credits that purchase to the ad. Converted product group lets a report roll those credited sales up into broad product families, so an advertiser can see not only how much an ad sold, but what kind of merchandise it sold.
Where the field sits
Amazon's unified reporting, which replaced the separate Sponsored Ads and Amazon DSP report pages, organises its fields into categories. The Product category holds 18 attributes, according to PPC Land's breakdown of the 156 unified report dimensions, published on November 12, 2026. They split into two mirrored sets. The advertised product fields describe "the product featured in the ad." The converted product fields describe "the product credited with the conversion."
Each set carries the same descriptors: ID, name, parent ID, brand, category, subcategory, product group and marketplace, with the advertised set adding a SKU. Converted product group is therefore one of eight converted product fields, sitting beside Converted product category and Converted product subcategory as the classification layer of that set.
Coverage is the first practical difference between the two sets. PPC Land's table marks the converted product fields for all five ad products in the reference: DVA+ (Amazon's programmatic display, video and audio buying, formerly Amazon DSP), Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display and Sponsored TV. The advertised product fields apply only to DVA+, Sponsored Display and Sponsored Products. The category counts follow from that: Sponsored Brands supports 9 of the 18 Product attributes, while Sponsored Products supports all 18 and DVA+ and Sponsored Display 17 each. For Sponsored Brands and Sponsored TV, which promote a brand or a video rather than one item, the converted fields are the only product-level view.
What a product group is
Amazon has not, as of November 2026, published a sentence-level definition of Converted product group in any source PPC Land or this article could verify. The meaning has to be read from how Amazon uses the term elsewhere.
Inside Amazon's catalogue, every ASIN (Amazon Standard Identification Number, the 10-character code Amazon assigns to every item in its catalogue) carries an internal classification usually called the GL product group, GL standing for general ledger. Seller Central forum threads describe it as a classification sellers cannot edit themselves, which appears as a "Product-Group" column in the fee preview report, with values such as "Books" or "Office Product." Misfiled items, such as stationery classed as books, can only be corrected through Seller Support. These descriptions come from community posts, not Amazon documentation.
The legacy Amazon DSP product report used the same vocabulary. Data connector Funnel lists "Product group," "Product category," "Product subcategory," "Brand name," "Parent ASIN" and "ASIN conversion type" among the DSP product report fields, without definitions. PPC Land notes that most converted product attributes "inherit DVA+ names such as 'Item name,' 'Parent ASIN' and 'Brand name,'" which places the DSP report as the ancestor of the converted set. On that reading, Converted product group is the unified-report successor of the DSP's "Product group" column, populated from the purchased ASIN's catalogue classification.
The group is distinct from the browse node, the customer-facing navigation tree that shoppers click through on Amazon's site. Browse nodes are many and deep, and one product can sit in several; a product group is a single, coarse, internally assigned label. The Benchmark fields in the same report use browse categories, so unified reporting carries both taxonomies.
How the value gets populated
The mechanics follow the attribution chain. A shopper interacts with an ad and buys within the attribution window. Amazon assigns the credit, records the purchased ASIN as the converted product, then looks up that ASIN's catalogue attributes, including its product group, and writes them into the row.
Two consequences follow. The value describes the purchased item, not the creative, so an ad for a kettle that sells a mug may show different advertised and converted groups. And the value is only as accurate as the catalogue entry: a misclassified ASIN reports sales under the wrong group, which the advertiser cannot fix from the Ads Console.
A companion dimension explains why the converted product appears at all. Product relevance "indicates whether the product was directly promoted in the campaign or attributed based on relevance to promoted products (halo)," according to Amazon's reference as quoted by PPC Land. Its former DVA+ name was "ASIN conversion type." Combined, the two fields let a report show, for instance, how much of a campaign's sales came from promoted products in one group and how much came as halo sales in another.
Restrictions on the field
Amazon's data availability page, revised on November 17, 2026, sets two limits that matter here. Advertised and converted product dimensions hold data only from February 13, 2025, and when a converted product dimension is selected, traffic metrics are "not available," according to PPC Land's report on the revised limits, published on November 18, 2026. Impressions and clicks belong to the ad, not to the item bought later, so a report broken out by converted product group shows conversion metrics such as purchases, units and sales, but not click-through rate or cost per click.
The same page caps daily reports at 120 days per pull from 24 months of history, so a daily year-on-year comparison by group needs several pulls.
Origin and evolution
The converted product concept predates unified reporting by years. Sponsored Products advertisers have long had a purchased product report, which Amazon describes as giving "details on the non-advertised SKUs a customer purchased after clicking your ad." Its columns were once named "7 day other SKU sales" and "7 day other SKU units"; Amazon renamed them "Sales (halo)" and "Units sold (halo)," and the identifier column is "Purchased ASIN." The report counts orders within seven days of a click and has a 60-day lookback window, according to Amazon's help page.
Sponsored Brands added an equivalent. On June 6, 2023, Amazon announced an ASIN-level breakdown of Sponsored Brands attributed purchases for book publishers, for campaigns created after February 11, 2023, noting that "book products don't have brand halo expansion." The wider attributed purchases report splits sales into "Promoted," where the ASIN appeared in the creative, and "Brand Halo," where it did not, according to analytics vendor Intentwise.
Unified reporting merged these strands. It opened in beta on November 11, 2025 and reached general availability on June 8, 2026, as PPC Land reported when it exited beta. Attribution also moved "from a conversion-based methodology to a traffic-based methodology." PPC Land's table gives Converted product ID the former names "ASIN" (DVA+) and "Purchased ASIN" (sponsored ads), confirming that the converted set absorbed both lineages. No former name for Converted product group itself is listed.
Why it matters
The field answers a basic retail media question: is advertising selling the intended products, or others? Brand halo metrics, as PPC Land's halo effect explainer sets out, "exclude the promoted products" and measure the effect on the rest of the brand. Converted product group gives that halo a shape. A housewares brand promoting blenders can see whether halo sales stay in kitchen appliances or reach groceries or home goods.
For multi-category sellers, the dimension also links advertising to finance. If product groups match the classification in Amazon's fee reports, a report by group can sit beside category margin data more easily than one listing thousands of ASINs.
Limitations and disputes
The largest limitation is definitional. Amazon's public reference, as reported, does not define product group, list its values or state whether they match the GL product groups visible in seller fee reports. Advertisers interpreting the field rely on inference from legacy reports and catalogue behaviour.
Classification quality is a second problem: product groups are hard for sellers to correct, and any error flows straight into ad reporting.
Halo attribution itself is contested. Crediting an ad with a purchase of a different product assumes the ad caused it, and controlled studies of search advertising have found spillover that also benefits competitors, as PPC Land's halo explainer notes. A converted product group full of halo sales may describe correlated shopping rather than incremental demand.
Finally, the field's history is short. Data begins on February 13, 2025, so longer trend analysis must rely on legacy reports, which Amazon scheduled to become read-only on December 17, 2026 and shut down on January 31, 2027. Amazon's June 2026 notice had given December 31, 2026 for the sunset, so the two dates conflict depending on the source and its date.
Commonly confused terms
Advertised product group uses the same classification but applies it to the product shown in the ad. It is unavailable for Sponsored Brands and Sponsored TV.
Converted product category and subcategory are the finer classification fields in the same set. Product group is the broadest label; category and subcategory narrow it.
Product relevance does not classify the product at all. It says whether the converted product was promoted or a halo item.
Product groups in Google Ads and Microsoft Advertising are something else entirely: subdivisions of a Shopping campaign's product feed, used to set bids. They are targeting structures built by advertisers, not catalogue labels assigned by a retailer.
Recent developments
Amazon renamed Amazon DSP as DVA on September 29, 2026, with DVA+ entering beta. The November 11, 2026 dimensions reference marked the converted product fields for every ad product, and the November 17 data availability revision added the traffic metric restriction on converted product dimensions, and Amazon's legacy report shutdown on January 31, 2027 makes the converted product fields the only place for this view once purchased product and attributed purchases reports close.
