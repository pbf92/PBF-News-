TITLE: Cable drops to 31.7% of US TV ad time, lowest in Comscore's series
OUTLET: PPC Land

Cable networks accounted for 31.7% of the time US television audiences spent exposed to advertising in September 2026, according to Comscore's Score Report, the lowest cable share in the 13 months of data the series has published. Broadcast climbed 2.3 percentage points from August to 45.5%, and streaming reached 22.8%, its highest reading so far.
In Short
Comscore's September 2026 Score Report shows cable's share of US TV ad exposure time falling to 31.7%, while broadcast rose to 45.5% and streaming reached 22.8%. The figures count how long ads were seen on in-home TV sets, not how much people watched or how much advertisers spent, so they show where TV ad time is actually delivered and which companies deliver it. Disney ABC moved to first place among owners, Hulu passed Tubi as the second-largest streaming service by ad time, and cable lost ground in every one of the ten largest local markets.
What the September edition shows
The September 2026 edition is the second monthly release of The Score Report, which Comscore launched on October 21 with August 2026 data. Comscore describes the report as "an independent view of advertising exposure across U.S. and local-market television, making it easier to compare ad activity across platforms, networks and markets." The report page carries a "Report Month" selector set to September 2026 and no publication date. Under the schedule Comscore sets out in its methodology, "a report published in October includes data for the August broadcast month," which places the September edition in November 2026.
Each report, according to Comscore, covers "a complete broadcast month consisting of either four or five weeks." The company does not list the dates. On the standard US broadcast calendar, in which a month starts on a Monday and ends on the last Sunday of the calendar month, September 2026 ran for four weeks, from August 31 to September 27, by PPC Land's reading. August 2026 was a five-week month, from July 27 to August 30. Because the report publishes shares rather than totals, the different month lengths do not mechanically distort the comparison.
According to Comscore, broadcast held 45.5% of Total U.S. Share of Ad Exposure Time in September, cable 31.7% and streaming 22.8%. Every chart in the report carries the same source note: "Comscore share of ad exposure time data by broadcast month. Includes measurements of all ads, including linear-originating, streaming-originating, national, and local for all 210 markets tracked by Comscore across broadcast networks, cable networks, and streaming services."
Shares from Comscore. September 2025 figures come from the trend chart in the August 2026 edition; the September edition's chart begins at October 2025. Changes and combined totals are PPC Land calculations.
The arithmetic of the month is simple. Cable gave up 2.8 points. Broadcast took 2.3 of them and streaming the remaining 0.5. Against September 2025, cable is down 5.6 points and streaming up 6.7, while broadcast is 1.1 points lower, by PPC Land's calculation. Over twelve months, then, about five-sixths of streaming's gain came out of cable rather than broadcast.
Taken together, linear TV - broadcast and cable combined - accounted for 77.2% of measured ad exposure time in September. A year earlier the figure was 83.9%.
Thirteen months of trend data
The September edition's trend chart covers October 2025 to September 2026. For the eleven months it shares with the August edition, October 2025 to August 2026, every figure is identical, so Comscore did not restate any earlier month.
Source: Comscore. The September 2025 row appears only in the August 2026 edition.
Streaming's path has two phases. It ranged between 16.1% and 18.5% from September 2025 to January 2026, then stepped up 4.5 points to 21.1% in February and has held above one-fifth of measured ad time every month since. The September 2026 reading of 22.8% is the highest in the series. Comscore has not explained the February step, which remains the largest single monthly movement for streaming in the data.
Broadcast's 45.5% is its highest share since January 2026, when it held 47.1%. Cable's 31.7% is the first reading below 34%; before September, its range across twelve months was 34.1% to 37.3%.
Is the September swing to broadcast seasonal? Comscore does not say, and with a single earlier September in the series the data cannot answer the question. In September 2025, broadcast held 46.6%, close to its levels in the following four months. Viewing data offer a parallel rather than a proof: Nielsen's October 2025 Gauge put broadcast at 22.9% of US TV viewing time, its highest share in nearly a year, driven primarily by NFL coverage on CBS, FOX and NBC. Football appears nowhere in Comscore's report, and CBS, one of the three NFL broadcast partners named in that coverage, lost share within broadcast in September.
How the Score Report is built
Comscore's methodology describes the report through seven elements. According to the company:
- Devices. "The Score Report measures ads exposed on a television device in-home, including Linear (Broadcast + Cable) and Streaming via smart TVs and TV-connected streaming devices." Ad exposure on desktops and laptops, mobile phones and tablets is not included.
- Data. The report "is compiled using Comscore's comprehensive measurement data, including references to both Linear (broadcast and cable television) and Streaming data sources to provide a unified view of advertising exposure across all platforms."
- Internet-delivered channel bundles. Live channel packages delivered over the internet, known as vMVPD services, are credited back to the networks. "For vMVPD services streaming linear content, the ads shown are credited to the cable network or broadcast station where the content originated," according to Comscore. Its example: "An ad shown on NBC viewed through YouTube TV is credited to NBC rather than YouTube."
- The metric. Share of Ad Exposure Time "represents an entity's portion of the total measurable television ad viewing time captured within The Score Report." Comscore calls it "The Score Report's key metric for measuring the percentage of total measurable television advertising viewership attributed to a publisher, platform, or owner." The calculation is "An entity's total ad exposure time ÷ all measurable advertising exposure captured within The Score Report reporting universe, during the reporting period."
- What it does not measure. The metric "measures advertising exposure, not overall content consumption, and does not indicate how much time audiences spend watching a service, network, show, or series. It is also not a measure of available advertising inventory, number of ad slots, advertising spend, unique reach, or frequency."
- Reporting period. "The Score Report is published monthly, with each report covering a complete broadcast month consisting of either four or five weeks."
- Company groupings. Companies are ranked two ways, by owner and by publisher parent company, which treat local broadcast stations differently (set out in the ownership section below).
Comscore also warns against reading the shares as a quality score. "Results may be influenced by audience scale, time spent with ad-supported content, and advertising load," the methodology states. "A higher share means that an entity accounts for a larger portion of measured advertising exposure, but it does not inherently indicate better or worse performance, advertising effectiveness, or viewer experience." A service with a light ad load or a large ad-free subscriber base can therefore hold a large audience and a small share.
These rules settle two questions raised in PPC Land's coverage of the first edition: whether YouTube's streaming share includes YouTube TV's channel bundle, and whether viewing on phones and computers counts. Neither does. Ads in network programming watched through YouTube TV count toward the network, and YouTube viewing outside the living-room TV set is outside the universe altogether.
Broadcast: FOX, ABC and NBC gain while CBS slips
Within broadcast, according to Comscore, ABC held 26.1% of ad exposure time in September, NBC 25.0%, CBS 21.2% and FOX 12.7%. The four networks account for 85.0% of broadcast ad time, by PPC Land's addition. In August, ABC, NBC and CBS sat within 1.4 points of one another. In September, CBS fell 2.5 points while FOX rose 2.2.
Within-platform shares from Comscore. The last column multiplies each network's share by broadcast's 45.5% of total ad exposure time.
Converted into shares of all US TV ad exposure time, the FOX broadcast network went from about 4.5% in August to 5.8% in September, ABC from 10.8% to 11.9% and NBC from 10.5% to 11.4%, while CBS slipped from 10.2% to 9.6%. Those three gains, about 3.2 points together by PPC Land's calculation, more than account for broadcast's 2.3-point rise. CBS and the smaller networks, The CW and ION among them, gave back the difference.
Cable: ESPN nearly doubles, FOX News eases
FOX News Channel remained the largest cable network by a wide margin, with 13.2% of cable ad exposure time, according to Comscore. That was down from 15.4% in August. On PPC Land's arithmetic, its share of all US TV ad exposure time fell from about 5.3% to 4.2%, a drop of roughly 1.1 points, or about two-fifths of cable's entire 2.8-point decline.
ESPN moved the other way. Its share of cable ad time nearly doubled, from 2.9% to 5.7%, lifting it from fifth to second, just ahead of MS NOW at 5.6%.
Within-platform shares from Comscore. USA, at 2.2% of cable in August, is not in the September top ten.
The top ten cable networks together held 44.6% of cable ad time in September, by PPC Land's addition. More than half of cable's ad exposure is therefore spread across networks outside the list, a long tail that the streaming chart does not have.
Streaming: Hulu moves into second
Streaming is the most concentrated of the three platforms. Comscore's chart ranks YouTube first, followed by Hulu at 14.4% of streaming ad exposure time and Tubi TV at 11.2%. Prime Video holds 7.4%, The Roku Channel 6.3%, Peacock 4.5%, Netflix 3.8%, Paramount+ and Pluto TV 2.7% each, and Disney+ 2.1%.
YouTube's 9.6% of all US ad exposure time in the publisher parent ranking equals about 42.1% of streaming's 22.8%, by PPC Land's calculation. The same method reproduces exactly the 44.4% Comscore published for YouTube in August, when it held 9.9% of the total and streaming 22.3%.
Within-platform shares from Comscore, except YouTube's September share of streaming, which PPC Land derived from Comscore's parent company figure.
Hulu's move is the largest in the table: 5.9 points in a month, from third to second. When Comscore launched the series, it described YouTube and Tubi as holding the top two streaming positions month after month, with third place changing hands, and it linked Hulu's August rise to an expansion of Hulu's ad-supported catalog, as PPC Land reported on October 21. In September, Hulu passed Tubi outright. Comscore gives no explanation for the September figure.
Netflix went the other way, from 6.3% of streaming ad time to 3.8%, and from sixth place to seventh. Disney+ fell from 3.0% to 2.1% and Peacock from 5.0% to 4.5%, while Paramount+ rose from 2.2% to 2.7%. The ten listed services account for about 97.2% of streaming ad exposure, leaving under 3% for every other ad-supported streaming service Comscore measures, by PPC Land's addition.
