TITLE: Non-Intubated Awake Anesthesia Reduces Complications in Uniportal Thoracoscopic Surgery Meta-Analysis Finds
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Adult patients undergoing uniportal video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery experience substantially fewer overall postoperative complications and pulmonary adverse events when managed with non-intubated awake anesthesia rather than conventional intubated general anesthesia, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in The Cardiothoracic Surgeon. The investigation, conducted by researchers Yasser Ali Kamal and Mohamed Shawky Zahran, synthesizes comparative clinical evidence across fourteen clinical studies encompassing 2,054 adult surgical patients. Uniportal video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery minimizes incisional trauma by utilizing a single small intercostal entry port to achieve diagnostic and therapeutic intrathoracic objectives. While thoracic surgical interventions have historically depended upon general anesthesia with endotracheal or double-lumen tube intubation and mechanical one-lung ventilation, emerging non-intubated approaches preserve spontaneous physiological respiration and natural diaphragm movement through regional nerve blocks accompanied by targeted conscious sedation. By systematically pooling comparative surgical cohorts, the authors demonstrated that avoiding tracheal instrumentation and chemical muscle relaxation yields measurable reductions in patient morbidity across both randomized clinical trials and observational cohorts.
The quantitative evaluation of primary clinical endpoints demonstrated marked statistical advantages for patients managed with non-intubated techniques across pooled surgical cohorts. Across seven comparative studies comprising 899 non-intubated patients and 513 intubated controls, non-intubated anesthesia achieved a statistically significant 53 percent reduction in total postoperative complications, yielding a pooled risk ratio of 0.47 with a 95 percent confidence interval extending from 0.32 to 0.69 and zero observed statistical heterogeneity. An identical relative reduction was documented for postoperative pulmonary complications across seven studies analyzing 900 non-intubated cases and 510 intubated control subjects. In that specific composite outcome, which monitored acute clinical events including persistent air leaks, post-resection pneumonia, pleural effusion, atelectasis, empyema, subcutaneous emphysema, bronchopleural fistula, and respiratory distress, non-intubated management yielded a risk ratio of 0.49 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.34 to 0.69 and an inconsistency index of zero percent. Mortality was similarly reduced across two reporting trials encompassing 688 total patients, demonstrating a risk ratio of 0.21, though investigators noted this specific survival outcome was heavily influenced by a single institutional study.
To establish a rigorous methodological framework, the systematic review was prospectively registered in the PROSPERO international registry under identification CRD420261282157 and executed in strict accordance with PRISMA 2020 reporting standards. On December 16, 2025, the investigators performed comprehensive electronic database queries spanning PubMed, the Web of Science Core Collection, Scopus, Ovid, and the Cochrane Library without publication year restrictions, supplementing automated retrieval with manual cross-referencing of bibliographic citations. Eligibility criteria were strictly restricted to adult patients aged eighteen years or older undergoing uniportal thoracoscopic procedures under non-intubated spontaneous ventilation, compared directly against general anesthesia cohorts managed with endotracheal or double-lumen endobronchial intubation. Pediatric cohorts, experimental animal models, multiportal thoracoscopic surgeries, and planned open thoracotomies were excluded from consideration. From an initial identification of 653 database records, two independent reviewers performed sequential title and abstract screening followed by full-text evaluation of 38 candidate publications, ultimately validating fourteen studies published between 2013 and 2025 across medical centers in Europe and Asia for quantitative and qualitative synthesis.
The methodological quality of the six randomized controlled trials was independently evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool, which identified some methodological concerns across all included trials rather than high risk of bias. Randomization sequence generation and allocation concealment demonstrated low risk in three trials, while earlier trials exhibited vulnerability to performance and detection bias arising from the practical impossibility of blinding surgical teams to the presence of an endotracheal airway device. Methodological rigor among the eight observational investigations was quantified via the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, where study quality scores ranged from 6 to 9 out of a maximum of 9 points, with four investigations meeting criteria for high methodological quality. To address baseline confounding inherent in retrospective designs, three observational studies incorporated propensity score matching algorithms; nevertheless, the review authors recognized that residual confounding by surgical indication could not be entirely ruled out. Publication bias assessments using funnel plots and Egger linear regression tests across reporting cohorts demonstrated symmetrical distribution for both total complications and postoperative pulmonary complications, indicating an absence of small-study publication bias.
Statistically significant benefits favoring the non-intubated approach extended to several secondary clinical recovery metrics, notably chest tube duration, emergence speed, and postoperative throat comfort. Analysis of four clinical studies revealed that non-intubated patients required significantly shorter chest tube or pleural drainage duration, achieving a weighted mean difference of negative 0.41 days, with a 95 percent confidence interval from negative 0.59 to negative 0.23 days and zero percent statistical heterogeneity. Emergence from sedation was similarly accelerated among non-intubated cohorts; pooled data from two randomized controlled trials demonstrated that awakening time was reduced by a mean difference of 5.44 minutes compared with intubated general anesthesia. Furthermore, evaluation of five studies demonstrated a marked reduction in the incidence of postoperative sore throat and pharyngeal discomfort, yielding a pooled risk ratio of 0.31 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.11 to 0.87. This pharyngeal benefit was directly attributed to the complete avoidance of rigid laryngoscopy, endotracheal tube placement, and mucosal cuff pressure within the sensitive upper airway passages.
In contrast to the clear protective signals observed for pulmonary morbidity and emergence speed, intraoperative respiratory safety monitoring revealed a borderline elevation in hypoxemic events during non-intubated surgical maintenance. Across three comparative studies reporting gas exchange complications, non-intubated anesthesia was associated with an approximate two-fold higher relative incidence of intraoperative hypoxemia, presenting a risk ratio of 1.92 with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 0.99 to 3.76, a borderline probability value of P = 0.05, and modest heterogeneity quantified at an I-squared value of 19 percent. Within the primary source definitions, hypoxemia was defined as an intraoperative arterial oxygen saturation falling below 90 percent or dropping below 92 percent for a duration of at least one minute. The authors emphasized that this observed physiological vulnerability highlights the essential necessity for rigorous real-time intraoperative monitoring, specialized thoracic anesthesiology expertise, and predefined institutional protocols enabling prompt conversion to endotracheal intubation should persistent hypoventilation or profound hypoxemia occur during surgical manipulation.
Several other operative, economic, and inpatient recovery outcomes failed to demonstrate statistically significant differences between non-intubated and intubated cohorts, frequently exhibiting extreme statistical heterogeneity across reporting centers. An analysis of eight studies evaluating operative duration demonstrated marked inconsistency across surgical centers, with an I-squared value reaching 99 percent; four studies observed faster operations under non-intubated conditions, three documented longer operative times, and one identified no measurable difference. Similarly, four studies assessing total anesthetic duration yielded an inconsistency index of 91 percent, reflecting institutional disparities in induction workflows and regional nerve block placement. Postoperative pain scores measured on visual analogue scales at twenty-four hours showed a non-significant trend toward lower pain but retained high heterogeneity at 90 percent. Hospital length of stay trended shorter in six reporting centers without reaching statistical significance across random-effects pooling. Economic costs harmonized to 2020 United States dollars across two randomized trials demonstrated an insignificant difference of negative 404.04 dollars, while re-operation rates were entirely comparable between arms, presenting a risk ratio of 0.58 across two studies.
Subgroup analyses stratified by study design and surgical procedure categories reaffirmed the consistency of pulmonary protection associated with spontaneous ventilation. When postoperative pulmonary complications were examined separately across three randomized controlled trials enrolling 483 patients and four observational studies enrolling 927 patients, both trial designs independently demonstrated consistent reductions in respiratory complications favoring non-intubated care, exhibiting zero statistical heterogeneity within either subgroup. Stratification by operative complexity further confirmed broad applicability across thoracic interventions. For lung resection procedures addressing pulmonary nodules or neoplasms, four studies comprising 317 patients demonstrated a 74 percent relative reduction in total postoperative complications and a 60 percent relative reduction in pulmonary complications. Among non-resectional thoracic procedures, three studies encompassing 1,095 patients demonstrated a 45 percent relative reduction in total complications and a 51 percent relative reduction in pulmonary complications. These subgroup findings substantiate that the perioperative physiological advantages of non-intubated anesthesia remain discernible across diverse operative resections and procedural variations.
The certainty of evidence evaluated through the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluation system ranged from moderate to very low across the clinical spectrum of measured outcomes. Primary endpoints including total postoperative complications, postoperative pulmonary complications, and overall perioperative mortality achieved moderate certainty ratings, having been downgraded primarily for risk of bias across mixed trial designs but strengthened by narrow confidence intervals and consistent effect directions. In contrast, secondary parameters such as visual analogue scale pain scores, hospital length of stay, duration of anesthesia, sore throat frequency, and intraoperative hypoxemia were categorized as low certainty due to wide confidence intervals and pronounced clinical heterogeneity. Highly variable metrics including operative duration, procedure-related financial cost, patient satisfaction, and re-operation rates were graded as very low certainty evidence. Sensitivity testing utilizing leave-one-out validation confirmed stable pooled effect estimates for complications, although the statistical significance of mortality was found to depend entirely upon the inclusion of a single historical study.
