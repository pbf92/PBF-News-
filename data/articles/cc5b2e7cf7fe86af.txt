TITLE: Review Highlights Multidimensional Strain and Adaptation Among Urostomy Family Caregivers
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A qualitative meta-synthesis published in Supportive Care in Cancer indicates that family caregivers of patients living with a urinary stoma experience persistent multidimensional burdens, ranging from immediate postoperative shock to long-term caregiving exhaustion, alongside recurring unmet needs for external assistance. Concurrently, the synthesis found that some caregivers achieve meaningful personal growth and life reconstruction by actively adapting to their caregiving responsibilities.
The study, conducted by Xueyuan Wu and colleagues, aimed to explore the caregiving experiences of family caregivers of patients with urinary stomas.
To identify relevant qualitative research, the investigative team executed a systematic literature search across seven English-language electronic databases: PubMed, Embase, ProQuest, the Cochrane Library, Web of Science, CINAHL, and EBSCO. The researchers also searched three major Chinese databases, including CNKI, Wanfang Data, and VIP, evaluating all qualitative studies published from database inception through April 2026.
The methodological quality of the included studies was assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute Critical Appraisal Checklist for Qualitative Research. The research team then integrated and synthesized the qualifying evidence using the Joanna Briggs Institute meta-aggregation approach, structuring and reporting their findings in accordance with the Enhancing Transparency in Reporting Qualitative Syntheses, or ENTREQ, guidelines.
A total of 11 qualitative studies met all inclusion criteria for the meta-synthesis. From these investigations, 32 discrete findings were extracted and aggregated into eight distinct categories, which subsequently formed three overarching synthesized findings: the accumulation of multidimensional burdens from postoperative shock to exhaustion, caregivers’ supportive care needs and experiences with external support, and personal growth and life reconstruction through active adaptation.
In evaluating these outcomes, the authors concluded that family caregivers encounter persistent shortages of professional, social, and family support while managing long-term care demands. To resolve these challenges, the researchers proposed developing integrated, multidimensional support strategies that combine professional clinical guidance, family networks, and community resources to address unmet caregiver needs, alleviate ongoing burdens, and facilitate active role adaptation.
