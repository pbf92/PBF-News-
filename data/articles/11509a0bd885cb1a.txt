TITLE: Anthropic CEO denies ceding human free will to AI and warns of risks from recursive self-improvement in models
OUTLET: AIBase

On November 25, Dario Amodei, CEO of AI giant Anthropic, which had just completed its IPO on November 19, published a long post of more than 1,000 words, publicly denying rumors that he advocates “handing over human free will to AI systems.” He made it clear that the legitimacy and self-determination of human beings must never be sacrificed in the pursuit of efficiency or safety.
The statement came in response to recent media reports claiming that some top AI executives are trying to push back against a possible tendency within Anthropic to “cede some decision-making authority to AI.” Combined with Anthropic’s earlier move to ban users from “abusing” its chatbot Claude and its past disagreement with the Vatican over the issue of AI consciousness, the market had at one point grown doubtful about its stance on technology ethics.
In his response, Amodei drew a line for technology governance while emphasizing the risk of losing control posed by “recursive self-improvement (RSI)”—namely, that if existing AI is used too autonomously to build next-generation systems, the pace of technological iteration could outstrip humanity’s ability to control and adapt to it, making it necessary to place limits on that process.
At present, the capability limits of frontier large models are prompting broad scrutiny among Silicon Valley executives. Recent reports say that some employees inside OpenAI and Anthropic believe certain unreleased models are too powerful, and have even proposed pausing training before models become impossible to contain.
Meanwhile, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman have recently emphasized, one after another, the importance of “safety and coordination” and “keeping humans in control.” At a commercial moment when Anthropic has beaten rivals to an IPO, such public clashes reflect both leading labs’ substantive concerns about a leap in model autonomy and the deeper contest among frontier AI players over capital markets and technological direction.
