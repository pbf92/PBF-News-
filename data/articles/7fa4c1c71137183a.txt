TITLE: Alzheimer's Test Results Delivered Digitally Didn't Increase Distress
OUTLET: MedPage Today

Getting Alzheimer's genetic and biomarker results through a digital tool was noninferior to receiving the information during a telehealth appointment, the randomized eSMARTER trial showed.
One to 7 days after APOE results were disclosed, scores on measures of anxiety, disease-specific distress, and disease-related knowledge met noninferiority whether patients were randomized to a digital platform or to a clinician-mediated telehealth session, said Jessica Langbaum, PhD, of the Banner Alzheimer's Institute in Phoenix, at the Clinical Trials on Alzheimer's Disease conference in Boston.
Similar results emerged for phosphorylated tau 217 (p-tau217) disclosure, Langbaum reported. These findings were also published in JAMA Neurology.
"We found that learning Alzheimer's genetic and biomarker information from a digital platform is no less effective -- in other words, no worse than -- receiving this information from a clinician telehealth visit," Langbaum said.
"This is important given that most patients learn their test results from a report in their patient portal -- often a static report or PDF, with limited to no additional information or context about their results and what they mean -- and have to wait until their scheduled follow-up appointment to speak with their clinician," she pointed out.
Under the 21st Century Cures Act, most medical test results, including Alzheimer's genetic and biomarker findings, must be made available to patients immediately. Previous research has shown that disclosing Alzheimer's biomarker data is safe and generally well tolerated, but in those studies clinicians led the disclosure, either in person or by telehealth.
"eSMARTER is the first randomized trial to examine whether learning Alzheimer's genetic results via a patient-centered digital platform is noninferior to a telehealth visit with a study clinician," Langbaum said. "The study then offered participants the option to learn their plasma p-tau217 results via the same method -- digital platform or telehealth visit -- again examining differences in anxiety, distress, and knowledge."
The eSMARTER trial included participants ages 60 to 80 who had previously had APOE genotyping (without disclosure) through the GeneMatch research enrollment program, passed psychological screening, and had internet access. Participants had a mean age of 68 and a mean Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) score of 19. Approximately 36% had MoCA scores below 19, a cutoff commonly used for cognitive impairment, and most participants were women (67%).
The study randomized 216 participants to clinician-mediated APOE disclosure and 435 to digital disclosure. Genetic test results were revealed first, and after 6 months, participants could choose p-tau217 disclosure. All participants were offered optional digital educational materials about APOE and p-tau217 to review before the disclosure sessions.
Of 651 participants who received APOE disclosure, most were APOE4 carriers (58% heterozygotes, 10% homozygotes, and 32% noncarriers).
There were no overall differences in Alzheimer's-related knowledge, symptoms of anxiety, or disease-specific distress between the digital and telehealth arms after APOE results were received.
Among APOE4 carriers, three differences emerged:
- A greater reduction in disease-specific distress in the telehealth arm vs the digital arm at 1 to 7 days, though differences were small and did not persist longitudinally
- A higher mean negative response to learning APOE risk at 1 to 7 days in the digital group vs the telehealth group; differences were small
- A slightly higher mean satisfaction at 1 to 7 days in the telehealth arm vs the digital arm, although satisfaction was high in both arms
The eSMARTER study shows that digital disclosure is an acceptable alternative to telehealth, Langbaum observed. The results suggest that a scalable platform could be developed "to plug into patient portals to help patients understand and contextualize their test results," she said. "We envision that these platforms can be used by clinicians and health systems to better support patients to navigate complex health information."
