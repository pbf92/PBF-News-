TITLE: Muse Lifts Instagram Ad Conversions: Forter 50% Share
OUTLET: explainx.ai

Meta's ad-free Muse agent is already lifting Instagram advertising ROI, CNBC reported on November 24, 2026, by turning saved posts into shopping workflows—even as Forter Intelligence independently shows Muse placing about half of agentic checkout orders on its merchant network.
Answer first: what changed on November 24
The story sits beside—but is not the same as—Muse in Instagram DMs, which documents product controls and five-country rollout. This post is about revenue mechanics and third-party order share.
The saved-post funnel CNBC describes
Instagram's bookmark habit predates Muse. CNBC's reporting adds why Meta cares now:
- Users save Reels, posts, and ads they might buy later.
- After the November 24 Collections update, saving can trigger a Muse prompt to organize items (also in TechCrunch's rollout recap).
- Muse turns scattered saves into folders, lists, and itineraries users can act on inside the agent.
- When a user returns and purchases, the original advertiser benefits—even though Muse never showed a new ad impression.
A senior Meta executive told CNBC, on background, that Muse "is still very valuable for our ads business because it raises conversions for advertisers." The executive tied lower friction to higher ROI on dollars already spent on Instagram.
CNBC also cites a person familiar with Meta (unnamed, unpublished data) that about 20% of daily Instagram users save at least one post per day, and that many saves are shoppable products or ads. Meta has not published that ratio in a press release or filing; treat both figures as CNBC-sourced internal metrics.
Zuckerberg shopping stats (earnings context)
CNBC anchors advertiser impact to remarks Mark Zuckerberg made on Meta's latest earnings call (exact quarter not restated in the article):
- 25% of Muse users shop through the agentic tool.
- Shopping is the most common Muse use case.
- Average purchase price exceeds $200.
- Meta plans a small fee on Muse transactions (fee schedule not in CNBC's piece).
Those lines matter for founders because they show Meta monetizing transactions and data, not only feed ads. Compare with Walmart's Black Friday Muse integration, where checkout rides Agent Gateway with explicit caps—another path to the same transaction fee thesis.
Forter's independent order share

CNBC cites Forter Intelligence for agentic market share. Forter operates separately from Meta and measures orders its fraud stack labels as agent-placed across thousands of merchants.
On Meta's Muse page at Forter (updated November 23, 2026):
Other agents (7-day shares on same page): Grok 23%, ChatGPT 18%, Instinct 4%, ChatGPT Agent 3%, Google 2%.
CNBC rounded Muse to 51% for the week ending November 22. The one-point gap is normal rounding across slightly different date windows—cite Forter's dated page when you need precision.
Scraper vs protocol checkout
Forter splits agents into:
- Protocol agents (ChatGPT Instant Checkout, Google) identified by referrer.
- Scraper agents (Muse, Grok, Instinct, ChatGPT Agent) that drive the merchant's own checkout pages.
Forter says scraper-style agents accounted for 69% of agentic orders over the latest 90 days. Muse's scraper behavior is why Amazon resistance and Walmart's open gateway land on opposite sides of the same trend.
Growth curve
Forter detected Muse orders starting September 21, 2026. The week of first detection showed 229% month-over-month growth in agentic orders on its network. By the end of the 90-day window, agentic volume was 9.2× the first week—partly because new agents joined, but Forter credits Muse as the curve changer.
For context on bench competition, see our Visa agentic commerce benchmark—lab pass rates differ from Forter's live checkout counts.
Why Meta can defer Muse feed ads
CNBC's sources sketch monetization sequencing:
- Phase one (now): Conversion lift on Instagram without Muse ads; transaction fees on agent checkouts per Zuckerberg's earnings remarks.
- Phase two (expected later): Ads inside Muse, likely starting in a feed-like surface rather than per-query answers.
- Strategic patience: Sources said Meta is not financially pressured to rush in-agent ads—consistent with how it scaled Reels before maxing ads.
For marketers, the near-term lever is creative that saves well: if saves trigger Muse organization, thumb-stopping catalog creative becomes retargetable agent memory.
Privacy and targeting implications
CNBC notes shopping intent inside Muse becomes a targeting asset for Instagram ads outside Muse. That is distinct from UK regulator warnings on personal agents but rhymes with Uber's fear of disintermediation: whoever owns the agent thread owns conversion data.
Builders shipping connectors should assume Meta will rank partners that close the loop on saves and DM side threads documented in the Instagram DM post.
Agentic commerce competitive map (November 2026)
None of these shares are global GMV—they are Forter's labeled orders. A merchant not on Forter could see a different mix.
What retailers should test this week
- Save-to-buy path: Run a small Instagram creative with obvious save CTA; measure Muse-organized lists if Meta exposes partner analytics (not confirmed in CNBC).
- Bot tolerance: Scraper agents trigger fraud and bot defenses; ensure checkout survives headless browsers.
- Open vs closed gate: Contrast Walmart Agent Gateway openness with platforms that block agents.
- Fee modeling: Use $219 Forter AOV and Zuckerberg's $200+ remark as sanity checks, not guarantees.
What is not confirmed
- Exact save-rate (20% DAU) — CNBC background only.
- Future Muse ad formats — executive expectation, not a product roadmap.
- Meta's cut on transactions — "small fee" language without basis points.
- Whether CNBC's 51% and Forter's 50% use identical merchant sets — they should be treated as the same vendor, adjacent windows.
How this fits Meta's November agent stack
Late November stacked distribution (DMs, Collections), retail pipes (Walmart), and now advertiser narrative (CNBC). Technical teams should read Meta FAIR AgentBox for HPC agents separately—that is research orchestration, not consumer checkout.
Traffic, influence, and orders: three different numbers
Forter's agentic commerce statistics page warns readers not to mix metrics:
CNBC's conversion lift story is closest to influenced sales logic—Muse helps people finish journeys that started on Instagram—while Forter's 50% measures agent-placed orders. A brand can see higher ROI on ads without appearing in Forter's table if checkout still happens manually on mobile Safari.
Advertiser playbook (without Muse ad units)
- Creative for saves: Use clear product identity in the first three seconds so a later Muse folder still maps to a SKU.
- Catalog hygiene: Broken deeplinks hurt twice when agents scrape pages—test with agent gateway patterns.
- Promo stacks: Black Friday connector campaigns plus save prompts can compound; watch inventory sync latency.
- Measurement: Ask agencies whether their dashboards separate Muse scraper orders, ChatGPT protocol checkouts, and human mobile web.
Risks merchants should model
- Bot friction: High Muse share means more headless Chromium sessions—tune WAF rules so you do not block paying customers.
- Fee stacking: Zuckerberg's transaction fee plus existing payment interchange may change unit economics on sub-$50 items.
- Regulatory: UK FCA agentic payments guidance shows regulators may treat autonomous checkout as authorization, not marketing.
Builders: connector vs scrape
If you are not Walmart-scale, decide whether to pursue protocol checkout (OpenAI-style) or HTML scrape tolerance (Muse-style). The Forter split—69% scraper orders over 90 days—suggests scraper agents dominate today, but protocol paths may catch up as Personal Agent Protocol backers ship shared rails.
