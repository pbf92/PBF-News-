WHO Launches Portal to Include Pregnant Women in Trials
The knowledge portal and a UK women's health initiative both aim to close long-standing gaps in clinical research participation.
Updated on Nov. 26, 2026 in Women’s Health
Live Poll

The World Health Organization launched a knowledge portal to support the inclusion of pregnant and lactating women in clinical trials, which have often excluded them. The portal was developed by the WHO Pregnant and Lactating Women Task Force and brings together guidance, tools and examples adaptable across disease areas.
Why it matters
Pregnant and breastfeeding women have often been excluded from clinical research, leaving significant evidence gaps about treatments used during these stages of life. The portal aims to help stakeholders foster best practices in research and development and contribute to higher-quality evidence.
Pregnant women made up 1.1% of 4,616 UK clinical trial applications submitted between 2019 and 2023, and breastfeeding women made up 0.6%. Only 4% of global clinical trials over the past decade allowed pregnant women, according to an ICTRP analysis, and the effect of this underrepresentation on treatment outcomes remains unmeasured.
The players
World Health Organization (WHO)
The United Nations agency responsible for international public health, which set up the Pregnant and Lactating Women Task Force and the knowledge portal.
Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA)
The UK's regulator for medicines and medical devices, which analyzed clinical trial applications and launched a Women's Health Programme to boost female participation in trials.
The details
The portal covers ethics, nonclinical research, clinical trial design, regulatory frameworks, surveillance, community engagement, epidemic preparedness and technical advocacy, and includes thematic toolkits for HIV, tuberculosis and vaccines. It was launched by WHO together with the Special Programme of Research, Development and Research Training in Human Reproduction (HRP), which is co-sponsored by UNDP, UNFPA, UNICEF, WHO and the World Bank.
Timeline
- 2019–2023: UK clinical trial applications analyzed for pregnant and breastfeeding representation.
- 16 and 17 November: DIA-MHRA Global Summit held during London Life Sciences Week.
- 25 November 2026: WHO knowledge portal launch reported.
Big Picture
The portal extends the work of the Special Programme of Research, Development and Research Training in Human Reproduction (HRP), which has sponsored reproductive health research since its establishment. It marks a shift from documenting the exclusion of pregnant and breastfeeding women toward providing practical guidance for including them in trials.
Clinicians and patients who rely on treatment decisions during pregnancy or breastfeeding face gaps in trial evidence that this effort aims to narrow. Future trials that include these groups may give clearer guidance on medication and vaccine safety in these periods.
The takeaway
Pregnant and breastfeeding women are often left out of the research that informs their own care. Asking whether a trial includes them is a useful question for patients and clinicians weighing treatment options.
