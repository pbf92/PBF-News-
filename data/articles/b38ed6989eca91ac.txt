TITLE: Review: Decades of Divas pays tribute to great female vocalists
OUTLET: Calgary Herald

Decades of Divas, Stage West’s holiday show, is one of its trademark concert musicals, this time featuring tributes to some of the top female pop vocalists of the past seven decades.
In the capable hands of director Lee Siegel and choreographer Stephan Dickson, the show sparkles as it should considering the list of great ladies it covers. Siegel and Dickson make certain the stage is always awash with energy and movement. Highlighting soloists could have made for static staging, but that is far from the case here.
Dickson uses the ensemble to back most of the soloists, and he has them dancing during the bulk of their songs, and it’s not just distraction. The movement and dance complement the songs, and remind us that movement was always an important part of these divas’ performances.
Sean D. Ellis’ set features video panels of all shapes and sizes as well as several levels, and Siegel makes good use of them with his projections. Sometimes he uses poignant videos, but at other times the stage is just awash with colour and shapes. He also wisely uses the levels so that the stage feels bigger than it is. David Smith’s lighting puts the finishing touches on the presentation.
In creating this show, Jason Pechet and Troy Goldthorp walk a tightrope. They had to choose which women to feature, leaving out some big names. Where are Diana Ross, Lady Gaga, Billie Holiday, Dolly Parton, Tina Turner and Dusty Springfield?
Even trickier was selecting the songs. How, for instance, can you ignore that Celine Dion sang My Heart Will Go On? If you are not going to feature it, at least have Alicia Barban exit to strains of that Titanic song. And you can’t ignore that Whitney Houston sang I Will Always Love You. It is her trademark song. Here’s an idea that will get Dolly on stage: she wrote that song. Let Vanessa Cobman as Whitney sing it with Hannah Smart or Jo Marillier impersonating Dolly.
Cobman has the daunting assignment of channelling not only Whitney but Aretha Franklin, Mariah Carey, Beyonce and Donna Summer. She definitely has the voice to do these legends justice, and she gets laughs when her Mariah gets decked out in holiday clothes to sing her Christmas ditty, All I Want for Christmas.
The show needs more humour, like the little bit of camp Barban brings to her version of Celine singing It’s All Coming Back to Me Now, complete with exaggerations of those famous hand gestures. Barban also teams up with Tristan Hernandez to sing Beauty and the Beast. Barban is great with the one-liners she has been given for Streisand and Adele, and Pechet and Goldthorp needed to give her a few more. Where is Streisand’s iconic “Life’s too short. Start with dessert!” especially at a dinner theatre?
Sarah Horsman does a dynamite Cher, Shania, Madonna and Britney, with help from Benjamin Toner’s wild costumes and Rachelle Quinn’s even wilder wigs. There is no question Horsman has studied videos of her divas, especially Madonna, because she has the movements down pat.
There’s a clever twist near the end of the show when Horsman’s Britney spars with Ana Persijn Alarcon’s Christina Aguilera. These graduates of the Mickey Mouse Club were tabloid darlings for much of the 2000s. Whether or not the rivalry was real, it was great to have them clash on stage.
Benjamin Doncom, Hernandez, Jason Lemmon and Aran Wilson-McAnally pay tribute to Elton John, Freddie Mercury, David Bowie and George Michael, acknowledging that men can be divas, too, but Pechet and Goldtrop are probably already working on that show.
Stage West is blessed to have Konrad Pluta as musical director for these shows. He knows how to add the necessary zing to these old favourites, and his bandmates, Jeremy Coates, Jeff Fafard and Carl Janzen, are some of the finest musicians in Calgary.
Decades of Divas works because of the talent Stage West has assembled backstage as well as on stage. It runs at the dinner theatre until Jan. 31.
