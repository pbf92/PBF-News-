TITLE: Do generative AI chatbots align with high-level evidence in dentistry? A comparison with Cochrane systematic reviews
OUTLET: Nature

Abstract
Aims/Objectives
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to support clinical decision-making; however, their alignment with high-level evidence remains uncertain. This cross-sectional methodological study evaluated the agreement between responses generated by ChatGPT, Gemini, and DeepSeek and Cochrane systematic reviews with meta-analyses in dentistry, and assessed short-term response stability.
Methods
The study was reported according to the Chatbot Assessment Reporting Tool (CHART) guideline. Eligible reviews included meta-analyses reporting dichotomous primary outcomes and objective clinical recommendations enabling a clearly defined direction of effect. Standardized PICO-based prompts were submitted to ChatGPT (GPT-4), Gemini (1.5 Flash), and DeepSeek (R1-V3.2-Exp) at three time points using independent chat sessions. Responses were classified as complete agreement, partial agreement, or complete disagreement with the reference standard, and stability was analyzed descriptively.
Results
Eleven systematic reviews generated 99 chatbot interactions. Gemini and DeepSeek demonstrated complete short-term response stability (100%), whereas ChatGPT produced consistent responses in 72.7% of scenarios. Complete agreement with the reference standard was observed in 45.5% of clinical scenarios for ChatGPT and Gemini and in 27.3% for DeepSeek, while complete disagreement occurred in 0%, 9.1%, and 27.3% of clinical scenarios, respectively. Main discrepancies involved omission of clinically relevant information, incomplete reporting of evidence certainty, and overestimation of treatment effects.
Discussion
Although LLMs demonstrated high short-term stability, their agreement with dental evidence varied considerably. ChatGPT showed the lowest frequency of complete disagreement, whereas DeepSeek had the highest.
Conclusions
These findings indicate that current LLMs may assist evidence retrieval and clinical education but require professional verification before clinical application.
