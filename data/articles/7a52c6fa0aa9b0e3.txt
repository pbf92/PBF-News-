TITLE: One Meta ad can now carry up to 5 existing posts
OUTLET: PPC Land

Advertisers who turn already-published posts into Meta ads can now place up to five of them in a single ad, according to a LinkedIn post published today by Bram Van der Hallen, who works in digital marketing at Edge.be and writes a newsletter on Meta advertising. Meta then decides which of the five each person sees. The Ads Manager notice shown in the post puts it in one line: "Now you can select up to 5 posts, and we'll show each person the one they're most likely to engage with." Until now, the "Use existing posts" route allowed one post per ad, so promoting five posts meant building five ads. The post does not cite a Meta announcement, and it gives no rollout date, market list or eligibility rules.
In Short
Meta now lets advertisers put up to five already-published posts into one ad, instead of building one ad per post, and its system decides which post each person sees. That matters to anyone who pays to push organic posts to a wider audience, because the choice of which post runs moves from the advertiser to Meta's prediction of engagement. You get fewer ads to build and manage, but the post does not say whether reports show how each of the five posts performed, or whether the pick still follows engagement when a campaign is set up for sales or leads.
What the post says
Van der Hallen opened with a single line: "NEW Meta Ads update: You can now add up to 5 existing posts to a single ad." The second paragraph set out the mechanism. "When using existing posts in your ads, Meta now allows you to select up to 5 different posts within the same ad," the post reads. "Meta will then automatically show each person the post they're most likely to engage with."
The third paragraph described what came before. "Until now, when choosing 'Use existing posts' instead of creating a new ad, you could only select one specific post per ad," Van der Hallen wrote. "If you wanted to promote 5 different organic posts, you had to create 5 separate ads."
The case for the change came last, and it was hedged. The setup "could potentially boost results from your promoted posts as delivery would be more personalized, based on predicted engagement," according to the post, which adds that it "also means less manual work when boost organic content and an overall simpler campaign setup." No figures support either point. The post offers no test results, no Meta performance estimate and no comparison with five separate ads.
By this evening, the post had drawn 192 reactions, 23 comments and 15 reposts.
What Ads Manager shows
The Ads Manager view Van der Hallen published, under the heading "Select up to 5 existing posts," sits in the Ad creative section of Meta's ad setup, which the interface describes with the line "Select and optimize your ad text, media and enhancements." Inside it, a field labelled Posts carries a counter reading "1 of 5 posts selected." Below the selected post sit a "Select posts" button and an "Enter post ID" link. Meta's notice, marked with a small lightning icon, reads: "Now you can select up to 5 posts, and we'll show each person the one they're most likely to engage with."
The rest of the section shows how much automation now sits beside a plain choice of posts. Directly under the posts field is an entry for Advantage+ creative video generation, with a "View terms" link and a prompt: "Add generated videos to diversify your creative. We'll tailor each video to fit different placements." A checkbox labelled "Ad includes media created or edited with AI" carries the note "Checking this box may add an AI info label to your ad." Further down, a line reads "Advantage+ creative enhancements (1/2)", next to an Edit button.
In the example Van der Hallen shared, a single post is selected. The interface therefore confirms the five-post ceiling and the wording of Meta's notice, but it does not show an ad with several posts loaded, how they rotate, or any results.
How the option works
According to the post and the Ads Manager view it shows, the setup has five elements:
- Route. The option applies when an advertiser chooses "Use existing posts" rather than creating a new ad, in the post's wording.
- Location. The posts sit in the "Posts" field of the "Ad creative" section of the ad.
- Selection. Posts are added through the "Select posts" button or by entering a post ID. A counter tracks the slots used, shown as "1 of 5 posts selected" in the example.
- Limit. Up to five posts can be selected in one ad. The previous limit was one.
- Delivery. With several posts selected, Meta shows each person the post it predicts that person is most likely to engage with. The advertiser picks the five; Meta picks among them, person by person.
Five ads into one: why the count matters
Paying to push content that already exists is one of the oldest forms of social advertising. Paid amplification buys distribution rather than production, and on Facebook and Instagram its everyday form is the boosted post. The new option does not change what is being bought. It changes how many ads it takes.
Why does the number of ads matter? Because Meta has rationed them. In September 2020, Facebook said it would enforce per-Page ad limits from February 2021, tiered by a Page's highest monthly spend in the previous year: 250 ads below $100,000, 1,000 up to $1 million, 5,000 up to $10 million and 20,000 above that. Every ad running or in review counted toward the limit, and each dynamic creative ad counted as one. Facebook justified the cap by saying that four in ten running ads failed to exit the learning phase, and it recommended six or fewer creatives per ad set.
Under those rules, five boosted posts run as five ads took five slots. The post does not say whether a single ad holding five posts counts as one ad, as a dynamic creative did. If it does, a Page in the 250-ad tier could promote five times as many posts within the same allowance. That is PPC Land's reading of the 2021 rules, not a statement from Meta or from Van der Hallen.
The cap itself may be loosening. In September, Van der Hallen reported an Ads Manager message telling one advertiser that the number of ads a Page can run was no longer limited, in an account running 286 ads against a 250 ceiling. Meta had not announced a change, and the same interface still showed the account as 36 ads over its limit.
Meta's current advice points toward fewer, larger units. A Meta for Business post on October 21 said that ad sets with fewer than about 50 conversions a week cost about 28% more per purchase in Meta's own analysis, and it pushed advertisers toward consolidation, without disclosing a sample or method. Folding five post ads into one fits that direction.
Something else shifts, though. Five separate ads in one ad set were never guaranteed equal delivery; how the budget spread across them was already up to Meta's system. The difference now is where the choice sits. It moves inside a single ad, and with it goes the ad-level, post-by-post view that five separate ads produced.
Engagement as the deciding signal
The notice names one criterion: the post each person is "most likely to engage with." Van der Hallen's text repeats it, describing delivery "based on predicted engagement." That is a narrower promise than it may look. Advertisers promote organic posts in campaigns set up for traffic, leads and sales as well as engagement, and the post does not say whether the choice among five posts follows the ad set's performance goal in those cases, or engagement every time.
Ahmed Sultan, a senior media buyer and performance marketing strategist, made the point in the comments. "Five posts in one ad means Meta picks the winner, and you learn less about why it won," Sultan wrote. "Fine for boosting organic. In lead gen I'd keep them apart a little longer." The post that draws engagement and the post that produces a lead a sales team closes are rarely the same, Sultan argued, before ending: "Engagement is applause. A qualified lead is a ticket sold."
Naman Khetawat, co-founder of Balistro, called it a "Useful update" and wrote that "Running one ad per boosted post was always a pain." Khetawat's doubt was about the selection itself: "Curious if Meta actually picks the best post per person or just pushes the one with the most likes."
Meta makes such choices at very large scale. Andromeda, the retrieval engine Meta introduced in December 2024, narrows tens of millions of eligible ads to a few thousand candidates per request before ranking. In October, Meta said a second-generation version had lifted Facebook conversions 2.4% since 2025, without giving a baseline or method, and said its generative tools had helped advertisers create hundreds of millions of new ads. A five-post ad hands Meta the same kind of choice using material that already exists, rather than new creative.
Not everyone has been persuaded that handing over such choices pays off. In November 2025, Van der Hallen himself questioned Andromeda-era campaign consolidation, arguing that reach grew without a matching gain in impact. On November 9 this year, Liz DeAngelis of Brainlabs warned that as Meta takes over more creative decisions, brands may not know what worked, with the learnings staying inside the platform.
Can advertisers see which post won?
The first comment under the post asked what most buyers would ask. "But can we see which ad actually performed?" wrote Putera Dewi Kusuma of Unithree. Gil David, who runs ads at High Voltage Digital, replied: "it's logical to see that but remember this is Meta, they'll probably hide it away." The post does not answer the question, and the interface view it shows covers setup only, not reporting.
Meta has answered it before for a related format. In July 2025, it introduced a Creative breakdown in Ads Manager for flexible format ads, which take up to 10 images and videos, with reach, impressions, cost per result and amount spent shown for each asset. Dynamic creative ads were excluded from that breakdown. Whether the same breakdown, or any per-post view, covers an ad built from several existing posts is not stated. Without one, results for five posts would arrive as a single ad-level line.

Boosting what already exists, across platforms
Meta is not alone in selling reach for posts already published. TikTok's Spark Ads run organic posts as ads, and since January TikTok has been phasing out Custom Identity, so new campaigns must run from verified accounts linked through TikTok Business Center. In July, X launched Mentions Boost, which lets Premium Business accounts pay to widen the reach of other users' posts that mention the brand, without paying the original poster.
Creator content is where the larger sums sit. Meta's partnership ads, which run under a creator's name alongside the brand's, passed a $15 billion annual revenue run rate after growing more than 97% year on year, chief financial officer Susan Li said on the third-quarter call on October 21. A Meta white paper dated October 7 claimed partnership ads cut the median cost per incremental purchase by 45% across 20 EMEA conversion lift studies, at 92% confidence. Van der Hallen's post refers to an advertiser's own organic posts. Whether creator posts used in partnership ads can be grouped five to an ad is not addressed.
