TITLE: Systematic Review Identifies Severe Shortage of Validated Spondyloarthritis Screening Tools
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Patients living with spondyloarthritis frequently face prolonged periods of being underdiagnosed before receiving appropriate clinical care. In response to these persistent diagnostic delays, researchers conducted a comprehensive systematic literature review to identify and evaluate all available screening and referral instruments developed for patients with spondyloarthritis. The investigation focused specifically on instruments designed for deployment across both the general population and diverse hospital-based settings, seeking to clarify how clinicians can detect the condition earlier.
The investigative team searched three major biomedical databases, querying Medline, EMBASE, and the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews from their inception through May 2026. The study tracked all screening tools and specialized questionnaires targeted at identifying suspected spondyloarthritis cases, mapping the identification and exclusion stages through a standardized PRISMA flow diagram.
To rigorously evaluate the methodological quality of the retrieved research and examine potential risks of bias, the authors applied the MethodologicAl Standards for Epidemiological Research scale. This standardized instrument, known as the MASTER scale, provided an objective framework to assess each candidate study across thirty-six distinct methodological items.
The initial database query yielded 325 articles, from which the team removed 34 duplicate records before proceeding to the preliminary assessment stage. Investigators then screened the titles and abstracts of the remaining 291 publications, leading to the exclusion of 154 papers that did not meet the thematic criteria. This left 137 articles eligible for full-text examination, from which 87 were selected for subsequent detailed evaluation.
Following comprehensive full-text review, the investigators determined that only five individual studies satisfied all predetermined inclusion criteria for final analysis.
Furthermore, the review noted that most existing instruments have been designed specifically for pre-selected patient cohorts with related conditions, such as psoriasis or chronic inflammatory bowel disease, rather than for broad general population screening. This leaves primary care clinicians with few validated instruments for unselected patient groups.
The review concluded that effective screening and referral mechanisms are essential tools enabling primary care practitioners to recognize suspected spondyloarthritis and direct patients toward specialist rheumatologists. Given the paucity of available instruments, the authors emphasized that substantial additional work is required, highlighting that future research must prioritize proper clinical validation and rigorous cross-cultural translation to optimize early detection and improve long-term referral strategies.
