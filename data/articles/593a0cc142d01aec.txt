TITLE: Multiple-Station Lymph Node Spread Links to Worse Resected Lung Cancer Survival
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and meta-analysis evaluating the ninth-edition tumor staging framework found that multiple-station pathological N2 involvement is associated with poorer overall survival and worse recurrence outcomes than single-station involvement following curative-intent resection for non-small cell lung cancer. The analysis synthesized published observational cohort data to examine the prognostic distinctions between these two anatomical nodal subgroups in resected disease.
In the ninth edition of the staging system, pathological N2 disease is subdivided into single-station involvement, designated pN2a, and multiple-station involvement, designated pN2b. Although guidelines established this structural division, the magnitude and consistency of differences in survival following surgical resection had remained uncertain across clinical literature, prompting an exhaustive quantitative assessment of post-resection clinical cohorts.
To clarify these survival differences, investigators performed systematic literature searches across five major electronic databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, the Web of Science Core Collection, Scopus, and the Cochrane Library. The database queries specifically covered studies published from January 1, 2023, through October 6, 2026. The researchers systematically evaluated the potential risk of bias across all eligible cohort studies using the Quality In Prognosis Studies assessment tool.
Study eligibility criteria restricted the meta-analysis to clinical cohorts comparing station-defined pN2b with pN2a disease in adult patients diagnosed with non-metastatic M0 non-small cell lung cancer who had pathologically confirmed N2 involvement following curative-intent surgical resection. Across the retrieved medical literature, ten retrospective cohorts met all required inclusion criteria, contributing 3,809 total patients, comprising 2,310 individuals with pN2a disease and 1,499 individuals with pN2b disease.
The primary endpoint was overall survival, while secondary endpoints included recurrence-free survival, disease-free survival, and freedom from recurrence. Hazard ratios were pooled using random-effects inverse-variance models. In the primary overall survival analysis, four studies contributed multivariable-adjusted hazard ratios, demonstrating poorer survival with pN2b, with a pooled hazard ratio of 1.58, a 95 percent confidence interval of 1.40 to 1.77, and an I-squared value of 0 percent.
An analysis incorporating all eight studies reporting overall survival yielded a similar association, showing a pooled hazard ratio of 1.50 with a 95 percent confidence interval from 1.37 to 1.65 and an I-squared value of 0 percent. Secondary recurrence outcomes also favored single-station disease, with pooled hazard ratios of 1.49 across five studies for recurrence-free survival, 1.39 across two studies for disease-free survival, and 1.70 across two studies for freedom from recurrence.
