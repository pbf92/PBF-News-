What matters today
CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler
CrowdStrike signed a merger agreement dated Nov 22 to acquire Zscaler, according to its Form 8-K. Each Zscaler share will be exchanged for 1.135 CrowdStrike shares. The company put the enterprise value at approximately $43.9 billion and the implied value at approximately $260.41 per Zscaler share, a premium of approximately 34% to Zscaler’s Nov 20 close.
After closing, CrowdStrike holders would own about 84.1% of the company and Zscaler holders about 15.9%. Holders of about 34% of Zscaler stock signed voting agreements. CrowdStrike targets about $1 billion in annualized revenueRevenue is total sales; operating profit is what the core business earns after costs and operating expenses; net income is the bottom line after interest, taxes and everything else. synergies within about 30 months and $500 million in cost synergies within 18 to 24 months after closing. The deal needs Zscaler shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike’s fiscal 2028. Zscaler would owe a $1.4 billion termination fee in specified cases.
CrowdStrike’s fiscal third-quarter results came out in a separate filing:
- Revenue and ARR: revenue $1.58 billion, up 28%; ending annual recurring revenue $6.26 billion, up 27%; net new ARR $419.2 million, which the company called a record.
- Profit: GAAPThe official accounting rules U.S. companies follow in their financial statements. Many companies also report 'non-GAAP' (adjusted) figures that exclude items such as one-time costs, so the two should be read separately. net income $24.9 million, against a loss of $26.8 million a year earlier; non-GAAP EPSNet income ÷ shares. The headline number in earnings reports, compared with the consensus estimate to judge a 'beat' or 'miss'. $0.34, against $0.24.
- Cash: free cash flowCash generated by operations minus capital spending. It's the money left for dividends, buybacks or paying down debt — often seen as a truer measure than accounting profit. $474.4 million, against $295.9 million.
- Guidance: fiscal 2027 revenue of $6,078.1 million to $6,084.1 million and non-GAAP EPS of $1.28; net new ARR growth guidance raised by 1,060 basis points to 45% at the midpoint.
In a Monday morning report, CNBC said CrowdStrike shares were down more than 7% in early trading and Zscaler up more than 15% premarket. The two companies hold a joint call at 8:00 AM ET.
Nvidia: Trump wants Huang back, investigations widen
President Trump posted on Truth Social on Sunday night: “I hope the Board of Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang. It would be the best thing for the Company, and also, for the U.S.A.” Nvidia’s board ruled out reinstating Huang on Nov 17. On CBS’s Face the Nation on Sunday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent did not back a return and said, “I think we have to see where the buck stops.”
Pressure from investigators has not eased. Reuters reported on Nov 20 that SEC enforcement staff were examining potential accounting issues at Nvidia; the SEC declined to comment. California Attorney General Rob Bonta said in a Bloomberg interview that the state’s probe of Nvidia’s chip sales to China covers possible criminal violations of state law.
Nvidia fell 14.01% on Friday to $125.53 and lost 22.92% for the week, which several outlets called its worst week since 2008. CNBC listed it up 9.7% premarket as of 6:02 AM ET.
Why Friday was so bad
CNBC tied Friday’s drop to profit-taking on the AI rally after Anthropic’s IPO, the report on Nvidia’s accounting, worries about AI spending and the risk of another Fed hike; it also noted midterm results had raised doubts about the AI buildout. The PHLX Semiconductor Index fell 10.73% on the day and 17.13% for the week. CNBC said the Nasdaq 100 hit bear-market territory intraday, 20% below its recent high, and the Nasdaq Composite turned negative for the year.
The S&P 500 closed 11.62% below its 52-week closing high of 7,818.93. Breadth was lopsided: on the NYSE, 1,774 stocks fell and 276 rose.
Asia split; Korea leaned on its rescue plan
Tokyo was closed for a holiday. Seoul’s KOSPI fell 3.31% to 4,708.54; a sell-side sidecar, which briefly halts program selling, was triggered at 11:33 AM local time. Samsung Electronics closed down 3.06% and SK Hynix down 2.52%. Korea’s finance minister held an emergency Sunday meeting and the government readied its stock market stabilization fund, Yahoo Finance and Korea Times reported. Hong Kong’s Hang Seng slipped 0.21%.
Why it matters for U.S. investors
- Sector gaps were wide. Technology (XLK) fell 7.27% on Friday while consumer staples (XLP) rose 0.67%. Funds that track the tech-heavy Nasdaq 100 lost more than broad or defensive funds last week, so whether this morning’s chip rebound holds matters most for them.
- Bonds cushioned Friday, not this morning. The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.92% on Friday from 5.04%. Early Monday it was back at 4.959%. If yields keep rising while stocks rebound, rate-sensitive holdings such as bond funds and dividend stocks could come under pressure.
- A Fed hike is close to fully priced. With CME FedWatch at about 96% for a quarter-point December hike, per CNBC, a hot PCE reading on Wednesday could add to pressure on growth stocks; a soft one could ease it.
- An all-stock deal shifts ownership. Zscaler holders would own about 15.9% of the combined company, so existing CrowdStrike holders’ stake is diluted to about 84.1%. CrowdStrike shares were down more than 7% in early trading after the announcement, CNBC said.
