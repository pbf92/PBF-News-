TITLE: Laparoscopic TAP Block Speeds Procedure Time with Similar Analgesia to Ultrasound
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis reveals that laparoscopic-assisted transversus abdominis plane block shortens block completion time while providing postoperative analgesia broadly similar to ultrasound-guided placement in patients undergoing laparoscopic colorectal surgery.
Transversus abdominis plane blocks are widely utilized as a component of multimodal analgesia regimens to manage pain following laparoscopic colorectal procedures. Although laparoscopic-assisted block administration serves as a practical alternative to traditional ultrasound-guided techniques, the comparative clinical effectiveness and perioperative recovery outcomes between these two interventional modalities have remained uncertain across surgical practice.
To resolve this clinical uncertainty, investigators conducted a systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials following the PRISMA 2020 reporting guidelines and registering the protocol in PROSPERO under registration CRD420261376522. The research team searched five comprehensive electronic bibliographic databases from their inception through April 1, 2026, without language restrictions, querying PubMed and MEDLINE via native PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, Embase, and Google Scholar to identify eligible comparative investigations. Methodological quality and bias were formally assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool, while the overall certainty of evidence across each outcome measure was evaluated using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework.
The final quantitative synthesis incorporated six randomized controlled trials enrolling an aggregate population of 760 patients. For the predefined primary outcome of cumulative 24-hour opioid consumption measured in morphine-milligram equivalents across five trials with 585 patients, the pooled effect estimate demonstrated no statistically significant difference between laparoscopic assistance and ultrasound guidance, yielding a mean difference of minus 4.47 milligrams with a 95 percent confidence interval from minus 10.16 to 1.23 milligrams and a p-value of 0.12. However, statistical heterogeneity across these trials was extreme, displaying an I-squared value of 96 percent and a Tau-squared of 38.64, which renders the true magnitude and precision of this primary consumption estimate highly uncertain.
To investigate the source of this substantial heterogeneity, the authors conducted reviewer-requested exploratory sensitivity analyses. Excluding the individual study by Zaghiyan and colleagues reduced observed heterogeneity to an I-squared value of approximately 75 percent while preserving the non-significant direction of the pooled treatment effect. Furthermore, conducting an additional sensitivity analysis that excluded randomized trials assessed at high overall risk of bias under Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 did not materially alter the overall clinical conclusion. Parallel evaluations of postoperative pain scores likewise showed no statistically significant differences between the two techniques across the assessed time points.
Evaluating technical efficiency and perioperative recovery metrics revealed that laparoscopic-assisted administration significantly accelerated procedural delivery, shortening overall block completion time by a statistically significant mean difference of minus 6.81 minutes, with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning minus 8.91 to minus 4.70 minutes compared to ultrasound guidance. In contrast, other broader perioperative recovery measures showed no significant divergence between the approaches; pooled analyses identified no statistically significant differences in total operative time, overall hospital length of stay, or the incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting.
Overall certainty of evidence ranged widely from moderate to very low across the evaluated surgical outcomes under the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation criteria. Because the pooled effect estimate for 24-hour opioid requirements carries marked uncertainty stemming from extreme statistical heterogeneity and a limited total number of randomized trials, the investigators concluded that surgical teams should base their selection of block technique on institutional workflow, available resources, and individual clinical expertise rather than an expectation of superior pain relief.
