TITLE: Stock Market Today: Nasdaq Slides 2.5% as Nvidia Turmoil and Chip Restrictions Hit Tech
OUTLET: Eurasia Business News

By William Collins, consultant in stock markets – Eurasia Business News, November 16, 2026. Article no 3271
U.S. stocks fell sharply Monday, November 16, as Nvidia’s leadership crisis, tighter chip-export restrictions and interest-rate concerns rattled investors. The Nasdaq Composite declined 2.49%, the S&P 500 lost 1.92%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 739.38 points. Nvidia closed 4.12% lower ahead of Tuesday’s closely watched earnings report.
Selling extended beyond equities. Oil settled more than 3% lower as recovering Middle Eastern exports reinforced expectations of excess supply. Gold touched its lowest level in more than a year, while Bitcoin remained volatile following a weekend plunge toward $62,000.
Stock Market Today: Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq Closing Results
Monday’s closing figures confirmed a difficult start to a consequential earnings week:
The S&P 500 and Nasdaq recorded their third consecutive losing sessions. Technology weakness drove the retreat, but the Dow’s substantial decline showed that concern extended beyond the semiconductor industry.
Wall Street’s VIX volatility index surged approximately 19%, according to CNN. The increase reflected demand for protection and heightened uncertainty—not a prediction of any particular outcome for Tuesday’s session.
Nvidia Stock Falls as Board Rejects Huang Comeback
Nvidia shares finished at $156.14, down $6.71, or 4.12%. CNN described the close as the company’s lowest since July 1, 2025, following its worst weekly performance in 19 months.
The leadership dispute remained central. Although Jensen Huang’s allies have organized calls, open letters and other efforts supporting his return, the board is not considering reinstating him, according to The Wall Street Journal.
President Donald Trump said Monday that he “would not mind” Huang returning. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, however, advocated recruiting an outsider and called for a substantial cultural overhaul. Those competing positions underscore political disagreement; they do not determine Nvidia’s succession decision.
Colette Kress remains interim CEO while the board pursues its search. Investors now must assess commercial performance alongside uncertainty about permanent leadership.
U.S. Tightens Rules on Overseas AI-Chip Access
The Commerce Department announced restrictions designed to address Chinese customers’ remote access to advanced American chips through overseas data centers. Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore and Brazil will face additional export restrictions, with licenses required for covered shipments. U.S. technology companies could receive exceptions.
The mechanism matters. Rather than describing the measure simply as a universal prohibition on remote computing, the Journal reported shipment-licensing requirements intended to restrict an overseas-access route.
For Nvidia and its customers, the financial consequences will depend on licensing decisions, exemptions and implementation. Monday’s announcement nevertheless increased uncertainty immediately before the company’s results, adding regulatory risk to the leadership concerns already weighing on its valuation.
Warsh Discusses AI Risks Without Giving Rate Guidance
Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh said staff were modeling scenarios exploring how AI could reshape the economy, including possible worker displacement and consequences from disappointing stock-market performance.
His prepared remarks did not provide a near-term interest-rate forecast. Warsh also stressed that the scenarios were illustrative rather than predictions. Consequently, the speech should not be presented as an announcement of another rate increase.
Even so, investors were already confronting expectations of restrictive policy after last week’s inflation releases. Monday’s decline therefore combined company-specific disruption with a broader reassessment of growth, borrowing costs and the potential economic consequences of AI investment.
Oil Prices Today: Brent and WTI Settle Sharply Lower
Crude oil weakened as improving export flows competed with continuing geopolitical hazards:
- Brent crude futures settled at $92.44 per barrel, down $3.40, or approximately 3.6%.
- WTI crude futures finished at $85.72, declining $2.84, or approximately 3.2%.
Reuters attributed the retreat to signs that shipments through the Strait of Hormuz were recovering, reinforcing expectations of a global supply surplus. Fresh tanker incidents nevertheless demonstrated that regional shipping risks had not disappeared.
These settlement prices supersede earlier intraday quotations. They show that supply-recovery expectations outweighed geopolitical support during Monday’s completed trading session.
Gold Price According to Kitco
Gold price touched $3,830.74 per troy ounce Monday, its lowest since October 2, 2025. That figure was a session low, not a final closing quotation.
Kitco’s earlier U.S. morning report placed spot gold near $3,872.70, down 0.28%, and silver near $47.260, down 2.82%. Elevated Treasury yields, dollar strength and expectations for tighter monetary policy pressured both metals.

Bitcoin Price: Recovery Does Not Erase Recent Pressure
Bitcoin recovered from a weekend low near $62,000 and traded around $64,000 Monday morning. CNBC displayed approximately $65,211 at 6:06 p.m. Eastern. Cryptocurrency trading continues after the stock-market close, so that later figure is not a standardized daily settlement.
The recovery followed six consecutive weekly declines. CNBC linked the preceding weakness to forced liquidations, deteriorating liquidity and reduced risk appetite, illustrating how crypto’s rebound coexisted with a fragile broader backdrop.
Tuesday’s Earnings Watchlist
The supplied calendar lists Disney, Home Depot, JD.com, FuboTV and Oaktree Specialty Lending before Tuesday’s opening bell. The retrieved company overview pages did not independently establish every release time, so schedules should be checked against issuer announcements.
Nvidia’s results arrive afterward, making Tuesday a test of consumer activity, credit conditions and AI demand. Investors will seek evidence that operating performance can withstand the combination of leadership uncertainty, regulatory change and restrictive financial conditions that dominated Monday’s selloff.
