TITLE: 30,000 churches face charity registration deadline
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The Church of England is seeking more time to register thousands of church charities, warning that the process is proving more challenging than expected.
The churches were previously exempt from registration with the Charity Commission, but in 2021 the regulator announced plans to bring them onto its register by March 2031.
So far, only several hundred have completed the process, with the majority still waiting to be registered.
The Archbishops’ Council told Civil Society it is now making the case for an extension, warning that the new requirements will create challenges for many churches.
It has already held talks with the Charity Commission and plans to meet government officials to discuss the issue.
The deadline was raised in Parliament last month by Labour MP and Second Church Estates Commissioner Marsha de Cordova.
She asked whether the government would consider extending the exemption by another ten years, and raised concerns about the impact on more than 9,000 smaller Church of England parish churches.
She also questioned whether the Charity Commission would receive additional funding and staff to manage the registrations.
In response, civil society minister Vicky Foxcroft said there were no current plans to extend the deadline.
She confirmed that around 30,000 church charities across all Christian denominations would need to register by 31 March 2031.
The requirement is part of charity regulations dating back to 1996. Under charity law, most organisations with an annual income of more than £5,000 must register with the Charity Commission.
However, qualifying churches with an income of £100,000 or less have historically been exempt, although they must still comply with charity law.
The previous registration deadline was March 2021, but the government granted a further 10 years.
At the time, the Charity Commission estimated there were around 35,000 churches needing to register – roughly five times its annual registration capacity. The Covid pandemic had also created additional challenges for smaller churches.
Registration will require churches to provide information about their charitable purposes, trustees, finances and how their activities benefit the public. They will also have ongoing reporting responsibilities.
The Charity Commission has described completing the work as “a substantial programme of work”.
It says the registrations will have significant resource implications and is exploring ways to make the process simpler, including the possibility of a separate digital system for churches.
