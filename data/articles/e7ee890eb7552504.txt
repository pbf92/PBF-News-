TITLE: Dow Drops Nearly 860 Points as Stock Market Selloff Deepens Ahead of Nvidia Earnings
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

U.S. stocks fell Tuesday as investors pulled back ahead of Nvidia's earnings report, while renewed warnings about higher interest rates added pressure to a market already struggling with doubts about the artificial intelligence boom.
The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 859.71 points, or 1.67%, to 50,748.46, according to preliminary closing figures. The S&P 500 lost 75.96 points, or 1.02%, to 7,341.23, while the Nasdaq Composite declined 180.42 points, or 0.74%, to 24,329.34. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq recorded their fourth consecutive declines, their longest losing streaks since September.
The losses followed a bruising Monday session, when the Dow shed about 739 points, the S&P 500 fell 1.9%, and the Nasdaq tumbled 2.5%. Across the two sessions, the Dow has surrendered nearly 1,600 points. Monday's retreat also hit smaller companies, with the Russell 2000 falling 1.7%.
Tuesday's selling extended beyond the technology companies that have dominated market attention. All 11 S&P 500 sectors were lower during afternoon trading. Software shares weighed on the Dow, while Salesforce and UnitedHealth were among the stocks contributing to its decline. The index briefly fell about 1,000 points before recovering some ground.
Nvidia remained the central corporate event on investors' calendars. Its quarterly results, scheduled after the closing bell, carried implications for chipmakers, cloud computing companies and the broader network of businesses tied to AI spending.
The report arrives as investors reassess the industry's momentum. Tuesday's session reflected mounting caution ahead of Nvidia's first earnings release without Jensen Huang leading the company. Anthropic's anticipated stock market debut added another major test of demand for AI investments later this week.
St. Louis Federal Reserve President Alberto Musalem said on Tuesday that the economy would likely require multiple rate increases before spring 2027 to bring inflation under control. He argued that the current benchmark rate, between 3.75% and 4%, could be as much as one percentage point below the level needed to restore price stability.
Higher borrowing costs can squeeze corporate earnings, discourage expansion and make bonds more competitive with stocks. They also affect the value investors assign to future profits, a particularly important consideration for growth companies whose valuations depend on earnings expected years from now.
October retail sales increased 0.2% from the previous month, while sales excluding autos and gasoline rose 0.5%. The figures suggested consumers continued spending, even as investors weighed whether persistent demand could complicate the inflation outlook.
