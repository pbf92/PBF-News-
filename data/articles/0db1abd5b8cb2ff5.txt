TITLE: Prehospital Ceftriaxone Fails to Cut Sepsis Mortality in PITSTOP trial
OUTLET: Contagion Live

What You Need to Know
Hospital mortality was 18.0% with prehospital ceftriaxone vs 18.4% with placebo (RR, 0.97; 95% CI, 0.81-1.17; P = .77), even though antibiotics were given about 3 hours earlier.
All 17 secondary outcomes were similar between groups, including 90-day mortality, ICU admission, and need for mechanical ventilation.
Adverse events related to the study drug were more common with ceftriaxone (6.2% vs 1.5%), and PITSTOP-Antibiotics is now the second randomized trial, after PHANTASi, to find no mortality benefit from prehospital antibiotics.
Looking at Ambulance-Administered Antibiotics for Sepsis
All 17 secondary outcomes were similar between groups.¹ Ninety-day mortality was 27.1% with ceftriaxone vs 25.2% with placebo (RR, 1.07; 95% CI, 0.93-1.24), and ICU admission occurred in 18.9% vs 17.2% (RR, 1.12; 95% CI, 0.93-1.30).¹ Median hospital length of stay was 8 days in both groups.
Acute kidney injury occurred in 37.8% of the ceftriaxone group and 34.2% of the placebo group (RR, 1.10; 95% CI, 0.98-1.24).¹ Vasopressor use within 24 hours was 18.7% vs 20.5% (RR, 0.91; 95% CI, 0.69-1.20).¹
Adverse events related to the study drug were more frequent with ceftriaxone (6.2% vs 1.5%; difference, 4.8%; 95% CI, 3.1%-6.4%).¹ Time from patient contact to study drug was 37.1 minutes with ceftriaxone and 38.9 minutes with placebo. On average, prehospital antibiotics came about 3 hours before in-hospital antibiotics, and resistance was rare.¹
The authors weighed several explanations for the null result, including antibiotic choice or route, enrollment of uninfected patients, and confounding in prior observational data.¹ They noted intramuscular and intravenous ceftriaxone have similar pharmacokinetics, and most enrolled patients had elevated lactate and an ED diagnosis of infection. Antibiotics given 3 hours earlier may simply offer no added benefit.¹
Blood cultures were not drawn before the study drug was given, and culture positivity was lower with ceftriaxone (40.1% vs 52.7%).¹ The investigators also acknowledged the trial was underpowered to detect small but potentially important mortality differences.¹
PITSTOP-Antibiotics is now the second randomized trial, after PHANTASi, to find no mortality benefit with prehospital antibiotics for suspected sepsis.¹,²
