TITLE: Bing search ads show Zoro's 9.3 out of 10 Bizrate rating
OUTLET: PPC Land

A paid listing for the online retailer Zoro on Bing carried an unusual final line: "Overall Customer Satisfaction 9.3 out of 10 - Bizrate.com." The score was not written by the advertiser. It came from Bizrate, a third-party review site, and it sat beneath the ad's headline, description and shipping details inside Bing's sponsored results.
Search Engine Watch published the sighting on November 12, 2026, in a short report by senior reporter Sachin Patel under the headline "Bing shows website ratings from external review sites in ads." PPC News Feed, an aggregator of paid search updates, followed on November 17, 2026, crediting Patel. Neither report includes a statement from Microsoft, and Microsoft has not publicly confirmed a new feature. What the two reports leave out is that Microsoft's own help documentation, in pages last updated in June and July 2026, already describes ads carrying ratings from Bizrate and other review sites, under features it calls consumer ratings and merchant ratings.
In Short
Bing has been seen showing a store's rating from the review site Bizrate inside a search ad, in this case a 9.3 out of 10 score for Zoro. This affects anyone advertising on Bing, because the rating comes from an outside site and the advertiser cannot edit it. Microsoft has not commented on the sighting, but its own help pages already describe automated rating extensions that name Bizrate as a source, so the open question is whether the format is new rather than the feature.
What the ad showed
The screenshot published by both outlets is the only example on record. It shows a single text ad, labelled "Sponsored," in what appears to be a Bing results page for a chair cushion query. The query itself is not stated in either report. Beneath the ad sits a "People also search for" module with queries such as "zoro supplies" and "zoro tools inc," and a right-hand column of related searches beginning "inexpensive chair," "cheapest place to" and "where to purchase."
The ad stacks five layers of text beneath the advertiser name and URL. Two of them were supplied by Bing rather than by the advertiser, judging by their format.
The rating line is notable for its scale. Bizrate's figure is expressed out of 10, not as stars. That matters for reasons that become clear in Microsoft's documentation, which describes one of its two rating features in terms of stars and the other without stating any scale at all.
Two reports, one example
The original report is brief. According to Search Engine Watch, "Bing is showing third-party website ratings, including ratings sourced from Bizrate, within its sponsored search results." Patel wrote: "Bing sponsored results are showing website ratings sourced from third-party sites. In the example I spotted, Bizrate was the source of the rating shown for the website in the ad." The piece then offered one line of interpretation: "Showing a rating like this can be helpful for website owners. A positive rating can build trust and interest with users, and that may help turn them into customers."
PPC News Feed's version, published five days later, used firmer wording. "Bing's sponsored results are now showing website ratings from third-party reviews," it stated. "An example observed featured Bizrate as the rating source for a website ad." The aggregator attributed a purpose to the change - "The inclusion of ratings such as these aims to aid website owners by potentially boosting consumer trust and engagement" - although no Microsoft source is cited for that aim. It also connected the sighting to an earlier one: "Notably, this change follows the October 2026 sighting of consumer review extensions in Bing Ads. Bing continues to explore ways to enhance ad relevance and trustworthiness by leveraging external ratings." It filed the item under Ecommerce, Lead Generation, Microsoft Advertising and Search.
Both reports describe the rating as a website rating. That distinction is meaningful. A website or store rating scores the merchant as a whole, covering delivery, service and checkout. A product rating scores the item. The Zoro example is a store-level score attached to an ad for a single product category.
Neither report states when the ad was seen, in which market, on which device, or how often the rating line appeared. Neither describes the change as a test or as a rollout, and neither quotes Microsoft.
What Microsoft's documentation already says
Microsoft Advertising groups this kind of enhancement under automated extensions, which its Learn documentation says were "formerly known as annotations." The relevant page was last updated on June 19, 2026, months before the November sighting. According to Microsoft, automated extensions are "automatically applied to your ads if Microsoft Advertising determines that the automated extension can improve your ad performance." The page adds: "All advertisers are eligible for automated extensions; however, they are not always guaranteed to show in the ads." On sourcing, it is explicit: "Certain data, like reviews and ratings, may be offered from third-party providers."
Two automated extensions on that page deal in ratings, and both name Bizrate.
Consumer ratings
According to Microsoft, "Consumer ratings are reviews that are added to eligible ads. When customers select the link for these ratings, they're taken to a third-party review page." The page lists five sources under the line "We base ratings from the following sources": ReviewCentre, ViewPoints, ShopperApproved, Bizrate and MrRebates. The extension applies to search advertising on computers, tablets and mobile devices in the markets Microsoft designates. The documentation gives no rating scale in its text, no minimum number of reviews and no minimum score.
Merchant ratings
The second feature sits under a heading for seller ratings. Microsoft's dedicated page, last updated on July 27, 2026, states: "Merchant ratings are Automated Extensions that display your business rating and link to highly rated reviews in your ads to show what people are saying about your business. Potential customers will be able to see your star rating and view reviews from third-party sources."
Unlike consumer ratings, merchant ratings come with thresholds. According to Microsoft: "Your business must have at least 30 unique reviews, each from the past 12 months, and a composite rating of at least 3.5 stars or higher. However, we may show ratings for merchants with fewer than 30 reviews if we have sufficient data from other sources to determine an accurate rating."
The source lists for merchant ratings are not consistent across Microsoft's own pages. The automated extensions page names 18 sources: Yahoo Shopping, Epinions, bizrate, Trustpilot, PriceGrabber, ViewPoints, Review Centre, Shopper Approved, eKomi, Feefo, Verified Reviews, Mr. Rebates, Ciao.co.uk, Shopzilla UK, Reviews.co.uk, Avis Verifies, Reevoo and Trusted Shop. The dedicated merchant ratings page names 11: Yahoo! Shopping, Epinions, bizrate, TrustPilot, PriceGrabber, ReviewCentre, ShopperApproved, eKomi, Feefo, Verified Reviews and Revoo.com. Bizrate appears on both.
Review extensions
A third feature, review extensions, is different in kind. Advertisers add these themselves. According to Microsoft's page, last updated on June 11, 2026, a review extension lets an advertiser "Share positive reviews from a reputable third-party source about your website in your ads." The same page states: "You can paraphrase or use an exact quote from the source." Each campaign and ad group can carry up to 20 review extensions, they can be set up in all Bing markets and they serve in all Bing markets except China. Bizrate Insights is among the approved review partners. Microsoft draws the line itself: "Review extensions are different from a seller or merchant rating, which is an automated extension."
Read against that table, the Zoro line looks more like a consumer rating than a merchant rating. It shows a numeric score out of 10 rather than stars, it names a single source from the consumer ratings list, and it carries a link to that source, which matches Microsoft's description of consumer ratings sending users to "a third-party review page." That is PPC Land's reading of the documentation, not a statement from Microsoft, which has not said which extension, if either, produced the line.
How merchant ratings are matched and controlled, according to Microsoft
Microsoft's documentation describes the merchant ratings process in five parts.
- Campaign type. According to Microsoft, "Your campaign should be set up as a Search campaign."
- Review volume and score. The business needs at least 30 unique reviews, each from the past 12 months, and a composite rating of at least 3.5 stars, though Microsoft says it may show ratings with fewer than 30 reviews when it has sufficient data from other sources.
- Matching. "We match these reviews to your ad by pairing the domain of the display URL to the advertiser's domain from the review source on Bing." In the Zoro example, the display URL is zoro.com.
- No editing. "Because merchant ratings are provided by third-party sources, you are unable to modify the rating."
- Opting out. "If you want to opt out of this feature, contact your Account Manager or contact support. If you change your mind later, you can always opt back in."
The general opt-out page for automated extensions, last updated on June 11, 2026, describes a self-service route instead. According to that page, an advertiser hovers over Campaigns in the left navigation, selects Ads & extensions, then Automated Extensions, then Manage Automated Extensions, chooses "Opt out of specific Automated Extensions," ticks the extensions to remove and selects Save. The documentation sets two limits on that route: "You can only opt out of Automated Extensions at the account level," and "Reporting is available at the Automated Extension level, and not at each individual feature." The automated extensions page adds that opting out of one extension "will opt out the account from all features under this extension."
The practical result is coarse. An advertiser that wants to remove a rating from one campaign cannot do so without removing it account-wide, and cannot isolate the rating's effect on its click-through rate in Microsoft's reporting, because reporting stops at the extension level.
A run of trust signals on Bing's sponsored results
The Zoro ad carried a second Bing-supplied line that deserves attention: "Site visitors: Over 100K in the past month." That label does not appear in Microsoft's automated extensions documentation. The closest entry is "Previous visits," which according to Microsoft "adds a line of text to your ad that shows customers how many times they have clicked through to your website over the last few months" - a personal history, not a site-wide count.
Site-wide visitor counts have surfaced on Bing before. Search Engine Roundtable reported on October 3, 2023, that Bing appeared to be testing a count of visitors in the past month on search ads, based on a screenshot posted by Khushal Bherwani. PPC News Feed reported on June 5, 2026, that Bing had introduced a "Visits on Site" label on sponsored results, again credited to Bherwani. Neither report cited Microsoft.
Review content followed. Search Engine Roundtable reported on September 30, 2026, that Bing's sponsored results were showing one-line consumer review snippets beneath ads, with the text drawn from a Trustpilot profile according to Bherwani, who spotted it. PPC News Feed's write-up of the same sighting, published on October 3, 2026, is the "October 2026 sighting" its November item refers to. According to PPC News Feed, its example was a Best Buy ad carrying a short customer comment about fast, efficient work. Microsoft's documentation lists a feature called consumer sentiment that shows "relevant reviews with positive comments about your business," without naming any source.
