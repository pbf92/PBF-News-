TITLE: NVIDIA (NASDAQ:NVDA) Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO and Board Director, Shares Jump 31% Premarket After Resignation Reversal
OUTLET: kalkine.com

Key Highlights
- NVIDIA's board unanimously reinstates Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board director.
- Shares rose 31.36% premarket to USD 164.90, after a 14.01% Friday decline.
- Board cites senior U.S. government input; President Trump backs the decision.
- Independent committee's investigation continues; new Chief Compliance Officer search begins.
NVIDIA's Board of Directors voted unanimously on Monday to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, chief executive officer and board member, effective immediately. The decision reverses course after Huang's resignation earlier this month, which followed a series of reports, and returns the company's founder to the top role. The announcement follows NVIDIA's Nov. 17 release of third quarter fiscal 2027 results and its expanded Supply chain commitments to support the AI infrastructure buildout.
Board Votes Unanimously to Reinstate Huang
The board reached its decision after a series of discussions and meetings with Huang, following which directors said they were satisfied with his candor and ability to lead the company. In the process, the board received input from senior levels of the United States government. Based on that input, directors said they are confident the reinstatement poses no national security risk.
Board Chair Harvey C. Jones said the board considers Huang the right leader for NVIDIA and views the reinstatement as serving stockholders, employees, partners and customers. Jones remains chair. He thanked Colette Kress for her service as interim CEO, a role she held alongside her continuing duties as chief financial officer.
Huang, for his part, described returning as an honor and thanked the board for its confidence, Jones for his Leadership as chair, and Kress and the executive staff for running the company during the interim period. He also thanked employees, shareholders, industry leaders and partners, and said he is ready to get back to work.
Premarket Trading Reverses Friday's Slide
NVIDIA (NASDAQ:NVDA) shares rose USD 39.37, or 31.36%, to USD 164.90 in premarket trading on Monday. The stock closed Friday at USD 125.53, down USD 20.45, or 14.01%, from a Thursday close of USD 145.98. The premarket price sits about 13% above that Thursday level, so the move erases Friday's decline and adds to it.
President Trump Backs the Decision
President Donald Trump praised the move in a post on his Social Media site, calling the board's choice "the correct and proper decision." He wrote that the United States needs Huang's leadership for the company and for the American economy.
Trump also stated that the U.S. government has "no reason to believe" Huang's return poses a national security risk, and that it holds no evidence Huang engaged in unlawful conduct. The statement lines up with the board's own account of its input from senior levels of government.
Investigation and Oversight Continue
The reinstatement does not end the board's inquiry. Following reporting that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, the board formed a committee made up solely of independent directors to investigate, and that committee retained independent counsel. The board reiterated that the investigation proceeds to conclusion. Huang supports it and has committed to cooperate.
The board also recently formed a National Security and Export Control Committee, again made up solely of independent directors, to oversee trade compliance and national security risks. That committee is engaging counsel with national security experience, and the counsel retains advisors with expertise in the field. The board announced the committee on Nov. 17, six days before Huang's return.
NVIDIA is also searching for a Chief Compliance Officer, a new position that oversees compliance across the company and reports to the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee. The company continues to cooperate with ongoing government investigations and inquiries.
What the Reinstatement Establishes
NVIDIA's leadership question is settled for now: Huang holds the CEO title, the president title and a board seat again, Kress returns fully to her CFO responsibilities, and Jones stays on as chair. The independent investigation, the national security committee and the compliance officer search remain in place alongside the leadership change, and the stock traded more than 31% higher in premarket trading on Monday.
