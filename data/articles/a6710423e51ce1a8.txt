TITLE: DISH Media sells Sling TV ads through Nexxen to reach regional agencies
OUTLET: PPC Land

Nexxen and DISH Media today announced a partnership that brings advertising inventory from DISH and Sling to buyers on Nexxen's platform. The release, issued through GlobeNewswire at 09:00 Eastern time with a New York dateline, presents the arrangement as access to live sports and entertainment programming for advertisers, and as a new source of demand for DISH Media. Neither company disclosed prices, volumes or a start date.
In Short
DISH Media, the ad sales arm behind the DISH satellite service and the Sling streaming service, is making its ad breaks available to buyers who use Nexxen's ad-buying software. It matters most to regional and independent agencies, the buyers DISH Media says it wants to reach, and to advertisers who want live sports and TV series with information about where each ad will run. What changes is the route: DISH and Sling ad space can now be bought through one more platform, though neither company has said how much of it, at what price or on what terms.
What the two companies announced
The release carries the headline "Nexxen and DISH Media Partner, Expanding Access to Premium TV Inventory and Audiences." Its subheading describes a partnership that "connects advertisers with DISH and Sling inventory," pairing premium programming and what it calls fully transparent bidstream signals with Nexxen's own data. The bidstream is the flow of bid requests sent to buyers during automated auctions, and the data riding on those requests is the part of the release that carries the most weight.
According to the release, Nexxen "announced today a strategic partnership with DISH Media, an indirect subsidiary of EchoStar Corporation. The collaboration brings DISH Media and Sling's premium, brand-safe inventory directly to Nexxen's buyers." Two outcomes are named. Advertisers, including those at regional and independent agencies and brands, gain access to "high-value household audiences across live sports and entertainment programming." DISH Media, for its part, gets what the release calls "a complementary source of demand."
The companies sit on opposite sides of the trade. Nexxen describes itself as an advertising technology platform made up of a demand-side platform and a supply-side platform, "with the Nexxen Data Platform at its core." It is headquartered in Israel, has offices across North America, Europe and Asia-Pacific, and trades on Nasdaq under the ticker NEXN. DISH Media is a seller. Its boilerplate says it "provides advertisers with intelligent solutions to efficiently maximize exposure to desired audiences across DISH and Sling while safeguarding consumer personal information." Sling is a vMVPD, a service that sells bundles of live channels over the internet, while DISH is a satellite pay-TV service, the older MVPD model that controls its own transmission path.
The deal at a glance
How the arrangement works, as described
According to the release, the partnership has four moving parts.
- Supply. DISH Media makes inventory from DISH and Sling available "directly" to buyers on Nexxen's platform. Fedyna states that "Every impression runs on DISH and Sling," which, on its face, keeps the ads inside DISH Media's own services.
- Signals. Metadata travels with each ad opportunity in the bidstream. The release says this "enables more precise visibility into where ads appear, empowering more informed buying decisions while maintaining consumer privacy."
- Data. Buyers apply Nexxen's own data to those opportunities. In the release's words: "By leveraging Nexxen's platform, advertisers can bring the company's insights and intelligence to campaigns across DISH and Sling."
- Demand. DISH Media receives bids from Nexxen's client base, which the release says includes regional and independent agencies.
The release does not say whether the inventory is connected through Nexxen's supply-side platform or offered to its demand-side platform directly. Nor does it say whether the DISH satellite inventory involved is the same set-top box supply DISH Media has offered programmatically since 2023.
Why DISH Media wants a different buyer
Fedyna's quote carries the commercial logic, and it is unusually direct about it.
"Nexxen brings a different kind of demand than we already have access to," said Mike Fedyna, VP, Revenue Strategy and Monetization, DISH Media, according to the release. "Their reach into regional and independent agencies builds on our mid-market business. Every impression runs on DISH and Sling in a brand-safe environment. That means the premium, live programming viewers actively seek out, from live sports to popular TV series across every genre. In a market where quality is hard to verify, that's a real advantage."
Two claims sit in that statement. The first concerns who buys. DISH Media has sold through several large platforms before. In May 2023 it launched DISH Connected, which let advertisers pair Sling TV streaming buys with DISH satellite set-top box inventory, according to TV Technology, with private auction deals running through The Trade Desk, Yahoo DSP and Magnite. Horizon Media, Havas Media, Stagwell and Camelot Strategic Marketing & Media were named as agency partners at the time. In February 2024, DISH Media gave PubMatic's supply path optimization partners access to Sling TV through private marketplace deals, according to PubMatic's announcement, and said it would pass genre or network signals in the bid stream; an executive from dentsu was quoted in that announcement. The agencies attached to those earlier deals are among the largest buyers in the market. Fedyna's case for Nexxen is about smaller ones.
The second claim concerns quality. Fedyna's argument is that every ad sold through the partnership runs on services DISH Media controls, in programming that viewers chose to watch. Questions about where streaming ads actually run are not new. PPC Land reported in September 2026, citing a Peer39 analysis from March 2026, that only 40% of CTV bid requests carried usable program-level signals. A July 2026 survey of 120 senior marketers found that 33% fully trusted most platform-reported CTV performance claims.
The phrase "brand-safe" appears twice, once in the release body and once in Fedyna's quote. Neither explains the standard used. Brand safety in streaming television usually rests on what the seller discloses about the program, the network and the app, which brings the argument back to the metadata.
What Nexxen gets
Ally Appelbaum, VP, Global CTV Partnerships at Nexxen, framed the deal around buyers. "Nexxen was built to connect unique data and premium media in ways that allow advertisers of all sizes to make the most of every campaign," Appelbaum said, according to the release. "By bringing DISH and Sling inventory onto our platform, we're giving buyers a more streamlined way to pair high-quality TV with the audience insights and transparency they need to plan confidently."
The phrase "advertisers of all sizes" lines up with Fedyna's point about regional and independent agencies. Nexxen has been adding that kind of client. In September 2026, the Chicago independent agency Scale Marketing routed more client budget to Nexxen's enterprise DSP, though commercial terms and budgets were not disclosed.
Sling is not entirely new to Nexxen's platform. In its third-quarter results release of November 12, 2026, Nexxen said it had expanded its CTV media footprint through recent integrations with premium partners, naming Disney and Sling TV among them. Today's release puts a name and a counterparty on that integration, and adds DISH to it.
Connected TV is growing faster than the rest of Nexxen's business. According to the company's results release, CTV revenue reached $34.1 million in the third quarter of 2026, up 39% year over year, and accounted for 35% of programmatic revenue, against 27% a year earlier.

Nexxen reaffirmed its full-year 2026 guidance at the same time: Contribution ex-TAC of $388 million to $402 million, programmatic revenue of $380 million to $393 million, and adjusted EBITDA of $122 million to $132 million. In May, with first-quarter results, the company had reported CTV revenue of $29.4 million and raised its Contribution ex-TAC guidance to $382 million to $397 million.
Nexxen's November announcements
The DISH Media release follows two other Nexxen partnership announcements since its third-quarter results, and it is the second in a week to involve information about what is on screen.
On November 19, Nexxen and Gracenote said Gracenote's content-based segments were available through Nexxen's supply-side platform, with segments covering genre, mood, theme, rating, language, cast, and sports and news categories, according to the announcement. That release did not mention DISH or Sling. Whether the Gracenote segments will apply to DISH and Sling inventory is not stated in either document.
The same direction runs through the wider market. Index Exchange began offering show-level impression reporting powered by Gracenote in January 2026, with Spectrum Reach among the first media partners. Gracenote had introduced program-level targeting for streaming TV through Content Connect in December 2025. FreeWheel introduced a series-level CTV reporting tool covering seven publishers in July 2026. Each of these pushes more information about the program into the transaction. The DISH Media release joins them in principle, though not yet in detail.
Live sports and the household pitch
Live sports are named twice in the release, once in the description of audiences and once in Fedyna's quote, which also refers to "the premium, live programming viewers actively seek out." Sling's channel lineups include national sports networks, and DISH Media's 2024 PubMatic announcement named ESPN, NFL Network, FS1, TBS and TNT among the networks available.
Programmatic access to live sport has been expanding across platforms. Magnite said in September 2026 that more than 5,800 advertisers bought live sports through its platform that had not bought it the year before, and that global live sports ad spend on its platform rose 56% year over year from January to July. PubMatic opened a live sports marketplace in July 2025. Nexxen launched its own Nexxen Sports suite in December 2025, according to ExchangeWire, with pre-built packages around events such as the 2026 FIFA World Cup.
The release's other phrase, "high-value household audiences," points to how Nexxen sells. Its data business is built on households: TV viewing data from automatic content recognition, which it has licensed to Yahoo DSP in the US, UK and Germany, and Acxiom household data added to Nexxen Discovery in July 2026. In May 2026, it added TCL and TiVo to its smart TV home screen ad network. DISH and Sling add programming to that mix, and DISH Media's own boilerplate refers to "addressable targeting" and "access to custom audiences on DISH and Sling." How DISH Media's subscriber data and Nexxen's data meet, if at all, is not described.
Why this matters for marketers
For regional and independent agencies, the practical change is access. Inventory that DISH Media has previously sold through private auctions and private marketplace deals on larger platforms becomes available through one more buying tool, one that Nexxen pitches at advertisers "of all sizes." For larger buyers, the change is smaller: DISH and Sling were already reachable through deals on The Trade Desk, Yahoo DSP, Magnite and PubMatic, according to DISH Media's 2023 and 2024 announcements.
The transparency claim is the part that will be tested. "Fully transparent bidstream signals" is a strong phrase, and the release supports it only in general terms. If the metadata includes show titles, genres, networks and live flags at the impression level, buyers would be able to see and report on placements in a way that a large share of CTV supply does not allow.
