TITLE: Manchester United v Brentford - Match Preview, Kick-off time & More
OUTLET: Brentford FC

Brentford travel to Old Trafford on Saturday to face Manchester United in what is Mauricio Pochettino's first home match in charge of the Red Devils (3pm kick-off GMT).
The fixture will not be televised in the UK.
Analysis, team news, match officials and more - here's everything you need to know ahead of the game.
Pre-match analysis
Stephen Gillett, Playmaker Stats: Fast start could be key
Keith Andrews will take charge of his 50th Premier League game as Brentford head coach on Saturday – and what better way to mark the occasion than by guiding the Bees to a first victory at Old Trafford in nearly 90 years?
Having climbed to sixth in the Premier League table courtesy of Monday night’s emphatic 4-0 win over Everton, the Bees will provide the opposition for Mauricio Pochettino’s first home game in charge of Manchester United.
Brentford have lost all five of their Premier League encounters with United at Old Trafford but have come agonisingly close to breaking their duck – most notably in October 2023, when two Scott McTominay goals deep into stoppage-time snatched victory from the west Londoners’ grasp.
Beaten 2-1 on each of their last three visits to the Theatre of Dreams, the Bees will travel north in good spirits having scored 20 goals across their opening 11 Premier League fixtures this season – only Brighton (26) and Manchester City (22) hitting the net more often.
It is also notable that when Brentford click into gear in attack, they are very hard to stop. Andrews’ side have inflicted convincing defeats upon Tottenham Hotspur, Chelsea and Everton this term, with only Brighton (four) recording more top-flight wins by a margin of three or more goals.
Increasingly impressive at the other end of the pitch, the Bees chalked up their fourth Premier League clean sheet of the season against Everton on Monday night, with only four teams – Arsenal, Leeds United, Brighton and Liverpool – having conceded fewer goals than Brentford (11) this term.
A fast start could be key on Saturday against a United side that has yet to score in the opening 15 minutes of a game this season but is susceptible to conceding early goals – as was the case in the 4-0 defeat to Liverpool at Anfield last weekend.
It was the third time in the space of six games that United had shipped four goals, and the Red Devils have kept just one clean sheet across all competitions this season, against Azerbaijani outfit Sabah in the Champions League.
United have conceded 34 goals in 16 games across all competitions this term, more than any other team competing in Europe’s top five leagues, and Pochettino will be keen to shore up his new side defensively. Brentford, however, will look to exploit any frailties.
Two of the Bees’ strikes against Everton on Monday night stemmed from set-pieces – a potential source of joy for Andrews’ side on Saturday given that United have conceded a league-high nine goals from dead-ball situations (including penalties) this term.
It should also encourage the Bees that only Crystal Palace (69) have conceded more shots on target in the Premier League this season than United (63), given that Brentford currently boast the best conversion rate from shots on target in the division (44 per cent).
The expected goals (xG) metric offers a fascinating insight into United’s struggles at both ends of the pitch this season, with the Red Devils conceding shots with an average xG value of 0.16 – the highest in the Premier League.
At the other end of the pitch, the converse is true. Although no team in the division averages more shots per game than United (18.5), the average xG value of those efforts (0.09) is the lowest in the top flight – fewer than seven per cent of their 200-plus shots finding the back of the net.
In Bruno Fernandes, Matheus Cunha and former Bee Bryan Mbeumo, Pochettino has considerable attacking talent at his disposal – and a passionate Old Trafford reception is no doubt in store for the new United boss.
Brentford’s challenge is to dampen that enthusiasm and apply pressure in the right places at the right times – if they do so, Andrews could bring up his half-century in style.
Scout report
Dan Long, Sky Sports: United's 'concerning' Premier League form
In the first half of 2026, it seemed the good times were back at Manchester United.
Club legend Michael Carrick replaced Ruben Amorim in early January, returning to the dugout for the first time since a caretaker spell in late 2021. He immediately had the fans on board after a 2-0 Manchester derby win in his first game.
“I am not getting carried away,” he told Sky Sports. “It is one result, but it needs to be a regular feeling with that level of performance.”
There were 16 Premier League games remaining after that; United won 11 and finished third - their highest position since 2022/23. Crucially, they secured a return to European football after a one-season absence.
Carrick was given a new two-year contract in May.
“Now it’s time to move forward together again, with ambition and a clear sense of purpose. Manchester United and our incredible supporters deserve to be challenging for the biggest honours again,” he said.
Six months on, he is no longer in the role, and the current aims could barely be further from those set out in the summer.
In the Champions League, they have won two and lost two, while they were dumped out of the Carabao Cup at the third-round stage by Brighton in September, having lost 3-2 despite going 2-0 up.
Most concerning, though, is their Premier League form. After 11 games, United sit second-bottom, one point from safety. In five of those games, they have drawn a blank; they scored more than one goal in only three of the others.
Carrick was dismissed on 12 November and Mauricio Pochettino was appointed six days later. The former Tottenham and Chelsea boss signed off his spell as USA chief with a 2-0 win over Haiti in Cincinnati before immediately jetting across the Atlantic to finally take the job he has long been linked with.
The 54-year-old threw himself straight into the role and told Sky Sports he was sleeping only three or four hours per night in the first few days. While jet lag will have played a significant part in that, being as prepared as possible for his first game against Liverpool was imperative.
Unfortunately for him, United were thumped 4-0, which has left them still searching for their first league win since 30 August, when they beat Ipswich 5-2 at Old Trafford.
It would be a brave call to back United for the drop, particularly with an elite new manager in charge and 27 more games to play. But Opta Analyst predicts them to finish 16th, with a current chance of relegation in excess of 27 per cent.
In the Dugout
Mauricio Pochettino
Mauricio Pochettino had a 17-year professional career, which started at Newell’s Old Boys in 1989 and concluded in 2006, after a second permanent spell with Espanyol, for whom he played over 300 times in all competitions. The defender also played for Paris Saint-Germain and Bordeaux, as well as earning 20 senior Argentina caps.
He retired at 34 and took his first job in management with Espanyol aged 36 in January 2009. In the latter half of that season, Pochettino kept the Periquitos in La Liga with a 10th-place finish. He stayed for almost four years before his contract was terminated by mutual consent in November 2012. Less than two months later, he was announced as Southampton’s new manager and, in his first full season in charge in 2013/14, he guided Saints to eighth.
That piqued Tottenham’s interest and, in May 2014, Pochettino signed a five-year contract in north London. Between 2015/16 and 2017/18, he guided the club to two third-place finishes and one second-place finish - their best-ever in the division. In 2018/19, they reached the Champions League final, where they were beaten by Liverpool. His biggest triumph at Spurs was arguably the success of Harry Kane, who scored over 150 goals during his time in charge.
Pochettino left Spurs in November 2019 and took over at former club PSG in January 2021. In his first few months, he won the Coupe de France and, in his only full season, he won the Ligue 1 title. Next came a season at Chelsea in 2023/24, where the Blues finished sixth in the Premier League and were runners-up in the Carabao Cup. He left Stamford Bridge by mutual consent in May 2024 after less than a year in the job.
In September 2024, Pochettino took on his first international job as USA head coach. In 2025, he took them to the CONCACAF Gold Cup final, where they were beaten 2-1 by Mexico and, earlier this year, he took them to the last 16 of the 2026 World Cup, where they were beaten 4-1 by Belgium. He won each of his last six matches before departing for Old Trafford.
The game plan
With Tyrone Marshall
Chief Manchester United correspondent for the Daily Mirror and the Manchester Evening News Tyrone Marshall explains why it is difficult to predict how the Red Devils will approach Saturday's game.
"It feels a little hard to say at the moment because it is so early," he told brentfordfc.com.
"Michael Carrick was building a team to try and control games and play patiently, slowly and have lots of possession, whereas Pochettino is high intensity and high energy. He wants them to press high and win the ball back high, so there is a bit of a change in strategy.
"Against Liverpool, because they had a man sent off so early, we did not really get to see if there were any changes. In terms of shape, they played seven minutes with 11 men, but in that time, I do not think anyone could work out if it was a back three or a back four, so it is still a bit uncertain.
"I would imagine at Old Trafford against Brentford, it would probably be a back four, but it does still feel like we are in that stage of learning about what he wants and he is still trying to get his ideas across to the players."
