TITLE: Google to test Gemini agent that flags AdMob setup errors
OUTLET: PPC Land

Google today introduced three AI tools for AdMob, its mobile app advertising and monetization platform, setting out a schedule that runs from software that developers can install now to account-level agents that will not act on a publisher's behalf until later in 2027. The announcement, published on Google's Keyword blog under the Publisher Connections section and written by Naomi Ben-Ayoun, General Manager of AdMob, covers a Gemini-built assistant called Ask AdMob, a set of AdMob agent skills for AI coding assistants, and an AdMob server for the Model Context Protocol, the open standard that lets AI agents call outside software.
Only one of the three is available today. The agent skills can be installed now, according to Google. Ask AdMob enters testing "over the next few months", and the MCP server "is in early testing and will be expanding to more developers early next year." Beyond those three, the post promises "a much broader and more advanced wave of AI tools for app developers" throughout 2027.
In Short
Google is adding AI helpers to AdMob, its app advertising business: a chat assistant inside the account that looks for setup mistakes and revenue gaps, ready-made instructions that coding agents can use to add ads to an app, and a connection that will let those agents change AdMob settings directly. If you make money from ads in a mobile app or game, these tools are aimed at the work of configuring ad networks, floor prices and blocking rules, which Google says pulls teams away from building their products. Only the coding instructions are usable today; the assistant starts testing in the coming months, and Google says every change it suggests will need a person's approval before it is applied.
Three tools, three timelines
Ben-Ayoun frames the release around a familiar complaint. "Building a successful mobile app means balancing a great user experience with ads monetization," the post opens. "But manually managing ad setups, fixing integration errors, and auditing brand safety pulls your team's focus away from building." The three products are pitched at different layers of that work: Ask AdMob at the account, the agent skills at the app's code, and the MCP server at the connection between the two.
The table below sets out what the post and Google's developer documentation say about each, as of today.
The post does not name markets, languages, account eligibility or pricing for any of the three.
Ask AdMob: an assistant inside the account
Ask AdMob is the piece aimed at the widest group of AdMob users, because it sits in the web interface rather than in a developer's toolchain. The post describes it this way: "Trained on AdMob monetization best practices, it can help fix issues, find revenue opportunities, and protect your brand safety like an AdMob product expert." Google gives three examples of what it will do.
Finding setup problems
The first is diagnostic. "The troubleshooting hub will help you spot setup anomalies and SDK integration issues," the post says. "Ask AdMob has visibility into how ads are loading in your app so it can highlight recommendations to resolve issues impacting your revenue." Google does not describe what data that visibility draws on, beyond ad loading in the app, or how far back it looks.
Not every loading problem starts with a publisher's configuration, however. In August, rewarded video ads failed to play in Android apps for eight days, from August 20 to August 28, 2026, in an incident Google logged at its lowest severity tier and whose final update said mitigation covered most users rather than all of them. The post does not say whether Ask AdMob will report platform-side incidents of that kind or only problems in the publisher's own setup.
Bidders, mediation groups and floor prices
The second example reaches into revenue. "Ask AdMob uses peer benchmarks to recommend high-value SDK bidders with predicted revenue uplifts to add to your setup and help optimize your mediation groups," according to the post. Google also describes a check on configurations that publishers enter by hand for outside networks: "For example, Ask AdMob will automatically check your third-party network configuration to catch costly typos and floor price discrepancies before they impact your yield."
That is the territory of the mediation platform, the software layer that decides which of several ad networks gets to fill each ad request in an app. AdMob runs its own demand through the same mediation it offers to publishers, and its auction combines real-time bidders with waterfall lines that publishers configure network by network, each with its own floor price. In July 2025, Google added InMobi, ironSource and Unity as AdMob bidding partners and introduced bidding eCPM floors at the mediation-group level, alongside mediation A/B tests that could return recommendations in as little as three days.
The post does not explain what "peer benchmarks" are, which apps count as peers, how predicted revenue uplifts are calculated, or whether Google's own AdMob Network demand is assessed against the same benchmarks as third-party bidders. Those details matter, because the assistant recommending bidders belongs to the company that also sells demand inside the same auction.
Brand safety rules in plain language
The third example concerns brand safety, the controls that decide which ads an app will show. "Instead of manually managing blocklists, you can configure custom brand safety rules using simple, natural-language prompts in your AdMob UI," the post says. It does not show an example prompt, list which existing controls the rules map to, or say how a rule written in plain language is translated into the category, advertiser and URL blocks AdMob already offers.
This is a shift in approach from what Google described for its web publishing products a year ago. In November 2025, Google Ad Manager introduced an AI brand safety system that learned from publishers' manual decisions in Ad Review Center and, at that stage, surfaced unwanted ads for review rather than blocking them automatically. The same announcement added an AI help chatbot to the Ad Manager, AdMob and AdSense help centers. Ask AdMob's version starts from the publisher's written instruction rather than from past review decisions.
Testing first, action later
Google is explicit that none of this is live. "We'll begin testing these capabilities over the next few months and continue evolving Ask AdMob with more advanced features to take action on your behalf later next year," according to the post. The sentence that follows sets the control model: "As always, you remain in control, reviewing and approving every recommendation before it's applied."
There is a tension in the wording. The same post says Ask AdMob "will automatically check" third-party network configurations, which suggests the checks run without being asked, while changes still wait for approval. The post does not say how a recommendation is presented, whether approvals can be delegated to other users on an account, or whether a record of approved changes is kept.
Agent skills: six modules for coding assistants
The second product moves the work out of the AdMob dashboard and into the code editor. "Previously, setting up ad placements meant clicking through web dashboards, copying and pasting ad unit IDs, and managing ad configuration files outside your codebase," the post says. "AdMob agent skills give your AI agent pre-built, tested playbooks to implement coding tasks like, migrating to the Google Mobile Ads Next-Gen SDK, validating your SDK implementation, and implementing new ad formats in your app."
The post links to Google's developer site for the details. The Android documentation page, titled "Use agent skills" and last updated on November 13, 2026, describes the skills as self-contained modules of knowledge, instructions and workflows for the Google Mobile Ads (GMA) Next-Gen SDK. It has tabs for Android, iOS and Unity, and names AI assistants that support skills, including Antigravity, Claude Code, Codex and Cursor. Six skills are listed.
Some of this predates today's post. Google's Ads Developer blog introduced agent skills for its advertising and measurement developer products on June 24, 2026, including skills for the Google Mobile Ads SDK. And when Google made the Next-Gen SDK its preferred Android SDK in July, PPC Land reported that the migration aids already included an agent skill named google-mobile-ads-android-migrate-to-next-gen. Today's post gathers those skills under the AdMob brand and places them next to two products that are still in testing. It also names them inconsistently: the summary at the top of the post refers to "AdMob agent tools", while the body says "agent skills".
The SDK deadline behind the migration skill
The migration skill points at a deadline that already applies to every Android app using AdMob's older library. Google's deprecation page for the legacy Android SDK, updated today, describes that SDK as being in maintenance mode and recommends moving to GMA Next-Gen. Its timetable is below.
According to Google's documentation, traffic from a sunset version risks automatic no-fill because ad serving stops. The Next-Gen SDK has its own schedule: version 1.x.x was released on April 14, 2026, with deprecation planned for the first quarter of 2028 and sunset for the second quarter of 2029, while the beta line released on July 17, 2025 is due to be deprecated in the first quarter of 2027. Google says exact Next-Gen sunset dates will be announced on its developer blog.
Google has published AI help for the older library as well. A separate documentation page, last updated September 11, 2026 and marked beta, covers integrating the legacy SDK with AI tools in Android Studio's Gemini, and states that it is not for the Next-Gen SDK.
The MCP server: read, write and manage
The third product is the one with the widest reach into accounts. "While agent skills can help you implement tasks today, we're also working on an AdMob MCP server to connect your developer environments directly with AdMob," according to the post. "Soon your AI agent will be able to read account data, write new ad units, and manage mediation settings. All you have to do is review and approve the updates before you push them to production. This is in early testing and will be expanding to more developers early next year."
Read access to account data would be useful on its own. The ability to create ad units and change mediation settings goes further: those are the objects that decide which networks compete for an app's inventory and at what price. PPC Land found no developer documentation for the AdMob server, or for Ask AdMob, on Google's developer site on the day of the announcement.
Google's earlier MCP work for advertisers moved more slowly on write access. The company released an open-source MCP server for the Google Ads API in October 2025 as a read-only tool with two functions. Its first write tools arrived only with version 0.0.6 on November 2, 2026, covering assets and labels, while campaigns, budgets, bids and keywords remained out of reach. The AdMob server, as described today, is meant to write ad units and mediation settings from the outset, although Google has not said which mediation settings are included or whether floors, network order and bidder lists are all in scope.
