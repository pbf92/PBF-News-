TITLE: 80% of UK Black Friday shoppers buy from unfamiliar stores, Criteo finds
OUTLET: PPC Land

Four in five UK shoppers who take part in Black Friday buy at least sometimes from online stores they do not use for the rest of the year, according to research from Criteo published in London on November 19, 2026, eight days before Black Friday falls on November 27. The UK figures come from the national breakdown of a September 2026 survey of 10,500 consumers in ten countries, whose global results Criteo first published on its blog on October 22 and updated on November 4. Across all ten markets, the equivalent share is three in four.
The research covers more than store loyalty. It measures how shoppers discover products, how they plan for the gift season and how many of them would hand part of that job to an AI assistant: 42% globally and 43% in the UK say they would use one to help choose a gift. Criteo, which sells advertising inside ChatGPT, attaches to those findings a separate figure it has used since March - that visits referred by AI chatbots convert at about 1.5 times the rate of other referral traffic - drawn from 500 US retailers observed in February.
In Short
Criteo asked 10,500 people in ten countries how they plan to shop this holiday season, and found that Black Friday pulls most of them, 75% globally and 80% in the UK, toward online stores they would not normally use. Many also said they would let an AI assistant help pick gifts, although that was a question about intentions rather than a record of what people had done. For retailers, the numbers describe a season in which customers are easier to win than usual, but the AI conversion figure attached to the research is nine months old and comes from a different study.
Two releases, one survey
Criteo has published the research twice, for two audiences.
The first version, a post titled "Black Friday and year-end shopping trends 2026: What Criteo's latest survey reveals," appeared on Criteo's blog on October 22, 2026, under the byline of Elizabeth Kim, a global content strategist at the company. A note on the page says it was updated on November 4, 2026. The post draws on what Criteo calls its Consumer Sentiment Insights and presents "four shopping trends marketers should know," illustrated with six data tables. Each table cites the same source: "Criteo Shopper Survey, September 2026."
The second version is a UK media alert, datelined London, 19 November 2026, and headlined "AI discovery and early decision-making: Criteo identifies the trends shaping 2026 holiday shopping." It condenses the material into three trends and swaps the global numbers for national ones. Its notes to editors are explicit about the substitution: "Figures cited in this media alert are from the UK country breakdown."
The survey covered the United States, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Brazil, Australia and Singapore, the ten country columns that appear in the blog's tables. The total base is 10,500. Two questions used smaller groups: 9,445 respondents answered the statements about AI shopping tools, and 7,820 people who shop on Black Friday answered the question about unfamiliar stores.
The media alert also leans on two pieces of separate research, which matters for reading its claims. One is the AI referral analysis of 500 US retailers. The other produced the statement that 96% of consumers still use other channels alongside AI. Neither comes from the September survey.
Black Friday loosens the ties
The finding that gives the UK release its sharpest number concerns loyalty. Criteo asked Black Friday shoppers: "On Black Friday, do you sometimes purchase from online stores that you usually don't purchase from during the rest of the year?" Globally, three in four said they did at least sometimes. In the UK, 80% did. According to the notes to editors, that figure combines "sometimes" and "most or all of the time" responses, so it captures a habit of occasional switching rather than a single purchase on a single day.
Price explains most of it. Among the reasons for trying a new store, according to Criteo, great deals ranked first at 52%, followed by free shipping at 41%. The blog post does not list the remaining options.
Singapore offered a regional comparison. There, 86% of shoppers who take part in Singles' Day, the November 11 sales event, purchase from online stores they do not usually buy from during the rest of the year, according to Criteo. The blog gives no equivalent Singles' Day figure for other markets.
The timing data suggests the window for reaching those shoppers is short. Nearly one-third of consumers, 31%, start thinking about Black Friday deals one to two weeks in advance, according to the survey, while another 21% begin two to four weeks ahead. Taken together, just over half start their deal planning within a month of the event (a PPC Land addition of the two shares).
The loyalty finding sits awkwardly beside other research PPC Land has covered this year. In August, Optimove reported that 53% of holiday shoppers planned to buy only from stores they had used the previous year, in a survey of 648 US consumers. The two studies asked different questions of different people. Optimove measured a plan for the whole season among a US sample screened by income; Criteo measured how often Black Friday shoppers in ten countries stray at least occasionally. Both can be true at once: a shopper can intend to stay loyal and still buy elsewhere when a discount is large enough.
UK interest in the event itself has been fading for years. In October, Marketing Signals reported that core "Black Friday" search interest in the UK had fallen 36% from its 2019 level, while searches about sale dates had risen. A smaller audience that shops around more freely is one reading of the two data points together, though neither study measured the other's question.
Planning gifts in September
The second strand of the research concerns gifts. Globally, 79% of consumers typically purchase gifts during the holidays and festive season, according to the survey. Among those gift shoppers, 76% plan to seek discounts and promotions and 75% expect to shop for presents online. Both figures combine "very likely" and "somewhat likely" responses.
UK shoppers were more focused on price. According to the media alert, 81% of UK consumers plan to look for discounts and promotions when buying gifts.
The media alert also makes a claim about timing: "Yet consumers are researching earlier this year, with many planning their wish lists ahead of the sales. As early as September, 59% had already thought about what gifts they might buy." The survey was fielded in September, so the 59% figure describes the state of UK shoppers' plans at the moment they answered. The material does not include a figure from any earlier year, which means it offers no measurement of whether planning has moved earlier. Criteo's conclusion follows from that premise: "By the time shoppers are ready to take advantage of an offer, many will have narrowed down their choices."
Price sensitivity is not unique to Criteo's sample. In October, Simon-Kucher found that US households planned to cut holiday budgets by 5.5%, with only 22% saying advertised discounts fully reflected true savings.
Where gift ideas start is less digital than the rest of the research might suggest. Search engines and physical stores tie as the most common starting points for gift discovery at 37% each, according to the blog, while 27% plan to turn to generative AI tools. "Still, shoppers aren't relying solely on digital channels for gift inspiration," the post notes.
The AI gift adviser, mostly hypothetical
The headline AI figure comes from a direct question: "Would you use an AI assistant to help choose a gift?" Globally, 42% said yes, and another 40% said they were open to the idea. In the UK, the figures were 43% and 36%. That leaves 18% of global respondents and 21% of UK respondents in neither group (PPC Land calculations from Criteo's figures). The UK media alert describes the shift as "creating a new point of discovery for retailers and brands."
The wording matters. The question asks about willingness, not past behavior, and the global post frames it as a possibility: "AI could play an even greater role in helping shoppers narrow their choices." How many shoppers have already used an assistant to pick a present? The material does not say.
Other questions show how far AI tools have already entered shopping. According to the survey, 59% of consumers find AI-powered chatbots helpful when shopping online, and 51% are comfortable with AI personalizing their shopping experiences. Among AI tools used for shopping, ChatGPT leads at 51%, followed by Google Gemini at 43%. The blog does not say which respondents answered the tools question or list the other tools.
Trust lags use. While 46% trust AI-driven product recommendations, 50% express concerns about privacy and 48% about misleading information, according to Criteo. Those percentages also combine "strongly agree" and "somewhat agree" responses, from the base of 9,445.
The concerns appear in a different order from an earlier Criteo study. Criteo's March 24 blog post on its 2026 Commerce & AI Trend Report put being misled by fake or biased content at 52% and privacy at 46% as the top concerns with AI-assisted shopping. That survey covered more than 6,000 shoppers, according to Criteo, against 10,500 in September, and used different wording, so the two sets of numbers are not directly comparable.
The September findings fit a pattern visible in other recent surveys. Shoppers welcome AI help with choosing; far fewer want it to complete the purchase. On November 12, Salesforce reported that 76% of shoppers do not want AI agents buying for them, and that only 19% of people with a personal AI agent had used it at checkout. In the UK, ACI Worldwide and YouGov found that 60% of adults would drop an AI shopping agent after one mistake.
Where discovery happens
Before the AI questions, the survey asked how people usually discover new products, with respondents free to select every channel that applied. Online retailers and marketplaces led at 51% globally, followed by search engines at 41% and social media or community forums at 39%. Generative AI tools such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini sat seventh at 26%, behind recommendations from friends and family, retail stores and online ads.

Several country results stand out. Brazil has the highest share of any market for online retailers and marketplaces, social media, online ads, voice assistants and generative AI tools, the last at 45% against 26% globally. South Korea is the only market where search engines, at 51%, beat online retailers and marketplaces. France and South Korea report very low figures for retail stores, 7% and 9%, against 30% globally and 38% in the UK and US; Criteo offers no explanation for the gap. Germany sits well below average on social media at 26%.
The UK profile is close to the global one, with higher figures for brand websites (29%) and TV ads (28%) and a lower one for search engines (36%).
Online ads appear twice in the research, with different results. As a usual discovery channel, they were selected by 28% of respondents. Asked separately, 81% of consumers globally said they discover relevant products through online ads at least sometimes. The first question asks for habitual channels; the second accepts occasional use.
That AI tools rank seventh as a discovery channel, and that 27% plan to use them to start gift searches, is broadly consistent with other data on where shopping begins. In May, a Smarty Marketing survey of 1,295 US consumers put ChatGPT third as a starting point for shopping searches at 7.26%, behind Google and Amazon.
