TITLE: GTA 6 is a critical masterpiece. So why all the hate?
OUTLET: Ynetnews

Grand Theft Auto VI launched Thursday to enormous fanfare, shattered sales records and earned a near-perfect score from critics. But now that players have had their say, it seems not everyone is impressed with Rockstar Games' long-awaited blockbuster.
Just three days after its release, GTA 6 has been hit by a wave of negative user ratings on Metacritic, dragging its score down to around 5 out of 10. The backlash has since escalated into a full-blown fan war, with another of gaming's most beloved titles, The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, caught in the crossfire. 
An unusually wide gap
GTA 6 earned a Metacritic score of 99 out of 100 based on 152 professional reviews, matching the record held by the original 1998 release of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. Yet on Saturday, its user score stood at just 5.6 out of 10, an extraordinary gap between critical acclaim and player ratings. 
The low rating is particularly striking given the game's commercial success. Rockstar reported $3.79 billion in worldwide retail sales on launch day alone, the biggest entertainment launch in history, according to the company. It also reported 32.5 million units sold before release. 
According to gaming website GGRecon, GTA 6 had received 34,804 user ratings on Metacritic by Saturday morning, many of them awarding the game a flat zero.
The phenomenon, known as "review bombing," occurs when users flood a game with extremely negative ratings, often to protest something or deliberately damage its reputation. Those leaving the reviews have not necessarily played the game, and their ratings sometimes reflect grievances that have little to do with its actual quality.
In GTA 6's case, some negative reviews raise genuine concerns about performance, bugs and controversial design choices. One of the biggest complaints is the game's 30 frames-per-second cap on consoles, with no option to play at 60 fps, even on the more powerful PlayStation 5 Pro.
Technical analysis by Digital Foundry found that the standard PS5 can struggle to maintain 30 fps in some situations, while the PS5 Pro delivers more consistent performance. 
Other complaints focus on the story and characters. Some users accuse Rockstar of making GTA too "woke," taking issue with the relationship between Jason and Lucia, the game's two protagonists. Others argue that the franchise has become more of a life simulator, with too much emphasis on managing a romantic relationship and too little on the criminal exploits traditionally associated with the series.
According to Forbes, the wave of negative reviews also included apparently AI-generated comments, some with their original prompts still visible, as well as users who openly admitted they had not played the game. Other suspicious activity involved newly created accounts whose only contribution was a zero rating for GTA 6.
Some reviews also contained racist and misogynistic remarks unrelated to the game itself. 
Signs of trouble emerged even before launch. On November 17, two days before the game hit stores, reports surfaced that Metacritic had removed fake user reviews posted before the public had any opportunity to play it.
Video Games Chronicle reported at the time that Metacritic had already been forced to remove fake reviews submitted ahead of the game's November 19 release. 
What does Zelda have to do with it?
Over the weekend, the remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, released earlier this month for Nintendo Switch 2, also became the target of a wave of negative ratings. This time, the apparent motive was revenge: Some GTA fans believed Nintendo supporters were responsible for bringing down Rockstar's user score and decided to retaliate.
According to reports from Forbes and Dexerto, suspicious accounts were found giving GTA 6 a zero while awarding the Zelda remake a perfect 10. In some cases, those were the only two games the accounts had rated, raising concerns about deliberate attempts to manipulate the rankings. 
But the dispute appears to go beyond a simple rivalry between fan communities. It has become a battle over a place in gaming history.
GTA 6's Metacritic score of 99 matches that of the original Ocarina of Time, released in 1998, which held the top spot on the review aggregation site for years. The new Zelda remake, meanwhile, became one of Metacritic's highest-rated games among users earlier this month, reaching 9.6 out of 10. 
That rating has since taken a hit. According to Notebookcheck, the Zelda remake's user score fell from 9.6 to 8.7 on Saturday after a wave of zero ratings.
Some commenters made little effort to conceal their motives. One openly identified as a GTA 6 fan and mocked Zelda supporters, while another blamed Nintendo fans for the negative reviews targeting Rockstar's game. 
However, despite the suspicious accounts and apparent retaliation, there is no conclusive evidence that Zelda fans organized the original campaign against GTA 6. It also remains unclear whether the negative ratings were coordinated or came from individual users acting independently.

So what do players really think?
To understand why GTA 6's low Metacritic user score may not reflect the broader reception to the game, it's worth looking at ratings elsewhere.
On the PlayStation Store, where users must own the game to rate it, GTA 6 received a score of 4.84 out of 5 over the weekend, based on approximately 170,000 ratings. The game also earned a similarly strong rating of 4.9 out of 5 on the Xbox Store. 
Metacritic, by contrast, allows registered users to submit ratings without proving they have purchased or played a game.
That doesn't mean everyone who gave GTA 6 a low score is being dishonest, nor does it mean everyone who bought it is necessarily satisfied. But the figures suggest that players who own the game have a considerably more favorable view of it than Metacritic's user score might indicate.
For Rockstar, which has already delivered one of the biggest commercial launches in entertainment history, the controversy is unlikely to significantly affect GTA 6's success.
Still, the dispute highlights how easily user ratings can become weapons in battles between rival fan communities, even when some of those taking part haven't played the games they're so quick to judge.
