TITLE: Nasdaq slides deeper as Nvidia rout drags S&P 500 and Dow into correction
OUTLET: Crypto Briefing

A brutal week for tech, led by Nvidia's leadership turmoil, has pushed every major US index more than 10% below its highs
Wall Street’s AI-fueled party has hit last call. As of November 20, 2026, the S&P 500, the Dow Jones Industrial Average, and the Nasdaq Composite all sit in correction territory, meaning each has fallen at least 10% from a recent high.
The Nasdaq is the one doing the heaviest lifting, and not in a good way. The tech-heavy index closed at 22,617.73, a full 18.1% below its October peak, and has now given back every gain it made this year.
The scoreboard nobody wanted to check
Start with the broadest gauge. The S&P 500 finished at 6,910.39, which leaves it 11.6% under the record it set on October 6.
That marks the index’s first correction since the spring of 2025. It also capped the S&P 500’s longest run of daily declines in over two years.
The Dow, often treated as the sleepy blue-chip cousin, wasn’t spared either. It closed at 48,147.33, down 11.4% from its August high.
Then there’s the Nasdaq. Beyond its 18.1% drop from the October peak, the Composite now sits 2.7% in the red for the year. The index also logged its worst week since March 2020.
The narrower Nasdaq-100 looks even rougher. It was roughly 19.8% below its October high, leaving it a whisker away from the 20% line that typically marks a bear market.

Nvidia’s very bad week
If you’re looking for the epicenter, it’s Nvidia. The chipmaker’s shares dropped 14% on November 20 alone, bringing the stock’s loss for the week to approximately 23%.
Zoom out further and the damage gets staggering. Nvidia has lost more than $2.5 trillion in market value since the beginning of October.
The trigger was leadership turmoil. CEO Jensen Huang resigned, and the company is also facing increased scrutiny over its financial practices.
The Motley Fool argued the Nvidia sell-off is at least partly justified, describing Huang as irreplaceable.
Why one stock can sink a whole market
Semiconductor and technology stocks drove the sell-off, and the concerns go beyond Nvidia’s executive suite. Investors are increasingly questioning whether the enormous sums being poured into artificial intelligence will actually pay off.
Companies have been spending heavily on data centers, chips, and infrastructure, betting that AI revenue will eventually justify the bill. The current sell-off suggests a growing number of investors want to see the receipts before they keep cheering.
What this means for investors
The most immediate lesson is about concentration. Portfolios heavily tilted toward tech and semiconductors have absorbed the sharpest losses, and the Nasdaq’s year-to-date slide into negative territory shows how quickly a market’s favorite trade can unwind.
For now, the numbers tell a blunt story. The S&P 500 is down 11.6% from its record, the Dow is off 11.4%, the Nasdaq has slid 18.1%, and the company at the heart of the AI boom has shed more than $2.5 trillion in value in a matter of weeks.
