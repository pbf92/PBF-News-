TITLE: Cochrane library warns of consequences under lower 2027 funding
OUTLET: Cochrane Eagle

The Cochrane Public Library is asking the Town for $233,000 in 2027 funding, warning that lower allocations could lead to reduced programming, shorter operating hours and staff layoffs.
During its Nov. 16 regular meeting, Cochrane Town Council received a presentation from the library board outlining three funding scenarios and the potential impacts on services.
Trustee Tom Hovland presented the options, identifying the $233,000 request, an 18 per cent increase, as the library’s preferred scenario. The additional funding would help maintain current service levels as use of the library grows, while also allowing it to begin addressing recommendations from a recently completed needs assessment.
“The reason why we want to do that is because we think that in order to deliver value for money for Cochranites, we need to actually make sure that some of those assessments are proven,” Hovland told council.
A significant portion of the request relates to new operational costs associated with Bill 28, the Libraries Act. According to the presentation, the legislation introduces additional oversight responsibilities and new rules related to access to certain library materials. The library estimates the non-optional costs associated with the changes at $52,000, taking effect Jan. 1, 2027.
“I think that everybody here knows that no regulation is free and we are learning that here,” Hovland said.
The preferred funding scenario would also help the library meet its minimum staffing requirements and begin capital fundraising independently of the Town.
The second scenario, a $206,000 or a 16 per cent increase, would cover operational costs but require moderate reductions to programming. It would not include investment in capital fundraising or implementation of the needs assessment recommendations.
A third option of $181,000, or 14 per cent, would also require a 13 per cent reduction in operating hours.
“That means we possibly close at 6 p.m.,” said board chair Chuck Love. “That is a huge crunch for evenings, of course, because that is when a lot of families actually come to the library.”
Love also outlined the consequences of a much smaller three per cent funding increase, estimated at $38,629.
“What will happen is we will probably have to close at least two days a week,” he told council. “There will be staff reductions and possible layoffs and many of the gains that we've made over the years would be set back, I'm estimating, about 10 years.”
Coun. Patrick Wilson asked whether reducing programming, rather than operating hours, could be an alternative under the three per cent scenario. Hovland said that approach would also undermine the library's value to residents.
“Programming is part of what makes the library effective,” he said. “You reduce the programming, you reduce the usage and the value that it's actually providing to residents.”
Hovland described the potential consequences of receiving only a three per cent increase as “catastrophic.”
Mayor Morgan Nagel questioned whether reducing staffing requirements could help avoid cuts to service hours. He also raised concerns about the cumulative effect of increasing funding requests from municipal departments and community partners.
“We have quite a number of partners and departments and if every department and partner keeps on coming to us with 10 per cent cost increases every single year, that number compounds very quickly,” he said. “We're really worried about the impact that's going to have on taxpayers.”
Coun. Marni Fedeyko, however, pointed to the library's history of receiving less funding than requested, suggesting the current ask reflects a need to catch up after years of limited increases.
“What they've asked for is maybe 15 per cent and consistently got one or two,” she said. “I don't think that they're going 18 per cent great guns into the future. It's that they're just trying to get caught up to what they've had taken away over many years.”
While no funding decision was made at the meeting, Council accepted the presentation as information as it continued deliberations on the 2027 budget.
