TITLE: Is Nico Paz a realistic target for Manchester United?
OUTLET: LiveScore

Is Nico Paz a realistic target for Manchester United?
It is truly hard not to get excited by Nico Paz.
A 6ft 1in dazzling midfielder with a clump of messy hair, styled to look as if he has just stepped out of a Playboy magazine from the early 2000s, the Argentine is one of the most effervescent players in world football.
There have been comparisons to Zinedine Zidane, Diego Maradona, and, of course, Lionel Messi, with Paz now being touted as the heir to the recently retired international stars' throne.
Stepping onto a pitch in a thin headband just behind his hairline, paired with long socks—something that has become increasingly out of fashion among modern young players—Paz turned 22 in September and has already played 107 games for club and country, scoring 31 times in the process.
But take away his goals. Take away the 25 assists he has made throughout his career, eight of which have come this season. And you are still left with a phenomenon of a player, who excels at finding pockets of space between opposition midfield and defensive lines, and possesses a touch that even Dimitar Berbatov would not turn his nose up at.
To put it simply, the young Argentine is one of the most invigorating players in world football.
Interest in Paz has steadily built, with Manchester United now having him high on their shortlist should Bruno Fernandes leave the club—per David Ornstein of The Athletic
It is unknown how long Como will be able to retain him.
The Italian outfit signed Paz from Real Madrid in 2024 for £5 million, with buy-back clauses applicable to the following three summers at £6.6m, £7.5m, and £8.3m respectively. These terms were renegotiated in June. Como would pay Madrid £50m, and the buy-back clause was changed to €66.7m, valid only for 2027.
Could a move be on for Nico Paz?
Whilst he excelled last season, the start of the 2026/27 campaign is where he has started to attract truly widespread attention, with his performance against Manchester United drawing plaudits from even the harshest of critics.
Fernandes, the club's current captain, has recently had his contract extension triggered, keeping him at the club until 2028, rather than the previously predetermined date of 2027. But that does not mean United would not look to cash in on an ageing Fernandes, who will be just months away from his 34th birthday by the time the summer of 2028 rolls around.
As Ornstein explains, United may not be Paz's first choice of destination, especially given the form shown by the club this year. Yet United's project and enduring appeal are attractive to Paz, and sharing the same representatives as head coach Mauricio Pochettino may prove beneficial.
Paz's eight goal contributions in the Champions League—including scoring twice and assisting once against United—can only be matched by Real Madrid's Kylian Mbappe.
The question currently whispering through the hallways of social media is what Paz could bring to United.
Whilst no transfer is ever guaranteed to work out—especially when young players command increasingly large sums—the possibilities are truly endless with the Argentine.
Predominantly playing in the centre of the park as a ball-dictating attacking midfielder, Paz's versatility is one of his greatest attributes. He has been deployed as a central midfielder, striker, and winger on both flanks during his short career thus far.
One of his biggest attacking weaknesses is his lack of explosive short-area bursts. In comparison to someone like Messi, the eight-time Ballon d'Or winner can use his slight frame to propel himself forward quickly. With Paz, standing at over 6ft, more power is required for these short athletic bursts. And while that may seem like a significant downside, the midfielder has found a way around it, instead opting to shield the ball from his opponents before gliding past them with his dazzling footwork.
To add to this, his work rate off the ball is where you truly see his value. Unlike many luxury players, Paz possesses an immense work rate without the ball, using his large frame to pressure defenders into making slight mistakes, often winning possession back. He has earned a duel success rate of 74.8 per Gradient FC.
The only truly glaring weakness in his game is his right foot. Predominantly left-footed, Paz struggles to shoot from distance with his right, though he is still able to utilise it over shorter distances.
For now, Paz to United is simply a pipe dream. With a lack of investment from INEOS, Ratcliffe, and the Glazer family—it will be a cold day in hell before a singular penny drops from their family's coffers into the club—it seems that a move for the Argentine is unlikely to happen, with it being ostensibly more probable that Como will be looking for in excess of £100m for their star player.
