TITLE: Artificial intelligence could identify psoriasis and assess its severity from a single photo
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Widely available artificial intelligence models could not only recognize psoriasis from a single photo but also assess its severity, according to an international study by researchers at Semmelweis University. The study, published in Frontiers in Medicine, found that ChatGPT-5 identified the skin condition and correctly assessed its severity with nearly 100 percent accuracy. However, the researchers caution that, despite the promising results, artificial intelligence cannot replace dermatologists, and anyone experiencing symptoms should seek professional medical advice.
Psoriasis is a lifelong skin condition that may worsen without proper treatment. However, patients do not always seek medical advice promptly, as they may mistake their symptoms for those of other skin conditions, such as eczema. In some countries, long waiting times for dermatologists can also delay diagnosis. As a result, some people may turn to artificial intelligence (AI) based models to get initial information about their symptoms.
To assess how reliable these models could identify psoriasis, researchers at Semmelweis University analyzed 303 standardized clinical photographs from 160 patients. Taken with a professional camera under controlled conditions, the photographs included 163 photographs showing psoriasis and 140 showing other skin conditions with similar symptoms. Rather than using software developed specifically for dermatology, the researchers tested four widely available AI models: ChatGPT-5, ChatGPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Gemini 2.5 Flash. The researchers compared the models’ ability to identify psoriasis, assess its severity, and recommend treatments. Dermatologists evaluated the results.
ChatGPT-5 was the most accurate model, identifying psoriasis in 93.1 percent of cases. ChatGPT-4o followed, with over 90 percent. Among the cases it correctly identified, ChatGPT -5 also accurately assessed disease severity in 93.3 percent of cases.
What is psoriasis?
Psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory skin condition that affects an estimated 200,000 people in Hungary and around 2 percent of the global population. Although genetic factors play a major role in its development, stress, obesity, and a diet high in sugar and saturated fat may also contribute to the onset or worsening of symptoms. The characteristic patches usually appear on the elbows, knees, and scalp, but in more severe cases, they can affect larger areas of the body.
“The results show that these AI models could perform certain medical analyses with a high degree of accuracy. However, relying solely on their answers can be risky, as AI can sound confident and convincing even when providing false information. Treatment decisions based on incorrect information could even put patients’ health at risk,” said Dr. Mehdi Boostani, former PhD student at Semmelweis University, researcher at the Roswell Park Comprehensive Cancer Center in Buffalo, and first author of the study.
The researcher also emphasized that the models were less reliable at recommending treatments: even the best-performing model, ChatGPT-5, suggested an appropriate treatment in 83.7 percent of cases in which it correctly identified psoriasis. According to the expert, this is partly because choosing the proper treatment is more complex than recognizing a skin condition.
“Making safe medical decisions requires more than just a photograph. Doctors also need to know patients’ medical histories, comorbidities, and whether certain treatments might be unsuitable for them. AI models do not have access to this information when they analyze photographs alone,” explained Dr. Norbert Kiss, assistant professor at the Department of Dermatology, Venereology and Dermatooncology at Semmelweis University and the last author of the study.
Despite these limitations, the researchers believe that AI could become a useful tool to help patients better understand their condition. The findings support this view: although the models did not always provide accurate diagnoses, they often gave reliable information. The researchers therefore hope that further advances in AI could help people recognize the signs of skin conditions and seek appropriate medical care sooner.
