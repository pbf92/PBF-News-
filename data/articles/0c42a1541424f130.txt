TITLE: Disney+ and Hulu bundle with ads drops to $4.99 a month for a year
OUTLET: PPC Land

Disney has cut the price of its Disney+ and Hulu bundle with advertising to $4.99 a month for 12 months for new and returning subscribers in the United States, in a Black Friday promotion that opened at midnight Pacific time on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, and closes at 11:59pm Pacific on Monday, November 30, according to Disney's offer page. The plan normally costs $12.99 a month. According to 9to5Google, which reported the offer on November 24, the Premium version of the same bundle is also discounted, to $14.99 a month for the first year from the usual $21.99.
The headline price is identical to the one Disney charged in its 2025 Black Friday promotion. What has moved is everything around it. In September, Disney raised the price of almost every Disney+ and Hulu plan except the bundle with ads, which left the gap between paying with money and paying with attention wider than before. This week's offer widens it further: for a year, a Premium subscriber on the promotion pays $10 a month more than an ad-plan subscriber on the promotion.
In Short
Disney is selling its Disney+ and Hulu bundle with ads for $4.99 a month for a full year, and the Premium version for $14.99, to people in the US who sign up between November 24 and November 30, 2026. By PPC Land's calculation, the discount on the ad plan is about 62%, against about 32% on Premium, so the deeper saving sits on the version that carries advertising. For advertisers, every sign-up is another household watching Disney's ad-supported streams, arriving weeks before Disney merges Hulu fully into the Disney+ app and at a time when its streaming ad prices are falling.
The offer, line by line
Two plans are on promotion. Disney's offer page, titled "2026 Disney+ Hulu Black Friday Deal" and revised on November 24, 2026, names them as the Disney+, Hulu Bundle (with ads) and the Disney+, Hulu Bundle Premium. ESPN is not part of either offer, according to the page.
*PPC Land calculation from the published monthly prices, before taxes.
9to5Google's Ben Schoon described the bundle offer plainly: "Normally $12.99 a month, the Disney+ & Hulu bundle is just $4.99/month for Black Friday, and that's a price that stays in place for a whole year." On the second plan, he wrote: "The $4.99/month deal is for the ad-supported plan, with the ad-free tier also discounted to $14.99/month for the first year, down from the usual $21.99. Not quite as big of a discount, but still some big savings over the course of a full year."
One detail separates the two descriptions. 9to5Google calls the Premium plan the ad-free tier. Disney's own offer page says ads will be served in select live and linear content on Premium. The plan is free of ads in the on-demand catalogue, not across everything it carries.
How the offer works
According to Disney's offer page, the promotion runs on six terms.
- Window. The offer became available at 3am Eastern, 12am Pacific, on Tuesday, November 24, 2026. It ends at 11:59pm Pacific on Monday, November 30, which is 2:59am Eastern on Tuesday, December 1.
- Eligibility. It is open to new and eligible returning subscribers who are US residents aged 18 or older. Current subscribers to Disney+, Hulu, ESPN or any of the bundles are not eligible.
- Price and length. The promotional price applies for 12 months on each plan.
- Renewal. After 12 months the subscription renews automatically at the regular monthly price, listed as $12.99 for the plan with ads and $21.99 for Premium, or at whatever the regular price is at that point.
- Cancellation. Subscribers can cancel at any time, with cancellation taking effect at the end of the billing period. There are no refunds or credits for partial months.
- Restrictions. The offer cannot be combined with a free trial or any other promotion, cannot be redeemed with a gift card, and a switch to another plan after redeeming it ends the discounted price.
The renewal term is the one with a long tail. A household that signs up on November 30 pays the promotional rate into late 2027, then moves to a price Disney has not yet set. Disney last raised its prices in September 2026, so "then-current" is not a formality.
The same $4.99, against a higher list
Disney ran an almost identical promotion a year ago. In 2025, the Disney+ and Hulu bundle with ads cost $4.99 a month for 12 months, down from $12.99, for new and eligible returning subscribers, with the offer running through Monday, December 1, 2025, according to MacRumors. MacRumors' account of that offer mentioned no discount on the Premium plan.
The regular price of the bundle with ads has stayed at $12.99 since then. Most other prices have not. On September 23, 2026, Disney raised the cost of its plans for new subscribers immediately and for existing subscribers from their next bill. The bundle with ads was the only plan in MacRumors' list left unchanged.

Two consequences follow from that table. First, the bundle with ads now costs only 50 cents a month more than Disney+ with ads or Hulu with ads on its own, leaving little price reason to buy either service alone. Second, the distance between the bundle with ads and the bundle without them grew from $7 a month to $9 a month in September. The Black Friday prices stretch that to $10 for the first year. Disney has not said whether the price structure is designed to steer subscribers toward advertising, but the arithmetic points in that direction.
Why the ad plan matters more than the discount
For marketers, the interesting part of a streaming promotion is not the consumer saving. It is the audience the promotion adds to the advertising side of the business.
The most recent count PPC Land has reported came in February, when coverage of Disney's first fiscal quarter cited 122 million ad-supported streaming subscribers. Advertising inside Disney's streaming segment then grew 12% to $821 million in the second quarter. By the fourth quarter, reported on November 17, growth had slowed: Disney's fiscal 2026 results showed $11.5 billion of advertising across the year, with streaming advertising up 6% in the quarter to $931 million, including an extra week. More impressions added 6 points to that growth. Lower prices took 6 points away.
That is the backdrop for a discount aimed at the plan with ads. More subscribers on that plan mean more ad impressions to sell. They do not, on Disney's own figures, guarantee higher ad revenue, because the company has been selling more inventory at lower prices. In the same filings, Disney attributed weaker rates to the growing supply of advertising across search, social media, online marketplaces and other ad-supported streaming services.
The company has also chosen this moment to show less. From the first quarter of fiscal 2027, reported in February, Disney will stop publishing a separate profit figure for Disney+ and Hulu, according to PPC Land's November 17 report. The quarterly results also give no count of ad-supported subscribers, no ad pricing and no split of streaming ad revenue between Disney+ and Hulu. How many of this week's sign-ups stay beyond the promotional year is unlikely to be visible from outside.
What arrives in December
The 9to5Google report puts the deal in the context of a changing service: "Hulu is set to be fully merged into Disney+ soon, and the platform is even adding 3D movies on Meta Quest headsets." Disney told investors on November 17 that subscribers will have a fully unified experience on Disney+ from December, including live TV and select premium add-ons, and that it expects benefits in engagement and churn over time. The step builds on the Hulu on Disney+ launch for US bundle subscribers in March 2024.
Chief executive Josh D'Amaro also confirmed on the November 17 call that Disney is exploring a free Disney+ product, which he said would reach more price-sensitive viewers and give the company more ad inventory to sell. A free tier would move Disney toward the AVOD model, where viewers pay nothing and advertisers fund the service. No date, market or format has been given. Read alongside that plan, a $4.99 plan with ads looks like a step on a price ladder that may soon start at zero.
Formats and tools already in place
The inventory a new subscriber generates is sold through formats Disney has been building through 2026. In June, Disney+ rolled out four interactive ad formats - Gateway Go, Pause Ads, Pause+ and Ad Selector. At the end of June, Disney moved its campaigns in Europe, the Middle East and Africa onto its global ad server, saying its customer base there had grown by more than 25% since the start of 2025. On the November 17 call, Disney said almost half of all new ad campaigns now flow through an automated ad sales tool launched in August.
What the take-up data shows
Disney's appetite for ad-plan subscribers is not new. In October 2023, the company said 50% of new subscribers were choosing the ad tier. In the UK, where this promotion does not apply, Barb figures published on November 12 showed the Disney+ ad tier reaching 3.9 million homes in the third quarter, up from 3.6 million, with the share of Disney+ homes on the ad plan rising from 40% to 43%.
Not every viewer is persuaded. The first comment under the 9to5Google post reads: "That's the ad-supported plan. It could be $0 and I wouldn't do it." One reader is not a market, and the Barb figures point the other way. Yet the comment states the trade-off Disney is pricing.

Only HBO Max matches Disney's 12-month term. Peacock and Paramount+ offer lower monthly prices, but for a quarter of the time or less. Two of the four are now under one owner: Skydance completed its takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery on October 6, and PPC Land's report on Skydance's $6bn yearly interest bill noted that Paramount+ and HBO Max will be sold together in a bundle from early 2027, under a single sales leadership. The two promotions still run separately this year, on different terms.
How the deal reached readers
The 9to5Google post is also an example of how these promotions are distributed. It carries the disclosure "FTC: We use income earning auto affiliate links," and its links to Disney+, Hulu, Peacock, Paramount+ and Apple pass through affiliate tracking services before reaching the subscription pages. Links of this kind pay the publisher a commission when a click turns into a sign-up, and Black Friday deal lists are one of the places where that spending by streaming services shows up. Neither Disney nor 9to5Google discloses the commission involved.
What the sources do not say
Neither 9to5Google nor Disney's offer page states how many subscribers took the 2025 offer, how many stayed after their promotional year, or whether this year's offer is aimed at any particular group beyond new and returning US adults. Disney does not say whether the promotion is available through partners or app stores, and the offer page gives no figure for the number of ads a subscriber on either plan will see.
