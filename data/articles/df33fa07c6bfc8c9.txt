TITLE: Video placement type
OUTLET: PPC Land

What the field is
Video placement type is a reporting dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that labels each video impression by the kind of environment the ad played in. It does not say which site or app carried the ad, nor where on a page it sat. It answers a narrower question: was the ad part of a video stream the viewer chose to watch, attached to a video that played alongside an article, a full-screen takeover, or a player with no editorial video at all? The field exists because those environments carry very different prices, completion rates and attention, and because sellers have spent years labelling cheaper inventory as the premium kind.
Amazon's unified reporting dimensions reference, updated on November 11, 2026, lists the field in its Supply category. PPC Land, in its count of the 156 unified report dimensions published on November 12, 2026, recorded that it separates "in-stream, accompanying content, interstitial, or out-stream (no content/standalone)." Those four labels are not Amazon's invention. They follow, almost word for word, the video placement subtypes that IAB Tech Lab wrote into OpenRTB in March 2023.
How the classification works
The value starts life in the bid request. When a publisher's supply-side platform (SSP) offers a video impression through OpenRTB, the real-time bidding protocol, it fills an attribute called plcmt inside the video object. Index Exchange's documentation, last updated May 4, 2026, lists the four values IAB Tech Lab recommends:
- 1 - Instream: the ad plays before, during or after video content, "with sound on by default, or the user has given clear intent to watch the video."
- 2 - Accompanying content: the ad plays around video content "with sound off by default", typically a player embedded beside an article that starts as it scrolls into view.
- 3 - Interstitial: the ad plays without video content and is "the primary focus of the page with sound off by default", usually occupying most of the screen.
- 4 - No content/standalone: the ad plays "without streaming video content such as within an article or banner", including floating players, slideshows and native feed units.
A demand-side platform (DSP) such as Amazon's reads that value, bids or declines, and records what it bought. Amazon's reporting then turns the stored signal into a readable label, with "out-stream" covering the standalone bucket. In a unified report, adding Video placement type splits a campaign's impressions, cost and completions into one row per value. Amazon's rule for fields that do not fit a product is that "its value appears as null", so display or sponsored ads rows carry no value.
The field rarely travels alone. Two companions sit beside it in the Supply category: Ad break position, which records "beginning (pre-roll), middle (mid-roll), or end (post-roll)", and Video player size, which runs from small to large plus unknown, both as summarised by PPC Land. In the OpenRTB request those correspond to separate attributes: startdelay (0 for pre-roll, -1 for a generic mid-roll, -2 for post-roll), the player width and height, and playbackmethod, which says whether the player started on page load with sound, muted, on click or on entering the viewport.
A worked example shows how the signals combine. A news site plays a clip only after a reader clicks it; the request should carry plcmt 1, startdelay 0 and a click-to-play method, and Amazon would report it as in-stream, pre-roll. The same publisher's muted player that starts when an article scrolls into view should carry plcmt 2, reported as accompanying content, even if the ad still plays before a short clip. A video unit that floats in the corner with no clip behind it is plcmt 4, reported as out-stream.
Who sets it and who reads it
The sell side decides the value. Publishers configure their players, and SSPs pass or rewrite the signal. Because the label determines which buyers bid and at what price, the incentive to round upwards is built in. The buy side reads it twice: before the auction, through targeting, and afterwards, through reporting. Amazon added in-stream and out-stream position targeting to Amazon DSP on December 19, 2024, according to its launch announcement, stating that the features "replace the previous Video Ad Format targeting option." The same notice said only in-stream targeting applied to Streaming TV "as all Streaming TV supply is in-stream based on OpenRTB standards", while online video could take either. Coverage began with third-party and Amazon Publisher Direct inventory, with Amazon owned-and-operated supply planned for the second quarter of 2025.
Origin and evolution
For most of programmatic video's history the label was blunt. OpenRTB 2.5, released in December 2016, carried a video placement attribute with five values: in-stream, in-banner, in-article, in-feed, and interstitial, slider or floating. Nothing in it said whether sound was on or whether anyone had chosen to watch. A muted autoplay player beside an article could, and often did, declare itself in-stream, and buyers paying in-stream prices had no field to tell the difference.
The commercial pressure came from audits. In June 2023, Adalytics reported that in some campaigns between 42% and 75% of TrueView in-stream spend went to Google video partner sites and apps that did not meet Google's own standards. Google rejected the findings, calling them "extremely inaccurate" in a company blog post. The dispute was never settled in public, but it put the meaning of "in-stream" at the centre of video buying.
IAB Tech Lab had already moved. Its March 2023 update to the digital video guidelines, shipped in OpenRTB release 2.6-202303, introduced plcmt and tied in-stream status to sound and viewer intent. Hillary Slattery, IAB Tech Lab's senior director of product management and programmatic, told AdExchanger that the Tech Lab "considers the video.placement field deprecated as of March 2023." Google required publishers to send plcmt in Ad Exchange bid requests from April 1, 2024, according to AdExchanger, and its video publisher policies took effect the same day, with a September 2024 revision barring autoplay until at least half the unit is visible.
Enforcement turned into open conflict on June 17, 2024, when The Trade Desk restricted access to Yahoo's open marketplace video inventory for some advertisers, arguing that Yahoo labelled accompanying content as in-stream. On February 4, 2025, IAB Tech Lab clarified the placement definitions, setting out concurrent conditions for in-stream around default sound, user intent and player layout. Adform adopted the new categories in its SSP on April 15, 2025, keeping the legacy field during migration.
Why it matters for marketers
The field is the after-the-fact check on a price premium. In-stream video, the slot buyers equate with television-style viewing, is generally priced above out-stream, which is why the label matters. If a report shows a campaign targeted at in-stream delivering heavily into accompanying content, either the targeting leaked or the supply was relabelled. Before plcmt, that distinction was not visible in most DSP reports at all.
It also changes how other numbers read. Completion rates and viewability for a muted player that starts on scroll are not comparable with those for a clip someone clicked. PPC Land's explainer on online video sets out how plcmt, startdelay and playbackmethod combine. Amazon's decision to expose the field in standard reporting, rather than only in custom data feeds, puts the split in front of advertisers who never read a bid request.
Limitations and disputes
The label is self-declared. Amazon reports what the supply path told it; it does not independently verify sound state or intent. Jounce Media reported that six of the top 10 DSPs had not adopted the new specification, according to AdExchanger's January 2025 coverage, and estimated accompanying content at about 30% of online video supply. When Google told AdExchanger that "Display & Video 360 is still recognizing both fields", it underlined that the old and new signals coexisted. Chris Kane, Jounce's founder, said it was "not appropriate" for an SSP to unilaterally downgrade a customer's label.
Definitions also blur at the edges. Browsers mute autoplay by default, so the sound-on test pushes almost all web autoplay out of in-stream; Magnite cited one prediction that "less than 10% of web video" would qualify. Some vendor guides even list the numeric values in a different order, with JW Player placing standalone at 3 and interstitial at 4, against the Index Exchange and IAB order. Amazon has not published, in a page that could be read for this article, whether its reported values are mapped one-to-one from plcmt or partly inferred from the legacy field.
How it differs from similar fields
Placement classification is the sponsored ads field that sorts results by page location, such as top of search or product pages, not by video environment. Placement name and Placement size identify the specific DVA+ slot and its dimensions. Ad break position records where in a break the ad ran, while Supply type separates Amazon owned-and-operated inventory from the open internet. Ad format, finally, describes the creative, not the environment it played in.
Recent developments
The industry is now trying to move past placement labels. On July 9, 2026, the IAB released its Redefining Media Types standard for public comment, proposing viewing-experience buckets plus eight impression-level attributes, including sound state, skippability and full-screen presentation, to be encoded in OpenRTB and AdCOM. The final version was published on September 28, 2026, according to the IAB. Amazon, meanwhile, extended VAST 4 support to third-party supply in 34 markets in September 2026 and published the unified dimensions reference on November 11. As of November 2026, Video placement type reports the 2023 taxonomy; whether Amazon adds the new attributes is not yet known.
