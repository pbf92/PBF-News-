TITLE: Nearly 3 in 4 Walmart test-and-treat visits come after 5 p.m. or on weekends
OUTLET: PPC Land

On November 18, 2026, Walmart said that nearly three-quarters of the flu, strep throat and COVID-19 visits handled by its pharmacy Testing & Treatment service take place on weekends or after 5 p.m. The figure sits inside a seasonal post that restates the service's reach: more than 1,500 of the retailer's roughly 4,600 pharmacies, in 24 states, where a pharmacist can assess symptoms, run a test, prescribe and fill the prescription in a single visit.
In Short
Walmart said on November 18 that most of its pharmacy test-and-treat visits for flu, strep throat and COVID-19 happen after 5 p.m. or on weekends, and that the service runs at more than 1,500 pharmacies in 24 states. It matters to health and consumer brands because the retailer is pulling cold-and-flu season into one trip that joins the pharmacy counter, prescriptions and its own seasonal shopping pages. The service still covers about a third of Walmart's pharmacies, what a pharmacist can test for depends on the state, and the post publishes no prices.
A seasonal post built around one trip
The post, titled "From Prevention to Treatment: Walmart Is Making Sick-Season Care Easier", appeared on Walmart's corporate newsroom on November 18 under its Health & Wellness heading. It carries no byline and lists a reading time of three minutes. Its subheading compresses the pitch into four words, "More Care. Fewer Stops.", followed by a claim of scale: flu and COVID-19 vaccines nationwide and Testing & Treatment at more than 1,500 pharmacies.
Nothing in it is a launch. The last expansion of the service, into Illinois, North Carolina and West Virginia, happened in September, and the post refers back to it rather than announcing new states. What the post adds is a framing, and one operating statistic.
The framing starts with the moment of illness. A fever, sore throat or cough, according to Walmart, "can turn an ordinary morning into a scramble: Who can stay home? Where do we go? What will it cost?" The answer the post offers is the store. "As cold and flu season gets underway, Walmart is working to make it easier for customers to take care of their health in a place that's already part of their everyday routines," it states.
To support the case, Walmart cites its own research. Nearly one-third of consumers surveyed, 31%, "reported cost-related barriers to managing their health," according to the post, while one in four, 25%, reported convenience-related barriers, "including transportation, missing work and the time required to visit a doctor's office." A footnote names the source as "Walmart, Customer Insights & Strategy - Annual Health & Wellness Usage & Attitude foundational study." It gives no sample size, fieldwork dates or question wording, so the two percentages cannot be set against outside surveys with any confidence.
The footprint behind the pitch
Most of the numbers in the post describe Walmart's physical reach rather than the service itself.
Set against each other, the figures show how much of the network the service has yet to reach. On Walmart's own numbers, Testing & Treatment runs at roughly a third of its pharmacies, about 33%, and in fewer than half of US states. The network averages a little over three pharmacists per pharmacy. Those calculations are PPC Land's, not Walmart's, and they rest on rounded figures ("approximately" and "more than") in the source.
The post leans on the pharmacist as the point of contact. "Our pharmacists are often one of the most accessible healthcare professionals in the communities we serve," said Kevin Host, senior vice president, Pharmacy, Walmart U.S. "Whether customers have questions about vaccines, need help understanding their symptoms or are looking for testing and treatment options, we're focused on making it easier for them to take the next step for their health."
How Testing & Treatment works, according to Walmart
According to Walmart, a Testing & Treatment visit follows four steps at participating pharmacies.
- A Walmart pharmacist assesses the customer's symptoms and determines whether testing is appropriate.
- Depending on location and eligibility, the pharmacist can test for flu, COVID-19 and/or strep throat.
- If treatment is clinically appropriate, the pharmacist can prescribe medication.
- The pharmacist fills the prescription during the same visit.
Access runs two ways. "Customers can schedule appointments online, and walk-ins are welcome at participating locations," the post states. The service has its own page on walmart.com, which the post links from the words Testing & Treatment.
The legal footing is spelled out in two places. In the body, Walmart writes: "These services are made possible by state policies that allow pharmacists to provide care within their education and training, giving patients another timely option, particularly in communities with limited access to healthcare professionals." The fine print at the bottom of the page reads, in full: "Testing & Treatment services vary by state and location and are subject to eligibility requirements and applicable state laws. In North Carolina and West Virginia, Testing & Treatment is available for flu and COVID-19 only. Immunization availability and eligibility vary by location, and state, age and health restrictions may apply."
The September additions are the only states the post names.
The service is not new. Walmart introduced it on August 29, 2023, in 12 states, according to the company's announcement at the time. Chain Drug Review listed them in September 2023 as Arkansas, Colorado, Florida, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Michigan, Minnesota, South Dakota, Tennessee and Washington. That 2023 announcement also carried a price: a cash-only visit starting at around $133 depending on the service, with health savings accounts accepted and insurance billing planned for later, according to Walmart. Becker's Hospital Review reported different per-test figures the same week. The November 18 post gives no price at all, and says nothing about insurance.
After 5 p.m. and on weekends
The timing statistic is the post's most specific claim about how the service is used. "Nearly three-quarters of Walmart's Testing & Treatment services for flu, strep throat or COVID-19 occur on weekends or after 5 p.m., helping customers access care at times that better fit their schedules," according to Walmart.
It is presented as a share, not a count. The post does not say how many visits it covers, over what period, or how the total splits between weekday evenings and weekends. Nor does it say whether the share differs between the three conditions.
Even so, it is operating data from Walmart's own pharmacy counters, a form of first-party data that the retailer has chosen to publish. The hours it describes, evenings and weekends, are the hours in which the post positions the pharmacy as an alternative to a separate trip to a doctor's office, the very trip its survey respondents named as a barrier. Why does that matter outside health care? For brands that time seasonal media to illness, it is a published indicator of when a pharmacy visit actually happens, at least at Walmart.
From clinics to counters
The post marks a narrower ambition than Walmart once had. The retailer said at the end of April 2024 that it would close its 51 Walmart Health centers in five states and shut its virtual care service, saying it did not have a sustainable business model to continue, according to NPR.
What has replaced the clinic model is a mix of pharmacy services and referral. In January 2026, Walmart launched Better Care Services, which it described at the time as a one-stop digital destination giving access to a curated network of third-party providers for urgent care and behavioral health, along with access to LillyDirect, according to Walmart's January 8 release on Business Wire. The same release paired the launch with price rollbacks on more than 1,000 wellness items.
The sick-season post folds that platform into the sick-season message. Better Care Services is "Walmart's digital destination for curated third-party health care providers," according to the post, where customers "can compare available providers for needs such as urgent care and connect directly with the option that works best for them." The post's closing line points readers to "virtual care through Better Care Services". Walmart, in other words, no longer employs the clinicians at that end of the journey; it lists them.
Other recent Walmart health items sit alongside the post as related stories. On September 15, 2026, Walmart announced a Health Ambassador pilot that "will explore how dedicated pharmacists can help rural customers navigate individual health goals, clinical services, nutrition resources and digital tools." The Shelby Report put the pilot at six months in five rural stores, in Lexington, Tennessee; Caro, Michigan; Franklin, Virginia; Fort Scott, Kansas; and Searcy, Arkansas, with a focus on diabetes, weight management and maternal health. On October 13, 2026, Walmart outlined 2027 benefits for its own associates that include $0 prescriptions and up to 12 weeks of paid NICU leave, according to the related story summary on the page.
Walmart is not alone in the format. When it launched Testing & Treatment in 2023, Becker's Hospital Review reported that Walgreens planned similar services in 13 states and that CVS planned to evaluate symptoms and prescribe cough suppressants in 10 states, without tests. The legal driver in each case is the same: state rules that widen what a pharmacist may do.
Vaccines, survival kits and the shopping pages
Prevention gets a shorter section. "Flu and COVID-19 immunizations are available at Walmart pharmacies nationwide," the post states, and pharmacists "can answer questions about recommended vaccines and help customers understand which options may be appropriate based on age, health history and other factors." The fine print adds that availability and eligibility vary by location, with state, age and health restrictions.
The post then moves from the counter to the aisle. "Beyond the pharmacy, customers can shop the Sick Day Survival Kit, explore the Kids' Health Shop or find seasonal essentials through the Cough, Cold & Flu Shop, with convenient pickup and delivery options available," it states. Each name links to a page on walmart.com.
The one-hour claim appears only in the link address of the Sick Day Survival Kit. The text of the post makes no delivery-time promise, and it names no brands or products.
Why the pharmacy counter matters to advertisers
For the advertising industry, the significance lies less in the service than in where it puts the shopper. A pharmacy visit is a store visit, and Walmart's stores feed its retail media network, Walmart Connect. Walmart Connect grew 43% in the US in the second quarter of fiscal 2027, while the global advertising business, including VIZIO, grew 38%. For the full fiscal year 2026, Walmart's global advertising revenue came close to $6.4 billion, up 46%.
The store base is also the measurement base. When Spotify ads arrived in Walmart DSP in October, Spotify said eligible campaigns could connect to outcomes across more than 4,600 Walmart stores. And since late October, Vibe advertisers can target Walmart shopper audiences built from store and web data. A post that steers cold-and-flu demand toward a single trip, counter first and shopping pages second, steers it into that system. For endemic advertisers in over-the-counter remedies, the seasonal shops named in the post are the pages where Walmart gathers that demand online.
There is a boundary, though. Assessments, test results and prescriptions are health records, and pharmacy data falls under federal privacy rules such as HIPAA, a different regime from ordinary purchase histories.
