TITLE: Anthropic’s $81.3 Billion IPO: What Claude Buyers and Partners Should Watch
OUTLET: Channel Insider

Claude’s first day on Wall Street had a $2 trillion moment.
Anthropic opened on Nasdaq Thursday at $111.50 a share, 34% above its $83 IPO price, briefly putting its fully diluted valuation near $2 trillion. By the closing bell, shares had eased to $97.17, still up 17% from the offer price and valuing the company at about $1.75 trillion on the same basis.
The debut makes Anthropic the first major frontier AI lab to go public, giving investors a direct bet on the business behind Claude.
For enterprise buyers and partners, the more useful numbers will come in its quarterly filings, where they can track how fast the business is growing and what it costs to keep it running.
A record IPO with a rocky finish
Anthropic raised approximately $81.3 billion through its public offering, surpassing the $75 billion raised by SpaceX in its June listing. A separate $26 billion private placement brought its total fundraising to approximately $107.3 billion.
The private placement attracted major technology companies, including Nvidia, Alphabet, Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, Micron and TSMC.
The company’s first trading session also exposed a gap between investor enthusiasm and market confidence. While Anthropic shares gained 17% from their IPO price, major technology stocks weakened, with the Nasdaq Composite falling roughly 2.3%.
Reuters reported that the company’s fully diluted valuation includes employee stock options and restricted stock units that have not yet vested. Based on outstanding shares, its market value reached approximately $1.5 trillion.
Enterprise AI is driving the valuation
Anthropic’s appeal goes beyond its Claude chatbot. The company has built much of its business around enterprise customers, particularly organizations using AI for software development and business operations.
Its financial growth has been substantial. In October, we reported that Anthropic generated $16.3 billion in revenue during the first half of 2026, compared with $4.6 billion for the entirety of 2025.
However, the spending required to sustain that growth remains a concern. Reuters reported that Anthropic has nearly $900 billion in computing commitments involving infrastructure partners Amazon and Google. The company also reported a $104 billion net loss during the first nine months of 2026.
For enterprise technology buyers, Anthropic’s biggest challenge may be proving that its AI business can grow without allowing infrastructure costs to consume its earnings.
The company needs enormous amounts of computing power to train and operate increasingly capable models. Its disclosed commitments also tie its future to a relatively small group of cloud and semiconductor suppliers.
That creates an important question for investors: Can Anthropic turn enterprise AI adoption into sustainable profits while continuing to spend heavily on computing infrastructure?
The company has also indicated that it may eventually use debt financing to support its expansion. CEO Dario Amodei told CNBC that Anthropic hopes to reach investment-grade status and sees debt as a financing option.
What the IPO means for enterprise IT buyers
Anthropic’s public listing gives enterprise customers, technology partners and competitors a new financial benchmark for evaluating the AI market.
For MSPs, MSSPs and enterprise IT teams, several developments deserve attention:
- AI procurement: Anthropic’s valuation reinforces investor expectations for enterprise AI adoption, potentially increasing pressure on businesses to demonstrate returns from their AI investments.
- Cloud infrastructure: Its substantial computing commitments underline how dependent advanced AI services remain on cloud providers and semiconductor suppliers.
- Vendor stability: Public financial reporting will give enterprise buyers more visibility into Anthropic’s revenue, losses and infrastructure spending when evaluating its long-term viability.
- AI governance: Anthropic’s safety commitments will face greater scrutiny as the company balances commercial growth with concerns about increasingly capable models.
The real test starts after the IPO
Anthropic’s debut is an important win for the enterprise AI market, but its first-day performance should not be mistaken for proof that the industry’s economics are settled.
Unlike established software companies, frontier AI developers face unusually high computing costs and must continually invest in more powerful models to remain competitive. Anthropic’s $900 billion in computing commitments make that challenge particularly significant.
There is also a distinction between investor enthusiasm and enterprise demand. A soaring share price does not automatically mean businesses will increase AI spending or renew expensive contracts.
The real test ahead is whether Anthropic can convert its enterprise momentum into predictable revenue and improved profitability. Its public filings will offer a closer look at that progress, while its performance could influence how investors value other AI companies, including OpenAI.
