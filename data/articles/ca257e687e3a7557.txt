TITLE: Cardiff-Menevia: First black Catholic bishop to be ordained in England and Wales
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Canon Benedict Koledoye has been appointed by Pope Leo XIV as the new Auxiliary Bishop of the Archdiocese of Cardiff-Menevia.
Born in Ado-Ekiti, Nigeria, he will become the first black priest ordained to the Episcopate in England and Wales.
Bishop-elect Benedict is currently chancellor of the Archdiocese and parish priest of St. Mary of the Angels, Holy Family and St Francis in Cardiff. He previously served as administrator of St Joseph’s Co-Cathedral in Swansea for a decade.
He was ordained as a priest in Nigeria’s Diocese of Ondo in 1998. He served as its co-ordinator for human rights and prison programmes, going on to direct the Justice, Development and Peace Commission after postgraduate study in Bradford.
The bishop-elect will support the pastoral and governance work of Archbishop Mark O'Toole, the diocesan bishop. He will take on the titular see of Gisipa (Gisipensis) which is in modern-day Tunisia.
A statement from the canon said: “I am deeply grateful to His Holiness, Pope Leo XIV, for appointing me Auxiliary Bishop of the Archdiocese of Cardiff-Menevia.
“The news, conveyed to me by the Apostolic Nuncio, came as a profound surprise. Mindful of my own limitations and unworthiness, I find strength and reassurance in the words of Scripture: 'No one takes this honour upon himself, but only when called by God' (Hebrews 5:4). Trusting in God's providence and the maternal intercession of the Blessed Virgin Mary, I humbly accept this call to serve.
“Since receiving this appointment, I have become ever more conscious of the great responsibility entrusted to a bishop. Yet I move forward with serenity and hope, knowing that I will be supported and guided by Archbishop Mark O'Toole as we work together in the service of God's people throughout Cardiff-Menevia.”
Archbishop Mark said: “It is a great joy for me that the Holy Father has appointed Canon Benedict Koledoye as Auxiliary Bishop. He is a priest well known to us all in the Archdiocese for his dedication and love for the Lord and the Church. We give thanks to God for the roots of his life and faith in Africa. The generous fruits of this we have experienced during the past fifteen years of his priestly ministry in South Wales.”
The Episcopal ordination is scheduled to take place in St David's Cathedral, Cardiff, on 2 February 2027 at noon.
