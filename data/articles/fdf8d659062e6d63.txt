TITLE: Weight-loss jabs are a ‘sticking plaster’: obesity expert says the real fix is our food environment
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

One in four UK adults lives with obesity, yet the public conversation is still dominated by the idea that weight is a matter of willpower. As interest in GLP-1 medications surges, a University of Warwick obesity specialist is warning that the injections, valuable as they are, treat the symptom and not the cause.
Dr Tom Barber, Professor of Endocrinology at the University of Warwick and lead of the obesity service at University Hospitals Coventry and Warwickshire NHS Trust, argues in a new book that the biggest driver of weight gain is the environment we live in, particularly the food on offer.
He said: “GLP-1 therapies are a valuable tool, but on their own they are a sticking plaster. We are all genetic misfits for our modern, obesogenic environment, and unless we reform that environment, particularly our food environment, we will keep treating the symptoms rather than the cause.”
Dr Barber sets out four points that he says the evidence contradicts:
- 1. Weight is not simply a lifestyle choice. Body weight is strongly influenced by genes that shape appetite and metabolism. Average weight has risen across the population in recent decades, but where an individual sits in that distribution remains largely genetically determined.
- 2. Sleep matters. Sleep deprivation boosts appetite and lowers metabolic rate. Trial evidence suggests that helping short sleepers get around an hour more a night can cut daily calorie intake noticeably, without telling them what to eat.
- 3. Treating individuals will not be enough. Evidence on the food environment is growing. Studies show people eat markedly more when the food on offer is ultra-processed, even when free to choose their own portions. In England, the soft drinks levy, which pushed manufacturers to cut sugar, has been linked to lower obesity rates among some groups of primary school girls.to enable a transition towards an environment that facilitates a healthy lifestyle, diet and body weight.
- 4. Regaining weight is biology, not weakness. After weight loss, appetite rises and metabolic rate falls, so the body pushes back toward its previous weight. Studies show people can still feel hungrier a year after dieting than they did before. Even people taking GLP-1 drugs tend to regain a substantial share of lost weight once treatment stops.
Dr Barber said: “Regaining weight after dieting is not greed, laziness or a weak will. It is biology. After weight loss, appetite goes up and metabolic rate comes down, so the body is working against you. We need to stop blaming people for that.”
Dr Barber has led the Coventry obesity service since 2010 and has published 176 scientific papers. His new book, Obesity: Life Beyond the GLP-1 Injection, covers appetite regulation, the gut microbiome, the limits of BMI, how to spot fad diets, and how to build less obesogenic environments across twelve illustrated chapters.

Notes to Editors
‘Obesity: Life Beyond the GLP-1 Injection’ is a newly launched book, written by Dr. Tom Barber. This book is written for everyone. As public awareness of obesity and weight-loss medications continues to grow, this timely publication aims to improve public understanding and awareness of obesity, challenge common misconceptions (including the ones outlined above), and provide an accessible, evidence-based overview of one of the most significant health challenges facing our society today.
The book addresses the gap between rapidly advancing obesity science and the often-confusing information available to the public. Across twelve chapters, it explores the complex biological, behavioural and environmental factors that influence obesity, lifestyle and management strategies and GLP-1 therapies; translating current scientific evidence into language accessible to both healthcare professionals and the wider public. Each chapter is beautifully illustrated with colourful figures that complement the text and bring it to life.
Topics covered include:
- The science of appetite regulation and metabolism
- The role of the gut microbiome and gut-brain axis
- Physical activity, muscle health, sleep and dietary fibre
- GLP-1 medications and other obesity treatments
- The limitations of BMI as a measure of health
- Societal stigma and misconceptions surrounding obesity
- How to identify evidence-based approaches and avoid fad diets
- Future strategies for creating healthier, less obesogenic environments
Importantly, the book emphasises that obesity is a complex chronic health condition rather than a simple consequence of lifestyle choices alone, helping readers better understand the biological and societal factors that contribute to weight gain and weight management.
