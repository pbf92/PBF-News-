TITLE: Family and friends shape young people's relationship with faith, study finds
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Family influence, friendships and personal belief all play an important role in keeping young people involved in religious communities, a new study in the US suggests.
Research by the Springtide Research Institute found that 60% of young people aged 13 to 25 still identify with the religious tradition in which they were raised.
The report, based on interviews with 51 young people and a survey of almost 3,000, highlights the influence of parents and grandparents in introducing children to faith through church attendance and conversations at home.
One 24-year-old evangelical Protestant woman said: “My mom would read the Bible to us every night before bed and in the mornings, so it was very much a part of our lives,”
Friendships were also found to be important, with youth groups and shared activities helping young people develop a sense of belonging.
One 16-year-old, who described herself as spiritual but not religious, said being part of a group led by people her own age made it easier to connect.
“So, I think it felt a lot easier to connect with them because it felt like they were kind of at our stages in life as well,” she said.
However, the research also found that some young people struggled to feel included, particularly in congregations where there were few people their age.
Personal belief was another important factor. Nearly half of those surveyed – 47% – said religion was among the most important things in their lives, or the most important.
An 18-year-old Catholic woman described how her understanding of the Mass had changed as she grew older.
“When I was little, I would just go to Mass and just go through the motions. I would never really think about what was actually happening or I would just go with my family. It was just part of my life, but it wasn’t really super significant to me in any way.
“And then I realized, like, ‘Wow, this is what this means, and this is why I do this.’ It was like, I’m doing this for me, not just because those around me are doing it.”
