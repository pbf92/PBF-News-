TITLE: Landmark publication makes PROLIFIC risk score publicly available to pulmonary fibrosis researchers
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

The Pulmonary Fibrosis Foundation and Prognostic Lung Fibrosis Consortium (PROLIFIC) today announced the publication of a landmark paper in The Lancet Respiratory Medicine detailing the development and validation of the PROLIFIC Risk Score, a novel blood biomarker-based tool designed to predict disease progression in people with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF).
For the first time, the publication makes the methodology and formula behind the PROLIFIC Risk Score publicly available, giving researchers the tools to apply and evaluate the score in their own IPF research. IPF is a life-threatening, progressive disease that causes scarring of the lungs and makes it increasingly difficult to breathe. More than 250,000 Americans are living with IPF and other interstitial lung diseases (ILD), underscoring the urgent need for better treatments.
“Publication of this study in The Lancet Respiratory Medicine is a major milestone for PROLIFIC and for the broader ILD research community,” said Peter Schafer, PhD, lead author of the study and Chair of PROLIFIC. “By publishing the full methodology and formula, researchers can begin evaluating and using the score in their own work while we continue advancing it through the FDA qualification pathway.”
Researchers evaluated 12 blood biomarkers reflecting three key processes involved in IPF: lung cell injury, scarring and inflammation. The resulting PROLIFIC Risk Score is designed to estimate the likelihood of disease progression, including lung function decline, lung transplantation or death within one year.
The score was derived and validated in two independent IPF cohorts totaling 886 participants, including 657 participants from the Pulmonary Fibrosis Foundation Patient Registry and 229 participants from the ISABELA Phase 3 clinical trials.
Addressing a Major Challenge in IPF Research
The clinical course of IPF can vary considerably from one patient to another, making it difficult to predict who will progress quickly and who may experience a slower course of disease. That uncertainty also presents challenges in clinical trials, where differences among participants can make it more difficult to evaluate the effects of an investigational treatment.
The PROLIFIC Risk Score was developed primarily as a drug development tool that could help researchers better characterize the prognosis of clinical trial participants and potentially improve the design and efficiency of IPF studies.
The score is currently progressing through the U.S. Food and Drug Administration’s Center for Drug Evaluation and Research Biomarker Qualification Program. In 2025, it became the first IPF biomarker accepted into the program, an early step toward potential qualification for use in drug development.
“The PFF Patient Registry was created so that the experiences and contributions of people living with pulmonary fibrosis and interstitial lung disease could accelerate research,” said Scott Staszak, President and CEO of the Pulmonary Fibrosis Foundation. “Seeing Registry data contribute directly to the development of the PROLIFIC Risk Score demonstrates exactly why participation in research matters.”
Expanding Beyond IPF
PROLIFIC is also evaluating whether similar biomarker approaches could have value in other forms of interstitial lung disease.
The consortium has analyzed additional samples, including those from the PROFILE study led by investigators at Imperial College London, as it explores the potential application of prognostic biomarkers beyond IPF.
“Our goal is to understand how these biomarkers can be used most effectively to advance research and drug development across pulmonary fibrosis and other interstitial lung diseases,” Schafer said.
The paper, “Multi-biomarker risk score for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis based on serum proteins by the Prognostic Lung Fibrosis Consortium (PROLIFIC): a risk score development and validation study,” is available in The Lancet Respiratory Medicine at https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanres/article/PIIS2213-2600(26)00297-3/fulltext.
Researchers interested in learning more about the PROLIFIC Risk Score can visit the PROLIFIC web page.

About PROLIFIC
The Prognostic Lung Fibrosis Consortium (PROLIFIC) brings together industry and nonprofit partners to develop biomarker tools for pulmonary fibrosis research and drug development. PROLIFIC is facilitated in part by the Pulmonary Fibrosis Foundation.
About the Pulmonary Fibrosis Foundation
The Pulmonary Fibrosis Foundation is committed to accelerating research, empowering our community, and transforming care so that everyone with pulmonary fibrosis can live a better life.
