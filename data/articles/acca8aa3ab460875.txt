TITLE: EasyJet flight makes emergency return to Edinburgh Airport after 'loud bang' heard
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

EasyJet flight makes emergency return to Edinburgh Airport after 'loud bang' heard
An easyJet plane declared an emergency and returned to Edinburgh Airport shortly after take-off
An aircraft travelling from Edinburgh to London landed safely after declaring an emergency shortly following take-off. Residents in the area reported hearing a "loud bang" as the jet went overhead.
The easyJet service was forced to return after experiencing a "technical issue" at approximately 9pm on Thursday, November 26. Aviation communication systems suggested engine failure, with alerts indicating an engine stall and dangerously elevated exhaust gas temperatures, according to reports.
The crew transmitted an emergency Squawk 7700 signal, an international aviation designation for a general emergency.
Aircraft Communications Addressing and Reporting System (ACARS) data showed that the left engine was shut down while in flight, Flight Drama reports.
The Airbus A321neo circled several times before touching down safely at Edinburgh Airport, where emergency response teams, including police and fire services, stood ready. Several nearby roads have been shut.
An easyJet spokesperson told the Manchester Evening News: "Flight EZY816 from Edinburgh to London Gatwick was required to return to Edinburgh due to a technical issue.
"The flight landed normally and was met by emergency services in line with procedures and a precautionary measure only. A replacement aircraft has been arranged to operate the flight to London Gatwick this evening.
"The safety and wellbeing of customers and crew is always easyJet's highest priority and easyJet operates its fleet of aircraft in strict compliance with manufacturers' guidelines."
This marks the second easyJet emergency incident in mid-flight within a 10-day period. Flight EZY897J departed Bristol on the morning of 18 November with an expected arrival time of 10.35am in Edinburgh.
Flight tracking information shows the aircraft performed a U-turn above the Brecon Beacons in South Wales, circled over the Bristol Channel, before making its way back to Bristol airport.
The Airbus A320 started transmitting the 7700 emergency code soon after departure. easyJet Flight Status confirmed the emergency resulted from a bird strike.
