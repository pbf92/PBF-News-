TITLE: Gambling ads reach YouTube Home and Search, 29 days after the Masthead
OUTLET: PPC Land

Google today changed YouTube's ad requirements to permit advertising on the Home and Search surfaces for three categories: certified online gambling advertisers, certified fantasy sports advertisers targeting the United States or Canada, and prediction markets. According to a change log entry in Google's Advertising Policies Help Center titled "Updates to YouTube Home and Search Ad Requirements (November 2026)," the update was posted and took effect on the same day, with no notice period. It arrives 29 days after YouTube began accepting certified online gambling ads on the Masthead, the reserved slot at the top of its homepage.
In Short
YouTube now allows ads from certified gambling operators, fantasy sports companies and prediction markets on its home feed and in its search results. That matters to casinos, sportsbooks, fantasy operators and event-contract exchanges, because Google's notices had until now opened only the Masthead slot at the top of YouTube's homepage to gambling ads. Each category needs its own Google certification, fantasy sports is held to the US and Canada, and every ad still has to clear local law and age restrictions.
What the notice says
The entry is brief: one framing sentence, three bullet points, a compliance line and an effective date. According to Google, "On November 19, 2026, YouTube's ad requirements will be updated to permit advertising on Home and Search for the following categories where permitted by the Google Ads policies and local law."
The future tense is a leftover. The page carries the line "(Posted on November 19, 2026)" and closes with "This policy change will take effect on November 19, 2026." Publication and effect fall on the same day.
The three categories are defined in Google's own wording. The first is "Online Gambling: Certified Online gambling advertisers (excluding advertisers of online-gambling promoting content who are not subject to mandatory state or national gambling registration requirements) meeting the requirements set out in the Gambling and games policy."
The second covers fantasy sports, which Google files under online non-casino games rather than under online gambling: "Online non-casino games: Certified Online non-casino game advertisers of Fantasy Sports who meet the requirements set out in the Gambling and games policy and target the United States or Canada."
The third is the most layered. "Prediction Markets: Prediction Markets advertisers who have complied with our applicable ad policies by obtaining a Prediction Markets certification to serve ads in the USA, or hold an Online gambling certification in other regions."
One sentence applies to all three: "All ads must fully comply with the applicable policies and adhere to all legal, jurisdictional, and age restrictions."
That is the whole of it. The notice does not define Home or Search, does not name the ad formats that serve there, and does not mention Shorts, the watch page, Google Discover or Google TV. Read against its title, it refers to YouTube's home feed and YouTube's search results. The page also carries the standard Help Center disclaimer that the English version is the official language used to enforce Google Ads policies, and it seeds Google's AI support box with three suggested questions, among them "Which regions allow fantasy sports ads on YouTube?"
Three categories, three gates
Each category reaches Home and Search through a different certification, and two of the three come with a geographic limit written into the notice itself.
The exclusion in the first row is aimed at content that promotes gambling rather than at operators themselves. Google's Gambling and games policy allows both online gambling and online gambling-promoting content in its listed countries, so affiliates, comparison sites and tipsters sit in the second group. Under today's notice, those that are subject to a mandatory state or national registration regime can qualify if certified, and those that are not cannot.
How eligibility works
According to the notice and the two policy pages it links, the path onto Home and Search runs through six conditions.
- Category. The advertiser falls into one of three categories: online gambling, online non-casino games limited to fantasy sports, or prediction markets.
- Certification. Online gambling requires certification under the Gambling and games policy. Fantasy sports requires certification as an online non-casino game advertiser under the same policy. Prediction markets require a Prediction Markets certification for the US, or an online gambling certification in other regions.
- Geography. Fantasy sports advertisers must target the United States or Canada. Prediction Markets certification is tied to the USA. Online gambling follows the country list in the Gambling and games policy, which according to Google treats any type of online gambling not listed for a particular country as ineligible for certification.
- Policy and local law. The allowance applies only where Google Ads policies and local law permit it, a qualifier written into the opening sentence of the notice.
- Compliance. All ads must fully comply with the applicable policies and with legal, jurisdictional and age restrictions. According to Google's Gambling and games policy, serving gambling ads without a valid certification may result in an account being prohibited from running ads.
- Application route. According to the Gambling and games policy page as it reads today, online gambling applications are no longer supported through the Google Ads Help Center and must be submitted from within the Google Ads account starting November 19, 2026, the same day as the YouTube change. Existing applications are not affected. The Prediction Markets policy routes applications through a Google Ads contact form, one per target location.
What changed since the Masthead notice
The closest earlier version is the October notice for the Masthead. Google posted "Updates to YouTube Masthead Ad Requirements (October 2026)" on October 7, and it took effect on October 21. PPC Land reported at the time that the notice permitted certified online gambling on the Masthead and described that as an expansion of the existing allowance for sports betting.
Placed side by side, the two notices show how much has moved in six weeks.
Three differences stand out. The grammatical gap that PPC Land flagged in October, where the Masthead sentence never said who was certified, is gone: the November bullet names "Certified Online gambling advertisers." The compliance line has widened from the Gambling and Games policy to "the applicable policies," which it has to, since prediction markets are governed by a separate policy page. And the 14-day runway has disappeared.
The exclusion for gambling-promoting content, by contrast, is carried over word for word. So is the absence of any surface beyond the one in the title: the October notice did not mention Google TV, and today's does not mention the Masthead.
The requirements page has not caught up
Google rewrote its YouTube and Discover Feed ad requirements page on August 11, 2026, the second restatement of the same page in 11 months, and said that update was for language and readability and did not change enforcement. The first reorganisation, on September 30, 2025, was framed the same way.
Today's notice is a different kind of change: it alters what may run, and where. Yet when PPC Land checked the YouTube and Discover Feed ad requirements page today, it contained no reference to gambling, fantasy sports, online non-casino games or prediction markets, and no definition of Home or Search. The only surface it defines is the Masthead, which it describes as the most prominent advertising placement Google offers and whose separate format requirements it says may be stricter than those for other surfaces. For now, the change log entry is the only Google document that sets out the Home and Search allowance.
From one slot to the feed
Google's published notices on gambling advertising at YouTube have moved outward in steps, and for most of the past four years the Masthead sat at the centre of them.
When Google introduced specific Masthead content requirements in June 2021, gambling assets were on the prohibited list, covering offline gambling, online gambling, online non-casino games and social casino games. The first exception came on September 6, 2022, when Google allowed sports betting ads on the Masthead provided they complied with the Gambling and Games policy. By April 2025, according to PPC Land, the YouTube Masthead carried sports betting and daily fantasy sports exceptions for Canada, India and the United States.
Google TV followed a parallel track. Its Masthead, the first slot of the carousel on the Google TV home screen, opened to daily fantasy sports and sports betting ads in the US on April 30, 2025, and extended to the United Kingdom, excluding Northern Ireland, and Brazil on November 13, 2025.
Then came October 21, 2026, when certified online gambling as a whole, not only sports betting, was allowed onto the YouTube Masthead. Today's notice moves beyond that single reserved placement to the home feed and to search results.
The order is worth noting. Google's own requirements describe the Masthead as its most prominent placement, with standards that may be stricter than those for other surfaces. Gambling nonetheless cleared that stricter surface first, and the broader ones only today.
Prediction markets: two routes in one sentence
Of the three categories, prediction markets carry the most history at Google and the most moving parts in today's notice.
Google opened its advertising to prediction markets on January 21, 2026, limited to the United States and to two types of advertiser: a Designated Contract Market authorised by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission whose primary business is listing exchange-listed event contracts, or a brokerage offering access to such contracts. Nevada was excluded from the start. The list of excluded states then grew. Google prohibited prediction market ads in Ohio on June 2, 2026, Michigan and New York followed in July, and Connecticut became the fifth excluded state on October 7.
When PPC Land reported the Connecticut change on October 11, Google's main Prediction Markets policy page still listed only four excluded states. As of today, the page lists the United States excluding Connecticut, Michigan, Nevada, New York and Ohio. It continues to prohibit binary options and fixed-return options, and markets concerning games of chance or lotteries.
Today's notice refers only to "a Prediction Markets certification to serve ads in the USA." It names none of the five states. Read literally, the carve-outs still apply through the opening qualifier, "where permitted by the Google Ads policies and local law," and through the certification itself, which is applied for one target location at a time. The notice simply does not restate them.
The second half of the bullet is newer territory. Outside the US, the notice accepts prediction markets advertisers who "hold an Online gambling certification in other regions." Google's Prediction Markets policy lists only the United States as an approved location, so outside the US the notice routes prediction markets through the gambling certification. Prediction market operators have argued in US courts and before state regulators that their products are federally regulated financial contracts rather than gambling. For Home and Search, outside the US, Google has placed them in the gambling column. Which regions count as "other regions" is not stated.
The approach is not unique to Google. Amazon Ads, in a policy page PPC Land covered last week, places prediction markets among the categories that must pass its gambling ad verification.
