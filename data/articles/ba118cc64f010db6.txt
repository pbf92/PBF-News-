TITLE: Long-standing Manchester Christmas Markets trader makes big change after "AI slop" backlash
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

A long-standing Manchester Christmas Markets trader has made a big change to its posters after growing backlash over the use of AI, but some people said they feel they have not done enough.
Since the popular markets opened across the city centre at the start of the month, a number of stalls have been criticised for their posters as they were accused of reaching 'new levels of AI sloppification' with mocked-up images and artwork, whilst local designers also called it a ‘real shame’.
One of those met with criticism online was Bratwurst King, which has been running since 1999 and has become one of the most notable sausage vendors with its four locations across the city’s Christmas markets.

When approached by the Manchester Evening News earlier this month, an owner said they understood that some people did not approve of the images but that it was too late to fix their marketing for this year. However, they vowed to use real imagery next year.
But in a post on social media on Thursday evening (November 26), the team behind the popular stalls have already made efforts to rectify their imagery, which some people suggested did not fully reflect the real products.
Sharing an update, the Bratwurst King team said: “You spoke, we listened! We heard your feedback about our AI-generated pictures, and we completely understand.
“From now on, no more AI - just REAL food, REAL pictures and the REAL Bratwurst King experience! What you see is what you get. Fresh off the grill, just the way it should be!”

Whilst the move was praised as a step in the right direction, people online commented that they felt the changes the company had made only related to the photographs used in their posters with the artwork and text still believed to be AI-generated.
One person said: “You were so close". One other commented: "The images may be real, but the rest of the poster design is ai, no?” Another said: "It’s clearly still ai."
One other responded: “The graphic design is still AI you can literally tell from the font.” Another wrote: “So close. Just write on a blank piece of paper at this point.”
Speaking to the M.E.N earlier this month, an owner of Bratwurst King said of their use of AI: "It is the first year we have used it like this, but we have tried to make sure everything looks right. We have already taken some images down because they did not look the same. We understand that some people do not like it, and we have had a few people come up to us and tell us that we shouldn't be using AI.
"At this point, we have already paid for the posters and we cannot replace them this year, but we are already planning to go back to photos next year. That is the plan."
Earlier this month, the M.E.N also went to the markets to ask other stallowners and visitors how they felt about the rising use of AI at stalls this year. Visitor Nick, who was attending the markets with a friend, said people can pick up on the use of AI and explained how he now intentionally seeks out places that don’t use it.

He said: “We’ve been coming here for years, and this year as soon as we came out we saw everywhere had the same AI-font, the same AI poster and it did put me off going to places, so I started hunting out places that weren’t using it.”
Manchester-based digital illustrator Laura Frances Heitzman said she fully accepted how business is difficult, which might see some owners consider the use of AI to save money, but said that it comes across as ‘lazy and obvious’ to many.
She said: “If it’s an AI-generated image of your food, that’s not what your food looks like. That should be a photo of your food. I understand, business is tough, but it’s been tough for me as well, and I haven't just decided I’m going to spend less time drawing to save money and using Chat GPT to do my art."
