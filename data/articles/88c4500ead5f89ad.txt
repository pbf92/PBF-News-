Major police update on Co-op Live investigation after gas released in crowd
Detectives are working to trace more than 100 people who were at the gig - and liaising with law enforcement agencies across Europe
Police investigating a series of robberies at a Co-op Live gig are liaising with law enforcement agencies across Europe, it was revealed today.
An unknown gas substance was released during last Sunday night's gig by US rapper Don Toliver, leading to a full evacuation of the venue, the UK's largest arena. One person was taken to hospital and 89 concertgoers were treated at the scene.
Police initially said tear gas was believed to have been released, but later described it as an 'unknown substance'.

It then emerged that nine reports of jewellery theft were received following the aborted gig, with Greater Manchester Police now saying the force suspects the substance was released in the crowd 'as part of' the robberies.
In an update issued on Friday, GMP said investigating detectives suspect 'a small group of organised criminals' targeted people in the crowd to commit robberies.
The force said there were 'similar incidents' at two previous Don Toliver gigs held in Europe.
GMP, in the update, said it was also working to trace more than 100 people who were at the gig 'to potentially assist with our enquiries'.
Assistant chief constable Tom Harding issued an update on Friday.
He said: "So far, we suspect that a small group of organised criminals targeted people in the crowd to commit a series of robberies on the night. We’re working to identify those suspects and the substance that we believe was released in the crowd as part of these incidents. All of this work is ongoing at pace.

"There are similar incidents at two Don Toliver gigs in Europe that are also relevant to our enquiries. We’re in contact with law enforcement involved in those investigations. No arrests have been made, but our detectives are working through footage captured on mobile phones and CCTV.
"There are over a hundred people at the gig that we are working to try and contact to potentially assist with our enquiries. We thank all those that have assisted us so far.
"We also know many more people were impacted on the night and afterwards, physically and psychologically. What happened on Sunday night and the imagery on our TVs and online will bring back painful memories of 2017.
"Thankfully, we are not dealing with an incident of such a devastating nature or motive; however, we understand the impact this is likely to have caused."
No arrests have been made and tests are ongoing to identify the unknown substance.
More than 12,000 people were inside the venue as a major incident was declared. Mobile phone video footage showed concert-goers struggling to breathe and coughing as they walked out of the venue.
A police scene at Co-op Live has now been lifted, with planned gigs at the venue going ahead as normal.
