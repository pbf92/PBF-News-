TITLE: Should I buy a gaming console just to play 'GTA 6'? That depends on your FOMO.
OUTLET: Mashable SEA

Grand Theft Auto VI is out extremely soon, if you hadn't heard. Are you prepared?
By that, we mean do you have a PlayStation 5 or Xbox Series console at the ready for Thursday's big launch? If not, you're going to miss out on the biggest gaming launch of this year and many other years. FOMO is a natural human reaction to these sorts of things, and here at Mashable, we fully understand the pain of not being sure whether or not you should buy a console just for GTA 6.
So, let's lay out the cases for and against doing that.
Why you should buy a console for GTA 6
The primary and most obvious reason to buy a console for GTA 6 is that it's the only real way to play it at launch on Nov. 19. There is technically another way that we'll cover in a little while, but for all intents and purposes, you cannot have the same experience everyone else is having without buying a PS5 or Xbox Series console.
We don't know this yet for sure, but there's a possibility that GTA 6 is an incredible video game. You probably wouldn't want to miss out on that. What are you going to do while everyone else is having an amazing time parading through Leonida, read books? Come on, that sounds boring.
One thing that's also worth remembering is that other video games exist. A lot of them, in fact. If you buy a PS5 or Xbox Series console, you'll have access to the last six years of current-gen releases, some of which are extremely good. They might even be better than GTA 6! As a fun bonus, through the magic of backward compatibility, ports, remasters, and remakes, you'll also be able to play countless video games from previous generations of hardware, too.
Lastly, both consoles (at least the models with disc drives) work as 4K Blu-ray players, if you're into collecting physical versions of movies. That's cool, too.
Both consoles have been hard to find in stock (thanks in large part to GTA 6), but the Xbox Series S and PS5 Digital Edition are both in stock at Amazon, as of this writing.
Why you shouldn't buy a console for GTA 6
First, foremost, in the biggest, boldest font you can imagine: You shouldn't buy a console just for GTA 6 because they're stupid expensive these days.
Both Xbox and PlayStation have recently increased the prices of their consoles due to real-world economic concerns. The cheapest model right now is a $499 Xbox Series S, and Rockstar hasn't shown us that version of the game yet, leading to speculation that the game is better on PS5, which starts at $599. If you want the best possible version at launch, you'll need a PS5 Pro, which costs $899 — and is currently out of stock at most retailers.
In other words, there just isn't a particularly affordable way to play GTA 6 traditionally at launch, unless you already own the hardware.
There are, of course, other reasons to not get a console for GTA 6. There exists a slim likelihood that the game just isn't that good, though again, we have no idea at the time of publication. It also won't have an online component at launch, and no one knows when it will get one. For a lot of people, GTA Online is the only GTA experience that matters, and that audience will have very little to do in the new game until some nebulous point in the future when Rockstar decides to cater to them.
Maybe you just don't care that much about non-GTA video games, either. If that's the case, it's hard to recommend spending this much money on a box that will only play one game.
Are there alternative ways to play GTA 6?
There are two main ways to play GTA 6 without buying a PS5 or Xbox Series machine. One of them doesn't sound all that great, while the other one will require some patience.
The former is Xbox Cloud Gaming, a game streaming service available to Xbox Game Pass subscribers. Starting at 1 a.m. ET on Nov. 19, you'll technically be able to stream GTA 6 to your older Xbox One console.
To be clear, there are multiple massive reasons why you might not want to do this. For starters, it involves a monthly subscription. Beyond that, Microsoft recently imposed strict monthly hour limits on Cloud Gaming. With this system, you will be able to play at most 15 hours of GTA 6 per month. Many people are going to play more than that in the first weekend it's out. Even if those aren't concerns, game streaming is subject to the limits of your home internet, meaning that latency and buffering can and will occur. That version of the game will also be visually downgraded to some degree, per Rockstar.
If game streaming (understandably) doesn't work for you, the last option is to wait for the PC version. Unfortunately, Rockstar has not said anything about when GTA 6 will launch on PC, though a recent report pegged it as coming in early 2027. That will likely be the best version of the game in terms of visuals, but only if you have the requisite hardware. For those who already have capable gaming PCs, this might be the best option for playing GTA 6, provided you're patient enough to wait. For those who don't, this is going to be even more expensive than buying a console, so maybe don't bother.
