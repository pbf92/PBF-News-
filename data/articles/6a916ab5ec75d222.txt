Digital platforms account for 98% of ad violations, Meta leads with 73.4%: ASCI

- The Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) processed 3,951 complaints and reviewed 2,308 advertisements from April to September 2026, with 94% requiring modifications, primarily due to violations on digital platforms, which accounted for 97.93% of issues identified.

The Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) investigated 3,951 complaints and scrutinised 2,308 advertisements between April and September 2026, with 94% of the ads processed requiring some form of modification.
Digital platforms accounted for 97.93% of advertising violations identified by the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) during April-September 2026, with Meta platforms emerging as the largest source of non-compliant advertisements, accounting for 73.4% of violations across digital media, according to ASCI's half-yearly complaints report for 2026-27.
The report highlights the growing challenge of monitoring advertising across digital platforms, particularly in categories such as offshore betting, personal care, real estate, food and beverages, and healthcare. It also points to continuing concerns around influencer marketing disclosures, misleading product claims and advertisements promoting products or services prohibited under Indian law.
During the six-month period, ASCI looked into 3,951 complaints and scrutinised 2,308 advertisements for potential violations of its advertising code. Of the advertisements processed, 94% required some form of modification, underlining the extent of non-compliance identified through the self-regulatory body's monitoring and complaint-resolution mechanisms.
The findings come amid increasing consumer participation in advertising regulation and a growing dependence on digital platforms for advertising distribution. ASCI recorded 1,347 complaints in 2026-27, compared with 1,243 in 2025-26 and 1,124 in 2024-25, indicating a consistent increase in complaints from the general public.
The report also found that 84% of complaints received during the April-September period were submitted through TARA, ASCI's digital grievance redressal platform.
Meta accounts for nearly three-fourths of digital advertising violations
Meta platforms accounted for the overwhelming majority of digital advertising violations identified by ASCI. Of the 2,173 advertisements classified under digital media in the report's platform-wise analysis, 1,563 were associated with Meta, representing 73.4% of digital media violations.
Websites accounted for 14.3% of digital violations, followed by property portals at 6.5% and Google at 4.3%. E-commerce platforms accounted for 1.3%, LinkedIn for 0.6%, OTT platforms for 0.1% and mobile applications for 0.05%.
Television accounted for 1.47% of the overall violations, print for 0.32% and other media for 0.46%.
ASCI found that 90% of the 1,563 advertisements identified on Meta were revenue-generating sponsored advertisements rather than promotions originating from brand handles. Further, 41% of these sponsored advertisements promoted prohibited categories, including offshore and illegal betting.
The advertisements were reported to the relevant government authorities and flagged to Meta for action.
The report raises concerns about the continued availability of sponsored advertisements promoting offshore and illegal betting to Indian consumers despite the introduction of the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025, which prohibits online money games.
According to ASCI, the findings point to the need for digital platforms to adopt more stringent and proactive mechanisms to identify, restrict and prevent the dissemination of advertisements promoting prohibited products and services.
The continued presence of such advertisements also highlights the enforcement challenges associated with regulatory restrictions in an increasingly digital advertising ecosystem.
Offshore betting, personal care and real estate lead violations
Seven advertising categories accounted for nearly 90% of the violations identified during the reporting period, with offshore and illegal betting emerging as the largest category.
ASCI identified 590 advertisements promoting offshore or illegal betting, followed by 456 in personal care, 360 in real estate, 312 in food and beverages, 95 in electronics and consumer durables, 82 in healthcare and 68 in education.
Offshore and illegal betting advertisements accounted for the highest number of violations, with 99% of the 590 cases identified through proactive surveillance. These included 20 influencer promotions.
Sponsored advertisements accounted for 98% of the betting-related cases, prompting ASCI to refer them to the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) and the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting (MIB) to facilitate takedowns and protect consumers.
The personal care category recorded the highest number of influencer-related violations, with 263 cases. The category was increasingly driven by influencer-led endorsements, with 75% of advertisements not contested by advertisers and subsequently modified or withdrawn following ASCI's intervention.
Influencer disclosure violations accounted for 58% of the cases in this category.
ASCI also identified misleading claims around skin and hair transformations, including advertisements making unsubstantiated claims relating to acne, pigmentation, hair fall and dandruff. Exaggerated before-and-after visuals and dramatic skin-tone transformation claims were flagged for potentially misleading consumers and reinforcing negative beauty stereotypes.
Environmental and product composition claims also emerged as areas of concern. According to the report, laboratory findings contradicted certain sustainability assertions and product claims, highlighting gaps in advertiser accountability.
In real estate, ASCI identified 360 advertisements violating the Telangana Real Estate Regulatory Authority (TGRERA) Act and/or its advertising code. Of these, 333 advertisements were flagged for missing mandatory disclosures, including project registration numbers, website details and approvals from competent authorities.
These advertisements were referred under an existing memorandum of understanding between ASCI and Telangana RERA. Another 27 advertisements were found to violate other provisions of the ASCI Code.
The food and beverages category recorded 312 violations, of which 79% were not contested by advertisers and were subsequently withdrawn or modified.
Influencer disclosure violations accounted for 58% of the cases in the category. Nutraceutical advertisements represented 74% of the total advertisements identified, with 82% of these complaints not being contested.
ASCI flagged advertisers for making therapeutic claims around disease-risk reduction, cures, health improvements, rapid weight loss and product superiority without adequate scientific substantiation.
In electronics and consumer durables, 79% of the 95 advertisements identified were flagged for influencer disclosure violations. The violations were largely driven by promotional campaigns accompanying the launch of new electronic products, particularly mobile phones.
ASCI refers 1,102 advertisements to regulators
The report also highlights the extent to which ASCI's monitoring activities have extended beyond its self-regulatory framework, with advertisements being referred to government authorities for further examination.
During April-September 2026, ASCI referred 633 advertisements to government authorities in categories where advertising is expressly prohibited under the law.
Of these, 590 betting and gambling advertisements were referred to I4C and the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting. Another 28 advertisements promoting ayurvedic products were referred to the Ministry of AYUSH for potential violations of the Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act.
Six alcohol advertisements were referred to state excise authorities, while four hospital and clinic advertisements were referred to the National Medical Commission for violations of its guidelines. Five advertisements were referred under other categories.
Separately, under its MoU with Telangana RERA, ASCI referred 333 real estate advertisements to the state regulator for violations of the RERA Act.
The report also records 136 advertisements that continued to remain non-compliant despite being found in violation of the ASCI Code. These were referred to the relevant regulatory authorities for examination under their respective provisions.
The report lists 126 referrals to the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, 126 to the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA), 12 to the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI), eight to MahaRERA and one to the Ministry of AYUSH. ASCI noted that some cases were referred to multiple regulators.
Influencer advertising remains a compliance challenge
Influencer marketing continued to be a significant area of concern, particularly around disclosure of material connections between influencers and brands.
Of the 659 advertisements processed for influencer-related violations, 95% required modification, highlighting the extent of non-compliance with ASCI's influencer advertising guidelines at the time of publication.
The report found that 83.5% of influencers corrected their posts without contesting the complaints, with modifications being made within an average of six business days.
As of November 17, 2026, the overall compliance rate among influencers stood at 95%, compared with 88% in the previous year.
However, 3.6% of the advertisements involved the promotion of products prohibited or restricted by law. ASCI also investigated 12 cases involving both inadequate disclosure of material connections and misleading claims.
Personal care accounted for the largest share of influencer violations, with 263 cases, followed by food and beverages at 181, electronics and consumer durables at 75, fashion and lifestyle at 28, and offshore and illegal betting at 23.
The report also found that 8% of influencer-related complaints were upheld, while 4.9% were dismissed. A substantial 83.5% of the cases were not contested.
The findings underline the need for brands, agencies and influencers to strengthen compliance processes at the campaign planning and content creation stages rather than relying on corrective action after advertisements have been published.
Complaint resolution time falls to 15 days
Alongside its monitoring efforts, ASCI reported an improvement in the time taken to investigate and resolve complaints.
The average turnaround time for formally investigated complaints fell to 15 days during April-September 2026, compared with 17 days in FY26. The organisation attributed the improvement to streamlined processes, technology investments and a sharper focus on expediting complaint resolution.
