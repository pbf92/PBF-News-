TITLE: Amazon agrees to buy Decart's AI chip software in a deal reported at $7.5bn
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon today signed an agreement to purchase the proprietary technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack, the software Israeli-founded startup Decart built to make AI models run faster and more cheaply on chips, and to bring Decart's three co-founders and their team of researchers and engineers into the company. The stated aim is to improve the performance of Amazon's own Trainium chips and of the AI workloads its customers run on them.
Amazon did not put a price on the transaction. Calcalist, the Israeli business daily that has tracked the sale since August, reported a value of $7.5 billion, to be paid in cash and stock over several installments. Bloomberg News reported a figure of about $7.5 billion, citing a person familiar with the matter, according to Reuters, which said Amazon declined to disclose the financial terms or comment on their value. Either figure would be close to double the roughly $4 billion at which Decart was valued in May.
In Short
Amazon has agreed to buy the technology behind Decart's optimization software, which makes AI models run faster and cheaper on chips, and to hire the startup's three co-founders and their engineers. The deal is part of Amazon's effort to make its own Trainium chips more competitive, and the cost of running AI on those chips sits underneath the AI tools that advertisers, publishers and agencies buy from Amazon and from companies such as Anthropic and Pinterest that rent its computing power. Amazon disclosed no price, but Calcalist and Bloomberg put it at about $7.5 billion, nearly double Decart's valuation in May and above the roughly $6 billion Anthropic discussed before walking away in September. The purchase still needs regulatory clearance, and Amazon has said nothing about a closing date or about what happens to Decart's video models and its customers on other companies' chips.
What Amazon signed
The announcement ran on Amazon's corporate news site under the headline "Amazon to purchase Decart's AI optimization technology to advance chip and model innovation for customers." It is credited to Amazon Staff and tagged, among other things, under Amazon Custom Chips and AWS.
Its central sentence is precise about what changes hands. Amazon "signed an agreement to purchase the proprietary technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack (DOS)," and will bring the Decart AI co-founders, Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev and Orian Leitersdorf, "along with their team of AI researchers and engineers, to Amazon." Nowhere does the post say Amazon is acquiring Decart the company. The word it uses for the deal is purchase, and the object of that purchase is a body of technology.
Amazon presents the people and the code as a single package. "With their deep expertise in full-stack performance optimization across hardware and software, Decart brings a strong connection between technology and talent that will help further Amazon's investment in custom silicon and optimize AI inference for its customers," the company wrote.
Two summary points open the post. The first says "Decart's technology will help Amazon further optimize both its inference and custom silicon performance so more customers can train and run AI models faster and at lower cost." The second dates the relationship: "Since 2025, Decart's team has worked closely with Amazon to deliver strong performance results on Trainium, using a full-stack approach that spans software and hardware."
The strategic case is set out in a single paragraph. "Amazon has long believed that inference speed and compute efficiency are central to the future of AI, and has advanced that vision through innovation across its custom silicon portfolio, from Graviton to Trainium," the post reads. "That work spans large language models, multimodal and agentic systems, and world models, alike." Amazon adds that it began working with Decart in 2025 and that the collaboration "quickly showed how much the two teams shared, from a full-stack view of performance to an appetite for solving some of the world's most challenging science and engineering problems."
Two executives are quoted. Peter DeSantis, whose title in the post is "SVP of foundational AI models, custom silicon, quantum computing, Amazon," spoke for the buyer.
"Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip," DeSantis said. "More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost. That's the pillar Trainium and our AI efforts are built on, and from our earliest work with Decart, we knew they were the kind of technologically fearless team who would help us push it even further."
Dean Leitersdorf, co-founder and chief executive of Decart, framed the move as a question of scale. "From day one, our mission has been to redefine the boundaries of what AI can do and to make it dramatically more efficient along the way," he said. "Joining Amazon gives our team the scale, the customers, and the infrastructure to pursue that mission faster than we could on our own. We're looking forward to exploring what we can build together."
DeSantis's title is itself informative. On December 17, 2025, Amazon announced that he would lead a new organization combining its AGI model work, custom silicon and quantum computing, as Rohit Prasad, who had led the AGI group, prepared to leave. The executive who speaks for the Decart purchase therefore oversees both the chips that DOS is meant to speed up and Amazon's own foundation models. The post does not say which part of that organization the Decart team will join.

How the transaction proceeds, according to Amazon
Read together, the post and its cautionary statement on forward-looking statements set out five stages, each with its own stated risk.
- Signing. Amazon has "signed an agreement." The cautionary statement refers to the deal as "the proposed purchase of the Decart Optimization Stack (DOS)."
- Hiring. The same statement speaks of "the anticipated hiring of members of the Decart team," and lists among the risks that "Amazon may be unable to hire and retain members of the Decart team." The word is members, not all.
- Regulatory clearance. "Completion of the transaction is subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory clearance." Amazon warns that "the regulatory approvals required for the transaction may not be obtained on the terms expected, on the anticipated schedule, or at all."
- Closing. According to the statement, "closing of the transaction may not occur or may be delayed, either as a result of litigation related to the transaction or otherwise."
- Integration. Amazon cautions that it "may be unable to integrate the acquired technology or achieve the anticipated benefits of the transaction such as anticipated improvements in the speed, performance, cost efficiency, and capabilities of AI workloads, Trainium and other Amazon custom silicon, and Amazon's chips and model offerings."
The statement also warns that Amazon "may assume unexpected risks and liabilities" and that completing the deal "may distract Amazon's management from other important matters." It points readers to the "Risk Factors" in Amazon's annual report on Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025. The integration clause is the only place in the document where the technology is called "acquired."
What the Decart Optimization Stack does
Amazon does not explain how DOS works. Other descriptions fill some of the gap.
According to The Next Web, which reported Decart's funding round on May 18, 2026, DOS runs on Nvidia GPUs, Google TPUs and Amazon's Trainium. Decart claimed the software reached 1,600 tokens per second, against an industry figure of around 200, and full-HD video inference at up to 100 frames per second. A second version, DOS 2.0, was announced alongside the round. None of those numbers come with a published test method.
The optimization business predates Decart's better-known video work. When TechCrunch reported Decart's Series A in December 2024, the company said its enterprise GPU optimization product was already bringing in millions of dollars in revenue, and claimed that workloads which might cost $100 an hour could be brought down to 25 cents an hour. According to Globes, Decart has described itself as profitable almost from its first day, with revenue from three lines: a GPU optimization service, an API service for model development, and research into real-time video.
That multi-chip reach is worth dwelling on. A tool that, by Decart's account, helped customers move workloads among Nvidia, Google and Amazon hardware is set to become the property of one of those three. Amazon's post mentions only Trainium and Graviton. It does not say whether DOS will continue to support other companies' chips, or whether customers using the software today will keep access to it.
A collaboration that started on Trainium
Decart's work with Amazon is public, and it has been about video as much as about optimization.
At AWS re:Invent in December 2025, during an infrastructure keynote given by DeSantis, then senior vice president of utility computing at AWS, Decart was presented as one of the first AI companies to deploy its models on Trainium3, according to TechStartups. Its real-time video model Lucy was made available on Amazon Bedrock. AI News reported that Decart had chosen Trainium2 for its performance, had early access to Trainium3, and that Leitersdorf said the new chip allowed up to four times faster frame generation at half the cost of GPUs. No baseline for the comparison was given. At the time, Lucy produced up to 30 frames per second with a time to first frame of around 40 milliseconds, and Decart set a target of 100 frames per second on Trainium3.
By May 2026, according to Globes and bne IntelliNews, an agreement with Amazon allowed AWS customers to use Decart's technology in media, commerce, advertising and robotics. The Next Web reported that Decart's Lucy2 model ran on Trainium3, and quoted Nafea Bshara, a vice president of Amazon's Annapurna Labs chip unit, as saying the model exceeded 80% model FLOPS utilization, a measure of how much of a chip's theoretical capacity a workload actually uses.
Today's post refers to that history only as "strong performance results on Trainium." Lucy, Oasis and Mirage, Decart's video and world-model products, are not mentioned.
From a Minecraft-style demo to a $4bn valuation
Decart was founded in 2023 by Dean Leitersdorf and Moshe Shalev. TechCrunch described the company in December 2024 as based in San Francisco with operations in Israel, and reported that Shalev had spent nearly 14 years building and running AI operations for the Israel Defense Forces' 8200 intelligence unit. Orian Leitersdorf, the third co-founder named by Amazon, does not appear in most earlier funding coverage.
The company came out of stealth in late 2024 with Oasis, a playable, AI-generated open world in the style of Minecraft, which it said had drawn millions of players. Mirage, a model that transforms live video in real time, followed in 2025, and Lucy after that. Each step brought a higher price.
The May valuation comes from The Wall Street Journal, as cited by Globes; PitchBook data put it at nearly $4 billion, according to Reuters. Total funding passed $450 million with the May round. Fortune reported in August 2025 that Decart had spent less than $10 million of the $153 million it had raised in the preceding 11 months.
On those figures, a $7.5 billion price would be about 1.9 times the May valuation and 25% above the roughly $6 billion Anthropic discussed in August, according to PPC Land's arithmetic. The comparison is imperfect: a valuation prices a whole company, while Amazon describes buying technology and hiring people.
