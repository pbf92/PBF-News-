TITLE: 'Let us love, pray and forgive': Russell Brand criticised for message protesting innocence
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Russell Brand has protested his innocence and said Christ is with him, in a message condemned by Downing Street as “clearly appalling”.
The former comedian, who was vocal about his conversion to Christianity after allegations about him in 2023, is serving a ten and a half year prison sentence after being convicted of rape and three sexual assaults.
The message was read out by his wife Laura Brand on their video podcast. In the edition released on Sunday, she is sitting holding a Bible on a sofa with a candle burning.
Laura opens the video reading from Philippians 1, written by Paul to believers in Philippi when he was imprisoned. She states that her husband is okay and “praying, unsurprisingly”, adding that “although Russell is not here now, at this moment, the Holy Spirit is moving and the Holy Spirit cannot be restrained.
The letter from Russell read: “Beloved brothers and sisters in Christ, God bless you for your support,” the message from Russell begins, “As you know, I am not guilty for the offences I have been jailed for, so something strange is happening. The good news is that Christ is with us when we choose him, and I do.”
“The good news is that I have already seen that the staff here [at Littlehey] are strong, they are kind, and they are dedicated. And as for my fellow prisoners, I now know that I am in no position to judge anyone. I don’t know which other men here are not guilty, and the ones that are, they are people that Christ died to save, like you and me. Let us all love, pray for and forgive one another as God forgave us.”
A Downing Street spokesman said: “We expect action to be taken if he’s found to have broken the rules.
“Prisoners are strictly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims and speaking to the media is only permitted in exceptional circumstances. Prisoners cannot get around these rules by having someone else publish their comments.”
Foreign secretary Ed Miliband told Sky News that it was “revolting… that [Brand] shows no remorse today in his comments. No remorse, no sympathy for the victims. Just disgusting comments.”
