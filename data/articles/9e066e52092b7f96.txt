TITLE: Nasdaq Falls 626 Points to Start Nvidia-Anthropic Week: Stock Market Today
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

Stocks gapped down at Monday's opening bell after a weekend filled with speculation about the future of the leader of the AI revolution, as Nvidia (NVDA) prepares to report results after Tuesday's closing bell without Jensen Huang. Anthropic is on track to complete its initial public offering (IPO) on Wednesday, while Fed Chair Kevin Warsh suggested the U.S. can't sustain its superpower status without leading on AI.
During opening remarks at a symposium on how artificial intelligence will work its way into the broader economy, Warsh said members of the central bank's research staff are studying what it means for workers, as well as the implications of a burst bubble.
The Fed chair said productivity growth is reasonably strong right now but hasn't broken out, suggesting AI's promise remains further in the future.
As Nick Timiraos of The Wall Street Journal reports, "Warsh's remarks repeatedly cast AI as a question of national power, a theme upon which Fed chairs haven't typically dwelled. He recalled the late economist Robert Mundell calling America's rise as a superpower the most important fact of the 20th century, and said for the U.S. today, 'it is a charge to keep.'"
Focused on the bigger picture, Warsh offered no commentary on recent price action, the short-term trajectory of inflation or the Fed's current stance on interest rates.
Is Jensen Huang still Nvidia's 'key man'?
Nvidia fell another 4.1% amid reports that big players at major Silicon Valley venture capital firms are privately advocating for Jensen Huang's return as CEO. NVDA is now down 10.1% since Huang resigned last Thursday.
Though President Donald Trump, Bill Ackman and Elon Musk have publicly expressed their support for the fallen executive, CNBC says the Nvidia board will not heed their call. The board is already dealing with as complex a succession crisis as you'll find in the real world these days.
Indeed, as Noah Weisberger and Yifei Liu of BCA Research conclude, the surprise exit of a CEO is a meaningfully negative event "particularly if the departure is driven by a legal or ethical issue."
Based on a total of 30 cases, Weisberger and Liu found that stocks underperform by about 7% in the month after an unexpected CEO departure, and 70% of them lag. The data show that legal/ethical departures cost about 13%. Stocks recover within a month when the cause of the departure is death or illness.
