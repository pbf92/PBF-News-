TITLE: CN looking to the future at Grain Expo
OUTLET: CochraneNow

Record Grain Movement Continues
CN Rail is moving grain at a record pace this crop year.
David Przednowek, Assistant Vice-President of Grain at CN Rail was one of the presenters during Tuesday's Grain Expo at Canadian Western Agribition.
Przednowek says CN has shipped 10.3 million metric tonnes of grain and processed grain products from Western Canada through the first 15 weeks of the crop year.
That compares with 9.5 million tonnes during the same period last year and 9.8 million tonnes two years ago.
According to Przednowek, a larger crop and stronger grain movement during August helped drive the increase.
Investments Target West Coast Bottlenecks
A major focus of CN's presentation was several infrastructure projects they are working on designed to improve traffic flow into Canada's key export gateways.
One project involves a 3.7-mile section of double track in British Columbia that once completed, will eliminate the final single-track segment along a 25-mile stretch leading into CN's Thornton Yard at the Port of Vancouver.
Przednowek says the addition will allow trains to move more efficiently in both directions while increasing overall capacity at the port.
CN expects the project to be in service in 2027.
The company is also expanding capacity at the Zanardi Bridge near Prince Rupert. The bridge is currently single track, but CN is adding an additional section of track that will increase traffic capacity from about 24 trains per day to more than 50.
Balancing Growth Across the Supply Chain
Przednowek notes overall rail performance depends on all parts of the grain supply chain working together.
CN's grain plan calls for average movement of 758,000 metric tonnes per week outside of winter conditions when demand, port capacity, and corridor balance are all aligned.
During the winter shipping season, and with the closure of the Great Lakes-St. Lawrence Seaway and the Port of Thunder Bay, that target falls to approximately 609,000 tonnes per week.
He says weekly supply chain scorecards help identify factors affecting performance and provide a clearer picture of how the entire grain handling system is functioning.
Planning Years Ahead
Przednowek says CN continuously forecasts future demand to identify where new capacity will be required.
He notes that environmental reviews, permitting requirements, and major construction projects can take years to complete, making long-term planning essential.
The investments being highlighted at Grain Expo are part of CN's broader effort to stay ahead of growing grain production and export demand across Western Canada.
