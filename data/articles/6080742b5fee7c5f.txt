TITLE: Former Open Doors chief Eddie Lyle remembered at Oxford memorial service
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Friends, family and Christian leaders have gathered in Oxford to remember Eddie Lyle, the former chief executive and president of Open Doors UK and Ireland.
The memorial service took place at St Andrew’s Church in North Oxford on Friday, with people also able to watch online.
Lyle died on 19 September, aged 73, following complications from heart surgery. He was surrounded by family and friends.
Speakers remembered his Christian faith, warmth and humour, as well as his commitment to supporting Christians facing persecution around the world.
In his eulogy, close friend and former colleague Ian Smith spoke of the “giant footprints” Lyle had left through his love for people and determination to help others fulfil their potential.
Lyle began his ministry with Youth for Christ before joining Release International, where he worked to support persecuted Christians.
He joined Open Doors UK and Ireland in 2005, eventually becoming its chief executive and later president. He spent more than two decades with the organisation, travelling to countries where Christians face persecution, including Vietnam and Nigeria.
His work also included founding Medic Assist International, which supports healthcare in persecuted communities, and co-authoring the book Witness: Sharing the Faith of the Persecuted Church with author Nick Page.
Former colleagues Rob and Marion White remembered his combination of bold vision and pastoral care, while former Open Doors chief executive Henrietta Blyth spoke of his wisdom and encouragement to Christian leaders.
Peter Martin, of the charity Hope Lebedyn, reflected on more than 35 years of friendship and mentoring, thanking Lyle for teaching him to choose faith over fear.
“You championed the underdog and oppressed and the marginalised who had no hope,” he said.
The service ended with each person attending being given a card bearing the name and country of a Christian facing persecution, as a reminder to pray for them.
Lyle is survived by his wife, Christine, and their son, Adam.
