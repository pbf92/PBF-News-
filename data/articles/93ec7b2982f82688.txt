TITLE: Andy Burnham urges 'very careful' approach to controversial Conversion Practices Bill
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has urged caution and a "very careful" approach to a controversial bill which would criminalise conversion therapy, saying that it was important experts analyse the impact of a "complex issue".
The proposed Conversion Practices Bill could limit discussions about sexuality or gender identity. Individuals or company bosses could face criminal liability if serious harm to a person's physical or mental health was alleged to have been caused by discussions about gender identity or sexuality.
The maximum prison sentence under the proposed bill is five years. If an alleged offence occurred within a company, a director or manager could also be held responsible.
More than 1,500 church leaders across denominations have urged the government to drop the bill, warning that if it becomes law, it could "criminalise" Christian teaching.
Burnham has made clear that he wants time to be taken to assess the bill as it passes through Parliament, with a report written by a joint committee of members of the House of Commons and House of Lords to be published in January, making recommendations to Downing Street.
Burnham said:“There has been a commitment, long-standing from previous prime ministers, to bring a bill to Parliament to deal with this issue, and it is in Parliament.
“It’s currently in what’s called pre-legislative scrutiny, and the reason why that matters, and me pointing to that matters, is because that is where a complex issue can be discussed with experts in a very careful way. So we need to have that conversation. But the bill that emerges, absolutely, I’m committed to bringing through Parliament.
“In preparation for this committee, we’ve made every reasonable inquiry to find out whether there are any recent or current cases, and we haven’t identified any. I have seen what has been published by the Government in terms of polling, but in terms of cases that have been seen by us and gone through the courts, we don’t have any recent or current.”
This month, Archbishop of Liverpool, Most Rev Archbishop John Sherrington, warned that he thought the bill could stop the Church "proclaim[ing] an authentic understanding of the human person".
Archbishop John added: “This Bill proposes to extend the legal definition of abusive practices in ways that threaten a person’s fundamental rights, including freedom of religion or belief and freedom of expression.
"The legislation will also create uncertainty around lawful pastoral care by the Church, family life and open conversations that can take place in a range of social settings."
Meanwhile, conservative shadow culture secretary Rebecca Paul believes that local authorities could launch an investigation and remove children from parents if they question their children's transgender identity.
"You will have the situation where a child, a young person," Paul cautioned, "could effectively say, 'well, if you don’t give me the money for those cross-sex hormones, or you don’t consent to let me on that puberty blocker trial, or you don’t use my pronouns, you’re causing me immense distress, and I’m going to report you to the police'.”
The Government has said that "survivor testimony, a range of evidence and expert opinion" has guided what they call a "decade long cross-party consensus" to ban "abusive conversion practices".
It added: “We have brought forward a draft Bill for pre-legislative scrutiny in order to get this right and end almost a decade of delay for LGBT people who should never have to suffer abuse in an effort to change who they are."
