TITLE: Malawi bishop accuses CofE of 'racial division' over slavery reparation scheme
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

An Anglican Bishop in Malawi has accused the Church of England of creating "racial division" over its slavery reparations plan.
Speaking to The Telegraph, Rt Rev Fanuel Magangani said the scheme, known as “Project Spire” would create tiers between those who are eligible for compensation and those who aren't. He said he would prefer to see a fund, open to applicants of all backgrounds, to help the Church grow around the world.
The Church of England set up Project Spire to address its past links to the trade and exploitation of enslaved African peoples. Church Commissioners have made a £100m funding commitment to “invest in a better future for all, working with and for communities affected by historic transatlantic slavery.” But critics say the money should be used instead for local parish ministry.
Bishop Fanuel said the proposals would create “segregation” within the Church because they would distinguish between people of African descent whose ancestors were enslaved through the transatlantic slave trade and other African communities whose ancestors suffered under different forms of slavery. He fears this risks creating divisions between people based on their historical experiences and ancestry.
In The Telegraph interview he described the British “not as the predators, but as protectors”, and argued that the history of slavery is much wider than the transatlantic trade. He said the Church should acknowledge suffering across different communities, rather than concentrating its response on one particular history.
Premier Christian News has contacted the Church of England for a response.
