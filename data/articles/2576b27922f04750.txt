Psychiatrist Urges Platforms to Curb Doomscrolling
A psychiatrist called on social media companies to reduce the harms of compulsive scrolling, citing research on problematic internet use.
Updated on Nov. 27, 2026 in Internet

A psychiatrist called on social media companies to curb the negative effects of doomscrolling, which has become problematic for people, especially those with pre-existing mental illness. A study published in The Lancet Psychiatry found problematic internet use may affect one in 10 people globally.
Why it matters
Behavioral algorithms in social media apps are designed for engagement and reinforce compulsive use, which can increase depression, anxiety and insecurity. The scale of the estimate suggests the issue reaches well beyond a small group of heavy users.
The Lancet Psychiatry study estimates problematic internet use affects about one in 10 people globally, covering social media, gaming, gambling, shopping, pornography and health-anxiety searches. The study's measurement methods and trend over time are not described in the available material.
The details
The psychiatrist said social media algorithms are built for engagement and play on users' worst fears and insecurities. Social comparison on these platforms can drive depression, anxiety and insecurity, and in young girls can feed eating disorders.
Timeline
- - Nov. 26, 2026: Psychiatrist discussed doomscrolling research in a NewsNation Live interview.
Big Picture
The call builds on the Lancet Psychiatry estimate that problematic internet use affects about one in 10 people worldwide, extending the research from prevalence to the design of engagement-driven recommendation systems.
Frequent scrolling on social platforms can add to anxiety and social comparison, particularly for people already managing a mental illness. Recognizing compulsive patterns in your own use is a practical first step toward limiting their effects.
The takeaway
Paying attention to how much time you spend scrolling, and how it affects your mood, can help you decide when to step away from social media.
