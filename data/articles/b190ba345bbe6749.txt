TITLE: Anthropic reportedly prices its IPO at $83 per share, valuing the AI giant at $1.5 trillion
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

OAKLAND, Calif. (AP) — Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 per share on Wednesday, an amount above the expected price range that values the artificial intelligence giant at $1.5 trillion.
The higher-than-anticipated pricing signals broad investor enthusiasm for AI stocks even amid some uncertainty in the market stemming from the ouster of Nvidia's CEO and worries about an AI bubble.
Anthropic said the offering consists of 980 million shares, roughly 956 million sold by the company and the rest by selling stockholders. Proceeds totaling $81 billion put Anthropic's IPO ahead of SpaceX, which raised about $75 billion in June.
The San Francisco-based maker of the Claude AI tool is set to make its debut Thursday on Wall Street. The company had set a price range of $70 to $78 per share, offering 825 million shares that will begin trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol "ANT."
Anthropic decided to barrel ahead with the IPO despite jitters among some investors over market volatility, a potential regulatory crackdown by a Democratic-led Congress next year and growing anti-AI sentiment among the public. Then there's the spectacular downfall of one of the industry's biggest cheerleaders, former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, over extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy.
"This is strong appetite for this major AI IPO in Anthropic, and it's a big win for the tech sector after a white-knuckle time with Nvidia. The pricing shows strong demand among investors," said Dan Ives, partner and senior managing director at Yorkville Ives & Co.
The company's rival, OpenAI, is not planning to go public until next year.
IPO offers a way to raise money without going deeper into debt
Anthropic already has taken on $6.2 billion in debt, which it acknowledges in financial filings is "substantial." Adding to the strain, the company is unprofitable, at least under standard accounting rules, suggesting that its resources to pay interest and principal are limited.
While the company has ample cash for now, it will likely run through it quickly if it cannot raise more soon, given its outsize spending plans. The company said it has already signed leases and other deals requiring it to spend $900 billion on computing power and infrastructure in the coming years, including $70 billion in the next 12 months.
An IPO is essentially a way to raise money, instead of going into further debt. And borrowing has gotten more expensive. That's partly due to inflation fears and partly due to the sector's massive cash needs, which have drawn comparisons to the Gilded Age splurge on railroads. Lenders fear that AI companies have taken on too much debt to expand while having no clear path to profitability.
