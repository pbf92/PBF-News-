TITLE: Landon Donovan and Tim Howard laugh off potential Mauricio Pochettino USMNT replacement
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

As Mauricio Pochettino nears the end of his short but sweet tenure with the U.S. Men's National Team, leaving after a little over two years in charge, talk has naturally turned to who will step in to replace him.
One candidate recently floated was former USMNT star and current Canada boss, Jesse Marsch. An outspoken and often divisive figure thanks to his often fiery temperament and tendency to wear his heart on his sleeve, the 53-year-old has previously claimed he was in line to take charge of the USMNT in 2023, even turning down a Premier League job to do so, only to then be shunned.
A decision which admittedly left him "angry", Marsch has since also ruffled feathers back home with his comments about U.S. President Donald Trump and how "In the U.S. sometimes we had to beg players to sing the national anthem."
As such, when quizzed on the chances of Marsch replacing the Manchester United-bound Pochettino was recently raised on Landon Donovan and Tim Howard's 'Unfiltered Soccer' podcast arose, the former couldn't help but chuckle.
"Did you ever watch Dumb and Dumber?...And he says, 'So you're telling me there's a chance?' There's no chance. There is no chance," Donovan said. "This is not Dumb and Dumber. Jesse Marsch has burned every bridge that ever existed in MLS, or sorry, with the national team. Now, 10 years down the road, is that possible and let bygones be…but there's no chance now."
Howard agreed, adding, "To what you said, Jesse has burned bridges. One, Jesse doesn't care, and two, I truly believe that Jesse believes what he says. I think he loves Canadian football and the federation and football in that country…
"I think Jesse felt hard done by the last go-around, and he's basically stuck his flag in. He planted his he just said, 'Boom that's it. I'm riding with Canada.'"
Marsch has also directly addressed this prospect during a recent press conference, saying, as per SI, "When I signed my contract to stay here [with Canada], it was with the full intention that, no matter who was coming or what was coming or anything, I'm not leaving.

"I'm staying here. I love this job. I love this team. I love this federation and the sport in this country."
Marsch is under contract with Canada through the next FIFA World Cup in 2030, having guided his side to the Round of 16 this past summer, where they were knocked out by Morocco.
Meanwhile, Pochettino is jumping ship, having only recently penned a new deal through to the next World Cup. The 54-year-old will now take charge of a wayward United side sitting 18th in the Premier League after 10 games - their lowest standing at this point in the season in 40 years. But before he jets off to Manchester, Pochettino has one last game to preside over, as the USMNT looks to secure its spot in the CONCACAF Nations League semi-finals in their quarter-final second leg against Haiti on Tuesday.
