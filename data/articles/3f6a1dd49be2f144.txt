TITLE: BADBOX fakes 40bn TV ad requests a day as Black Friday budgets rise up to 30%
OUTLET: PPC Land

Monday, November 23, 2026 opened Thanksgiving week with money moving and money leaking. Media buyers told Digiday that brands will spend 10% to 30% more on digital media over Black Friday and Cyber Monday than last year, much of it on retail media, search, connected television and YouTube. On the same screens, HUMAN Security disclosed BADBOX 3.0, a fraud operation hidden in cheap Chinese TV boxes that peaked at about 40 billion fake ad requests a day. A study said 29% of the products on Amazon's curated holiday deal lists were no cheaper than in the previous month, which Amazon called false. Agents moved closer to the till: AWS let AI agents pay Marketplace sellers per request on the AWS bill, Index Exchange explained what the Model Context Protocol means for buyers, Google changed its WebMCP interface in Chrome, and AWS's chief executive argued that AI safety needs engineering, not trust. Google documented 12 bots it had been running unannounced, OpenAI dropped a line saying ChatGPT's product results are not influenced by partnerships, Pinterest opened its inventory to Microsoft Advertising, and France gave its media regulator power to fine platforms up to 1% of global turnover in press pay disputes. Holiday creative leaned on Scrooge, a gecko and the family dog.

The fraud in the living room
The biggest ad fraud operation yet disclosed lived inside cheap television boxes. PPC Land reported that HUMAN traces up to 40 billion fraudulent ad requests a day to cheap TV boxes. HUMAN Security's Satori Threat Intelligence and Research Team said BADBOX 3.0 hid video ads in uncertified streaming boxes made in mainland China, peaking at about 40 billion bid requests a day. That is more than three times the 12 billion daily peak of VASTFLUX, the operation HUMAN dismantled in 2023, and at its height it was more than 4% of the connected TV traffic HUMAN observed. HUMAN called it "the largest ad fraud operation by bid volume ever publicly disclosed."
The mechanics were simple and invisible to owners. Malicious add-ons played video ads in windows nobody could see, while sending bid requests that impersonated well-known TV apps. Other add-ons enrolled the boxes in eleven residential proxy networks, ten commercial services plus one, PXYEDGE, that HUMAN says the operators run. "The device owner pays for the bandwidth, and someone else sells access to the address," HUMAN wrote. It linked the module catalogue to TXCZ, a Chinese electronics maker, and listed warning signs: traffic concentrated in a few device models, residential proxy addresses, shared IPs, and daily surges at nearly the same hour. HUMAN does not name the impersonated apps, the box models or the number of infected devices, and it warns that the operators have rebuilt after every takedown.
Digiday's Ronan Shields asked what it cost. Digiday reported in The Rundown on BADBOX 3.0 that the financial damage is unclear, because bid requests are offers, not billable impressions. Shailin Dhar of Futureproof TMT said his connected TV audits since 2024 show an average fill rate of about 2%, and Digiday's illustrative arithmetic at a 0.5% fill rate and a $3 CPM gives about $600,000 a day, which Dhar stressed is not evidence of actual earnings. He questioned the safeguards in private marketplaces and curated deals. "My personal estimate is roughly a 90% chance that at least one comparable operation is running without public identification," he said. For buyers paying premium prices for television inventory, the lesson is that a device ID and an app name prove very little.
Television's other problem is waste, not fraud. PPC Land reported that half the homes Samba and OpenX reached online missed the TV ads. Across 11 campaigns run through their partnership, on average 50% of households reached by Samba-built digital audiences bought through OpenX were not reached by the same brands' linear TV campaigns, and in some campaigns 90% of digital impressions went to untouched homes. The lightest-viewing third of households received 3% of linear impressions but nearly 30% of the digital ones, while heavy viewers saw the same linear ads at 5.5 times the rate of light viewers. "Linear TV remains effective at building awareness at scale, but it still misses a significant portion of consumers," said Phillip Keefe of Samba. The release gives no campaign dates, advertisers, spend or control group.
Satellite and streaming inventory found a new route to buyers. PPC Land reported that DISH Media sells Sling TV ads through Nexxen to reach regional agencies. The partnership puts DISH satellite and Sling streaming inventory on Nexxen's platform, with metadata travelling in the bidstream. "Nexxen brings a different kind of demand than we already have access to," said Mike Fedyna of DISH Media, pointing to regional and independent agencies. No pricing, volumes, start date or exclusivity terms were disclosed. Nexxen's connected TV revenue rose 39% to $34.1m in the third quarter. PPC Land set the deal against a Peer39 finding that only 40% of connected TV bid requests carried usable programme-level signals, and a Jamloop survey in which 33% of marketers fully trusted most platform-reported claims. "In a market where quality is hard to verify, that's a real advantage," Fedyna said, which on the day BADBOX was disclosed sounded less like a slogan than a warning.
Streaming services are turning trailers into tills. PPC Land reported that Disney+ turns the Avengers: Doomsday trailer into a ticket counter. From November 23, subscribers in the US can buy tickets to Marvel Studios' Avengers: Doomsday through Fandango inside the Disney+ app, more than three weeks before the film's December 18 release. There are four routes: a mobile notification or on-screen QR code after the trailer, pause ads, a new Tickets tab on the film's page, and a banner in a countdown collection. The trailer prompt carries an "Ad" badge. Disney cited more than 420 million hours of Marvel viewing on Disney+ in August, a record month. Fandango's payments, Disney's commission and the data flows were not disclosed. A streaming service that can sell a cinema ticket from a paused screen is showing advertisers what shoppable television looks like when the seller owns both ends.
Black Friday, measured
Budgets are rising into the holiday weekend. Digiday reported that brands boost Black Friday CTV, retail and search spending, with five media buyers expecting 10% to 30% more digital spend over Black Friday and Cyber Monday than in 2025. Bain Capital estimates US companies will record $85bn in sales over the five days, about 8.5% of 2026 holiday spending. The caveats are real: Fluent says 32% of shoppers started holiday buying before Black Friday, and Wayvia found discounted listings rose 56% between October 1 and October 20, so some agencies are spreading budgets over a longer window. PMG data showed that brands discounting 75% of their product set grew October revenue 16%, while those discounting 25% or less saw revenue fall 11%. AdCellerant sees connected TV CPMs up as much as 18% in some regions because of midterm political spending, and Tinuiti expects clients to spend 11% to 15% more with Google. "We expect higher spend across direct-response channels this year, though it varies by category and brand," said Brett Fischer of Collective Measures. The National Retail Federation projects 182.7 million Americans shopping over the five days, down from 186.9 million in 2025.
Brands are also reshuffling tactics. Digiday reported in D+ Research on brands' holiday marketing tactics that 45% of 90 brand and retailer professionals plan to use connected TV and streaming ads this season, up from 23% in 2025, and 33% plan TV ads, up from 13%. Brand experiences, the most used tactic last year, fell to 41% from 50%, gift guides fell 19 points and influencer hauls and unboxing videos fell 14 points. Yet gift guides, at 31%, still rank as the best-performing tactic historically. "Gift guides can play an important role in holiday and especially bridging LLM research and AI overviews," said Laura Brodie of Ritual, who warned that streaming is risky for small brands that only start at holiday time.
Shoppers are less loyal than ever in the week itself. PPC Land reported that 80% of UK Black Friday shoppers buy from unfamiliar stores, Criteo finds. Criteo's September survey of 10,500 consumers in ten countries found 80% of UK Black Friday shoppers at least sometimes buy from online stores they do not normally use, against 75% globally. Some 43% in the UK would use an AI assistant to help choose a gift, and 81% of UK gift shoppers plan to look for discounts. Generative AI tools rank seventh among discovery sources globally, at 26%, behind retailers and marketplaces at 51%, and only 46% trust AI-driven product recommendations. "By the time shoppers are ready to take advantage of an offer, many will have narrowed down their choices," Criteo said. The company reported third-quarter revenue down 11% to $416m on November 4.
The deals themselves came under challenge. PPC Land reported that Amazon faces a study saying 29% of 351 holiday "deals" were no cheaper. Groundwork Collaborative's report "Deal or Decoy?", published on November 16, tracked 351 products on Amazon's curated Black Friday and Cyber Monday lists in 2024 and 2025 using Keepa price data. Against a 30-day window, 29.1% had a sale price equal to or above their lowest price in the previous month, and 9.4% cost more during the sale; on a 90-day window the share rose to 57.3%. UGG Butte boots cost $179.98 on Cyber Monday 2025, against $98.40 about four weeks earlier. "If our findings hold across its sale inventory, millions of Americans could overpay for goods marketed as bargains," the report says. Amazon called Groundwork a political advocacy group and its findings flawed and false, said the sample was tiny next to more than 10 million deals, and said 98% of the cited products were priced the same or lower than in the previous month. Groundwork wants US retailers to disclose the 30-day lowest price, as the EU has required since May 28, 2022.
Amazon is also tightening who may sell. PPC Land reported that Amazon blocks third-party Victoria's Secret sellers from November 26. A notice shared on LinkedIn by Ephraim Rosenberg of ASGTG says that from Thanksgiving Day "Victoria's Secret is no longer eligible for third party sellers to list on Amazon.com," because the brand "distributes products only through direct-to-consumer channels." Sellers can sell remaining stock or delete listings, and Amazon says account health will not be affected. PPC Land contrasted that reason with Victoria's Secret's annual report, which lists 562 international partner-operated stores under franchise, licence and wholesale arrangements. Digital channels generated $2.042bn, or 31%, of the brand's fiscal 2025 net sales.
Amazon's television business is selling the day itself. Adweek reported on Amazon's Alan Moss and how its Black Friday sports lineup and shoppable ads are winning advertisers. Prime Video's Black Friday is a 15-hour marathon: the PGA Tour's Skins Game at 9am Eastern, Black Friday Football between the Denver Broncos and the Pittsburgh Steelers at 3pm, and two NBA Emirates Cup games in the evening, with more than 100 unique advertisers across the lineup.
Americans say they will protect celebrations before restaurants. PPC Land reported that dining out would be the first cut for 29% of Americans, an Edible poll finds.
