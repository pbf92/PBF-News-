Stock Market Today: Markets Off to a Strong Black Friday Start
This post may contain links from our sponsors and affiliates, and Flywheel Publishing may receive compensation for actions taken through them.
Chipmakers are leading a Black Friday rebound after a rough November for tech. As of 8:06 a.m. ET, S&P 500 futures were up 1.7% and Nasdaq-100 futures had jumped 2.4%.
Premarket, NVIDIA (NASDAQ:NVDA | NVDA Price Prediction) rose nearly 3% as concerns about AI profitability eased. The S&P 500 has only a half session to hold those gains. Stocks close at 1 p.m. ET, and bonds close at 2 p.m.
Dow Futures Point to Best Week Since April 2025
Dow futures rallied 600 points, which puts the blue-chip index on track for its best week since April 2025. In delayed premarket trading, the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (NYSEARCA:SPY) was up 1.5% near $727.
A 4% Intraday Nasdaq Plunge Set Up Today’s Bounce
On Wednesday, the Nasdaq Composite fell more than 4% intraday before recovering to close lower, and Nvidia sank. Analysts blamed the chance of higher rates and concerns about heavy, debt-driven spending on AI data centers. That expansion still has to be powered, cooled, and wired by someone, and we rounded up seven of those suppliers in a free report here.
Most of the damage landed in tech. The Invesco QQQ Trust (NASDAQ:QQQ) tracks the Nasdaq-100. It is down 9% over the past month, compared with 5% for SPY.
The VIX, Wall Street’s fear gauge, closed Tuesday at 26[1]. That is still high, but it sits well below the 36[1] peak from Nov. 20.
Falling rates are helping. The 10-year Treasury yield ended Wednesday at 4.85%, down from 5.15% on Nov. 13. Lower long-term yields make future tech profits worth more today.
Inflation could get in the way: October’s PCE index rose to 3.8%, and the Fed raised the top of its rate range to 4%[2] in September.
Materials and Small Caps Gained While Tech Sold Off
Money has been moving away from tech. The Materials Select Sector SPDR ETF (NYSEARCA:XLB) is up 3% over the past week, while QQQ slid 2%.
So far this year, XLB has gained 16% and the iShares Russell 2000 ETF (NYSEARCA:IWM) has gained 11%. QQQ is up just 5%.
SPY owners carry more chip exposure than they may realize. Nvidia made up 8% of the fund at its last fact sheet. Swings in chip stocks move the index more than the typical stock does.
Tariffs and Oil Could Test the Rally
No major earnings or economic reports are scheduled for today. The Trump administration has proposed new tariffs that could raise levies on China to 40% if talks fail. Brent crude rose to $92 after Houthi attacks on Saudi energy facilities. If the Nasdaq keeps its gains through the early close while yields keep falling, that would be the clearest sign yet that the tech selloff is cooling.
