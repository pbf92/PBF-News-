TITLE: YouTube Videos on EAT-Lancet Diet Show Moderate Quality and Rare Reliability
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A cross-sectional content analysis evaluating digital communication surrounding sustainable nutrition has found that YouTube videos explaining the EAT-Lancet diet are predominantly of moderate informational quality, with high reliability remaining exceedingly rare across the platform. Conducted by researcher Nadide Gizem Tarakçı Filiz from the Department of Nutrition and Dietetics at Fenerbahçe University in Istanbul, Türkiye, and published in Frontiers in Nutrition, the investigation systematically examined how the planetary health diet framework is represented to the public. Evaluating 67 eligible English-language videos retrieved on May 1, 2026, the study revealed that 59.7 percent of the analyzed material fell into the medium total quality tier under the standardized DISCERN evaluation framework, while 20.9 percent scored as low quality and only 19.4 percent attained a high quality rating. More strikingly, when scrutinizing the specific reliability domain of the assessment, which inspects source transparency, clinical attribution, and uncertainty signaling, just 9.0 percent of the sample achieved high scores, highlighting significant deficits in how complex food system recommendations are digitally presented.
The research is situated within the pressing global imperative to address the double burden of dietary disease and environmental degradation through systemic dietary transformation. Food systems are recognized as fundamental drivers of planetary boundaries and chronic health conditions, with inadequate and unbalanced dietary patterns linked to an estimated 11 million deaths and 255 million disability-adjusted life years globally in 2017 according to referenced Global Burden of Disease data. Simultaneously, contemporary agriculture and food distribution networks generate approximately one-third of global greenhouse gas emissions alongside severe pressures on land use, freshwater reserves, and biodiversity. The EAT-Lancet Commission introduced its reference planetary health diet to model an optimal balance between human physiological wellness and environmental carrying capacity, emphasizing an abundance of whole grains, legumes, fruits, vegetables, nuts, and unsaturated fats, while strictly restricting red meat, processed meats, and added sugars. With the release of an updated 2025 commission report reinforcing equity, fairness, and socioeconomic accessibility, understanding public dissemination of this multidimensional framework has become a critical public health priority.
To investigate how this guidance translates into online consumer media, the researcher conducted a systematically structured retrieval protocol utilizing YouTube in incognito mode without user login history or algorithmic personalization. Employing four targeted query strings—including EAT-Lancet Diet, EAT-Lancet Healthy Diet, EAT-Lancet Planetary Health Diet, and EAT-Lancet Reference Diet—the initial search yielded 158 total records across the four search operations. After removing 21 exact duplicate entries, the remaining 137 records underwent rigorous screening against defined inclusion criteria requiring explicit thematic focus on the EAT-Lancet Commission, its reports, or the planetary health diet framework. The evaluation subsequently excluded 3 re-uploaded video duplicates, 28 non-English presentations, and 39 videos lacking direct topical alignment with the dietary framework, resulting in an analytical sample of 67 distinct videos. The final corpus comprised 56 conventional long-form YouTube videos and 11 short-form productions published in the YouTube Shorts format, permitting an exploratory comparative evaluation between communication architectures.
Quality and credibility evaluations were conducted using the established 16-item DISCERN instrument, an internationally validated scoring system engineered to appraise the reliability and information quality of consumer health guidance. The questionnaire grades attributes on a five-point Likert scale across three core divisions: items 1 through 8 determine publication reliability, items 9 through 15 gauge information quality regarding nutritional and lifestyle alternatives, and item 16 measures overall structural quality. Each video was evaluated independently by the author and two additional trained independent coders, establishing a three-rater panel. The three individual total scores were summed and averaged, yielding continuous compound metrics as well as categorical designations based on established thresholds from prior nutrition research: low quality at 37 points or fewer, medium quality between 38 and 58 points, and high quality at 59 points or greater. Inter-rater reliability analysis demonstrated exceptional concordance across scorers, with an average-measures absolute-agreement intraclass correlation coefficient ICC(A,3) of 0.917 for total DISCERN scores, 0.892 for reliability, 0.900 for information quality, and 0.849 for overall quality ratings.
The study also extracted and cataloged extensive structural metadata and categorical variables, with high inter-coder agreement confirmed via Fleiss kappa statistics ranging from 0.854 to 0.912. Engagement parameters revealed extreme rightward skew across viewers, characterized by a mean view count of 15,765 but a median of only 473 views, alongside an interquartile range of 4,972 and a maximum of 332,973. While mean duration reached 22.7 minutes, the median playback time was 8.48 minutes with an interquartile range of 26.6 minutes. Comment counts across 60 accessible videos yielded a modest median of 2.5 responses, whereas accessible like tallies across 65 videos reflected a median of 11.0 interactions. Categorical analyses identified educational presentations as the dominant narrative archetype, accounting for 58.2 percent of the corpus, while cooking shows, video blogs, and podcasts represented 20.9 percent, and journalistic news broadcasts constituted an identical 20.9 percent. Crucially, the entire sample contained zero personal experiential testimonial narratives, underscoring that EAT-Lancet communication operates largely as a didactic public policy debate rather than an individualized lifestyle testimonial.
A critical observation within the source profiling was the predominant role played by unverified individuals contrasted with an acute scarcity of academic institutions. Individual users and laypersons produced nearly half of all analyzed material at 49.3 percent, followed by non-profit organizations at 31.3 percent and commercial for-profit entities at 13.4 percent. In stark contrast, recognized academic institutions and universities generated only 4.5 percent of the sample, while dedicated healthcare delivery organizations accounted for an extremely modest 1.5 percent. Regarding speaker attribution, multi-speaker panels and interview panels were the most frequent format at 32.8 percent, whereas credentialed health professionals, dietitians, and nutritionists delivered 19.4 percent of videos. Specialized scientific researchers fronted 13.4 percent, unidentified presenters accounted for 13.4 percent, general actors and digital influencers represented 3.0 percent, and various other speakers made up 17.9 percent. Chefs, professional cooks, and fitness trainers were completely absent from the identified speaker roster.
Inferential statistical testing evaluated bivariate associations between categorical descriptive factors and DISCERN quality bands using Fisher exact tests adjusted for multiple testing across a family of twenty distinct analyses using the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate procedure. After applying false discovery rate adjustments, speaker classification demonstrated a statistically significant relationship with total DISCERN quality, yielding an adjusted p-value of 0.008 with a moderate-to-strong Cramer V effect size of 0.450, as well as with overall quality at an adjusted p-value of 0.003 and a Cramer V of 0.480. Presentations featuring multiple speakers comprised the largest share of the highest quality tier, with 50.0 percent of multi-speaker videos achieving high total DISCERN scores. Conversely, presentations helmed by health professionals, dietitians, and nutritionists had zero entries in the high total quality bracket, with 69.2 percent falling into the medium tier and 30.8 percent categorized as low quality, suggesting that conventional one-on-one counseling narratives frequently fail to address the systemic ecological and economic complexities of the planetary health diet.
The investigation also captured an exploratory divergence between conventional long-form YouTube presentations and compact YouTube Shorts. Following statistical correction, video architecture remained significantly associated with both total DISCERN quality categories, showing an adjusted p-value of 0.008 and a Cramer V of 0.478, and overall quality ratings, showing an adjusted p-value of 0.047 and a Cramer V of 0.385. Among standard long-form videos, 23.2 percent placed in the high quality tier, 64.3 percent occupied the medium tier, and 12.5 percent fell into the low bracket. In comparison, not a single YouTube Short achieved a high total quality classification, while 63.6 percent were designated as low quality and 36.4 percent were rated as medium quality. Sub-scale analyses indicated that differences in reliability and information quality across formats did not retain statistical significance after false discovery rate adjustment, recording adjusted values of 0.072 and 0.058, respectively. The author cautioned that these format observations must remain strictly hypothesis-generating given the limited sample of only eleven Shorts.
Analysis of public audience metrics demonstrated that commercial platform popularity fails to reflect scientific rigor or informational adequacy. Non-parametric Spearman rank-order correlation coefficients indicated that total view counts, audience comment volumes, and video upload years bore no statistically significant relationship with DISCERN quality indices. Public like tallies demonstrated only a weak, isolated positive correlation with the informational quality sub-scale, recording a correlation coefficient rho of 0.252, while exhibiting no significant correlation with overall reliability or total score. In contrast, total video length displayed substantial positive correlations with total DISCERN scores at a rho of 0.758, information quality at 0.704, reliability at 0.631, and overall quality at 0.614. The paper emphasized that this association reflects structural measurement opportunities embedded within the DISCERN instrument, as extensive broadcasts naturally possess greater airtime to cite literature, discuss alternative choices, and delineate clinical limitations, without necessarily guaranteeing empirical veracity.
Discursive stance coding revealed that 56.7 percent of the evaluated videos expressed an overtly supportive perspective toward the EAT-Lancet diet, while 22.4 percent advanced critical stances and 20.9 percent maintained neutral perspectives. Supportive videos exhibited higher descriptive proportions in the high total quality bracket at 26.3 percent, compared with 14.3 percent for neutral videos and 6.7 percent for critical videos. However, following Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate corrections, stance toward the dietary framework demonstrated no statistically significant association with total DISCERN quality, yielding an adjusted p-value of 0.699, nor with reliability at 0.732, information quality at 0.119, or overall quality at 0.140. Likewise, message categorization—contrasting didactic educational presentations with journalistic news and culinary podcasts—exhibited no statistically significant inferential association with DISCERN quality parameters, recording an adjusted p-value of 0.561 for total score, demonstrating that adopting an educational tone does not guarantee high quality communication.
