TITLE: How a CT Fellowship Helped One Young Pastor Find His Creative Community
OUTLET: Christianity Today

In 2024, Elijah Ramzy stumbled across an ad for CT’s inaugural Young Storytellers Fellowship. The message alerted him that it was the last day before applications closed.
Elijah, a pastor and writer from the Dallas area, loves all things creative. Whether short stories, devotionals, essays, or poetry, writing is his outlet for processing what he learns as a minister. At that time, Elijah was searching for a way to connect with other Christian storytellers and to find artistic resources in his own local community.
Having heard of CT’s positive reputation over the years, Elijah knew he had to jump at the chance. He applied by the fellowship’s deadline. Not long after, he found out he was accepted.
“It was really sweet,” Elijah told CT about the moment of his acceptance.
As part of the fellowship, Elijah attended in-person, immersive gatherings designed to foster the connections he was looking for.
“Whenever they introduced us to different pastors, different civic leaders, it opened my eyes to what creativity and faith in different areas could look like,” he said.
Born in Garland, Texas, Elijah is the son of a first-generation Salvadoran American mother and an African American father, and he was raised in a Black Pentecostal church. He came to faith in middle school on a mission trip to Arlington, Texas, and while away for college, he felt the call to ministry. Now he pastors a small church plant called Disciple First Church, also in Garland.
“I love getting to pastor in the same city that helped raise me,” said Elijah.
The fellowship helped Elijah build relationships that he wouldn’t have been able to otherwise. He was inducted into a cohort of young Christian creatives like himself, and alongside them, he was equipped as a storyteller for the next generation. The fellowship connected Elijah with pastors near him who care about creativity; he also met public figures like Michael Wear, founder of the Center for Christianity and Public Life, and members of the And Campaign, a Christian civic organization that provides a nonpartisan framework for believers to engage faithfully in politics.
After his time with CT, Elijah hosted an Inkwell Locals event at his church, which brought together Christian creatives of all ages and church backgrounds in the Dallas area. Passionate about Christian civil engagement that does not fall into partisan tribalism, Elijah has also been involved with the Dallas chapter of the And Campaign because of the networking opportunities offered by the Young Storytellers Fellowship.
Beyond Elijah’s time as a fellow, CT continues to serve as an influence for his ministry, particularly as he navigates a polarizing political climate.
“I think [CT] is a faithful, clear, and unifying voice that all believers can really latch onto,” Elijah said. “Specifically in the Bible Belt area, there’s a lot of political sway that goes specifically one way, and to pull against that is hard sometimes.”
“But having a resource like Christianity Today, their articles or their podcasts, is helpful because if there’s a rub … I can provide a resource that’s super healthy,” he continued. “It’s not necessarily my words, but it’s faithful to what I want to communicate.”
Elijah listens regularly to The Bulletin, CT’s flagship news podcast, and uses resources provided by CT Leaders (formerly CT Pastors). His favorite CT articles include coverage of how church communities provided disaster relief after Hurricane Helene and continue to combat homelessness and addiction in Portland, Oregon.
“Engaging with CT, with the fellowship and in general, has helped with getting a bigger vision for how churches can help in their communities,” Elijah said. “I’m relatively young, in my late 20s, and building out what it means for me to be a pastor in my local context.”
Inspired by CT’s voice, Elijah began working for an organization called Culture Makers, a faith-rooted mentorship program for students in Garland public schools. Many of the mentored students, now numbering over 300, attend church because of the impact made by Culture Makers.
“It’s a beautiful thing,” said Elijah. “That’s been my favorite way that God’s been moving in the world.”
Reflecting on what he experienced as a Young Storyteller Fellow, Elijah shared how encouraged he was by the support he has received from CT as a creative and ministry leader.
“CT is for churches. CT is for pastors. I just want to get that across as much as I can,” said Elijah. “I really believe in the work that y’all do.”
