TITLE: USC professor Azad M. Madni elected fellow of the International Academy of Medical and Biological Engineering
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Azad M. Madni, University Professor and the Northrop Grumman Foundation Fred O'Green Chair in Engineering at the University of Southern California, has been elected a Fellow of the International Academy of Medical and Biological Engineering (IAMBE).
Election to IAMBE, an honorary organization affiliated with the International Federation for Medical and Biological Engineering, is among the field's highest international distinctions. The academy recognizes individuals whose careers have made exceptional contributions to medical and biological engineering. Under IAMBE's charter and bylaws, active elected Fellows are limited to 250 worldwide.
Madni was recognized for pioneering transdisciplinary, model-based systems engineering, human-AI teaming and digital twin frameworks that address complex challenges in medicine, biomedical therapeutics and health care delivery. His research brings aerospace-grade systems rigor to biomedical innovation, from early advances in adaptive myoelectric signal classification for multifunctional prostheses to intelligent systems that strengthen clinical decision-making and biomedical device reliability.
“Being inducted into the International Academy of Medical and Biological Engineering is a profound honor,” Madni said. “Healthcare and biomedical engineering are inherently complex, human-centered systems. By integrating model-based systems engineering, digital twins and augmented intelligence, we can accelerate medical innovation and deliver safer, more personalized care to patients around the globe.”
At USC, Madni holds joint appointments in the Viterbi School of Engineering, the Keck School of Medicine of USC's Department of Ophthalmology and the USC Rossier School of Education. He is also a faculty affiliate of the Ginsburg Institute for Biomedical Therapeutics and founding director of USC's Distributed Autonomy and Intelligent Systems Laboratory and Ph.D. program in systems engineering.
Madni is a member of the National Academy of Engineering, a Fellow of The World Academy of Sciences, an International Fellow of the Canadian Academy of Engineering and a Fellow of the American Institute for Medical and Biological Engineering. He has received approximately 100 honors and awards, including the National Academy of Engineering's Bernard M. Gordon Prize for Innovation in Engineering and Technology Education, the John Fritz Medal, the IEEE Simon Ramo Medal and the ASME Spirit of St. Louis Medal.
“Professor Madni’s election to the IAMBE is a fitting and well-deserved honor that recognizes both his many contributions to biomedical engineering and the global impact of his work. Through his pioneering work in transdisciplinary systems engineering, he has created new pathways by which advancements in the medical field are not only achievable but directly impact and improve lives around the world. His spirit of innovation and commitment to societal impact embody the values at the heart of USC and Viterbi,” said Gaurav Sukhatme, dean (interim) of the USC Viterbi School of Engineering.
About the International Academy of Medical and Biological Engineering
The International Academy of Medical and Biological Engineering comprises Fellows recognized for outstanding contributions to medical and biological engineering. Affiliated with the International Federation for Medical and Biological Engineering, IAMBE promotes the global advancement of biomedical engineering research, education and clinical translation.
