TITLE: 'Unprecedented. Unbelievable. Unmissable.' The 'GTA 6' reviews are in — here’s what they're saying
OUTLET: Yahoo Tech

The much-anticipated release of Grand Theft Auto VI is just hours away. And critics who have spent weeks playing it before the public say it's worth the hype.
The open-world format allows gamers to explore Vice City, the sun-drenched fictional Miami-like metropolis in the Florida-like state of Leonida, as the Bonnie-and-Clyde-like criminal duo Jason and Lucia.
"Rockstar's Vice City is a masterpiece I never want to leave," PC Mag's Jordan Minor wrote in his review. "Grand Theft Auto VI is simply one of the best-looking video games I've ever seen."
He wasn't alone.
"Unprecedented. Unbelievable. Unmissable," declared IGN's Luke Reilly. "This is a world that feels more lived-in and real than anything I've ever played."
Same for the Guardian's Keza MacDonald, who gave it a glowing review.
"In GTA VI, Rockstar has created the most extraordinarily, indulgently detailed virtual world that we have yet seen," MacDonald wrote, adding: "Reviewing it feels a bit like trying to review, say, Paris: it contains so many potential experiences that it's hard to know where to start."
Here's what other top gaming critics are saying about GTA VI.
Yussef Cole, the New York Times:
This is a game about love and relationships, their joy and heartache, and the work required to make them successful.
The surprising depth to which these themes are explored makes for a memorable and endearing experience in Grand Theft Auto VI, which largely lives up to its sky-high expectations by inverting the series's reputation for destruction.
Zack Zwiezen, Kotaku:
The best part of Grand Theft Auto 6 isn't what I expected. It's not the crime, or the action, or the massive world to explore, though all of those parts are excellent and better than ever. The real standout of GTA 6, the part of the game that kept me hooked and which I won't forget anytime soon, is its central duo, Jason and Lucia.
Not only do they have very different motivations than past GTA protagonists, wanting to create a better life for themselves and escape their life of crime instead of vying to become crime lords or get revenge, but they are easily Rockstar's most fully fleshed-out, believable, and relatable characters.
Giovanni Colantonio, Polygon:
Through Jason and Lucia's genuine chemistry, GTA 6 gives you a better reason to work through Leonida's daily grind. Yes, there's always another bank to rob or car to steal, but that's not the reason you wake up in the morning. It's to prove that love can survive in a cruel culture that tries so hard to beat it out of people. So be it if you have to play dirty to keep pace with the cold-hearted crooks who make their fortune from someone else's pain. It shouldn't be this hard to settle down with your lover and enjoy a sunset together in the rotten social climate that Rockstar has been eager to lampoon for decades at this point.
People will hem and haw about Grand Theft Auto's purported glamorization of lawlessness. They always have; they always will. But it will be different this time. GTA 6's empathy is bound to be the most controversial thing about it.
Kyle Orland, ArsTechnica:
The reimagined and expanded Vice City is still the grand, crude, satirical criminal playground you'd expect after 25 years of 3D Grand Theft Auto games. And that world is as full, detailed, technically impressive, and polished as you would expect after this latest installment received a reported billion-dollar budget and roughly a decade of development work involving thousands of Rockstar staffers.
But it's the scenes between the over-the-top and somewhat repetitive action scenes that really make this Grand Theft Auto stand out. Almost every explosive gunfight or high-speed car chase is counterpointed by a sublimely dull, lightly interactive moment of mere routine existence: grilling burgers with pals by the water, playing fetch with a random dog, helping a neighbor tune up a car, or joking around over 18 holes of mini golf.
Taken together, the juxtaposition lends a sense of surreal hyperreality to the goings-on in Vice City and its environs. Each of the game's two sides gives the other a chance to breathe, forming a series of emotional peaks and valleys that stretch across a truly gargantuan amount of detailed simulation and pure gaming content. Grand Theft Auto VI never lets you get too settled with a budding life of crime or a budding family life. And it's all the better for it.
Gene Park, The Washington Post:
After enough years of playing games, you know what to expect. You recognize the tricks, anticipate the limits and learn which doors will never open. Other games are still immensely entertaining, but you stop wondering.
"Grand Theft Auto VI" brings the wonder back. I wanted to speak to everyone, follow every street and open every door. My experience with games kept telling me where the possibilities could end. Then "GTA VI" would do something that would shock and delight. Writing about this game feels like sending dispatches from the future. I've been desperate to tell people what happened there, as if I've returned from a new life.
Tamoor Hussain, GameSpot:
Only Rockstar commands the time and money necessary to make a game as dedicated to small details. And only Rockstar can execute on the realizing [of] such a granular vision unimpeachably. The obsession with the minutiae both explains and justifies the 13-year wait. This game is the culmination of everything the developer has previously achieved in the open-world space that pushes the genre to heights that, in all likelihood, no other developer will be able to reach.
GTA 6 is an astonishing open-world action game that I'd wager won't be outdone until Grand Theft Auto 7.
