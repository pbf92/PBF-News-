TITLE: Lancet Microbe: Scope, Metrics & Author Guide
OUTLET: CASRAI

On this page
The Lancet Microbe is a monthly, peer-reviewed Gold open-access medical journal published by Elsevier that launched in May 2020 to cover clinical microbiology, infectious disease dynamics, antimicrobial resistance, and microbial pathogenesis across all scales. Operating under the editorial standards of The Lancet family, the journal bridges laboratory microbiological research and clinical medical practice, making its complete article inventory freely accessible under Creative Commons licenses.
What Is The Lancet Microbe?
The Lancet Microbe was established in May 2020 as a dedicated platform for original research, reviews, and commentaries addressing microbial pathogens and infectious agents. Published exclusively in an online format under electronic ISSN 2666-5247, the journal emerged during a transformative period in global infectious disease surveillance, debuting its inaugural issue amidst the acute early phase of the COVID-19 pandemic. While its early volumes provided critical insights into emerging coronaviruses, the founding mandate of the journal was deliberately designed to encompass the entirety of pathogen biology rather than a single outbreak.
As an official specialty title within The Lancet publishing group, the journal inherits the editorial ethos, rigorous methodological verification, and global health advocacy established by its parent imprint. In scientometric evaluations, readers often examine The Lancet’s Journal Impact Factor when assessing the broader editorial network; however, The Lancet Microbe maintains its own dedicated editorial office, distinct scope parameters, and specialized review mechanisms tailored specifically to the needs of laboratory microbiologists, infectious disease clinicians, and epidemiologists.
The core editorial objective of the journal is to synthesize molecular microbiological discoveries with translational clinical outcomes. Unlike basic microbiology journals that focus purely on microbial physiology or environmental isolates, The Lancet Microbe prioritizes studies that have direct implications for human health, therapeutic interventions, diagnostic accuracy, or disease prevention policies. This translational orientation positions the journal as a key bridge between experimental bench science and patient-centered clinical practice.
Who Publishes The Lancet Microbe and What Does It Cover?
The Lancet Microbe is published by Elsevier Ltd. from its editorial offices in the United Kingdom. Editorial management is conducted by an in-house team of full-time professional scientific editors, led by inaugural Editor-in-Chief Onisillos Sekkides. Unlike academic journals where active faculty members handle submissions as part-time section editors, The Lancet Microbe employs professional editors who manage the triage, peer review coordination, and final editorial decisions without competing commercial or institutional conflicts of interest. This team is supported by an international editorial advisory board comprising leading clinical microbiologists, virologists, and public health researchers worldwide.
The journal’s thematic scope covers the complete lifecycle and pathology of infectious agents, including:
- Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR): Molecular mechanisms of resistance, plasmid transfer dynamics, global genomic surveillance of multidrug-resistant isolates, and novel therapeutic stewardship strategies.
- Pathogen Genomics and Evolution: Whole-genome sequencing, phylodynamics, viral and bacterial lineage tracking, and the identification of novel virulence factors.
- Human Microbiome and Host Interactions: Structural dynamics of the gut, respiratory, and urogenital microbiomes in relation to clinical disease, immune modulation, and therapeutic interventions such as fecal microbiota transplantation.
- Clinical Diagnostics: Development, validation, and field implementation of point-of-care assays, molecular diagnostics, metagenomic sequencing, and phenotypic susceptibility testing platforms.
- Epidemiology and Outbreak Science: Transmission chains, zoonotic spillovers, hospital-acquired infection surveillance, and population-level intervention trials.
Researchers frequently ask how The Lancet Microbe relates to its well-established sister title, The Lancet Infectious Diseases (launched in 2001). While both journals operate within the infectious disease ecosystem, their remits are clearly differentiated. The Lancet Infectious Diseases primarily publishes late-phase clinical trials, clinical management algorithms, health economics evaluations, and global disease burden studies. In contrast, The Lancet Microbe focuses on the biology, genomics, and diagnostics of the pathogen itself, providing the fundamental and translational evidence base upon which clinical trials and therapeutic protocols are developed.
Journal Impact Metrics and Indexing Status
Despite its relatively recent establishment in 2020, The Lancet Microbe has rapidly accumulated citation influence across biomedical databases. The journal is formally indexed in major international bibliographic repositories, including MEDLINE, PubMed, PubMed Central (PMC), Scopus, and Clarivate’s Science Citation Index Expanded (SCIE). In the National Library of Medicine catalog (NLM ID 101769019), the publication is classified under the broad medical subject headings of Bacteriology, Microbiology, Parasitology, and Virology.
In Clarivate’s Journal Citation Reports (JCR), The Lancet Microbe holds a Journal Impact Factor of approximately 21.9, placing it firmly in the highest quartile (Q1) across both the Microbiology and Infectious Diseases subject categories. For researchers evaluating publication venues, consulting Journal Citation Reports quartile rankings provides verified context on how citation distributions compare across disciplinary cohorts. The journal’s high metric standing reflects its selectivity, stringent methodological screening, and rapid dissemination of high-interest pathogen research.
Bibliometric performance in Elsevier’s Scopus database similarly demonstrates substantial citation velocity. The journal maintains a Scopus CiteScore metric between 18.4 and 22.9, placing it among the top percentiles of indexed microbiology serials. All research articles deposit their full XML and PDF records directly into PubMed Central upon release, ensuring full compliance with national and international open-access deposit requirements.
Publishing Model, Open Access Licenses, and APC Costs
The Lancet Microbe operates on an exclusively Gold open access model. Unlike hybrid journals that publish a combination of subscription-locked articles and optional open-access papers, every article in The Lancet Microbe is immediately and permanently accessible to readers worldwide without paywalls or subscription gates. The journal has been awarded the Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ) Seal, recognizing its adherence to high standards of digital archiving, licensing transparency, and publishing integrity.
To finance editorial evaluation, peer review administration, professional typesetting, and long-term digital preservation through CLOCKSS and Portico, the journal levies an article processing charge (APC). The standard list price APC is $8,570 USD (excluding applicable local taxes). Authors are not charged any submission fees, page charges, or color figure levies; the single APC covers the entire publishing workflow following formal peer-reviewed acceptance.
Because an APC of this magnitude represents a substantial expense for academic labs, several funding and waiver mechanisms exist:
- Research4Life Waivers: Corresponding authors based in countries classified under Research4Life Group A automatically receive a 100% fee waiver, while authors in Group B countries are eligible for a 50% waiver.
- Institutional Transformative Agreements: Hundreds of research universities, national consortia, and hospital systems maintain transformative read-and-publish agreements with Elsevier that cover author APCs directly from centralized library funds.
- Funder Mandate Compliance: The journal’s default CC BY 4.0 license aligns fully with open-access mandates from major research funders, including the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the Wellcome Trust, the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation, the European Research Council, and cOAlition S (Plan S). Authors may also select CC BY-NC 4.0 or CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 licenses when permitted by their funding stipulations.
How to Submit and Navigate the Author Workflow
Manuscript submissions to The Lancet Microbe are processed exclusively through Elsevier’s online submission portal, Editorial Manager. Authors must ensure their work satisfies the journal’s structural, stylistic, and reporting guidelines before uploading files to the portal. The standard author workflow consists of the following sequential stages:
- Pre-Submission Preparation and Scope Screening: Authors should assess whether their manuscript presents practice-changing findings or novel mechanistic insights. The editorial office strongly recommends reviewing recent issues to ensure the study design meets the expected methodological rigor and statistical validation.
- Drafting the Structured Abstract: Original research articles must feature a structured abstract of no more than 300 words, organized under five mandatory headings: Background, Methods, Findings, Interpretation, and Funding. Unstructured or narrative abstracts are returned to authors for correction prior to review.
- Constructing the Research in Context Panel: A signature feature of all Lancet journals is the mandatory ‘Research in Context’ summary box. Authors must provide concise bullet points under three explicit subheadings: (a) Evidence before this study, summarizing prior literature searches and existing evidence gaps; (b) Added value of this study, detailing the specific new knowledge provided by the current work; and (c) Implications of all the available evidence, explaining how the findings inform clinical guidelines, diagnostic protocols, or future research directions.
- Manuscript Length and Formatting: Standard original research Articles are generally limited to 3,500 words of main text (excluding abstract, references, and tables), while randomized controlled trials may extend up to 4,500 words. Reference lists are typically restricted to 30 citations in Vancouver numbered format, citing references consecutively with superscript numerals.
- Mandatory Author Declarations: Submissions must include an explicit contributors statement indicating the specific tasks performed by each author and identifying the corresponding authors who accessed and verified the underlying raw data. Authors must also complete standard ICMJE conflict of interest disclosure forms and provide a comprehensive data sharing statement identifying publicly accessible repository accession numbers (such as GenBank, SRA, or ProteomeXchange).
- Editorial Triage and Single Anonymous Review: In-house editors evaluate incoming submissions. Papers meeting editorial criteria are dispatched for single anonymous peer review, where reviewers know the author identities but reviewer identities remain masked. Editorial decisions (reject, revise, or accept) are communicated with detailed reviewer comments.
- Post-Acceptance Production and Proofing: Accepted manuscripts undergo professional copyediting, typesetting, and figure redrawing by The Lancet production staff. Authors receive electronic page proofs with a tight turnaround window (typically 48 hours) prior to online-first publication.
