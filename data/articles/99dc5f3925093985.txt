TITLE: World of Fallout Exhibit at the Las Vegas Atomic Museum Expands – and It’s Full of Easter Eggs for Fans
OUTLET: Xbox Wire

This past weekend, the National Atomic Testing Museum in Las Vegas, Nevada (aka, “Old Vegas”) doubled the size of its “World of Fallout” exhibit, which has been open to the public since November 2025. The exhibit juxtaposes recreated objects from the Fallout universe with the real-life artifacts from the nuclear era that inspired the game’s design. Playfully dark satire of Atomic Age retrofuturism and Cold War paranoia has always been a central pillar of the Fallout franchise, making the Atomic Museum a uniquely fitting place to explore the relationship between the games, and now the Prime Video series, and the real-world history that inspired them. One year after its launch, the exhibit has now doubled its footprint with two new galleries, featuring an interactive Overseer’s Desk, a fan-made, full-size Mister Handy robot, on-camera costumes from Season 2 of Prime Video’s “Fallout,” and more.
Below are some tips for what to check out in the newly expanded exhibit, and some of our favorite easter eggs tying together the Fallout universe with real-world history.
Always Be Scavenging
Just like playing a Fallout game, the best way to experience the exhibit is to take your time and pay attention to the little things. Even before reaching the World of Fallout galleries, you can take part in a Fallout Finds, an interactive scavenger hunt throughout the museum that encourages you to track down Fallout-themed items and scan their QR codes. Once you’re in the World of Fallout, be sure to keep scavenging, hidden historical artifacts and supply caches to be found tucked away in certain drawers and cabinets.
The Perks of Being a Hacker
Again, just like in the games, there are terminals to hack at the new Overseer’s Desk. Succeed, and you’ll get to read strange and spooky real-life anecdotes from the long history of the Nevada Test Site. Even if you’re a Fallout hacking master and have no trouble cracking the code, be sure to fail deliberately … you’ll want to see the warning that pops up
Signed Work
The exhibit features replicas of the in-game signs for New Vegas and the Lucky 38 casino, complete with classic Vegas neon. These were created in partnership with the University of Nevada, Las Vegas by students and staff in its Entertainment, Engineering, and Design program, providing what was no doubt a fun and valuable hands-on learning experience.
Fact or Fiction
One of the exhibit’s main elements juxtaposes real historical objects, like posters and pulp books, with their fictional Fallout equivalents. You might be surprised at how little Fallout exaggerates some parts of ’50s material culture.
Hands Off Mister Handy
Visitors to the exhibit are greeted by a life-size Mister Handy robot replica, created by fan fabricator, Dragonfoot3D, who also made the Deathclaw and terminal replicas.. Unlike some other parts of the exhibit, this unique museum piece is not to be touched, and his eyes will react threateningly if you get too close, to underline the point.
Photo Bombers
Pay particular attention to photos around the exhibit for some fun cameos. The Vault features an autographed photo of Edward Teller, father of the hydrogen bomb (memorably played by Benny Safdie in “Oppenheimer”), given to Troy Wade, one of the museum founders and a key employee at the Nevada Test Site. There is also a discreetly tucked-away photo of some Bethesda staff who helped make this exhibit possible.
Audio Log
Actor/crooner Perry Como’s music hasn’t graced the games themselves yet, but it sits right in their midcentury musical wheelhouse, as evidenced by songs being prominently featured in the Season 1 opening and Season 2 finale of Prime Video’s “Fallout.” There’s another real-world connection too; Como was born in Canonsburg, Pennsylvania, which hosted important nuclear material processing facilities in the ’40s and ’50s. Hidden in the exhibit, you’ll find Perry Como sheet music alongside a vial that once held radium paint produced in Canonsburg.
Gary
High-Perception fans may notice “GARY” spelled out in a stack of blocks. It’s a nod to Vault 108 in Fallout 3, where a cloning experiment left the vault overrun by hostile copies of the same unfortunate dweller, all named Gary, and all only able to say one thing …“Gary.”
The expanded World of Fallout exhibit is open now at the National Atomic Testing Museum in Las Vegas, Nevada. Find out more and how to visit on the official website.
