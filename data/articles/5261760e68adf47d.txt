TITLE: Palonosetron and Granisetron Reduce Nausea and Vomiting After Laparoscopic Gynecological Surgery
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis evaluating seven 5-HT3 receptor antagonists has determined that specific agents selectively reduce postoperative nausea and vomiting among patients undergoing laparoscopic gynecological surgery under general anesthesia. The synthesized evidence demonstrated that palonosetron, ramosetron, and ondansetron significantly decreased the incidence of postoperative nausea, whereas granisetron, palonosetron, and ondansetron showed superior efficacy in preventing postoperative vomiting compared with placebo.
Postoperative nausea and vomiting represent frequent complications following general anesthesia in laparoscopic gynecological procedures. While 5-HT3 receptor antagonists are commonly administered for pharmacological prophylaxis in clinical settings, medical researchers noted that no definitive clinical consensus had been established regarding the optimal agent among the available alternatives. To address this uncertainty, the investigators conducted a comprehensive comparative assessment to evaluate both the comparative efficacy and safety profiles of seven 5-HT3 receptor antagonists using network meta-analytic modeling.
To assemble the trial evidence, the researchers systematically queried four major medical databases: PubMed, EMBASE, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science. Eligible studies were limited to randomized controlled trials involving patients undergoing gynecological laparoscopic surgery. The analytical protocol established six primary clinical endpoints to assess patient recovery: the incidences of acute nausea, late nausea, overall nausea, acute vomiting, late vomiting, and overall vomiting across the postoperative period.
The quantitative synthesis ultimately incorporated 38 eligible randomized controlled trials comprising a pooled cohort of 4,415 patients undergoing laparoscopic gynecological surgery. The network compared placebo against seven distinct pharmacological agents within the 5-HT3 receptor antagonist class. Statistical evaluations were performed using a Bayesian random-effects model to estimate risk ratios alongside 95% credible intervals for all dichotomous outcome measures. The authors confirmed that the Bayesian models converged successfully, and node-splitting tests demonstrated no significant inconsistency across the direct and indirect evidence loops. Surface under the cumulative ranking curve values and rankograms were generated to rank the relative performance of each intervention.
In the evaluation of nausea outcomes, specific agents demonstrated marked reductions in risk relative to placebo during both early and later recovery intervals. For acute nausea, palonosetron significantly lowered risk with a risk ratio of 0.44 and a 95% credible interval of 0.28 to 0.68, achieving the highest surface under the cumulative ranking curve value among all assessed medications. Ramosetron also significantly reduced acute nausea risk with a risk ratio of 0.55 and a 95% credible interval of 0.31 to 0.91, while ondansetron yielded a risk ratio of 0.71 with a 95% credible interval of 0.58 to 0.87. When assessing late nausea, ramosetron outperformed placebo with a risk ratio of 0.38 and a 95% credible interval of 0.19 to 0.76, accompanied by palonosetron with a risk ratio of 0.43 and a 95% credible interval of 0.25 to 0.73, and ondansetron with a risk ratio of 0.60 and a 95% credible interval of 0.41 to 0.87.
Analyses targeting vomiting prevention identified granisetron as the leading pharmacological option in cumulative probability rankings. For acute vomiting, granisetron, palonosetron, and ondansetron were each statistically superior to placebo, with granisetron securing the top surface under the cumulative ranking curve value. In the analysis of late vomiting, granisetron substantially reduced risk compared to placebo, presenting a risk ratio of 0.21 and a 95% credible interval of 0.03 to 0.81, and again obtained the highest ranking on the cumulative ranking curve. Palonosetron likewise significantly reduced late vomiting risk with a risk ratio of 0.25 and a 95% credible interval of 0.13 to 0.48, followed by ondansetron with a risk ratio of 0.42 and a 95% credible interval of 0.28 to 0.60.
Regarding safety assessments, the investigators found that none of the evaluated 5-HT3 receptor antagonists produced a statistically significant increase in the risk of headache relative to placebo. In their final assessment, the authors concluded that ondansetron, palonosetron, and ramosetron provide effective nausea reduction, whereas granisetron, ondansetron, and palonosetron provide effective vomiting reduction after laparoscopic gynecological surgery. However, the study authors emphasized that these findings must be interpreted with caution due to the potential risk of chance findings arising from multiple comparisons and the presence of incomplete adverse event reporting across the included trial literature. The systematic review protocol was prospectively registered in PROSPERO under the identifier CRD420261442904.
