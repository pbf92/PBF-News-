Subtotal colectomy with cecorectal anastomosis as good as total colectomy IRA for Refractory Slow-Transit Constipation: Trial Results
A new study published in the Journal of American Medical Association showed that subtotal colectomy with cecorectal anastomosis (SC-CRA) is as effective as total colectomy with ileorectal anastomosis (TC-IRA) in terms of weekly complete spontaneous bowel movements (WCS) after 12 months.
Slow-transit constipation (STC) is a severe form of constipation characterized by delayed movement of stool through the colon. For patients who do not respond to medical treatment, surgery may be necessary. Although TC-IRA is the standard surgical approach, complications such as diarrhea, fecal incontinence, and bowel obstruction remain important concerns. Hence, this research was carried out to analyze the 1-year Wexner Constipation Score of individuals with STC who had SC-CRA versus TC-IRA.
A randomized clinical trial was carried out in 12 tertiary medical centers located in eight different provinces of China from April 2022 to April 2025. A total of 252 adult individuals with chronic constipation, experiencing less than one natural bowel movement per week, and relying on laxatives or other drugs for an extended period were recruited. The participants were divided randomly into two groups: SC-CRA (126 patients) and TC-IRA (126 patients).
At 12 months, the adjusted mean WCS was 7.01 in the SC-CRA group and 6.84 in the TC-IRA group. The difference between the two procedures fell within the prespecified noninferiority margin, indicating that SC-CRA was not clinically inferior to TC-IRA for the primary outcome. Early postoperative fecal incontinence at three months was significantly less frequent following SC-CRA than TC-IRA, affecting 8.7% and 21.4% of patients, respectively.
Perioperative morbidity was comparable between the groups, occurring in 10.3% of patients undergoing SC-CRA and 12.7% undergoing TC-IRA. Quality-of-life scores at 12 months were also similar. However, patient-reported satisfaction at one year was slightly higher following TC-IRA, at 97.5%, compared with 95.8% after SC-CRA.
Overall, the results indicate that subtotal colectomy with colorectal anastomosis could serve as an alternative surgical choice for patients with persistent slow transit constipation, especially for those for whom minimizing early postoperative urinary incontinence is a crucial factor. Extended monitoring is necessary to determine whether the advantages of preserving a portion of the large intestine persist over time.
