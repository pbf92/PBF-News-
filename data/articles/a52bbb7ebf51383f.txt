TITLE: The Biggest Entertainment Launch Ever, 'GTA 6' Hides a Secret Tom Cruise Thriller Mission
OUTLET: MovieWeb

When Avengers: Endgame broke the global box office opening record, earning $1.2 billion in a single weekend, Hollywood knew it had the hit of hits on its hands. And it did. But a certain video game just launched with the biggest debut in entertainment history.
Grand Theft Auto 6, Rockstar Games' latest crime epic, made an almost surreal $3.79 billion in its first day on the market. The magnitude of the game's release cannot be overstated. Yet so much of its DNA is built around movies, with dozens of cinematic winks interspersed across its massive playable map. Fans are finding all sorts of easter eggs, but one particular nod is going viral on social media today, showcasing Tom Cruise's enduring fame — a quick side mission loaded with references to the 2004 Cruise-led thriller, Collateral.
'Collateral' Is A Mid-2000s Action Hit With A Villainous Tom Cruise
In Collateral, Jamie Foxx plays cab driver Max Durocher. One eventful night, Max picks up Vincent, Tom Cruise's character. Despite Vincent's early claims to be a real estate broker, it soon becomes apparent that he is, in fact, a deranged hitman using Max's car to bring down several of his targets.
Grand Theft Auto 6's side mission doesn't exactly go into as much depth; it is, after all, merely an easily missable ten-minute detour. But the comparisons are obvious. It starts when a stranger seemingly enters the player's car at random, brandishing a gun. They look quite a bit like Tom Cruise, particularly as he appears in Collateral.
"You drive when I say drive, you stop when I say stop." — Vincent
As the player is forced to comply with the gun-toting stranger's demands, the stranger recites several key lines from the movie. Perhaps most recognizable is the above quote, though lines about running a red light and other dialogue snippets make the player's kidnapper seem increasingly like Tom Cruise's killer character.
Things do diverge as the mission goes on; while the Cruise stand-in cites Vincent's "yo, homie" line before opening fire on his targets, this is apparently the last time players will see him. If you attempt to follow him afterward, you'll find some corpses left in "Vincent's" wake, as well as a stash of cash and hard drugs.
Still, it's all unmistakably an ode to Collateral. With an 86% on Rotten Tomatoes, contemporary critics hailed both Cruise's and Foxx's performances, as well as the film's direction from Michael Mann. Mann, who directed films such as The Last of the Mohicans, Heat, and Ali, kept Collateral moving at a fast pace with its tight action sequences and growing sense of dread. It seems the movie has stuck in the minds of Rockstar Games' writers for the past 22 years, and players can now experience what it's like to have a grey-suited Tom Cruise tell them to start driving, no questions asked.
Grand Theft Auto 6 Is Bursting With Movie References
While no numbers have officially been released, analysts estimate that Grand Theft Auto 6 cost an eye-opening $1 billion to produce. While that is a truly astronomical number even amid rising costs across the gaming industry, it has clearly given the game's writers ample room to pack in a ton of pop culture references. A comic book found within the game's world, 'RX-Men', contains plenty of knockoff X-Men characters with pharmaceutically inspired names like Placebo and Side Effect.
On the cinema side, fans have found what appears to be Red Hulk from Captain America: Brave New World. Cutscenes are often staged and framed to echo memorable movie moments, such as Pulp Fiction's trunk shot. Paul Walker's car from the original Fast and the Furious is hidden in the game world, and the Saw franchise gets some eerie representation. Television icons like Dexter have also snuck themselves in.
