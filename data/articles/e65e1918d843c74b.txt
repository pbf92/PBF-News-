TITLE: ‘Grand Theft Auto 6’ Makes a Mockery Out of Right-Wing Anti-Trans Hysteria
OUTLET: Them

It’s the highest-rated video game of all time and it’s mocking right-wing anti-transgender talking points. We’re talking, of course, about the newly-released Grand Theft Auto 6, which generated $1.6 billion in preorder sales before its release on Thursday.
In one screenshot that has already gone viral, a billboard in the game’s fictional Vice City features an anti-trans message that parodies the right-wing obsession with guns and trans people, as well as the use of anti-trans rhetoric to shore up political support.
“Step 1: They take your guns. Step 2: Forced gender reassignment surgery,” the billboard, paid for by a fictional parody of the National Rifle Association called the American Rifle Membership, reads.
The idea that left-wingers are “coming for our guns” has been a recurring GOP talking point in political campaigning for decades — one that even forced 2024 Democratic nominee Kamala Harris to highlight her gun ownership publicly many times during her presidential campaign.
The billboard also uses hyperbole to parody the false right-wing idea, parroted by President Donald Trump without any evidence, that schools are secretly forcing students to become trans.
However, the satire of anti-queer hysteria doesn’t end there. As many people online have pointed out, the game also mocks the right’s more recent obsession with furries by featuring anti-furry propaganda throughout the game.
In one viral clip, a radio DJ is heard talking about Leonida, the fictional U.S. state modeled after Florida that contains Vice City, and its “No Paws Clause,” with fictional governor Mike Jimenez declaring the state “furry-free.”
Another ad from an anti-furry association warns people to “act accordingly” if they hear someone dressed as an animal use the words “uwu,” “rawr” or “hewwo.”
In its ongoing culture war, the GOP has often conflated trans identity with the furry lifestyle, even going so far as to advance legislation targeted at curbing the nonexistent problem of furries in schools. In September, Alabama Senator Tommy Tuberville delivered a bizarre, nonsensical speech about trans people that veered into fearmongering invective against the possibility of furries piloting commercial aircraft.
Though the ads are parodying right-wing talking points, some trans people online felt that the satire was not visible enough to be satire and feared that it might be mistaken as an endorsement of right-wing anti-trans ideology; others observed that the Grand Theft Auto series has long been known for its satire.
The game parodies other aspects of the conservative media sphere, including radio hosts who sound almost uncannily like Alex Jones and Nick Fuentes, per Newsweek.
