● Olympic Games 2028: Los Angeles ● In an interview with the Los Angeles Times, L.A. Police Chief Jim McDonnell said the total security cost for the 2028 Games will be from $3.5-4 billion and that the cost for his department alone will be about $1.3 billion.
That’s up from $732.2 million estimated in a May budget memo sent to the Los Angeles City Council. McDonnell, whom Mayor-elect Nithya Raman has said she wants to fire, explained about 9,400 officers in total will be needed for the busiest days of the Olympic Games, of which the LAPD could supply 25-33% at most.
So far, the Federal government has allocated $1 billion in 2025, expected to begin to be distributed in 2027.
● Football ● With the U.S. men’s National Team’s 2-0 win over Haiti in Cincinnati on Tuesday, Mauricio Pochettino (ARG) ended his term as the coach of the American team with a 23-12-2 record (W-L-T). In fact, his teams did well against all opponents except for those from Europe (UEFA):
By year:
● 2024: 4 matches: 3-1-0
● 2025: 18 matches: 10-6-2
● 2026: 15 matches: 11-5-0
By region:
● CONCACAF: 16 matches ~ 11-4-1
● Africa: 1 match ~ 1-0-0
● Asia & Oceania: 5 matches ~ 4-1-0
● South America: 7 matches ~ 6-0-1
● UEFA: 8 matches: 1-7-0
So Pochettino’s teams won or drew 24 of 29 (22-5-2) matches against everyone except UEFA teams, against which the U.S. was 1-7, beating Bosnia & Herzegovina in the 2026 FIFA World Cup round-of-32 match and otherwise losing to Belgium (2), Germany, Portugal, Switzerland and Turkey (2).
Against the U.S.’s regional arch-rivals, Pochettino’s teams were 1-2 vs. Mexico and 1-1 vs. Canada.
Compared with the other U.S. head coaches this century, Pochettino’s time was short:
● 74 matches: 44-17-13 ~ Gregg Berhalter (2018-22, 2023-24)
● 148 matches: 81-32-35 ~ Bruce Arena (1998-2006, 2017)
● 98 matches: 55-27-16 ~ Jurgen Klinsmann (2011-16)
● 80 matches: 43-25-12 ~ Bob Bradley (2007-11)
Pochettino’s teams won 61% of their games and averaged 2.08 goals per match, the most of any non-interim U.S. coach ever!
● Sailing ● Singapore’s Formula Kite World Champ Max Maeder was named the World Sailing men’s “Sailor of the Year” in the awards ceremony in Qingdao (CHN). Italian Maria Giubilei won the women’s annual award as one-half of the Nacra 17 World Champions, with Gianluigi Ugolini.
● Swimming ● Unique new partnership for USA Swimming, which announced last week the addition of Houston-based Transwestern Sports & Entertainment as its “Official Real Estate Services Provider.”
This is focused on local swim clubs, where the federation’s members are competing with other sports organizations, such as the Amateur Athletic Union (AAU) for swimmers. The announcement notes:
“The partnership will connect USA Swimming member clubs with Transwestern’s real estate advisory expertise to help identify opportunities for new aquatic facilities, swim lesson pools, and club expansion across the country. The collaboration represents an important step in USA Swimming’s broader strategic effort to support the business operations and long-term sustainability of its member clubs while creating new opportunities for participation in the sport.”
The agreement has nothing to do with international medals, but everything to do with the future success of USA Swimming.
● Weightlifting ● A provisional suspension for the use of “exogenous testosterone and adiol(s)” by Ukrainian lifter Kamila Konotop, the 2023 Worlds silver medalist in the women’s 59 kg class.
The International Testing Agency said the positive came from an out-of-competition test on 7 September 2026. She can request a test of her B-sample, or contest the violation.
