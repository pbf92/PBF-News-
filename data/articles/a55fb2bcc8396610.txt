TITLE: SiriusXM's new hunting and fishing channel leaves out older car radios
OUTLET: PPC Land

SiriusXM and Outdoor Sportsman Group today launched Outdoor Channel Radio, a 24/7 audio channel built from the hunting, fishing and shooting programs of the Outdoor Channel television network and its sister brands. It is available in the SiriusXM app and in vehicles fitted with SiriusXM's latest-generation 360L radios; cars with older satellite-only receivers are not on that list. The joint announcement, issued at 9:30 AM EST from Denver and New York, names eight shows and podcasts, from live MLF bass-fishing competition to Gun Stories with Joe Mantegna. It names no channel number, no subscription plan and no advertising arrangement.
In Short
SiriusXM has added Outdoor Channel Radio, a round-the-clock channel of hunting, fishing and shooting shows and podcasts drawn from the Outdoor Channel TV network and its sister brands. It reaches only people listening through the SiriusXM app or in a car with a 360L radio, so many subscribers with older in-car receivers cannot tune in from the dashboard. For outdoor brands and audio buyers it is a new home for a specialist audience, but SiriusXM has not said which plans include it, whether it carries ads, or how many people are expected to listen.
What the channel carries
According to the release, Outdoor Channel Radio is "a new exclusive SiriusXM channel delivering 24/7 programming for outdoor enthusiasts across North America." Its lineup draws on "the very best of Outdoor Channel's category leading programming and related content from OSG's other iconic media brands," with OSG standing for Outdoor Sportsman Group, of which Outdoor Channel is a division.
The release describes the material as "premium podcasts and audio content" and lists eight titles. Three are named as podcasts. One is live competition, and Wardens is called "the popular series." The rest are listed by name only.
MLF refers to Major League Fishing; the release does not spell out the abbreviation. It is the only item the release marks as live. The release does not say how often MLF events will air live, or whether they will be simulcast with television coverage.
The release adds that "Outdoor Channel Radio's daily programming lineup is specially curated to optimize the experience for a listening audience." That sentence is the only description of how the channel is assembled. There is no schedule, no list of hours, and no indication of whether television series such as Wardens are carried as audio from the broadcast episodes or reworked for radio. No hosts are named beyond those in the program titles.
SiriusXM's subheadline calls the service "SiriusXM's newest 24/7 sports channel," which places it alongside NFL Radio, NBA Radio and the company's other sports outlets in its own taxonomy. Jared Fox, SVP, Sports Programming, Content Marketing & Partnerships for SiriusXM, framed it differently. "At SiriusXM, we pride ourselves on having something for every interest and passion, and we're thrilled to expand into a new content genre with Outdoor Channel Radio," he said. "Together, we're creating the premier audio destination for hunting, fishing, and shooting sports," Fox added, describing the aim as "ensuring fans never miss a moment of the outdoor lifestyle content they love, whether they're on the road, out on the water, or in the field."
Where it plays: the app and 360L
The release confines distribution to one sentence: "Outdoor Channel Radio is available now to listeners on the SiriusXM app and in vehicles equipped with SiriusXM's latest generation 360L radios." RadioInsight, a radio trade publication, reported on the morning of the launch that the channel sits on channel 836 for 360L vehicles and the app. SiriusXM's own announcement gives no number.
360L is the version of SiriusXM's service that reaches a car by two routes at once, combining the one-way satellite broadcast with an internet connection through the vehicle's cellular modem. That second route is what allows SiriusXM to carry channels that never go up on the satellite. PPC Land's explainer recorded that 360L was in more than 50% of new SiriusXM-enabled vehicles in the fourth quarter of 2025, across a fleet of about 180 million enabled vehicles, and that roughly 20% of self-pay subscribers were on 360L as of July 30, 2026. On a PPC Land reading of those figures, about four in five self-pay subscribers can reach the new channel only through the app.
That pattern has become routine for SiriusXM's sports additions this year. When the company took on 42 Audacy stations across 29 US markets in July 2026, nine of the 23 sports stations went to all subscribers, while the other 14 and all 19 news and talk stations were limited to the app or 360L vehicles. The 2026-27 NBA package reserved home and away broadcasts of the same game for app and 360L listeners.
A comparison with wrestling, another specialist genre, shows a different choice. In June 2025, SiriusXM put Pro Wrestling Nation 24/7 on channel 156 across its radios and mobile apps from July 1, 2025, and the same slot became WWE Radio on July 27, 2026. Outdoor Channel Radio starts without satellite carriage.
Mike Carney, President & CEO of Outdoor Sportsman Group, described the reach in broad terms. "It's truly rewarding to see our Outdoor Channel Radio efforts come to fruition," he said, "and now tens of millions of SiriusXM listeners will have the opportunity to discover Outdoor Channel's unmatched mix of best-in-class hunting, fishing, shooting and outdoor adventure programs and podcasts." The release does not say how many of those listeners have app access on their plan.
The phrase "across North America" raises a further question the release does not answer. It does not say whether the channel is offered to SiriusXM listeners in Canada, where Outdoor Sportsman Group runs a Canadian version of Sportsman Channel.

Unlike the NBA and MLS announcements, today's release names no plan. Whether the channel sits inside the $5-a-month Sports Pass, which SiriusXM began selling on September 1, is not stated.
How the channel reaches listeners
According to the release, the channel is assembled and delivered in four stages:
- Source material. Programming comes from Outdoor Channel and from "related content from OSG's other iconic media brands," including the Guns & Ammo Buzz Podcast, named after one of OSG's magazines.
- Curation. The daily lineup is "specially curated to optimize the experience for a listening audience." No schedule or hours are given.
- Distribution. The channel is carried in the SiriusXM app and in vehicles with 360L radios. RadioInsight places it on channel 836; the release names no number.
- Access. The release directs listeners to siriusxm.com and names no subscription tier, price or trial.
Outdoor Sportsman Group's growing list of screens
Outdoor Channel has been on air since 1994, according to the release, which calls it "the independent cable network" and says it "maintains the largest outdoor TV footprint in the country" and is "available in more than 50 countries internationally." Its parent describes itself as "comprised of the world's foremost media and entertainment brands for outdoor adventure enthusiasts."
Audio is the latest distribution route for a company that has been spreading since early 2025 beyond its linear TV networks. The release refers to "the newly launched GAME & FISH TV," a FAST channel. OSG's own announcement put that launch on February 13, 2025, when the channel started on OutdoorChannel.com, GameandFishMag.com, MyOutdoorTV and Samsung TV Plus. OSG added Prime Video on January 22, 2026 and The Roku Channel on April 1, 2026, when it said its distribution partners had more than 430 million monthly active users combined, a figure for the platforms rather than for the channel's viewers. "Newly launched" therefore covers a channel that is 21 months old.
OSG's podcasts already appear on Apple Podcasts, Spotify and YouTube, according to the group's own website. The release calls the SiriusXM channel "exclusive" but does not say whether any of the named podcasts will be withdrawn from other platforms, or whether exclusivity applies only to the channel as a packaged stream.
What it means for SiriusXM's advertising business
The release does not mention SiriusXM Media, AdsWizz, sponsors or ad load. The context for buyers comes from SiriusXM's own recent results.
In the third quarter, advertising on the satellite and streaming service that carries the SiriusXM name fell 5% to $37 million from $39 million, against total company advertising of $456 million. SiriusXM attributed the drop to softer demand from news programming and direct-to-consumer advertisers, partly offset by continued strength in sports. Monthly active sports listeners rose 4%, average sports listening time among 360L subscribers rose 7%, and listening to WWE Radio had grown nearly 20% since its launch. Sports, in other words, is the category SiriusXM points to when its own ad sales soften, and today's channel is filed under sports.
Podcast advertising grew faster, up 20% in the third quarter after 37% in the first and 30% in the second. Three of the eight titles on the new channel are podcasts. The release does not say whether SiriusXM will represent them for advertising, as it does for shows in its podcast network.
The reach figure in SiriusXM's boilerplate also needs context. The release says the company "reaches approximately 255 million monthly listeners across its platforms." At its Built With Audio event on October 6, 2026, SiriusXM Media gave that number as unduplicated US monthly listeners, up from 170 million, after adding the YouTube audio inventory it sells. In July 2025, when it launched SiriusXM Play, the company cited about 160 million. The increase came with the addition of YouTube audio, where Outdoor Channel Radio does not play. Carney's "tens of millions" is the more relevant scale for this channel.
There is also a second fishing product in SiriusXM's November. On November 2, the company began selling its Fish Mapping app for $199 a year without a satellite plan, alongside a $109.99-a-month satellite marine service. Fox's "out on the water" line could describe the same customer. The two announcements do not refer to each other.
What the announcement leaves out
The release gives no channel number, plan list, price, schedule, ad load, sponsor, audience estimate, contract length or financial terms. It does not say whether the channel is available to SiriusXM subscribers in Canada, whether it is included in Sports Pass, or whether the named podcasts remain on other platforms.
