TITLE: ‘GTA VI’ Industry Domination Will Not End Anytime Soon
OUTLET: The Hollywood Reporter

If you’re just waking up from a coma, we’ve got good news. If you’ve been in the coma since 2013, we’ve got really good news.
After 13 years and what has felt like as many delays, Grand Theft Auto VI comes out on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S at midnight (your local time). Finally. Pre-launch, GTA VI is already a smash hit, approaching $1.7 billion in pre-sales (and more than 17 million units sold), according to Sensor Tower data. It is also getting perfect review scores.
Yes, Rockstar is feeling like rockstars.
Probably no single piece of media has been as highly anticipated as GTA VI, developed and published by Take-Two Interactive imprint Rockstar Games. Some of the excitement is measurable. Last week, GTA VI ranked number one on Screen Engine/ASI’s “Global Attention Share” chart, claiming 5.64 percent of the pie and booting Minecraft out of the spot it held for 45 straight weeks. It was the first time an unreleased game reach first place. Actually, it was the first time an unreleased game ranked better than fourth place (2019’s Call of Duty: Modern Warfare).
GTA VI has now spent 15 weeks in the international research firm’s global top 10, obliterating Modern Warfare‘s record of three weeks. (Screen Engine/ASI balances Google search volume, Twitch and YouTube traffic, and Steam activity to arrive at a proprietary percentage of attention across thousands of games; tracking began in 2019.)
Colan Neese, Screen Engine/ASI’s senior vice president of gaming, tells The Hollywood Reporter he expects GTA VI‘s Global Attention Share will exceed 10 percent when the current week’s numbers come out on Tuesday, Nov. 24. Neese predicts GTA VI will occupy his top spot through at least January 2027.
Gene Park, the digital culture and video games critic for The Washington Post, has already beaten Grand Theft Auto VI. It took him 55 hours of gameplay. (Park and select other reviewers received preview codes — and serious NDAs — from Rockstar.)
But there is so much more for Park in the seemingly endless playground that is Vice City: GTA VI‘s setting based on real-life Miami. Park has since tacked on another 115 hours of gameplay, and is in no way bored.
When we spoke on Wednesday, Park was in the middle of a second full run through the game. This time, he is deliberately making different choices for the protagonists Jason and Lucia. The diverse results, Park says, have been staggering. (Also, he just discovered a zoo after 170 hours of gameplay — that should give you a sense of GTA VI‘s sheer scale.)
There are “just two ways” GTA VI “could have disappointed,” Joost van Dreunen, a video-game industry strategist and NYU professor, told THR. The first would have taken “some kind of awful geopolitical event.” Boy does it feel like we came close during this past week. The second is series of crippling bugs a la Cyberpunk 2077. Rockstar would have never let that happen, van Dreunen said. The publisher would have just delayed GTA VI a third time.
As it is, GTA VI is awesome. After a baker’s-dozen years and an $80-$100 price tag, it had to be. The anticipation (and those delays) has created a “novel… simultaneous synchronized event” experience for a single-player video game, van Dreunen said, which was not possible prior to the digital age. (Rockstar is not releasing GTA VI discs.)
Park believes it will be “a couple of months at the very least” before we get an Online option. GTA V‘s Online component still brings in $300-$500 million per year in revenue, per van Dreunen, through Shark Cash Cards. (You pay real money for in-game currency; Grand Theft Auto Online launched just two weeks after GTA V.)
Van Dreunen says GTA VI will be the dominant video game for “easily” a year, and probably longer. Its coming (but not announced) PC launch and inevitable GTA Online integration (also not announced) will keep the champagne corks popping like, well, Rockstar’s parent company’s stock. Shares of Take-Two Interactive’s Software (TTWO) are up more than 7 percent (from Tuesday’s market close) on those glowing GTA VI reviews — and Wednesday was a down day for the major index funds.
