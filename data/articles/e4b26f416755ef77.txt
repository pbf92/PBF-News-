TITLE: Manchester United’s debt tops $1.45B despite cost-cutting efforts
OUTLET: Sports Business Journal

Premier League club Manchester United have confirmed that the “club’s debt remains in excess of” $1.45B in their latest financial figures, according to Steven Railston of the MANCHESTER EVENING NEWS. United released their first-quarter financial results on Thursday afternoon, confirming that non-current borrowings as of Sept. 30 were $759.1M, up from $632.5M in the same quarter the previous year. The club also have “a rolling credit facility” -- akin to a credit card -- and its balance stands at $272.3M compared to $352.5M in the same quarter the previous year. In addition, the club have $437.7M in “trade and other payables,” which is “mostly made up of outstanding transfer fees.” United have “reduced costs across the club to save money,” and they believe that the latest figures are “evidence of the benefits of their ‘off-pitch transformation’, driven by disciplined management and improved operational efficiency.” Club sources said that United are “focused on improving profitability to create a stronger financial platform to support investment in football and the club’s ambition to win major trophies” (MANCHESTER EVENING NEWS, 11/19).
A CLOSER LOOK: In London, Richard Jolly writes ManU’s net finance costs for a three-month period to the end of September were $19.1M, meaning they will spend almost $79M a year in servicing the debt the club has, as “a consequence of the leveraged buyout of the club by the Glazer family in 2005.” For the first quarter of the financial year, they made an operating loss of $3.9M and had total revenues of $192.2M. The figures do not include the cost of paying off manager Michael Carrick after his sacking, or the compensation required to hire USMNT coach Mauricio Pochettino and his backroom staff, thought to be at least $9.9M (London INDEPENDENT, 11/19).
