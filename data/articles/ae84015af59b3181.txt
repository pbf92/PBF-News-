TITLE: Readability, quality, and transparency of Turkish-language erectile dysfunction websites: a cross-sectional study
OUTLET: Nature

Abstract
Erectile dysfunction is highly prevalent, yet many affected men avoid professional help and turn to the internet for information. Although online erectile dysfunction information has been studied in English, evaluations in other languages remain limited, and, to our knowledge, none has examined Turkish-language websites. This cross-sectional study assessed the readability, quality, and transparency of Turkish-language erectile dysfunction websites. Google.com.tr was searched in incognito mode using three Turkish terms; 96 websites met the inclusion criteria. Readability was assessed with the Atesman and Bezirci-Yılmaz formulas, and quality and transparency with the DISCERN instrument and the Journal of the American Medical Association benchmark criteria, scored by two independent urologists (inter-rater reliability by intraclass correlation coefficient and kappa). The median Atesman score was 42.04 (“difficult”), with 84.4% of sites difficult or very difficult to read. The median DISCERN score was 58.00 (“good”), with 87.5% rated good or excellent, but attribution was met by only 6.8% (median benchmark score 2.00). Physician sites scored higher than hospital sites on DISCERN (p < 0.001) and the benchmark criteria (p = 0.004), whereas readability was comparable and showed no correlation with quality. Professionally sourced Turkish erectile dysfunction websites achieved good DISCERN scores but were written above the reading level of most patients; improving readability and transparency should be priorities.
