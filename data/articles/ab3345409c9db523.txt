TITLE: AI-facilitated radar signal processing: A comprehensive review
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Radar has long been one of the most important sensing technologies in aerospace, remote sensing, marine monitoring, intelligent transportation, and emerging integrated sensing and communication systems. As an active electromagnetic sensing technology, radar can operate day and night and under complex weather conditions, providing critical information about targets, environments, and motion states. However, as application scenarios become more dynamic and complex, traditional radar signal processing methods based mainly on statistical models and handcrafted features face increasing limitations.
The research team led by Professor Yu Liu at Tsinghua University has recently published a comprehensive review in Chinese Journal of Aeronautics (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cja.2026.104203), systematically summarizing how artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming radar signal processing. The review provides a broad and structured overview of the field, covering the full radar signal processing chain from waveform design and target detection to target recognition, tracking, clutter suppression, anti-jamming, radar imaging, and intelligent hardware deployment.
The review highlights several key technical directions. In waveform design, AI can support multi-objective optimization under hardware constraints and help radar systems adapt their transmitted signals according to the environment. For target detection, AI models such as CNNs, Transformers, and GNNs can learn discriminative features from raw signals, frequency-domain data, time-frequency maps, and multi-domain fusion inputs. For target recognition, AI enables more effective modeling of HRRP, SAR, polarization, and time-frequency features. For target tracking, learning-based methods can improve trajectory prediction, data association, and multi-source fusion under complex motion and missing measurements. For clutter and interference suppression, AI can adaptively recognize and counter diverse jamming threats and learn clutter structures to enhance target-background discriminability. For radar imaging, hybrid model-data driven approaches integrate physical priors with learning to improve resolution and robustness under non-ideal conditions while preserving interpretability. In addition, intelligent hardware deployment must balance accuracy, latency, power consumption, and cost across heterogeneous hardware platforms including GPUs, FPGAs, ASICs, and DSPs.
The paper also emphasizes that AI is not only improving individual algorithms, but also expanding the application boundaries of radar. AI-facilitated radar is expected to play a growing role in healthcare monitoring, intelligent transportation, marine surveillance, and integrated sensing and communication. These applications require radar systems to extract cross-domain information, fuse multimodal data, and make adaptive decisions in real time.
At the same time, the review points out that AI-facilitated radar still faces challenges before large-scale deployment. Radar datasets are often limited, imbalanced, noisy, and difficult to label. Models trained in one environment may not generalize well to another platform, frequency band, sea state, terrain, or target posture. Many deep networks are also computationally expensive and difficult to deploy on resource-limited radar hardware. In addition, the black-box nature of AI models remains a concern for high-reliability applications.
Looking ahead, the team identifies several future research priorities. Lightweight AI models, including pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, low-rank adaptation, and hardware-friendly network design, will be essential for real-time radar deployment. Physics-guided deep learning will help combine electromagnetic theory, detection principles, imaging models, and motion models with data-driven learning. Multimodal sensing and pre-trained large models may further enhance radar’s ability to understand complex environments. Finally, cognitive radar and distributed intelligent radar networks will push radar systems toward greater autonomy, collaboration, and adaptability.
