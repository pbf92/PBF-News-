S&P 500 recovery stalls as AI stocks fall again
Nvidia’s leadership reversal has not ended the pressure on AI stocks. In his Nov. 24 report, StoneX analyst Fawad Razaqzada said Nvidia was down around 5% and Anthropic had fallen below its $83 IPO price as the previous day’s relief rally lost momentum.
Razaqzada’s analysis tied the renewed weakness to unresolved questions about Nvidia’s governance, chip export restrictions and the prospect of another Fed rate increase. Jensen Huang’s return as chief executive had not removed those pressures.
He identified 7,224–7,298 as a former support area that the futures-based index he follows needed to reclaim. He wanted a close above its 200-day moving average and 7,421 before calling a bottom. Those are technical conditions for his recovery view, not year-end forecasts or the cash-index value used to settle the contracts.
Yardeni cuts his S&P 500 target to 7,500
Ed Yardeni has lowered his year-end S&P 500 target to 7,500 from 7,900, having previously reduced it from 8,400 in September. His forecast remains for a recovery, but a smaller one before the year ends.
In Yardeni Research’s market call, Yardeni and Toby Hearst said the index had fallen 11.6% from its Oct. 6 record by the previous Friday. The Nasdaq-100’s decline from its Oct. 5 high was 19.8%. They still saw a correction rather than a bear market because they did not expect an impending recession.
Their concern was semiconductor earnings. Tighter export controls could weaken chip demand and prices after a sharp drop in valuations. They put the S&P 500’s forward price-to-earnings ratio at 16.4 and the semiconductor industry’s at 9.4 that Friday.
The revised 7,500 target would still leave the index 9.6% above its 2025 finish of 6,845.50.
Other strategists still see a finish near 8,000
Yardeni’s reduced target sits below the November forecasts from Argus Research and LPL Research. Both firms’ outlooks predate the latest selloff; their dates matter when you compare them with the live odds.
Fed officials flag AI financing and inflation risks
The Federal Reserve’s October meeting minutes describe another risk to the AI recovery: debt used to finance the buildout. Staff highlighted off-balance-sheet leasing and financing arrangements between AI companies that could amplify losses if investment returns disappoint.
Almost all meeting participants discussed the risks of disappointing AI returns. Most noted rapid growth in AI-related debt and the complexity of its financing. The minutes did not name individual companies in that discussion.
Officials also saw AI investment as a source of inflation pressure. Many said demand associated with the buildout was straining supply and raising prices of technology-related consumer goods. Almost all judged that further rate increases would likely be appropriate under their baseline outlooks.
The minutes also recorded a buffer against a more severe downturn: strong bank and household balance sheets.
Comparing the full S&P 500 closing-range forecast
Kalshi and Gemini offer mostly 200-point closing ranges. Polymarket offers broader 500-point middle ranges, alongside lower and upper outcomes. A higher probability on one of those wider intervals is not a like-for-like comparison with a narrower range.
Yardeni’s 7,500 target fits inside Kalshi’s and Gemini’s 7,400–7,599.99 interval. A stronger recovery to 8,000 would instead fit their 8,000–8,199.99 interval. Buying the first range does not cover both outcomes.
The lower ranges also remain part of the forecast. A close around last year’s 6,845.50 finish would sit inside the 6,800–6,999.99 interval on Kalshi and Gemini, and the 6,500–7,000 bracket on Polymarket. Compare those outcomes with Yardeni’s recovery forecast without turning any range’s midpoint into a precise index target.
How the S&P 500 year-end contracts settle
On Kalshi, S&P close price end of 2026? asks where the index finishes on Dec. 31 at 4 p.m. EST. A Yes position on 7,400–7,599.99 needs the closing index value to fall inside that range.
The range series names S&P Dow Jones Indices as its settlement source.
The separate question, S&P price at year-end?, asks whether the index finishes above individual thresholds at the same time. For the 7,500 contract, its detailed condition says the index must be above 7,500.
More than one threshold can qualify. A finish of 7,650 would satisfy the conditions above 7,000, above 7,400 and above 7,500. Do not add their implied probabilities together as though they were alternative finishing ranges.
The range contracts close on Dec. 31, but their terms allow expiration at the first release of the data or one week after that date, whichever comes sooner. The time trading ends and the time settlement is completed are not necessarily identical.
On Gemini Predictions, S&P close price end of 2026? also uses the Dec. 31 index value at 4 p.m. EST. Its listed outcomes include the same 7,400–7,599.99 range. Gemini’s market page says the contracts expire at the first release of the data or one week after Dec. 31, whichever comes sooner.
On Polymarket, What will S&P 500 (SPX) close at end of 2026? uses the official close on December’s final trading day, with Yahoo Finance’s historical closing prices as its source. Its middle ranges are 500 points wide, rather than the mostly 200-point intervals offered by Kalshi and Gemini.
Polymarket assigns an exact boundary value to the higher bracket. A shortened final session still uses its official close. If no official closing price is published, its rules specify the last valid on-exchange trade price of the regular session. These are closing-price contracts, not contracts on the highest level the index reaches during December.
How to trade an S&P 500 year-end range
If your view is that the S&P 500 will finish around Yardeni’s 7,500 target, the 7,400–7,599.99 range is one way to express it. If you expect a much stronger finish, an above-7,500 threshold position expresses a different view: it does not impose an upper boundary.

For example, on Kalshi’s S&P close price end of 2026? market, buying 500 Yes contracts at 20¢ on the 7,400–7,599.99 range costs $100 before fees. The example entry price implies a 20% probability that the index finishes inside that range; it is not a current quote.
If the index finishes at 7,500 and the outcome settles Yes, the contracts return $500, a $400 profit before fees. A finish at 7,650 falls outside the range: the contracts return nothing and you lose the $100 purchase cost before fees.
You can instead sell before settlement. If another trader buys those same 500 contracts at 30¢ each, you receive $150 and realize a $50 profit before fees.
For example, you might buy before the Nov. 25 PCE release because you expect the 7,400–7,599.99 range to become more expensive, then sell after the release. That trade depends on your entry and exit prices. Only a position held through settlement depends on the final index close.
The range’s upper boundary is the main risk that a simple bullish label misses. Stronger stock performance can make a particular range less likely if traders begin expecting a finish above it. Choose the contract that matches the level you expect, rather than treating every Yes position as a trade on stocks going up.
S&P 500 dates to watch before the year-end close
Inflation and jobs releases will reach traders on both sides of the Fed’s final scheduled meeting of the year. The BEA release schedule, BLS December calendar and Fed meeting calendar set out the remaining dates:
- Nov. 25, 8:30 a.m. ET: October personal income and outlays, including PCE inflation, plus the second estimate of third-quarter GDP and corporate profits.
- Dec. 4, 8:30 a.m. ET: November jobs report, including payrolls and unemployment.
- Dec. 8–9: The final scheduled Fed meeting of the year, with the decision at 2 p.m. ET on Dec. 9 and the press conference at 2:30 p.m.
- Dec. 10, 8:30 a.m. ET: November CPI inflation, arriving after the Fed decision.
- Dec. 23, 8:30 a.m. ET: November personal income and outlays, including PCE inflation, plus the third estimate of third-quarter GDP and updated corporate profits.
- Dec. 31, 4 p.m. EST: The index observation specified by Kalshi’s and Gemini’s year-end contracts. Polymarket uses December’s final trading session’s official close.
The order matters: policymakers will have November’s jobs report before their meeting, but November CPI arrives the next morning. The Dec. 23 PCE release gives traders another inflation reading after the decision and before year-end settlement.

S&P trading expands from year-end markets to perpetuals
Kalshi launched its US 500 perpetual future on Oct. 6, adding leveraged long and short exposure to large U.S. companies alongside its fixed-outcome prediction markets. Our report on the stock-index perpetual launch explains the expansion beyond Yes/No contracts.
The benchmark is important: the launch contract tracks the MerQube US Large Cap Index, not the S&P 500. Kalshi’s contract filing describes a total-return index covering 500 large U.S. companies. It is a separate product from the S&P closing-range contracts above, not a perpetual version of their year-end settlement question.
With a perpetual, you trade ongoing price movements without a fixed expiration date or a finishing-range boundary. Funding payments between long and short positions help keep the contract aligned with its benchmark. Leverage magnifies gains and losses, and collateral requirements can force you out before a recovery. Your result therefore depends on the price move, funding and fees, rather than whether December’s final close lands inside one range.
