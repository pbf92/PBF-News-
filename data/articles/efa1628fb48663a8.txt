TITLE: BBC accused of treating religious broadcasting as less important in Daily Service shake-up
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Former BBC broadcaster Roger Bolton has said he believes the BBC needs to "ask themselves" whether they think religious programming is important after deciding to move Daily Service from a prominent time slot.
The programme will be pulled from prominent morning slots on flagship news stations Radio 4 and Radio 4 Extra to an early morning 5:45am broadcast on local radio stations.
Daily Service has stood on the Radio 4 schedule for 98 years, first airing in 1928.
Bolton, a Christian and former editor of the corporation's investigative television show Panorama, told Premier Christian News that the BBC needs to evaluate its mission as the taxpayer-funded public broadcaster.
"I think this is inevitable, because the BBC are not asking themselves 'what is public broadcasting, what is the BBC for?', he said.
"It is regarding itself as a big business, everybody takes 10% off and then they look at numbers: young people, we want them, old people, not so important they aren't the future.
"There has been no intelligent discussion about the role of religious broadcasting on the BBC, there is no head of religion to make these arguments.
"The thing that is very important is not to let the BBC regard itself as a big business only, to be market-led, it is to say 'what do we think is important to society?' "
The issue was raised in the Church of England's General Synod meeting on Wednesday, where Rev Canon Phillip Billson, of Manchester South and Stretford, called for the decision to be reversed.
Canon Billson, who is a former editor of Daily Service, has called for the BBC to reinstate the programme to a prominent time slot and for it to appoint a head of religion and ethics radio.
Bolton supports the proposal and argues that the BBC has a decision to make about whether religious programming is a key pillar of their radio output.
"Do we think that an understanding of faith, and in particular the Christian faith, is important," he asked.
"If you take that view, you have to find places to come across by serendipity. The problem about our digital future is that will be a lot of choice there, but a lot of people will not know how to find them.
"The digital stations will make sure that the most important entertainment shows are prominent, but if you want to find out about something less popular, how will you do so?
"It starts off saying "it's available, no one is going there", as opposed to having a regular place when you're switched on and it's unexpected and you make a date with it."
A BBC spokesman told The Telegraph: “We know how much listeners value The Daily Service and the BBC will continue to provide a daily act of Christian worship on BBC Sounds.
"From Spring 2027, it will be broadcast across all BBC Local Radio stations, Monday to Friday from 5.45am to 6am, when the audience reach is broadly comparable to Radio 4 Extra’s current audience at 9.45am. All episodes will also be available on BBC Sounds, so listeners can hear the programme at a time that suits them.”
