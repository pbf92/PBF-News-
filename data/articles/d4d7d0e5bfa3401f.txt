TITLE: 1,486 newsletters on Ghost charge exactly the platform's $5 default
OUTLET: PPC Land

Paid newsletters and membership sites charge a median of $7 a month, and 72% of them sit between $5 and $8, according to a count of 20,868 paid publications published on November 18, 2026 by Magic Pages, a company that sells hosting for the Ghost publishing software. Among the 4,289 Ghost sites in the sample that can take payments, 1,486 charge exactly $5 a month and $50 a year, the price Ghost assigns to every new paid tier.
The study, titled What digital & print publishers charge in 2026, takes every paid publication priced in dollars in Substack's public directory and in Ghost's Explore directory, records the lowest monthly price each one advertises, and sets the result beside 4,004 print magazine issues sold in seven independent shops in the UK and Germany. Prices were collected on October 6 and 7, 2026. Jannis Fedoruk-Betschki, founder of Magic Pages, introduced the data in a blog post on November 18 under the headline What do paid newsletters charge? We counted 20,868.
The interest is not neutral, and the study says so. "Magic Pages sells hosting for Ghost and earns money on every publication it hosts, so we have a stake in this comparison," the fee section states. The prices themselves come from public listings and public APIs; according to Magic Pages, no customer data was used.
In Short
A Ghost hosting company counted the advertised prices of 20,868 paid newsletters on Substack and Ghost and found most charge $5 to $8 a month, with a median of $7. The figures matter to anyone selling subscriptions or buying newsletter audiences, because they show price follows topic far more than audience size: finance newsletters charge a median of $29, comics $5, and Substack's largest publications charge the same $7 as its smallest. Fees also differ, with a publisher keeping between $5.75 and $6.45 of a $7 payment depending on the platform, though the comparison comes from a company with a commercial stake in it.
Where the prices cluster
Thirty-five percent of the 20,868 publications charge exactly $5 a month, the single most common price. A quarter charge $5 or less, half charge $7 or less, three-quarters charge $8 or less, and nine in ten charge $15 or less, according to Magic Pages. Twenty percent charge $10 or more, and 996 publications charge $30 or more.
Source: Magic Pages, 20,868 paid publications priced in US dollars, October 2026.
Why five dollars? Two platform rules point at it. Substack's support page, updated August 17, 2026, sets a floor: "The minimum subscription price on Substack is $5 per month and $30 per year. These minimums apply to US-dollar Stripe accounts; pricing in other currencies may vary." Substack's "Going paid checklist" from August 8, 2022 adds a benchmark: "A standard offering is $5/month, $50/year, and $250 for founding members."
Ghost, for its part, ships a default. According to Magic Pages, the "Default Product" in Ghost's source code is priced at 500 and 5,000 cents a month and a year, a setting unchanged since October 2022, while the admin accepts prices as low as $1. That explains the 1,486 Ghost sites at exactly $5 and $50. The study cannot tell whether those publishers chose the price or never touched it. Take them out and 30% of the remaining 19,382 publications charge $5, with the median unchanged at $7. A further 4% of all paid publications charge less than $5, and every one of them is on Ghost, the only one of the two platforms that allows it.
By PPC Land's calculation, the 1,486 sites amount to about 35% of the 4,289 Ghost sites in the sample.
Topic matters more than audience
Finance is the outlier. Its 824 publications charge a median of $29 a month, the highest of the 29 topics Magic Pages built, and 63% of them charge $20 or more. Its top quartile is $50. Business and crypto follow at a median of $10 each, with 27% of their publications charging $20 or more. In every other topic that share is 15% or less, and the other 26 topics have medians between $5 and $8.
At the bottom, five topics share a median of $5: comics, fiction, games, humor, and film, TV and entertainment. In comics, 62% of publications charge exactly $5. "What surprised us is how much more topic matters," Fedoruk-Betschki wrote in the blog post. In 20 of the 29 topics the top quartile is exactly $8.
Source: Magic Pages. Topic counts sum to 20,655, because a Substack publication counts in every category it lists (516 list more than one) and 729 Ghost sites match no topic.
Audience size, by contrast, barely moves the number. On Substack the median sits between $7 and $7.99 in every subscriber band, and the 61 publications showing tens of thousands of paid subscribers charge the same $7 median as the 11,310 that show no count at all. On Ghost the median is $5 in every band under 10,000 subscribers and $7 at 10,000 or more. Magic Pages keeps the two platforms apart here because the counts measure different things: Substack shows a rough count of paid subscribers from 100 up, which a publication can hide, while Ghost shows a rounded count of free and paid subscribers together, only where the publisher chooses to display it.
Source: Magic Pages. 16,579 Substack publications; 858 Ghost publications showing a subscriber count.
Frequency and activity
Posting more often goes with a slightly higher price. Publications whose typical gap between posts is a day or less, or several posts a week, have a median of $8; weekly ones $7; those posting every few weeks or less often $6. Activity was read on October 31, 2026, from each publication's 50 most recent posts, and Magic Pages could read the archive of 20,599 of the 20,868. The 17,565 that posted in the 90 days before that reading have a median of $7, while the 3,034 quieter ones have a median of $5. The number of authors makes no difference: the median is $7 whether one person or six or more wrote the latest 50 posts.
Source: Magic Pages, activity read October 31, 2026; prices from October 7, 2026.
Every one of these comparisons sets different publications side by side at a single moment. As the study notes, that cannot show what happens to one newsletter's price as it grows or posts more often.
Prices in euros and pounds
The dollar figures leave out a fifth of the market. Of 26,536 publications taking payments on October 7, 2026, 21% priced their lowest monthly plan in another currency. The euro median is €6 and the pound median £5.
Source: Magic Pages, all publications taking payments on October 7, 2026.
Yearly plans, trials and founding tiers
Almost every paid publication sells a year as well as a month. Of the 20,868, 98.1% offer a yearly plan, and 60.4% of those price it at exactly ten monthly payments, the familiar "two months free." The median yearly price is $69, and the median discount against twelve monthly payments is 16.7%. A small group, 1.5%, has switched monthly billing off entirely, so only the year is on sale.
Source: Magic Pages, 20,480 publications with a yearly plan.
Trials and premium tiers are common too: 81.7% of paid publications offer a free trial, a more expensive option or both. A free trial is offered by 55.1%. The platforms handle them differently. Substack offers a 7-day free trial at the paywall as one setting, switched on with a toggle labeled "Offer 7-day free trials in paywall," and 64.9% of Substack publications have it on. Ghost sets trials per tier, and only 17.3% of Ghost sites taking payments offer one on their cheapest tier.
The more expensive option takes a different form on each platform. On Substack it is the founding member plan, a yearly plan priced above the regular one: 79.0% of Substack publications have one, at a median of $200 a year, or 3.0 times their regular yearly price. The most common founding prices are $240, $150 and $100, against the $250 in Substack's checklist. On Ghost the equivalent is a second paid tier, which 37.8% of Ghost sites taking payments have, priced at a median 2.2 times the cheapest tier's yearly price. Most Ghost sites keep things simple: 2,666 of 4,289, or 62.2%, offer one monthly price; 973 offer two, 425 three and 225 four or more.
What the platforms keep
The fee comparison is where Magic Pages' stake is most direct, and the numbers deserve to be read with the method attached. The study takes one $7 monthly payment, the median price, on a US card, before any monthly plan cost and before tax, and applies each platform's list rates as published on October 7, 2026. Card processing and the platform's own cut take between 8% and 18% of that payment.
Source: Magic Pages, platform pricing and help pages read October 7, 2026. *Figures include Stripe's 0.7% Billing fee on recurring payments, which Substack's help page lists; Magic Pages says the other platforms run subscriptions through Stripe without mentioning the fee, so including it is the study's assumption. Without it, each starred figure rises by 0.7% of the price.
Processing costs about the same everywhere, according to the study. The difference lies in the cut: Ghost, beehiiv and Buttondown take none and charge a monthly fee instead, Memberful takes 4.9%, and Patreon and Substack take 10%. Stripe's Billing charge itself moved in 2024, when Stripe raised the fee from 0.5% to 0.7% of billing volume as it retired its Billing Starter plan.
A percentage cut and a flat fee trade places at a predictable point. Magic Pages puts it at ten times the monthly fee in paid revenue: its own Ghost hosting, at $14.50 a month, matches a 10% cut at $145 a month in revenue, or 21 subscribers at $7, while beehiiv's Lite plan at $49 matches it at $490 a month, or 70 subscribers at $7. beehiiv's plan costs more once a list passes 1,000 subscribers. PPC Land reported in October that beehiiv's Lite plan rose by $30 a month at 10,000 subscribers after its plan changes took effect on October 1, 2026. The study's table also leaves out costs that can be large: currency conversion, international cards, tax and purchases made inside Apple's app. That last one is not small. When Substack opened iOS in-app subscriptions in August 2025, Apple's commission was up to 30% in the first year, and in-app prices were raised by about 30% to cover it.
Patreon's figure assumes its standard plan for new creators. According to the Patreon pages the study cites, legacy creators may pay 5%, 8% or 11%, a 2.5% fee applies when a member pays in another currency, US payouts cost $0.25, and Apple takes 30% of purchases in the iOS app.
How many readers pay, and how long they stay
No public directory shows how many readers pay or how long they stay, and the study says as much. It fills the gap with figures from other publishers, each describing a different population. They are not comparable with each other, or with the price data.
Source: as cited in the Magic Pages study.
The gap between beehiiv's 0.62% median conversion rate and the 3% to 10% in Substack's older checklist is wide, and the two describe different things: one a measured median across a platform, the other guidance. beehiiv's $10 median price is also higher than the $7 Magic Pages found across Substack and Ghost, though again the populations differ. The churn range beehiiv reports, up to 16.67% a month in its money category, sits beside the finding that finance commands the highest prices in the Magic Pages sample.
