Britain's adults are more likely to reach their mid-20s without having experienced physical intimacy. According to the study, a digital shift in sexual behavior has increased drastically. It suggests that the share of women aged 16–24 who have never had a physical relationship has nearly doubled in little more than a decade. Young women are increasingly turning to gadgets for pleasure.
Key findings on Britain's sexual behavior
The report finds more adults have experienced no physical intimacy, alongside a decline in new partnerships. According to the Lancet report, 20% of people in the United Kingdom have no partners (ages 16–44).
For the first time, 44% of women aged 16–24 reported having no partner, compared with 39.6% having one partner. The share of women having no partner in earlier surveys was just 10-13%.
Same-sex behavior has reportedly jumped, according to the study. Same-sex intimacy rose from 6.8% to 14.0% for women and from 4.0% to 12.3% for men among ages 16-44.
Solo activity is also on the rise. Among 16-44 year olds, past-year solo activity rose from 41.4% to 58.7% for women and from 77.9% to 84.2% for men.
Physical intimacy has also become less frequent. Average frequency among 16–44-year-olds has halved since 1990. It was 4 times per month in 1990, to 2 times per month in 2022–24.
Same-sex relationships have significantly increased. Among 16–44-year-olds, the share reporting same-sex intimacy rose from 6.8% to 14.0% for women and from 4.0% to 12.3% for men. Same-sex experience, attraction, and LGB identity rose across all age groups.
The study underlines a digital shift where intimacy is increasingly becoming a part of online life rather than the real world.
Is there a solution to fix the UK's intimacy crisis?
The Lancet study raises concerns for the future about AI-generated adult content and AI companions. As AI expands, digital literacy and protection against online threats will become increasingly important. The findings have implications for public health, and they warn that health services and education will need to adapt.
How the US compares
A separate study on the US population offers a different snapshot. Published March 30, 2026, in the Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy, it used the World Health Organization's SHAPE questionnaire on intimate health and experiences. The online survey included 2,555 English-speaking US residents aged 18 to 94.
Results of this study on the US population showed high sexual wantedness at 89%, but the overall satisfaction rate was reportedly only 56%. "Gender differences emerged: women and gender-diverse participants reported lower pleasure, higher sexual violence exposure, and reduced safety in public spaces compared to men," said the study.
