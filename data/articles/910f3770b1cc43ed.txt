TITLE: Gaming vicar: Don't write off GTA 6 players
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Gaming vicar Rev Simon Archer won't be playing GTA VI but he feels that Christians should meet the moment and embrace those playing the game with a Christian message.
Rev Archer, Vicar of Arbory and Castletown on the Isle of Man, is a streamer as well as member of clergy, who is clear that he views video games as a mission field.
GTA VI is the latest release in the Grand Theft Auto series in 13 years, releasing on midnight in the UK on Thursday 19 November.
Christian influencers have come out to argue that Christians should not play the game, which has been rated PEGI 18, due to strong themes of violence, crime and sex which are expected to be in the game.
The Manx vicar thinks that we can have questions about engaging with the title but shouldn't dismiss the cultural power of the game and the opportunity to spread the gospel.
Rev Archer said: "We want to have discussions about it and what we don't want to do is immediately dismiss everybody who might be excited about it, including people in our congregations, other Christians, people who aren't Christians. We want to engage with it because we can be very quick to dismiss, and it's possible that we're just the sort of people maybe sometimes who would dismiss all kinds of gaming and particularly this title.
"I would have my questions around it, but as with all these sorts of games and anything that might be a little controversial, including films, books, or the movies that out there, and that might be something that will be good for a discussion and debate rather than just immediately discounting the games as dangerous or a problem.
"Having been a gamer and streamer and engaging with these online communities, we can have some really, really good discussions just by being present there."
It's been reported that the launch of the game could cost the UK economy £12m and that 83,000 people have booked annual leave, according to BrightHR.
It's estimated that predecessor game GTA V have generated more than $8.5bn (£6.4bn) since it was released and that the budget to develop the sixth edition of GTA has cost between $1.5-2bn (£1.5bn)
American Christian influencer Bryce Crawford said that he thinks Christians shouldn't play the game, asking why Christians would want to play the explicit game.
"I think there is more of a net negative to [playing] GTA," the social media star said. Rev Simon won't be playing GTA VI either, but says that he's played violent video games before.
"I won't be playing the GTA VI, I have drawn a line and that's kind of where I do draw the line. We can we can talk about the hypocrisy of games I've played in the past which have been games like Escape from Tarkov and some shooters which you might question.
"I'd say that those games were more about competitiveness than the actual violence in the games and it gave me an opportunity to play multiplayer online games where I'd be talking to other people.
"I do find it difficult for to kind of justify playing a game with which includes quite a lot of violence, but also just questionable actions within the game that I would have to ask: 'how is this forming me?' "
