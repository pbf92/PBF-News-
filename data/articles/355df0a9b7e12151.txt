Sales of "Grand Theft Auto VI" generated a record with $3.79 billion in first-day retail sales, maker Rockstar Games said Nov. 20, underscoring the enormous demand for the first new installment in the blockbuster series in 13 years.
The worldwide figure was based on company estimates through the end of the day Nov. 19, the day the game launched. Rockstar also said in a release that it sold 32.5 million units prerelease, setting a record across platforms.

Rockstar Games announces that "Grand Theft Auto VI" has generated a record $3.79 billion in first-day retail sales. The game launched Thursday, marking the first new installment in the series in 13 years. Rockstar reports selling 32.5 million units prerelease, setting a record across platforms. The launch far surpasses that of "GTA V," which made $1 billion in its first three days back in 2013. The latest installment returns to a fictionalized Miami, focusing on characters Lucia Caminos and Jason Duval. High diesel prices have put pressure on small U.S. trucking businesses. Many already were hurting from a yearslong slump marked by weak demand and depressed rates. This year, the U.S. war with Iran drove up one of their largest and least avoidable daily expenses. Some are selling trucks, revamping routes and trying anything they can to stay afloat. Small trucking companies have less room to absorb volatile fuel prices. Large trucking companies often buy diesel at negotiated discounts and have bigger reserves to cover unanticipated costs. Record turkey prices and higher fuel costs could make Thanksgiving dinner more expensive this year, according to a survey released Nov. 18. The American Farm Bureau Federation said the cost of an average Thanksgiving feast for 10 will rise 6% from last year to $58.63. The group tracks grocery prices using hundreds of volunteers in all 50 states. The Farm Bureau said it's the first time since 2022 that Americans will see the price of the meal increase. The group said higher diesel fuel prices have made it more expensive for farmers to process and transport Thanksgiving staples. Turkey is also seeing higher demand, which is raising prices.
