TITLE: Lina Ghotmeh: archaeology of the future
OUTLET: Aeon

The Lebanese architect Lina Ghotmeh is known for works that preserve memories of the past but are built for the future. These notably include her design for the Estonian National Museum (completed in 2016), which transformed a former Soviet airfield, and Stone Garden (completed in 2020), an apartment building in her home city of Beirut, whose facade openings, filled with greenery, evoke bullet scars and shelling. This short documentary from the London-based director Stephen Pook deconstructs Ghotmeh’s approach to architecture, revealing how her designs and thoughtful use of materials are born of a process in which history matters as much as the physical environment. In particular, the film focuses on her aesthetics and philosophy in the context of another work that demands deep sensitivity to the past and a vision for the future: her redevelopment of the Western Range at the British Museum in London, which constitutes about a third of the iconic museum’s total gallery space.
