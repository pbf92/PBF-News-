TITLE: Advertised product category
OUTLET: PPC Land

Advertised product category is a dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that labels each row of a report with the retail catalogue category of the product an ad promoted. If a campaign advertises a frying pan, a toaster and a bath towel, the field lets the spend, clicks and sales from that campaign be broken out by the kind of goods behind each ad rather than only by campaign or product code. It exists because Amazon's reporting is built around ASINs (Amazon Standard Identification Numbers, the retailer's product codes), and an advertiser with hundreds or thousands of them needs a coarser grouping to read results. The advertiser does not set the value; it comes from Amazon's catalogue.
Where the field sits
Amazon replaced its separate sponsored ads and programmatic report pages with unified reporting, which opened in beta on November 11, 2025 and reached general availability on June 8, 2026, when the system exited beta. At that launch the template gallery already included an Advertised Product report and a Converted Product report.
Amazon's dimensions reference, a Support Center page updated on November 11, 2026, lists 156 attributes in 11 categories. One of those categories is Product, with 18 attributes. Nine of them describe the advertised product: ID, name, parent ID, brand, category, subcategory, product group, marketplace and SKU (stock keeping unit, the seller's own product code). These apply to DVA+, Sponsored Display and Sponsored Products, but not to Sponsored Brands or Sponsored TV, with advertised product name carrying the Sponsored Products marker alone. DVA+ is Amazon's programmatic display, video and audio buying, sold as Amazon DSP (demand-side platform) until September 29, 2026.
The other nine Product attributes describe the converted product, the item that was actually bought, and these apply to all five ad products. The reference states that when a dimension does not apply to an ad product, its value appears as null. A Sponsored Brands row in a report that includes Advertised product category will therefore carry an empty cell, not a category.
How the value is populated
The field is derived, not entered. A campaign promotes one or more ASINs; each ASIN sits in Amazon's catalogue with a set of classification attributes; reporting looks up the category attached to the promoted ASIN and writes it onto the row. When a report is grouped by Advertised product category, the metrics for every ASIN in the same category are summed into a single line.
Amazon's catalogue has more than one way of classifying a product, which is where the field becomes harder to pin down. Shoppers see browse nodes, the navigation tree on the storefront. "Amazon uses Browse Nodes to divide products into different categories," according to Amalytix, an Amazon analytics vendor, and each node has a numeric ID that "describes a unique (sub)category". Separately, the product type governs which data fields a listing must fill in. Vendors setting up items in Vendor Central are also asked for "the product category, subcategory, and recommended browse category", according to the same source. Underneath sits a product group, often called the GL, a classification that a seller in an Amazon forum described as "a sub category that is not editable" and which appears as a "Product-Group" column in Amazon's fee preview report.
Unified reporting exposes three of these levels for the advertised product: category, subcategory and product group. Amazon's public reference does not set out how they nest, which taxonomy each is drawn from, or whether the category matches what a shopper sees in the storefront menu. PPC Land's explainer on the benchmark fields says only that "a separate unified reporting dimension describes the category of each advertised product itself", distinct from the browse category used to pick benchmark peers. As of November 2026, the precise source of the value is undocumented in Amazon's public help pages.
A worked example shows how the field changes a report. A kitchenware brand running Sponsored Products across 40 ASINs might see total spend of $10,000 and attributed sales of $40,000, a return on ad spend of 4.0. Grouped by Advertised product category, the same data could show cookware delivering $30,000 of sales on $6,000 of spend (5.0) and small appliances $10,000 on $4,000 (2.5).
Origin and evolution
Category reporting is older on the programmatic side than on the sponsored ads side. Amazon DSP's legacy Products report offered Product category, Product subcategory and Product group dimensions alongside ASIN, Parent ASIN and Brand name, according to the dimension lists published by the data connector companies Funnel and Adverity. Amazon's own self-service DSP reporting guide of August 2022 did not list category columns: its product dimension showed promoted ASIN, brand name, ASIN, parent ASIN and ASIN conversion type. The date on which DSP added the category fields has not been published.
Sponsored ads followed a narrower model. Funnel's documentation of the legacy Sponsored Products Advertised Product report lists campaign, ad group and ad identifiers plus "Advertised ASIN" and "Advertised SKU", and no category dimension. Sponsored Display's equivalent report was built the same way. Unified reporting therefore brought a field long familiar to DSP buyers into sponsored ads reporting under a new name. Amazon describes the change in general terms; its November 2025 beta notice said it had "simplified metric names and definitions across campaign types and ad formats".
Category-level analysis also appeared in measurement products. On April 14, 2026, Amazon announced that Omnichannel Metrics, its US measurement product for Amazon DSP, "produces category breakouts at both the study and campaign level", surfacing sales beyond Amazon for the most purchased product categories. That breakout is a study output, not the reporting dimension, but it reflects the same demand to see results by type of goods.
Why it matters
For sellers and vendors with broad catalogues, category is the natural unit of budget planning. A dimension that rolls ASIN-level results up to category lets a report answer the question those managers ask without a hand-built lookup table.
The field also matters for comparison across ad products. Because the same dimension is available for Sponsored Products, Sponsored Display and DVA+, a brand can, in principle, see how one category performed across search, display and programmatic channels in a single report. Before unification, combining DSP product reports with sponsored ads reports meant mapping ASINs to categories outside Amazon's tools. PPC Land's count of the reference notes that Sponsored Products carries 85 of the 156 dimensions, and the advertised product set is one of the places where sponsored ads reporting gained depth.
Pairing it with Converted product category exposes halo effects. An ad for a frying pan may lead to the purchase of a spatula from the same brand. Amazon's 2022 DSP guide explains that product reports can show brands different from the promoted one as "Brand Halo" when associated ASINs carry a different or unregistered brand. Comparing advertised and converted categories in one report shows how much of the attributed revenue came from goods other than those advertised.
Limitations and disputes
The definition is not published. Amazon's dimensions reference names the field, but its public pages consulted for this article give no definition beyond the label, no list of possible values and no statement of which catalogue taxonomy supplies them. Third-party connector documentation describes the DSP category dimension in generic terms, such as "the category to which a product belongs".
Catalogue errors carry through. The category comes from Amazon's classification of the ASIN, which can be wrong. In one seller forum thread, products that were office supplies had been labelled as books; Brand Registry Support corrected the classification on request and said changes would appear "within 24 to 48 hours". Until such a fix, every report grouped by category misfiles the spend.
Coverage is partial. The field returns null for Sponsored Brands and Sponsored TV. Sponsored Brands ads typically feature several products in one creative, which may explain the gap, though Amazon has not said so. Any cross-product category view therefore excludes two ad products.
Taxonomies differ by marketplace. Category trees are built per store, so the same ASIN can sit under different labels on amazon.com and amazon.de. Reports that combine marketplaces need the Advertised product marketplace field alongside category to avoid merging unlike rows.
Attribution remains Amazon's own. Category grouping does not change how sales are credited. The metrics beside the label are still attributed by Amazon to its own ads.
What it is not
Benchmark browse category path names the category Amazon used to select peer brands for a campaign's benchmark percentiles. It describes a comparison set, not the product advertised.
Converted product category labels the product that was purchased. It applies to all five ad products and can differ from the advertised category whenever a halo purchase occurs.
Advertised product subcategory and product group are sibling fields at different levels of classification. Category alone groups more coarsely than subcategory.
Category targeting in Sponsored Products and Sponsored Display is a setting that chooses where ads appear, based on browse categories of other products. It controls delivery; the reporting dimension only describes what was promoted.
Recent developments
Amazon's dimensions reference, updated on November 11, 2026, set out the advertised product fields and their product markers. On November 17, 2026, Amazon revised its data availability page, and PPC Land reported that daily unified reports are limited to 120 days per pull from 24 months of history, so long category trend analysis has to be run at monthly grain or stitched from several pulls. Legacy report pages are scheduled to become read-only on December 17, 2026 and close on January 31, 2027, when unmigrated reports will be lost.
