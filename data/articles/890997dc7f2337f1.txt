TITLE: Review Finds Severe Methodological Flaws in Chinese Medicine Single-Arm Clinical Trials
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive methodological scoping review examining clinical evaluations of traditional Chinese medicine has identified widespread vulnerabilities in how researchers formulate, justify, and report objective performance criteria for single-arm trials. Investigating the published literature across major international and Chinese biomedical databases from their inception through mid-December 2025, the research team found that single-arm trials in traditional Chinese medicine increasingly utilize external historical benchmarks rather than concurrent control groups to evaluate therapeutic efficacy and safety. However, an in-depth appraisal of all twelve eligible studies revealed pervasive shortcomings across key methodological dimensions, including a routine failure to quantify statistical uncertainty, inadequate verification of population comparability, absence of multidisciplinary expert validation, and frequent reliance on non-standardized composite endpoints. These critical evidentiary deficiencies substantially limit the interpretability of observed treatment outcomes, posing severe challenges to the credibility of regulatory decisions and clinical inferences derived from non-randomized traditional Chinese medicine investigations.
In clinical evaluations of traditional Chinese medicine modalities, the randomized controlled trial remains the undisputed gold standard for generating definitive evidence of therapeutic efficacy and clinical safety. Despite this status, the practical implementation of randomized controlled trials within traditional Chinese medicine frequently encounters formidable obstacles arising from the intrinsic nature of the interventions, pressing ethical constraints, and practical feasibility barriers. In particular, the core clinical philosophy of traditional Chinese medicine relies heavily on individualized pattern differentiation and flexible treatment regimens, a therapeutic paradigm that strongly resists rigid protocol standardization. Furthermore, in clinical scenarios involving severe, progressive, or refractory medical conditions, assigning participants to placebo control arms can become ethically untenable, while extended recruitment timelines often delay study completion. In response to these methodological challenges, clinical investigators have increasingly explored single-arm trials that measure therapeutic performance against a pre-specified objective performance criterion, commonly designated as a target value trial, offering shorter execution timelines and ethical acceptability without requiring concurrent control participants.
The methodological validity and evidentiary strength of any single-arm trial depend entirely on the scientific integrity of the pre-specified objective performance criterion chosen to represent the benchmark of therapeutic success. In clinical research methodology, an objective performance criterion is typically established using historical clinical data, such as published trial reports or disease registry records, established clinical practice guidelines, or formal consensus panels. This external value is intended to serve as a reliable mathematical estimate of the anticipated clinical outcome under standard medical care or natural disease history in the absence of the experimental therapy. Consequently, setting an objective performance criterion cannot be treated as a trivial administrative or technical routine; if a benchmark is calibrated either too optimistically high or too permissively low, it introduces substantial systemic bias into the final statistical analysis, potentially obscuring meaningful clinical benefits or exaggerating ineffective treatments regardless of how rigorously the subsequent clinical trial is executed.
To rigorously investigate prevailing practices and underlying methodological weaknesses in this domain, investigators conducted a systematic scoping review guided by the formal JBI scoping review methodology and reported in strict accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews guidelines. The research team searched six prominent medical and scientific databases—including PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, the China National Knowledge Infrastructure, the VIP database, and the Wanfang database—from database inception through December 14, 2025, without applying language restrictions. The systematic search strategy combined terminology for objective performance criteria, target values, performance goals, historical controls, external controls, and single-arm trials with traditional Chinese medicine terms, supplemented by exhaustive manual screening of reference lists from eligible trial publications and relevant systematic reviews. Studies were deemed eligible if they explicitly adopted a single-arm clinical trial design utilizing an objective performance criterion to evaluate traditional Chinese medicine interventions, including herbal decoctions, proprietary preparations, and acupuncture.
The multi-stage literature screening workflow, conducted using EndNote 21 for citation management and duplicate elimination alongside standardized Excel 2010 data extraction sheets, initially retrieved 413 records across the queried databases. After removing duplicate entries, reviewers screened 293 unique records by title and abstract, ultimately seeking the full text of 16 potentially relevant articles. Although one full-text report could not be retrieved despite exhaustive attempts, assessment of the remaining 15 reports resulted in 11 eligible trial reports, to which two additional publications were added via supplementary reference tracking. In total, the authors identified 13 publications representing 12 unique single-arm clinical trials that satisfied all inclusion criteria. Extracted study parameters included first author identity, publication year, publication medium, precise clinical conditions, specific therapeutic modalities, quantitative sample sizes, source literature underpinning the objective performance criterion, reported primary outcomes, expert validation procedures, confidence intervals, and explicit analytical assessments of reference population comparability.
All twelve included single-arm trials were conducted within mainland China and published between 2019 and 2025, collectively enrolling 1134 patient participants with individual study sample sizes ranging from 34 to 251 individuals. Four of the twelve studies, representing 33.3 percent of the evidence base, were organized as multicentre clinical investigations, whereas the remaining eight were single-centre projects. The clinical targets evaluated across these studies spanned diverse organ systems and medical specialties, including nephrology disorders such as refractory membranous nephropathy and idiopathic membranous nephropathy, neurological conditions including ischaemic stroke and post-stroke cognitive and motor dysfunction, gynecological disorders such as endometriosis, adenomyosis, and abnormal uterine bleeding in polycystic ovary syndrome, gastrointestinal conditions such as functional constipation and irritable bowel syndrome, refractory macular oedema secondary to retinal vein occlusion, and triple-negative breast cancer.
Regarding therapeutic interventions, the vast majority of evaluated trials investigated traditional Chinese herbal decoctions, which accounted for nine of the twelve studies, or 75.0 percent of the total sample. Two studies, representing 16.7 percent, evaluated commercially approved proprietary Chinese medicines, while a single study, comprising 8.3 percent, investigated an integrative protocol combining periocular acupuncture with structured physical rehabilitation training. With respect to publication dissemination channels, five studies appeared in recognized peer-reviewed Chinese-language professional journals, two studies were published in international English-language medical journals across three distinct publications that included one published trial protocol, and five studies were documented within academic master’s or doctoral dissertations from leading Chinese institutions specializing in traditional Chinese medicine. A temporal analysis demonstrated clear contemporary acceleration in adoption, with nine of the twelve studies, or 75.0 percent, appearing between 2022 and 2025, demonstrating growing enthusiasm for single-arm target value designs in traditional Chinese medicine research.
Despite this expanding clinical application, critical analysis revealed substantial methodological instability in the sources used to derive objective performance criteria. Exactly half of the twelve included studies derived their historical target values from prior clinical trials, comprising three randomized controlled trials, two prospective observational cohort studies, and one patient registry study. Five additional trials, representing 41.7 percent, derived their criteria from published systematic reviews and meta-analyses, while one study relied on an informal synthesis of clinical experience combined with selected medical literature. The clinical regimens serving as the benchmark references within these source documents varied widely, encompassing conventional Western pharmaceutical therapies such as rituximab, dienogest, and cyproterone acetate combined with ethinylestradiol, placebo control arms extracted from earlier clinical literature, and integrative protocols combining acupuncture or herbal medicine with standard pharmacotherapy.
Severe reporting deficiencies were particularly evident regarding the mathematical expression of uncertainty and the external validation of target criteria. Among the twelve analyzed trials, only three studies, or 25.0 percent, reported a 95 percent confidence interval for the established objective performance criterion, while the remaining nine studies adopted single point estimates without any mathematical representation of statistical dispersion. Furthermore, only one single trial, representing 8.3 percent of the corpus, explicitly reported that its objective performance criterion had been reviewed and confirmed through formal expert consensus discussions. Only four studies, or 33.3 percent, provided explicit methodological justification addressing demographic and clinical comparability between the enrolled trial cohort and the external historical reference population, and only two studies simultaneously reported both a 95 percent confidence interval and population comparability arguments, whereas seven studies reported neither feature.
When the scoping review authors critically scrutinized population comparability using source documents, ten of the twelve studies exhibited limited comparability with their historical reference populations, while the remaining two studies provided insufficient baseline reporting to enable any meaningful comparability assessment. The observed mismatches primarily involved fundamental discrepancies in underlying disease duration, baseline illness severity, clinical practice settings, or the scheduled timing of primary outcome assessments. In addition, outcome alignment exhibited critical irregularities: while ten trials aligned their objective performance criteria with pre-specified primary outcomes, one trial derived its criterion from a secondary endpoint, and another trial established distinct target criteria across multiple outcomes without clearly differentiating between primary and secondary endpoints. Among the eleven primary endpoints utilized across the ten aligned studies, four relied on non-standardized, investigator-defined composite efficacy rates, such as general clinical remission, rather than objective biological markers or validated clinical functional scales.
