TITLE: ASCI flags 1,563 Meta ads for violations, 41% of sponsored ads promoted illegal betting
OUTLET: Social Samosa

The Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) found 1,563 advertisements on Meta platforms in violation of its code between April and September 2026, with 90% of them being revenue-generating sponsored ads rather than posts from brand handles. Of these sponsored advertisements, 41% promoted prohibited categories, including offshore and illegal betting, according to ASCI’s Half-Yearly Complaints Report.
ASCI said these advertisements were reported to the relevant regulators and flagged to Meta for action. Despite the introduction of the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025, which prohibits online money games, its monitoring found that sponsored advertisements promoting offshore and illegal betting continued to be accessible to Indian consumers across digital platforms.
The report said digital platforms accounted for 97.93% of the 2,173 advertisements found in violation during the period. Meta accounted for 73.4% of digital media violations, followed by websites at 14.3%, property portals at 6.5%, Google at 4.3%, e-commerce at 1.3%, LinkedIn at 0.6%, OTT at 0.1% and mobile apps at 0.05%. Some advertisements appeared on multiple platforms.
ASCI said the findings highlight the need for digital platforms to take stricter and more proactive measures to identify, restrict and prevent the spread of such advertisements.
Advertisements referred to regulators
During the six-month period, ASCI referred 633 ads to government authorities for categories in which advertising is expressly prohibited by law. This included 590 betting and gambling advertisements referred to the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) and the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting (MIB), 28 ayurvedic product advertisements referred to the Ministry of AYUSH for violations of the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, six alcohol advertisements referred to State Excise authorities and four hospital and clinic advertisements referred to the National Medical Council. Five advertisements were referred under other categories.
Under a memorandum of understanding with Telangana RERA, 333 real estate advertisements were also referred to the Telangana Real Estate Regulatory Authority for violations of the RERA Act.
A further 136 advertisements that violated the ASCI Code and remained non-compliant were referred to regulatory authorities for examination. These included 126 cases referred to the MIB, 126 to the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA), 12 to the Food Safety and Standards Authority, eight to MahaRERA and one to the Ministry of AYUSH. Some cases were referred to multiple regulators.
Seven categories account for 90% of violations
Seven categories accounted for 90% of the advertisements found in violation. Offshore and illegal betting topped the list with 590 cases, followed by personal care with 456, real estate with 360, food and beverages with 312, electronics and consumer durables with 95, health care with 82 and education with 68.
Of the 590 offshore and illegal betting cases, 99% were identified through proactive monitoring, including 20 influencer promotions. Sponsored advertisements accounted for 98% of these cases, prompting ASCI to refer them to I4C and the MIB.
Personal care recorded the highest number of influencer-related violations, with 263 cases. The category also had the highest number of uncontested cases. ASCI said 75% of advertisements in the category were modified or withdrawn after its intervention, while 58% involved violations of influencer disclosure guidelines.
Other concerns included unsubstantiated skin and hair transformation claims relating to acne, pigmentation, hair fall and dandruff. Exaggerated before-and-after visuals and skin-tone transformation claims were also flagged. Environmental and product composition claims emerged as areas of concern, with laboratory findings contradicting some sustainability and product assertions.
In food and beverages, 79% of the 312 advertisements were not contested and were withdrawn or modified following ASCI’s intimation. Of these, 58% involved influencer disclosure violations. Nutraceutical products accounted for 74% of advertisements in the category, and 82% of those advertisements were not contested. ASCI also flagged therapeutic claims relating to disease risk reduction, cures, health improvements, rapid weight loss and product superiority that lacked adequate scientific substantiation.
In electronics and consumer durables, 79% of the 95 advertisements were flagged for influencer disclosure violations, largely in promotional campaigns accompanying new product launches, particularly mobile phones.
ASCI processes 3,951 complaints
Overall, ASCI looked into 3,951 complaints and scrutinised 2,308 advertisements for potential violations of its code during April-September 2026. Of the advertisements processed, 94% required some form of modification.
The report said 85% of violations were identified through proactive monitoring, while 98% of identified violations originated on digital media. It also found that 72% of advertisements misled consumers through dishonest claims and 30% promoted harmful products or unsafe situations. The same advertisement could violate multiple ASCI codes.
ASCI recorded an average resolution time of 15 days for formally investigated complaints, compared with 17 days in FY 2025-26. The share of cases that advertisers did not contest rose to 71% during the reporting period, from 61% in FY 2025-26. Such cases had an average closure time of eight days.
The overall voluntary compliance rate remained at 86%, the same as in FY 2025-26, compared with 83% in FY 2024-25 and 80% in FY 2023-24.
Influencer ad compliance reaches 95%
Of the 659 advertisements processed for influencer-related violations, 95% required modification. ASCI said 83.5% of influencers corrected their posts without contesting the complaints, making the changes within an average of six business days.
The overall compliance rate among influencers stood at 95% as of November 17, 2026, compared with 88% in the previous year. However, 3.6% of the advertisements involved the promotion of products prohibited or restricted by law. Twelve cases were investigated for both inadequate disclosure of material connections and misleading claims.
The report also noted that 84% of complaints received during the reporting period were submitted through ASCI’s TARA platform. The number of complaints from the general public increased from 1,124 in FY 2024-25 to 1,243 in FY 2025-26 and 1,347 in FY 2026-27.
ASCI said its findings point to continued consumer exposure to misleading claims and illegal betting promotions in digital advertising, and called for a shift from reactive corrections to preventive compliance.
