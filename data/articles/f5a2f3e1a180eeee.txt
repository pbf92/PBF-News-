TITLE: Woman alleges grooming and sexual abuse by Church of Ireland youth leader in civil action
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Warning: this article discusses distressing details of a sexual nature and mention of self-harm.
A woman who has taken civil action against her former church over allegations of grooming and sexual abuse by a Church of Ireland youth leader when she was a teen, said she feels failed by those she says should have protected her.
Jessica, not her real name, has spoken to BBC News NI alleging she was coerced into months of non-consensual sex with a "much older", married man when she was a member of a youth group in a Anglican Church of Ireland parish in County Antrim, Northern Ireland.
She said that she was first sexually assaullted when she was 17 years old, and that after she turned 18 years old, she became his "toy".
The Diocese of Connor, which Jessica's former church belongs to, told BBC News NI that it would be inappropriate to comment on the allegations due to legal proceedings.
"I believe he saw my vulnerability. He was witness to what was going on in my life and how I was struggling to cope with various things," she said.
"It's only been in hindsight that I've realised that actually that behaviour from him was in fact grooming, and that was him manipulating me and getting alongside me for the wrong intentions."
The alleged abuse happened about 20 years ago and Jessica didn't disclose the alleged abuse at the time. The youth leader informed the church in County Antrim that he had a consensual affair with Jessica, an admission Jessica says was made as he was under investigation for another issue which led to his standing down as a youth leader.
Jessica said that after confronting a senior member of the church years later, she was told that she had been "groomed and taken advantage of".
The Church of Ireland has defended itself against Jessica's claim that she was groomed in a civil court.
In reference to when the former youth leader first allegedly assaulted her, Jessica said: "He betrayed me spiritually, emotionally and physically. It was a crowded room full of people.
"We were just watching TV at that point and the room was quite dark so no-one else was able to see. And he put his hand down my trousers and inside my underwear.
"I just froze. I went numb. I think I potentially stopped breathing for a few seconds. I was just in utter shock and couldn't feel my legs. My whole system literally shut down.
"From then on I basically became his toy and he had such a hold over me that I just felt so stuck. Anything he said went."
Jessica said that she contemplated taking her own life and that the unnamed former youth leader threatened to take his own life if Jessica spoke out.
Jessica reported her allegations to Police Service Northern Ireland years later, but was told the Public Prosecution Service (PPS) had chosen not to prosecute after pursuing an allegation, due to the time which had passed between the alleged abuse and her report.
The PPS told the BBC that a "significant passage of time does not prevent the effective prosecution of sexual offences".
