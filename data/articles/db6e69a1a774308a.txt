TITLE: Stock Market Midday, Nov. 18: Nvidia Doubles Revenue, but Wall Street's Sell-Off Continues
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

Wall Street's recent sell-off continued on Wednesday, November 18, as another round of strong corporate earnings, headlined by Nvidia (NASDAQ:NVDA), failed to restore investor confidence. As of 11:32 a.m. ET, the S&P 500 (SNPINDEX:^GSPC) was down 0.48% to 7,305, while the Nasdaq Composite (NASDAQINDEX:^IXIC) edged 0.08% lower to 24,308. The Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJINDICES:^DJI) led the declines, falling 1.17% to 50,167 as weakness in healthcare and software stocks pushed the index closer to the psychologically important 50,000 level.
Selling pressure extended beyond equities, with gold tumbling 3.10% to $3,744.30. Meanwhile, the 10-year Treasury yield eased slightly to 5.10%, remaining near recent highs. Industrial and consumer discretionary stocks provided some pockets of strength, while communication services lagged. Despite encouraging corporate earnings, elevated valuations and persistently high borrowing costs continue to weigh on broader investor sentiment.
Today's biggest moves
Retail stocks provided a bright spot amid the broader market sell-off, with The TJX Companies (NYSE:TJX) surging 10.40% and Target (NYSE:TGT) climbing 5.57% following encouraging quarterly earnings and raised full-year guidance. The results offer some reassurance about the strength of consumer spending heading into the critical holiday shopping season.
Elsewhere, Take-Two Interactive (NASDAQ:TTWO) jumped 8.37% following positive reception for its highly anticipated Grand Theft Auto VI. Conversely, UnitedHealth Group (NYSE:UNH) plunged 7.14%, extending its recent losses and contributing significantly to the Dow's underperformance as investors grow increasingly concerned about rising medical costs and pressure on profit margins.
What this means for investors
Perhaps the most concerning development for investors is Wall Street's muted reaction to Nvidia's impressive quarterly earnings. Despite reporting year-over-year revenue growth of more than 100%, reaching $115.84 billion, Nvidia was trading 2.12% lower at midday. The negative reaction suggests that much of the anticipated growth in AI may already be reflected in stock prices, leaving technology companies increasingly vulnerable to valuation concerns.
Meanwhile, encouraging results from Target and TJX offer some reassurance that consumer spending remains resilient despite elevated borrowing costs. Investors will get another glimpse into consumer health when Walmart (NASDAQ:WMT) reports earnings on Thursday, providing further insight into household spending heading into the holiday shopping season.
