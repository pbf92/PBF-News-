TITLE: ‘GTA VI’: Adin Ross, Kai Cenat, and More React to First Time Playing Game
OUTLET: Yahoo Tech

The anticipation for Grand Theft Auto VI has swelled as popular streamers and first players are sharing clips from the game across various regions.
After over a decade of anticipation, the latest release from Rockstar Games first unlocked at midnight local time on Thursday, Nov. 19 in New Zealand. The game is now available to play across the U.S., Europe, Australia, Japan and a handful of other global territories.

Adin Ross, Kai Cenat, iShowSpeed and xQc were among the first big streamers streaming through New Zealand's early unlock.
During Speed's livestream, fans got a laugh out of the streamer making GTA VI character Jason take a shower and being joined by co-protagonist Lucia. "Don't stop the rain, girl," Speed sang during the steamy scene.
Ross and Kai also held interactive livestreams of their gameplay and took suggestions from their respective chats. xQc, however, spent most of his time watching TV in the game and having Jason renovate his home.

With the video game also having a fictional radio conversation between far-right personalities Nick Fuentes and Alex Jones, the latter gave GTA VI his stamp of approval with a tweet that read, "I like it a lot…"
Fuentes was less convinced and gave his reaction during a livestream, according to Newsweek. "People are saying this is me and Alex Jones… Is that supposed to be me? Doesn't really sound like me," he reportedly said.

Another scene from the game shows a dog randomly jet-skiing to Jagged Edge's 2001 hit "Where The Party At," a coveted anthem among parties in Miami, where the setting of GTA VI is loosely based.
Notable artists in pop, Latin music and rap have also been heard on GTA VI radio stations, like Miami native JT, formerly of City Girls. The game has a companion soundtrack, which features Cardi B, Travis Scott, Rauw Alejandro, Zara Larsson and more.
Gameplays even showed confrontations that you don't see everyday, like an elderly woman on a mobility scooter who holds up a man at gunpoint.
