Surge in support for Burnham’s handling of Brexit – YouGov poll
- A recent YouGov survey reveals that public approval of the government's handling of Brexit has risen to 30 per cent, a six-point increase in a week and its highest level since May 2022, following a new deal negotiated with the European Union.
- The landmark £17bn agreement secured by prime minister Andy Burnham will introduce a long-awaited youth mobility scheme and eliminate food and drink border checks to bolster the British economy.
- Public sentiment remains sharply divided, with 51 per cent of Remain voters now stating Brexit is being handled well, while 74 per cent of Leave voters believe the issue is being handled badly.
- Although Mr Burnham has initiated informal discussions regarding options for the UK's long-term relationship with the bloc, he has ruled out returning to the single market or customs union before the next election.
- Prominent political figures, including former prime ministers Rishi Sunak, Baroness Theresa May and Sir Tony Blair, have publicly warned against reopening the divisive debate surrounding Britain rejoining the EU.
