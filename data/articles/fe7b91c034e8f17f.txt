TITLE: Google puts Target and Albertsons grocery carts inside Search and Gemini
OUTLET: PPC Land

Google yesterday began rolling out Connected Apps for four grocery names - Albertsons, Safeway, Shaw's and Target - in Search and the Gemini app in the United States, so that a holiday shopping list can become a retailer's cart through a conversation with Google's AI. The same post, published on the company's News from Google blog, said Experian's credit tools are moving from Gemini into Search, that H&R Block can calculate taxes and estimate refunds from Search, and that Angi and Thumbtack accounts can be linked in Search within weeks. It runs to four paragraphs and contains no figures.
In Short
Google added grocery apps from Albertsons, Safeway, Shaw's and Target to Search and the Gemini app in the US, so its AI can fill a shopping cart from a plain request, and it named Experian, H&R Block, Angi and Thumbtack as further partners in Search. This matters to grocers and to the brands that advertise with them, because the moment a shopper picks products can now happen inside Google rather than on the retailer's own site or app. Holiday grocery orders can start inside Google as the rollout reaches US users, but Google has not said how products are chosen, who processes payment or whether ads will appear.
Four paragraphs, eight names
The post is dated November 20, 2026, filed under Search and headlined "Get your year-end tasks done with new Connected Apps." It carries no byline. Its register is domestic rather than technical: Thanksgiving groceries, appetizers for a holiday party, a leaky faucet and last-minute decorations are the use cases.
Grocery comes first. According to Google, the new Connected Apps are rolling out "through Search and the Gemini app" in the US to help with grocery runs, "including Albertsons, Safeway, Shaw's, and Target." The word "including" leaves open whether other grocers are part of the same rollout. Albertsons, Safeway and Shaw's are banners of one company, Albertsons Companies, and the post does not say whether they work as three separate apps or as one app with a choice of store.
What the apps do is stated in a single sentence: they help build an order and check out, according to the post. The next line names the plumbing, Google's Universal Commerce Protocol, the open standard through which AI agents find products and complete purchases with merchants. "Powered by the Universal Commerce Protocol, these apps can help you get more done this holiday season," Google wrote, linking the phrase to its January announcement of the standard.
Finance follows. "Experian is already live on the Gemini app, and now we're rolling it out to Search with the ability to help U.S. users check their credit rating and shop for a personal loan," the post states. Tax gets one sentence of its own: "U.S. users can also turn to H&R Block to calculate their taxes and estimate their refunds right from Search."
Home services close the post, and they are the only items described as still to come. In the coming weeks, according to Google, people will be able to link their Angi and Thumbtack accounts in Search to find local professionals, for jobs ranging from a leaky faucet to decorations put up before guests arrive.
The partners at a glance
How the grocery flow works in Google's example
Google published an example alongside the post, built around a Target order in AI Mode and split into two panels labeled "Explore ideas on Search" and "Follow-up to add items to cart." According to that example, the flow has five steps:
- Request. In the AI Mode tab of Google Search, a user describes a need and names the retailer: "I have friends staying over this weekend and need to prep quick, easy brunch for the whole group tomorrow. what are some specific items from Target I can stock up on."
- Suggestions. AI Mode answers with a plan built on "a mix of heat-and-serve savory mains, bakery pastries, fresh fruit, and batch drinks from Target," followed by product cards grouped by category. The first card under "Bakery & Pastries" is Favorite Day Petite Butter Croissants at $6.49, sold by Target and rated 4.8 stars from 32 reviews, with "Prep" and "Details" notes beneath it.
- Invocation. A follow-up prompt begins with "@Target" and reads: "add two bagels and cream cheese, strawberries, croissants, cold brew and juice to my cart for pickup."
- Confirmation. AI Mode replies that it has added two Thomas Everything Bagels, two Philadelphia Original Cream Cheese Spread, Fresh Strawberries, Butter Croissants, Good & Gather Cold Brew Coffee and Tropicana Pure Premium Pulp Free Orange Juice for pickup at a store named Nashville West, in Nashville, Tennessee.
- Cart. A Target panel inside the answer shows a sub-total of $37.49 for "8 items total," with line items and quantities: Thomas Everything Bagels in an 18oz six-count pack at $3.99 each, quantity two, for $7.98; and Philadelphia Original Cream Cheese Spread in 8oz tubs at $4.19 each, quantity two, for $8.38.
The example ends at the cart. It shows no sign-in, no payment screen, no substitution choice and no pickup slot, so the step the post describes as checking out "with ease" is not depicted.
Two details in it deserve attention. The request asked for "two bagels and cream cheese"; the cart holds two six-count packs, or 12 bagels, and two tubs of cream cheese, and nothing in the example shows the user confirming those quantities before the items went in. And two of the six products, the Favorite Day croissants and the Good & Gather cold brew, are Target's own labels. How AI Mode chooses between a retailer's private brands and national ones is not explained.
The "@Target" prompt matters for a different reason. In Google's documentation, typing "@" followed by an app's name is how an app is called up in Gemini, while the Search help page, as PPC Land reported on October 31, said users name the app in a request or pick it from a list, and are offered the chance to connect it first if they have not. That reporting also found Connected Apps in Search limited to AI Mode, to the US, to English and to signed-in users, with an app linked once. Google's example today shows the "@" invocation working inside Search as well.
From Instacart to the grocers themselves
Grocery is where Connected Apps in Search began. On July 16, Google brought connected apps into AI Mode with Instacart, Canva and YouTube Music, with AI Mode assembling the cart and the purchase completed in Instacart's app or website. By late October, the Search help page listed five apps - Instacart, Canva, YouTube Music, Yahoo Fantasy Football and Sleeper Fantasy Football - and said users might be sent to the partner's app to finish a task.
As of late October, then, the only grocery app listed for Search was a marketplace that fills orders from many chains. The four names in today's post are retailers with apps of their own. Instacart, meanwhile, is changing hands: DoorDash agreed on October 29 to buy it for about $17 billion, in a deal expected to close as late as 2028.
The protocol under the new grocery apps has a longer history. Google launched the Universal Commerce Protocol on January 11, co-developed with Shopify, Etsy, Wayfair, Target and Walmart, and the same day Target and Walmart announced checkout inside Gemini and AI Mode. Adoption of this form of agentic commerce has been uneven. In May, a scan of more than 3 million sites by Originality.ai found only 26 with a public UCP file, none of them on the primary domains of the co-developers. On October 15, Google's Universal Cart reached US Search and the Gemini app, a cart that spans retailers and hands checkout to Google Pay or the merchant's site through the protocol.
Walmart is absent from the list. It co-developed the protocol and announced Gemini checkout in January, yet it is not among today's grocery names, and the post does not explain why. Walmart has meanwhile been building out its own assistant: on October 30, it added holiday features to Sparky, including carts built from photos of handwritten shopping lists.
The developer route was set out three weeks ago. When Google opened the Connected Apps Platform preview on October 28, it said developers integrate through a cloud-hosted MCP server or Android's AppFunctions APIs, register and test their apps in a Google Connected Console, submit them for review, and choose whether each one goes live in Search, the Gemini app or both. Google said that day it had launched more than 50 Connected Apps across the two products. Today's post does not say which route the grocers used.
Credit, taxes and a plumber
Experian's move into Search had been promised. Experian was one of 14 connected apps Google began rolling out in Gemini on September 23, and the October 28 post named it alongside Adobe, Localiza and Peloton as Gemini partners due to reach Search within weeks. Its Search rollout began today, 23 days later by PPC Land's count. Adobe, Localiza and Peloton are not mentioned today.
Credit and lending are sensitive ground for a search engine. The post describes two functions, checking a credit rating and shopping for a personal loan, and stops there. Which lenders appear, how loan offers are ordered and what credit information passes between Experian and Google are not covered.
H&R Block's entry is narrower. It is described only in Search, and only for calculating taxes and estimating refunds; filing a return is not mentioned, nor is the Gemini app.
Angi and Thumbtack both match homeowners with local contractors, and both are described in terms of account linking. That puts them on pages where Google sells its own product for the same trades: Local Services Ads, the pay-per-lead format used by plumbers, electricians and other service businesses. The post does not say how an Angi or Thumbtack result will sit alongside those ads. Amazon has courted the same partner. In December 2025, it said Angi would join Alexa+ during 2026, together with Expedia, Yelp and Square.
What it means for retail media
Grocers have spent years building advertising businesses on their own sites and apps. A retail media network sells brands sponsored placements where shoppers browse and buy, and its value rests on those shoppers turning up. Target reported advertising revenue of $296 million for its third quarter on November 18, up 23%, a line that understates its Roundel network because some of what brands pay is booked as lower costs rather than revenue. Albertsons Media Collective, the network that covers Albertsons, Safeway, Shaw's and the company's other banners, joined Google's Commerce Media Suite on April 27, routing shopper data from more than 2,200 stores into YouTube and Display & Video 360.
Connected Apps change who stands at the shelf. In Google's example, AI Mode picked the products, and the cart put two Target labels next to Thomas, Philadelphia and Tropicana. Who sells the eye-level slot when an AI fills the basket? Google's Connected Apps policy, last updated September 28 according to the same October 31 reporting, bars partners from serving third-party advertising or promotional material through their integrations. Whether that rule keeps a grocer's sponsored products out of a cart built in AI Mode is a question the post does not touch.
Conversion is the other unknown. Walmart said in March that in-chat checkout through ChatGPT converted three times lower than click-out links. A June survey of 2,080 UK adults by ACI Worldwide and YouGov found that 60% would drop an AI shopping agent after one mistake. A grocery order, with its sizes, quantities and substitutions, offers plenty of room for small errors, and the bagels in Google's own example show how much rides on interpretation.
Google's holiday pitch has also shifted in a year. In November 2025, it introduced agentic checkout with Google Pay and Let Google Call, which phones local stores on a shopper's behalf.
