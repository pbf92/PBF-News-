DraftKings Short Interest Rises as Prediction Markets Grow
According to CNBC, DraftKings shares have fallen more than 60% since September 2025, as trading volume on prediction markets grew with the popularity of sports-related event contracts. Short interest in DraftKings rose 17% since the 2026 NFL season began, as of Nov. 16, according to S3 Partners estimates. Just under 10.8% of the shares available to trade, or 44.4 million shares, were sold short. The stock fell more than 20% on Nov. 6 after its latest earnings report. S3 director of research Sam Pierson said short sellers added 1.8 million shares after DraftKings cut its profit outlook to fund its prediction-markets push, and continued adding shares with the stock at a 52-week low.
