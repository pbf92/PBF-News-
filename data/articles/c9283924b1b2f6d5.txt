TITLE: KERV.ai, Macmillan Publishers and Chomps are among this year’s Work/Life Awards finalists
OUTLET: Modern Retail

This year’s Work/Life Awards finalists reflect a landscape where companies are rethinking workplace culture as a driver of business performance, employee engagement and innovation. Across industries, organizations are giving employees a more active role in shaping workplace policies and priorities, building recognition and connection into the everyday employee experience and extending their missions through community involvement and social impact.
KERV.ai earned a finalist spot in the Most Innovative Culture category for making employee input and cross-functional collaboration central to its workplace culture. Its quarterly HR listening tours, culture committee and real-time feedback programs gave employees opportunities to influence company policies and engagement initiatives. The emphasis on employee voice also extended to the business. Employees across product, engineering, client services and creative identified an opportunity to improve contextual targeting, leading to the development of Moment Match Engine, a video technology solution built through cross-departmental collaboration.
Macmillan Publishers is nominated in the Most Committed to Social Good category for connecting its workplace culture to a broader mission of expanding access to books, literacy and intellectual freedom. Through its MacImpact corporate social responsibility program, the publisher supports employee volunteering, charitable giving and community partnerships. In 2025, employees took part in 27 volunteer events and contributed 494 volunteer hours, a 60% increase from the previous year. Its employee-led Yours to Read Committee also advocates for access to diverse literature and the freedom to read.
Chomps, a finalist for Most Committed to Employee Appreciation, focused on maintaining employee connection as its remote-first workforce grew to more than 200 people across 30-plus states. The company brings employees together through annual retreats, departmental offsites and company-wide gatherings, while recognition programs such as Chompian of the Quarter and Chompian of the Year celebrate employee contributions. Investments in wellness, mental health days and professional development further extend appreciation beyond formal recognition.
Together, this year’s nominees represent a workplace landscape in which culture is shaped by employee participation, meaningful recognition and a commitment to a broader purpose beyond business goals. As expectations of employers continue to evolve, their approaches offer examples of how organizations can build more connected, engaged and purpose-driven workplaces.
