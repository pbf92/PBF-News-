TITLE: Ideal Transfers for Mauricio Pochettino and Manchester United in the 2027 Winter Window
OUTLET: Bleacher Report

Mauricio Pochettino's first transfer window at Manchester United simply needs to save the club from Premier League relegation.
The Red Devils are on a fast-track to the Championship with one win in their opening 10 matches. January's window needs to be about immediate improvement with players who are good enough to come in and do a job, while maintaining value for a long-term future at the club.
It's going to cost serious money to do such business mid-season.
Here's a selection of players Poch should look at as he takes his first sip from the most obvious poisoned chalice in sports...
Alex Freeman, Villarreal
1 of 6
Arguably Man United's most pressing need is an immediate replacement for Diogo Dalot. Someone who can come in and understand Poch's style without too much transition.
Hell, someone who can just do the basics without playing that Dalot-styled inside pass that keeps setting up chances for the opposition.
Poch's time with the USMNT ensures he will be linked to numerous American stars. Alex Freeman should be the most intriguing. The Villarreal full-back has the type of qualities United seem allergic to at the back; he's athletic, quick, and very good on the ball. He can also play on either side.
The 22-year-old is much-admired by Poch, who praised his humility, ability to listen and noted he can be "one of the best" players in the world if he continues the way he's going. That's legitimate admiration and shouldn't be taken lightly.
Lewis Hall, Newcastle United
2 of 6
Now, an "ideal" transfer for Man United would be Newcastle's Lewis Hall. Just like it would have been in the summer, before he signed a new contract at St. James' Park. "Ideal" doesn't mean "likely".
He is the perfect profile of left-back for a Champions League-level club, although the Magpies' bad start to the campaign and his commitment has cooled reports that he might move on. At 22 years old he could have at least a decade of first-team football in him.
Hall's attacking instincts have contributed to Newcastle's best moments this season. Three assists and a goal across 18 matches are the kind of numbers Luke Shaw will struggle to hit by the end of May. Hall also plays with an aggression that constantly pushes the team forward, even if Newcastle have struggled to capitalise on it.
However, considering Man United's position, relatively poor finances and Poch's history, expect to see Fulham's Antonee Robinson mentioned a lot more instead.
Jarrad Branthwaite, Everton
3 of 6
It says a lot about Man United's current squad that so many potential signings are just players who have a strong, physical profile.
Poch's defensive options are bumbling. He'll quickly need to replenish the entire backline, but at least one centreback needs to come in during the winter window.
Everton's Jarrad Branthwaite is a player United have coveted for a long time, even placing a £50 million bid for him two years ago. Since then, the 24-year-old has had to deal with recurring injury issues, missing 31 games last season.
Although he's also sat out of five so far this campaign, he's been in excellent form across the nine Premier League appearances he has made.
Links to Fulham's Calvin Bassey are underwhelming. He's a good player, but United shouldn't be targeting players who could move to a mid-table Premier League side without people batting an eyelid. More ambition needs to be shown - and that means spending.
Morgan Gibbs-White, Nottingham Forest
4 of 6
Bruno Fernandes' time at Old Trafford is coming to an end. Two contract rejections and record-breaking form has gone out the window for a player who just looks fed up to play for the club these days. That's fine - he deserves more than to scrap it out in a relegation dogfight.
That means Poch needs to identify an all-action midfielder who can create and contribute across the entire pitch.
One of his first signings at Tottenham was Dele Alli, a player whose tremendous quality lifted Spurs to the Champions League final before a fall from grace that now has him more prominently watched as a streamer than an active footballer. Poch got magic out of him, though.
Morgan Gibbs-White should be considered the ideal pick-up, although Nottingham Forest owner Evangelos Marinakis would likely make things very difficult, just like he did when Spurs were interested last year.
MGW isn't playing particularly well in a Forest side that have only scored nine goals in as many games, but he's still contributed to four of those with three assists and a goal for himself.
At 26 years old he should be involved in battles to qualify for the Champions League, not stay in the Premier League (not that Man United can help with that right now).
Eli Junior Kroupi, Bournemouth
5 of 6
There was a time when Man United competed for the best young talent in the world. Imagine if Wayne Rooney was coming through as a 16-year-old right now. There's no chance in hell they'd get him.
That realisation should be a wake up call if the club ever wants to get back to acting like the elite entity that dominated for so long.
Winning the race for Eli Junior Kroupi would be considered a huge coup. The versatile Bournemouth forward continues to be linked with Arsenal, Barcelona and others. He likely would have moved in the summer for huge money if he didn't get injured.
The 13 goals he scored in his debut Premier League season is the most by any teenager in history, beating Robbie Fowler.
He has serious pedigree that even a completely stacked French national team recognises. The fact he can play anywhere across the frontline and No. 10 is going to make him an important addition for any side that eventually splashes the estimated £100 million fee to get him away from the Vitality Stadium.
Man United of old would do it…
Vangelis Pavlidis, Benfica
6 of 6
Benjamin Sesko's injury issues have played a sizeable part in United's struggles this season.
Not having an alternative to Bryan Mbeumo means Michael Carrick entered most matches without an opportunity to mix things up or change the style of play. Although Joshua Zirkzee has performed well, he's heading towards the exit as soon as United land extra firepower.
Benfica's Vangelis Pavlidis should be on the club's radar. The Greek striker has remained prolific for years, scoring consistently across the Dutch league, Portuguese league, European competitions and on the international stage. His seven goals in the Champions League two seasons ago included a hat-trick against Barcelona.
Pavlidis is a great interlinker of play, a trait that would work well with Bruno, Mbeumo and Matheus Cunha buzzing around him. He'd allow each to get closer to goal, something Carrick struggled with in Sesko's absence. Most importantly, Pavlidis is just an excellent finisher who should be gettable.
United shouldn't settle for Folarin Balogun, who continues to be linked, just because of his association with Poch. They already have one striker who could play for Everton in Zirkzee. The ambition needs to be higher.
