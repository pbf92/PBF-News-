TITLE: Mesenchymal Stem Cells Improve Cardiac Function After Acute Myocardial Infarction in Trials
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and meta-analysis of twelve clinical trials indicates that mesenchymal stem cell transplantation improves cardiac function without increasing the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events in patients with acute myocardial infarction. The pooled investigation, evaluated as low-to-moderate-certainty evidence, demonstrated measurable improvements across both primary and secondary outcome measures when comparing mesenchymal stem cell therapy directly against control groups. Specifically, the application of mesenchymal stem cells was manifested through an increase in LVEF alongside decreases in LVEDV and LVESV, providing systematic clinical evidence regarding the comprehensive effects and safety profile of this cellular intervention following an acute myocardial infarction event.
Acute myocardial infarction represents a very common type of cardiovascular disease that seriously endangers patients’ lives and health, significantly increases the incidence of the disease, and imposes a heavy burden on the social economy. Despite the widespread clinical impact of the condition, clinical application protocols for administering mesenchymal stem cells in individuals with acute myocardial infarction have not yet been established. To address this clinical gap, a research team conducted a systematic meta-analysis designed to evaluate the comprehensive effects of mesenchymal stem cell treatment in individuals diagnosed with acute myocardial infarction, focusing on therapeutic efficacy and safety parameters.
To assemble the evidence base, the investigators conducted a comprehensive systematic search across four biomedical databases: PubMed, Web of Science, EMBASE, and the Cochrane Library. The search timeframe covered the literature from the establishment of each respective database through January 2026. The identified literature was subsequently screened strictly according to the PICOS principle, which encompasses Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome, and Study design. Through this structured screening process, twelve clinical trials meeting the inclusion criteria were identified and selected for extraction and combined quantitative evaluation.
Following the selection of the eligible trials, the researchers carried out a quality evaluation to assess the risk of bias across each included study. Data regarding outcome indicators were then extracted from each individual study for combined statistical analysis, with the outcomes of concern focused specifically on cardiac function and adverse events. The statistical synthesis of the extracted data was performed using Review Manager 5.4 software. In addition, the overall quality of the accumulated evidence was evaluated using the GRADE method to determine the certainty of the findings.
The combined analysis of the included trials demonstrated measurable improvements across both primary and secondary outcome measures in patients receiving mesenchymal stem cell transplantation. In detailed subgroup analyses, mesenchymal stem cell transplantation in patients with acute myocardial infarction was shown to significantly increase LVEF compared with the control group at less than 6 months, at 6 months, and at 12 months. Furthermore, the subgroup findings revealed that the therapy significantly decreased LVEDV and LVESV at 6 months, and significantly reduced the WMSI at 12 months compared to control conditions.
Subgroup analyses also evaluated the route of administration, demonstrating that both the intracoronary and the intravenous routes favored improvements in LVEF for patients undergoing treatment. Alongside these functional improvements, the meta-analysis established that mesenchymal stem cell therapy did not increase the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events when compared with control groups. The absence of an increased risk of major adverse cardiovascular events indicates that the observed enhancements in cardiac function were achieved without introducing heightened cardiovascular safety risks for patients treated with mesenchymal stem cells.
In their overall conclusion, the researchers reported that low-to-moderate-certainty evidence suggests that the application of mesenchymal stem cells in the treatment of acute myocardial infarction is both effective and safe. This therapeutic benefit was specifically manifested as an increase in LVEF, together with a decrease in LVEDV and LVESV. Although the subgroup analysis results provide certain references for the rational clinical selection of mesenchymal stem cell treatment, the authors emphasized that more standardized research is still needed to confirm these findings and establish definitive clinical protocols.
