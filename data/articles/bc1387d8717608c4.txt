TITLE: Biyuanshu Oral Liquid May Relieve Symptoms in Chronic Rhinosinusitis Meta-Analysis
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis published in Frontiers in Pharmacology indicates that Biyuanshu oral liquid, a standardized Chinese herbal formula, may confer clinical benefits as an adjunctive treatment for patients diagnosed with chronic rhinosinusitis. Conducted by researchers from the Sixth People’s Hospital of Longgang District and Zhejiang Provincial People’s Hospital, the synthesis evaluated nineteen randomized controlled trials encompassing 2,861 participants across healthcare centers in China. The pooled statistical analyses demonstrated that the addition of Biyuanshu oral liquid to standard therapeutic regimens produced marked improvements across primary subjective complaints, including nasal obstruction, rhinorrhea, and persistent headache, while concurrently increasing the clinician-reported overall effective rate. Furthermore, exploratory assessments revealed statistically significant reductions in radiographic sinus opacification and improvements in validated disease-specific quality of life scores. Nevertheless, the investigators emphasized that the certainty of the current evidence base ranges between moderate and very low under the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation framework, primarily driven by substantial statistical heterogeneity, small study cohorts, and incomplete methodological reporting regarding allocation concealment and investigator blinding.
Chronic rhinosinusitis represents a prevalent, highly heterogeneous inflammatory disorder affecting the mucosal linings of the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses, posing a profound global healthcare burden and significantly diminishing daily quality of life. The condition is clinically characterized by cardinal symptoms including persistent nasal congestion, purulent rhinorrhea, facial pain or heaviness, chronic headaches, and a diminished sense of smell. Epidemiological estimates from international systematic investigations indicate that the global prevalence of chronic rhinosinusitis stands at approximately 8.71 percent, with historical observational data documenting an upward trend across recent decades. Beyond localized nasal morbidity, the disease frequently induces severe sleep disruption, reduced professional productivity, emotional exhaustion, and repetitive utilization of outpatient healthcare services. Current international consensus guidelines recommend a multimodal therapeutic foundation centered on regular nasal saline irrigation, topical intranasal corticosteroids, judicious courses of systemic antibiotics or oral glucocorticoids for acute exacerbations, functional endoscopic sinus surgery for refractory anatomical disease, and monoclonal biologics targeting type 2 inflammatory pathways in severe cases with nasal polyps. However, high costs, potential side effects, postoperative recurrence, and incomplete responses to standard pharmacological agents sustain an active search for safe and effective adjunctive therapies.
Biyuanshu oral liquid is a standardized, sugar-free proprietary Chinese medicinal preparation manufactured in ten-milliliter vials and widely prescribed in Chinese clinical practice for inflammatory upper respiratory conditions, rhinitis, and sinusitis. The commercial formulation comprises thirteen botanical and fungal ingredients: Xanthium strumarium, Magnolia biondii, Mentha canadensis, Angelica dahurica, Scutellaria baicalensis, Gardenia jasminoides, Bupleurum chinense, Asarum heterotropoides, Ligusticum chuanxiong, Astragalus mongholicus, Clematis armandi, Platycodon grandiflorus, and Wolfiporia cocos. Pharmacological investigations of the constituent botanical extracts indicate that specific plant metabolites, such as the flavonoid baicalin from Scutellaria baicalensis, suppress inflammatory signaling via nuclear factor kappa B pathways, reduce inflammatory cell infiltration, and help balance regulatory T cell and helper T cell activity. Additional active compounds, including geniposide from Gardenia jasminoides and volatile oils from Angelicae Dahuricae Radix, have shown analgesic, immunomodulatory, and anti-inflammatory properties in preclinical experimental models by downregulating tumor necrosis factor alpha, cyclooxygenase-2, and interleukin-6. Moreover, laboratory evaluations have observed that Biyuanshu oral liquid inhibits the formation of Pseudomonas aeruginosa bacterial biofilms in vitro, offering a plausible biological mechanism for alleviating recalcitrant mucosal colonization, though the preparation demonstrated limited efficacy against established mature biofilms.
To rigorously evaluate the clinical efficacy and tolerability of the oral liquid, the research team designed a systematic review protocol registered in the PROSPERO international prospective register of systematic reviews under identifier CRD420261440773, adhering strictly to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines and the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions. The investigators executed comprehensive literature searches across nine Chinese and English electronic databases from inception through June 30, 2026, including PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, the Cochrane Library, the China National Knowledge Infrastructure, the Wanfang Database, the VIP Chinese Science and Technology Journal Database, and the China Biology Medicine database. Eligible trials were restricted to prospective randomized controlled designs examining patients diagnosed with chronic rhinosinusitis who received Biyuanshu oral liquid as monotherapy or adjunctive therapy compared against control groups receiving conventional pharmacological therapies, placebos, or standard medical care alone. Observational cohorts, single-arm series, animal experiments, laboratory assays, and trials utilizing non-liquid dosage forms were systematically excluded. Dual independent reviewers conducted study selection and data extraction, resolving discrepancies through consensus or third-party arbitration.
The structured database search initially identified 542 records, which were subsequently refined to 49 unique full-text articles after duplicate removal and preliminary title and abstract screening. Following rigorous full-text appraisal, thirty candidate articles were excluded due to missing control groups, non-qualifying formulations, or absent outcome measurements, yielding nineteen randomized controlled trials published between 2012 and 2023 for quantitative meta-analysis. In aggregate, the nineteen included trials enrolled 2,861 patients, comprising 1,431 individuals assigned to Biyuanshu experimental groups and 1,430 assigned to comparator control groups. Sixteen studies evaluated adult patient cohorts, one study evaluated pediatric patients, and two studies investigated mixed populations of adults and children. Active intervention courses ranged from two weeks up to twelve weeks, with the typical therapeutic regimen consisting of ten milliliters of Biyuanshu oral liquid administered three times daily, though select studies administered twice-daily doses. Control regimens predominantly reflected conventional clinical interventions, featuring macrolide antibiotics such as clarithromycin and roxithromycin, second-generation cephalosporins like cefixime, amoxicillin combined with clavulanate or metronidazole, topical intranasal budesonide, and auxiliary decongestant or mucolytic agents.
Quantitative synthesis of primary nasal symptoms revealed statistically significant benefits favoring Biyuanshu oral liquid across every evaluated manifestation. Nasal congestion scores, reported across seven randomized trials involving 625 participants, showed a pronounced reduction in the treatment arm compared to controls, yielding a standardized mean difference of minus 2.47 with a 95 percent confidence interval from minus 3.84 to minus 1.10. Rhinorrhea symptom scores, also derived from seven clinical trials with 625 patients, demonstrated significant relief in the Biyuanshu cohorts, exhibiting a standardized mean difference of minus 1.69 and a 95 percent confidence interval spanning minus 2.42 to minus 0.97. Similarly, headache scores pooled across seven studies involving 625 patients confirmed an advantageous symptom reduction, yielding a standardized mean difference of minus 2.26 with a 95 percent confidence interval from minus 3.65 to minus 0.87. However, the researchers noted extreme statistical heterogeneity across all three symptom categories, with inconsistency indices exceeding ninety percent. Sequential leave-one-out sensitivity analyses confirmed that the direction of effect remained robust, though an isolated sensitivity analysis for rhinorrhea revealed that removing a 2015 study by Dai and Ma dramatically reduced heterogeneity to 25.0 percent while preserving significant clinical efficacy.
Analysis of secondary clinical endpoints demonstrated consistent therapeutic advantages associated with Biyuanshu oral liquid administration. The overall effective rate, assessed across seventeen randomized trials comprising 2,589 patients, was significantly superior in patients receiving Biyuanshu oral liquid compared to controls, achieving a pooled risk ratio of 1.18 with a narrow 95 percent confidence interval from 1.13 to 1.22 and low between-study heterogeneity at 24.5 percent. Objective radiological evaluation utilizing the Lund-Mackay computed tomography scoring system, examined in four trials involving 408 participants, demonstrated an average reduction of 1.70 points in the Biyuanshu cohort, with a 95 percent confidence interval between minus 2.34 and minus 1.05. Patient-reported disease-specific quality of life, measured via the Sino-Nasal Outcome Test-20 across two clinical trials enrolling 286 individuals, exhibited a statistically meaningful mean difference of minus 4.50 points favoring Biyuanshu with a 95 percent confidence interval from minus 5.83 to minus 3.17. Begg’s rank correlation test and Peters’s regression test for small-study effects on the overall effective rate yielded p-values of 0.091 and 0.161 respectively, showing no statistically clear proof of publication bias among the larger comparative trials.
To investigate underlying sources of variability across the evidence base, the meta-analysts performed exploratory subgroup analyses stratified by patient demographics, treatment duration, and intervention strategy. When examined across age groups, Biyuanshu oral liquid yielded comparable improvements in the overall effective rate among adult patients with a risk ratio of 1.17, mixed populations with a risk ratio of 1.23, and pediatric cohorts with a risk ratio of 1.16, with between-group difference testing showing no statistical significance. Stratification by treatment length indicated that therapeutic courses exceeding fifteen days achieved an overall effective rate risk ratio of 1.18, which did not differ significantly from courses lasting fifteen days or fewer, which demonstrated a risk ratio of 1.21. Regarding intervention design, combining Biyuanshu oral liquid with conventional Western medicine yielded a statistically significant overall effective rate risk ratio of 1.21 with a 95 percent confidence interval from 1.16 to 1.26 across seventeen trials, whereas Biyuanshu monotherapy evaluated across two studies showed a non-significant risk ratio of 1.11 spanning 0.93 to 1.31. Although combination regimens produced numerically superior response rates, formal between-subgroup testing failed to achieve statistical significance, leaving the comparative superiority of combination regimens over monotherapy unestablished.
