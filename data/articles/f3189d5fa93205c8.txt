Man City charges meeting interrupted as Premier League send 'furious messages'
The Premier League interrupted a Digital, Culture, Media and Sport Committee meeting in Westminster where the Manchester City case was discussed
Premier League bosses interrupted a Commons select committee hearing to deny claims they have spent £500million in legal fees on the Manchester City case. The charges had been discussed as MPs questioned Independent Football Regulator (IFR) chair David Kogan and chief executive Richard Monks.
City were charged in February 2023 with 115 alleged breaches of financial rules spanning nine seasons between 2009 and 2018, with the club accused of inflating sponsorship deals and concealing payments to players and managers.
Following a private hearing in late 2024, an independent commission published its verdict on September 29, finding City guilty on 114 of the 115 charges. It was ruled the club used "sham" contracts to inflate revenues by more than £900m. City have strenuously denied all wrongdoing and lodged a comprehensive appeal on October 1.
The Premier League's legal costs incurred in this case were quoted at a meeting on Wednesday at around £500m. But Digital, Culture, Media and Sport Committee chair Dame Caroline Dinenage was forced to step in, saying that she had received "furious messages" denying that figure.
Over an hour into the session, held in Portcullis House, Westminster, Kogan was discussing the Premier League-EFL backstop when he was stopped by Dame Caroline.

MPs had also been grilling Kogan and Monks on whether the IFR - only formed in 2025 - could force City's owners to sell the club and how the watchdog would fund a massive court battle if sued.
She said: "Can I just intervene and just say that the Premier League is desperate for me to say that they haven't spent £500m fighting the Man City case.
"I'm saying it because I am getting furious messages from the Premier League to tell you that. I would like to see them bring such diligence to the issue of funding so we wouldn't need a backstop."
The claim was made earlier in the meeting. Kogan replied in jest: "I have to say, I didn't realise this was an interactive session. Are they criticising anything that I've said?" Dame Caroline responded: "To be honest, I haven't engaged with them."
Reports suggest that the figure incurred by the Premier League in this specific case could be up to £50m. This has not yet been confirmed, though City could be forced to repay the actual amount depending on the outcome of their appeal.
The appeal hearing is set to go ahead mid-way through next month and could take several days. The findings from that appeal and whether City's guilty verdict will be upheld are expected to be handed down in the New Year.
A decision on sanctions will then be made if the appeal proves unsuccessful. The Premier League rulebook gives the independent commission the power to impose whatever sanction is deemed appropriate, from points deductions to outright expulsion from the league.
When lodging their appeal, City stated the commission's ruling contained "clear material errors of law, principle and fact." It is not a re-trial and City must prove legal or procedural errors.
City also have the right to lodge a separate appeal against whatever sanctions they might face.
