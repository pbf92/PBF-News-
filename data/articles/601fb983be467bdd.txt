TITLE: Wegovy lowered BMI in children ages 6 to 11—but vomiting was common and weight rose after treatment stopped
OUTLET: Medical Daily

Children ages 6 to 11 with obesity who took weekly semaglutide, the drug sold as Wegovy, lowered their body mass index (BMI) by an average of 13.5% over 68 weeks, while BMI rose 2.7% among children on placebo. The phase 3 STEP Young results were published Nov. 16 in The New England Journal of Medicine and presented at ObesityWeek 2026.
The trial also showed the trade-offs and how quickly some gains can fade. Seventy percent of children on semaglutide had vomiting, compared with 29% on placebo, and BMI rose after treatment stopped.
Wegovy is approved in the United States only for people 12 and older. For parents of a young child with severe obesity, the results add solid data to a hard decision, but they do not make the drug an approved option.
Results Across Two Years
The trial enrolled 167 children, with 112 assigned to semaglutide and 55 to placebo. Their average age was 10, and 59% were girls, Healio reported. More than 85% had class II or III obesity at the start. Both groups also received lifestyle counseling.
The 13.5% figure counts every child, whether or not they kept taking the drug. When Novo Nordisk calculated results assuming children stayed on treatment, BMI fell 17.4%, the figure the company highlighted in its STEP Young results announcement.
Counting all children, 38.4% on semaglutide were no longer classified as having obesity at 68 weeks, compared with 2.1% on placebo. About three in four lowered their BMI by at least 10%.
The benefit narrowed over time. At two years, BMI was down 9.4% with semaglutide and up 8.4% with placebo, so the gap between groups held. During a seven-week break after treatment ended, BMI rose in the semaglutide group, and longer off-drug data are not yet available.
The drug also improved several health markers. Waist size, triglycerides, blood pressure and a marker of inflammation all fell more on semaglutide than on placebo. Lead author Dr. Ania Jastreboff of the Yale School of Medicine said treatment "was accompanied by improvements in multiple cardiometabolic measures."
Side Effects, Growth and Puberty
Nearly every child reported some side effect, 96% on semaglutide and 93% on placebo. Stomach problems were most common. Besides vomiting, 62% of children on semaglutide had diarrhea, and 58% had nausea, compared with 35% and 24% on placebo. Most were mild to moderate and occurred more often as doses were increased.
Few children stopped because of side effects: 4% on semaglutide and 2% on placebo. Serious adverse events occurred in 9% of each group, and psychiatric side effects were similar between groups.
Growth was a central concern, since these children are still developing. Researchers saw no effects on puberty, pubertal development, or bone mineral density, said Dr. Antje Körner of the University of Leipzig, a trial investigator. Height was slightly greater in the semaglutide group. Wegovy carries a boxed warning about thyroid tumors and should not be used by people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer.
MedicalDaily Evidence Check: STEP Young was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 167 children published in NEJM. It found substantial BMI reductions over 68 weeks and two years. It did not show what happens over many years or after the drug is stopped long term. Novo Nordisk funded the trial.
Expert Views and Guideline Context
Dr. Daniel Hsia, division chief of pediatric endocrinology at Emory University and Children's Healthcare of Atlanta, who was not involved in the trial, said the results could change practice. "This supports a paradigm shift moving away from watchful waiting to earlier treatment of obesity," he told Healio. He also said key questions remain, including which children benefit most and what happens beyond two years. Hsia reported advising Eli Lilly and serving as a researcher for Lilly and Novo Nordisk.
Guidelines differ. The American Academy of Pediatrics' 2023 guideline recommends offering weight-loss medication to adolescents 12 and older, and Healio noted it allows clinicians to consider medication as young as 8 alongside lifestyle treatment. World Health Organization guidance issued Oct. 7 advises against obesity medication for children 9 and younger and says approved medicines may be considered for those 10 to 19 only after a supervised lifestyle program has not worked, Becker's Hospital Review reported.
Novo Nordisk's announcement did not say when the company might seek FDA approval for younger children. The two-year results were supportive findings that were not adjusted for multiple comparisons, and the company's headline figures should be read cautiously, according to Contemporary Pediatrics.
Questions Parents Can Bring to the Pediatrician
About one in five U.S. children and teens has obesity. Families of younger children with severe obesity, or with prediabetes, high blood pressure, or sleep apnea, may be most interested in these findings.
A doctor can prescribe Wegovy off-label to a child under 12, but insurers rarely cover drugs outside approved uses, and brand-name GLP-1 drugs can cost hundreds of dollars a month without coverage. Parents can ask the pediatrician whether a child qualifies for a pediatric weight management program, how growth and nutrition would be monitored, and what the plan would be if the drug were stopped.
Families should avoid compounded or online versions sold without a prescription from a licensed clinician. Children already on a GLP-1 drug should not stop without medical advice. Vomiting that will not stop, severe stomach pain, or signs of dehydration, such as very little urine or unusual sleepiness, need prompt medical care.
The next milestones are longer off-treatment data and any FDA filing. MedicalDaily will report on both.
Key Questions Answered
What did the STEP Young trial find? Children 6 to 11 on semaglutide lowered BMI 13.5% over 68 weeks, while BMI rose 2.7% on placebo.
Is Wegovy approved for children under 12? No. In the United States, it is approved only for people 12 and older.
What side effects did children have? Mostly stomach problems. Vomiting affected 70%, diarrhea 62%, and nausea 58% of children on semaglutide.
Did it affect growth or puberty? Investigators reported no effects on puberty or bone density during the trial.
What happened after the drug stopped? BMI rose during a seven-week break. Longer follow-up data are not yet available.
Who funded the trial? Novo Nordisk, which makes Wegovy.
