Bitcoin Outperforms as Nasdaq Falls 2% Ahead of Nvidia Earnings: Analyst Buys the Dip
Bitcoin outperformed at the open while the Nasdaq fell more than 2% ahead of Nvidia's earnings, according to Kitco commentary published on November 16, 2026. The commentary argued that with sentiment so negative, the best risk-to-reward setup was to the upside.
The author noted having flagged red dots on Bitcoin's momentum wave for weeks, first on the one-week chart and then on the two-week chart, and said he would not fight that momentum or buy Bitcoin as a short-term trade until it showed real strength. A sharp decline followed, with Bitcoin falling to about $62,000 on Sunday, its lowest level since July. Roughly $2 billion in leveraged positions were liquidated, mostly longs, and Bitcoin closed the week down about 14%. It was down more than 20% from its level on November 4 before bouncing back to around $64,500. A red dot also appeared on the monthly chart, a significant timeframe for that signal.
At the open, bulls and bears fought over the Nasdaq while Bitcoin held its bounce. The Nasdaq then appeared to break down, reaching a low that was down 2.15% on the day, which the author identified as the point where he bought the dip. He reasoned that the Nasdaq eventually attracts dip buyers and that tech stocks rarely decline three days in a row, entering the position as a day trade or short-term swing that was currently green.
The author pointed to the chart pattern after the large selloff candle on November 5, when the Nasdaq bounced and then pulled back again. If it does not make a new low below that candle, that may have marked the bottom, and the index was sitting in a lull. He also cited a large amount of negative sentiment already priced in, noting that Nvidia reports earnings the next day in its first report without Jensen Huang as CEO, that Anthropic's IPO is expected during the week, that Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is speaking, and that Treasury yields remain above 5%. With that much fear present, he argued that any positive news could spark a bounce.
The author said he closed his remaining altcoin shorts, noting that many altcoins made new lows over the weekend, some at all-time lows, and that he was looking for a bounce there as well. He also bought the dip heavily in gold and silver, with gold trading around $3,860 and silver around $46.50 that morning. While crypto could keep going lower, he expected gold and silver to find a bottom, possibly over the weekend.
With Thanksgiving the following week in the United States and Wall Street winding down for the holidays, the author said he did not expect extreme moves in either direction, since crypto follows stocks. His bias was to the long side of stocks, but he expected mostly sideways chop. If Nvidia's earnings cause a price dip, he expected it to be bought up rather quickly, which could offer an opportunity for those without exposure. He described sideways chop as a good accumulation period and reiterated that it was a good time to dollar cost average, meaning buying a fixed amount on a regular schedule regardless of price, which he said makes sense at these levels for the long term.
On the short term, the author said the Nasdaq, Bitcoin and gold may have already put in their local bottoms, and he was prepared to go long. His short-term thesis was bullish, though he expected mostly sideways chop likely until Christmas, and he was buying at the lower end of the range. Because the future is uncertain, he said he is heavier in gold and silver, believing they carry less downside risk, while Bitcoin, the Nasdaq and the S&P 500 are either quick long trades or candidates for dollar cost averaging.
