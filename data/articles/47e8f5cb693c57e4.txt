TITLE: Content language
OUTLET: PPC Land

What the field is
Content language is a reporting dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that labels each row of programmatic delivery data with the language of the media surrounding the ad. If a DVA+ campaign served an impression inside a Spanish-language news app, a German recipe site or a French-dubbed streaming episode, the field is meant to carry that language, so that impressions, clicks, cost and conversions can be split by the language of the environment rather than by the language of the creative or the shopper's browser.
The field exists because language and geography do not line up. A US campaign reaches large Spanish-speaking audiences, and a campaign in Belgium or Switzerland touches three or four languages inside one country. Country fields cannot show which of those audiences an ad reached.
Amazon placed the field in the Supply category of the unified reporting dimensions reference its Support Center published on November 11, 2026. PPC Land's count of the 156 unified report dimensions, published on November 12, 2026, lists it among seven supply fields that have no former name, a group covering "supply type, sellers, publishers and content language." The field is new to Amazon's console reports rather than a renamed legacy column. Neither source quoted here reproduces Amazon's exact definition.
Where the language comes from
The field sits downstream of the bid request. In open-market programmatic trading, a publisher or its supply-side platform (SSP) sends a bid request to demand-side platforms (DSPs) under the OpenRTB protocol maintained by IAB Tech Lab. A website request carries a Site object and an app request carries an App object; each can contain a Content object describing the material being consumed, such as an article or a video episode.
Within that object, the OpenRTB 2.6 specification defines two language fields. The first, language, holds the "content language using ISO-639-1-alpha-2", the two-letter codes such as "en", "es" or "de". The second, langb, holds the content language as an Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) BCP 47 tag, a richer format that can add a region or script, for example "pt-BR" for Brazilian Portuguese or "zh-Hant" for Chinese in traditional characters. The specification says only one of the two "should be present." The Device object carries a parallel pair for the browser language, and wlang and wlangb list languages the publisher accepts in creatives.
The distinction matters for Content language. A reader in Madrid with a phone set to English who opens a Spanish newspaper generates a request whose device language is "en" and whose content language is "es". A dimension built on the Content object reports Spanish; one built on the Device object reports English.
Amazon has not published which source populates its field. Three routes are plausible and are used across the industry: the value the publisher declares in the bid request, a value the SSP or exchange infers by scanning the page, or a value assigned by Amazon's own contextual classification. Google Ad Manager, for comparison, states in its help documentation that "our technology can determine the primary language of a page," rather than relying on a declared tag. Adobe Advertising's DSP offers a comparable report column, "Language (as per ISO 639)," defined as "the language of the content using ISO-639-1-alpha-2," which follows the OpenRTB format directly.
How it appears in reports
Content language is an attribute, not a metric. In unified reporting an advertiser chooses dimensions, then metrics such as impressions, clicks, total cost, detail page views, purchases and return on ad spend (ROAS). Adding Content language splits each campaign's totals into one row per language value.
Consider a hypothetical US DVA+ campaign delivering 10 million impressions. Grouped by Country, it shows one row. Grouped by Content language, it might show 8.6 million impressions in English content, 1.2 million in Spanish and 200,000 with no value. With an English-only creative, the Spanish rows mark spend where the message may have landed poorly, or an audience large enough to justify a Spanish version.
The field works best with siblings: with Ad language, it shows whether creative and content matched; with Ad group language targeting, whether a setting held in delivery; with Site or app, Publisher name and Supply source, which properties and exchanges supplied each language.
Which products carry it
The field belongs to programmatic supply. PPC Land's analysis found that the Supply category holds 35 attributes, of which 34 carry the DVA+ marker, while only two apply to Sponsored Products and one each to Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display. DVA+ is the AI-led campaign type that merges the Amazon DSP, Sponsored Display and Sponsored TV buying experiences; it remains in a US closed beta, with general availability planned in 35 markets in the first quarter of 2027. The article does not mark Content language per product, but the totals leave little room for sponsored ads, which run mainly on Amazon's store in a known marketplace language. Amazon's rule for dimensions that do not apply to a product is that "its value appears as null."
On the buy side, the users are advertisers, agencies and reporting tools pulling DVA+ data through the console or the Reporting API. On the sell side, the language signal originates with publishers and SSPs, and with Amazon itself for owned inventory such as Prime Video, Twitch and Fire TV, where Amazon knows the catalogue metadata of what it streams.
Supermetrics and Funnel list no content-language field in their Amazon DSP references as of November 2026.
Origin and evolution
Language has been part of the programmatic bid request for more than a decade. The Content and Device language fields date from the OpenRTB 2.x line, which IAB Tech Lab developed through the 2010s. PPC Land's explainer on language targeting records that OpenRTB 2.5, released in 2016, added wlang and a language field on the creative, and that OpenRTB 2.6, released in 2022, added the BCP 47 variants langb and wlangb. Those BCP 47 fields addressed a known weakness: two-letter codes cannot separate European from Brazilian Portuguese or Simplified from Traditional Chinese, distinctions that matter to advertisers buying by market.
Amazon's reporting moved more slowly. The legacy Amazon DSP reports exposed content metadata piecemeal; in 2025 Amazon expanded content transparency reporting to show content type, rating and title for Twitch impressions delivered on or after June 16, 2025, without a language attribute. Amazon opened the unified reporting beta in November 2025 and reached general availability on June 8, 2026, as PPC Land reported in its coverage of unified reporting exiting beta. The November 11, 2026 dimensions reference is the first public Amazon document found that names Content language as a report field.
Why it matters for marketers
Language is a targeting decision that many platforms are now taking out of advertisers' hands. Google announced on August 14, 2026 that it would remove the campaign-level language setting from Search and Performance Max campaigns starting in September, PPC Land reported, with Ginny Marvin, Google's Ads Product Liaison, writing that "the campaign-level setting has become redundant." As systems infer language automatically, a reporting field that shows where ads actually ran becomes the remaining check.
It also bears on brand suitability, since classification tools that read page text perform unevenly across languages, and on connected TV (CTV), where it is one of few signals of which linguistic audience a programme reached.
Limitations and disputes
The largest gap is provenance. Amazon has not said whether the value is declared by publishers, inferred by SSPs or assigned by Amazon, and the three routes produce different error patterns. Publisher declarations can be missing or wrong; an English template around a Hindi article may be tagged "en". Automated detection struggles with short, image-heavy or mixed-language pages.
Video is harder still. A streaming title can carry several audio tracks and subtitle languages, and a single "content language" value cannot say which one the viewer chose. OpenRTB offers no standard way to express that.
Format adds friction. If Amazon reports two-letter ISO codes, regional variants collapse into one row; if it reports BCP 47 tags, the same language can split across "es", "es-US" and "es-MX". Amazon has not published a value list. Rows without a value are likely, and how large that unknown share runs will vary by exchange.
Finally, the field is reporting only. A Content language result cannot be acted on directly; changes have to go through targeting settings, inclusion and exclusion lists, or creative.
How it differs from similar fields
Ad language is the language of the creative itself, a DVA+-only field in the Ad category. Content language describes the surroundings, so the two can differ for the same impression. Ad group language targeting is the language setting configured on the ad group, formerly the DSP line item; it records intent, while Content language records delivery. Device or browser language, the OpenRTB device.language signal, describes the user's settings rather than the material on screen, and has no separate unified reporting field. Country and Region describe where the impression was served, which is not the same as the language consumed there.
Recent developments
Content language arrived with the November 11, 2026 dimensions reference, which PPC Land analysed on November 12. The same week Amazon moved its final legacy reporting shutdown: legacy reports become read-only on December 17, 2026 and unmigrated reports will be deleted on January 31, 2027. From January 1, 2027, display impressions on Amazon Store placements will count only once on screen, and Amazon expects about 63% fewer impressions on average, changing the base against which any language split is read. DVA+ is due for broader release in early 2027.
