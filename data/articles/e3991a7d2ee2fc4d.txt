TITLE: Evidence Gaps Persist in Myelofibrosis Care, Systematic Review Shows
OUTLET: The American Journal of Managed Care

Evidence Gaps Persist in Myelofibrosis Care, Systematic Review Shows
Key Takeaways
- JAK inhibitor evidence in lower-risk symptomatic disease largely came from uncontrolled ruxolitinib cohorts, showing heterogeneous spleen and symptom responses with frequent treatment-emergent anemia.
- In anemic myelofibrosis, pacritinib and momelotinib improved 24-week transfusion independence versus comparators, without significant overall survival differences in randomized trials.
SHOW MORE
A systematic review of 43 studies supporting new myelofibrosis practice guidelines found low- or very low-certainty evidence for nearly all outcomes.
Nearly every outcome examined in the evidence base for the American Society of Hematology (ASH) 2026 guidelines on myelofibrosis was supported by low- or very low-certainty data, according to a systematic review published in Blood Advances.1 Across 43 studies covering 10 clinical questions, Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors were linked to better spleen, symptom, and transfusion outcomes, but the review did not find a clearly demonstrated survival benefit with these agents.
The analysis, led by the Mayo Clinic Evidence-Based Practice Center alongside members of the ASH guideline panel, informed the 10 recommendations the society released in October.1,2 The authors also reported that early allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation (HCT) carried more deaths in the first year than a delayed approach but was associated with better survival over the long term.
Unmet Need and Practice Variation in Myelofibrosis
Myelofibrosis is a rare myeloproliferative neoplasm in which the bone marrow develops scar tissue, and ASH estimates that it affects up to 6 per 100,000 people.2 Patients can experience an enlarged spleen, low blood counts, and debilitating constitutional symptoms, and they face a risk of progression to acute myeloid leukemia.1,2
HCT is the only therapy with curative potential, whereas JAK inhibitors are the backbone of nontransplant care.1 Four JAK inhibitors are approved, including momelotinib (Ojjaara; GSK), which the FDA approved in 2023 for intermediate- or high-risk myelofibrosis with anemia regardless of prior therapy.1,3
For the review, a medical librarian searched MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane databases, and Scopus for English-language studies from 2000 through January 31, 2025. Of 3007 records identified, 347 underwent full-text review, and 43 unique studies were included. Two reviewers independently screened each study, random-effects meta-analyses were run when data allowed, and each outcome was rated using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation approach. ASH funded the work.
JAK Inhibitor Outcomes in Lower-Risk and Anemic Myelofibrosis
Evidence for symptomatic lower-risk disease came from 7 single-arm observational studies of 1005 patients treated with ruxolitinib (Jakafi; Incyte), none of which had an untreated control group. Spleen response at roughly 6 months ranged from 26.8% to 63.8%, and 21% to 80% of patients had a total symptom score reduction of at least 50%. Anemia of any grade was reported in 35.6% to 79% of patients.
In patients whose disease was marked by anemia, 2 randomized trials favored a JAK inhibitor for transfusion independence at 24 weeks, with rates of 37% for pacritinib (Vonjo; Sobi) vs 7% for best available therapy and 30% for momelotinib vs 20% for danazol. Neither trial showed a significant difference in overall survival. In a separate open-label trial of 156 patients with ruxolitinib-associated anemia, 43% of those who switched to momelotinib reached transfusion independence at 24 weeks compared with 21% of those on best available therapy, although at least 35% spleen volume reduction was uncommon in both arms (7% vs 6%).
Extended genetic testing also carried prognostic weight. Pooled data from 15 observational cohorts linked ASXL1, EZH2, SRSF2, and IDH1/2 mutations individually to significantly shorter overall survival, with HRs from 1.50 to 4.5, whereas DNMT3A mutations showed no significant link. By contrast, data on starting a JAK inhibitor in patients with high-risk disease but no symptoms were limited to 19 patients in 2 retrospective series.
Transplant Timing and Pretransplant JAK Inhibitor Exposure
Three retrospective studies of 2265 patients with intermediate-2 or high-risk disease compared early with delayed HCT. In the largest cohort (n = 1928), survival at 1 year was 61% with transplant vs 79% without (relative risk [RR], 0.75; 95% CI, 0.67-0.85), but the advantage shifted to transplant at 5 years (RR, 1.39; 95% CI, 1.12-1.73) and 10 years (RR, 2.39; 95% CI, 1.79-3.19). “However, the decision of when to counsel a patient to proceed with HCT remains difficult,” the authors wrote, adding that transplant risk models such as the Myelofibrosis Transplant Scoring System are still evolving.
Across 3 studies of 500 transplant recipients, prior JAK inhibitor use showed no significant association with 1-year mortality in pooled analysis (RR, 0.55; 95% CI, 0.24-1.30), nor with acute or chronic graft-vs-host disease or graft failure. For supportive care, no study directly tested aspirin in myelofibrosis, so the review drew on indirect data from polycythemia vera and essential thrombocythemia, and iron chelation data came from 4 observational studies.
The authors identified 2 main limitations: an evidence base that was both thin and low in certainty, and the need to borrow indirect data from patients with polycythemia vera and essential thrombocythemia. Most included studies were observational or lacked a comparator, and end point definitions varied across them. The authors also cautioned that investigational therapies now in clinical testing could shift the treatment landscape quickly, which may require updating both the review and the guideline recommendations.
“The certainty of evidence was very low or low across nearly all outcomes, reflecting the rarity of the disease and the predominance of observational data, and underscoring the need for prospective comparative studies to address current clinical equipoise,” the authors concluded.
