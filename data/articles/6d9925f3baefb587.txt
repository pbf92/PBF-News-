TITLE: Motor Control Exercise Benefits for Low Back Pain Lack Robust Evidence
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and meta-analysis evaluating exercise therapies for adults with non-specific chronic low back pain has found that observed neuromuscular advantages favoring motor control exercises over general strength training are limited by very low certainty evidence and sensitive analytical models. While both exercise modalities are frequently prescribed to manage persistent back symptoms and are recognized for improving pain and disability, their comparative influence on underlying lumbopelvic neuromuscular mechanisms has remained an unresolved clinical question.
To assess how these exercise regimens compare in altering physiological characteristics, researchers investigated four primary domains: muscle morphology, neuromuscular coordination, movement performance, and spinal alignment and posture. The review team registered their methodology prospectively in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews, known as PROSPERO, on January 18, 2025, under registration code CRD42025631927. The investigation was subsequently published on November 28, 2026, in the open-access journal BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders.
The authors conducted systematic literature searches across three major biomedical databases: PubMed, CINAHL, and SPORTDiscus, spanning from each database inception through February 2026. The search protocol specifically targeted randomized controlled trials involving adult participants diagnosed with non-specific chronic low back pain who underwent interventions lasting at least four weeks. To ensure methodological rigor, two reviewers independently handled the screening of retrieved studies, extracted relevant quantitative data, appraised potential risk of bias using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool, and evaluated the certainty of evidence across outcomes using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework.
From an initial retrieval of 5,603 records, the authors identified 18 reports derived from 10 distinct randomized controlled trials that fulfilled all specified eligibility requirements. Across these trials, the researchers cataloged 39 separate outcome measures examining lumbopelvic properties.
In the primary random-effects meta-analyses, pooled estimates demonstrated statistically significant differences favoring motor control exercise in two specific assessments: transversus abdominis activation capacity, which demonstrated a mean difference of 3.41 millimeters of mercury, and trunk flexor endurance, which showed a mean difference of 8.83 seconds. Despite these initial findings, the apparent advantages disappeared under sensitivity testing. When the investigators applied Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman confidence interval adjustments, neither transversus abdominis activation nor trunk flexor endurance retained statistical significance. Furthermore, no statistically significant between-group differences emerged for any of the other meta-analyzed outcomes.
Beyond the quantitative pooling, the researchers conducted a narrative synthesis of measures that could not be meta-analyzed. This qualitative synthesis identified several individual between-group differences favoring motor control exercise in selected metrics concerning muscle morphology, movement control, muscular strength, and postural control. Nevertheless, the investigators emphasized that the majority of these isolated neuromuscular advantages were reported within single, uncorroborated trial reports rather than replicated across multiple independent cohorts.
The certainty of evidence was ultimately graded as very low across all assessed outcome measures. The evaluation identified substantial limitations, including high risk of bias within individual studies, inconsistency between reports, indirectness of outcome measures, and imprecision in effect estimates. While within-group improvements were frequently documented for both motor control exercises and general strength training across multiple neuromuscular domains, the authors concluded that existing data fail to demonstrate robust between-group superiority, highlighting an urgent need for high-quality trials to establish clinical relevance.
