TITLE: Anthropic stock falls below IPO price; CEO sells $280 million in shares
OUTLET: Yahoo Finance

Anthropic (ANT) stock plunged roughly 13% to $76 in morning trading on Tuesday, Nov. 24, falling below its $83 initial public offering price less than a week after its stock market debut.
The decline came amid a broader pullback in technology stocks following Monday's sharp rally, fueled by Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia's chief executive officer.
While Huang's reinstatement eased concerns about Nvidia's future, investors remain worried about stretched AI valuations, massive spending on AI infrastructure and whether that spending will generate sufficient returns.
Anthropic shares have now fallen roughly 22% from their first-day closing price of $97.17.
Meanwhile, regulatory filings released Monday showed that Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and President Daniela Amodei each sold roughly $280 million worth of shares as part of last week's IPO.
Anthropic CEO sells $280 million worth of stock
Insider purchases are typically viewed as a sign of confidence in a company's future. Insider sales, however, are less straightforward, as executives may sell shares for personal financial reasons, including diversification and liquidity.
Dario and Daniela Amodei each sold 3,378,378 shares at the IPO price of $83 on Nov. 20, according to two separate Form 4 filings with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

All seven Anthropic co-founders were scheduled to sell an identical number of shares as part of the offering, worth a combined $1.96 billion.
Investors may also be watching when more insider shares become eligible for sale.
Under Anthropic's lock-up agreements, the founders can sell up to 29% of their covered holdings following the release of the company's first-quarter 2027 earnings or 180 days after the prospectus date, whichever comes first.
The AI company has yet to announce a date for its first quarterly earnings report as a public company.

Anthropic raised a record $93.5 billion in its IPO
Founded in 2021 by seven former OpenAI employees, Anthropic develops large language models and operates Claude, a rival to OpenAI's ChatGPT and Google's Gemini.
The company makes money through paid subscriptions, enterprise contracts and selling access to its AI models to software developers. Its Claude Code tool has also become popular among software engineers.
