TITLE: Google's $99.99 AI plan now spares up to five more people YouTube ads
OUTLET: PPC Land

Google on November 18, 2026 upgraded the YouTube benefit bundled into Google AI Ultra, its most expensive consumer AI subscription, from YouTube Premium Individual to YouTube Premium Family, so that a single Ultra subscriber can now extend ad-free YouTube and YouTube Music to up to five other people in the same household at no extra cost. The change applies to both Ultra tiers, the $99.99-a-month Ultra 5x and the $199.99-a-month Ultra 20x, according to 9to5Google, while the $19.99-a-month AI Pro plan continues to carry only YouTube Premium Lite.
In Short
Google's top AI subscription, AI Ultra, now includes the YouTube Premium Family plan instead of the single-person plan it carried before. That matters because each Ultra household can now put up to six accounts on ad-free YouTube and YouTube Music, and Premium viewers cannot be reached with YouTube ads or targeted as a segment. Nothing changes for AI Pro subscribers, who still get Premium Lite, and Google has not said how many Ultra subscribers there are.
What Google changed
The announcement came first from Shimrit Ben-Yair, whose X profile says Ben-Yair leads Google Photos and Google's AI subscriptions. The post, published on X on the afternoon of November 18 Pacific time, framed the change as a response to subscriber requests rather than a product launch.
"AI Ultra subscriber update - and this is one of the features you all have been pinging me the most about! No, it's not ANOTHER model launch today," Ben-Yair wrote. "Your Ultra plan now comes with YouTube Premium Family. AI Ultra previously included the YT Premium individual plan, but now you can also share ad-free video and music streaming with up to 5 other family members in your household!"
The remark about model launches was pointed. The same day, Google's Josh Woodward introduced Gemini 4 Neon on X as "Frontier-grade intelligence that's faster, cheaper, and follows complex instructions well," and 9to5Google reported that Neon had gone live for AI Pro subscribers. Ben-Yair's post was an Ultra-only change on a day otherwise dominated by a model that also reached the cheaper tier.
9to5Google's Abner Li reported the change 23 minutes after the post. "Google today updated its flagship AI Ultra tier to include YouTube Premium Family after previously limiting subscribers to the Individual plan," the report said, adding that the perk covers both the Ultra 5x plan at $99.99 a month and Ultra 20x at $199.99 a month. According to 9to5Google, Google described the family plan as a highly requested feature.
Google's help documentation for Ultra reflected the change by November 19. The page "Use Google AI Ultra benefits" lists "YouTube Premium Family Plan" among the plan's benefits and says Ultra members in certain countries and regions now have access to "YouTube Premium for their entire family group at no extra cost."
What the family plan includes
According to 9to5Google, YouTube Premium Family costs $26.99 a month when bought on its own and allows up to five family members in the household to be added. It offers ad-free YouTube and YouTube Music with background playback and offline downloads, as well as other benefits such as Ask Music, YouTube Music's AI discovery feature. YouTube kept its upgraded Ask Music chat available only to people who pay for YouTube Premium or YouTube Music Premium when it presented the feature on September 23, 2026, so the family upgrade also widens access to that feature inside Ultra households.
Google's help page describes the benefit in similar terms: members can watch videos ad-free, offline and in the background, and get uninterrupted access to the YouTube Music app.
AI Pro, by contrast, includes only Premium Lite, which according to 9to5Google excludes YouTube Music benefits. PPC Land reported in July that Premium Lite still shows ads on music content and Shorts and is sold in 63 countries, against 120 for full Premium.

Set against the $50 step from AI Pro with 10 TB to Ultra 5x, a household that would otherwise buy Premium Family separately recovers just over half the difference. 9to5Google drew the same conclusion in plainer terms, writing that the change "makes the upgrade from Pro to Ultra a more compelling offering if you need the storage or are a big Gemini user with several family members."
How the benefit works, according to Google
Google's help pages for Ultra set out how the YouTube benefit is shared and who can receive it. According to that documentation, the process has the following steps and conditions:
- Purchase by the family manager. "Google AI Ultra must be purchased by the family manager to share YouTube Premium family benefits with the family group," the help page states. The benefit flows through a Google family group, not through YouTube's own family settings.
- Members in the household. Ben-Yair's post and 9to5Google both put the limit at five people besides the subscriber, living in the same household. The help pages do not state a number.
- Age. The "Get a Google AI Ultra membership" page lists the YouTube Premium Family Plan among the features that require users to be over 13 years old or the applicable age in their country.
- A second Ultra subscriber in the family. A family member who has bought Ultra can start a separate family group to share the benefit with members of that household. The help page adds a constraint: "However, you can only switch family plans every 12 months."
- Countries. The YouTube benefit has its own list of countries and regions, separate from the list where Ultra itself is supported. The YouTube list includes the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, India, Japan and Brazil. South Korea and Iceland appear on the Ultra list but not on the YouTube list, so Ultra subscribers there do not receive the benefit.
- Account type and billing route. Ultra requires a personal Google Account. According to the membership page, subscribers who signed up through a third-party partner or Pixel Pass cannot upgrade, and those billed through Apple's App Store must cancel that subscription first.
- Add-on route. The help page also notes that YouTube Premium can be bought as an add-on to a Google One membership, separately from Ultra.
The cancellation section of the membership page lists what subscribers lose when Ultra ends, including expanded storage and Gemini features. It does not name YouTube Premium.
Why it matters for advertisers
For the marketing community, the significance sits on the other side of the screen. Premium removes platform-served advertising across YouTube, YouTube Kids and YouTube Music. Every account that moves from the free tier into an Ultra family group leaves the pool of viewers who can be reached with YouTube ad inventory.
PPC Land examined that blind spot in January, describing Premium members as the audience advertisers can't buy on YouTube: neither Google Ads nor Display & Video 360 offers Premium subscriber status as a targeting segment, so advertisers cannot see how many of their intended viewers have opted out of ads altogether.
The scale is not small. PPC Land's January analysis counted 125 million Music and Premium subscribers as of March 2025. At the end of October 2026, Alphabet reported more than 150 million YouTube Music and Premium subscribers, including trials, in the same quarter that YouTube ad revenue rose 9% to $11.21 billion, its slowest growth of 2026. Alphabet does not split paying subscribers from trial users, and Google has not published how many people subscribe to Ultra, so the number of accounts the family upgrade will move off ads cannot be calculated from public data.
What can be measured is the multiplier. Before November 18, an Ultra subscription removed at most one account from YouTube's ad-supported audience. It can now remove up to six. For a household already paying for Premium Family, the change is a saving rather than a shift in ad exposure. For a household where only the Ultra subscriber had Premium, five more people can now watch without ads.
The move also says something about where Google is placing value. Alphabet said in February that it had passed 325 million paid subscriptions across its consumer services. Folding a $26.99 family plan into an AI subscription ties several household members to one payment, which turns cancellation into a household decision, a structure that can reduce churn in Google's most expensive tier. Ad-free viewing becomes part of the case for AI spending as well as a product sold on its own.
Other platforms have gone the opposite way. Amazon in March renamed its US Prime Video ad-free option Prime Video Ultra and raised it from $2.99 to $4.99 a month, effective April 10, 2026, charging more for the same exit from ads. Google is not cutting the price of YouTube Premium; it is including the family version at no extra cost inside a $99.99 product. In streaming terms, YouTube's AVOD business keeps its free-tier audience, while Ultra households move further out of reach.
Ultra pulls away from Pro
The family upgrade is the latest in a run of 2026 changes that separate Ultra from the cheaper plans. Google restricted its Gemini Spark agent for Mac to US Ultra subscribers when it launched on June 30, 2026. On October 13, Gemini 4 Argon and a tasks mode reached the Gemini app for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, a release shared by both paid tiers. On November 10, Gemini 4 Carbon began rolling out in AI Mode for Ultra subscribers only.
The pattern has left Pro subscribers watching from below. Replies under Ben-Yair's post and comments on 9to5Google's report asked whether Pro would receive full Premium. "No love for AI Pro? Alright then," one commenter wrote. Another wished Pro subscribers could upgrade from Lite to full Premium "for a discounted fee." Another reader asked whether the change applies in Germany; Google's country list for the YouTube benefit includes it. At least one user replying to the post said the news had arrived by email from YouTube.
What the sources do not say
None of the sources explains what happens to household members who already pay for Premium on their own, or to an existing YouTube Premium family group when the manager starts receiving the benefit through Ultra. Non-US prices, the YouTube benefit attached to the $49.99 AI Pro with 10 TB plan, and whether Premium survives cancellation of Ultra are also not addressed. Google has given no figure for Ultra subscribers.
