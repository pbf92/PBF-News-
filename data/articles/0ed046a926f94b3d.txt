TITLE: The serious questions Co-op Live needs to answer after gas incident at concert
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

Co-op Live Manchester concertgoers were heard shouting 'I can't breathe' when a gas substance was dispersed into the crowd.
The major incident was declared during a huge gig on a night which many report that bag checks were not being carried out. The venue is yet to explain how this could have happened.
Questions over security at the UK's largest indoor arena are being asked after 89 concertgoers were treated following the emergency incident during a concert by US rapper Don Toliver.

Safety and security at gigs came under the spotlight nationally in the aftermath of the terrorist bombing atrocity following the Ariana Grande concert at Manchester Arena in 2017, in which 22 lives were lost.
But it appears that what was initially described by police as suspected tear gas was taken into Co-op Live ahead of the Sunday night show.
Mobile phone footage seen by the Manchester Evening News also clearly shows a flare being ignited and emitting red-tinged smoke in the crowd during the concert.
Greater Manchester Police (GMP) said it would be liaising with the venue over security as part of its ongoing investigation into the incident.
There were more than 12,000 people inside the venue as an evacuation was ordered, although many dozens walked out immediately as the incident unfolded. Mobile phone video footage showed people in the crowd coughing, spluttering and covering their faces.
Shouts of 'what's that smell', 'I can't breathe' and 'I can taste it' could be heard as concertgoers flocked through the doors to get outside.
Amid limited information being issued about what actually happened, concerns over Co-op Live's security operation are now being raised. One concertgoer said he was 'sprayed' directly in the face with what he believed was tear gas by someone holding a 'canister'.
GMP said 89 people subsequently 'displayed symptoms believed to be linked to the exposure'. One woman was taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary for assessment and treatment, the force added, but there's been no update on her condition.
The remaining 88 underwent 'dry decontamination' at the scene and were then taken to the Etihad Stadium late on Sunday night.
The Manchester Evening News understands people were asked to remove their shoes and outer clothing before being given hazardous material suits to wear after wipe downs. Images show paramedics assessing people inside a sports hall at the stadium.
What actually happened isn't yet known, with police now working to establish whether any crime was committed. There have been no arrests so far.
The substance involved - initially said by police to be tear gas - also hasn't been confirmed, with GMP saying testing was continuing. The force said enquiries remained ongoing 'to establish the nature of the substance, where it came from and any associated risk'.
Police also said 'further testing' would take place on items found at the venue in the aftermath on Monday, but refused to say what those items were.

The Manchester Evening News has been contacted by a large number of people directly questioning security at the venue in the aftermath of the gig.
Charlie Pearce, 19, a bricklayer from Warrington, said he was 'sprayed' directly in the face with what he believed was tear gas by someone holding a canister. He said he believes he was 'targeted' due to his founding of an online forum called UKRagers, which promotes mosh pits.
Charlie, at the gig with his girlfriend said: "I was in a mosh pit and I heard someone shouting behind me. Someone then tapped me on the shoulder in an aggressive manner. When I turned around, I saw a white or silverish canister in my face. A split second after I turned around, someone sprayed out of it.
"My eyes were on fire. I couldn't see anything. It went all down the back of my throat, nose and mouth. At that moment, I just ran out. I was screaming for help from medics as I really couldn't breathe or see anything. All the effects I have had, they are the effects of tear gas."
He told how his eyes were washed with saline and of being given antihistamines and water. Charlie said he was then taken to the sports hall where, wrapped in tin foil, he was checked over again before the effects began to die down.
Charlie accused the venue of 'failing in its duty of public safety.' "How has someone actually taken that into the venue?" he asked.
Vic Fearon, 51, attended the gig with her daughter Charlotte, 21. While Vic had her bag searched - leading to a bag of Jelly Babies and a bottle of water being confiscated - she said her daughter was only asked what was inside her handbag, before security staff 'took her word for it'.
Supermarket manager and seasoned gig-goer Jessica Moult-Boffey, 23, from Orrell in Wigan, was at the gig with her mum. "A lot of my friends were in standing and not one of them got their bag checked," she said. "One of my friends sneaked a flare in."
Other fans told of their security concerns. Ben Stevenson, 23, from Barnsley, said: "The biggest issue with it all was security was dreadful, no bag checks at all. No pat downs or anything. The first thing we said when we went in was that there was no security."
Another concertgoer claimed he was able to take a bottle of water, a can of Monster, a flag and two balaclavas inside without challenge.
Asked for a comment regarding security concerns, Co-op Live pointed towards the information on its 'frictionless security screening' and bag search policy on its website. The venue said its screening systems detect prohibited items without visitors needing to empty their pockets, while small bags are allowed in the arena.
It's understood mass screening technology - often out of sight, but fully operational - is used to detect specific items.
Canada-based company Xtract One supplied security gates at Co-op Live offering what it described as 'fast and frictionless fan screening that optimizes their experience without compromising security, with an AI-powered, multi-layered security solution that secures all patrons'.
CEO Peter Evans said in a statement to the M.E.N.: "At this time, out of respect for our customer and our agreements with those, we cannot comment on the incident."
North West Ambulance Service (NWAS) said it declared a major incident at 10.25pm. Emergency crews including its Hazardous Area Response Team were sent to the scene.
NWAS said clinicians reported people experiencing eye and respiratory symptoms, with 'decontamination' carried out as a precaution.
A 33-year-old man from Scotland at the gig with family said a security guard at Co-op Live told him 'someone has let a gas off'. He said the sports hall at the Etihad was being used as a makeshift medical centre with another area of the campus used as a makeshift 'quarantine area.'
The man said there were around 40 other gig-goers there in 'haz-mat suits.' He said: "There were a fair few with sore eyes, quite a few with respiratory issues, a lot of coughing."
He said after a police officer took their details they were allowed to leave, but had to walk barefoot back to the sports hall to collect their clothes and shoes, which had been placed in clear plastic bags. The man said his bag was manually searched by security on entering the venue, but those of many others near him weren't.
The major incident status was stood down by NWAS at just before 3.30am on Monday. Greater Manchester Police also declared a major incident, which was stood down at 4am.
GMP said it was an 'isolated incident and there is no wider threat to the public'.
Assistant Chief Constable Tom Harding said: "Enquiries remain ongoing to establish the nature of the substance, where it came from and any associated risk. Testing will take place throughout the day as the investigation into the cause of this incident progresses.
"We are trying to establish whether any criminality has occurred in relation to this incident. I would like to thank the public who attended for their patience whilst we responded to this incident. There were over 12,000 people in attendance during this concert who were safely escorted from the arena and made their way home.
"Our coordinated response meant colleagues from the emergency services and partner agencies could tackle this incident swiftly and safely."
A Co-op Live spokesperson added: "Following an incident in the crowd at last night's show, we are working closely with Greater Manchester Police to identify the cause and action appropriately. Our priority remains the safety and security of all visitors to Co-op Live, and we will provide more information once we have an update from GMP."
NWAS, meanwhile, urged anyone who was at the gig who feels unwell to seek medical advice via NHS 111 or online.
