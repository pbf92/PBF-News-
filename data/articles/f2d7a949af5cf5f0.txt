TITLE: First defeat and first red for Ferguson as Glentoran falter
OUTLET: BBC Sport

- Published
It was a night of unfortunate firsts for Duncan Ferguson at Solitude on Friday.
The Everton great suffered his first defeat as Glentoran boss as they were edged out 3-2 by Cliftonville in an Irish Premiership thriller.
He also received his first red card as a manager, after picking up nine during his playing career, in the first half for protesting the decision to award Cliftonville's first goal after he perceived a foul in the build-up.
The defeat ended Glentoran's eight-game unbeaten run in the league and leaves them well off the pace in pursuit of their first league title since 2009.
While he has delivered with his animated touchline antics, Ferguson has so far struggled to get much out of his side on the pitch.
'The manager got frustrated because it was a foul'
The Glens were second best for much of the first half in north Belfast, with Cliftonville dominating possession and creating the better chances.
Still, the awarding of the Reds' first goal was controversial as it looked like defender Jarlath O'Rourke had been pushed by Liam McStravick, who then teed up Joe Sheridan to score the opener on 38 minutes.
Ferguson was incensed that the goal stood, protesting while his side went up the other end and scored an equaliser through Cal Weatherup's deflected strike a minute later.
Such was the vehemence of his words towards the officials, referee Ben McMaster brandished a straight red card and Ferguson was forced to make the long walk to the tunnel all while being jeered by Cliftonville supporters.
"I think the manager got frustrated because it was a foul. It changes the dynamic [the sending off], he's a leader, I've then got to step in and fill his boots and it's a big set of boots to fill," said Ferguson's assistant Gary Bollan.
His side looked to have received a stern talking to from Bollan at half-time as they were much more competitive after the break.
They responded to Brian Healy putting the Reds ahead once again with a fine James Douglas strike on 86 minutes, which looked to be enough to secure a point and continue their unbeaten run.
However, they failed to stop midfielder Sheridan, who waltzed into the box before firing into the top corner two minutes later to seal a win.
Glentoran's fifth league defeat of the campaign leaves them 14 points behind pacesetters Linfield.
Former Crusaders captain Colin Coates, who was on co-commentary duties for the game for BBC Sport NI, believes Ferguson and the club have "a lot of work to do" to get back challenging for major honours.
"Look at the squad, there is work to do in January and next summer," he added.
"Duncan Ferguson will use this next few months to assess what he has, what he wants to keep and what he needs to bring in to make them competitive. Every time I watch them I want more from them.
"There is a real lack of quality particularly at the top end they don't look like they can hurt teams aside from Jordan Stewart. A lot of work and soul searching to do and players will be playing for their futures."
Stewart was suspended for the game at Solitude and was a big miss while Ferguson will hope to get more out of Conor McMenamin and the fit-again Pat Hoban, who both came on in the second half.
The Glens face two games against Larne, one in the BetMcLean Cup on Tuesday and one in the league next Friday and Ferguson will hope to mastermind two big results to really kick-start his reign.
Sheridan shines for Reds
For Cliftonville, it was a case of another big performance under the lights on a Friday in front of a raucous Solitude crowd.
The Reds beat Larne 4-2 at the same venue a fortnight ago and key in their two big wins in recent weeks has been teenage midfielder Sheridan, who is growing in stature for Jim Magilton.
The 19-year-old broke into the senior squad last season after a fruitful six-month spell at Queen's University in the 2024-25 campaign.
He won the club's Young Player of the Year award last season and was a rotation option, but he has kicked on again this year and firmly established himself in a midfield three with captain Rory Hale and Dean McMaster.
The next step for the talented Northern Ireland under-21 international is adding goals and assists to his game and he certainly delivered on Friday.
Former Glentoran captain Paul Leeman said he showed "composure" for both his goals, first to curl home from close range, then to weave his way into the box and place the ball in the top corner.
"It was a really good performance from him. He's becoming a mainstay in the team," Leeman added.
The victory moves Cliftonville to within a point of the Glens and with games against struggling Ballymena United and Bangor to come, they will hope to embark upon another winning run to continue their surge up the table.
