TITLE: Mateus Fernandes is proof of a Roberto De Zerbi concept neither Manchester United nor Daniel Levy understood
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

When Tottenham Hotspur spent 85 million pounds to secure the signature of young West Ham United defensive midfielder Mateus Fernandes alongside the 100 million pound blockbuster signing of former Serie A champion Sandro Tonali from Newcastle United, many derided Roberto De Zerbi and Spurs for overpaying for Fernandes, asserting that Spurs cannot simply buy their way to success.
And most of the criticisms, ironically enough, came from Manchester United, who were probably the second most interested team in Fernandes. Maybe it was sour grapes because Man United really, really wanted a young all around midfielder who could destroy plays and yet create and progress as well, but their fan base was laughing hard at Spurs for the signing, stating it would bite them in the behind.
Man United signed a trio of midfielders in Youri Tielemans, Andrey Santos, and Carlos Baleba. Individually, they were less expensive than Fernandes. And individually, all of them have been much, much worse than Fernandes, and they are now joining the likes of Mason Mount and Manuel Ugarte as the latest flop signings to come to Old Trafford for huge price tags but contribute absolutely nothing to the midfield.
Mateus Fernandes is levels above Youri Tielemans and Andrey Santos
Daniel Levy used to sign multiple players in a position instead of the best possible solution, thinking that he was finding value by signing a couple of players for 30-40 million pounds instead of one 85 million pound super beast who can be a world class Premier League player for the next decade. And then Spurs ended up with guys like Yves Bissouma who ended up saddling the squad for years without any material benefit to their hopes of Champions League football.
Well, Manchester United, instead of spending 85 million pounds on one amazing Mateus Fernandes, decided to spend 85 million pounds on Youri Tielemans and Chelsea cast off Andrey Santos. Tielemans looks finished and like a player Aston Villa were happy to sell before everyone knew hew as finished, while Santos looks like the kind of player Chelsea were more than willing to give up on to a Big Six rival. In short, they stink.
Meanwhile, Fernandes is absolute quality, and nobody is questioning why Roberto De Zerbi signed him instead of going the Daniel Levy route and signing up a couple of players out of the bargain bin - or doing what Man United did and spending that 85 million stones on two birds who can't even fly.
