UnitedHealth Stocks Fall 4.62% as CMS Proposes Wider Medicare Advantage Audits
UnitedHealth Group UNH shares fell 4.62% to $392.96 at 9:50 a.m. ET in regular trading on November 17, a day after federal health officials proposed a sweeping anti-fraud rule that would expand Medicare Advantage audits. The prior close was $412.02. Managed care peers Humana and CVS Health also traded lower.
The Comprehensive Regulations to Uncover Suspicious Healthcare proposal, known as CRUSH, would codify risk adjustment data validation audits without a fee-for-service adjuster and extrapolate findings starting with payment year 2018, the CMS fact sheet states. CMS estimates the approach would recover about $9 billion per payment year on average, and HHS projects nearly $90 billion in federal savings from 2027 to 2036. Comments are due January 19, 2027.
Extrapolation is the key change, because a coding error found in a sample can be multiplied across an entire contract. For the industry, $9 billion a year means clawing back revenue that plans booked years ago. GuruFocus gives UnitedHealth a GF Score of 85/100, with strong profitability and momentum but an average balance sheet. Its GF Value of $429.73 implies a 9% discount, which GuruFocus still rates fairly valued, so the selloff has not yet created deep value. The next test is whether UnitedHealth quantifies or reserves for its 2018 to 2024 audit exposure before the comment period closes.
