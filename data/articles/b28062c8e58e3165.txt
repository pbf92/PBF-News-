TITLE: Appetite Suppressants Linked to Substantial Nutrient Shortfalls in Small Exploratory Study
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Prescription appetite-suppressing medications, including Wegovy and Mounjaro, are associated with inadequate intakes of essential nutrients and overall energy in some patients, according to an exploratory observational study published in the open access journal BMJ Nutrition Prevention & Health. The research highlights that while incretin-based therapies serve as effective clinical treatments for obesity and type 2 diabetes, their broader effects on daily dietary composition remain poorly documented. The authors observed that appetite suppression can dramatically curtail total food consumption, creating a scenario where patients inadvertently fail to meet core nutritional benchmarks while receiving little guidance or oversight from healthcare clinicians.
To investigate the nutritional and clinical experiences of people taking these treatments, investigators conducted an exploratory service evaluation involving 14 adult patients registered at a single general practice in England. The participant group comprised ten individuals receiving private prescriptions and four receiving prescriptions through the National Health Service. Dietary consumption was evaluated using the validated myfood24 dietary recall diary over a two-day period. Researchers estimated each participant’s average daily intake across 28 distinct nutrients, benchmarking the values against sex-specific reference nutrient intake recommendations established by the United Kingdom government.
The dietary assessment revealed marked reductions in total daily energy intake among the participants. The cohort recorded an average daily energy consumption of 1,231 kilocalories, which corresponded to only 58.5 percent of their sex-specific estimated energy requirements. More than half of the study participants consumed less than two-thirds of their estimated daily energy needs, and three individuals recorded extreme reductions below 800 kilocalories per day. Recorded foods were also evaluated for industrial processing, with ultra-processed food intake serving as a proxy measure of poor diet quality. Participants derived an average of 33 percent of their total energy from ultra-processed items, although the proportion varied widely across the sample, reaching as high as 84 percent in one individual.
Alongside low caloric intake, the investigators observed widespread micronutrient shortfalls when examining the 28 recorded nutrients. Average intakes fell below two-thirds of the recommended reference threshold for at least one nutrient in ten of the 14 patients. When investigators separately evaluated six specific focus nutrients—fibre, iron, vitamin D, potassium, protein, and magnesium—eight participants failed to reach two-thirds of the reference nutrient intake for three or more of them, with one person falling below this threshold across all six. More than three-quarters consumed less than two-thirds of the recommended levels for both dietary fibre and vitamin D, half fell below the threshold for iron, and all 14 participants failed to reach the recommended minimum of 400 grams of daily fruit and vegetable intake. In contrast, protein intake remained robust, averaging 153 percent of the reference nutrient intake, with only a single participant failing to meet the recommended protein target.
Patient feedback collected alongside dietary tracking consistently attributed sharp reductions in eating and nutritional consumption directly to pharmaceutical appetite suppression. Several participants reported positive experiences, including a welcome reduction in intrusive food noise and the capacity to enjoy meals without guilt. However, participants also identified notable unintended consequences, such as diminished pleasure from food, unexpected declines in alcohol consumption, and avoidance of social dining situations triggered by gastrointestinal symptoms like nausea or diarrhoea. Two individuals specifically expressed shock and distress when the dietary diary exposed the severe extent of their caloric deficits, noting that they had remained entirely unaware of their extreme underconsumption prior to completing the structured tracking tool.
Qualitative feedback additionally revealed substantial deficits in ongoing clinical monitoring and support across the cohort. Many participants reported an absence of structured follow-up appointments to track nutritional status or manage emergent treatment complications. Inadequate medication reviews left individuals to cope with unmanaged adverse effects, including bouts of dizziness and low blood pressure, prompting several patients to alter their dosing schedules independently or rely on online peer groups for medical advice. The researchers noted that these experiences illustrate a wider deficit in professional oversight, specifically regarding the safe integration of weight-reduction pharmacotherapies with existing medical regimens and concurrent treatments.
The study authors pointed out that existing United Kingdom dietary advice is anchored to the Eatwell Guide, a weight-neutral educational framework that was never designed to address malnutrition in obesity or support individuals prescribed incretin-based medications. Because no dedicated nutritional monitoring standard exists for this class of drugs, patients frequently navigate significant physiological changes without tailored dietary guidance. Furthermore, the researchers emphasized that because ten of the 14 participants obtained their prescriptions through private providers rather than the public health system, nutritional safety netting and formal clinical oversight appear especially fragile outside established National Health Service pathways.
The researchers stressed that the findings originate from a small observational study conducted within a single general practice located in a relatively affluent area, meaning the results are hypothesis-generating and cannot demonstrate direct cause and effect or represent all clinical settings. Nevertheless, the study team concluded that some individuals taking appetite suppressants enter very low energy states and experience substantial micronutrient deficits despite consuming adequate protein. They emphasized that structured nutritional monitoring and clear clinical guidance are urgently needed within incretin treatment pathways to prevent unrecognised nutritional deficiencies and ensure long-term treatment safety.
