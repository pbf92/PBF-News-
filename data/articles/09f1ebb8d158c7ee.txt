TITLE: Meta-Analysis Compares Ab Externo and Ab Interno XEN45 Gel Stent Implantation
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

In a newly published systematic review and meta-analysis of comparative clinical studies, researchers evaluated the surgical outcomes of ab externo versus ab interno implantation of the XEN45 gel stent in adult patients with glaucoma. Across six retrospective cohort investigations comprising 681 eyes, ab externo placement demonstrated a statistically significant advantage in lowering intraocular pressure at eight to twelve months of follow-up compared with the ab interno approach. Specifically, the pooled random-effects model identified a weighted mean difference in intraocular pressure reduction of 3.06 millimeters of mercury favoring the ab externo approach, with a 95 percent confidence interval extending from 0.99 to 5.13 millimeters of mercury. However, moderate statistical heterogeneity was observed across the cohorts, and the corresponding prediction interval ranged broadly from minus 1.26 to 7.38 millimeters of mercury, indicating substantial uncertainty regarding the consistency of this effect across diverse clinical environments.
Glaucoma represents a progressive optic neuropathy and stands as a leading global cause of irreversible blindness, currently affecting an estimated 80.5 million people worldwide. Lowering intraocular pressure remains the sole clinically established therapeutic strategy to halt or delay disease progression, a principle reinforced by historical evidence such as the Early Manifest Glaucoma Trial, where every additional millimeter of mercury in initial pressure reduction corresponded to an estimated ten percent decrease in the risk of disease progression. Although conventional incisional filtration procedures such as trabeculectomy and aqueous shunt implantation deliver substantial pressure reduction, they remain linked to notable visual quality decline and frequent postoperative complications.
The XEN Gel Stent, manufactured by AbbVie Inc., is a six-millimeter cross-linked gelatin tube designed as a subconjunctival drainage device that provides a less invasive pathway for aqueous humor filtration compared with traditional trabeculectomy. The device can be positioned through two distinct surgical routes that differ fundamentally in their tissue handling and entry trajectories.
Prior individual comparative studies examining these two implantation routes yielded inconsistent clinical findings, with some centers demonstrating enhanced pressure lowering with ab externo placement while others reported equivalent pressure reductions and variable postoperative needling requirements. To rigorously synthesize within-study comparative evidence, investigators searched major biomedical databases, including PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, ClinicalTrials.gov, and the World Health Organization International Clinical Trials Registry Platform, from their inception through May 2026. The search identified 354 initial records, from which six retrospective comparative studies met the prespecified criteria of evaluating adult cohorts followed for at least eight months. Within these cohorts, patient mean ages ranged from 61.8 to 75.0 years, female representation ranged from 39.0 to 70.0 percent, prior incisional glaucoma surgery ranged between zero and 38.3 percent, and mean baseline intraocular pressures spanned from 18.5 to 29.2 millimeters of mercury.
The statistical methodology incorporated random-effects meta-analytic models using restricted maximum likelihood estimation and the Knapp-Hartung adjustment, analyzing continuous parameters as weighted mean differences and binary endpoints as odds ratios with 95 percent confidence intervals. For changes in intraocular pressure and ocular hypotensive medications, missing standard deviations of mean changes were imputed using a baseline correlation coefficient of 0.5, alongside sensitivity evaluations using values of 0.25 and 0.75, both of which confirmed the numerical stability of the results. Primary efficacy was measured at the latest recorded visit within an eight- to twelve-month postoperative window, chosen because individual trial durations clustered predominantly around one year, with only one study contributing outcomes extending beyond twelve months.
While the primary pooled analysis demonstrated a statistically superior pressure reduction with ab externo implantation, the reduction in daily ocular hypotensive medications showed no meaningful difference between surgical approaches. Across the pooled cohorts, the weighted mean difference in medication reduction was 0.31 medications, with a 95 percent confidence interval of minus 0.11 to 0.73, accompanied by substantial heterogeneity characterized by an I-squared value of 63.7 percent. Similarly, four studies reporting complete surgical success without medications and qualified surgical success with medications permitted revealed no significant between-group differences. The pooled odds ratio for complete success was 1.75 with a confidence interval of 0.51 to 5.96, while qualified success yielded an odds ratio of 2.09 with a confidence interval of 0.62 to 6.98, both exhibiting moderate heterogeneity and wide intervals driven by divergent study-specific success definitions.
Patients undergoing ab externo implantation experienced significantly reduced odds of undergoing postoperative needling, reflected by an odds ratio of 0.41 and a 95 percent confidence interval spanning 0.19 to 0.89. Nevertheless, substantial statistical heterogeneity was present, with an I-squared statistic of 60.7 percent and a wide prediction interval ranging from 0.09 to 1.95 that crossed the neutral threshold. The clinical indication for initiating bleb revision or digital massage was non-standardized across the included retrospective cohorts, depending extensively upon individual surgeon judgment and postoperative monitoring protocols.
To investigate the technical roots of needling heterogeneity, the researchers conducted a post hoc exploratory subgroup analysis stratifying ab externo procedures according to intraoperative management of Tenon’s capsule. Procedures designated as incorporating strategic Tenon’s modulation, defined by intentional surgical separation or dissection of Tenon’s capsule from the underlying episclera to fashion an unobstructed filtration pocket, exhibited diminished between-study heterogeneity, with an I-squared value dropping to 16.4 percent across two studies. In contrast, techniques categorized as passive modulation involved minimal tissue manipulation without targeted sub-Tenon dissection.
An evaluation of postoperative safety endpoints revealed no significant disparities between ab externo and ab interno routes regarding vision-threatening complications. Across the analyzed cohorts, the odds ratio for clinically meaningful hypotony was 0.73 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.37 to 1.44 and zero detected heterogeneity. Choroidal effusion occurred at similar rates, yielding an odds ratio of 1.55 with a confidence interval of 0.44 to 5.41. Stent-related complications, including mechanical exposure, distal migration, or luminary obstruction, showed an odds ratio of 0.57 with an extremely wide confidence interval from 0.03 to 13.08 and marked heterogeneity of 76.6 percent. Documented postoperative infections were rare across the collective datasets, consisting solely of one recorded case of endophthalmitis occurring within an ab externo treatment cohort.
The methodological framework of the evidence base was complicated by the pragmatic inclusion of one study by Do and colleagues, which classified comparative cohorts strictly according to conjunctival management rather than pure anatomical insertion routes.
Methodological quality assessments performed via the ROBINS-I instrument rated the risk of bias across the six studies as moderate to serious, stemming predominantly from non-randomized allocation, confounding by surgical indication, and unstandardized postoperative regimens. When assessed through the GRADE system, the overall certainty of evidence was rated as very low across every primary and secondary clinical outcome. Because all included literature comprised non-randomized observational designs, baseline evidence quality commenced at a low grade and was further downgraded due to residual confounding, variable definitions of surgical success, and imprecision. The authors concluded that current published literature does not establish the definitive superiority of either route, emphasizing that prospective randomized clinical trials with standardized tissue dissection and needling protocols remain vital to disentangle the independent effects of surgical trajectory and tissue handling.
