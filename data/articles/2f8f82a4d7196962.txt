TITLE: Orthognathic Mixed Reality Templates Show Sub-Two-Millimeter Deviations in Early Scoping Review
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A scoping review evaluating mixed reality and augmented reality systems as intraoperative navigation-transfer templates in orthognathic surgery has found that the technology demonstrates technical feasibility, generally achieving deviations between sub-millimetre levels and two millimetres under favourable operative conditions. The comprehensive investigation mapped how these head-mounted and optical systems are deployed to translate virtual surgical planning directly into clinical and laboratory settings. However, the study emphasized that the current evidence base remains heterogeneous and dominated by early-phase investigations, meaning the reported figures should not be interpreted as validated or pooled accuracy estimates for routine surgical adoption.
The scoping review protocol involved comprehensive literature searches across PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from their respective inception dates up to 19 September 2026, alongside supplementary manual and citation searches. To ensure methodological rigor during the identification process, two reviewers independently performed the record screening and data charting phases, while any disagreements arising between them were formally resolved by consulting a third independent reviewer.
Across the surveyed databases and supplementary citation tracks, the initial search queries yielded a total of 364 records, comprising 363 entries retrieved from electronic databases and one additional study identified through citation searching. Following the removal of 13 duplicate entries, the reviewers screened the titles and abstracts of the remaining 350 records. A total of 23 full reports were sought for retrieval, all 23 of which were successfully obtained, and subsequent eligibility assessments led to the exclusion of three reports, ultimately yielding 20 studies that met all inclusion criteria.
Within the final selection of 20 papers, 19 investigations were retrieved directly from database queries and one was obtained through citation tracking. The included literature consisted of 12 clinical investigations alongside eight preclinical or phantom laboratory studies. Mixed reality and augmented reality setups across these studies functioned primarily as intraoperative adjuncts designed to assist surgical teams in transferring planned virtual osteotomy lines, repositioning maxillary or mandibular anatomical segments, conducting occlusion verification, and placing fixation plates or patient-specific implants.
Regarding the technological infrastructure documented across the literature, optical-see-through head-mounted displays emerged as the most frequently reported hardware category, with the HoloLens and HoloLens 2 platforms representing the predominant wearable devices. In terms of spatial alignment strategies, surgical teams relied more heavily on marker-based registration workflows than on markerless tracking modalities such as direct anatomical teeth-matching or surface-matching approaches.
Reported accuracy outcomes across the included trials varied considerably depending on the specific surgical workflow task and the categorization of geometric discrepancies. Because error metrics differed substantially across platforms, the review reported data separately across three distinct measurement categories: registration or tracking error, surface-distance error, and final postoperative repositioning error, explicitly avoiding the calculation of an overarching single pooled accuracy range.
Study design characteristics among the analyzed literature revealed that 12 of the 20 included investigations were single-arm accuracy trials evaluated exclusively against a reference standard, comparing the planned virtual surgical plan against postoperative imaging. Only eight of the 20 studies incorporated a concurrent comparator arm. Concluding their mapping, the authors underlined that well-designed comparative trials featuring standardized, clinically anchored outcome thresholds are necessary before mixed reality navigation templates can be broadly validated in orthognathic surgery.
