OUTLET: Medical Dialogues

Prophylactic Mesh Reduces Incisional Hernias After Hepatobiliary Surgery, finds study
A new study published in the Journal of American Medical Association showed that without worsening postoperative complications, preventive synthetic mesh implantation during abdominal wall closure dramatically decreased the likelihood of incisional hernia following hepatobiliary surgery via a subcostal incision.
Incisional hernias after hepatobiliary and pancreatic surgery may occur in up to 40% of cases. There is insufficient data supporting the use of prophylactic mesh in subcostal incisions. This study was conducted. To determine if prophylactic mesh implantation during abdominal wall closure minimizes the risk of incisional hernias.
This clinical trial was carried out at Virgen Macarena Hospital in Seville, Spain, between July 2020 and December 2024. Patients scheduled for open right subcostal access who had two or more risk factors for incisional hernias were taken into consideration. Emergency surgery, metastatic illness, or an incurable malignant tumor discovered after the procedure were among the exclusion criteria. Following surgery, follow-up was conducted at 1, 3, 6, 12, and 24 months. Conventional abdominal wall closure vs conventional closure with prophylactic synthetic mesh implantation was part of the intervention. After 12 to 24 months, radiographic incisional hernia was the main outcome measure.
144 patients were randomized to either synthetic mesh-reinforced closure (n = 75) or conventional closure (n = 69); 66 patients were allocated to the mesh group and 73 patients to the nonmesh group following the exclusion of 5 patients due to intraoperative exclusion criteria.
57 patients in the mesh group and 60 in the nonmesh group were examined at the 12-month follow-up; 5.2% of patients in the mesh group and 21% of patients in the nonmesh group had radiological incisional hernias, indicating a substantial decrease in the incidence of incisional hernias.
49 patients in the mesh group and 48 patients in the nonmesh group were examined after a 24-month follow-up period; the radiological incisional hernia rate was considerably higher in the no-mesh group, with 18 (37.5%) patients and 4 (8.1%) patients in the mesh group.
There were no differences found between the groups in terms of surgical site incidents, postoperative complications, or postoperative discomfort. Functional recovery significantly improved for patients in the mesh group. According to this study, using a J incision resulted in a much greater risk of incisional hernias. Overall, prophylactic mesh implantation considerably decreased the overall risk of incisional hernia following hepatobiliary incisions while not increasing postoperative complications.
Source:
Bellido-Luque, J., Sanchez-Matamoros Martín, I., Reguera-Rosal, J., Balla, A., Morales-Conde, S., & Nogales Muñoz, A. (2026). Prophylactic mesh placement and incisional hernia incidence in hepatobiliopancreatic surgery: A randomized clinical trial: A randomized clinical trial. JAMA Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamasurg.2026.4012
