TITLE: Rockstar's GTA 6 food scenes draw criticism for looking too simple
OUTLET: A 90s Kid

GTA 6 has more than 350 places to grab a snack or a meal, but its dining scenes are drawing criticism for serving up little visual variety. Polygon’s assessment is that Rockstar’s food presentation falls flat whether players are eating alone or making a date night of it with Jason and Lucia.
The complaint is about the meal, not the menu’s size. In Polygon’s account, eating a hamburger generally looks little different from putting away a protein bar. That makes the sheer number of food stops a less appetizing prospect: plenty of places to eat, without much distinction in the act of eating.
A cupboard snack sets the tone
The report describes the early hours as a diet largely made up of protein bars, apples, and bagged snacks taken from the cupboard in Jason’s Key Lento bungalow. Those are suitably modest provisions for a quick bite. The criticism is that going out for something more substantial does not produce a correspondingly richer dining scene.
That distinction matters to the argument. A large restaurant count describes how many opportunities players have to stop for food; it does not, by itself, make those stops memorable. Here, the reported disappointment concerns the payoff on screen rather than the availability of somewhere to eat.
Rockstar’s own food detail sets the bar
Polygon points to the Prairie Box combo from in-game fast-food spot Chaco’s as evidence that Rockstar can create extremely detailed food. Its criticism therefore rests on a contrast within the studio’s capabilities, rather than a demand for something outside its established craft.
The piece also argues that food in games need not grant temporary stat buffs or unlock dialogue to stick in the memory. That is the sharper point: sometimes the reward for ordering dinner can simply be a dinner worth looking at.
