TITLE: Christians in Jordan cancel Christmas celebrations to honour church terror attack victims
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Christmas celebrations in Jordanian churches are being “cancelled”, as a mark of respect for the families of those killed in a terror attack.
Nine people died when gunmen opened fire during a funeral at St George Greek Orthodox Church in Hammoud, on 23 October. Three people were sentenced to death by Jordan’s state security court for their role in the terror attack on Sunday.
Churches will also remember a security officer killed in a separate stabbing outside the embassy of Sri Lanka on 5 November.
A statement from the Council of Church Leaders in Jordan said: “Christmas celebrations are cancelled in solidarity with the martyrs of the nation… and prayers remain a testament to hope and peace.”
It called on Christians to mark the festival with “simplicity and dignity”; Christmas trees and candles should act as a symbol of hope, with a memorial space to remember those killed. The Council urged prayers for the murdered and their families, King Abdullah II bin Al-Hussein, and the nation of Jordan.
Regular services, prayer and clergy pastoral duties will continue as normal.
