Congressman Buys Apple Stock Again, Shows Off Power of Dividend Reinvestments
When it comes to tracking the stock purchases by members of Congress, some trades are new investments, but others might be dividend reinvestments.
One member of Congress disclosed a new trade in Apple Inc AAPL stock, a now regular quarterly tradition.
• Apple stock is showing upward movement. Why are AAPL shares climbing?
Congressman Buys Apple Stock (Again)
Rep. Ed Case (D-Hawaii) disclosed buying $1,000 to $15,000 in Apple shares recently, according to data from the Benzinga Government Trades page.
The Nov. 16 filing attributes the trade to his spouse. It was the fourth Apple purchase this year, extending the streak of quarterly buys to 11 consecutive quarters dating back to 2024.
The transaction does not say how many Apple shares were bought, other than that the $1,000 to $15,000 trading range is where the purchase falls.
Reinvesting Dividends
Apple currently pays out 27 cents per share in dividends each quarter, or $1.08 per share on an annual basis. The company regularly raises the dividend payout by one cent each year. The dividend represents an annual yield of 0.31%, which is a small yield, but comes in the technology sector where many companies don’t pay out dividends at all.
Investors who own stocks that pay out dividends can keep the dividend payouts and use them as income, to invest in other investments, to offset taxes, or for other strategies.
Some investors utilize dividend reinvestment plans, which reinvest the dividend payouts back into the same stock.
The congressman disclosed owning $1 million to $5 million in Apple stock as of the end of 2025.
While it’s unknown exactly how many shares of Apple he and his wife owned, the $1 million amount would be good enough to own 2,824 shares today based on the current $354.07 share price.
Investing the 27 cents per share in dividends back into the stock would represent $762.48, which would be good enough to buy 2.15 AAPL shares.
