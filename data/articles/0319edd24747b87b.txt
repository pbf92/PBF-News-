Activist Extends Hunger Strike Against Anthropic AI Research
A protest against advanced artificial intelligence development has intensified outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters, where activist Elliot Callender began a hunger strike on November 23, 2026. He is demanding that the company suspend recursive self-improvement research, redirect substantial financial resources toward international AI regulation, and introduce prominent warnings about potential AI risks.
Callender announced the protest through his X account, stating that he would continue refusing food until Anthropic fulfilled at least one of his demands. The action followed demonstrations at the company's headquarters on November 19, coinciding with its public-market debut.
His primary demand concerns recursive self-improvement (RSI), a process in which artificial intelligence systems contribute to designing or training more capable successor systems. Critics have raised concerns about the potential consequences of increasingly autonomous AI development.
According to Callender's November 23 statement, he wants Anthropic to stop all research related to RSI. He has also demanded that the company allocate at least 80 percent of its cash and revenue toward lobbying for a bilateral AI treaty.
Another condition involves supporting an international pause on frontier AI development. Callender also wants Anthropic to display prominent warnings on its products, accompanied by explanations of instrumental convergence and arguments concerning AI-related existential risks.
Callender specified the wording he wants included in those warnings:
"might kill everyone," Callender said in describing the warning he wants Anthropic to display to its users.
He has also indicated that he could maintain the hunger strike for more than 40 days. If Anthropic does not address his demands, Callender has said he intends to organize a similar protest targeting OpenAI. No such demonstration had been identified in the reporting reviewed.
The hunger strike followed a larger demonstration on November 19, when approximately 100 protesters gathered outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters, according to Agence France-Presse (AFP).
Four people were arrested after demonstrators blocked the building's entrance, according to reports from KQED and KTVU. Police confirmed that arrests had occurred but could not verify the exact number or charges while the investigation remained active, KQED reported.
Demonstrations also took place in New York on the same day, according to AFP. Participants raised concerns about AI safety, corporate influence, and the potential catastrophic consequences of increasingly autonomous artificial intelligence systems.
Anthropic has disputed the suggestion that its current research has reached the stage that protesters fear. The company told AFP that it had not yet reached the recursive self-improvement stage.
The distinction is central to the disagreement between the company and its critics. While activists are raising concerns about the future capabilities and potential risks of advanced AI, their claims do not establish that Anthropic has already developed autonomous recursive self-improvement capabilities.
Callender has also expanded his campaign to California's state government. He has encouraged residents to contact Governor Gavin Newsom and request a special legislative session addressing recursive self-improvement and its potential risks.
No response from Newsom's office or announcement of a scheduled legislative session was identified in the reporting reviewed. Similarly, no public response from Anthropic addressing Callender's specific hunger-strike demands had been identified.
Information about Callender's health condition and whether he is receiving medical supervision during the protest was also unavailable in the reporting reviewed.
