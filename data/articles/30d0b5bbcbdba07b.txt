TITLE: Exercise Testing Helps Detect Hidden Symptoms in Severe Aortic Stenosis, suggests study
OUTLET: Medical Dialogues

Exercise Testing Helps Detect Hidden Symptoms in Severe Aortic Stenosis, suggests study
A new study published in the Journal of American Medical Association showed that treadmill stress testing (TST) was shown to be safe and effective in finding symptoms and indications for aortic valve replacement (AVR) in around 15% of individuals with asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis (AS).
Exercise stress testing is advised for individuals with asymptomatic severe AS to help identify symptoms and guide therapeutic scheduling, although it is seldom used in clinical practice. This registry-based follow-up to the Evaluation of TAVR Compared to Surveillance for Patients With Asymptomatic Severe Aortic Stenosis (EARLY TAVR) trial examines how TST, which was used during EARLY TAVR screening to confirm asymptomatic status, influenced subsequent AVR and clinical outcomes.
The EARLY TAVR trial's prespecified TST registry included 75 clinical sites nationwide. Standardized TST was performed on individuals with severe AS who seemed to be asymptomatic between July 2017 and December 2021. While individuals with positive TST values were asked to sign up for a prospective registry and were monitored for two years, those with normal TST results were randomly assigned to either clinical monitoring or transcatheter aortic valve replacement. 962 of the 1250 patients that were screened satisfied the trial's requirements. Of them, 146 (15.2%) got a positive TST test and 816 (84.8%) had a normal TST result. 105 of them agreed to sign up for the EARLY TAVR Treadmill Registry.
80 participants (76.2%) were male, and the mean (SD) age of the 105 patients included in this research was 76.1 (6.5) years. With no fatalities, syncope, or cardioversions recorded, TST was determined to be safe. Higher peak velocity, poorer ejection fraction, prior coronary artery bypass, and prior stroke were multivariable baseline predictors of a positive TST.
At one and two years, the rates of AVR among patients with positive TST findings were 79.9% and 85.9%, respectively. Patients with a class I indication for AVR (symptoms during testing) and those with a class IIa indication (reduction in systolic blood pressure) had comparable rates of death and AVR. Among patients with asymptomatic severe AS, using a TST to unmask symptoms or poor hemodynamic or electrical response was shown to be safe and revealed a significant proportion of patients (1 of 6) with a proven AVR indication.
