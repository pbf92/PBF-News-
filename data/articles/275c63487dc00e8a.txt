TITLE: Mauricio Pochettino offers one-word USMNT endorsement for Pep Guardiola
OUTLET: New York Post

CINCINNATI, Ohio — In mid-September, a few days before opening a two-week camp, some of the U.S. men’s national team’s top brass attended the NYCFC-Red Bulls match at Yankee Stadium alongside Pep Guardiola.
They sat, in order: assistant coach Jesús Pérez, Mauricio Pochettino, U.S. Soccer Federation CEO JT Batson, then Guardiola.
“You know what I noticed?” Pochettino recalled of Guardiola, half-joking, half-not, to a small group of reporters Monday, including The Post. “Good relationship with JT.”
Guardiola’s name, of course, has already been prominent in headlines as a potential successor to Pochettino, who will coach his last game with the national team Tuesday night against Haiti before taking charge of Manchester United. Before any coaching search has properly gotten underway, reports have indicated both that the U.S. Soccer Federation is likely to reach out to Guardiola, and that the legendary manager is likely to decline their approach.
What advice would Pochettino give Guardiola, should he — or anyone else, for that matter — ring him up at some point over the coming months to ask about the job?
“Take. Take,” Pochettino said. “If someone asked me — take. Like Pep, take.”
In a wide-ranging conversation for more than an hour, Pochettino indicated mixed feelings about being a national team manager, admitting he missed the daily grind and the spotlight of managing in England’s Premier League. As for the U.S. national team specifically, though, he feels it’s a job anyone should want — and that factors like the new National Training Center and the team’s performance on home soil this summer changed the perception of the job among elite European coaches.
“It’s a perfect moment to arrive with the federation,” he said. “Everything is in the right direction. I think it’s, of course, take. Take because it’s really good people, good players. The players can fit from the start.
“… Now the vision from Europe, I promise, is completely different after the World Cup. Being head coach [of] USA now is more prestigious. If Manchester United — at the level of Real Madrid or Barcelona — is coming for the coach of the USA, you start thinking, if you are another coach, maybe you are not working, [like Antonio] Conte, or I don’t know — you say, oh it’s a great opportunity, because it’s not only the value to be coaching.
“If Pochettino, from Chelsea, comes here, [then] Manchester United. The vision is completely different. It’s because the job was good, and people start to pay attention.”
The USSF has plenty of time to find the right man for the job, with under-23 coach Steve Cherundolo set to lead an MLS-heavy December camp and the next international window not until March. Interesting as it is to debate now whether Pochettino’s tenure was a success, the answer could change dramatically based on how the next person does.
If the U.S. sweeps through Concacaf, performs well at the Copa América in two summers and utilizes the young talent Pochettino brought in over his last two windows for a good showing at the 2030 World Cup, he’ll be viewed as having given his successor a platform to build on.
If the team flops, the question of what two years under Pochettino ultimately bought the program will prove prescient.
“I think [we want] somebody who’s just as ambitious as Mauricio was,” center back Chris Richards said. “I think that was one thing he really came into the federation with, was ambition. A lot of goals for not just this team, but also what the future of this team looks like.”
That ambition, everyone agrees, needs to be part of what Pochettino’s successor brings.
So too, Weston McKennie said, must be the central foundation of Pochettino’s culture: that no one is entitled to be on the roster.
“He made it very clear that it doesn’t matter who you are or what you’ve done for this team already. Your spot is still something you have to earn every camp and every game,” McKennie said. “When the next coach comes in, I think that’s gonna be a big thing as well, to not let players get complacent, ’cause playing for the national team is an honor. It’s not something that’s given.
“I think between the players, we can keep holding each other accountable, holding each other responsible for the things that we do on and off the field.”
Building on that foundation, Pochettino and players believe, will be key for the next manager.
“If that has continuity,” Pochettino said. “It’s the beginning of the new soccer in USA. That can be.
“Of course, I can say that it’s very optimistic.”
