TITLE: Primary care clinicians favor harm reduction for alcohol abuse disorder but face barriers to prescribing medications
OUTLET: EurekAlert!

Primary Care Clinicians Favor Harm Reduction for Alcohol Abuse Disorder But Face Barriers to Prescribing Medications
Alcohol use disorder (AUD) affected roughly 28 million people in the United States in 2024 and is a leading cause of preventable death in the U.S. Despite primary care clinicians often being the first to identify AUD, research suggests that screening happens in only 2.6% of all primary care visits domestically. Only 3.6% of primary care clinicians prescribe medications to treat AUD. Researchers interviewed 13 family medicine and internal medicine physicians who treat patients with AUD in urban, underserved populations across the San Francisco Bay Area.
Researchers found:
- The physicians generally favored harm reduction—helping patients drink less or more safely rather than requiring them to quit entirely—and saw an abstinence-only approach as a barrier to treatment.
- Naltrexone, which can reduce cravings and heavy drinking, was the medication mentioned most often.
- Some physicians said they had limited knowledge of or experience with these medications.
- A monthly injectable form of naltrexone was popular with patients but hard to deliver because of cost and the need for coordination and support staff.
- The physicians stressed that strong patient-clinician relationships and behavioral therapy are essential for medications to work.
Why It Matters: More clinician education, stronger patient-clinician relationships and better access to long-acting medications could improve treatment of alcohol use disorder in primary care.
Primary Care Clinicians’ Perspectives on Treatment for Alcohol Use Disorder: A Qualitative Study
