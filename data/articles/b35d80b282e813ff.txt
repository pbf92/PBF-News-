TITLE: Introduction: Name Above All Names
OUTLET: Christianity Today

Four times my wife and I sat together in a delivery room, gazing into the face of a new Waldron baby. What a moment! A first breath, first tears, fidgeting in wide open spaces after nine months in the womb. And now for a name. Their surname was a given, but what would we call them?
It’s not like you can swap a name out a week later. It goes on the birth certificate and follows that son or daughter throughout their life. As a result, parents typically name their children with intentionality. It is a way of passing on familial memory, signaling hope for a child’s future, and instilling in them a sense of belonging.
More often than not, names hold a lot of significance. From the earliest moments of creation, titles were given by God to all that was made: light, darkness, earth, and seas. The first man—Adam—was called upon to name the animals, preparing him for the gift of one like him, bone of his bones and flesh of his flesh. Adam called her “woman” because she was “‘taken out of man’” (Gen. 2:23). Then, together, they named their children—Abel, Cain, Seth—awestruck by the bundle of potential they held, just like my wife and I in the delivery room millennia later.
So, what is the name of the one who spoke all things into existence? And what does it tell us about our maker’s makeup—identity, attributes, will, and ways?
It turns out, our Creator disclosed many names, each revealing a facet of glory that he alone possesses. Altogether, they allow us to gain glimpses of his heartfelt desire to be seen, worshiped, and loved. In response to his revelation, not long after the biblical story was underway, we’re told, “people began to call on the name of the Lord” (Gen. 4:26). For them, the name of God and the person of God were indistinguishable.
To call upon the name was to reach in faith for the presence of God, who introduced himself in a surprisingly personal way. Despite the incomprehensible grandeur of all that our creator is, he tells his people, You can call me YHWH, the most common Hebrew name used for him in the Old Testament. Interestingly enough, Jewish people refused to utter this name in its original form out of reverence, a practice that can obscure the intimacy God seemed to be offering.
If we consider the many ways God has revealed himself through the vast array of names contained in Scripture, we discover that he is intensely personal, availing himself in all the aspects of life where we are most needy and vulnerable. He is our provider and healer, along with being the way, the truth, and the life (just a few of his many titles).
Throughout the coming weeks of Advent, you are invited to sit and reflect upon who our God has revealed himself to be, culminating in the one who “became flesh and made his dwelling among us” (John 1:14). May your time spent with this devotional prompt you to join believers throughout history who have humbly and confidently called upon the name of the Lord and put their trust in him (Ps. 9:10).
