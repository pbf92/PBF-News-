TITLE: Systematic Review Evaluates Radical Cystectomy and Emerging Alternatives in Bladder Cancer
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Radical cystectomy accompanied by cisplatin-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy remains the necessary standard of care for most patients diagnosed with muscle-invasive bladder cancer, according to a systematic review and international expert consensus published in Advances in Therapy. While surgical removal of the bladder combined with preoperative chemotherapy has served as the foundational therapeutic approach for three decades, recent oncologic developments have prompted researchers to reassess the necessity and timing of cystectomy. The systematic investigation sought to evaluate the current body of evidence supporting radical cystectomy and formulate clear guidance on when the surgical procedure remains indispensable versus when personalized, response-adapted strategies might safely be explored.
To establish these parameters, investigators carried out a comprehensive systematic review spanning medical databases including PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from January 2000 through January 2025. Following predefined inclusion criteria, the search yielded 147 publications deemed eligible for qualitative synthesis. The researchers coupled this literature analysis with a formal expert consensus process using a modified Delphi methodology. This consensus panel comprised seven urologic oncologists representing four nations—Germany, Japan, Thailand, and India—all concurrently affiliated with the University Hospital Schleswig-Holstein in Lübeck. Across three deliberative rounds, the expert panel operated under a strict agreement threshold of at least 75 percent to establish consensus recommendations regarding patient management.
The synthesized evidence reinforced the foundational role of established systemic regimens prior to surgical intervention. The literature confirmed that neoadjuvant chemotherapy administered before radical cystectomy provides an absolute survival benefit of 5 to 8 percent, supported by robust level 1 evidence.
The evaluation highlighted critical advances in perioperative systemic therapy, specifically detailing the integration of novel immune checkpoint inhibitors alongside standard chemotherapy. This therapeutic combination gained formal regulatory approval from the United States Food and Drug Administration in March 2025 and the European Commission in July 2025, subsequently being incorporated as a new standard of care into European Association of Urology and National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines.
Evidence derived from propensity-matched analyses indicated that trimodal therapy achieves a 5-year survival rate similar to radical cystectomy in carefully selected patient cohorts.
The authors emphasized that response-adapted de-escalation strategies—particularly the biomarker-guided omission of surgical resection—remain strictly investigational. According to the expert panel, such organ-preserving protocols must only be conducted within prospective clinical trials supported by mandatory salvage cystectomy infrastructure. Looking ahead, the panel noted that future integration of molecular profiling and circulating tumour DNA monitoring will be essential to refine patient selection and determine who can safely forego surgery.
The authors also acknowledged important methodological limitations within their work. Notably, the systematic review protocol was not prospectively registered on the PROSPERO database, which the panel formally documented as an inherent constraint of the project.
