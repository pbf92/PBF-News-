Stablecoins in Financial Services: From Roadmap to Rails
Stablecoins are no longer only a roadmap item for banks, payments firms and asset managers. The harder question is how to make token-based settlement and treasury use cases work within real safeguarding, operational and regulatory controls.
A conversation hosted in 2 days at the Finance Magnates Summit London 2026 will move from potential use cases to safeguarding, issuer selection, reconciliation and settlement.
This session gathers banks, custodians, asset managers and fintechs with stablecoins already in production to talk through what adoption looks like in practice. At Finance Magnates Summit London 2026, the session on 24 November will examine the operational requirements behind stablecoin adoption, including safeguarding, issuer selection and settlement.
Stablecoins have moved from a subject of strategic interest to an operational question for financial services firms. Banks, custodians, asset managers and payment providers are considering how tokenised value can be held, transferred and used within existing controls. The session will focus on what happens when a roadmap becomes a real service.
The discussion will address safeguarding expectations, issuer selection and the practicalities of using stablecoins for treasury, collateral, client funds and settlement. Regulatory frameworks such as MiCA, alongside UK requirements, influence how firms assess the opportunities and responsibilities. Technology is only part of the challenge. Governance and operational processes determine whether a use case can be sustained.
For attendees evaluating stablecoin applications, the session offers a grounded way to separate potential from readiness. It will consider where use cases are already taking shape and which questions must be answered before wider deployment across payments, trading and investment services.
Join the session in two days at 15:15 - 15:50 on 24 November, and see what else the agenda has to offer.
The transition from pilots to everyday stablecoin use depends on operational detail. Firms must consider how assets are held, which counterparties and issuers they rely on, and how safeguarding, reconciliation and settlement fit into existing control frameworks.
For attendees exploring payments, treasury or digital-asset infrastructure, the session offers a comparative look at use cases rather than treating stablecoins as a single solution.
