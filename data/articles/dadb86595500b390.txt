TITLE: Cochrane International Mobility - Ellinor Lyberg and Hanneli Timothy
OUTLET: Cochrane Collaboration

Getting involved in Cochrane’s work means becoming part of a global community.
The Cochrane International Mobility programme connects successful applicants with a placement in a host Cochrane Group, learning more about the production, use, and knowledge translation of Cochrane reviews. The programme offers opportunities for learning and training not only for participants but also for host staff.
In this series, we profile those that have participated in the Cochrane International Mobility programme and learn more about their experiences.
Name: Ellinor Lyberg & Hanneli Timothy
Location: Sweden
Cochrane International Mobility location: Cochrane Croatia
How did you first learn about Cochrane?
Throughout medical school in Sweden, we’ve used Cochrane's interactive learning modules to learn about evidence-based research and statistics. It wasn't until we took part in the International Mobility Programme, though, that we really understood the depth of Cochrane's work and how far its global network reaches.
What was your experience with Cochrane International Mobility?
Dr Bruschettini mentored us while we were writing a research project during our third year of medical school. He introduced us to the Cochrane International Mobility Programme and arranged for us to spend a month in Split, working with Dr Poklepovic, Dr Zakarija-Grković and their colleagues. We took part in research on site at the medical faculty and learned about the ongoing projects at Cochrane Croatia. We were also included in a Cochrane Review Group, where we screened articles alongside people from around the world. It gave us a fresh view of evidence-based medicine and the researchers behind it. All in all, it was a very rewarding experience, and one we definitely would recommend.
Do you have any words of advice to anyone considering a Cochrane International Mobility experience?
Go for it! It helped us build valuable contacts and gain great experience, and it truly opened our eyes to the possibility of doing research beyond the clinic.
What did you take home from this experience?
A deeper understanding and appreciation of how evidence is graded, along with real insight into the work of Cochrane. We'll carry this with us throughout our careers, and we hope to take part in more projects in the future
