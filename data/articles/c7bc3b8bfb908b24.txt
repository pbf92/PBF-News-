TITLE: Google DeepMind’s Gemini agents cut estimated quantum simulation costs by up to 11.8×
OUTLET: EdTech Innovation Hub

Researchers used the Lean proof assistant to verify the redesigned circuits, which still require more computing power than early error-corrected quantum machines are expected to provide
A quantum calculation used to model laser-driven fusion could require 4,338 error-corrected qubits, down from 5,650 in an earlier design, according to researchers at Google DeepMind and Google Quantum AI. Teams of Gemini 4 Carbon agents found a way to reduce both the memory needed for the calculation and the number of operations it would take, bringing the combined resource estimate down by a factor of 11.8.
The collaboration, described in the preprint Discovering formally verified quantum programs with AI, covered five problems. They ranged from routines used in quantum search and arithmetic to simulations in fusion, photochemistry, and drug metabolism. For the scientific simulations, the researchers compared designs using a cost measure that combines the number of error-corrected qubits with the count of a particular quantum operation.
How the researchers checked circuits they could not run
The researchers started by writing specifications that defined what each program had to do and how its computing cost would be counted. The agents then proposed circuits and produced mathematical proofs that their designs met those requirements. Lean checked the proofs, while a separate verification step confirmed that each submitted result held against the original specification.
The agents also shared verified solutions and unsuccessful ideas through a wiki. They discussed proposals at scheduled virtual meetings, where other teams could pick them up. The final photochemistry circuit, for example, combined ideas from three agent teams. The largest single improvement started as a proposal from one team that a second team refined and proved.
What changed in the simulations
The fusion calculation estimates how quickly charged particles lose energy in the fuel used for inertial confinement fusion. In photochemistry, the agents redesigned a circuit for simulating the behavior of a light-sensitive molecule. That brought the estimated computing cost down by a factor of 8.22.
For a calculation involving cytochrome P450, an enzyme involved in breaking down medicines, the estimated cost fell by a factor of 2.49. The new circuit would use 333 error-corrected qubits, compared with 481 in the baseline.
The paper also reports that a parallel implementation of Grover’s search algorithm could reduce the expected number of query rounds by a factor of up to 2.57. For an arcsine routine used to prepare quantum states, the researchers report a roughly 290-fold reduction in estimated computing cost. That figure applies to the arithmetic routine.
Google DeepMind says the scientific simulations still exceed the projected resources of first-generation error-corrected quantum computers. The team has released the Lean library, problem specifications, and verified solutions on GitHub so other researchers can inspect the proofs and build on the work.
