'Dangerous' warning issued to pilot before Mississippi plane crash killed family before Thanksgiving
Pilot Brent Stokes, 50, received a warning from air traffic control of heavy rains and adverse weather
An air traffic controller issued a chilling warning to a pilot flying his family home from a fishing trip in Mississippi moments before he fatally crashed into the sea.
Pilot Brent Stokes, 50, was alerted to treacherous weather conditions before his plane plummeted into the waves near Horn Island on Tuesday afternoon.
The father was at the controls of a single-engine Cessna 210L, carrying his son Hunter Stokes, 16, sister Arlyn Knox, 43, brother-in-law Michael Knox, 43, and their sons Charles Holden Knox, 12, and Truett Knox, 10. All six died in the tragedy.
Brent had been cautioned by air traffic control about heavy rainfall and dangerous weather conditions as he guided the aircraft along the coastline.
Authorities confirmed the pilot reported no difficulties upon departing New Orleans and never issued a distress call, according to the Daily Mail. However, officials revealed he had been warned of 'precipitation and adverse weather in the area' by an air traffic controller at Gulfport-Biloxi International Airport just moments before the crash.
Investigators established that the aircraft departed New Orleans Lakefront Airport in Louisiana shortly after 2pm, bound for Florida, before losing contact with the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) at approximately 3.45pm.
FlightRadar24 data indicated the plane was due to touch down just before 7pm at Fort Myers Page Field Airport.
The FAA confirmed the aircraft began descending at around 10,000ft per minute approximately 30 minutes after take-off. Approximately two hours later, the US Coast Guard confirmed the discovery of several pieces of wreckage, including the plane cabin, three life jackets and a life raft near Horn Island, though there were no signs of the passengers.
The search came to an end 'when divers from the Mississippi Department of Marine Resources (MDMR) located the remains of all six individuals within the fuselage of the aircraft. '.
The National Transportation Safety Board is heading the investigation into the cause of the crash, with the FAA providing assistance.
According to FAA records, the Cessna was owned by a Cape Coral-based firm called EAB Services LLC.
Brent was the president of a marine contracting company based in Fort Myers, the Daily Mail reports. According to the company's website, he was an 'avid fisherman and life time boater'.
Arlyn and Michael Knox, meanwhile, were the founders of Knox Technologies, a Fort Myers-based IT firm they established in 2012.
A statement issued by their families on Wednesday read: "They died doing what they loved: celebrating the start of a wonderful holiday season with a fishing trip together, before heading home to be with the rest of our family.
"In the middle of our grief, we are holding on to that, and to how much love they gave us and how much love surrounds us now. We will carry them with us always."
Turning Point USA, the conservative youth organisation established by political and media personality Charlie Kirk, expressed it was 'heartbroken' at the passing of Hunter Stokes, whom it identified as the president of its Fort Myers High School chapter.
According to the Mail, the organisation shared a tribute on X, stating: "Hunter was a young patriot who loved his country and led by example."
The Knox brothers were both pupils at St. Francis Xavier Catholic School. Its principal John Gulley responded to the families' tragic deaths by saying: "These children and their family have been a part of the St. Francis Xavier Catholic School community for several generations, and their loss will be irreplaceable.
"As a community and a school, we will endeavor to honor their memory and cherish their lives by continuing to "Be Like Christ" to one another every day and never forget the light and love they shared with us.'".
Gulley concluded: "In this time of Thanksgiving, we pray with thanks for our loved ones, and we pray for those suffering and processing this tragic loss."
