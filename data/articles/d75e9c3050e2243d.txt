TITLE: Non-Pharmacological Care Reduces Anxiety and Depression After Kidney Transplantation
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Non-pharmacological interventions designed to assist kidney transplant recipients with adaptation to everyday life were associated with reductions in anxiety and depression, according to a systematic review published in BMC Nephrology. However, the synthesized trial evidence indicated that these non-drug strategies did not produce measurable improvements in quality of life or fatigue when compared directly with standard medical care.
Adapting to everyday life after kidney transplantation requires recipients to navigate complex physical, psychosocial, and behavioral challenges. While diverse non-pharmacological interventions have previously been tested to support recipients during this transition, both their overall clinical effectiveness and their feasibility have remained unclear across outpatient nephrology care settings.
To evaluate these interventions, researchers systematically searched MEDLINE, Embase, PsycINFO, CINAHL, and the Cochrane Library for randomized controlled trials conducted in outpatient nephrology settings. From an initial identification pool of 11,303 screened records, the review team included 17 randomized controlled trials that encompassed a cumulative total of 1,208 participants.
The systematic review designated quality of life as its primary outcome measure. In addition, the research protocol examined several secondary outcomes, encompassing anxiety, depression, fatigue, well-being, all-cause mortality, and intervention feasibility measures. All identified clinical and psychological endpoints were assessed at the longest available follow-up period documented within each trial.
To appraise methodological rigor, the investigators evaluated the risk of bias in the included trials using the RoB 2 tool. Furthermore, the certainty of evidence for each outcome was formally appraised using the GRADE framework, allowing researchers to evaluate the robustness of the pooled statistical findings across the body of literature.
The pooled meta-analyses demonstrated no significant effect on patient quality of life, registering a standardized mean difference of −0.05 with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning from −0.48 to 0.37. The analyses also revealed no effect on fatigue, yielding a standardized mean difference of −0.60 with a 95 percent confidence interval from −1.28 to 0.09.
In contrast, non-pharmacological interventions achieved statistically significant reductions in psychological symptoms. Anxiety was reduced with a pooled standardized mean difference of −1.36 and a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from −2.46 to −0.27. Similarly, depression was reduced, showing a standardized mean difference of −0.93 with a 95 percent confidence interval between −1.83 and −0.03.
Despite observed reductions in anxiety and depression, the authors noted that heterogeneity across the trials was substantial. In addition, the overall certainty of evidence was rated as very low under GRADE assessments. The authors concluded that future research should use patient-specific core outcome sets, incorporate longer follow-up periods, and report feasibility outcomes to properly evaluate interventions supporting post-transplant adaptation.
