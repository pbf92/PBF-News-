TITLE: New Blood Test Could Potentially Detect CTE In Living People
OUTLET: 411Mania.

New Blood Test Could Potentially Detect CTE In Living People
A new blood test reportedly has the potential to detect CTE in living people for the first time ever. ESPN reports that two studies published in the Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) said that the test could detect advanced stages of the condition in the living, the first time it would able to be detected beyond a post-mortem examination.
One study was published on Thursday by researchers at Boston University and Banner Health. The study was based on a small sample of 21 former American-based football players — 12 professional players, four college players, and one high school player — who donated blood while they were live. The players were determined after they died to have CTE, and researchers detected a marker of CTE by examining the levels of a specific protein in the blood.
Boston University CTE Center co-director Michael Alosco, who was the co-senior author of the study, said that the test was a “game changer” diagnosing CTE and added, “I’m cautiously optimistic that we can really understand this finding and move it forward in the next five years or so.”
A separate study was published in JAMA last Friday by a research group from Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis, which was based on 11 cases. It identified the same markers for CTE. The tests did not help detect CTE in the earlier stages of the condition, only at advanced stages.
CTE has of course been a significant issue in professional wrestling as well, most famously with Chris Benoit but also with a number of other wrestlers. Earlier this year, Steve “Mongo” McMichael was diagnosed with CTE after his passing.
CTE is a degenerative brain disease that has been found in athletes who play in contact sports, combat veterans, and others exposed to repetitive head trauma. The disease causes violent mood swings, impulsive behavior, and depression.
