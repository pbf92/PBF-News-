TITLE: Two-thirds of Americans think major tech outages have happened more often than usual lately
OUTLET: YouGov

Two-thirds (66%) of Americans think major technology outages affecting services have occurred much or somewhat more often than usual in the last few weeks, the latest Economist / YouGov Poll finds. 53% think the technology outages in the last few weeks are related to one another, while only 20% think they are just coincidences.
65% are very or somewhat worried about major cyberattacks on computer systems in the U.S., including 27% who are very worried. Only 9% aren't worried at all. Adults 65 and older are more likely than those under 30 to be very worried (35% vs. 18%).
One-quarter (25%) think the problems with air traffic control that led to halted flights across the U.S. on November 13 were caused by a cyberattack by bad actors; 31% think they were not and 44% aren't sure. The Federal Aviation Administration has said the outage was caused by a malfunctioning router and that it found no evidence of a cyberattack.
47% strongly or somewhat approve of how the Trump administration handled the incident, 32% disapprove, and 21% aren't sure. 80% of Republicans approve of the Trump administration's handling of the outage, compared with 22% of Democrats. MAGA Republicans are more likely than non-MAGA Republicans to approve (86% vs. 68%).
74% rate the overall safety record of airline travel in the U.S. as excellent or good, similar to the share that said so in late March (77%). 85% of Republicans and 68% of Democrats rate it as excellent or good.
65% strongly or somewhat favor the Modern Skies Act, which would allocate $30 billion to airport upgrades and modernization of air traffic control systems, and 12% oppose it. 62% of Democrats and 77% of Republicans favor it.
