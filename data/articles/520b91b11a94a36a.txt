Anthropic Nov. 19 IPO: What Do Investors Need to Know Ahead of Nasdaq Debut?
Anthropic is set to list on Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT," with pricing scheduled for November 18 and trading on November 19 Eastern Time. Driven by strong institutional demand and sevenfold oversubscription, the final IPO price may exceed the $70 to $78 range to top $80 per share, pushing its valuation to $1.5 trillion to $1.6 trillion. Combined public and private fundraising could surpass $90 billion. While Claude’s annualized revenue run rate reached approximately $81 billion, massive infrastructure and compute commitments totaling $900.9 billion present ongoing profit pressures and fixed-cost risks amid intensifying AI model competition.
TradingKey - Anthropic is just one final step away from listing on the Nasdaq, but investor enthusiasm for the Claude developer is rapidly shifting its IPO pricing expectations.
Anthropic updated its prospectus again on November 17, with the filing maintaining an offering price range of $70 to $78 per share and confirming that the company has been approved to trade on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker symbol "ANT". According to the current schedule, the company expects to finalize pricing on November 18 Eastern Time and officially list on November 19.
However, the latest market reports indicate that the final offering price could well exceed the upper limit of the prospectus range. According to The Wall Street Journal, citing people familiar with the matter, Anthropic may raise the IPO price to above $80 per share, with advisers discussing valuation scenarios of around $1.5 trillion to $1.6 trillion. If finalized, this would be significantly higher than the fully diluted valuation of roughly $1.33 trillion implied by the original price range.
Subscription demand for Anthropic's IPO has neared seven times the number of shares on offer, with multiple orders reaching or exceeding $10 billion. Such a high oversubscription multiple provides the conditions for the company to price above its initial price range.
Brad Gerstner, founder of Altimeter Capital, disclosed on November 16 that his fund submitted a $5 billion order for Anthropic's IPO. This amount is more than five times Altimeter's previous largest order when participating in an IPO.
Early investors in Anthropic have also demonstrated a strong appetite to maintain their stakes. Venture capital firms including Bessemer Venture Partners, Menlo Ventures, and Accel plan to increase their investments in the IPO, according to people familiar with the matter cited by The Wall Street Journal.
This differs from traditional IPOs, where early investors typically trim their holdings and exit through the listing. Anthropic was founded less than six years ago, meaning some early institutional investors have held their stakes for a relatively short time. Meanwhile, commercial revenue from Claude continues to grow rapidly, making some shareholders more inclined to expand their investments rather than immediately cash out all their gains.
Compared with institutional capital, retail investor enthusiasm has been relatively subdued, yet to replicate the buying frenzy seen during SpaceX's listing. Anthropic plans to allocate up to 20% of its public offering shares to retail investors, signaling that the company still seeks to broaden its public shareholder base; however, institutional funds are still expected to dominate the IPO pricing and first-day performance.
According to the latest prospectus, Anthropic plans to publicly issue 825 million shares of Class A common stock, with approximately 801.35 million shares offered by the company and about 23.65 million shares sold by existing shareholders. Based on a price range of $70 to $78 per share, the size of the public offering would be approximately $57.75 billion to $64.35 billion.
Meanwhile, Google (GOOGL), Nvidia (NVDA), AMD (AMD), Broadcom (AVGO), Micron (MU), Microsoft (MSFT), and TSMC (TSM) affiliates have agreed to participate in a concurrent private placement totaling $26 billion. Among them, Google and Nvidia will each invest $10 billion, AMD will subscribe for $2.5 billion, Broadcom, Micron, and Microsoft will each invest $1 billion, and the TSMC affiliate will invest $500 million.
At $78 per share, the overall financing arrangement across the public offering and private placement would be approximately $90.4 billion, surpassing the $75 billion base offering size of SpaceX's listing and positioning it to become one of the world's largest listing financing transactions. Based on the same price, Anthropic's fully diluted valuation would be approximately $1.4 trillion; if the final offering price rises above $80, its fully diluted valuation could further increase to between $1.5 trillion and $1.6 trillion.
The filing updated on November 17 also explicitly disclosed for the first time the ownership stakes held by Amazon and Google. Following the completion of the IPO and private placement, Amazon is expected to hold approximately 14.7% of Anthropic's total share capital, and Google about 13.7%, with the two companies holding a combined 28.4%. If Amazon converts all relevant notes into stock in the future without regard to ownership caps, its potential ownership stake could rise to 23.8%.
On the one hand, this equity structure reflects long-term support for Anthropic from large tech companies; on the other, it highlights the deep ties between its business and its cloud computing partners. Amazon and Google are not only major investors in Anthropic, but also compute providers, sales channels, and commercial partners. Investors need to evaluate whether these relationships can continue to reduce the company's infrastructure costs and whether potential conflicts of interest could influence Anthropic's choices among different chips and cloud platforms.
Anthropic's rapid growth is a key factor supporting its high valuation. According to the prospectus, as of October 31, 2026, the company's annualized revenue run rate was approximately $81 billion, up by about $10 billion from the end of September. Enterprise customers expanding their use of the Claude API, Claude Code, and other products served as the primary driver of revenue growth.
However, the annualized revenue run rate is not equivalent to realized annual revenue. This metric primarily extrapolates usage-based revenue from the most recent 28 days and single-month subscription revenue across an entire year, meaning it may be affected by seasonality, short-term customer usage, and early rapid expansion. Anthropic also cautioned in its prospectus that this figure is not a substitute for GAAP revenue, nor does it guarantee that the company will necessarily maintain the same pace of growth in the future.
Compute spending, meanwhile, represents the other side of the coin that ANT investors must confront. As of the end of September and including commitments entered into subsequently, commitments for computing resources, infrastructure, and leases totaled approximately $900.9 billion, of which roughly $451.6 billion is expected to be incurred by the end of 2030, with about $70.7 billion coming due over the next 12 months.
These commitments ensure that Anthropic secures the chips, power, and data center capacity needed to train and run frontier models, but they also saddle the company with massive fixed costs. If demand growth for Claude slows, price competition among models intensifies, or more efficient technologies reduce customer compute demand, portions of the reserved capacity may fail to translate into corresponding revenue.
The public market also needs to assess whether Anthropic can maintain its pricing power amid competition from OpenAI, Google Gemini, xAI, Meta, and open-source models. Once performance gaps between AI models narrow, enterprise customers may deploy multiple vendors and shift workloads to lower-priced platforms, thereby squeezing Anthropic's profit margins.
