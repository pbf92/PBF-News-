TITLE: Amazon lets advertisers change bids by where and when a video ad plays
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon has added two video settings to the software connection that advertisers and their technology partners use to raise or lower bids on its ad-buying platform, according to a release note dated November 16, 2026. The settings, called videoPlacement and rollPosition, let a bid rule react to where a video ad appears on a page or in an app, and to whether it runs before, during or after the video a person is watching. Amazon's developer guide limits both to online video ad groups.
In Short
Amazon now lets advertisers who connect their own tools to its ad platform raise or lower bids based on where a video ad shows up and whether it plays before, during or after a video. This affects brands, agencies and software firms that buy video ads on websites and apps through Amazon, because an ad inside a video someone chose to watch and an ad in a small player beside an article can now carry different bid weights. The settings accept only fixed values and work only in online video ad groups, so streaming TV ad groups are not covered, and Amazon's pages do not say how large an adjustment can be.
What Amazon published
The entry sits in the Amazon Ads API release notes under the heading "DSP Bid Adjustments API supports video dimensions." It is short. According to Amazon, it reads in full:
"Customers using the DSP Bid Adjustments APIs can now upscale or downscale their bids based on where a video ad plays (in-stream, alongside other content, interstitial, or standalone) and when it plays relative to the video content (pre-roll, mid-roll, or post-roll). This enables customers to more finely tune their bidding for online video (OLV) campaigns."
A second paragraph points readers elsewhere: "For more details, see the two new dimensions -- videoPlacement, rollPosition -- in the bid adjustment dimensions guide."
The note shares its November 16 date with three other entries: a first-party session cookie for the Amazon Ad Tag, five Netflix attribution metrics in Amazon DSP reports, and a beta of Review Requests through the API. Some entries in Amazon's release notes appear before the date printed on them. PPC Land reported the Ad Tag cookie on November 14 and the review-request ads on November 12, both ahead of that date.
What the note does not include is equally plain. There is no country list, no mention of the Amazon Ads console, no example rule and no word on how large an adjustment can be.
Bid adjustments in plain terms
A bid adjustment is an instruction that tells a buying system to value some ad opportunities more and others less. An advertiser running a video campaign might, for example, want to pay more when an ad plays before a film clip someone chose to watch, and less when the same ad appears in a small player on a news page. Search marketers know the idea as a bid modifier, and Amazon's own endpoints carry the name bidmodifier.
On Amazon's demand-side platform, the system that buys ad space across Amazon properties and outside websites and apps, bid adjustments have existed since April 2025, according to Amazon's release notes. Amazon's documentation calls the building blocks of a rule "terms." A term names one or more dimensions, such as country, device type or deal, and the values that should trigger a change. Until November 16, none of the published dimensions described the video slot itself.
Amazon's overview page frames the feature this way: bid adjustments "let you adjust bids up or down for a specific criteria at bidding time for a single line item." The same page says they "work in tandem with Amazon DSP automated optimization" rather than replacing it.
The two new dimensions, value by value
Amazon's guide, titled "Bid adjustments dimensions," places both new entries in a category of their own called Video. Each accepts four values.
The wording in the guide is brief. For videoPlacement, the definition reads: "Where the video ad plays: in streaming video content, in a player alongside other content, as an interstitial, or standalone without video content." The values cell adds: "Valid Values: IN_STREAM, ACCOMPANYING_CONTENT, INTERSTITIAL, NO_CONTENT_STANDALONE." and "Values are case insensitive. Online video (OLV) ad groups only."
For rollPosition, the definition reads: "Whether the video ad plays before, during, or after the video content." Its notes are longer, and one of them sets a rule the release note leaves out: "Requires videoPlacement in the same term." The notes end, as the first entry does, with "Online video (OLV) ad groups only."
Two details deserve attention. First, the guide lists four roll positions, while the release note names three. UNDEFINED is accepted but never explained, so the documentation does not say which ads Amazon would sort into that bucket. Second, the dependency between the two settings means a rule cannot target a mid-roll slot on its own. It has to name a placement type alongside it, inside the same term.
Fixed lists and error codes
The video entries join a small group of dimensions that Amazon treats strictly. According to the guide, "The below dimensions only allow the following enumerations, in the exact data type specified. If anything else is provided (for example, String instead of Integer, or an incorrect value), the API will respond with a 4xx client error." A 4xx error is the family of codes a server returns when the problem lies with the request.
Six dimensions now fall under that rule. All six use text values.
For both video dimensions the explanation column says the same thing: "There are currently 4 valid formats. Values must choose from the list." The word "currently" leaves room for more values later; nothing on the page says whether any are planned.
Matching is forgiving on capital letters. The guide states: "All the string type dimensions are case insensitive matching except appId." So "pre_roll" and "PRE_ROLL" should be read the same way.
With the two additions, the full list runs to 21 dimensions in six categories, by PPC Land's count of the guide's table: eight for supply, site and slot (domain, appId, appName, dealId, slotSize, slotPosition, supplySource, supplySourceType), five for geography (country, region, dma, city, postalCode), four for environment (deviceType, deviceMake, operatingSystem, browser), one for ad format (adFormat), two for video (videoPlacement, rollPosition) and one for audiences (behavioralSegment). A downloadable spreadsheet, ADSPBidAdjustmentsDimensionValueReference.xlsx, holds the accepted values for the open-ended dimensions.
How a bid adjustment works, according to Amazon's documentation
Amazon spreads the mechanics across its release notes and three guide pages: an overview, the dimensions guide and a page on rate and size limits. Read together, they describe seven stages.
- Scope. The overview says adjustments are applied at the line item level and change bids "at bidding time." The dimensions guide, written for the newer API, speaks of ad groups instead. PPC Land reported on November 12 that in Amazon's unified reporting, DSP order, line item and creative become campaign, ad group and ad, which explains the two vocabularies.
- Create a rule. A rule is created with a POST request to /dsp/rules/bidmodifier. According to the limits page, one rule can hold up to 1,000 terms, its total payload can reach 1 MB, and its description can run to 1,024 characters. An advertiser can create up to 2,000 rules.
- Write the terms. Each term combines dimensions and values. Strict dimensions accept only listed values, rollPosition must appear with videoPlacement in the same term, and each dimension can carry up to 1,000 distinct values. For audience terms, the guide says segments must already be selected in the campaign's Audience settings.
- Attach the rule to ad groups. A PUT request to the rule's associations endpoint links it to ad groups, up to 1,000 ad group IDs per call. Separate endpoints list and validate associations.
- Update when needed. Since September 26, 2025, according to Amazon, the UpdateBidAdjustmentRule operation edits an existing rule. "Each rule update generates a new version ID," the note says, and associated ad groups "automatically migrate to the new version, while the previous version becomes inactive."
- Allow for delay. The limits page warns that the service "does not guarantee strong consistency for create, update, or delete operations within a 5-minute window." Since June 3, 2026, competing writes to the same rule return HTTP 423 ConcurrentModificationException, an error Amazon marks as retryable, instead of a 500 server error.
- Report the results. The April 14, 2025 launch note introduced a dspBidAdjustment report type in the reporting API. Version IDs, according to the September 2025 note, let a rule's performance be tracked in bid adjustment reports.
Bids, or predicted value?
One sentence in the overview complicates the release note's language. The note speaks of customers who "upscale or downscale their bids." The overview says adjustments change "the predicted value of the impressions, not the final bids." It adds that adjusted bids are capped by the optional maximum average CPM field when one is set.
The distinction matters. Amazon's automated bidding still sets the final price. A rule that raises the value of slots before a video tells the system those impressions are worth more; it does not fix a bid amount. How far that signal moves the final price is not stated on any of the pages.
The limits, in one table
Amazon's page "Rate and size limits for the ADSP bid adjustments API" lists the following ceilings. None is specific to the new video dimensions.
Request quotas are set per endpoint. Listing rules is held to five requests per second; creating, reading and deleting rules, and managing, listing and validating associations, are held to ten. Requests above the quota are throttled. Higher limits can be requested through Amazon's support page, with the subject line "ADSP Bid Adjustments API Quota Increase for [Limit Type]."
Five releases in 19 months
The video dimensions are the latest step in a feature Amazon has extended piece by piece. The release notes record five entries since the launch.
The launch note set the tone. According to Amazon, bid adjustments "support both simple adjustments and complex bidding algorithms through single and multi-dimensional specifications," so that advertisers "can now maintain nuanced and tailored bidding within a single line item, while still taking full advantage of Amazon DSP automated bid optimization."
The November note borrows its structure from July 2025, almost line for line. That earlier entry said customers "can now target specific supply sources, source type, and deals to upscale or downscale their bids," and that this "enables customers to more finely tune the parameters of their bidding to their specific use case and increase their campaign performance." The November version drops the promise of better performance and speaks only of finer bidding.
Where online video sits in Amazon's buying system
The phrase "Online video (OLV) ad groups only" carries weight because Amazon sorts video into more than one bucket. On June 11, 2026, Amazon said its DSP campaign management APIs support audio "alongside Display, Streaming TV, and Online Video inventory types," which PPC Land covered when Amazon DSP added audio campaigns to the API. Streaming TV is its own inventory type. Under the guide's wording, the new video dimensions do not reach it.
Online video, in this sense, means video ads on websites and in apps, outside television screens and social feeds. Amazon's own properties feed into that pool. An October 18, 2024 note said that from November 1 that year, online video campaigns would "automatically have all eligible Amazon inventory sources included," naming Echo Show, Fire TV, IMDb and Twitch.
