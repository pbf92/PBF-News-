Synchrony Financial Puts Lending Discipline First, Sees Walmart as Growth Opportunity
Synchrony Financial SYF is prioritizing lending discipline over faster loan growth as it navigates consumer affordability pressures, Chief Financial Officer Brian Wenzel said at TD’s Financials & Fintech Conference.
Wenzel said the company aims to maintain a loss rate of 5.5% to 6% and does not need to chase growth at the expense of risk-adjusted returns. He described Synchrony’s customers as resilient, while noting that affordability remains a major issue for many households.
“You just want to be measured at this point in time,” Wenzel said. He added that Synchrony looks at growth as part of a broader assessment of whether it is deploying capital at appropriate returns.
Credit Performance and Consumer Spending
Synchrony tightened credit after deterioration across the industry following what Wenzel described as an oversupply of credit in 2021, 2022 and early 2023. TD analyst Moshe Orenbuch noted that the company had previously discussed unwinding about 30% of that tightening.
Wenzel said Synchrony had adjusted its credit standards to return to its long-term framework and would watch how the broader economy develops. He said payment rates were strong and improving across credit grades and generations, while delinquency flows were stable.
Consumers remain disciplined about spending, particularly on bigger-ticket purchases, he said. Although discretionary spending may be below levels seen a few years ago, Wenzel said it is growing faster than nondiscretionary purchases for Synchrony.
Wenzel cited historical averages since 2021 of 7% receivables growth, a 3% return on assets and a 25% return on tangible common equity. He said retailer share arrangements, under which Synchrony and its partners share portfolio economics, help protect returns during weaker periods while limiting some upside when conditions are strong.
Walmart Program and Partnership Strategy
Synchrony sees its relaunched Walmart program as a multiyear growth opportunity. Wenzel said early adopters have included highly engaged Walmart customers, including Walmart+ members, and that the program’s 5% in-store value proposition has resonated with them.
He said the current program differs from the earlier partnership in its rewards, digital placement and connection to Walmart+. Synchrony exited the previous program in 2019.
Getting the product into the front-end checkout process is an important next step that could reach additional customers, Wenzel said. Customers can already apply in stores through money centers or QR codes. He said Synchrony believes the Walmart program can become one of its top 10 and aspires to return it to the top five, where it had previously ranked.
More broadly, Wenzel described competition in Synchrony’s markets as generally rational. He said the company is interested in partnerships that emphasize customer loyalty and are strategically important to the retailer, but it will remain disciplined on pricing rather than enter a price war.
Capital Priorities and Regulation
Synchrony’s common equity Tier 1 capital ratio was in the upper 12% range, above its 11% target, Wenzel said. The company has gradually reduced the ratio from the 17% to 18% range in 2016 and intends to continue approaching its target over time.
Wenzel said the first priority for deploying capital is organic growth in risk-weighted assets, followed by maintaining an appropriate dividend. The company can also consider acquisitions, portfolio purchases and share repurchases, subject to disciplined pricing.
Asked about the political and regulatory environment, Wenzel said Synchrony expects to engage with lawmakers and regulators and believes it can have a productive relationship with Congress. He also said he expects the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau to become more active, which the company does not necessarily view negatively if it acts within its congressional authority.
Preparing for Agentic Commerce
Wenzel said Synchrony is working on how its private-label and co-branded products can be discovered and used in digital wallets as AI-enabled, or agentic, shopping develops. He emphasized the need to verify consumers and establish that transactions are authorized.
It remains unclear whether agentic shopping will be led by AI companies, merchants, marketplaces or browsers, he said. Synchrony is pursuing several approaches rather than relying on one potential winner.
About Synchrony Financial SYF
Synchrony Financial is a consumer financial services company that provides credit products and related services through partnerships with retailers, healthcare providers and other businesses. Its offerings include private-label and co-branded credit cards, installment loans and promotional financing options. The company also provides consumer banking products, including savings accounts and certificates of deposit. Its financing solutions serve customers in areas such as retail, home improvement, automotive, health and wellness, and digital commerce, primarily in the United States.
