TITLE: Alibaba and Temu cut marketing as AI engines start handing out coupons
OUTLET: PPC Land

Friday, November 20, 2026 brought a quieter flow of news and a clear message from China's two biggest online marketplaces: the spending war is cooling. Alibaba cut sales and marketing costs by 38% in its latest quarter while revenue from merchants on Taobao and Tmall fell 5%, and Temu's owner PDD cut marketing by 17% as revenue stalled, even as its own advertising income overtook commissions. In the West, AI assistants started doing a job coupon sites have done for two decades, serving promo codes straight into answers a week before Black Friday. Google told advertisers that ad surcharges in France and Italy will rise in January and Brazil will get a new one, Google Analytics let marketers plan budgets by individual ad platform, and Google's head of Search said thin pages are losing their audience in AI search. Amazon Web Services moved fraud screening and device data inside its private auction network, Databricks lined up nine ad platforms to receive customer-value signals, and Brazil's privacy regulator named eight kinds of platform presumed to fall under its child safety law. Holiday advertising went back to basics: Coca-Cola dropped AI imagery from its seasonal spot and Meta pitched smart glasses to parents. The day's thread was retreat and redeployment, as companies pulled money from one place to put it somewhere they could measure better.

China's marketplaces stop paying for growth
Alibaba's quarter showed a company spending far less to keep shoppers and earning far more from computing. PPC Land reported that Alibaba's Taobao and Tmall merchant revenue dropped 5% as cloud jumped 53% in the quarter to September 30. Group revenue rose 10% to RMB271.5bn. Customer management revenue, the money merchants pay Alibaba on Taobao and Tmall, much of it for advertising and search placement, fell 5% to RMB75.0bn, about $11.2bn. Alibaba said it grew 1% on a like-for-like basis once a new business development programme, which books some subsidies as a reduction in revenue, is stripped out. PPC Land estimated that accounting effect at RMB4bn to RMB5bn.
The cost line moved further. Sales and marketing expenses fell 38% to RMB41.3bn from RMB66.5bn, dropping from 26.8% of revenue to 15.2%. The money is going into computing instead: AI Cloud and Compute Services revenue rose 53% to RMB61.0bn, adjusted earnings for that unit rose 121% to RMB8.8bn, and capital spending climbed 60% to RMB50.5bn, leaving free cash flow negative at RMB34.5bn. Management set a target of 30% to 45% cloud growth for fiscal 2028, but said it could not yet estimate the effect of a US export rule issued on November 16.
Alibaba's executives described a more careful shopper. "Consumers are becoming more cautious in their spending, and certain categories continue to face intense competition," said Jiang Fan, chief executive of the e-commerce business group, speaking through an interpreter. Eddie Wu, the group chief executive, pointed to a new merchant agent launched in October: "We recently launched QwenWork E-Commerce, and the percentage of paying e-commerce users continues to rise." He also warned about where AI applications are heading: "if all the apps are basically running on the same model, then those apps are very easy to replicate." International e-commerce revenue fell 8% to RMB25.7bn, mainly because of AliExpress and the European Union's customs reform, and Alibaba booked a provision for a EUR550m Digital Services Act fine tied to AliExpress. It disclosed no gross merchandise volume, take rate or advertising breakdown.
Temu's owner told a similar story from a different angle. PPC Land reported that Temu owner PDD cut marketing spend 17% as revenue stalled. Revenue was flat at RMB108.3bn, about $16.2bn, in the quarter to September 30. Inside that total, the balance shifted: online marketing services and other revenue rose 12% to RMB59.8bn, while transaction services, mostly commissions, fell 12% to RMB48.5bn. A year earlier, commissions had been the larger line. PDD has, in effect, become more of an advertising company than a commission business.
Sales and marketing expenses fell 16.6% to RMB25.3bn, and total operating expenses fell 12%, while research and development rose 16.8% to RMB5.1bn. Operating profit climbed 24.5% to RMB31.2bn, lifting the operating margin to 28.8% from 23.1%. Yet net income attributable to shareholders rose only 0.7% to RMB29.5bn, because interest and investment income fell 37.1% and income tax rose 64.8%. The release did not explain the swing between revenue lines, the tax increase or a RMB9.5bn nine-month loss under other items, and it gave no split between Pinduoduo and Temu.
PDD's executives played down the quarter. "Single-quarter metrics should not be extrapolated as a reliable indicator of future trends," said Jun Liu, vice president of finance. Co-chief executive Jiazhen Zhao said of the competitive field that "no platform is pulling back," while Lei Chen, the other co-chief executive, acknowledged the strain on sellers: "SME merchants are indeed facing considerable pressure and challenges." PPC Land set the results beside JD.com's 26% marketing cut and the European Union's duty and Digital Services Act actions against Temu. For Western ad platforms that have counted Chinese marketplaces among their biggest buyers, both reports point the same way: less money spent on acquiring shoppers, more effort spent on logistics, merchants and owned advertising.
AI answers start handing out the codes
The coupon business may be the next to feel the force of AI answers. Search Engine Roundtable reported that Google, ChatGPT and other AI engines now provide coupon codes directly in their answers, so shoppers can copy a code without visiting a coupon site. Glenn Gabe and Metehan Yesilyurt shared screenshots. Gabe said the free version of ChatGPT shows coupon cards with a "copy code" button, while the paid version gave codes without cards. "That's concerning... Then users wouldn't have to click through," Gabe wrote.
Yesilyurt described a ChatGPT answer to a web hosting coupon query that returned a card for each brand, Bluehost, Hostinger, Cloudways, HostPapa and another host, each with a discount, a price, an end date of December 7, a code with a Copy button and a "Get deal" button. One code, BLACKFRIDAYREDDIT, appeared to come from community or affiliate pages. Google's AI Mode listed codes as text with source cards from HostAdvice, Reddit and Hostinger, and Claude listed them as text citing Gizmodo and Cybernews. Yesilyurt summed up what is at stake for coupon publishers, whose model, he wrote, is the click: a visitor arrives for the code, the site sets a cookie and earns the commission. He was unsure whether ChatGPT's cards were a test or a rollout, and advised coupon sites to track which codes appear and to promote exclusive codes, and brands to publish current codes on official promotion pages.
Barry Schwartz noted that Google has shown coupon-style content in ads for about a decade, through promotion extensions, clippable coupons and loyalty codes, and that merchants already supply codes through structured data that can feed Search, Shopping, AI Overviews and agentic checkout in AI Mode. He recalled that coupon sites were hit by Google's site reputation abuse policy and asked whether withholding codes from bots could lead to penalties. With Black Friday on November 27, the timing could hardly be sharper for affiliate publishers.
OpenAI may also be reshaping its front door. Search Engine Roundtable reported that OpenAI is testing a card design on ChatGPT, a "try something new" panel that previews functions such as news cards, comics, event planning, meeting agendas, cooking help, travel itineraries, book recommendations and quizzes. Radu Oncescu spotted it on November 16: "OpenAI seems to be rolling out the new "Nova" interface for ChatGPT users on desktop (web) and non-logged-in," he wrote. OpenAI made no statement. A home screen that suggests tasks rather than waiting for a query would push more users towards the shopping and planning prompts that advertisers care about.
Google's head of Search offered her own view of what survives in AI search. PPC Land reported that shallow pages draw less interest in AI search, Google's Liz Reid says, in an interview on Patrick McGinnis's FOMO Sapiens podcast. Reid said interest is falling in pages that only restate surface facts, while content built on expertise, personal experience, community or in-depth research still draws an audience. "People don't want purely AI, they want AI in the web," she said. Her test for publishers was whether a human would actually want to read a page "or is it just a means to an end?"
Reid named preferred sources and creator follows as Google's tools for connecting publishers and users, said videos rank higher in India than in the US for the same query because of heavier voice and video use, and said AI Overviews were held to the featured-snippet quality bar at launch. She gave no traffic, click or revenue figures and declined to give her team's size. PPC Land set her comments against studies she did not address: a field experiment that found AI Mode users clicked through to outside sites 18.8 percentage points less often, research showing AI Overviews cut outbound organic clicks by 39.8%, and an Ahrefs finding of 58% lower click-through rates for top pages. Preferred sources passed 600,000 unique sources by August 20, 2026.
The search industry may soon get an address of its own. Search Engine Roundtable reported that a .seo top-level domain may be coming soon. XYZ.COM LLC, which operates .xyz, applied for it in ICANN's new round of generic domains, and it was confirmed on the final list after String Confirmation Day in October. Only one application was filed, so no auction is needed. Domaintechnik.at expects "a launch possible no earlier than late 2027 and open registration realistically in 2028." Schwartz reminded readers that Google treats new top-level domains like any generic domain, with no ranking benefit.
Google adjusts the bill and the plan
Google advertisers in four countries will see different surcharges from January. PPC News Feed reported that Google Ads is adjusting surcharges for France, Italy, Turkey and Brazil from January 1, 2027. The Regulatory Operating Costs surcharge in France rises from 2% to 3%, Italy's rises from 2.5% to 3%, Brazil gets a new 2.9% surcharge, and Turkey's falls from 4.5% to 2.25%. "These changes impact advertisers based on where their ads are served, not their business location," the report said, and the increases in France and Italy "reflect the costs for complying with Digital Services Taxes." Advertisers receive notification emails, and 2027 invoices will show the new rates. Thomas Eccel spotted the change, which PPC Times also reported. For a brand spending heavily in France, a one-point rise on top of the media price is a cost increase that no bidding strategy can optimise away.
Planning tools are getting more granular. PPC Land reported that ad platforms get their own line in Google Analytics budget plans. The cross-channel budgeting tool, in beta since January 16, 2026, can now break plans down by individual ad platform rather than only by channel group, so paid social can be split into Meta, TikTok and Pinterest. "Cross-channel budgeting in Google Analytics now supports source platform dimensions," Google Analytics said on LinkedIn. Projection plans track in-flight performance against a budget, conversion or revenue target, while scenario plans build future media plans.
