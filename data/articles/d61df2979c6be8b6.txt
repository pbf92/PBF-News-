Method for modelling SA4 level regional estimates
The LFS is designed to produce high quality estimates at National, and State and territory levels. It is not designed to produce high quality SA4 level regional estimates. The ABS models these estimates to provide further localised information on regional labour markets.
Modelled SA4 estimates are produced using a Rao-Yu time-series model. This model uses a real-world relationship between the administrative data and the survey estimates that is observed across SA4s. The model also makes use of the correlation of labour force status within SA4s and over time. The administrative data sources used include de-identified Single Touch Payroll (STP) and Pay As You Go (PAYG) data from the Australian Taxation Office, as well as Youth Allowance and JobSeeker data from the Department of Social Services. The STP data is only available from 2020, with PAYG data used for periods prior.
The PAYG source data used to model the employed series between 2012 and 2020 is subject to some small level shifts between financial years due to end of financial year reporting of job end dates. To account for this and ensure PAYG can be used as a predictor in the Rao-Yu model, the PAYG source data is benchmarked to state level Labour Force employment estimates. 
Modelled employment estimates where PAYG data is used will remain as a static series (2012 to 2019) whereas periods using STP data will continue to change month to month as a product of the time series model.
The employment and unemployment series are calibrated to the employment, unemployment and not in labour force totals for each state and territory (and Australia) published in Labour Force, Australia.
The modelled series produced by the Rao-Yu model is based on original survey data and retains seasonal variation inherent in the direct estimates. By combining survey data with administrative data in the model, the modelled estimates contain reduced noise compared to the direct survey estimates. Moving averages can help smooth short-term fluctuations to remove noise when dealing with data quality limitations. However, moving averages are not the equivalent of a seasonally adjusted series as both seasonal and non-seasonal variation can be removed.
Detailed methodology on the model is available in the Methodology Advisory Committee paper A Rao-Yu model for small area estimation of labour force statistics. Advice on using regional estimates, including the modelled labour force survey estimates, is available in the Regional labour market data guide.
