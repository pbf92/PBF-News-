These 3 stocks have emerged winners amidst the AI rout
An Oakland-based bleach maker, a budget airline and the brewer behind Twisted Tea have given investors somewhere else to look as the artificial intelligence (AI) trade unravels.
The Nasdaq Composite ended Wednesday, the last US session before Thanksgiving, down roughly 10% versus Oct. 26. Yet, Clorox, JetBlue, and Boston Beer have gained over the same stretch.
Their recent rallies have different fundamentals to lean on – from supermarket shelf space to first-class fares and better margins on fewer cases sold.
Clorox stock has rallied on a steadier product line
Clorox (CLX) shares have climbed nearly 28% since Oct. 26.
Cleaning supplies have an obvious appeal during a tech sell-off. Households keep replacing them, and demand has little connection to what cloud companies spend on data centers.
Clorox also reported that its overall market share improved in its fiscal Q1 – with newer cleaning products contributing to growth. The catch is that a good quarter for sales can still leave investors with a tight profit outlook.
Much of CLX’s reported growth came from its GOJO deal and a weak year-earlier comparison after a software transition disrupted shipments.
Management now expects adjusted earnings near the bottom of its $5.70-to-$6.00 guided range as costs rise. The next test is whether recent market-share gains translate into better margins.
JetBlue is getting more revenue out of its flights
JetBlue (JBLU) stock has rallied nearly 60% from its Oct. 26 close.
The low starting point helps explain the size of the move, but so does a tangible improvement in the air carrier’s underlying business.
JBLU said stronger revenue offset about 87% of higher Q3 fuel costs. In Fort Lauderdale, revenue per unit of capacity rose nearly 15% even as the airline expanded capacity by more than 65%.
That gives JetBlue’s turnaround some measurable progress, and its domestic first-class service, due to start Dec. 8, gives it another way to earn more from each flight.
Fuel remains a major obstacle. JetBlue expects an adjusted operating loss for 2026, leaving investors to judge whether the airline can maintain stronger fares as it adds seats.
Boston Beer is squeezing more profit from weaker sales
Boston Beer (SAM) shares have risen about 8% over the same stretch.
The brewer raised the floor of its 2026 adjusted earnings forecast by 50 cents in October, to $9 a share, and kept the ceiling at $10.50. The brighter outlook came even as Q3 revenue fell 3.2%.
Its gross margin widened to 51.7%, leaving the firm with more of each sales dollar after production costs.
Demand is the tougher part of the story. Shipments of Twisted Tea, Truly and Samuel Adams all fell, while Sun Cruiser and Angry Orchard grew. Better margins give Boston Beer room to manage that shift.
For the rally to carry further, investors will want to see whether the growing brands can make up for the volume lost elsewhere in the portfolio.
