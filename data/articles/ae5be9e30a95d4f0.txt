TITLE: Evidence Map Finds Acupuncture Research for Cancer Nausea Hampered by Bias
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive scoping review and evidence map analyzing hundreds of clinical investigations has characterized the global research landscape of acupuncture and moxibustion therapies for cancer therapy-induced nausea and vomiting, finding that widespread methodological shortcomings and structural evidence gaps continue to impede consistent clinical practice recommendations. While non-pharmacological modalities such as manual needling, acupressure, and moxibustion have been widely investigated as supportive interventions for oncology patients undergoing emetogenic regimens, existing clinical practice guidelines have long maintained conflicting stances. Some international panels have issued weak or conditional recommendations due to concerns regarding data volume and experimental quality, while others have endorsed acupoint stimulation as a feasible component of routine clinical care. To delineate the roots of these discrepancies, investigators conducted an exhaustive systematic appraisal of randomized controlled trials and systematic reviews published through late October 2025, mapping the distribution of study designs, target populations, intervention modalities, and evaluative endpoints while critically assessing the rigor of the underlying literature.
The study protocol systematically screened biomedical and clinical trial literature across seven major electronic databases, including PubMed, EMBASE, the Cochrane Library, the China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Wanfang Data, CQVIP, and the China Biomedical Literature Database, alongside manual searches of relevant reference lists. Eligible investigations comprised randomized controlled trials, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses evaluating acupuncture, moxibustion, or related acupoint-based interventions for nausea and vomiting induced by chemotherapy or radiotherapy in cancer patients with no pre-existing gastrointestinal symptoms from unrelated causes. Following the screening of 21,801 unique citations, the authors identified 987 potentially eligible articles for full-text inspection, ultimately including 574 unique randomized controlled trials and 47 systematic reviews in the final qualitative synthesis and evidence map.
Publication rates demonstrated a sustained upward trajectory over multiple decades, with primary randomized trial publications reaching an apex in 2014 and systematic review production peaking in 2021. Among the 574 included randomized trials, 525 studies accounting for 91.46 percent were published in the Chinese language, whereas 49 studies representing 8.54 percent were published in English. In contrast, language distribution across the 47 systematic reviews was comparatively balanced, with 26 reviews published in Chinese and 21 published in English. Prospectively registered trial protocols were exceptionally rare among primary investigations, documented in only 21 randomized trials or 3.66 percent of the trial cohort, with 553 trials failing to report prospective registration. Similarly, formal protocol registration was documented in only 12 systematic reviews, representing 25.53 percent of the synthesis literature, leaving nearly three-quarters of all published reviews without verifiable prospective registrations.
Patient sample sizes across the identified primary trials were predominantly modest, posing substantial statistical limitations regarding trial power and effect estimation. A total of 414 randomized controlled trials, representing 72.13 percent of all included primary studies, enrolled fewer than 100 participants, while 137 trials enrolled between 100 and 199 patients. Larger experimental cohorts were exceedingly rare, with 18 trials enrolling between 200 and 300 patients and only five trials, or 0.87 percent, enrolling more than 300 participants. Across the 47 systematic reviews, seven reviews synthesized data from 500 or fewer total trial participants, six reviews pooled between 501 and 1,000 subjects, and 29 reviews captured cumulative sample sizes exceeding 1,000 participants, while five reviews did not provide explicit aggregated participant totals.
Clinical demographic analyses highlighted severe imbalances in the oncology populations and treatment environments evaluated in primary trials. Adult patients formed the vast majority of trial subjects across 507 studies, or 88.33 percent of the trial base, with 504 studies omitting explicit age classifications and only three trials specifically isolating older adults aged 60 and above. In contrast, pediatric and adolescent cancer patients under 18 years of age were investigated in only 13 trials, representing 2.26 percent of the corpus, while 54 studies completely omitted participant age reporting. Furthermore, the antineoplastic therapy contexts showed extreme skewing toward chemotherapy, which was examined in 566 randomized trials accounting for 98.61 percent of the literature. Radiotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting was evaluated in only five trials, or 0.87 percent, and concurrent chemoradiotherapy was targeted in just three trials, representing 0.52 percent. Consequently, current clinical knowledge regarding acupoint stimulation in oncology settings is almost entirely confined to adult chemotherapy administration, leaving pediatric care and radiation-associated toxicity severely understudied.
Examination of clinical protocols revealed substantial diversity across acupuncture and moxibustion modalities and control designs. In trials evaluating single or combined intervention categories, moxibustion therapy emerged as the most frequently studied individual modality, evaluated in 140 trials or 24.39 percent of the primary literature. Other common modalities included acupoint application in 125 trials, manual acupuncture in 123 trials, auricular acupressure in 117 trials, and acupoint injection in 90 trials, with non-invasive acupressure evaluated in 66 trials and electroacupuncture in 30 trials. Advanced or specialized techniques, including transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation in 21 trials, acupoint embedding therapy in 13 trials, and warm needling in seven trials, appeared far less frequently. In terms of overall treatment architecture, acupuncture modalities were overwhelmingly tested as adjunctive interventions rather than monotherapies; 315 trials evaluated a single acupuncture modality combined with conventional Western medicine, and 96 trials tested multi-modality acupuncture regimens alongside Western medicine. In comparison, single-modality acupuncture alone was examined in 101 trials and multi-modality acupuncture alone in 34 trials. Correspondingly, conventional antiemetic medication served as the control arm in 476 trials, representing 82.93 percent of all comparator arms, while sham interventions were employed in only 47 trials, usual care in 40 trials, alternative acupuncture modalities in 37 trials, and untreated controls in 30 trials.
The endpoints tracked across the primary trial literature exhibited marked variation in measurement approaches and secondary outcome selection. Primary gastrointestinal outcomes were evaluated in every included trial, but the structural reporting methods varied substantially: 426 trials, representing 74.22 percent of the corpus, reported nausea and vomiting as a joint or composite symptom, whereas 134 trials reported nausea and vomiting as separate, distinct clinical endpoints, and 14 trials measured vomiting alone. Secondary clinical domains tracked alongside emetic symptoms included health-related quality of life, evaluated in 196 trials or 34.15 percent, and antineoplastic- or antiemetic-induced gastrointestinal adverse effects, monitored in 127 trials or 22.13 percent. Less frequently evaluated parameters included neurological or psychiatric adverse events in 68 trials, traditional Chinese medicine symptom scores in 45 trials, laboratory indicators in 43 trials, hematologic toxicities in 40 trials, and treatment acceptability or health economic outcomes in 44 trials. Adverse events directly attributable to acupuncture or moxibustion were recorded in only 47 trials, demonstrating that comprehensive safety reporting remains infrequent.
Independent methodological quality evaluations using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool uncovered widespread vulnerabilities across the 574 included randomized controlled trials. Overall methodological quality was judged to carry some concerns in 368 trials, representing 64.11 percent of the literature, and high risk of bias in 203 trials, accounting for 35.37 percent, leaving a mere three trials, or 0.52 percent, classified at low overall risk of bias. Domain-specific scrutiny demonstrated that the randomization process carried some concerns in 514 trials, representing 89.55 percent of all studies, with only 60 trials demonstrating low risk. Deviations from intended experimental interventions generated some concerns in 360 trials and high risk in 198 trials, with low risk confirmed in just 16 trials. In the measurement of emetic outcomes, some concerns were identified in 347 trials and high risk in 172 trials, while 55 trials attained low risk. The selection of reported results represented the most ubiquitous vulnerability, with 560 trials, or 97.56 percent, judged to raise some concerns and only 14 trials demonstrating low reporting bias, reflecting a near-total absence of prospective outcome registration protocols.
Appraisal of the 47 systematic reviews and meta-analyses using the validated AMSTAR-2 instrument similarly demonstrated profound methodological weaknesses across published synthesis literature. Forty systematic reviews, representing 85.11 percent of the aggregate review literature, were judged to be of critically low methodological quality, while four reviews were rated as low quality and only three achieved a moderate quality rating, with zero reviews attaining high overall methodological quality. Evaluation across critical AMSTAR-2 standards showed that 35 reviews failed to mention or document a prospective review protocol, 36 reviews omitted searches of grey literature sources, and 44 reviews failed to provide a comprehensive list of studies excluded during full-text assessment with explicit exclusion rationales. Furthermore, two reviews omitted risk of bias assessments entirely, three reviews failed to account for study-level risk of bias when interpreting synthesis results, and 21 reviews failed to conduct adequate investigations of potential publication bias. These extensive review-level deficiencies confirm that existing meta-analytic summaries have frequently mirrored and compounded the flaws of their underlying primary trials.
The review authors underscored several clinical, structural, and methodological implications arising from their evidence map, explaining why clinical guideline panels continue to treat acupuncture and moxibustion with therapeutic skepticism despite decades of ostensibly positive trial publications. The absence of standardized measurement tools and the frequent reliance on idiosyncratic, composite outcome scores prevent meaningful cross-study comparisons and pooled meta-analytic certainty. To resolve these chronic evidentiary deficits, future oncology trials must prioritize adequately powered sample sizes, enroll underrepresented cohorts including pediatric patients and those receiving radiotherapy, and adhere strictly to prospective registration frameworks. Methodological alignment also requires investigators to deliberately match control designs to explicit clinical questions, adopting rigorous sham controls when testing specific physiological efficacy under ideal conditions, and structured add-on designs when evaluating pragmatic effectiveness within routine clinical oncology workflows.
