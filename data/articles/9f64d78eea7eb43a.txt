Why tech stocks are up today
Chip and AI stocks are rising sharply on Monday after last week's selloff. Nvidia traded above its Thursday close at 1:02 p.m. ET, on a day when its board brought back Jensen Huang as chief executive.
Nvidia was up 31.11% at 1:02 p.m. ET, more than erasing its 14.01% fall on Friday. At 8:55 a.m. ET, its board said it had reinstated Huang as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately (Nvidia release). Huang had resigned 11 days earlier, on November 12, and Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, became interim CEO (Nvidia release of November 12).
The board said its committee of independent directors will finish its investigation into reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. Huang supports that investigation, the company said. StockTitan's article on Huang's return has the details.
Other chip stocks rose too. Against Friday's close, Micron was up 19.23% and AMD 8.23% at 1:02 p.m. ET, while Broadcom was up 2.09%. KLA, which makes equipment used to build chips, was up 14.00% at 1:02 p.m. ET.

CrowdStrike Zscaler deal: what Zscaler shareholders get
Under the deal terms, Zscaler holders would get 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share if the deal closes, so the offer's value rises and falls with CrowdStrike's stock. With CrowdStrike lower today, the offer is worth less than the $260.41 a share the companies gave at Friday's prices.
The two cybersecurity companies announced the deal at 5:32 a.m. ET, two minutes after their quarterly results. It values Zscaler at about $43.9 billion as an enterprise value, which counts debt and subtracts cash. The $260.41 a share is about 34% above Zscaler's Friday close, using CrowdStrike's own Friday close of $229.44 (CrowdStrike and Zscaler release).
At 1:02 p.m. ET, Zscaler traded at $226.72, about 3.4% below the $234.76 that 1.135 CrowdStrike shares were worth at that minute. That value moves with every CrowdStrike trade, so the gap changes through the day. StockTitan's article on what Zscaler holders get in the CrowdStrike deal explains the fixed share swap, which has no price floor.
The deal needs approval from Zscaler shareholders and regulators. The companies expect it to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, which runs from August 2027 to January 2028. Zscaler founder Jay Chaudhry and his affiliates, who hold about 34% of Zscaler's shares, agreed to vote for it. Because CrowdStrike pays in new shares, Zscaler's holders would own about 15.9% of the combined company.
Zscaler's 8-K filing at 10:01 a.m. ET adds two terms. Zscaler would owe CrowdStrike a $1.4 billion termination fee in some cases, such as its board changing its recommendation or taking a better offer. Either side can end the agreement if the deal has not closed by November 23, 2027. That date moves to February 23, 2028, if only antitrust or foreign investment approvals are missing then. A US government shutdown that delays the antitrust review can push it later, day for day, by up to 60 days in total (Zscaler 8-K).
CrowdStrike's third-quarter revenue rose 28% to $1.58 billion, and it raised its full-year outlook (CrowdStrike results release). Zscaler's first-quarter revenue rose 20%, and because of the deal it withdrew its outlook (Zscaler results release). The two companies set a joint call for 8:00 a.m. ET today, and Friday's market page covered the talks reports that came before the deal.
