TITLE: 'Current law doesn't adequately protect': Bishops speak on Conversion Practices Bill
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A Parliamentary Committee has heard about the lifelong trauma which people deal with because of conversion therapy.
The Bishop of Manchester told the Joint Committee on the Draft Conversion Practices Bill that there were cases of people within his own diocese still bearing the scars.
“These practices are ones I’ve long been a supporter of a ban on, and I think these practices are ones that current law doesn’t adequately protect people from,” Bishop Dr David Walker said.
The Bishop of Bath and Wells, Rt Revd Michael Beasley, said he also knows people who have been damaged by the practice but says he isn’t aware of any cases in his diocese since he was appointed four years ago.
“If it were, I would accept that to come under the full force of the Church’s work in safeguarding, because such practice is not acceptable,” he said.
Discussion largely focused on the wording of the draft bill, with the Bishop of Bath and Wells telling the committee that while it’s important the law criminalise coercive practices, the current wording isn’t precise enough.
He even expressed that if it passed in its current form, he could envision himself second-guessing whether he would have to stay silent when being asked for prayer and pastoral advice.
Professor Mark Hill KC, who specialises in ecclesiastical law and religious freedom, told MPs and Peers he also has concerns about the wording of the bill.
“The bill as drafted casts the net so wide that all sorts of innocent and ordinary fulfilment of religious practice would be caught and would be stigmatised as being a conversion practice,” he said.
Rev.Dr Helen Hall told the committee at a different session during the day that she doesn’t think pastoral care is threatened by the bill as it’s written, and an exception for pastoral care isn’t necessary.
“An ordinary conversation that somebody is just trying to chat through something isn’t trying to change or alter somebody. It’s not abusive; I just think it wouldn’t be caught.”
