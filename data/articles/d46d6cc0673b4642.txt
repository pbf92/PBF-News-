Nasdaq and S&P 500 Fall Ahead of Nvidia Earnings
Concerns surrounding artificial intelligence stocks and upcoming corporate developments weighed on Wall Street as U.S. markets opened lower on Monday, November 16, 2026. Investors focused on Nvidia's quarterly earnings and the anticipated market debut of Anthropic, two developments expected to test confidence in the AI sector.
By 9:34 a.m. ET (2:34 p.m. GMT), the Nasdaq Composite had dropped 362 points, or 1.5%. The S&P 500 declined 69 points, or 0.9%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 60 points, or 0.1%.
The weakness followed sharp declines in South Korea and Japan, where shares of companies exposed to AI-related semiconductor demand retreated. European equities moved slightly higher, reflecting their comparatively lower exposure to the artificial intelligence sector.
Nvidia shares fell more than 1% during Monday's session, extending pressure from the previous week. According to the source report, the stock lost more than 12% last week, eliminating over $500 billion in market value and pushing its capitalization below $4 trillion for the first time since 2025.
The company's leadership transition has added uncertainty to its business outlook. Former longtime CEO Jensen Huang was removed from the company's leadership following allegations involving extramarital affairs, including an alleged relationship with a Chinese spy.
Huang's departure has raised questions about Nvidia's future direction. The company occupies a central position in the AI industry because it supplies the semiconductors powering the technology boom and provides financial support to some of its customers.
Investors are now preparing for Nvidia's fiscal third-quarter financial results, scheduled for release after the market closes on Tuesday. Analysts at Vital Knowledge identified the company's growth outlook as a major focus for the upcoming announcement.
The analysts said investors would pay particular attention to whether Nvidia maintains its projection of 70% annual sales growth in its next fiscal year. The guidance could provide an important indication of the company's expected business performance.
Beyond financial results, potential changes to U.S. technology export regulations remain another concern for investors. The analysts at Vital Knowledge highlighted the possibility of significantly tighter restrictions on advanced AI semiconductor shipments.
The analysts warned that "investors should [...] be focused on (and worried about) the potential for Washington to dramatically strengthen AI chip export rules."
Concerns about enforcement have intensified following revelations that some Chinese companies can obtain advanced Nvidia semiconductors through data centers located in Southeast Asia. According to the source report, those arrangements allow access to technology despite existing U.S. export restrictions.
