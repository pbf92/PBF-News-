For the first half of the year, momentum was in technology growth stocks and AI Revolution infrastructure stocks. Semiconductors, memory and storage, engineering companies building data centers, companies powering, cooling, and making the racks for the data centers were all rallying.
But now, in the second half of the year, the story has flipped. The AI Revolution stocks no longer have upward momentum. There’s a new sheriff in town and it’s the consumer discretionary stocks, specifically in travel and retail.
The consumer is still spending. That’s good news for the consumer discretionary companies.
Where should investors be looking for the top stocks?
3 Retail and Travel Stocks for Your Watch List
1. Royal Caribbean Cruises Ltd. (RCL)
Royal Caribbean operates ocean and river cruises under the Royal Caribbean, Celebrity, and Silversea brands. On Oct 27, 2026, Royal Caribbean reported its third quarter 2026 results. Demand for cruising remained strong. 2027 bookings were pacing ahead of historical levels and at higher rates.
On Sep 23, 2026, Royal Caribbean announced it would acquire a 50% equity interest in the all-inclusive Caribbean giant Sandals and Beaches Resorts for $3 billion. The company is also providing a new year-round season in Europe.
Earnings are expected to jump 14.7% in 2026 after rising 32.5% in 2025.
Shares of Royal Caribbean have been stuck in a narrow trading range the last two years but are up 11.5% over the last month when the rest of the market has sold off. It has an attractive forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of just 17.1.
Royal Caribbean is shareholder friendly. It pays a dividend yielding about 2%.
Should investors keep Royal Caribbean on their watch list?
2. Abercrombie & Fitch Co. (ANF)
Abercrombie & Fitch operates 750 stores across North America, Europe, Asia, and the Middle East selling apparel and accessories under the Abercrombie and Hollister brands. On Nov 24, 2026, it reported its third quarter 2026 results and saw record third quarter sales of $1.4 billion, up 11%.
Comparable store sales rose 6%. The Comparables are usually one of a retailers’ most important data points.
Abercrombie & Fitch is so bullish it raised full year guidance saying it was “energized” by the momentum across the business.
Shares of Abercrombie & Fitch are breaking out again to new 5-year highs. It’s up 120% in the last 6 months. But the stock remains cheap as earnings are expected to jump 36% this year. It trades with a forward P/E of just 12.5.
Is it time to look at Abercrombie & Fitch again?
3. Burlington Stores, Inc. (BURL)
Burlington Stores operates 1,330 stores in 47 states, Washington DC and Puerto Rico. It sells products at up to 60% off other retailers’ prices in women’s apparel, baby, beauty, footwear, accessories, home, toys, gifts, coats, menswear, and youth apparel.
On Nov 24, 2026, Burlington Stores reported its third quarter 2026 earnings. Sales were up 8% after they were up 7% last year. Comparable sales were up 1% which management called disappointing.
Sales were on track in September but an unseasonably warm October dented sales. The company guided EPS down for Q4 due to higher fuel surcharges and freight costs. But Burlington Stores remains bullish on their longer-term growth trends.
Shares of Burlington Stores are off their highs and are down 0.9% for the month. But this is still outperforming the large cap indexes, which are in the red for the month.
Burlington Stores is expected to grow earnings by 25.7% in 2026 after growing them 19.5% in 2025. It has an attractive valuation with a forward P/E of 19.9.
Should a discount retailer like Burlington Stores be on your short list to end the year?
