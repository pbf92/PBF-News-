TITLE: GTA 6 Forgot to Include the #1 Reason to Go to Actual Florida
OUTLET: Polygon.com

Grand Theft Auto 6's Leonida, the game's version of Florida, is a veritable cornucopia bursting with stories to uncover. You can pursue the central love story of Jason and Lucia but also go birdwatching, enjoy a game of pool, or even play multiple video games within GTA 6 itself. Consistent throughout all of these activities is Rockstar's signature satirical writing that takes aim at every aspect of society. That's what makes the rare omissions in the game so noticeable. When it comes to Florida, it's especially strange there's no sign of Disney World.
While Disney World is based in Orlando, spread across four theme parks and two water parks. The Walt Disney Corporation has an economic impact on the entire state of Florida that seems hard to ignore in a project of this scale. According to an Oxford Economics study from 2023, the company generated over a quarter of a million jobs in the state. To put that in perspective, the report noted that this accounts for one out of every 32 jobs in Florida. Disney World also generated $40 billion in economic impact across the state.
That massive economic footprint has legislative and cultural repercussions for Florida and has since the resort opened in 1971. Experts have noted how Disney, directly and indirectly, lobbies for and against political actions across the state to serve its own interests. A very public example of this was seen when the company brought a lawsuit against Governor Ron DeSantis in 2023. For better and worse, Disney World has helped establish the culture and politics of Florida we know today. That deeply ingrained history seems like a strange thing to ignore, especially in a game so clearly about the American dream and who is allowed to control it in Florida.
It's an absence that many players have noticed in GTA 6, to the point VGC asked Rockstar North's co-studio head Rob Nelson about it. Nelson responded by saying, "“It’s about whether there’s a narrative reason to spend that time to make a fully functioning theme park. We just figure out what we do need to support the story and support the feeling of being in Leonida." He added that “A theme park would represent a lot of work that we didn’t have, and we didn’t have a strong narrative reason to send them there.”
It is, of course, true that GTA 6 couldn't include everything under the sun in its representation of Florida culture. But leaving out one of the state's biggest players feels purposeful. What’s more, many of the optional quests and discoveries in GTA 6 don't necessarily have a narrative reason to exist in the way Nelson argues absolves the absence of a Disney stand-in.
This kind of omission, however, isn't new for Rockstar. The series' two most prominent settings are Los Santos (within San Andreas) and Vice City (within Leonida), direct analogues to the greater Los Angeles and Miami regions, respectively. These happen to be where Disney's two American outposts are, with the original Disneyland in Anaheim. Even in previous entries, an analogue to a Disney park does not exist. The closest the series has ever gotten comes in 2002’s GTA: Vice City, in which players can find billboards for Fun World that bear the signature Disney font and a picture of a fantasy castle. The Funland theme park does exist in GTA 4, but that is inspired by Coney Island rather than a Disney park.
Despite Nelson's explanations, it does feel there is an obvious alternative reason why Disney World did not make the cut for GTA 6. In his review for Polygon, Giovanni Colantonio pointed out that while the game questions why some people must play by the rules while others don't, Rockstar itself "goes out of its way to avoid lampooning powerful political figures like Donald Trump, but it’s not shy about dunking on rural rednecks and people living in the slums, struggling with substance use disorders." The Walt Disney Company is one of the biggest and most powerful entities in the world, not just in Florida. A satire of the company may not have gotten Rockstar in hot water with the house of mouse, but there is a chance it could have. From a business perspective, it might make sense to avoid the possibility entirely.
Even with that omission, GTA 6's Leonida is still teeming with interesting perspectives on almost every aspect of our current lived experiences. If you are still itching for something that takes a more critical look at Disney, however, check out The Owl House creator Dana Terrace's new animated series Knights of Guinevere. That is, of course, if you aren't too busy watching animated shows within GTA 6. Because, yes, you can also do that.
