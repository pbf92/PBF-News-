TITLE: Lufthansa Group licenses Platform 195 software for 72-touchpoint ad network
OUTLET: PPC Land

Lufthansa Group Media, the advertising network the airline group opened in September, will run its campaigns on technology from Platform 195, a London company that builds advertising businesses for travel brands, according to an announcement made today in London and Frankfurt. Oscar Bravo, a Lufthansa Group subsidiary, is licensing Platform 195's Uplift software so that Lufthansa Group Media's own team can plan, activate, optimize and report on lounge, web and Wi-Fi portal campaigns from one place.
The first campaigns on the new platform run in Lufthansa lounges from December. Advertising in the Lufthansa Group Wi-Fi portal comes later, as the group's onboard connectivity, already live on selected flights, spreads across around 850 aircraft by 2029. The announcement carries no financial terms, no contract length and no revenue target.
In Short
Lufthansa Group has picked software from Platform 195, a travel advertising specialist, to run the ad network that sells space in its lounges, on its websites and on the page passengers see when they log on to onboard Wi-Fi. It matters for brands that want to reach Lufthansa, SWISS and Austrian Airlines travelers, because the group is keeping the sales team in-house on licensed technology rather than handing its inventory to an outside media seller. Lounge campaigns go through the new system from December, while Wi-Fi ads wait for a connectivity rollout that runs to 2029.
What the agreement covers
The deal is a license, not an outsourcing contract. Oscar Bravo is licensing Platform 195's Uplift retail media technology, and the release is explicit about who does the work: "Its own team runs the network, with Uplift technology powering it." Platform 195, according to the announcement, works with more than 40 travel brands.
Lufthansa Group Media brings together 72 advertising touchpoints and reaches around 135 million passengers a year across nine airlines and five home markets, according to the release, which describes the Lufthansa Group as Europe's largest airline group. Operations start with three of those airlines - Lufthansa, SWISS and Austrian Airlines - across Germany, Switzerland, Austria and the US. The network is "built to extend across the rest of the group," the announcement states. No timetable is given for the other six carriers.
Iva Strauss, Senior Director Loyalty, Airline Harmonization and Experiences at Lufthansa Group, set out the order of work. "We're starting where our passengers spend real time with us, in our lounges and increasingly onboard. From there we'll build out across the whole journey and across our airlines, so brands can reach travellers in a way that adds to their trip."
The executive quoted for the group on its advertising network holds a loyalty title rather than a media or commercial one. The release does not say whether Miles & More data will be used to target or measure campaigns.
The nine airlines named in the release are Lufthansa, Austrian Airlines, SWISS, Brussels Airlines, ITA Airways, Discover Airlines, Eurowings, Edelweiss and Air Dolomiti. The United States is not one of the five home markets but is among the launch markets; the 2027 plans in the release include lounge screens at New York JFK and Washington Dulles.
Lounges first, then the Wi-Fi portal
December is when lounge campaigns start running through Uplift. It is not when lounge advertising starts. When Lufthansa Group Media went live in mid-September, its materials already listed lounge screens for sale, as PPC Land reported on September 26: 59 screens in 30 German Lufthansa lounges reaching up to 590,000 guests a month, with a starting price of EUR 40,600 a month, and 32 screens across 12 SWISS lounges in Zurich and Geneva reaching 120,000 guests a month. Screens in airport lounges sit in the same family as digital out-of-home, where one play reaches everyone in the room rather than a single logged-in user. Airport screens have been sold that way for some time: JCDecaux opened a global programmatic offer across more than 3,000 airport screens in February 2024.

The Wi-Fi portal is the larger prize and the slower one. Advertising there will follow, according to the release, as Lufthansa Group Wi-Fi, "which it describes as the fastest inflight connectivity," rolls out across around 850 aircraft by 2029. The connectivity runs on Starlink. Lufthansa Group said on January 13, 2026 that it would fit its fleet with the service, confirmed on August 10, 2026 that it would be free for Miles & More and Travel ID users in every cabin, and flew the first Starlink-equipped flight, LH234 from Frankfurt to Rome, on August 19, 2026. The Wi-Fi already carries one commercial partner: Mastercard, which sponsored the earlier FlyNet service, also sponsors Lufthansa Group Wi-Fi.
That free-with-login model matters for advertisers. A passenger who signs in with a loyalty or Travel ID account to get online is an identified user, which puts the portal closer to the first-party data environments that retailers sell against than to an anonymous seatback screen. Whether Lufthansa Group Media plans to use that link is not addressed.
Timo Schulte, Chief Operating Officer of Oscar Bravo, framed the deal around speed and control. "Our job at Oscar Bravo is to give Lufthansa Group Media the foundations to grow quickly. We wanted a partner that understands travel and lets our own team run the business, so we can start with our lounges and add onboard Wi-Fi as connectivity rolls out."
The 2027 plans
The release lists two additions for 2027. Placements will come to the websites and apps of all nine airlines. Brand screens are planned for the group's new lounge in Terminal 6 at New York JFK and its refurbished lounge at Washington Dulles, "where premium transatlantic travellers spend at least 90 minutes." Brands and agencies seeking early access to 2027 inventory, including the first Wi-Fi portal placements, are directed to ambientmedia.lufthansa.com/en.

How the arrangement works
According to the release and Platform 195's published product pages, the operating model has five parts.
- License holder. Oscar Bravo, the Lufthansa Group subsidiary, licenses Uplift from Platform 195. Oscar Bravo describes itself as keeping digital marketing in-house for its "partners at Lufthansa Group."
- Who sells and runs campaigns. Lufthansa Group Media's own team runs the network. Platform 195 supplies the technology rather than the sales force.
- What the software does. The release says the team can "plan, activate, optimise and report on lounge, web and Wi-Fi portal campaigns from one place." Platform 195's website describes Uplift as covering the process from proposals to invoicing, in five modules: sales pipeline and campaign planning; omni-channel activation; asset and media management; reporting, analytics and invoicing; and cross-team collaboration.
- Initial scope. Lufthansa, SWISS and Austrian Airlines across Germany, Switzerland, Austria and the US, with the system built to extend to the other six airlines.
- Advertiser access. Brands and agencies contact Lufthansa Group Media through ambientmedia.lufthansa.com/en for 2027 inventory, including the first Wi-Fi portal placements.
Platform 195's product pages describe two layers. Uplift Ads covers native formats, sponsored listings, banners, video and in-store screens on a company's own channels, with search, geographic, contextual and behavioral targeting, server-side delivery, and CPM, CPC and auction pricing. Uplift Ads+ extends to connected TV, digital audio, out-of-home, paid social and programmatic display through what the company calls curated partnerships with media owners. Uplift is offered as a self-serve platform or as a managed service. The release does not say which of these components Oscar Bravo has licensed, or whether advertisers will be able to book Lufthansa inventory themselves.
Who Platform 195 is
Platform 195 describes itself as a travel retail media and full-funnel marketing specialist. Its Uplift technology, according to the release, "helps airlines, tour operators and online travel agencies build and grow retail media businesses across every channel," and its Amplify agency "delivers data-driven, creative marketing for travel brands." The client names on its website are mostly tour operators, online travel agencies and holiday brands, among them easyJet holidays, TUI, Thomas Cook, Etihad Holidays, Travel Republic and Hays Travel.
The company therefore works on both sides of travel advertising: software for businesses that sell media, and an agency for travel brands that buy it. The release does not say whether Amplify will place campaigns on Lufthansa Group Media.
Stuart Adamson, CEO and Founder of Platform 195, said: "Uplift is built to connect the whole travel journey on one platform, from the lounge to the website to onboard Wi-Fi. Lufthansa Group has built the team to make the most of that, and we're delighted to be powering Lufthansa Group Media as it grows."
Why it matters for marketers
The release places the deal in a wider shift, with airlines across Europe looking "beyond the ticket for new revenue, with high-speed connectivity turning hours in the air into prime advertising time." That shift has a US head start. United Airlines launched Kinective Media on June 7, 2024 and, ten days later, picked Magnite's SpringServe ad server to deliver personalized ads on its inflight entertainment system. Online travel companies have moved the same way: Expedia Group Advertising put 200 petabytes of traveler intent signals into Magnite's programmatic channels under a partnership announced on April 16, 2026.
Each of these is a retail media network built on something other than a shop: a seat, a booking, a lounge visit. The broader category, commerce media, now includes card networks too. Mastercard unveiled a commerce media network on October 1, 2025, and Mastercard is also the sponsor of Lufthansa Group's Wi-Fi.
Connectivity is the other half of the story. Lufthansa chose Starlink. Delta signed with Amazon Leo on March 31, 2026 for 500 aircraft from 2028, with free Wi-Fi for SkyMiles members. Faster connections bring more passengers to the login page, and in Lufthansa's case that page is the inventory the group plans to sell.
Then there is measurement, where travel media has no settled rulebook. IAB Europe opened a first draft of Travel Media Measurement Standards for comment on September 17, 2026, with feedback closing on October 23. The draft counts gross bookings before cancellations as sales and sets a 30-day lookback for display and offsite ads. Its working group listed Booking.com, Kayak, Expedia, Skyscanner, Marriott Media, Koddi and Sojern. No airline was among them. Lounge screens and Wi-Fi portal placements sell attention in a controlled setting rather than clicks on a product listing. Will Lufthansa Group Media tie that exposure to bookings, and under which standard? The release does not say.
What the announcement leaves out
The release gives no financial terms, contract length or revenue targets. It does not say which Uplift modules are licensed, whether advertisers get self-serve access, what data drives targeting, how results are measured, what prices apply on the new system, or when the six airlines beyond Lufthansa, SWISS and Austrian join. The passenger figure of around 135 million matches the Lufthansa Group Media website; the group's own media guide used 130 million when PPC Land reviewed it in September.
