TITLE: Anthropic and OpenAI claim breakthroughs on a 47-year-old fundamental math problem
OUTLET: Notebookcheck

Anthropic and OpenAI have independently released mathematical proofs addressing the same long-standing problem in computational complexity theory, marking a potentially major milestone for AI-assisted research. Both companies published their findings on the VP versus VNP problem, introduced by mathematician Leslie Valiant in 1979. The challenge is closely related to the famous P versus NP question, although the results still need careful examination by human mathematicians.
Anthropic quietly published its findings on GitHub, reporting a proof that VNP is not contained within VP. The work was produced by an internal research version of its Claude AI model, which also wrote the accompanying paper and a formal verification of the proof. Formalization translates a mathematical argument into computer code that can be checked systematically. In this case, the proof was formalized using Lean 4, a programming language designed for mathematical verification. Anthropic acknowledged that no human mathematician had yet reviewed the proof.
OpenAI published its own paper addressing the same problem on GitHub later that day. Its work also included a formalization in Lean 4, although the two proofs differ in several important respects. According to the upload timestamps, Anthropic's submission appeared approximately six hours before OpenAI's.
Both papers argue that VP is not equal to the broader class VNP. They approach the problem differently, but both claim to establish that certain mathematical computations cannot be performed efficiently using relatively small arithmetic circuits. Their formal proofs provide a way to check the logical steps of the arguments, although the mathematical claims still need independent scrutiny.
The goal of the research is to establish that some computational problems are fundamentally harder than others and cannot be solved efficiently, regardless of how clever an algorithm might be. This is considerably more difficult than demonstrating that a problem can be solved quickly: proving that a problem is intrinsically hard requires showing that no sufficiently efficient algorithm exists.
VP versus VNP is an algebraic counterpart to the much better-known P versus NP problem. Both deal with the difficulty of solving computational problems, but VP and VNP describe complexity classes involving mathematical computations over polynomials rather than conventional binary logic.
The distinction matters because P versus NP is one of the most important open questions in computer science. It asks whether every problem whose solution can be checked efficiently can also be solved efficiently. A proof that P equals NP would have enormous implications for computing, optimization, cryptography and many other fields. VP versus VNP is a related, more structured problem that could help mathematicians develop new techniques for approaching the broader question.
Researchers have welcomed the AI-generated results while warning against treating them as an immediate solution to P versus NP. Tom Gur of the University of Cambridge described the progress as unprecedented, saying that decades of mathematical work appeared to have advanced within days. However, he also stressed that the mathematical community needs more time to study the proofs and determine how broadly their techniques can be applied.
Varun Kanade of the University of Oxford believes the results could bring researchers closer to solving P versus NP, but says new techniques will probably still be necessary. Ben Livshits of Imperial College London, meanwhile, noted that the rapid progress raises broader questions about the future of theoretical computer science and the role of AI in mathematical research.
The two announcements follow other recent claims of major mathematical breakthroughs involving AI, including a reported proof of the Riemann hypothesis. Together, they highlight how AI systems are beginning to tackle problems that have challenged mathematicians for decades. Still, independent verification will be essential before these results can be considered established mathematical breakthroughs.
