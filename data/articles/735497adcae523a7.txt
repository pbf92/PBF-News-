Companies issue bonds to raise capital without giving up ownership, as they would by selling shares. By issuing bonds, a company borrows money and agrees to pay interest (coupons) and repay the principal at a fixed maturity date.
Businesses use bond financing to:
– Fund growth (expansion, acquisitions, new projects)
– Refinance existing debt at better rates
– Manage cash flow without issuing new shares
Compared to stocks, bonds allow MINISO Group Holding Ltd. to access large amounts of capital while maintaining full control of the business.
Bonds and stocks are two ways to invest in MINISO Group Holding Ltd., but they work very differently.
Here are the key differences:
– Ownership vs. lending: Stocks represent ownership in MINISO Group Holding Ltd., while bonds are a loan you give to the company
– Returns: Stocks may offer dividends and capital gains, while bonds pay fixed interest (coupon) and return principal at maturity
– Risk: Stocks are generally more volatile and can lose value, whereas bonds are considered lower risk
– Priority: Bondholders are paid before shareholders if the company goes bankrupt

– Coupon frequency: Semi-annual
– Based on face value: Calculated from the bond's face value, not its market price

A bond rating is an assessment of an issuer's creditworthiness — its ability to repay debt and make interest payments on time.
Ratings are typically assigned by credit rating agencies (e.g., S&P, Fitch) based on factors such as financial strength, debt levels, and economic conditions.
Common rating scale:
– Investment grade: AAA to BBB (lower risk)
– Speculative (high-yield): BB and below (higher risk, higher potential return)
Ratings help investors evaluate default risk and can influence bond yields — lower ratings usually mean higher yields.
The price of MINISO Group Holding Ltd. bonds changes based on the following factors:
– Interest rates: When rates rise, bond prices usually fall — and vice versa
– Credit quality: Changes in MINISO Group Holding Ltd.'s financial strength or credit rating can impact prices
– Market demand: Higher demand for safer assets can push bond prices up
– Time to maturity: Longer-term bonds are more sensitive to interest rate changes
– Inflation expectations: Higher inflation can reduce bond attractiveness and lower prices
