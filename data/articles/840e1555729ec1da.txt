TITLE: London train knifeman Anthony Williams guilty of attempting to murder seven
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

London train knifeman Anthony Williams guilty of attempting to murder 14 people
Anthony Williams was found guilty of 14 charges of attempted murder after a trial at Cambridge Crown Court
A knifeman who stabbed 10 people on a London-bound train has been found guilty of attempted murder.
Anthony Williams, 33, was convicted at Cambridge Crown Court today over the attack on an LNER train on November 1, 2025, which was forced to make an unscheduled stop at Huntingdon.
He was also later found guilty of attempting to murder three people in Peterborough on October 31, as well as a boy on a DLR train approaching Pontoon Dock station in London in the early hours of November 1. Williams had already admitted wounding 12 victims with intent to cause serious bodily harm and attempting to do so to two people in Peterborough.
During the trial, jurors were shown CCTV footage of passenger Stephen Crean attempting to wrestle a knife from Williams during the train attack. Prosecutor Christopher Paxton KC told the court that Mr Crean recalled Williams asking him: "Do you want to die?"
Passengers desperately tried to defend themselves as the attack unfolded, with some using frying pans and toasters as shields. Others hid in locked toilets, store cupboards or beneath seats, the court heard. Train crew member Samir Zitouni was stabbed multiple times after attempting to stop Williams with a frying pan.
Medics treating him for "catastrophic blood loss" feared he might "stop breathing", jurors heard. Another passenger, Kevin Deely, suffered stab wounds to his scalp and midriff. One of the injuries penetrated his stomach by more than 9cm, the court was told.
Mr Paxton said: "The defendant, Mr Williams, armed with a knife, caused terror and fear on an unimaginable scale, moving up and down a busy train, from which there was no escape, stabbing several people as he did so."
During the trial, jurors heard that psychiatrists instructed by both the prosecution and defence agreed Williams was suffering from acute psychosis caused by schizophrenia at the time of the attacks. The jury was tasked with deciding whether Williams intended to kill each of the alleged victims.
Summing up the evidence, Mr Justice Calver KC told jurors that a "person who is mentally ill, acting psychotically under a delusion, can still form an intent to kill".
Mr Paxton argued that Williams' alleged intention to kill was clear from both his words and the violence he inflicted. He told jurors the intention "lay bare not just in his words, some of which I've reminded you of, but in the slashes and stabs he inflicted not on the devil but on real people".
Defence barrister Gillian Jones KC said Williams had been "in the clutches of an acute psychotic episode". She argued his actions during the attacks were "consistent with a delusional belief he was under attack".
Ms Jones told jurors Williams "didn't intend to kill, he didn't intend to bring about death".
Williams was found guilty of 14 counts of attempted murder in total. He will be sentenced at a later date.
