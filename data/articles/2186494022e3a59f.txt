TITLE: Boujee Brits go mad for expensive beauty advent calendars, IMRG research finds
OUTLET: Retail Technology Innovation Hub

Since 2010, advent calendars have evolved from children’s chocolate products into high value adult purchases, with beauty now one of the most prominent categories.
IMRG, a UK e-commerce association which tracks online retail performance across more than £30 billion of online spend from 230 retailers, analysed beauty advent calendar performance among its top 15 beauty retailers that sell these products. 14 of the 15 retailers recorded positive WoW revenue growth in the week their calendar was released.
Earlier launches were associated with the strongest uplift, suggesting retailers may benefit from getting in front of shoppers before the market becomes more crowded with Christmas campaigns. Beauty retailers launching before 15 September saw a +97.2% WoW revenue increase, compared with +47% among late-September launches and +53.1% in early October.
The uplift was strongest among mid-sized beauty retailers, at +140% WoW, compared with +21.7% among larger beauty retailers with turnover above £100 million.
At an average price of £180, beauty advent calendars cost more than four times the £40 average beauty basket value. Release week ABV increased by +20.8% WoW, with some beauty retailers seeing increases of more than £80.
Beauty only retailers also outperformed multi-category retailers. Revenue growth was +84.8% compared with +69.1%, while ABV growth was +38% compared with +15% - differences of 15.7 and 23 percentage points respectively.
Andy Mulcahy, Strategy & Insight Director at IMRG, comments: “It can be difficult to predict how products will evolve, and which will really capture the attention of UK shoppers. The humble advent calendar, which used to cost a few pounds and exclusively contained chocolate, has mutated into a high cost, premium purchase - and customers can’t get enough."
"In an era of economic turmoil and suppressed disposable spend, this has been one area that people don’t appear willing to compromise on; they may cost £180 on average, but many of them sell out their ranges completely. Did not see that coming.”
