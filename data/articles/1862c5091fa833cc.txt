TITLE: GTA 6 Graphics Settings Leak: 4 Consoles, 0 Confirmed
OUTLET: shattered.io

A deleted Pastebin document attributed to GTAForums dataminer Tex2 reportedly lists GTA 6 graphics settings for four consoles. KitGuru reported that the deleted leak may have revealed Rockstar’s graphics settings for the PlayStation 5, PlayStation 5 Pro, Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S. Rockstar Games has not confirmed any of it.
MP1st, The Times of India, iXBT.games and GamesRadar+ also carried the story, which kept it circulating on November 27, 2026. The document is gone, so readers cannot check the original page. That gap matters, and this analysis treats every detail as a report, not as a fact about the shipping game.
The timing adds fuel. Players are still debating what a possible PC version could look like, a thread we followed in our report on the GTA 6 leak for PC gamers and its November build. A console settings list gives that debate new material, even if it proves nothing about PC.
What the Deleted GTA 6 Leak Reportedly Contains
The core claim is simple. A Pastebin document, since deleted, reportedly carried graphics presets for the PS5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S versions of Grand Theft Auto VI. It did not leak a build, a trailer or a story detail. It leaked a settings list, the kind of data a developer keeps in internal config files or debug menus.
According to the reports, the list covered texture quality, shadow quality, terrain detail, hair quality, global illumination, NPC behavior, pedestrian density and physics quality. Those eight areas span both rendering and simulation. Some tax the GPU, others tax the CPU, and a few hit both. A preset that touches all of them tells you how a studio balances the whole machine, not only the pretty parts.
The presence of NPC behavior, pedestrian density and physics quality stands out. Those options suggest the document describes the full game simulation and not just the visual layer. Open-world games live and die by how many people, cars and objects the engine can keep alive at once, so the CPU side of the list may matter as much as the shadows.
Nothing in the available coverage supplies resolution targets, frame rates or a full value-by-value table that we can verify. Outlets described the document in broad strokes, and we do the same. Any site that prints a complete table of exact values is working from a page that no longer exists.
Who Is Tex2 and Why Attribution Matters
Reports attribute the Pastebin to Tex2, a member of GTAForums who is described as a dataminer. Dataminers pull information out of game files, patches and builds. Their track records vary widely, and the strongest ones show their work. A deleted paste with no surviving original shows very little.
The attribution itself comes secondhand. Outlets repeat the name because earlier posts and forum threads pointed to it, and not because anyone has proven who posted the paste. Rockstar has not said whether the document is authentic, whether it came from a current build or whether it reflects an older internal state.
Journalists use a rough ladder for leaks. The top rung is a document the publisher confirms. The next is material several independent outlets inspected directly. The bottom rung is a description of something nobody can open any more. This story sits near the bottom, which is why the fact pattern stays modest: four platforms, a few named features and no official word.
Repetition across outlets does not lift a leak up the ladder. Five sites summarizing the same vanished page equal one source, counted five times. Readers should keep that in mind each time a headline treats a leak as settled when its own publisher has not touched it.
PS5 and Xbox Series X: Identical Mixed Presets
One of the more interesting claims is that the PS5 and Xbox Series X settings match. Reports say both consoles use the same mixture of medium and high presets. If that holds, Rockstar treats the two flagship consoles as one performance tier, which is how many multiplatform studios work when the hardware is close.
Parity of that kind is a design decision as much as a technical one. Matching presets let a studio tune once, test once and promise the same experience to two audiences. It also removes the argument over which console runs the game better, at least on paper. Real-world results can still differ because of drivers, memory handling and system software.
The medium and high mixture also tells a story. Neither console reportedly sits at the top of the stack. Rockstar appears to leave headroom above the standard consoles, which would fit a game built to run on hardware that launched years before it. A studio that ships on a fixed platform tends to pick settings that hold frame pacing steady in the worst scenes, not the best ones.
Tech4Gamers framed the leak as settings across all platforms, and the shared PS5 and Series X tier is the piece that most supports that framing. Still, a mixed medium and high label says little by itself. Without the underlying values, no one can say which individual options sit at medium and which at high.
Xbox Series S: Low and Medium Presets
The reported Xbox Series S settings use a mixture of low and medium presets. That places the smaller Xbox one full step below its bigger sibling and the PlayStation consoles. Few people expect the cheaper console to match the flagships, but a spread of this kind shows how much work Rockstar may have put into scaling the game down.
Microsoft built the Series S as a lower-cost entry point with less graphics power and a smaller memory pool than the Series X. Developers must ship on both. In practice, the Series S often becomes the console that sets the floor for a whole Xbox release. A low and medium mixture suggests the floor for this game sits lower than the ceiling by a visible margin.
Expect fans to read the preset gap as a quality warning. That reaction deserves some caution. Low and medium in a modern engine can still look strong, and presets often trade invisible costs, like shadow distance or particle counts, for stable frame rates. Players will judge by screenshots and footage, not by labels in a leaked list.
We covered the console side of the launch when we looked at the five launch issues Rockstar logged on PS5 and Xbox Series. Performance on the weakest console tends to drive support tickets and review bombs, so the Series S tier will draw the most scrutiny once real comparisons arrive.
PS5 Pro: Ray-Traced Shadows and Enhanced BVH Quality
The PS5 Pro entry is the most specific part of the leak. Reports say its preset includes ray-traced directional shadows, ray-traced local shadows and enhanced BVH quality. Those three items point to a ray-tracing-heavy tier that the standard consoles do not reportedly share in the same form.
Directional and local shadows
Directional shadows come from the main light source, which in an outdoor game is usually the sun. Local shadows come from smaller lights such as street lamps, car headlights and neon signs. Ray tracing both means the console computes shadow rays instead of relying only on shadow maps. The payoff is sharper contact shadows and fewer visible artifacts, and the cost is raw compute.
A game set in a sprawling modern city, with heavy night scenes and constant artificial light, gives local shadows a lot to do. That is analysis, not a leaked fact. Still, it explains why a studio might reserve this feature for the strongest console.
What enhanced BVH quality means
A bounding volume hierarchy, or BVH, is the data structure a GPU uses to find which triangles a ray hits. Better BVH quality usually means more detail in what rays can see, at the price of memory and build time. An enhanced BVH setting on the Pro implies that ray-traced effects can reach further or resolve finer geometry than on the other consoles.
None of this tells us what the Pro version looks like in motion, how it performs, or whether Rockstar enables these features in every mode. The sources do not list frame rates or resolutions for any console. They only say what the presets include.

What Rockstar Has and Has Not Confirmed
Rockstar Games has not confirmed the leaked graphics settings. That single sentence is the most important fact in the story, and several outlets repeat it. The company has made no public statement on the Pastebin, the attribution or any listed preset.
Read the bottom four rows twice. A reader who sees a screenshot of an exact table of values and a claim about PC requirements should assume it is invented or inferred. The available coverage supports none of those items.
The PC Question: Interpretation, Not Confirmation
Several outlets leaned into the PC angle. Club386’s headline said the leaked console settings show a lot of room for improvement on PC, and Polygon ran a piece titled as great news for PC gamers with a catch. Those framings are interpretations of a console preset list. They do not describe PC requirements, PC-exclusive effects or a PC release.
The logic behind the headline is easy to follow. If consoles run a medium and high mix, then a strong gaming PC could push settings higher, and that gap implies extra visual headroom. Developers do often add higher tiers for PC. But Rockstar has announced no PC version in our sources, and the leak contains no PC data at all.
The surrounding context is messy. Take-Two’s takedowns hit the Leonida recompilation project hard, as we detailed in our piece on the DMCA reach of one repo and 2,900+ forks. TechSpot reported that unofficial PC ports were already rendering in-game graphics despite those takedowns. Neither development reveals Rockstar’s plans for an official release.
Beyond that, Polygon’s coverage of the PC angle carries the same caveat as everyone else’s. The catch in its framing is the one we have stated throughout. Without an official PC announcement, a console settings list can only hint at what a future version might do.
Historical Context: Rockstar and Leaks
Rockstar has dealt with leaks for years, and GTA 6 has drawn plenty of them. Footage, files and fan-made reconstructions have circulated around the game before and after launch. We covered one thread of that history in our report on the GTA 6 hacker case and the judge’s warning.
The company has often answered with silence on the content and legal action on the distribution. The takedown campaign against the Leonida project fits that pattern. A deleted Pastebin fits it too, since a paste that disappears quickly leaves little to fight over. Whether Rockstar’s lawyers, the poster or the platform deleted this document is not stated in the reports we reviewed.
