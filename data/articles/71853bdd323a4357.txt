TITLE: Bending Spoons agrees to buy Mailchimp from Intuit for $3.68bn in cash
OUTLET: PPC Land

Bending Spoons today agreed to buy Mailchimp, the email marketing automation platform for businesses, from Intuit at an enterprise value of $3.68 billion in an all-cash transaction. Intuit will put $368 million of its proceeds back into newly issued Bending Spoons equity. The Milan-based company, listed on Nasdaq under the ticker BSP, expects the deal to close in early 2027, once regulators have approved it.
In Short
Bending Spoons, the Italian company that owns Vimeo, AOL, Eventbrite and WeTransfer, has agreed to buy Mailchimp from Intuit for $3.68 billion in cash, and Intuit will take $368 million of that back as Bending Spoons shares. The price is less than a third of the roughly $12 billion Intuit paid for Mailchimp in 2021, and the buyer says its usual approach to the companies it buys is a deep overhaul of teams, technology, marketing and monetization, which matters to the small businesses and marketers who send their email through the platform. Nothing changes until the deal closes, expected in early 2027 after regulatory approvals, and Bending Spoons says it will invest substantially in Mailchimp after that.
The terms
The announcement was distributed through Business Wire at 4:00 PM Eastern Standard Time under a Milan dateline. According to Bending Spoons, Bending Spoons S.p.A. has entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Mailchimp from Intuit, which the release calls "the global financial technology platform", at an enterprise value of $3.68 billion. The consideration is entirely cash.
The structure has a twist. Intuit is not walking away completely. In connection with the transaction, according to the release, "Intuit will invest $368 million of its proceeds into newly issued Bending Spoons equity." That reinvestment equals exactly 10% of the headline enterprise value, by PPC Land's calculation. On the headline numbers alone, Intuit would be left with about $3.31 billion once the reinvestment is deducted. That figure is also PPC Land's calculation. It ignores any adjustments for cash, debt and working capital, which an enterprise value usually leaves out and the release does not break down.
Both legs of the deal are conditional. "The acquisition, including the investment, is subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory approvals," according to Bending Spoons. The release does not name the authorities whose clearance is required.
The legal mechanics take a single sentence. Under the terms of the agreement, "which was unanimously approved by the board of directors of Bending Spoons, Bending Spoons will acquire the Mailchimp business by purchasing 100% of The Rocket Science Group, LLC and other assets and liabilities of the Mailchimp business." The Rocket Science Group is the legal entity that operates Mailchimp. The release does not list the other assets and liabilities that move with it. Nor does it say whether Intuit's board had to approve the sale, or describe any vote outside Bending Spoons' own board.
From $12 billion to $3.68 billion
The price is the headline that will travel. Intuit bought Mailchimp in 2021 "for the sum of $12 billion in cash and stocks", according to EmailTooltester, an email software review site run by the Tooltester Network. Set against that figure, the new enterprise value of $3.68 billion is about 31% of what Intuit paid, a gap of roughly $8.32 billion. Both numbers are PPC Land calculations, and they come with caveats. An enterprise value agreed in 2026 and a 2021 purchase price paid partly in Intuit stock are different measures. Intuit's share price has moved over five years. And neither the release nor any statement from Intuit inside it says whether Intuit expects to book a gain or a loss.
What was Mailchimp earning? Neither company says. EmailTooltester has compiled revenue estimates from press reports, citing AdAge, TechCrunch, Forbes, Inc.com, the New York Times and Zippia, and it presents them as estimates rather than reported figures. Its series shows growth up to 2020 and a decline after that.
Taking the most recent of those estimates, $700 million for 2023, the enterprise value comes to about 5.3 times revenue. That is a PPC Land calculation built on a third-party estimate that is three years old, so it is a rough guide at best. For comparison, PPC Land calculated that Salesforce's $5.66 billion all-cash agreement to buy Braze, signed on October 20, 2026, valued that customer engagement platform at about 7.7 times its trailing annual revenue of $738.2 million.
What Bending Spoons does with what it buys
Bending Spoons is unusually explicit about its method. The company "is built on the conviction that operational excellence enables efficient growth through acquisitions," according to the release. It "acquires digital businesses, implements deep transformations and ongoing optimizations to sustainably expand earnings, and reinvests in additional acquisitions, thereby continuing the compounding cycle." It says it has followed this strategy for more than a decade and "to date, has never sold a material business."
The description of what happens after a purchase is blunter still. "The transformation is typically deep and entails reorganizing teams, overhauling technology, redesigning user interfaces, accelerating product development, and enhancing marketing and monetization," according to Bending Spoons. "AI is often both a central component of the vision and a key tool in implementing the transformation." The company attributes its performance to what it calls its Platform, made up of its people, proprietary technologies and proprietary data.
Its main businesses, as listed in the release, are Airtable, AOL, Brightcove, Eventbrite, Evernote, Miro, Tractive, Vimeo and WeTransfer.
The record of those transformations is public. Bending Spoons agreed on September 10, 2025 to buy Vimeo for $1.38 billion in cash, or $7.85 a share. In January 2026 Vimeo cut the majority of its workforce, including open source contributors, and Bending Spoons confirmed the cuts without giving a number. WeTransfer, acquired in 2024, ran into trouble in July 2025, when users objected to new terms of service containing machine learning language. WeTransfer rewrote the terms and removed the machine learning and AI references on July 15, 2025. At $3.68 billion, the Mailchimp enterprise value is more than two and a half times the Vimeo price.

Mailchimp's market position
How big is the business changing hands? The most detailed public picture comes from third parties, and it is dated. EmailTooltester's page "Mailchimp Market Share", by Roberta Phillips and Robert Brandl, carries a date of November 4, 2026, but its subtitle covers "The Evolution From 2020 - 2024" and its most recent market share figures are from May 2024.
Those figures come from Datanyze, a technology-detection service that counts the web domains on which it finds each provider. According to EmailTooltester, Datanyze put Mailchimp's share of the email marketing market at 67.54% across 935,771 domains in May 2024. That measures presence on websites. It does not measure revenue, emails sent or active accounts.
Source: Datanyze domain counts, as published by EmailTooltester.
The share fell by about five percentage points between January 2022 and May 2024. EmailTooltester's explanation is that Mailchimp reduced the allowances of its free plan several times, pushing users towards other services built on a freemium model, and raised prices steadily, making it "one of the more expensive entry-level email service providers in the market." That is the site's interpretation, not a finding from Datanyze.
The biggest gainer was Klaviyo, whose share rose from 1.43% in June 2020 to 11.86% in May 2024. EmailTooltester attributes that to Klaviyo's deep integration with Shopify. It adds that its own "Email Detective" data found Klaviyo was the main platform that Mailchimp users were switching to. Klaviyo had more than 205,000 brands as customers at June 30, 2026, and its revenue passed $1.2 billion in 2025.
BuiltWith, which EmailTooltester describes as working from a smaller sample than Datanyze, shows Mailchimp usage peaking in 2020, and according to the site, "Since 2022, Mailchimp has been on a clear downward trajectory." Search interest tells a similar story. Ahrefs estimates cited by EmailTooltester put global monthly searches for Mailchimp at 4.1 million in March 2020 and 1.9 million in May 2024, with US searches falling from 1.2 million to 563,000. EmailTooltester links the March 2020 peak to the start of the Covid-19 pandemic, when businesses rushed to email customers about closures and deliveries.
The page is not internally consistent. Its prose gives Constant Contact 5.08% and calls it Mailchimp's biggest competitor, while its own table shows 5.89% in May 2024 and ranks Klaviyo second. Every share figure above therefore belongs to EmailTooltester and Datanyze, and none of it has been confirmed by Intuit or Bending Spoons.
The last official numbers
Mailchimp stopped publishing annual reports and has taken down earlier editions, according to EmailTooltester. The last figures the site could gather come from the 2020 report: 14 million active users in December 2020, up from 12.3 million a year earlier; almost 14,000 sign-ups a day; 333,635,013,935 emails sent during the year; and 55 languages used in campaigns. Mailchimp customers recorded 515.3 million ecommerce orders worth $61 billion in revenue that year, according to the same report as cited by EmailTooltester, and abandoned cart automations generated more than $314.6 million. Those numbers are six years old. The release announcing the sale gives no current user count at all, describing Mailchimp only as having "helped millions of businesses."
What the executives said
Luca Ferrari, Bending Spoons' chief executive and co-founder, framed the purchase around the brand. "Mailchimp is a powerful brand, built over two decades of reshaping the email marketing category and redefining how companies grow their audiences," he said, according to the release. "We're honored to welcome it to Bending Spoons and excited about what we can accomplish together. After closing, we'll invest substantially in the business, building on the powerful, easy-to-use tools customers already value and rely on."
Jack Tam, a senior vice president at Intuit, spoke for the seller. "Mailchimp has helped millions of businesses grow and acquire customers through email marketing automation, and we are grateful to the Mailchimp team for what they have built and the impact they have had on customers and Intuit," he said. "We are confident Bending Spoons will lead Mailchimp to continued success in this next chapter. We are focused on ensuring a smooth transition for Mailchimp's customers, partners, and employees."
