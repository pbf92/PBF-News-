TITLE: Anthropic prepares for historic IPO amid market uncertainty
OUTLET: Northeastern Global News

Anthropic is gearing up for what many industry experts are describing as an historic initial public offering, or IPO, that could be a watershed moment for the burgeoning artificial intelligence industry at a time of considerable market uncertainty.
The company, which is responsible for the popular AI chatbot Claude, is seeking to raise more than $61 billion through the sale of shares, with an initial price range of $70 to $78 per share, according to its S-1 filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission, also known as a prospectus.
Reporting from the Wall Street Journal and CNBC on Wednesday, when the company was pricing its shares, suggested it was considering increasing the price of its offering amid significant investor demand.
“I think Anthropic is currently at the forefront of this industry,” said Karthik Krishnan, an associate professor of finance at Northeastern University, who studies IPOs.
The timing of the IPO
The Anthropic IPO could inject some fresh enthusiasm into markets at a time when the AI industry is experiencing turbulence. One of the industry’s key leaders, Nvidia founder Jensen Huang, stepped down as CEO of the company last week amid national news media reports that he had an affair with an alleged Chinese spy and allegedly with two Nvidia employees, at least one of them a direct report. Nvidia designs the graphics processing units and other specialized chips that power much of the world’s artificial intelligence infrastructure.
Markets have been reeling ever since, with Nvidia stock falling about 30% over the past month.
“The market is certainly looking more depressed right now,” Krishnan said.
Timing an IPO is often an incredibly difficult decision, Krishnan said. Companies preparing to go public have to weigh market volatility against pressure from investors looking to reap rewards on their investments, as well as employees anticipating the financial windfall of a public listing.
Investors had expected Anthropic to move forward with its IPO sooner in October, according to the Wall Street Journal.
“The reality is that, for a venture-backed company with a lot of investors looking for liquidity,” the decision of when to go public “becomes more limited and restricted,” Krishnan said. “They may have to enter the market under less-than-ideal circumstances.”
The high stakes of Anthropic’s growth
Anthropic is entering the public markets with enormous ambitions, eye-popping revenue growth and a valuation that reflects investors’ sky-high expectations for artificial intelligence.
But the company’s multibillion-dollar infrastructure commitments and uncertain path to long-term profitability leave unclear whether its business can sustain the expectations built into its price, Krishnan said.
“This is a rapidly evolving and fickle industry,” he said, adding that there are few historical precedents for a new industry going public at this scale.
One comparable period was the dot-com boom of the late 1990s, when a wave of nascent internet companies rushed to go public. In 2000, the market collapsed in dramatic fashion, wiping out trillions of dollars in the market value of these ventures.
The first generative AI tools were made publicly available just a few years ago, beginning with OpenAI’s ChatGPT in November 2022. About four months later, Anthropic introduced its own large language model Claude, which it described as a “next-generation AI assistant” capable of a wide variety of “conversational and text processing tasks.”
“I’m sure many companies knew about [LLMs], but the broader public” wasn’t aware of them until more recently, Krishnan said. “Now the companies making them are pursuing multibillion-dollar IPOs.”
A new wave of AI IPOs
Krishnan said Anthropic’s IPO, which comes after SpaceX went public on June 12 in what was then the largest IPO in history, could open the flood gates to other AI companies seeking public money, including international entrants from around the world that could challenge U.S. companies for market share and investor capital.
That increasing competition could push companies like Anthropic to expand beyond their core focus, which is on developing frontier AI models, into consumer- and business-facing products, such as coding assistants, legal research tools and workplace productivity software.
“That is another reason why I think these companies are moving beyond large language models and providing end-user services like Claude for legal work and Claude Desktop,” Krishnan said. Some of those services are available to users with subscriptions; others, such as Claude Desktop, do not require one.
“They’re diversifying beyond their core business model,” he said.
OpenAI is planning its IPO for sometime in 2027, although it has not announced an official date. The company confidentially filed draft IPO paperwork with the Securities and Exchange Commission in June 2026, but CEO Sam Altman subsequently ruled out a public listing this year.
