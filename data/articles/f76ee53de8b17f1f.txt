TITLE: Meta's Muse AI can now shop at Walmart, but Walmart picks the products
OUTLET: PPC Land

Walmart on November 24, 2026, opened its Black Friday deals to Muse, Meta's AI agent app, so that customers can find products, fill a basket and pay without leaving the conversation. The connection runs through the Walmart Agent Gateway, a dedicated route Walmart built for outside AI agents, and it comes with one detail that matters to brands selling at Walmart: Muse hands over the shopping list, and Walmart decides which products come back.
In Short
Meta's Muse app can now shop Walmart's Black Friday deals and pay for them inside the chat, using a card already saved in a Walmart account. Muse passes your request to Walmart, Walmart picks the matching products, and you set a spending limit and decide which orders need your approval. For brands, a sale made through Meta's agent at Walmart now depends on what Walmart's own systems choose to show, not on Muse's own search of the web.
What Walmart announced
The announcement appeared on Walmart's corporate newsroom on November 24, 2026, under the title "Walmart's Black Friday Deals Continue. Meta's Muse Can Now Shop Them, Too." It sits in the Innovation section, is labelled a three-minute read and carries no byline, no named spokesperson and no quotes from Meta.
"Starting today, customers can shop Walmart's Black Friday deals directly through Muse, Meta's personal AI agent, which can help them find items, build a basket and complete their purchase," according to Walmart. A personal AI agent is software that carries out tasks for one person, such as booking or buying, instead of only answering questions. Meta released Muse in the US on September 8, 2026, with a rule that it must ask before sensitive actions such as completing a purchase.
The timing is plain. Black Friday falls on November 27 this year, three days after the post. Walmart's headline says its deals "continue", which means the deal events were already running before Muse was added as a way in.
Walmart calls the result "a big step forward for shopping with AI, bringing product discovery and purchasing in one experience." That is the company's own description. The post gives no figures for how many customers have connected Muse, how many orders agents have placed or how those orders convert.
The gateway behind it
Muse does not browse Walmart.com the way a person would. It connects to the Walmart Agent Gateway, which Walmart first described on October 21, 2026, when it set out Agent Profiles, the gateway and rules for AI agents, with spending limits such as $50 or $100. At that point the gateway was a beta for companies and developers, open by invitation only. Walmart said then that it worked through MCP, a widely used technical standard that lets AI tools connect to outside services in a predictable way. The November 24 post does not repeat the technical details. It names Muse as the first agent to use the route for shoppers.
Walmart also promises the deals and perks customers already know. "You'll find Walmart's Every Day Low Prices and Black Friday deals, along with the membership benefits you're used to, like free delivery," the post states. Every Day Low Prices is Walmart's long-standing name for keeping regular prices low instead of relying on frequent sales.
How shopping with Muse works, according to Walmart
The post sets out three steps under the heading "How to shop Walmart with Muse - including our Black Friday Deals." They describe what Walmart's documentation says happens on screen.
- Ask Muse for something. According to Walmart, the customer tells Muse what they need, "For example, ask it to find a great TV deal or show you which Walmart Black Friday deals are available for delivery." Muse then helps explore products and build the basket.
- Set the rules. "Muse will take you to Walmart to sign in, then bring you back to Muse," the post states. "The first time you add an item to your cart, you'll set up your Agent Profile on Walmart within your Walmart account, including a spending limit you're comfortable with." The sign-in happens on Walmart, not inside Meta's app.
- Check out. The purchase is completed "within the conversation using a card saved in your Walmart account, with the same Walmart prices you'd find on Walmart.com." Walmart adds: "And if something isn't quite right, Walmart's return policy still applies."
Elsewhere in the post, Walmart describes what an Agent Profile allows. According to the company, a customer can "set spending limits, approve purchases and check your purchase history to see which purchases were made by your agent." In other words, orders placed by Muse are marked separately from the customer's own orders inside the Walmart account.
What the product images show
Walmart illustrated the post with phone screens. They are marketing images, not a full specification, but they record the wording customers are likely to see.
Two details in the images go further than the text. First, the approval choices are expressed as an approval threshold: orders above the chosen amount wait for the shopper's sign-off, and a $0 setting would mean every order waits. Second, the demo order total of $23.72 is $3.77 higher than the $19.95 item price, a difference of about 19% by PPC Land's calculation; the screen does not say whether that is tax, a fee or something else.
Walmart does the choosing
The most consequential sentence in the post is about who picks the products. "Instead of searching for each item yourself, Muse connects to the Walmart Agent Gateway, where it passes your shopping list to Walmart," according to Walmart. "Walmart then does the shopping, finding products that match your request."
That sets the order of operations. Muse understands the request and talks to the customer. Walmart's systems decide which items, from Walmart's own catalogue, are a match. The results card in the demo is labelled "Walmart", not Muse.
The arrangement differs from how Muse has found products elsewhere. When the research firm Profound tested Muse in early October, it reported that the agent filled four of its six product recommendations from the live web and two from Meta's own catalogue, from what Profound called a small, exploratory sample. For a Walmart request routed through the gateway, Walmart's post says the matching is Walmart's job.
Data is the other half of the arrangement. "For more than 60 years, Walmart has worked to earn customers' trust by helping them save money and live better," the post states. "The Walmart Agent Gateway brings that same commitment to AI shopping, helping agents find relevant Walmart products without sharing customers' broader purchase history with others." The sentence is narrow. It covers the customer's wider purchase history, which stays with Walmart. It does not say what Meta sees of the Muse conversation itself, which in the demo includes the request, the products shown, the cart and the order total. The consent text adds one flow in the other direction: Muse will share the customer's allow-or-deny choice with Walmart.
What has changed since October
Walmart's October 21 post described the plan. The November 24 post describes it working with a named agent. The comparison below draws the October column from PPC Land's report on that announcement.
One small wording difference stands out. In October, a profile was to be created automatically the first time an agent used the gateway. The November 24 post ties set-up to the first time an item goes into the cart. Both versions put the profile inside the customer's Walmart account, and both make the spending limit a setting the customer controls.
Why it matters for marketers
One door closes, another opens
Retailers are splitting into two camps on AI shopping agents, and Muse has been at the centre of it. Amazon said on September 20 that it had cut Muse off from buying on Amazon.com. eBay's user agreement now bars buy-for-me agents from placing orders without eBay's prior permission. On November 17, GeekWire reported that Muse's shopping instructions tell the agent to leave Amazon out unless a user asks for it, and point general merchandise toward Walmart and Target.
Walmart has taken the opposite route to Amazon. It is not blocking agents; it is building a lane for them, with its own sign-in, its own product matching and its own record of which orders an agent placed. That is a different idea of agentic commerce from an agent that browses a store's pages like a person and clicks through checkout.
Muse is not a small source of shoppers
The size of the agent matters to the retailer that lets it in. Sensor Tower estimated that Muse passed 10 million US downloads within 40 days of its September 8 release. On Meta's third-quarter call on October 21, executives said more than 10 million people in the US and Canada had set up Muse, and that Meta expects to be paid a fee based on the transactions Muse helps complete. On the same call, Susan Li, Meta's chief financial officer, said Meta had previewed a Personal Agent Protocol, an open standard for agents and businesses, with partners including Walmart. HUMAN Security's October data put Muse at 59% of the AI agent traffic it measured.
Meta has been adding retail partners outside the US as well. Loblaw became the first Canadian retailer to connect its catalogue to Muse on November 18, a day after Meta launched Muse in Australia with nine local partners, from Canva to Kogan.
Walmart's second try at checkout inside a chatbot
This is not Walmart's first experiment with buying inside someone else's AI app. In January, Walmart and Target brought checkout into Google's Gemini app and AI Mode. In March, a Walmart executive said products bought directly inside ChatGPT converted three times lower than products where shoppers clicked through to Walmart's site. Muse checkout differs on paper: the customer signs in to Walmart, the card comes from the Walmart account and the order is filed under a Walmart Agent Profile. Whether that changes the conversion picture is something the post does not address.
Where the advertising money sits
Walmart's ad business is the reason marketers will watch who controls the product list. Walmart Connect, the company's retail media network, grew 36% in the third quarter, excluding VIZIO and Vibe, while Walmart US ecommerce sales grew 23%, as PPC Land reported on November 19. A retail media network sells ads on a retailer's own site and apps, using what it knows about its shoppers.
Under the gateway model, the customer's purchase history stays with Walmart and the product matching is done by Walmart. For brands, that keeps the decisive step inside a system Walmart controls, rather than inside Meta's agent. The post does not say whether sponsored products, the paid listings that appear in Walmart search, can appear in the results Walmart returns to Muse.
Sparky stays in the picture
Walmart is not handing the season to outside agents. The post presents four routes for Black Friday: in stores, online, with Sparky, and through an agent. Sparky is Walmart's own shopping assistant, which since late October can turn a photo of a handwritten list into a cart. According to the post, Sparky can now also help shoppers find their way through a store and use images and voice. The closing lines frame Muse as a start: "And Muse is just the beginning. The Walmart Agent Gateway will make it possible to shop Walmart through more apps and agents, with additional experiences on the way."
What the post does not say
The post does not name the countries covered, though it refers only to Walmart.com; it does not mention Sam's Club, which the October terms covered, or Canada, where Muse is also available. It gives no date for the next agents, no figures on use, nothing on whether Meta earns a fee on Walmart orders, nothing on how Walmart ranks products for an agent or whether ads appear in those results, and nothing on whether Muse's WhatsApp version can use the gateway. Meta did not comment in the post.
