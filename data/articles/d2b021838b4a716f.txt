TITLE: Amazon and Google will own nearly 30% of Anthropic after today’s IPO, netting a roughly $260 billion paper windfall
OUTLET: Fortune

Amazon and Google spent the last three years building AI models to compete with Anthropic. As of Thursday morning, they also own 28% of it.
When Anthropic starts trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, Amazon will hold 14.6% of the Claude maker and Google 13.4%, according to Anthropic’s final prospectus. All of it is Class N stock, which has no vote. Anthropic’s seven co-founders keep control through a single share of a special class of stock, held by an LLC they organized. Google’s stake includes about $10 billion of a $26 billion private placement at the $83 IPO price, alongside Nvidia, Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, Micron and TSMC.
It’s evolving into a kind of frenemy relationship, or a coopetition, competition and cooperation bundled together. Amazon and Google rent Anthropic its chips, resell its models and keep a cut, and own a big piece of the equity, all while selling their own models against Claude.
Anthropic says in its filing that it has partnerships with Amazon, Google and Microsoft, which “simultaneously serve as our investors, customers, distribution partners, and competitors,” creating conflicts of interest it calls “difficult to fully anticipate or mitigate.” Lina Khan, who opened an FTC inquiry into these deals as the agency’s chair, told Axios this week the stakes create “the deep kind of conflicts” regulators worried about.
Fortune’s pricing story put Anthropic’s valuation at $1.5 trillion. At $83 a share, Amazon’s 1.94 billion shares are worth about $161 billion and Google’s 1.77 billion about $147 billion. Amazon has put about $18 billion into Anthropic, and Google about $23 billion before the IPO; add Google’s $10 billion placement and the two have put in roughly $51 billion. Net of that, they’re sitting on a paper gain of roughly $260 billion, more than three times the $81.3 billion that Anthropic’s IPO will raise.
That doesn’t count all of Amazon’s convertible notes, which can only convert while its stake stays at or below 12% of Anthropic, a cap Amazon chose not to waive for the IPO. Converted in full, the notes would lift Amazon to about 3.48 billion shares, or 23.6% of the company, per the prospectus: roughly $128 billion more at the IPO price.
Much of the gain is already on their books. Amazon booked $62.9 billion of gains on private investments in the first nine months of 2026, mostly from its Anthropic stock. Alphabet has reported a $117 billion unrealized gain on its private stake.
The landlord, the toll booth and the shareholder
Anthropic’s Big Tech investors have a multi-faceted relationship with the company. Start with the rent. Anthropic has committed to spend at least about $121.3 billion with Google between April 2026 and July 2033, “regardless of actual usage,” the prospectus says. It has committed at least $110 billion to Amazon between May 2026 and April 2036, on the same terms.
Then the toll booth. Plenty of Claude customers buy it through their Amazon, Google or Microsoft cloud accounts, and the cloud provider keeps a fee. Sales through those cloud marketplaces made up about 44% of Anthropic’s revenue in the first nine months of 2026, up from 11% in 2023, according to the prospectus. The filing also says the arrangements give the clouds “visibility into our pricing and commercial terms across the platforms they operate.” Fees to those channel partners rose by $2.7 billion in the first half of the year, Bloomberg reported from an earlier version of the filing.
Anthropic’s prospectus also warns that the cloud providers may prioritize their own compute needs and those of other customers, “including competitors in which they have invested,” and may gain insight into Anthropic’s operations that could inform their own competing efforts. For Google, the competition is Gemini, sold to many of the same enterprise buyers Claude is chasing. For Amazon, it’s the Nova models.
Why nonvoting
Microsoft did something similar in 1997 when it rescued Apple from the brink of bankruptcy It put $150 million into Apple “non-voting preferred stock” while selling Windows against the Mac, Engadget recounted. It had sold the whole stake by 2003.
Today’s version comes with a regulator problem. Without votes, Amazon and Google don’t control Anthropic on paper. Washington has looked at this before. In January 2025, an FTC staff report on Amazon’s and Google’s Anthropic partnerships, along with Microsoft’s backing of OpenAI, warned the deals could raise switching costs and give cloud providers unique access to sensitive information. Two months later, the Justice Department dropped its bid to make Google sell its AI investments in the search case, Reuters reported. Anthropic had told the court a forced sale would harm “both Anthropic and competition more generally.”
