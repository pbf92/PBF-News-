TITLE: Amazon's live shopping beta asks brands for 50 Mbps and 1080p video
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon Ads on November 20, 2026 updated two Support Center pages that set out how brands in its Amazon Live beta should run shoppable livestreams inside their Amazon Brand Stores, from the speed of the internet connection to the order in which products appear on screen. The pages, titled "Live Shopping" and "Live Shopping best practices," both carry the same notice: "Amazon Live is in beta and available for select advertisers in the US." Neither page gives a first-publication date or a change log, so what changed on November 20 is not recorded.
The requirements are specific. According to Amazon, a stream should come from a location with at least 50 Mbps of upload and download speed, at 1920 x 1080 resolution, 30 frames per second, a 4.5 Mbps bitrate and a two-second keyframe interval. Brands are told to put their highest-priority product first, place deal products in the first four slots and feature up to 10 products in total. The plan for finding viewers relies on paid media: Sponsored Brands, Display and DVA+ campaigns pointed at the Brand Store page where the stream appears, plus posts on social media.
In Short
Amazon has published detailed instructions for brands that sell through live video inside their own Amazon Brand Stores, a feature still in a test phase for selected US advertisers. The pages matter to brands and agencies weighing live shopping on Amazon, because they set out the equipment, the internet speed, the product order and the paid campaigns Amazon expects to bring in viewers. A stream's only organic home named in the pages is the brand's own Store, so reach beyond that page depends on the advertising and social posts Amazon recommends.
Two pages, one beta
Amazon's description of the format is the same on both pages, with one addition on the second. "Amazon Live is a creative advertising format that gives brands an interactive live shopping tool to engage customers with immersive storytelling," the overview page states. The best practices page adds that the tool should "drive product consideration and discovery with educational and inspirational content experiences."
The overview is direct about the commercial purpose. "Viewers can shop while they watch, making live shopping a powerful way to build awareness and increase consideration," according to Amazon. That is the company's own characterization; neither page offers viewing, conversion or sales data to support it.
Four occasions are named as suitable. Amazon lists product launches, "showcasing features and benefits through interactive demonstrations that answer customer questions in real time"; brand storytelling, "sharing the people, values, and craftsmanship behind your products"; customer education through demonstrations, tips and tutorials; and seasonal campaigns, "creating excitement during key shopping moments like holidays, Prime Day, or back-to-school season."
Both pages sit in the Support Center under Building campaigns. The best practices page is split into four tabs, labeled Technical requirements, Policies and guidelines, Content and Other considerations. Each page lists the same three related topics: the other Live Shopping page, a setup guide titled "Add the Live Shopping tile to your Brand Store and create a livestream (beta)," and Live Events Optimizer.
The technical bar
The connection requirement opens the Technical requirements tab. "Livestream from a location with an internet speed of at least 50 Mbps upload and download so your stream stays stable," the page states. The settings that follow are precise enough to put in a table.
The recommended equipment list has six items: "A camera with proper lighting," "A quality microphone or audio system," "Backup recording capability," "Closed captioning software," "A backup internet connection" and "OBS or similar encoding software (optional)." On production, the page asks for two to three camera angles and clear lighting and sound, adding: "We recommend lavalier microphones."
The encoder choice is the only place where the page allows a lower standard. According to Amazon, 1080p "gives viewers the best experience," while 720p "helps prevent buffering and keeps your broadcast smooth" for streams on shared Wi-Fi.
Some arithmetic puts the figures in proportion. By PPC Land's calculation, the 4.5 Mbps bitrate uses 45% of the 10 Mbps upload that Amazon cites for 1080p, and 9% of the 50 Mbps location requirement. A two-second keyframe interval at 30 frames per second means one full reference frame every 60 frames.
What goes on screen
The Content tab reads like a short producer's brief. "Show, don't tell. Let your products shine on screen," it begins, before asking for close-ups and B-roll "to highlight features and benefits" and setting a pace: "Spend 1 to 3 minutes on each product."
Product order gets four rules of its own, written in terms of the ASIN, Amazon's catalogue identifier.
At one to three minutes per product, a stream with the full 10 products would spend between 10 and 30 minutes on demonstrations, and one limited to the five highlighted products between 5 and 15 minutes, by PPC Land's calculation. Amazon gives no recommended stream length.
The set and the hosts get a line each. Brands are asked to "Create a clean, on-brand setup that supports your story," and to "Pick relatable hosts. Authenticity builds trust." The tab closes with "Use a conversational tone and share personal stories."
The tab is silent on questions other platforms have settled in writing. TikTok Shop, for one, published LIVE quality rules in May 2026 that ban AI voices and limit still images to half the screen. Amazon's Content tab says nothing about synthetic hosts, generated voices or static frames.
Paying for an audience
The overview page is plain about where a stream lives and how its reach is meant to grow. "Your livestream appears in your Brand Store, where customers can discover it organically," it states. "To maximize reach, combine Amazon Live with paid advertising through Sponsored Brands or DVA+ directing your campaign to the page of your brand store featuring your livestream."
The best practices page describes the route through the Amazon Ads console. According to Amazon, clicking Create a campaign on the Live Shopping page "connects you to Campaign Manager, where you can create Sponsored Brands, Display, or DVA+ campaigns." Those campaigns should "use the brand store landing page URL where your livestream will appear." Campaign Manager is the single interface for sponsored ads and Amazon DSP that Amazon put into beta in November 2025. The page does not say whether "Display" means Sponsored Display, which Amazon repositioned beside DVA+ on September 29, 2026, or display buying inside DVA+ itself.
Promotion is expected on both sides of the broadcast. "Drive tune-in before your stream goes live and extend engagement afterward by promoting the video-on-demand (VOD) content," the overview reads, with Sponsored Brands and DVA+ used "to amplify reach across the Amazon Ads canvas." Brands are also told to "share your streams on social media platforms to drive incremental traffic to your Brand Store."
Creative guidance follows, and the Other considerations tab splits it by channel.
The Instagram line is unusual for an Amazon Ads page. It treats Meta's app as part of the media plan, favoring static formats there and keeping video for user-generated content style executions. A further point on the same tab argues that "real people interacting with products consistently drive higher engagement than static product shots." The tab also advises starting "with the formats and channels that have historically performed well for your brand, then expand and test new options as you scale."
None of these claims comes with a measurement. The 50/50 split "tends to drive the best results," but the page does not define the result, the period, the campaigns studied or the metric.
Two of the paid routes come with their own conditions. DVA+, Amazon's combined display, video and audio campaign type, was in US closed beta when PPC Land examined its four-step campaign builder in September 2026, and Amazon caps its automated DVA+ campaigns at 20 products. For Sponsored Brands, Amazon in November 2025 added a Brand Store page views metric, the kind of figure that would show how much traffic a campaign sends to the page carrying a stream.
How a Live Shopping stream works, according to Amazon
Read together, the two pages describe a sequence of seven steps.
- Access. Amazon Live is in beta for "select advertisers in the US." The pages do not say how advertisers are selected.
- Setup. The livestream and the Live Shopping tile are created in the brand's Store. Amazon documents those steps on a separate page, "Add the Live Shopping tile to your Brand Store and create a livestream (beta)."
- Technical preparation. The stream runs from a location with at least 50 Mbps upload and download, at 1920 x 1080, 30 fps, 4.5 Mbps and a two-second keyframe interval. Encoder users choose 1080p with 10 Mbps or more of upload, or 720p on shared Wi-Fi.
- Product order. The highest-priority ASIN goes first, the rest follow the order of discussion, deal ASINs take the first four slots, and up to 5 ASINs are highlighted out of up to 10 in total.
- Before the stream. On the Live Shopping page, Create a campaign opens Campaign Manager, where Sponsored Brands, Display or DVA+ campaigns are built with the Brand Store page URL of the stream as the landing page. Social posts add outside traffic.
- During the stream. According to Amazon, brands "manage your product carousel by highlighting different items as you discuss them" and "Monitor and respond to viewer chat messages in real time to encourage participation." Hosts ask viewers to follow the brand.
- After the stream. The recording stays available as video-on-demand content, and the overview asks brands to "extend engagement afterward by promoting the video-on-demand (VOD) content."
Followers as a second channel
The last section of the best practices page deals with the Follow button. Brands are told to "encourage customers throughout your livestream to hit the 'follow' button to stay up-to-date with your content."
The incentive has two parts. "Customers who follow your brand receive a push notification from the Amazon Shopping App when you go live if they opt-in to receive notifications," according to Amazon. And: "Brands can also increase their chance to surface on placements across Amazon for customers who follow them." The page names no placements and gives no figure for how much a follow changes a brand's chances.
Amazon has tried brand-owned feeds before. It deprecated Posts on June 3, 2025 and shut the service on July 31, 2025, citing declining impressions and a redesign of its search and detail pages. Live Shopping gives followers a different hook: a notification when a stream starts.
Where the pages do not line up
Several details differ between the pages, or between tabs on the same page.
The speed figures are the most visible. The Technical requirements tab asks for 50 Mbps of upload and download at the streaming location, while the encoder guidance on the same tab recommends 1080p for anyone with "10 Mbps or more of upload speed." The higher figure is five times the lower one. Which applies? The page does not explain whether 50 Mbps is a requirement for the beta and 10 Mbps a minimum for encoder quality, or how the two relate.
Resolution follows the same pattern. The video settings list 1920 x 1080 without qualification, and a few lines down 720p is offered for shared Wi-Fi. Whether a 720p stream meets the requirement is not stated.
The list of paid formats changes between pages. The overview names "Sponsored Brands or DVA+," and the best practices page names "Sponsored Brands, Display, or DVA+." Instagram appears only on the Other considerations tab.
Live Events Optimizer, listed as a related topic on both pages, raises its own question. PPC Land
