TITLE: More than 100 people arrested in day of action across city centre
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

More than 100 people arrested in day of action across city centre
The operation saw the highest number of arrests since AVRO began in the Greater Manchester Police
More than 100 people have been arrested in a day of action across city centre, as police say the operation has seen the highest number of arrests since operation AVRO began in the Greater Manchester Police.
Officers arrested 116 people and executed 30 warrants across 24 hours today (November 20), starting at midnight, with officers executing warrants at addresses linked to criminality following intelligence gathered through policing and information provided by members of the public.
Early-morning warrants were executed across Manchester, with officers finding drugs, weapons and cash. A cannabis farm containing 77 plants, with an estimated street value of around £64,000, was recovered at an address on Blackley New Road, Higher Blackley.
Officers also seized £12,500 worth of nitrous oxide from a business on Fog Lane, Didsbury and arrested three people on suspicion of concern to supply.
Chief Superintendent David Meeney, from GMP's City of Manchester district, said: "Operation AVRO allows us to bring together specialist and neighbourhood policing resources to tackle the issues that matter most to our communities. It is positive to see that yesterday’s operation saw the highest number of arrests of any of the City of Manchester AVROs.
"Through proactive enforcement, intelligence-led operations and visible patrols, we are able to disrupt offenders, take weapons and drugs off the streets and make Manchester a safer place for those who live, work and visit here.
"The results achieved throughout the day demonstrate our continued commitment to preventing crime, protecting vulnerable people and relentlessly pursuing those who seek to cause harm in our communities."
Officers also conducted patrols across the city centre, speaking to members of the public about the use of e-bikes and e-scooters and seizing any that were not legal. During the operation, officers stopped 18 bikes with six e-bikes and scooters being seized in total across the city region.

As part of Operation Hurricane, Greater Manchester Police is encouraging anyone considering purchasing these vehicles as Christmas gifts to ensure they comply with legal requirements before buying them.
Illegal e-bikes and e-scooters used on public roads can be seized and destroyed, potentially leaving owners significantly out of pocket.
A knife arch was also deployed in New Islington, recovering weapons and taking enforcement action where offences were identified.
Alongside warrant activity, officers worked with Trading Standards to target the sale of illegal and illicit goods. Approximately £25,000 of illicit goods were seized.
Roads policing officers were also deployed throughout the day, issuing 34 Traffic Offence Reports and seizing 26 vehicles.
Additional overt and covert patrols took place across the Manchester Christmas Markets, providing reassurance to visitors while targeting criminality and anti-social behaviour.
The activity demonstrated the wide range of policing tactics used every day to keep people safe and prevent crime across Manchester.
