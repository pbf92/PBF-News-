TITLE: Loblaw to allow shopping with Meta’s Muse AI assistant
OUTLET: The Globe and Mail

Grocery retailer Loblaw Cos. Ltd. L-T has struck a new AI partnership with Meta Platforms Inc. META-Q, allowing customers to use the tech company’s personal assistant, Muse, to shop its online catalogue and place orders without visiting the retailer’s own websites or its app.
Loblaw is the first Canadian retailer to partner directly with Muse. Canadian tech company Shopify Inc. SHOP-T has also integrated its e-commerce platform with the AI agent, opening up its shopping capabilities to Shopify stores.
Partnerships like these are growing, as more tech companies are building AI agents that can access websites and handle tasks for their users. OpenAI, Anthropic and Instinct have all released personal agents recently. This month, Google GOOG-Q announced it was expanding people’s ability to shop through its AI chatbot Gemini in Canada, offering direct checkout in partnership with major Canadian retailers – including Shoppers Drug Mart, which Loblaw owns.
And earlier this year, Loblaw also announced a partnership with OpenAI, allowing people to search for products and build a shopping cart through ChatGPT – although that partnership still required customers to click through to the PC Express website or app to complete the purchase. In Muse’s case, the AI agent can check out on people’s behalf if they give it permission.
Canadians are increasingly using these AI agents to look up recipes, plan meals, and make purchasing decisions, Loblaw chief digital officer Lauren Steinberg said in an interview at the time of the ChatGPT partnership. “So, for us, rather than asking Canadians to change their behaviour, our approach is, we’ll adapt to it.”
Still, some businesses – including retailers and financial institutions – have been cautious about allowing AI agents to have access to their websites, as concerns mount about safety, and agents threaten to disrupt companies’ relationships with their customers. Amazon.com Inc. AMZN-Q, for example, opted to block Muse from shopping on its site.
Some of Canada’s largest banks have also pushed back: Royal Bank of Canada RY-T, Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce CM-T, Bank of Montreal BMO-T, Bank of Nova Scotia BNS-T and Toronto-Dominion Bank TD-T have all either blocked or limited third-party agents’ access to their online banking services, amid security concerns.
For retailers, agents are becoming an important channel for customers to find them, as people increasingly use AI to research and compare products. But as those agents encroach on more of the shopping process – deciding which retailers to feature in search results, and processing credit-card transactions when customers opt for those products – they also pose a risk for retailers in building and maintaining brand loyalty.
According to a recent survey from Mastercard, 41 per cent of Canadians reported that they are open to shopping with AI, and half of those people would consider giving an agent permission to make purchases for them.
