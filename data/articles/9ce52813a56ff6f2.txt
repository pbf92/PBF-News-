TITLE: Nearly half of Germans oppose Berlin's aid to Ukraine, poll finds
OUTLET: RBC-Ukraine

Nearly half of Germans oppose Berlin's aid to Ukraine, poll finds
Nearly half of Germans support reducing or completely halting financial, military, and humanitarian aid to Ukraine. At the same time, there are significantly fewer supporters of continuing aid, according to the results of a survey by the YouGov research institute, commissioned by Die Welt.
49% of respondents believe that Berlin should reduce or completely halt aid to Kyiv. Of these, 25% advocate for a complete halt to support, while another 24% want it reduced.
At the same time, 39% of respondents support continuing aid to Ukraine. Specifically, 30% favor maintaining it at the current level, while 9% would like to see an increase in support. Another 11% were undecided.
Opinions also vary by region. In western Germany, 42% of those surveyed want to maintain or increase aid to Ukraine, while in the east of the country, only 25% feel this way. The strongest support for Kyiv comes from young people aged 18 to 29 and people over 70.
According to the German government, since the start of Russia's full-scale invasion, Berlin has provided Ukraine with over 45.7 billion euros in non-military aid and 57.8 billion euros in military support. Germany has also pledged to continue funding Ukraine in the coming years.
Do Germans want Merz to negotiate with Putin
Pollsters also surveyed public opinion on a possible meeting between German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and Russian President Vladimir Putin.
49% of those surveyed want such a meeting to take place as soon as possible, even if Moscow does not make any concessions in advance.
Another 23% believe that negotiations are possible only after certain concessions from Russia. At the same time, 9% oppose any dialogue between Merz and Putin at this stage, while 18% are undecided.
Support for negotiations to begin as soon as possible is highest among Germans aged 50 and older. The figure is particularly high in the 60–69 age group, where 61% of respondents favor a meeting without preconditions.
The YouGov poll was conducted online from November 13 to 16. A total of 2,329 German citizens aged 18 and older participated in the survey.
