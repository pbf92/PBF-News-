TITLE: Anthropic Priced Its IPO at $83. Here's What I'd Pay for the Stock.
OUTLET: The Motley Fool

By Matt DiLallo –
Key Points
- Anthropic's revenue is growing at an extraordinary pace as businesses adopt Claude and its AI tools.
- Its IPO price already assumes it will become one of the world's largest and most profitable technology companies.
- My five- and 10-year models suggest investors should be selective about their entry price, despite the company's exceptional growth potential.
Anthropic's (ANT +0.00%) initial public offering (IPO) is one of the biggest events in the AI industry. The developer of Claude has grown its revenue at an extraordinary pace as businesses adopt its AI tools. The AI giant just priced its IPO at $83 per share, above its expected range of $70 to $78 per share.
However, that price already assumes considerable success. Even if Anthropic becomes one of the world's most successful AI companies, its stock could still deliver disappointing returns from here.
I ran the numbers on what Anthropic's stock could be worth over the next five and 10 years. Here's what they suggest, and the price I'd be willing to pay for the AI stock.
Image source: Getty Images.
Anthropic's growth has been extraordinary
Anthropic recorded $33.2 billion in revenue in just the first nine months of 2026, up a blistering 1,200% compared to the same period last year. Its third-quarter revenue alone was around $17 billion, and its annualized revenue run rate reached $81 billion by Oct. 31, up $10 billion from the prior month.
Enterprise adoption of Claude, its coding tools, API usage, and integrations give Anthropic several avenues for continued growth. However, AI infrastructure and model training are extraordinarily expensive. Anthropic committed to spending a staggering $895.4 billion on compute and infrastructure via purchase commitments and lease agreements. Meanwhile, Anthropic's revenue growth will also almost certainly slow as its business scales.
The IPO valuation already anticipates enormous success
At $83 per share, Anthropic has a valuation of about $1.5 trillion. That's around 18 times its $81 billion run rate.
That run rate isn't the same as reported annual sales. It extrapolates recent results, which inflates a company growing this quickly. Meanwhile, compute costs, research spending, and future capital needs make it difficult to turn that revenue into sustainable profits. Additionally, stock-based compensation adds dilution, which will be a headwind to per-share earnings growth when the company finally becomes profitable.
The crucial question isn't whether Anthropic is a great company. It's whether its growth can justify a valuation this high.

My base case assumes Anthropic grows its revenue to $400 billion by 2031 with an 18% net margin and a 25x earnings multiple. That would be a remarkable business, yet it would only lift the stock to $91, about 10% above the IPO price, or roughly 2% annually. Meanwhile, my bear case assumes slower growth, thinner margins, and a lower multiple, which would send the shares down roughly 73%. It would take my bull case, with a 24% net margin and a 30x earnings multiple, to deliver a strong return of about 19% annually.
The base case return isn't compelling. Anthropic could execute extremely well and still deliver underwhelming returns because the IPO price already reflects so much success. That's the key takeaway: explosive business growth doesn't guarantee an attractive investment return.

Here's what I'd pay for Anthropic stock
I want to earn at least 10% annually from a stock like this using conservative assumptions. For Anthropic's base-case price of $91 in 2031 to deliver that return, I'd need to buy at around $56 per share. That's roughly 30% below the IPO price.
That's why I'd rather wait for a better entry point than chase an elevated first-day price. I'd prefer to buy closer to $55-$60 per share. Even then, I'd want to see that Anthropic can scale its revenue, produce sustainable margins, and defend its position against rival AI platforms.
Anthropic is exceptional, but I wouldn't pay $83
Anthropic could become one of the most important technology companies of the next decade, and its revenue growth and enterprise adoption are extraordinary. However, the IPO price already reflects that potential, and my base case points to modest returns over the next five years.
I'd rather risk missing the initial excitement than overpay for an exceptional business. The stock gets much more interesting to me at $55 to $60 per share, as long as the underlying growth outlook remains intact.
