Rockstar Just Confirmed Where It Stands On The GTA 6 FPS Controversy
If you're part of the "Grand Theft Auto 6" mob that's been waiting so eagerly for the groundbreaking game to launch, you may have already heard that dreams of playing the game in a silky-smooth 60 FPS mode have been dashed. This news has caused a great deal of frustration among gamers globally, but the truth is that there's so much detail in "GTA 6" that none of the current flagship consoles can handle a 60 FPS experience. The technology just doesn't exist to support it yet.
Rockstar revealed this disappointing bit of news earlier this month, but if you consider it from the game-maker's perspective, it makes sense. Rockstar's developers have made use of ray-traced reflections and shadows, as well as global illumination. Both concepts help create ultrarealistic lighting in games and simulate how real-world rays of light travel through a scene. Global illumination is a lighting technique that simulates how light interacts with objects, and ray tracing realistically simulates reflections and soft shadows. Right now, ray-traced shadows are only available to PlayStation 5 Pro gamers on "GTA 6."
When used together, these techniques are resource-heavy, so trying to add more frames per second to an already demanding simulation would only result in a degraded experience. Rockstar would have had to ensure smooth gameplay on different consoles, and because not even the best-in-class PS5 Pro can handle it, it was always going to crank the frame rates down to keep the experience immersive regardless of individual settings. You can turn off motion blur in "GTA 6" in settings to bring out the game's best level of detail, though.
How far off is a console that could support GTA 6 in 60 FPS?
The biggest limitation that causes "GTA 6" to hit a wall at 60 FPS on consoles is the CPU bottleneck. Sony is reportedly working on the PlayStation 6, and there have been leaks that suggest that the necessary CPU performance to run "GTA 6" at 60 FPS is built into the package. According to a leak published by Moore's Law is Dead on YouTube, Sony intends to upgrade the current Zen 2 CPU cores with a four-generation leap, reportedly going for Zen 6.
If this rumor turns out to be true, it could have cores dedicated to OS operations while reserving higher-performing cores for gaming. This will likely enable Rockstar to add a performance mode to "GTA 6" that would make 60 FPS gameplay available. The PS6 could land in about a year, reportedly lined up for a late 2027 or early 2028 launch.
Regardless of what leaks are suggesting, it will be in Sony's best interest to build a console that supports the most demanding games out there, and "GTA 6" has already set a pretty solid target that Sony can aim for. In the meantime, "GTA 6" is still worth playing on the Xbox Series X|S and PlayStation 5.
