TITLE: Instagram tests Direct, a chat app that needs no Instagram account
OUTLET: PPC Land

Meta on November 16, 2026 began testing Direct, a standalone messaging app from Instagram, in Türkiye and Thailand. The app runs on Android and iOS, handles one-on-one and group chats, and can be used with or without an Instagram account. Anyone who signs in with Instagram credentials sees existing conversations sync across both apps; anyone who signs up with only an email address or phone number can be reached solely by their contacts.
In Short
Instagram has released a separate chat app called Direct, for now only in Türkiye and Thailand, that people can use even if they do not have an Instagram account. It matters because Meta is offering private messaging outside its feed-and-ads app, in a space where only contacts can start a conversation, and the company has said nothing yet about where business messages or ads sit there. What changes for now is limited to two countries: users must be at least 13, teens who log in with Instagram keep their Teen Account messaging limits, and parents can see who a supervised teen is messaging.
What Meta announced
The announcement, published in the Meta Newsroom under the title "Introducing Direct: An App Just for Messaging", describes Direct as "a new standalone messaging app brought to you by Instagram that gives people simple, fun ways to chat with friends and family." Meta framed the product around close relationships rather than reach. "People rely on messaging to stay in touch with friends and family, and we built Direct to provide a dedicated space for these close connections," according to the company.
The name is not new. Instagram tested a standalone app called Direct from December 2017 in six markets - Uruguay, Chile, Türkiye, Italy, Portugal and Israel - according to TechCrunch, which reported on May 16, 2019 that the test was being rolled back and that conversations would move back into the main Instagram app automatically. TechCrunch described that earlier version as a Snapchat clone. Türkiye is the one market common to both tests.
The 2026 app is built around five creative tools, which Meta lists by name. Draw lets people sketch directly inside chat threads. Notes surfaces "quick updates from friends right at the top of your inbox." Stickers allows people to create custom stickers. Voice Effects adds audio effects to replies, with Meta naming "crowd laughter or underwater sounds" as examples. Themes personalises chat backgrounds.
What the product images show
The images accompanying the post add detail that the text leaves out. One screenshot shows voice messages of eight and 20 seconds, each with a "View transcription" link, labelled "Sent with Fishbowl effect", "Sent with Grandma effect" and "Sent with Chipmunk effect"; one carries a "1x" speed label. A second shows a group chat named "besties" with a hand-drawn sun overlaid on a shared photo and a colour palette along the bottom. A third shows the inbox itself: a "Direct" wordmark, a search bar, a horizontal row of Notes with short status lines such as "Song of the day?", and two tabs labelled "Messages" and "Hidden". Voice and video call buttons appear at the top of both conversations shown. Meta's text does not mention transcription, call features or the Hidden tab.
Availability and account options
How access works, according to Meta
The announcement sets out the access path in a single section titled "How to Access Direct". According to Meta, it works in five steps:
- Direct is distributed on Android and iOS, and the test is limited to Türkiye and Thailand.
- A new user can sign up with an email address or a phone number. An Instagram account is not required.
- An existing Instagram user can log in with the same username and password. In that case, "your existing chats will sync across both apps."
- A user who joins without an Instagram account can only be messaged or added to groups by contacts. "If you use Direct without an Instagram account, only your contacts can start a chat with you or add you to a group," the post states.
- Contacts are added by entering "their exact username or scan their QR code in person."
The requirement for an exact username matters more than it might appear. There is no suggestion in the post of a searchable directory, contact-book matching or message requests from strangers - the mechanisms that, on most messaging services, let people who have never met start a conversation. A QR code that must be scanned "in person" assumes the two people are standing in the same room.
The protection rules, word for word
Meta placed the safety rules under the heading "Protections Built in From the Start". The core rule reads: "Only your contacts and people you're already connected to can start a chat with you or add you to a group on Direct." The post adds that "People can also report, block, restrict, or remove contacts at any time, and we'll take action if someone breaks our rules."
On younger users, the text is specific about intent but not about method. "We'll use technology to detect and act on potentially inappropriate interactions with young people," according to Meta. "And parents can use our supervision tools to see who their teen is messaging, adjust their safety settings, and set daily time limits." Teens who sign in through Instagram "receive all the same messaging protections they have in Teen Accounts."
That last commitment links Direct to a system Instagram introduced in September 2024. When Instagram unveiled Teen Accounts, the messaging default limited communication to followers and existing connections. Direct's general rule - only contacts and existing connections can open a chat - resembles that teen default, applied to every user regardless of age. Is that a teen setting extended to adults, or simply how a contacts-only messenger works? The post does not say.
The parental controls also differ from the obligations Meta accepted in the United States this year. Under the consent judgment covered when Meta agreed to block teens from Facebook and Instagram after two hours a day on August 27, 2026, messaging was excluded from the two-hour daily cap. Direct gives parents their own daily time limit inside a messaging-only app, though the test markets sit outside the US jurisdictions covered by that settlement.
Age 13, and what happens below it
"You must be at least 13 to use Direct," the announcement states. Thirteen is the threshold below which the US COPPA statute restricts the collection of children's personal information without verifiable parental consent, and it is the same minimum Meta applies on Instagram and Facebook.
Enforcement follows a two-step logic. "We'll use technology to proactively identify accounts that may belong to someone under 13 and require them to verify their age," according to Meta. "If their age is not verified, the account will be deleted." The post does not name the technology, the verification methods offered, or how long an account holder has to respond.
Meta has been building this kind of age assurance for some time. On May 5, 2026, Meta described visual analysis of photos and videos for cues such as height and bone structure to estimate whether an account belongs to someone under 13, available then only in select countries. On November 9, 2026, Instagram extended AI age detection worldwide, moving users it suspects are 13 to 17 into Teen Accounts even when their stated birthday says otherwise. That announcement sits in the "Recent News" column next to the Direct post. Whether Direct, for users who never link an Instagram profile, has the same signals to work with is not addressed.
The post also promises a simpler route for reporting. Meta wrote that it is "making it easier for our community to report underage accounts by simplifying our reporting process," and that this "includes making it easier to submit a report on our Help Center." The old process has drawn criticism. In April 2026, the European Commission issued preliminary findings under the Digital Services Act that Meta failed to diligently address risks to under-13s on Instagram and Facebook, noting that its under-13 reporting form took up to seven clicks, was not pre-filled and had no reliable follow-up. The Direct post does not refer to the Commission, and Türkiye and Thailand are outside the EU.
Türkiye's under-15 rule
The choice of Türkiye as a test market raises a question the announcement does not answer. On October 10, 2026, Türkiye published a regulation in Official Gazette Issue No. 33396 that bars social media accounts for under-15s from November 1. Issued by the Presidency of Cybersecurity under Law No. 5651, it applies to all domestic and foreign social media providers accessible from Türkiye, with no user-count threshold and no named platforms. Users aged 15 to 17 must receive a segregated service in which, among other limits, messages are restricted to accounts they follow and notifications telling a sender that a message has been seen are banned.
Age checks under that regulation run through the state. Türkiye ordered state age tokens for social media from November 1, issued via the e-Government Portal and carrying only whether a user is 15 or older and 18 or older. New accounts are checked from November 1, 2026; existing accounts must be verified by March 31, 2027.
Meta announced the Direct test in Türkiye 15 days after that regulation took effect, with a stated minimum age of 13. Meta's post does not mention the Turkish rules, does not say whether it treats Direct as a social media service under Law No. 5651, and does not describe any Türkiye-specific age threshold or verification step. Thailand receives no country-specific detail either.
Encryption goes unmentioned
Nowhere in the announcement does the word "encryption" appear. That absence has context. In March 2026, Meta's Help Centre said that end-to-end encrypted messaging on Instagram would no longer be supported after May 8, 2026. That feature had been opt-in and available only in some areas. Direct syncs existing Instagram chats, and Meta says it will "use technology to detect and act on potentially inappropriate interactions with young people." The post does not state whether Direct messages are end-to-end encrypted, either by default or as an option.
Meta's other standalone apps
Direct is the latest in a series of single-purpose apps Meta has spun out of its main products. Instagram announced its video editor Edits in January 2025, with an App Store release date of March 13, 2025, when Instagram launched the Edits app. In September 2025, Instagram launched a dedicated iPad app that puts the conversation list and an open thread side by side. In May 2026, Meta's Forum app launched quietly in the US App Store as a Groups-centred product that required an existing Facebook account. In July 2026, Marketplace sellers gained a separate Meta app called Seller, limited to the United States and users aged 18 and older.
Direct breaks from Forum on one point: it does not require an account on the parent app. That puts it closer to recent WhatsApp changes aimed at people outside the service. On June 29, 2026, WhatsApp opened username reservations so that new contacts no longer need a phone number, with no searchable directory. On November 5, 2026, WhatsApp event invites began reaching people without a WhatsApp account, 22 days after guest calling from a browser. Direct's model of exact usernames and in-person QR codes follows the same instinct: contact by deliberate exchange, not by discovery.
Messaging has also been spreading across Meta's other surfaces. In July 2025, Threads launched direct messaging with safety restrictions, initially limited to users aged 18 and older who followed each other on Threads or were mutual followers from Instagram. In November 2024, Instagram's DM update added location sharing and more than 300 stickers across 17 packs, so stickers were part of Instagram's inbox well before Direct appeared.
