TITLE: Amazon ad reports now show what advertisers pay Barometer to vet podcasts
OUTLET: PPC Land

Amazon Ads on November 17, 2026 added two fields to the Amazon DSP reports in version 1 of its advertising API that record how much advertisers pay Barometer, a company that rates podcast episodes so that brands can avoid unsuitable ones before an ad is bought. The change arrived six weeks after Barometer said its episode ratings had become available inside DVA+, Amazon's campaign type for display, video and audio ads.
In Short
Amazon added two report fields on November 17, 2026 that show what an advertiser paid Barometer, a company that checks podcast episodes before an ad bids on them. It matters to brands and agencies buying podcast ads through Amazon, because outside vendors' screening charges are billed on top of the media at a published price per thousand impressions, and Barometer's charge had no named field of its own in these reports before. The fee now appears as its own number, in the campaign's budget currency and in a converted currency, next to the fields Amazon already keeps for other outside vendors such as Adelaide and Comscore.
What Amazon changed
The entry in Amazon's release notes for its advertising API is short. "Amazon DSP reports in Ads API v1 now include fees for the Barometer third-party prebid service, alongside the existing third-party prebid fee metrics," according to Amazon. It then lists two new metrics:
- "metric.thirdPartyPrebidFeesBarometer: Third-party prebid fees (Barometer), in the budget currency"
- "metric.thirdPartyPrebidFeesBarometerConverted: Third-party prebid fees (Barometer, converted), in the currency selected for conversion"
That is the whole announcement. It names no price, no markets and no start date for the underlying data.
The word that matters in the field names is "prebid". Barometer's service is a form of pre-bid filtering: the check happens before Amazon's buying system places a bid on an ad slot. If an episode fails the advertiser's rules, the ad never bids on it.
The two currencies solve a plain bookkeeping problem. A campaign budget is set in one currency, such as dollars or euros, and the first field reports Barometer's charge in that currency. The second field reports the same charge in a currency picked for the report, which lets an agency running campaigns in several currencies add them up in one. Amazon uses the same pairing for the other fee fields in this family.
Who Barometer is
Barometer describes itself as a third-party, AI-powered contextual engine that provides brand suitability ratings and contextual targeting for podcasts and other newer channels, including generative AI, according to the company's October 6, 2026 announcement. Tamara Zubatiy is its founder and chief executive, according to the same release. The podcast trade site Podnews lists the company in its directory of brand suitability tools, with an address in Atlanta, Georgia. The October release itself carried a New York dateline.
In April 2023, Barometer raised $2.25 million in a seed round led by Knoll Ventures, BDMI and HearstLab, bringing its total funding since 2019 to about $4.2 million, according to AdExchanger. Its main product at the time used AI to analyze podcast transcripts.
Barometer works at the level of single episodes rather than whole shows. That distinction is the point of the product. A show can run hundreds of episodes, and one of them may cover a topic a brand wants to avoid; blocking the whole show to avoid that one episode throws away the rest of its audience. In January 2026, Acast and Barometer launched episode-level podcast targeting ahead of programmatic bids, with Barometer scoring each Acast episode at upload against IAB categories and its own definitions for long-form audio, covering new episodes and the back catalog. In August 2026, Basis opened its buying platform to Barometer's episode-level classification, tagging content to version 3.0 of the IAB Content Taxonomy. And in October, SiriusXM Media named Barometer among the partners working on audio-native suitability signals at the podcast episode level, without a launch date.
Podnews' directory also lists a partnership with AdsWizz in March 2026, Magellan AI as Barometer's exclusive measurement partner for programmatic campaigns from September 16, and a brand suitability partnership with PRX from September 17.
Six weeks from launch to line item
On October 6, 2026, Barometer announced that its targeting and brand safety segments were available within Amazon Ads DVA+ for podcast campaigns. The company described the product as a pre-bid brand suitability check that tests each podcast episode against a brand's guidelines, according to the release. It covered all on-demand audio inventory in DVA+, including Amazon-owned audio and partner supply from SiriusXM, Spotify and iHeartMedia, and Barometer said support for video was planned for 2026.
The release made no mention of price, fees or billing. It did include two anonymized case studies. In one, a financial services brand increased the number of podcast shows it could buy by more than 140%, to 2,000 shows, after moving to episode-level targeting, according to Barometer. In the other, an advertiser reached 2,700 shows and more than 5 million new households, with over 85% incremental reach compared with streaming video and audio. Neither advertiser was named, and the release did not describe how those figures were measured.
Forty-two days later, by PPC Land's count, Amazon added the fields that put a number on what Barometer charges. The gap matters only as a record: the release note does not say how Barometer fees were reported between October 6 and November 17, or whether the new fields cover spending from before November 17.
The podcast supply that Barometer screens on Amazon has grown in steps. Amazon's buying platform gained SiriusXM Media's US audio supply through the AdsWizz exchange in September 2025, with podcasts to follow. The ART19 Podcast Audience Network, with more than 1,000 shows, was added for US advertisers in January 2026, and Spotify added podcast inventory to its direct integration in July 2026. When Amazon opened audio campaigns to its API in June 2026, podcasts were still excluded from that route.
How outside screening fees are charged
Amazon's pricing transparency guide describes Amazon Display, Video and Audio campaigns, formerly Amazon DSP campaigns, as using a cost-plus-fees model, with separate fees for features an advertiser chooses to switch on. According to the guide, charges for third-party pre-bid targeting are invoiced at the vendor's published CPM price, meaning a set price per thousand impressions. The same guide says Amazon handles the billing between the advertiser and the outside service provider.
That arrangement took years to arrive. When Amazon's buying platform first added pre-bid targeting from DoubleVerify, Integral Ad Science and Oracle Data Cloud in April 2020, Amazon said billing was not yet available for third-party targeting and filtering. By September 19, 2023, when Amazon added Integral Ad Science's Context Control products for brand suitability and contextual targeting on outside supply, the terms were set out in the release note: "Advertisers will pay a CPM fee from their campaign budgets for the use of these products."
The newest vendors have followed the same path into reporting. Adelaide brought its attention-based pre-bid segments to Amazon's buying platform in June 2026. On August 26, 2026, Amazon's release notes added the matching fee fields: "The Third-party fees metric family adds metric.thirdPartyPrebidFeesAdelaide and metric.thirdPartyPrebidFeesAdelaideConverted, which report the third-party prebid fees for using Adelaide." Barometer's fields follow the same naming pattern, with the vendor's name at the end.
The fee fields around it
Amazon's reference page for the Third-party fees family, part of the metric library for version 1 of its reporting API, states that the family contains 48 fields. The documented fields carry the currency data type and list Amazon DSP as the only eligible program. The table records the vendors and fee slots that Amazon names, with the equivalent column names from its older DSP reports where the page gives them.
All field names in the table start with "metric." in the API.
The "absorbed" fields need a word of explanation. According to Amazon's descriptions, an absorbed fee is itemized in reports but excluded from total costs. In other words, the charge is shown for the record but does not add to the cost figure the advertiser sees. The release notes list no absorbed version for either Barometer or Adelaide. Each prebid vendor has two fields, one per currency.
A notice at the top of Amazon's older DSP reporting metrics page says the Amazon DSP v2 reporting API will be deprecated on June 30, 2027. That page lists separate fee columns for DoubleVerify and Integral Ad Science, along with automotive data and a 3PFees column that it describes as the total CPM charges for using third-party data providers.
Where agency fees come in
The fee line also matters to agencies. On November 11, Amazon updated its help page on agency fees, and PPC Land reported that agencies can use the legacy per-campaign fee setting only until January 31, 2027. The page lists five calculation methods. Four are markups on supply cost, the price of the media itself, and each adds one more cost line to the base. The broadest is "% of Supply Cost + Total Audience Fee + 3P Prebid", and Amazon's page defines 3P prebid fees as fees for using third-party prebid targeting in campaigns.
Amazon labels the Barometer charge "Third-party prebid fees (Barometer)". On Amazon's own definitions, an agency that charges its client on the broadest base would therefore calculate its agency fee on top of what the client paid Barometer, as well as on the media and audience data.
Two days after the Barometer note, on November 19, 2026, Amazon's release notes opened the same fee settings to an API. "Flexible agency fee management is now in open beta in all DVA+ (ADSP) marketplaces," according to Amazon. "Five calculation methods support both markup and margin pricing: four markup methods based on supply cost and one margin method (% of Budget)." The same entry says that "all agency fees roll up into the Agency fees column in reports." Taken together, the two notes mean the vendor's charge and the agency's charge can each be read as a separate figure.
How the charge reaches a report
Read together, Amazon's and Barometer's documents describe the path in five steps.
- An advertiser buying podcast ads in DVA+ selects Barometer's targeting or brand safety segments, which Barometer says have been available there since October 6, 2026.
- Before a bid is placed, each episode is checked against the advertiser's suitability settings, and episodes that fail are skipped, according to Barometer's description of its pre-bid product.
- Amazon invoices third-party pre-bid targeting at the vendor's published CPM price and handles the billing between advertiser and vendor, according to its pricing transparency guide.
- The charge appears in Amazon DSP reports in Ads API v1 as metric.thirdPartyPrebidFeesBarometer, in the budget currency, and metric.thirdPartyPrebidFeesBarometerConverted, in the currency selected for conversion, according to the November 17 release note.
- Where an agency uses the "% of Supply Cost + Total Audience Fee + 3P Prebid" method, its fee is calculated on a base that includes third-party prebid fees, and it is reported in the Agency fees column, according to Amazon's agency fee page and the November 19 release note.
