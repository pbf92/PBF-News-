TITLE: How long it took me to drive the entire GTA 6 map - and all the mad things that happened on the way
OUTLET: GAMINGbible

Grand Theft Auto VI is several times larger than anything Rockstar Games has released so far, and it really feels that way when you’re exploring the open world.
However, I decided to take a road trip from one end of the map to the other to test how big it really is.
Starting in GTA VI's opening area, Key Lento, my journey concluded in Bonanza. There are some islands further North of this location, but Bonanza is the furthest you can go in a car before having to swap to boats or aircraft.
The journey was also done in the Stainer given to you by the Ultimate Edition, with the best engine upgrades and a full tank of gas.

In total, I’m shocked to say that it only took me 10 real-world minutes to arrive in Bonanza from Key Lento.
That’s with me leaving the in-game GPS to determine the quickest route, so the freeway was used.
It’s worth remembering that the Stainer isn’t a supercar either. My upgraded model is pretty speedy, but it’d be easily outclassed by a lot of other in-game vehicles.
There were even a few crashes along the way to impede my progress, but in the end, they hardly affected how long it took.
Had I taken some more explorative routes, the journey would have probably taken considerably longer, but if speed is the aim of your game, it won’t take you long at all to circle Leonida entirely.

While driving across GTA 6’s entire map was fast, it was also memorable
As I said before, there were a few crashes on my journey to Bonanza and all three of them required me to fix my vehicle.
The first was caused by distraction. An influencer was standing atop a highway bridge and because I was staring at him on the approach, I didn’t see the enormous truck parked up ahead.
While ordering a freshly repaired vehicle, I used the wait time to help the influencer take a sick selfie for Snapmatic, which almost ended in disaster when he nearly fell to his death. I helped him up though, don’t worry.
The second crash was due to severe road rage, and surprisingly it wasn’t the Grand Theft Auto player at fault … While zipping through traffic, I accidentally scraped someone’s car, and while the offence barely registered in my brain, the other driver was clearly flooded with rage.
They chased me down the highway at speed and in a last-ditch effort for payback, they collided with one of my rear wheels and sent me rolling off the road… Another call to Scooter Bros was made.
The final crash was near the end of my journey. Because Bonanza is in Lummox County, the terrain becomes a nightmare for vehicles not built for off-roading.
It didn’t help that GTA VI purposefully made all roads narrower for realism, so what should have been the home-stretch led to me losing control and hurtling down a hill into some trees. By that point, I could see the end in sight and huffed it to the goal.
The whole journey, to me, summed up exactly why GTA VI is so impressive. What should have been a simple, leisurely drive was made memorable by random interactions that you could have never predicted.
It’s why exploring Leonida is so compelling, and why you should probably avoid the freeways if you want to see all the best stuff.
Next time I might walk...
