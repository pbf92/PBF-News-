TITLE: Critics say 'GTA 6' delivers, fans are mixed
OUTLET: www.thepostathens.com

After 13 years of waiting, “Grand Theft Auto VI” finally arrived Nov. 19, and it looks like Rockstar Games has delivered to the enormous expectations. As many expected, “GTA VI” has already made an immediate impact, debuting with a 99 on Metacritic and taking the title of the highest-rated game of all time from The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (1998), which held that position for 28 years.
The praise has not been limited to Metacritic, either. A roundup shared by X account @DiscussingFilm showed an impressive number of perfect scores from major gaming and entertainment outlets, with reviewers awarding 10/10s or 5/5s across the board, even from infamously tough critics like IGN and EDGE magazine. As X user @iampassword replied, “Getting a 10/10 from EDGE Magazine is genuinely insane; they’ve given out less than 30 perfect scores in over 30 years.”
That level of critical acclaim has put “GTA VI” in a league of its own, but it’s worth looking at how it compares to the other major releases of 2026. It’s already been a strong year for gaming, with several highly anticipated titles earning impressive reviews of their own, even if Rockstar’s latest release has managed to outscore them all.
Capcom’s “Resident Evil Requiem,” released in February, was a major success in its own right. The ninth mainline entry in the horror franchise earned an average score of 89 on Metacritic, while IGN awarded it a 9/10. The most recent competition came from Nintendo’s “Ocarina of Time” remake, which reached an impressive 95 on Metacritic, making it the highest-rated game of 2026 for a brief moment before “GTA VI” arrived just two weeks later.
If the reviews alone seemed impressive, the commercial performance proved to be even more staggering. According to Forbes, Rockstar announced that “GTA VI” generated an estimated $3.8 billion in worldwide retail sales on its first day, potentially making it the biggest entertainment launch in history. The game also reportedly sold 32.5 million copies before release. For comparison, “Grand Theft Auto V” generated approximately $800 million on its first day in 2013, meaning the sequel pulled in nearly five times as much revenue in its opening 24 hours.
Despite the near-perfect critic scores and revenue, “GTA VI” has not escaped criticism entirely. One of the biggest talking points has been performance. Output Lag’s review found that the game targets 30 frames per second across consoles, including the PlayStation 5 Pro. For context, many modern games aim for 60 frames per second, which produces noticeably smoother movement and camera transitions. Although the drops are not particularly severe, as Video Games Chronicle’s analysis highlights, the 30 frame rate cap is still a little disappointing, especially for players accustomed to the smoother 60 frame rate experience.
Player reactions have also been considerably less positive than those of professional reviewers, most notably on Metacritic. Whether these user reviews are genuine is unclear, as Dexerto recently reported that “GTA VI” became caught up in a review-bombing battle involving fans of the “Ocarina of Time” remake. Given Metacritic allows users to rate games without proving ownership, it’s now increasingly harder to separate criticism from coordinated attempts to lower scores for either game.
Interestingly, player feedback looks considerably different on the PlayStation Store, where “GTA VI” currently holds a 4.81/5 rating. Unlike Metacritic, PlayStation reviews are tied to purchases, meaning users generally need to own the game to leave a rating. While neither score tells the complete story, the difference suggests that the reception among actual players is similarly positive to critics after all. That being said, there have been valid questions raised about the near-universal praise of “GTA VI.”
One reviewer on Metacritic, whose review has been circulating on X, acknowledged that “GTA VI” was not without its flaws, going as far as to explicitly say “GTA VI” “isn’t a perfect game,” but still awarded it a perfect 100/100. The decision, highlighted by X user @_rawrell, has sparked debate about what a perfect review score should represent and whether a game with noticeable shortcomings can truly deserve one.
Still, the overall picture is hard to ignore. There seems to be an equal amount of genuine love for the game as there is hate, which is expected with a game as massive culturally as “GTA VI” is and will continue to be. Regardless of the reviews, both negative and positive, one thing is clear: “GTA VI” has already secured its place as one of the biggest releases in entertainment history.
