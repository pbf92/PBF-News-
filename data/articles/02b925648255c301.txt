Nvidia Lost $901 Billion Last Week. It Won Back All of It Before the Opening Bell
This post may contain links from our sponsors and affiliates, and Flywheel Publishing may receive compensation for actions taken through them.
Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) lost about $901 billion of market value last week, a record one-week loss in dollars, as its shares fell from $162.85 to $125.53. On Monday it won all of that back before the opening bell. At $163.32 as of 9:20 a.m. ET, up 30% after the board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, Nvidia is worth about $3.94 trillion, up roughly $913 billion from Friday’s $3.03 trillion.
That puts the stock back above where it closed on Nov. 13, the Friday before the sell-off week. It’s still 32% below its Oct. 6 record of $239.24, when the company was worth about $5.78 trillion.
Why a single morning can move it this much
Nvidia has about 24.1 billion shares outstanding, so every $1 move in the stock changes its value by about $24.1 billion. Monday’s jump of about $37.79 a share is worth more than the entire market value of most companies in the S&P 500.
The trigger was the board’s 8:55 a.m. release. It voted unanimously to reinstate Huang as president, CEO and director, effective immediately, 12 days after accepting his resignation, and said that after input from senior levels of the U.S. government it is confident his return “poses no national security risk.”
What the price buys now
Even after the jump, Nvidia trades at about 18 times its earnings of the past four quarters ($9.31 a share), based on the figures Alpha Vantage carried at Friday’s close. On analysts’ forward estimates, the stock went into Monday at 7.93 times earnings. At 9:20 a.m. ET’s price that is about 10.3 times.
Analyst ratings were lopsided going into Monday. Of the 62 analysts in that data, 59 rated the stock a buy or strong buy, and their average price target was $244.18, about 50% above where the stock traded this morning.
What did not change
The board’s investigation into reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort continues “to conclusion,” and Nvidia’s quarterly report for the period ended in October has been delayed. The company said it has formed a National Security and Export Control Committee and is searching for a chief compliance officer.
For a holder, the round trip is the lesson of the past two weeks. Anyone who sold at Friday’s close of $125.53 to stop the bleeding missed a 30% move that came in a single morning, before the opening bell.
