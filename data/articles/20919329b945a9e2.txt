TITLE: Cathedrals and churches to illuminate red for persecuted Christians
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Cathedrals and churches across the UK will be illuminated red on Wednesday evening, in solidarity with persecuted and suffering Christians around the world.
Catholic charity Aid to the Church in Need (ACN) is marking the tenth anniversary of its Red Wednesday campaign, to raise awareness and support for Christians who face violence, oppression and discrimination.
It comes after ACN’s report, Persecuted and Forgotten?, which warned that some of Cristianity’s oldest communities face an increasingly uncertain future with millions having been forced from their homes through conflict, extremism and authoritarian repression.
St George’s Cathedral in Southwark, London, will host the main event, featuring light displays and live music. Mass will be celebrated by Firas Lutfi, who has ministered to communities throughout war in Syria.
ACN’s Courage to Be Christian Award will be presented to Sr Annie Demerjian, a Syrian religious sister who has been dedicated to supporting families affected by violence, poverty and displacement. Having partnered with the charity, she will share her testimony of the realities facing Christians and all who suffer in Syria.
Catholic cathedrals across England, Wales and Scotland will also be participating, including a service with hundreds of school pupils and teachers at St Chad’s Cathedral, Birmingham. An Anglican Evensong will also be held at St Bartholomew the Great in Smithfield, London, on 19 November.
Supporters of the charity have been fundraising for Red Wednesday, including a man who cycled nearly 3,000 miles from Westminster Cathedral to Jerusalem.
