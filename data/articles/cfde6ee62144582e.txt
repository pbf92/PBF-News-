TITLE: Anthropic Is Pressing on With Thursday IPO, Despite a Spooked Market
OUTLET: SFist

OpenAI's decision to delay its IPO likely to next year is seeming wiser by the day, as Anthropic is now poised to have its much ballyhooed debut on Wall Street this week, at just about the worst time for the AI industry in recent memory.
Anthropic is set to have its initial public offering on Thursday morning, as the Associated Press reports, an event that has been anticipated for much of this year, and which the company hopes will raise over $60 billion.
At one point in early June, it looked like we'd be seeing dueling IPOs from competitors OpenAI and Anthropic by this point in the year, with both companies having filed initial paperwork with the SEC. And the popularity of Anthropic's Claude AI model has only grown since those Super Bowl ads earlier this year, which presaged the company's rise to a valuation of $965 billion.
Anthropic is now targeting a valuation in the $1.5 trillion to $1.6 trillion range following Thursday's IPO. That puts it close to the range of SpaceX's valuation at IPO, which was over $1.7 trillion and briefly at $2.1 trillion that day in June. SpaceX's market cap and stock price peaked on October 6 at $2.3 trillion, and it's been volatile ever since.
Anthropic's initial share price was said to be between $70 and $78, and is more than likely going to push past $80 in the first day of trading. CNBC reported today that the IPO price may actually be higher than that initially stated range.
As the AP notes, another company who's share price has mostly trending downward over the past two weeks due its own internal crisis, Nvidia, was planning to purchase $10 billion in Anthropic stock in the IPO. Nvidia's own valuation has plummeted over $1 trillion over the past month — it peaked on October 9 at $5.54 trillion, and today the company is worth around $3.71 trillion, thanks to a stock price that has dropped consistently over the last several days.
The volatility in the market can cut both ways for Anthropic, as the AP explains. "It might be signaling the much debated AI bubble could burst soon so it's better to sell stock now, fast," the AP writes. "And history shows demand for IPOs can disappear quickly."
If that is true, OpenAI's delay may end up being the unwise choice, especially if it needs to raise cash next year.
Jitters around AI stocks extend beyond Nvdia, as well. As the AP notes, the American public showed broad hostility to data centers in this election cycle, and a backlash over the loss of jobs could be the next thing to come. Newly elected members of Congress may end up being more hostile to the industry as new regulations get drafted next year.
One AI stock, CoreWeave, has seen wild swings in value, the AP reports, because of investor fears that it won't be able to borrow the funds it needs for its short-term growth.
Anthropic, the company, is a mere five years old, after being founded by a group of OpenAI defectors including Dario Amodei, his sister Daniela, and AI researcher Jared Kaplan.
According to its prospectus, for the nine months that ended September 30, Anthropic earned $33.24 billion in revenue, up about 1,200% from the same period last year. Anthropic also revealed an $80 billion loss in that prospectus, for the first six months of this year, and said that its AI models could, if not appropriately guardrailed, pose "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity."
