TITLE: Glutamine Lowers Pulmonary Infections and Improves Immune Markers After Gastrectomy
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis evaluating clinical data from thirty-four randomized controlled trials indicates that perioperative glutamine supplementation significantly strengthens immune function and nutritional status while decreasing specific clinical complications in adult patients undergoing surgical resection for gastric cancer. Published by a research team from Chengdu University in Frontiers in Nutrition, the pooled analysis synthesized data from 2,464 surgical patients to determine the independent physiological effects of glutamine administered as a solitary immunonutritional adjunct alongside conventional postoperative nutritional support regimens. The synthesis demonstrated that patients receiving supplemental glutamine achieved marked elevations across essential humoral and cellular immune parameters, accompanied by substantial improvements in key circulating serum proteins. Beyond biochemical indicators, the intervention conferred tangible clinical advantages during the recovery phase, reducing the relative risk of postoperative pulmonary infections by sixty percent and significantly curtailing overall hospitalization duration compared with standard or isocaloric control nutrition protocols without glutamine.
Gastric cancer continues to rank among the most pervasive digestive malignancies and represents a principal contributor to cancer-related morbidity and mortality worldwide. While contemporary oncological management increasingly incorporates multimodal interventions including systemic chemotherapy, targeted biological therapies, and immune checkpoint inhibitors, radical surgical gastrectomy remains the primary curative therapeutic option for patients presenting with resectable localized disease. Nonetheless, candidates undergoing gastrectomy are often in a catabolic state prior to surgery, driven by chronic tumor-related consumption, increased nutritional risk, inadequate protein reserves, and impaired immune function. The surgical resection itself imposes immense operative trauma, inducing severe neuroendocrine dysregulation, systemic inflammatory cascade activation, and acute immunosuppression. The compounding physiological burdens of general anesthesia, extensive tissue disruption, and elevated metabolic repair demands further accelerate hypermetabolism, aggravate negative nitrogen balance, and depress cellular and humoral defenses, heightening vulnerability to infections and delayed healing.
Glutamine serves as a conditionally essential amino acid that plays an indispensable regulatory role in preserving intestinal mucosal barrier integrity, supplying critical metabolic substrate to proliferating lymphocytes and macrophages, and modulating inflammatory responses. Under physiological homeostasis, endogenous glutamine production satisfies cellular requirements; however, severe surgical trauma, systemic infection, and acute hypermetabolic states rapidly deplete circulating pools beyond endogenous biosynthetic capacity. To rigorously isolate the specific clinical influence of glutamine, the researchers executed a protocol registered prospectively in the PROSPERO database under identifier CRD420261345534, conforming strictly to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses guidelines. Comprehensive systematic literature searches spanned inception through March 9, 2026, across major Chinese repositories—including CNKI, Wanfang Data, VIP, and SinoMed—and primary international medical indexing databases, including PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, the Cochrane Library, and Ovid MEDLINE. Studies combining glutamine with other active immunonutritional substances were systematically excluded.
The quantitative synthesis ultimately encompassed thirty-four randomized controlled trials published between 2003 and 2025, with individual study sample sizes ranging from 30 to 148 participants. All included trials were conducted across clinical centers within China and enrolled adult surgical patients undergoing partial, total, or radical gastrectomy. Formulations evaluated in the intervention arms comprised alanyl-glutamine dipeptide intravenous infusions, enteral glutamine granules, and compound glutamine preparations, administered either enterally or parenterally in addition to baseline postoperative nutrition. Enteral feeding constituted the predominant delivery pathway across the assembled trials. In the vast majority of trial protocols, glutamine administration was initiated within the initial twenty-four postoperative hours and maintained for a standard seven-day course. Dosages generally ranged between 0.3 and 0.5 grams per kilogram of body weight daily, or approximately 30 grams per day, establishing a consistent basis for comparative stratified analyses.
Evaluation of humoral immune parameters revealed uniform, statistically significant enhancements attributable to glutamine supplementation. Pooled analysis of seventeen studies evaluating 1,132 patients demonstrated a substantial increase in circulating immunoglobulin G levels, yielding a mean difference of 1.74 (95% CI: 1.27 to 2.21; p < 0.00001) under a random-effects model, which was selected to accommodate substantial observed statistical heterogeneity. Assessment of immunoglobulin M across fourteen trials encompassing 881 individuals demonstrated a significant increase, with a mean difference of 0.19 (95% CI: 0.12 to 0.25; p < 0.00001) analyzed through a fixed-effect model. Similarly, sixteen trials evaluating 1,002 surgical patients demonstrated elevated immunoglobulin A concentrations, yielding a pooled mean difference of 0.43 (95% CI: 0.35 to 0.50; p < 0.00001).
Subgroup analyses stratified by intervention intensity highlighted notable dose-dependent variations in humoral immune reconstitution. For immunoglobulin G, high-dose regimens defined as at least 0.3 grams per kilogram daily or 30 grams daily generated a more pronounced elevation (MD: 2.10; 95% CI: 1.73 to 2.47; p < 0.00001) with markedly reduced heterogeneity, whereas low-dose protocols produced a smaller increment (MD: 1.16; 95% CI: 0.49 to 1.83; p = 0.0007), establishing a statistically significant between-subgroup divergence (p = 0.02). Immunoglobulin M mirrored this pattern, exhibiting significant improvements solely within high-dose cohorts (MD: 0.25; p < 0.001) compared to nonsignificant increases in low-dose arms (MD: 0.09; p = 0.07). Conversely, immunoglobulin A demonstrated equivalent gains across both dosage strata without significant between-group divergence. Route of administration also explained heterogeneity in immunoglobulin G outcomes (p = 0.04), where parenteral infusion produced greater pooled increases (MD: 2.44) than enteral delivery (MD: 1.47), although both pathways achieved statistical significance.
Analysis of cellular immune markers demonstrated that glutamine supplementation counteracted the profound T-lymphocyte suppression typical of extensive upper gastrointestinal resections. Pooling data from twenty-three trials encompassing 1,587 patients showed a significant elevation in CD4+ T-cell percentages, with a pooled mean difference of 5.46 percentage points (95% CI: 4.49 to 6.44; p < 0.00001) under random-effects modeling. Concurrently, twenty-two studies involving 1,457 individuals identified a modest, borderline statistically significant reduction in CD8+ levels, showing a mean difference of -1.40 (95% CI: -2.77 to -0.02; p = 0.05). Consequently, the overall CD4+/CD8+ ratio, evaluated across twenty studies involving 1,332 participants, increased significantly by a mean difference of 0.30 (95% CI: 0.20 to 0.39; p < 0.00001).
Stratified analyses of cellular outcomes revealed distinct biological responses depending on dosing and administration schedules. For CD8+ T lymphocytes, high-dose glutamine produced a statistically significant reduction (MD: -2.62; 95% CI: -4.33 to -0.91; p = 0.003), whereas low-dose supplementation exhibited no significant suppressive effect (MD: 0.50; 95% CI: -0.78 to 1.78; p = 0.45), generating a statistically significant between-subgroup difference (p = 0.004). In contrast, CD4+ lymphocyte percentages and the CD4+/CD8+ ratio increased across both dosage levels without significant between-subgroup divergence. Timing of intervention initiation exerted a borderline significant influence on CD4+ recovery (p = 0.05), where administration initiated preoperatively yielded an MD of 4.02, intervention within twenty-four hours postoperatively yielded an MD of 4.89, and administration at or beyond twenty-four hours yielded an MD of 6.43, demonstrating that supplementation schedule contributes to inter-study variability.
Circulating biochemical markers of nutritional status demonstrated marked postoperative preservation following glutamine intervention. Prealbumin concentrations, reported in sixteen trials with 1,123 patients, exhibited an overall mean difference of 26.18 (95% CI: 19.73 to 32.63; p < 0.00001). Serum albumin, documented across twenty-two trials comprising 1,665 subjects, showed a mean difference of 3.06 (95% CI: 2.42 to 3.70; p < 0.00001), with high-dose regimens inducing greater increases (MD: 3.61) than low-dose protocols (MD: 2.30; between-group p = 0.02). Transferrin, measured in nine studies including 507 patients, rose by a mean difference of 0.14 (95% CI: 0.09 to 0.19; p < 0.00001). Sensitivity testing excluding a trial by Song conducted in 2009 that extended past seven days confirmed the robustness of prealbumin gains (MD: 26.65; p < 0.00001), indicating that visceral protein synthesis is reliably supported during acute convalescence.
Direct assessment of short-term clinical endpoints illustrated tangible benefits alongside important therapeutic boundaries. Synthesis of nine studies involving 777 patients demonstrated that glutamine supplementation significantly curtailed the incidence of postoperative pulmonary infections, yielding a pooled risk ratio of 0.40 (95% CI: 0.21 to 0.75; p = 0.004) under a fixed-effect model. Furthermore, meta-analysis of eleven trials comprising 771 patients established a significant reduction in overall hospital stay, with treated patients being discharged an average of 1.64 days sooner than controls (MD: -1.64; 95% CI: -2.34 to -0.93; p < 0.00001). However, pooled estimates failed to confirm statistically significant reductions in anastomotic leakage across five studies with 464 patients (RR: 0.39; 95% CI: 0.13 to 1.14; p = 0.09) or surgical site infections across five trials with 518 patients (RR: 0.45; 95% CI: 0.18 to 1.10; p = 0.08), leaving the efficacy of glutamine against direct wound complications inconclusive.
Methodological quality assessment using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool highlighted notable reporting limitations across the source literature. While eleven trials explicitly documented appropriate random sequence generation utilizing random number tables, the remaining twenty-three studies provided insufficient procedural descriptions. Allocation concealment was explicitly confirmed in only a single trial utilizing sealed envelopes, and details regarding blinding of trial participants, personnel, and outcome assessors were largely omitted. Consequently, multiple risk-of-bias domains were categorized as having an unclear risk across most included studies. Quantitative evaluation for potential publication bias was conducted for outcomes supported by more than ten datasets using Stata 18. Egger linear regression tests and visual inspection of funnel plots demonstrated no significant small-study effects for immunoglobulin G (p = 0.36) or the CD4+/CD8+ ratio (p = 0.51), indicating that systemic reporting bias was unlikely to drive the primary statistical conclusions.
