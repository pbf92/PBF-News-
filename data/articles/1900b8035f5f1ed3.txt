US Stocks Diverge: Dow Falls 369 Points in Continued Slide, Nvidia's Strong Earnings Underpin Nasdaq
In the morning session of the New York stock market on the 18th, direction diverged between economically sensitive stocks and tech shares. The blue-chip Dow Jones Industrial Average was down 369.50 points from the previous close at 50,389.92 as of 10:05 a.m., extending its decline, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite turned higher, gaining 138.77 points to 24,467.15.
Note: Dow figure as of 10:05 a.m.; Nasdaq figure at the same time.
On the 18th, major US retailers including Target and Lowe's reported earnings in succession. Caution over additional rate hikes by the US Federal Reserve also weighed on the market, with selling in defensive names such as healthcare and retail stocks leading the way from the opening bell. The Dow opened lower.
Retail earnings were generally solid. Target's August–October 2026 quarter saw same-store sales rise 5.6% year-on-year (vs. market expectation of 3.61% growth), with earnings per share of $2.65 (vs. market expectation of $2.08, including a $0.50 tariff refund), and full-year EPS guidance of $10.65–$11.40. Lowe's also reported earnings that beat market expectations.
On monetary policy, Fed Governor Waller said in a CNBC television interview on the 18th that he is "very concerned" about inflation moving in an undesirable direction. He also noted that price increases are "broadening" and indicated that "a more aggressive approach is needed" to achieve price stability. He said the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) should raise rates by 0.5% at its December 8–9 meeting, and argued that even if the committee settles for 0.25%, it should clearly signal an additional hike in January. "We may need to surprise people," he said, also indicating that the three rate cuts implemented in 2025 need to be reversed. Markets have fully priced in a 0.25% hike in December, with additional hikes in January and 2027 also being anticipated. Growing expectations for a year-end rate hike are capping upside for the stock market.
On the other hand, Nvidia's strong earnings provided support for the market. The company's August–October 2026 quarter results, announced on the 17th, showed revenue of $115.844 billion and net income of $65.287 billion, both record quarterly highs. For the November 2026–January 2027 quarter, the company also provided revenue guidance of around $130 billion, up 91% and above market expectations.
Note: The Nov–Jan guidance includes one more week of business days than usual and does not factor in any China data center revenue. Gross margin guidance is 71.0–72.0%, expected to decline from 74.8% this quarter.
Following the strong earnings and bullish guidance, the company's shares rose in after-hours trading. Concerns about the outlook for artificial intelligence (AI)-related companies eased, and buying flowed into AI and semiconductor-related names.
That said, Nvidia's stock closed at $155.46 on the 17th, down about 17% year-to-date and roughly 35% below its October high of around $240. The lack of a permanent successor CEO and uncertainty surrounding its China business have weighed on the stock. In this earnings report, the company disclosed that it has halted shipments of its China-bound data center product "H200." August–October revenue from H200 in China was $2.4 billion, and the November–January guidance does not factor in any China data center revenue. Founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on November 12, and the board has denied he will return. This was the company's first earnings announcement since his resignation.
Among Dow components, UnitedHealth Group fell more than 4%, leading the Dow's decline. Salesforce and Chevron also traded weakly, while Disney held firm.
The healthcare stock selloff has a specific catalyst. The US Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) on the 18th published a proposed anti-fraud rule called "CRUSH" that would strengthen audits of insurers and could require them to return overpayments. Humana, Cigna, and CVS Health all fell in tandem.
The same divergence is occurring in the Japanese market. In the Tokyo stock market on the 18th, the Nikkei Stock Average rebounded for the first time in three trading days, rising 163.85 yen to 59,024.28. The gain came as buying flowed into semiconductor-related stocks following Nvidia's strong earnings. However, stocks outside semiconductors were generally weak, and the TOPIX fell 16.02 points to 3,824.00. Advancing issues accounted for 26% while declining issues made up 72%, with selling dominating despite the index's rise.
In the market, expectations for AI-related growth continue to support tech stocks, while caution over rising interest rates weighs on a broad range of sectors. Investors appear poised to look for clues on the pace of rate hikes through comments from Fed officials and upcoming inflation data.
