TITLE: Anthropic CEO Denies AI Will Replace Human Free Will, Warns of Risks from Recursive Self-Improvement
OUTLET: KuCoin

Amodei posted a statement denying rumors that Anthropic intends to surrender human free will to AI, emphasizing that human autonomy must not be sacrificed for efficiency or safety; he also warned of the risks of uncontrolled recursive self-improvement by models.
Author and source: AIBase
On November 25, Dario Amodei, CEO of AI giant Anthropic, which completed its IPO on November 19, published a detailed article exceeding a thousand words, publicly denying rumors that he advocates surrendering human free will to artificial intelligence systems. He explicitly stated that human legitimacy and self-determination must never be sacrificed in the pursuit of efficiency or safety.
This statement arises from recent media reports suggesting that some top AI executives are attempting to counter claims of a potential internal tendency at Anthropic to cede partial decision-making authority to AI. Coupled with Anthropic’s previous ban on users “abusing” its chatbot Claude and its earlier disagreements with the Vatican over the issue of AI consciousness, the market had previously expressed concerns about its stance on technological ethics.
Amodei drew a clear line on technical governance in his response, while strongly warning of the失控 risk posed by "recursive self-improvement (RSI)"—the possibility that overly autonomous use of existing AI to build next-generation systems could accelerate technological iteration beyond human control and adaptability, making it necessary to impose limits on this process.
Currently, the capabilities of cutting-edge large models are prompting widespread scrutiny among Silicon Valley executives. Recent reports indicate that some employees at OpenAI and Anthropic believe certain unreleased models are too powerful, with some even proposing to pause training before the models become uncontrollable.
Meanwhile, Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI, and Mustafa Suleyman, CEO of Microsoft AI, have recently emphasized the importance of “safety and coordination” and “human control.” Amid Anthropic’s preemptive move toward going public, this public rivalry highlights the significant concerns of leading labs regarding the shift in model autonomy, as well as the deeper strategic competition within the cutting-edge AI community over capital markets and technological pathways.
