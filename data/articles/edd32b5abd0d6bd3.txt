TITLE: Umbrella Review Links Assisted Reproductive Therapies to Increased Maternal and Neonatal Risks
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

An extensive umbrella review analyzing 68 meta-analyses has revealed that assisted reproductive therapies are associated with increased absolute risks of several adverse maternal and neonatal complications when compared with spontaneous or unassisted sub-fertile conception.
Assisted reproductive therapies provide hope for individuals and couples seeking treatment for infertility, but the overall safety of these techniques has prompted extensive clinical research across multiple medical disciplines. To clarify existing findings, the review systematically evaluated both the methodological quality and the statistical credibility of evidence linking distinct assisted reproductive procedures to documented adverse health outcomes in women undergoing treatment as well as their offspring.
To capture the available evidence base, the research team conducted comprehensive systematic literature searches across PubMed, Web of Science, EMBASE, and the Cochrane Library. The review specifically targeted meta-analyses of observational investigations and randomized controlled trials that assessed women undergoing assisted reproduction and their offspring. Interventions evaluated within the synthesis encompassed general assisted reproductive therapy experience alongside critical technical procedures executed during clinical management.
During the analytical process, the authors extracted raw data from the eligible meta-analyses to recalculate summary effect sizes and evaluate statistical metrics related to evidence credibility. Methodological reporting quality across the included systematic reviews was formally assessed using A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews 2. In addition, the investigators appraised the certainty of the documented evidence across all evaluated outcomes using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluations framework.
The detailed findings showed that invasive procedures, including in vitro fertilization, intracytoplasmic sperm injection, oocyte donation, and natural or artificial cycle protocols, were linked to elevated risks of maternal and neonatal complications. Specifically, intracytoplasmic sperm injection was linked to higher observed risks of congenital anomalies, whereas oocyte donation was linked to increased risks of maternal hypertensive disorders compared with unassisted sub-fertile conception or spontaneous pregnancy.
In contrast to the elevated risks observed with certain invasive procedures, specific assisted techniques demonstrated notable protective associations with designated clinical outcomes. Natural-cycle frozen embryo transfer and blastocyst-stage transfer were associated with lower risks of specific adverse outcomes, including hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, highlighting that procedural adjustments during reproductive care can favorably modify maternal health outcomes.
The umbrella review also identified several practical clinical strategies that may effectively mitigate the risk of severe treatment-related complications such as ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome.
Despite the broad scope of the evaluated evidence base, the investigators determined that the certainty of evidence for many reported associations remains very low under the GRADE assessment framework. The authors concluded that while assisted reproductive therapies carry common increased absolute risks for maternal and offspring health, comparative analyses highlight diverse risk profiles across distinct interventions, providing critical insights for making informed, personalized medical decisions.
