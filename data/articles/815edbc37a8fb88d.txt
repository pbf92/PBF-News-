TITLE: How a piece of Detroit ended up inside GTA VI
OUTLET: ClickOnDetroit | WDIV Local 4

What to know:
- Detroit-based 1xRUN helped bring work by more than 50 real-world artists into Grand Theft Auto VI, with art appearing on cars, stickers, billboards, and around Vice City.
- The featured artists include Detroit-connected Denial and Tristan Eaton, plus the collective Faile and Shepard Fairey.
- 1xRUN founder Jesse Cory says the game offers a global showcase for Detroit’s creative community; Rockstar reported $3.79 billion in first-day retail sales.
A Detroit company played a key role in making Grand Theft Auto VI the most popular video game of all time.
Detroit-based art company 1xRUN helped curate street art, murals, and tattoos for GTA VI, bringing work by more than 50 real-world artists into the game.
“There is a piece of Detroit, really, in the game,” said 1xRUN founder Jesse Cory.
The company licenses, publishes, and curates contemporary art, and has produced hundreds of murals across Detroit, including projects in Eastern Market.
Cory said 1xRUN was contacted about bringing real-world art into the game and began connecting artists with the game’s producers during its creative process.
Players can spot the work on cars, stickers, billboards, and throughout Vice City.
One in-game van features artwork by Denial, an artist with ties to Detroit and Windsor.
Other work includes art by Detroit-born Tristan Eaton, a tattoo shop created by the artist collective Faile, and a piece by Shepard Fairey, who is also known as Obey.
Faile is known internationally for its art and has a Detroit space called Deluxx Fluxx, Cory said.
For Cory, seeing the artists’ work in a game with a global audience is a chance to highlight Detroit’s creative community.
“As a Detroit-based business, it’s really exciting to be a part of these projects that, you know, are global,” he said.
Rockstar Games said Grand Theft Auto 6 brought in $3.79 billion in retail sales on its first day.
