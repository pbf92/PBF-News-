TITLE: 31% of US homes with LG TVs plan bigger holiday budgets, 65% in Brazil
OUTLET: PPC Land

LG Ad Solutions said on November 24 that its holiday shopping research, "From Watch List to Wish List," is now available in five markets: the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia and Brazil. The research describes homes with at least one LG television and covers three subjects: how much they watch over the holidays, what they plan to buy, and how they say they respond to the advertising on their screens.
LG tied the release to the calendar. Black Friday falls on November 27 this year, three days after the announcement, and the company put the question to advertisers bluntly: "Black Friday is upon us. Shipping deadlines are approaching. And the window to reach holiday shoppers is closing fast. With media plans largely set, where can advertisers focus final investments?"
LG's answer rests on a survey of 2,000 LG TV households, 400 in each country, carried out between September 29 and October 9, 2026. The company pairs the survey with viewing figures recorded on LG televisions during last year's season, from November 27, 2025 to January 4, 2026, and with click rates from ads it ran on the LG TV home screen in December 2025. Each country gets its own report, and all five follow the same structure, section by section. Read side by side, they show how differently the same kind of household behaves from one market to the next.
In Short
LG's advertising business surveyed 2,000 homes that own an LG TV in the US, Canada, the UK, Australia and Brazil, and set the answers next to viewing data from LG sets during last year's holidays. Between 79% and 90% of these homes say they shop or look up products while the TV is on at the holidays, and most say they are more open to ads for deals and gifts at this time of year. Spending plans split sharply by country: 65% in Brazil and 60% in the UK expect to spend more than last year, against 31% in the US, where 19% expect to spend less. The survey covers only LG owners, does not name the company that carried it out, and is used by LG to sell its own ad space, so the figures describe LG's audience rather than shoppers in general.
How the research was put together
According to the methodology notes at the end of each country report, the research rests on four sets of figures.
- The survey. LG calls it the "2026 Holiday Retail Survey of LG TV households." It reached 400 households in each of the five countries, "part of a five-market study (n=2,000)," and was "Fielded September 29 to October 9, 2026."
- The screening rule. "Every respondent has at least one LG TV at home; all passed the survey's attention check." An attention check is a question designed to catch people who click through a survey without reading it.
- The viewing index. LG measured total viewing hours on LG televisions in each country, counting both scheduled broadcast channels, known as linear TV, and streaming. It did so for each of the 39 days from Thanksgiving 2025 to January 4, 2026. Each day is set against "the same weekday on non-holiday days year-round," with 100 as normal. An index of 125 therefore means 25% more viewing hours than an ordinary day of the same weekday.
- The click rates. The ad performance figure comes from "LG Ad Solutions, December 2025 Home Screen roadblock campaigns. Impression-weighted CTR." A roadblock is a booking in which one advertiser takes over a placement for a set period. Here the placement is the LG home screen, the menu that appears when the set is switched on.
Two definitions matter for reading the results. Spending is "Planned holiday spending compared with last year, as reported by respondents." And when the reports say people took action after an ad, they mean "Actions in the past 3 months after seeing a product, brand or retailer advertised on TV or streaming." Unless noted, every percentage is a share of the 400 households surveyed in that country.
A sample of 400 has limits. For a random sample of that size, the usual margin of error is close to five percentage points either way, a PPC Land calculation. LG does not say how respondents were recruited, so that figure is a best case, and gaps of a few points between countries may reflect chance rather than a real difference.
Spending: three markets plan more, two hold steady
The clearest split in the research is money. In Brazil, 65% of LG TV households plan to spend more on the holidays than last year. In the UK the figure is 60%, and in Australia 58%. In the US it is 31%, and in Canada 32%.
Source: LG Ad Solutions country reports. LG's headline shares can differ by a point from the sum of the bars because of rounding. The four US answers add up to 97%, and the report does not say where the other 3% went.
The way the reports present these numbers differs too. The Brazil, UK and Australia reports lead with the share planning to spend more and print a ratio of spenders to savers. The US report is headed "Holiday Spending Remains Resilient" and leads instead with the 78% who "plan to hold or grow" their budget. The Canada report does the same with 85%. Neither North American report prints a ratio. PPC Land's own calculation puts it at about 1.6 households planning to spend more for every one planning to spend less in the US, and about 2.3 to 1 in Canada. The US share planning to cut back, 19%, is the highest of the five markets.
That cautious US picture lines up with other research. In October, Simon-Kucher found that US households expect to spend $1,018 on the holidays, 5.5% less than last year, based on 1,501 consumers surveyed in August. The populations differ: Simon-Kucher asked US consumers at large, LG asked only its own customers.
Two reports add a further measure. In the US, 83% "say they definitely plan to shop for holiday gifts this year." In Canada the figure is 80%. The other three reports do not give it.
What LG's sets recorded last season
The viewing index is the one part of the research that does not rely on what people say. It shows what LG televisions actually played, and the five countries look nothing alike.
Source: LG Ad Solutions, viewing on LG TVs, scheduled and streaming hours, November 27, 2025 to January 4, 2026. 100 = the same weekday on ordinary days of the year.
In the UK, viewing ran above normal on every one of the 39 days. In the US it did so on 36. In Brazil, by contrast, only 13 days beat the normal level, and LG's own headline for that market is "Weekends Drive Holiday Viewing," pointing to Sundays. The Brazil chart shows viewing falling well below normal around New Year's Day. Australia sits in between, with 19 days above normal; according to LG, "Australian viewing jumps 18% above normal on Boxing Day and 17% on New Year's Day."
The reports offer no explanation for the split. One factor they do not mention is the calendar: December and January are summer months in Australia and Brazil, and the main school holidays fall in them.
In the US, the highest viewing day of the US season was Thanksgiving itself, at 25% above normal, while Black Friday reached 15% above normal. Adobe's forecast for this year puts US online spending on Thanksgiving at $6.9 billion, Black Friday at $12.9 billion and Cyber Monday at $15.1 billion, within a record $275.1 billion holiday season.
What households say about their viewing
The survey asked households whether they watch more TV during the holidays than the rest of the year.
Source: LG Ad Solutions country reports. The last column is from the viewing index; the others are from the survey.
Put next to each other, the two measures do not move together, a PPC Land comparison. Australians and Brazilians are among the most likely to say they watch more over the holidays, yet their sets recorded above-normal viewing on the fewest days. In the US, fewer than half say they watch more, yet the sets ran above normal on 36 of 39 days. Neither measure proves the other wrong. The survey asks about this coming season and the sets recorded last season; the index adds up hours across all LG sets in a country, so heavy viewers in some homes can lift the total while most homes change little. The US report also carries a slide headed "The Holidays Bring Households Together Around the TV" without giving a share for watching with family.
What people want to watch is more consistent. Holiday movies and specials and general movies take the top two places in every country, in one order or the other.
Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Boxing Day
The survey also asked when households plan to start and which sales they intend to shop. Because it closed on October 9, the October figure is an expectation, not a record of what happened.
Source: LG Ad Solutions country reports. "Not asked" means the report does not include that event.
Black Friday leads everywhere. Brazil shows the starkest pattern: the latest starters of the five, with only 19% expecting to begin before November, and then the strongest commitment to a single day, with 86% planning to shop Black Friday against 26% for Cyber Monday. The US is the one market where the two days are close, at 66% and 60%. Boxing Day remains a large event in Australia, where half of LG TV households plan to shop it.
Shopping with the TV on
Large majorities say they shop or look up products while watching TV over the holidays. Many do it on a phone, tablet or laptop at the same time, a habit the industry calls using a second screen.
Source: LG Ad Solutions country reports.
Commercial breaks are the most common moment in four of the five countries. In Australia, more people say they shop or browse during the programme itself (46%) than during the breaks. Brazil's list includes an answer the others do not, "Before starting a show," at 32%.
LG presents the habit as good news for TV advertising. The US slide is headed "The Shopping Journey Continues While the TV Is On." A second screen can also pull eyes away from the ads on the first one. Omdia found in September that nearly three in four US TV viewers regularly use a smartphone while the TV is on, with the sharpest growth among viewers aged 55 to 64.
How households say they respond to ads, and what they clicked
The survey's ad questions produce high numbers across the board, particularly outside North America.
Source: LG Ad Solutions country reports. The first four columns are survey answers; the click rate comes from LG's December 2025 home screen roadblock campaigns.
The "took action" figures are broad. They count any action in the previous three months after seeing a product, brand or retailer advertised on TV or streaming, and the reports do not list which actions qualify.
The click rates are the only figures in the research drawn from real ad campaigns. A click-through rate is the share of ad views that led to a click, which on a television means pressing a button on the remote. At 0.36%, Australia's home screen ads drew about 36 clicks for every 10,000 times they were shown. At 0.15%, Brazil's drew about 15, a PPC Land calculation. The reports describe every one of these rates in the same words; the Brazil version reads: "Campaigns during the holiday season delivered a 0.15% CTR, highlighting strong engagement with holiday audiences." None of the reports gives a rate for other months of the year, so there is no way to tell from the research whether December rates were higher than usual.
The survey and the clicks also point in different directions, a PPC Land comparison. Brazilian households gave the most enthusiastic answers about holiday ads and UK households were close behind, yet home screen ads in those two markets drew the lowest click rates of the five. Australia, with similar survey answers, had the highest. The US sat near the bottom on the survey questions and near the top on clicks. The reports do not say what a click led to, or whether it produced a sale.
What goes on the list
According to LG, households plan to buy in about four categories each in the US, Canada and Brazil, and about five in the UK and Australia.
