TITLE: Ukrainian FA to blame for failed drugs test - Mudryk
OUTLET: BBC Sport

- Published
On-loan Tottenham winger Mykhailo Mudryk says he blames the administration of the Ukraine national team for his failed drugs test and subsequent ban from football.
The 25-year-old, on a season-long loan from Chelsea, was suspended from November 2024 until July 2026 after testing positive for the banned substance meldonium, which he believes entered his system while on international duty.
Having initially faced a four-year ban from the Football Association (FA), his appeal to the Court of Arbitration for Sport was successful.
He returned to football after accepting he had breached anti-doping rules and agreeing to a suspension equivalent to the time he had already served.
After appearing for Chelsea during a pre-season tour of Asia, Mudryk joined Tottenham on transfer deadline day to reunite with his former Shakhtar Donetsk manager Roberto de Zerbi.
In a lengthy interview with the Ukrainian YouTube channel Guest of Gordon, Mudryk was asked who he blamed for his absence from the game.
"The environment, the situation in the Ukrainian national team," he replied. "It should never have existed. I probably haven't said it publicly yet, but I'm saying it now.
"I had never heard of a drug like meldonium. It is a drug you can only get in Eastern Europe. It could only have got into my body in Ukraine. There is no such drug in England."
He explained how he had only been in Ukraine to be with the national team and "it points to the fact it was in a national team camp".
Asked specifically whether he blamed the national team, Mudryk replied: "Yes, exactly."
He also said Ukrainian Association of Football (UAF) president Andriy Shevchenko offered little support, blamed the association for leaks that emerged during the case and claimed its handling of the matter delayed his return to football.
Mudryk also said he spent millions of pounds on legal fees, coaching, pitch hire and private physiotherapy while being unable to access services at Chelsea.
He added: "It was a bolt from the blue. I couldn't believe it [the positive test] was true.
"It was like falling off a skyscraper. In a dream you would hit the ground. In life, I just kept on falling. It hit me very hard, mentally."
Mudryk said he cried and drank alcohol after the initial suspension but never doubted that he would eventually return to top-level football.
BBC Sport has contacted the UAF for comment.
Last year a UAF spokesperson told BBC Sport it was "in close contact with Mudryk's lawyers" but had not been contacted by the FA about the case.
