TITLE: OpenAI Paid $773.6 Million for Effingham County Land, Records Show
OUTLET: Effingham Herald

SPRINGFIELD, Ga. — When the Effingham County Industrial Development Authority discussed the sale of land for OpenAI's massive Project Camellia data center in August, the figure presented was approximately $350 million.
But newly obtained financial documents show OpenAI affiliate Octans GA LLC paid $773.6 million for approximately 1,440 acres at the Savannah Gateway Industrial Hub in July — more than twice that amount.
The IDA, which owned the property, received approximately $346.5 million. Another $406 million went to or was directed on behalf of Savannah Industrial Development LLC (SID), the private company responsible for developing and marketing the industrial hub.
The financial details emerged amid litigation in Effingham County Superior Court involving the IDA's handling of Project Camellia agreements.
The lawsuit, filed by environmental organization One Hundred Miles Inc. and five Effingham County residents, alleges the IDA violated Georgia's Open Meetings Act and Open Records Act by failing to provide adequate public notice of a July 21 meeting, improperly discussing portions of the project behind closed doors and failing to publicly approve certain land-sale agreements.
According to court filings, attorneys for the plaintiffs requested records related to the land sale on three occasions — Aug. 18, Aug. 26 and Aug. 31 — but the IDA did not produce the requested documents. The plaintiffs subsequently included an Open Records Act claim in their lawsuit seeking access to the records.
On Nov. 12, both the IDA and Octans filed motions asking a judge to dismiss the lawsuit. The IDA also disclosed that it had released previously withheld land-sale agreements to the plaintiffs' attorneys that day.
The records were provided nearly three months after the first request and on the same day the IDA's response to the lawsuit was due. The IDA argued that the Open Records Act claim should be dismissed as moot because the requested agreements had been produced.
The IDA denies violating state law and argues that releasing the documents resolves the public records portion of the case. Octans wants to be removed from the lawsuit because the plaintiffs have not accused the company of wrongdoing.
A judge has not ruled on either motion.
The Herald previously reported a figure of approximately $350 million based on information provided by the IDA during its Aug. 20 meeting. That amount closely matches the authority's share of the proceeds, rather than the full purchase price.
The Herald has requested explanations from IDA CEO Brandt Herndon and SID and is awaiting their responses.
Following the money
The transaction involved two sections of the Savannah Gateway Industrial Hub, known as Areas 3 and 4.
Octans paid $407.2 million for one section and $366.5 million for the other.
Closing documents identify approximately $346.5 million in proceeds to the IDA and $406 million in payments made to or directed on behalf of SID.
SID-related payments included $388.3 million directed to companies handling tax-deferred property exchanges and $17.7 million in agency fees.
The transaction also involved $5.25 million in real estate broker commissions and approximately $15.5 million placed in escrow under separate agreements.
The escrow was funded from the parties' proceeds. The documents do not establish how much of SID's share represented profit or how much covered its development expenses.
They do, however, establish that the total transaction was worth more than twice the figure previously reported.
Who is SID?
Savannah Industrial Development LLC is a private company registered in Colorado in 2016 and affiliated with Denver-based OmniTRAX, part of The Broe Group, which is involved in railroad operations, real estate and infrastructure development.
SID serves as the master developer of the Savannah Gateway Industrial Hub under an agreement with the IDA dating to 2016.
The agreement gave SID responsibility for developing and marketing the property. The IDA also granted the company power of attorney, authorizing it to negotiate and complete certain land transactions on the authority's behalf.
That authority allowed SID to sign documents transferring approximately 1,440 acres of IDA-owned land to Octans in July.
The agreement also established how proceeds from land sales would be divided.
Under its terms, SID could recover certain development expenses before the remaining proceeds were split between the company and the IDA.
For the OpenAI transaction, the documents identify approximately $59.9 million in previously accumulated development costs that SID was entitled to recover.
The remaining net proceeds were to be divided equally after applicable expenses and adjustments.
Although the IDA owned the property, its agreement with SID gave the private developer a substantial financial interest in the sale.
SID's role has also drawn scrutiny in lawsuits challenging the handling of Project Camellia. Plaintiffs have questioned the approval and disclosure of land-sale agreements, including transactions carried out by SID under its authority from the IDA. Those allegations remain unresolved.
