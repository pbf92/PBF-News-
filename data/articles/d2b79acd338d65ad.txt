TITLE: Diabetic Foot Ulcers Carry Thirty-One Percent Major Cardiovascular Event Risk
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Patients with diabetes-related foot ulcers face a substantial risk of major adverse cardiovascular events that progressively increases over time, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in Frontiers in Endocrinology. Analyzing data from clinical cohort studies, researchers determined that the pooled cumulative incidence of composite major adverse cardiovascular events reached 31 percent. The research team, from the Air Force General Hospital PLA in Beijing, synthesized published evidence to quantify cardiovascular complications and summarize potential risk factors in this population. By evaluating longitudinal cohort studies, the meta-analysis offers a comprehensive assessment of cardiovascular outcomes following the occurrence of diabetes-related foot ulceration.
To perform the assessment, investigators executed a systematic literature search across four electronic databases: PUBMED, EMBASE, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library. The literature retrieval covered the period from each database’s inception through August 2026, targeting all cohort studies relevant to diabetes-related foot ulcers and major adverse cardiovascular events. Following screening, 13 cohort studies encompassing 16,636 patients met the inclusion criteria. Across these included studies, the maximum reported follow-up durations ranged from 1 to 10.9 years, providing observational data across both short-term and extended monitoring periods.
In the study protocol, major adverse cardiovascular events were defined as a composite endpoint consisting of cardiovascular mortality, non-fatal myocardial infarction, and non-fatal stroke. The methodological quality of each included cohort study was appraised using Newcastle-Ottawa Scale standards. The investigators conducted their primary meta-analysis under a random-effects model to synthesize pooled incidence rates. Furthermore, the team reconstructed individual patient data from published Kaplan–Meier curves to generate time-stratified estimates of cumulative event incidences over specific follow-up intervals.
The random-effects meta-analysis found that the pooled cumulative incidence of composite major adverse cardiovascular events was 31 percent, with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 13 percent to 49 percent. When evaluated by individual component outcomes, cardiovascular mortality represented the largest single contributor, exhibiting an overall incidence rate of 18 percent with a 95 percent confidence interval of 11 percent to 26 percent. Non-fatal myocardial infarction and non-fatal stroke demonstrated identical overall incidence rates of 8 percent, with 95 percent confidence intervals of 3 percent to 13 percent and 4 percent to 12 percent, respectively.
To examine how risk unfolds over time, the researchers conducted a time-stratified individual patient data analysis spanning the first 5 years of follow-up. This 5-year duration represented the longest follow-up period during which at least three cohorts continued to contribute at-risk patients to the reconstructed survival calculations. At 1 year of follow-up, the cumulative incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events was 6.3 percent, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 5.5 percent to 7.1 percent. By 3 years, the cumulative incidence expanded to 19.7 percent, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 18.3 percent to 21.0 percent. At 5 years, the cumulative incidence reached 31.2 percent, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 29.6 percent to 32.8 percent.
The numerical progression demonstrated an accelerating early-to-intermediate risk trajectory, particularly highlighting an accelerated accumulation of cardiovascular events between 1 and 5 years. Alongside quantifying event rates, the study identified several clinical variables and conditions as potential risk factors for major adverse cardiovascular events in this population. These potential risk factors included metabolic dysfunction-related fatty liver disease, high-sensitivity troponin T, and estimated glomerular filtration rate. Additionally, atrial fibrillation, a documented history of coronary artery disease, age, and chronic kidney disease were recognized as potential risk factors associated with major adverse cardiovascular events.
In their conclusions, the authors stated that the incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events in patients with diabetes-related foot ulcers rises progressively over time, marked by cumulative rates of 6.3 percent, 19.7 percent, and 31.2 percent at 1, 3, and 5 years, respectively. The investigators noted that this persistently increasing trajectory during the first 5 years, particularly the accelerated accumulation observed between 1 and 5 years, highlights the clinical importance of early and sustained cardiovascular risk assessment and intervention in patients presenting with diabetes-related foot ulcers.
