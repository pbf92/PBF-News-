TITLE: Chile's oldest Anglican church to become museum dedicated to British influence
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

Chile's oldest Anglican church will be preserved as a museum to celebrate the role of the British Anglicans buildling religious tolerance in Chile.
St Paul’s Cathedral, Catedral de San Pablo in Spanish, brought together the early immigrant British community which settled in the central port city of Valparaíso,120km away from the capital Santiago.
After years and years of attempting to repair the church which has stood since 1858, a cultural centre has been transferred into management of the building.
Destino Valparaíso, which has the Immigrant Museum in their building across the street from St Paul's Cathedral on Cerro Concepción, will take over the cathedral built on a centrepiece hill in the port city.
“Chile didn’t have freedom of religion when the British arrived in the 1820s, and they wanted to practice their faith, which was Anglican,” Eduardo Dib, executive director of Destino Valparaíso, told Religion Unplugged.
As the Chilean independence movement from Spain began, and free trade was declared in Valparaíso by the First Junta Government in 1811, a wave of immigration built and peaked in the second half of the 19th century.
British immigrants were the most influential immigrant group in the city, and started building a cemetery in 1823. The "Dissidents Cemetery", as it was known, was the only place where Protestants were allowed to be buried.
St Paul's was built to be discreet with no main entrance, small windows and no spire, cross or bell tower. A Lutheran church was built later on in the 19th century by Germans 41 years after St Paul's was built, and was the first Protestant church in South America with a bell tower. The Church and the state was later separated in Chile's 1925 constitution.
St Paul's was designed by British designer William Henry Lloyd and was a place of worship under the Diocese of the Falkland Islands, like most South American Protestant churches, until 1963. The cathedral now belongs to the Anglican Diocese of Valparaíso.
Michelle Prain, a professor at Universidad Adolfo Ibáñez, said: “St. Paul’s is a witness to the tensions between a clerical state that gradually became liberalized, and people of different faiths."
It was declared a historic monument in 1979 by the Chilean government and became a cathedral in 2016.
The neo-gothic cathedral will be handed over to Destino Valparaíso on 30 November, and before then, the roof, floors, pews and stained-glass windows will have been restored.
