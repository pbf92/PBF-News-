TITLE: Discord's December 15 policy names ad IDs it collects on sponsored content
OUTLET: PPC Land

Discord on November 23, 2026 published new versions of its Terms of Service, Privacy Policy and Paid Services Terms, all taking effect on December 15, 2026. The rewritten privacy policy, for the first time, lists advertising identifiers and click-level data among the information the company collects when people engage with sponsored content, and it says that personalized ads on the platform can be switched on by default in some regions. The company began emailing users about the changes on November 25.
According to Discord, the update reflects "how our business and services are evolving," addresses "changes in applicable laws" and provides "greater clarity about using our services." The company's summary post, titled "Important Policy Updates," says that "most of it isn't" new. A line-by-line comparison of the three documents against the versions that took effect on September 29, 2025 shows a different weighting: the bulk of the new text sits in the advertising, machine learning, age assurance and dispute sections, and several of the changes extend Discord's discretion rather than describing a product that already exists.
In Short
Discord has rewritten its main user documents, and the new rules apply from December 15, 2026. The changes matter most for advertisers running Quests and other sponsored formats, because the privacy policy now spells out that Discord can collect advertising IDs, click IDs, impressions, clicks and conversions, and that ad personalization depends on a user's age and region. Anyone who keeps using Discord after December 15 accepts the new terms, including a broader arbitration clause that people outside the US and Canada can now opt into.
What Discord says is changing
The summary post groups the update into three buckets. The first, headed "Previously announced changes," covers two features announced earlier in the autumn. Under Discoverable content on the web, admins of servers listed on Discord's Server Discovery can choose which channels appear on a public Server Web Page. According to the post, Channel Summaries, "automated summaries of active conversations," are being rolled out now under the current terms. Channel Messages, which "lets messages from public channels in Discoverable servers appear on the web," will start rolling out gradually only after the new terms take effect, and "only messages sent after that point are included."
The second item in that bucket is age assurance. According to the post, Discord launched a new approach to age assurance and a teen experience in September, and the post says the privacy policy now explains how data is handled in that process, while the Terms of Service "give parents and guardians a clearer picture of Discord and the protections in place for teens," including more information on Family Centre.
The second bucket, "Clearer explanations of how things already work," covers Nitro partner perks and privacy. According to the post, the Paid Services Terms now make clear "that third-party partner perks are limited-time offers, not permanent benefits," although "we're not removing any current perks." On privacy, the post says the update adds "more detail about how we use data to build and improve the machine learning models behind safety, personalisation and other Discord features, and the settings you can use to control whether your data helps improve those models."
The third bucket, "A few legal updates," lists a "standard security disclaimer," "a written-notice path for intellectual property claims," the option for users outside the US and Canada "to choose arbitration," and clarification of how users in the European Economic Area can use an official out-of-court dispute settlement body under the Digital Services Act. The post also mentions updated local information for users in certain regions.
The email sent on November 25 states the choice plainly: "If you don't agree to the updated terms, you can choose to stop using our services and delete your Discord account at any time."
Sponsored content: what the privacy policy now says
The advertising changes are spread across five sections of the privacy policy. Taken together, they describe a measurement loop that the 2025 text only hinted at.
Advertising identifiers
The section on information Discord collects automatically gains a new sentence. The 2025 version described device data as "information like your IP address, operating system information, browser information and information about your device settings, such as your microphone and/or camera." The December 2026 version keeps that and adds: "If you interact with sponsored content, this may also include, consistent with your settings, information like device ID, advertising ID (such as IDFA or Android Ad ID) or similar unique IDs (such as a click or interaction ID)."
The phrase "consistent with your settings" is not defined further in that paragraph. On iOS, an advertising ID is normally available to an app only after the user grants permission through Apple's tracking prompt, but the policy does not describe how Discord's own settings interact with that permission.
Data from advertisers and partners
In the section on information received from other sources, the 2025 sentence read: "We may also receive information about you from advertisers and third-party data providers, such as demographic information and how you engage with other products and services outside of Discord." The new version adds "partners" to that list and a new sentence: "If you engage with third-party sponsored content, we may receive interaction and measurement data (such as impressions, clicks and conversions) from these sources."
That is the first time the word "conversions" appears in the document. For a gaming advertiser buying Quests, a conversion is typically an install, a sign-up or a purchase that happens off Discord, which means the data flows back into Discord from the advertiser's side.
Consent depends on age and place
The general section on how Discord uses information now carries two new sentences after the existing note that some jurisdictions rely on consent: "For example, depending on where you live and how old you are, we may deliver personalised sponsored content after you provide consent. But in other regions, sponsored content may be personalised by default (and you can disable it)."
The policy does not list which regions fall into which group. The "With your consent" section, unchanged from 2025, still says Discord may use personal information with consent "such as to send you marketing communications and personalize sponsored content where legally required."
Automated systems for measurement and fraud
The 2025 paragraph headed "To deliver relevant sponsored content" was two sentences long. It read: "We use your information to help us surface sponsored content (like Quests and other sponsored formats) that may be of interest to you. As discussed in the 'How to control your privacy' section below, you can choose to what extent we will use your personal information to personalise your Discord experience."
The new paragraph shifts from "We use" to "We may use," and adds a sentence about automation: "We may also use automated systems to process information about your activity or interactions with sponsored content and personalise, measure campaign performance and prevent fraud." The closing sentence now applies only to people who qualify: "If you are eligible to see personalised sponsored content, you can choose the extent to which we use your personal information to personalise your Discord experience."
The Terms of Service carry a matching line. The section describing what users can expect from Discord now says: "Sometimes we may use automated systems to rank or show you things like notifications or sponsored content; how we do that and your options to modify or turn it off are described in our Privacy Policy and Help Centre." The same section changes "sponsored content" in the personalization sentence to "sponsored content on behalf of third parties."
A separate sponsored content setting
The privacy controls section is restructured. In 2025, ad personalization sat inside a single control: "We offer certain settings that control whether and how we may personalise Discord for you, such as offering you relevant recommendations for in-app content and features or delivering relevant sponsored content." The new version splits it into two named controls, "Limit our ability to personalise your Discord product experience" and "Limit how we deliver sponsored content." The second reads: "We offer certain sponsored content settings that allow you to control your experience with sponsored content on Discord, including to limit how we use your information to deliver relevant sponsored content to you on Discord."
Discord's own advertising on other platforms also gets a small change. The 2025 text said Discord uses information "to assist in the delivery of our advertising." The new text says "to assist in and personalise the delivery of our advertising."
One part of the ad section did not change. The 2025 policy already said Discord "may also provide information to analytics and measurement partners to measure the effectiveness of sponsored content if you engage with it (for example, by starting a Quest or clicking on other sponsored content)." That sentence is identical in the new version, as is the statement that "Our business is funded through subscriptions, paid products and sponsored content (like Quests and other sponsored formats)" and the promise: "We don't make money from selling your personal information to third parties."
Machine learning: more models, no mention of AI
The summary post's reference to machine learning maps onto four edits. The section on improving services now says Discord uses information "to develop features or machine learning models that make Discord safer and better to use." The 2025 version said "to develop features that make Discord safer and better to use."
A new sentence under the heading "To protect our services," one of the uses Discord lists under its legitimate business interests, says: "This may include using information to build or improve models that help us determine a user's age group and deliver age-appropriate experiences." The paragraph links to a page describing how Discord's age estimation model works.
The product personalization section adds: "It also includes the use of automated systems to rank or show you things like notifications." And the privacy setting for service improvement is rewritten. The 2025 setting limited "the information we collect to help us understand how users use our services, what features or products they may want, or to otherwise help us improve our business." The new one limits "how we use your information for the purposes of understanding how users interact with our services, what features or products they may want, leverage machine learning or to otherwise help us improve Discord."
What the documents leave out is as notable as what they add. None of the three new texts uses the words "artificial intelligence," "AI," "generative" or "training." The policy speaks of models for safety, age estimation, ranking and personalization. It does not say whether user content is used to build large language models, and it does not say whether Discord licenses content to outside AI developers.
There is one sentence with a possible bearing on that question, in the disclosure section: "This also means that other Discord users with access to your content may use it, including with tools or features that enable them to connect to and share it with other services." The 2025 version had no equivalent. The section on third-party services also now describes apps that provide "analytics about your interactions with the server," alongside the existing examples of moderation and games.
