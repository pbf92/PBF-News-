TITLE: Albo out of touch: poll
OUTLET: Yahoo News Australia

Voters view Anthony Albanese as less trustworthy and more disconnected from everyday Australians than his opposition and One Nation counterparts, according to a new poll.
The latest Capital Brief/DemosAU poll published on Tuesday showed 49 per cent of respondents did not consider the Prime Minister trustworthy, while 52 per cent found him out of touch with ordinary Australians.
Forty-three per cent also did not think he had "a vision for Australia" – the same number who saw him as arrogant.
One Nation leader Pauline Hanson led in all rankings, with 44 per cent finding her arrogant and 34 per cent trustworthy, while 43 per cent thought she was in touch and 49 per cent felt she had a vision for the country.
Angus Taylor was viewed as the least arrogant, with 25 per cent agreeing with that description.
However, he also had the highest share of "don't know" responses.
The results come after Labor's core support fell again in the latest News24 Pulse/YouGov poll, dropping to a low of 24 per cent for the second time.
One Nation led the major parties at 26 per cent, with the Coalition third at 22 per cent and the Greens at 15 per cent.
On a two-party-preferred basis, Labor retained a slim lead over the Coalition 51-49, while the Albanese government continued to push ahead of One Nation 56-44.
The Opposition Leader led Mr Albanese as preferred prime minister for a second consecutive poll, with Mr Taylor leading 41 per cent to 40 per cent.
But Mr Albanese has increased his lead over One Nation leader Pauline Hanson, ahead 52-38.
Labor's polling woes come as Australians grapple with rising living costs, sticky inflation and climbing interest rates.
In a ministerial statement on Monday, Treasurer Jim Chalmers warned the economy would get worse before it got better, but stressed "we still have a lot going for us".
"We have the 55th biggest population in the world but the 14th biggest economy," he said.
"We have the fourth biggest pool of retirement savings in super and before long it will be the top two.
"We have an enviable combination of advantages in energy, industry, geology and geography, and demography.
"Our economy is growing faster than almost every G7 economy. The private sector is driving growth. Our debt is lower than every G7 country."
Mr Chalmers promised modest net savings in the upcoming mid year budget update due next month.
