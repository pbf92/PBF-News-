Jensen Huang Said Nvidia’s Blackwell Chips Were Too Big to Smuggle. Bloomberg Just Found Them in China.
This post may contain links from our sponsors and affiliates, and Flywheel Publishing may receive compensation for actions taken through them.
I’m conservative on NVIDIA (NASDAQ:NVDA | NVDA Price Prediction). A Nov. 27 Bloomberg report identified restricted Blackwell AI hardware at a Shanghai data center linked to Alice Huang. Jensen Huang previously suggested such equipment was too large to smuggle, and a fully equipped GB300 rack weighs approximately 3,300 pounds. Bloomberg has not established how the hardware got to China. Washington will still ask the question, and that is a problem for shareholders.
Shares were trading at $147.13 in the morning, down 1.45% on the day and 31.88% over the past month.
Bull Case I Don’t Buy
Q3 revenue came in at $115.84 billion[1], beating out the $108.73 billion consensus. Q4 guidance is $130.0 billion ±3%[1], and management raised its fiscal 2028 growth outlook to approximately 75%[2]. Analysts still have 59 Buy ratings on the stock.
Three Red Flags Wall Street Is Discounting
Export Controls Will Tighten
Bloomberg reviewed Chinese regulatory filings identifying GB300 NVL72 and GB200 systems, and one was pledged as collateral for a $14 million loan. Blackwell exports to China require U.S. government authorization, and Megaspeed, the company Alice Huang founded, was recently blacklisted. Each documented leak makes stricter end-use checks more likely, and those checks raise compliance costs for distributors and customers. China generated $11.05 billion[1] of Q3 revenue. On the new rules, Kress said: “We’re still going to spend some time studying those to understand the different pieces of them.”[2]
Concentration Sits in the Footnotes
Three direct customers made up 14%, 11% and 11%[1] of Q3 revenue. Guarantee exposure reached $118 billion[1], supply commitments hit $390 billion, and DSO extended to 64 days. Those obligations stay fixed even if regulatory friction slows a major buyer.
Governance Is Still Unsettled
In November the board said it “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO”[2]. Huang was reinstated anyway, while an independent board committee investigation[1] disclosed in the Q3 release remains a stated risk.
Catalysts to Mark Before Year-End
Huang testifies before the Senate on Dec. 3. Prediction markets put the probability of a BIS export-control penalty by March 31, 2027 at 22.5%, and the probability of U.S. approval for Blackwell sales to China by the end of 2027 at just 20.5%. The Q4 report is expected February 23, 2027.
What Could Prove Me Wrong
I’d reconsider my view after clean Senate testimony with no penalty, followed by a Q4 beat with gross margin holding within the guided 71.0% to 72.0%[1] range.
My Bottom Line Before Dec. 3
I put my conviction at 7/10. I see raised regulatory risk until after the testimony. Investors exploring NVIDIA’s competitive landscape can compare it with Advanced Micro Devices (NASDAQ:AMD) or Broadcom (NASDAQ:AVGO), both of which NVIDIA names as competitors in its own filings.
A 3,300-pound rack was supposed to be impossible to hide. Now that one has turned up in China, I expect regulators to set NVIDIA’s ceiling.
