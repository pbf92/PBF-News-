Huw Edwards admits he 'lost the plot' after 'fall from grace' in new Channel 4 documentary
Huw Edwards – Guilty follows the disgraced former BBC newsreader as he navigates life in the wake of his conviction for making indecent images of children
Huw Edwards has admitted he 'lost the plot' while facing tough questions about his criminal behaviour in a new Channel 4 documentary. Huw Edwards – Guilty follows the disgraced newsreader as he attempts to rebuild his life in the aftermath of his conviction for making indecent images of children.
Shot over the course of almost a year, the documentary marks Edwards's return to TV following the collapse of his career as a broadcast journalist, and captures him at his rural Welsh home.
In a trailer for the programme, Edwards is questioned by an off-camera producer, who asks: "What are you doing?". Sounding exasperated, he replies: "You want me to analyse what I was doing? I mean, seriously?".

Later in the trailer, the interviewer presses further: "What do you think you were doing?". Edwards responds: "I'd lost the plot."
The interviewer replies: "Well, come on," to which Edwards says: "No, not come on!".
Throughout the documentary, the former newsreader is "forced to confront his loss of fame, declining health, fractured relationships and his crimes".
Sacha Mirzoeff, senior commissioning editor at Channel 4, said: "Channel 4 has an established reputation for tackling controversial subjects through exemplary film-making.
"Huw Edwards – Guilty is no exception in its quest for answers from a man who is trying to come to terms with his abuse of power."
Director Patrick Forbes stated: "This was an important film to make. It's rare to find a subject that touches on so many significant contemporary issues – whether it's celebrity culture or the power of the media – as well as enduring questions about crime and punishment."
Huw Edwards – Guilty will air on Channel 4 on Tuesday December 1 at 9pm.
Edwards was most recognised for fronting the BBC's News At Ten over several decades, announcing some of the nation's most significant stories to British viewers, including the death of Queen Elizabeth II.
His fall from grace began following a July 2023 report by The Sun which claimed a "top BBC star" had given money to a teenager in exchange for explicit images. Days afterwards, his wife identified him as the broadcaster at the heart of the controversy.
Edwards stepped down in April 2024 and faced charges in June 2024 following a Metropolitan Police inquiry.
A month later, he admitted making indecent images of children and having 41 photographs on WhatsApp, including seven of the most serious type.
He received a six-month prison term, suspended for two years.
