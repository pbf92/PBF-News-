TITLE: Molecular Central Nervous System Infection Tests Show Uncertain Clinical Diagnostic Accuracy
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A systematic review and diagnostic test accuracy meta-analysis has determined that current scientific evidence evaluating molecular diagnostic techniques for post-neurosurgical central nervous system infections remains limited by methodological uncertainty. Although molecular pathogen detection tools are increasingly considered as potential aids in clinical evaluation, pooled analyses indicate that observed diagnostic performance depends heavily on the comparator or reference standard used across investigations, preventing definitive confirmation of their clinical superiority or overall clinical utility.
Accurately identifying post-neurosurgical central nervous system infections represents a persistent clinical dilemma because expected postoperative inflammatory reactions can closely resemble infectious processes. In addition, conventional microbiological culture of cerebrospinal fluid exhibits restricted diagnostic sensitivity within various clinical environments. While advanced molecular microbiological platforms are designed to enhance pathogen identification, investigators emphasize that high concordance with conventional laboratory culture does not necessarily establish true diagnostic accuracy for active infection.
To evaluate the existing body of evidence, investigators conducted comprehensive searches across PubMed, Embase, Scopus, the Cochrane Library, and the Web of Science Core Collection from database inception through August 2026. The systematic search identified eligible investigations that evaluated molecular pathogen-detection approaches specifically among patients presenting with suspected post-neurosurgical central nervous system infections. Methodological quality across the candidate publications was formally appraised using the established QUADAS-2 assessment tool.
The quantitative synthesis applied two distinct analytical frameworks to evaluate performance: one comparing molecular tools directly against conventional microbiological culture, and another evaluating performance against clinical or composite diagnostic criteria. Pooled estimates were derived using random-effects diagnostic meta-analysis models, complemented by hierarchical summary receiver operating characteristic analyses. Researchers also conducted exploratory evaluations examining potential incorporation bias, different molecular platforms, and the potential impact of antimicrobial exposure across the gathered studies.
From an initial pool of 16 included studies, 12 unique reports contributed data to quantitative meta-analyses. Within the conventional culture comparator framework, seven studies provided quantitative data yielding a pooled positive percent agreement of 0.84, with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning from 0.68 to 0.95. The corresponding pooled negative percent agreement was 0.82, with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 0.59 to 0.98, demonstrating that molecular testing results do not completely align with conventional microbiological cultures.
When assessed against clinical or composite reference standards across six contributing studies, molecular assays achieved a pooled sensitivity of 0.74, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.55 to 0.90, and a pooled specificity of 0.97, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.90 to 1.00. The associated positive likelihood ratio was estimated at 18.32, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 4.70 to 71.38, while the negative likelihood ratio stood at 0.30, with a 95 percent confidence interval between 0.16 and 0.56.
The evaluation identified substantial methodological factors affecting data interpretation, including incorporation bias in existing literature. In an exploratory sensitivity analysis that excluded a study with documented incorporation bias, the pooled sensitivity decreased to 0.68, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.52 to 0.82, while specificity remained high at 0.95, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.87 to 1.00. Marked heterogeneity across studies, variations in analytical units, and uncertainty surrounding the true independence of reference standards further constrained the certainty of these findings.
The study authors concluded that while molecular tools can offer complementary pathogen detection data, available statistical estimates do not justify claims of definitive clinical utility or clear superiority over existing modalities. The researchers emphasized that robust, well-designed prospective multicenter studies utilizing standardized, independent reference standards and patient-level analytic frameworks are essential to clarify actual diagnostic performance and establish whether molecular testing meaningfully enhances clinical decision-making and patient outcomes.
