TITLE: BuzzBallz-slurping driver killed girlfriend and another passenger in crash
OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

A rogue driver who slurped a BuzzBallz cocktail before smashing his souped-up Skoda Fabia into a digger and a dumper truck at a set of roadworks killed two of his passengers, including his girlfriend.
Apollo Simms - who has a history of bad driving - had been drinking the tequila-based drink when he veered onto the wrong side of the road at 84mph. A witness heard him say: "I think I passed out driving the car - it's crashed."
The 26-year-old lost control of his adapted vehicle - which had sports seats and an injection system to give his engine more power - before crashed into a digger and a dumper truck at a set of roadworks, ending up on a wall.

Simms' two rear seat passengers - one of them his girlfriend - suffered fatal head and neck injuries in the crash on the A57 Hyde Road in Gorton on August 5 earlier this year. Natasha Denman, 24, and Jack Cartwright, 23, died at the scene.
On Friday (November 20), a judge jailed Simms, from Longsight. Manchester Crown Court heard he spewed up bright blue vomit in the back of an ambulance following the horror crash, admitting he had been drinking BuzzBallz. A bottle was found almost empty in the footwell of the Skoda.
The court heard Ms Denman, Mr Cartwright, Simms and Simms' brother Osiris - his front seat passenger - had spent the evening together. CCTV captured the Skoda in the car park of the Hare and Hounds pub earlier in the evening, prosecutor Gavin Howie said.
Simms' Skoda was travelling at an estimated 84mph on the 30mph limit A57 Hyde Road in Gorton when it overtook another car in wet conditions.
He went onto the wrong side of the road before losing control and swerving back onto the correct side and into roadworks.
The car smashed into a digger and a dumper truck, ending up partially on a wall at the side of the road. A van driver heading in the opposite direction later told police he had to apply his brakes when he saw Simms' Fabia heading towards him, hearing the screech of tyres and a 'loud bang'.
An occupant of a nearby home, who came out after hearing a crash, saw the defendant pacing up and down on his phone and overheard him saying: "I think I passed out driving the car - it's crashed."
Simms and his brother were heard having a 'heated conversation' before the defendant fled the scene, Mr Howie told the court.
Mr Cartwright's mother Donna, who lives close by, went out of her home and watched as paramedics worked on her son. He died at the scene.
Ms Denman's mother Melanie, who also lives in the area, also went to the scene of the crash and was told her daughter had died.

At 11.09pm paramedics were called to another nearby home, following a call from the defendant's father that his son had suffered cracked ribs in a car crash.
In the back of the ambulance Simms spewed up bright blue vomit, admitting he had been drinking BuzzBallz. When he was later arrested on suspicion of causing death by dangerous driving, he made no comment to officers.
A roadside breath test - taken some 90 minutes after the crash - revealed 49 micrograms of alcohol per 100ml of breath, the legal limit being 35. Simms refused to provide a sample of blood when he was taken to hospital.
Police searched his Fabia. In the footwell, they found a large bottle of BuzzBallz, a pre-mixed tequila cocktail with an alcohol content of 13.5 per cent. It was a quarter full, according to the prosecutor.
After an examination of the scene of the crash, a police collision expert estimated the Fabia had been travelling at 84mph. The court heard the vehicle had been modified with extra lighting, sports seats and a 'performance' injection system which gave the car 25 per cent more power.
Simms has a number of previous convictions, most of them for driving offences. In September 2022, he was handed a community order after being convicted drug-driving; careless driving; threatening behaviour; and failing to stop.
In February 2023, he was handed a suspended prison sentence for failing to stop; careless driving; and driving without insurance and while disqualified.
He was on court bail at the time of the Hyde Road crash. In February this year, he was charged with driving without a licence; driving without insurance; driving without a licence; and possession of a bladed article.
His barrister Mark Friend told the court: "Apollo Simms recognises the enormity of his actions and he recognises the sheer significance of the consequences, the ongoing consequences for those that his actions affected."
He said his client's early guilty pleas were 'expressions of genuine remorse' while character testimonials handed to the judge showed a 'positive, decent, selfless side' to his client.
"The only fact he could change if he could re-write history would be to ensure that the only loss of life was his own," said Mr Friend.
His Honour Judge Alan Conrad KC told the defendant: "The way in which over time you have ignored and disobeyed the rules of the road means it may only have been a matter of time before your driving ended up killing somebody and here you caused the death of two people by your grossly excessive speed when impaired by alcohol. This is a very bad case of its type."
His two victims were 'young people with their whole lives ahead of them', he said, adding: "It's a feature of this close you were close to both of them but it's your dangerous driving that caused their deaths and so much grief all around."
Simms, of Tealby Road, Longsight, held his head in his hands for much of the hearing. He was jailed for 14 years, plus a further three years on licence, having admitted two counts of causing death by dangerous driving; and failing to provide a specimen for analysis. He was also banned from driving or 19 years.
Simms will be eligible for parole two-thirds of the way through the 14-year sentence.
