TITLE: EXCLUSIVE | Former Manchester United striker Louis Saha on the Red Devils’ recurring problems: ‘The foundation of the club has been tarnished’
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

Get French Football News sat down with former Manchester United forward Louis Saha to talk about the various managerial changes, what the Red Devils are lacking to return to the top, and the board's decision to leave Old Trafford.
Do you think changing the manager is really the solution to Man United's problems?
I just hope that Pochettino starts well. I hope that he manages to do magical things and bring that impact that new managers have, because yes, players of course want to impress their new manager.
I don't know what can be changed in the formation because I think they were set up correctly, in my opinion. So I don't see how he can manage things differently, to be honest.
I'm a bit surprised by the timing. But yeah, there is definitely a problem in the way they make decisions and in the way they back the manager. So if in November they allowed Pochettino to come in and sign big players in January, why didn't they do it before?
This is a very difficult moment for anyone who loves football and loves Man United, because you feel like the foundation of the club has been tarnished, especially given the way they treated Michael Carrick which was hard to watch.
What do you think Manchester United are lacking at the moment to get back to the top?
It's difficult because, to be honest, three or four months ago I was very hopeful. I had seen some real glimpses of a return to form with games last year where maybe it wasn't super shiny, but the foundation was there.
Every game they seemed to be able to apply some pressure and maintain some control of games, whereas now it has just collapsed. I don't know why, and they need to get back to basics to actually improve form. Especially in the game against Roma, they won that game, but they completely changed faces in the second half, and why is that? I don't know.
In those moments, you tend to question their ability to approach games correctly. I don't think it is going to be magical in a few months, but I do think that if you make the right signings, you still need two or three to balance things and create that confidence factor.
When you look left, right, and centre, you want to feel like this player is going to compensate, or that one is going to perform so well. We need players who are at the very top of their game, who are almost a guarantee in their role.
At this moment in time, I can't even point to Bruno Fernandes for that, so it is tough to see where that dominance will come from. It is a moment where the club needs to get organised and make sure we get the best players, the best staff, and the best infrastructure.
That's part of the nature of this club, so until they do that, it's going to be tough.
As someone who played at Old Trafford, what do you think about the fact that the club is set to move to a new ground in the coming years?
Leaving Old Trafford gives me mixed feelings. We have to move forward as a club. They want a better experience and better facilities for people to recognise how good and how strong this club is. The history of this club relies on Old Trafford for sure.
It's been an amazing place for success. But at the same time, the main concern is the team, the performance on the field, and I think that's a big concern. Wherever the investment goes, the billions need to be invested in the team and in how we can really help them perform.
So I do understand both perspectives. I do think that it's important that the club remains competitive in the Premier League, though it's crazy to even have that kind of talk about this club. Yes, the fans really are worried and I understand why.
