TITLE: Teledermatology Shows High Accuracy for Detecting Cutaneous Melanoma in Meta-Analysis
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Teledermatology achieves high overall diagnostic accuracy when identifying cutaneous melanoma and may serve as an effective clinical triage tool, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis published in Archives of Dermatological Research. Cutaneous melanoma is recognized as the most lethal kind of skin cancer, making patient prognosis critically dependent on early detection and prompt medical treatment. To determine how effectively remote evaluations identify these malignancies, researchers synthesized evidence from multiple investigations to evaluate pooled diagnostic performance metrics.
The systematic review evaluated a total of 10 articles encompassing 5,697 patients to establish the broader diagnostic capabilities of remote dermatological assessments. The primary objective of the investigation was to determine pooled estimates of clinical accuracy across available literature, assessing whether remote digital consultations could reliably distinguish malignant melanoma from other skin conditions within diverse healthcare environments.
To identify all relevant literature, the investigators systematically searched multiple medical databases up to October 2025, including PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, the China National Knowledge Infrastructure, and Web of Science. The methodological quality and potential risk of bias for each included study were rigorously assessed using the Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies-2 tool, ensuring standardized evaluation across the included research base.
Using a random-effects meta-analysis model, the researchers determined that teledermatology achieved a pooled sensitivity of 0.93, with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 0.87 to 0.97. The corresponding pooled specificity was calculated at 0.89, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.72 to 0.96. In addition, analysis of the summary receiver operating characteristic curve demonstrated an area under the curve of 0.96, with a 95 percent confidence interval between 0.94 and 0.98.
The statistical assessment further quantified diagnostic performance through likelihood ratios and odds metrics. The pooled positive likelihood ratio was estimated at 8.75, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 3.04 to 25.17, while the pooled negative likelihood ratio was 0.07, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.04 to 0.15. The overall diagnostic odds ratio across the evaluated studies reached 118.78, with a 95 percent confidence interval extending from 28.97 to 486.98.
To identify potential sources of heterogeneity, the authors conducted stratified subgroup analyses examining geographic region, study design, and reference standard methodology. These assessments indicated that teledermatology demonstrated higher diagnostic accuracy in investigations conducted within Oceania, in prospective research designs, and in studies that utilized a mixed reference standard combining direct face-to-face clinical assessment with histopathology.
Publication bias was formally evaluated using Deeks’ funnel plot analysis, which produced a p-value of 0.29, indicating no statistically significant evidence of publication bias among the reviewed studies. Despite the high pooled sensitivity and specificity supporting teledermatology as a triage tool, the authors cautioned that substantial heterogeneity observed across the included studies necessitates careful and measured interpretation of the findings.
