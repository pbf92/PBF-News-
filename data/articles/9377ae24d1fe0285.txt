TITLE: Archbishop calls for unity as Church faces five years of difficult questions
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The Archbishop of Canterbury has called on the Church of England to remain united despite its differences, as a newly elected General Synod begins its five-year term.
In her Presidential Address at Church House in Westminster, Most Rev Sarah Mullally said members must learn to walk together, even when they disagree.
Drawing on the biblical account of two disciples meeting the risen Jesus on the road to Emmaus, she described the Synod as being “together on the way”.
She said: “Our common task in this Synod is to enable the Church’s mission to flourish.”
Archbishop Sarah acknowledged that differences and disagreements within the Church could be painful, but urged members not to withdraw from one another.
She said unity was a gift from God, and that members should choose to remain alongside one another, recognising their differences while continuing to love one another.
The Archbishop also called on the Church to offer a visible example of unity to a society she described as divided and anxious.
She said the Church should be a place of hospitality for people searching for somewhere to belong, offering hope through the reconciling love of Christ.
The address comes as 487 newly elected members begin the 12th General Synod since the body was established in 1970.
They will serve for five years, during which they will consider legislation, worship and issues affecting the Church of England nationally.
The new Synod faces a number of significant challenges from the outset.
Its first group of sessions includes a safeguarding update, alongside discussions about the funding of the Church’s mission, the future of church buildings and clergy wellbeing.
On Wednesday, members are due to debate concerns about the condition of church buildings, with a motion calling for a national strategy to support their preservation, maintenance and development.
They will also hear about the Church’s approach to funding its mission, as well as a report on clergy care and wellbeing.
The King formally inaugurated the new Synod earlier today, following a service at Westminster Abbey.
It is the first time Charles has inaugurated a new General Synod during his reign, and the first such ceremony since Dame Sarah became Archbishop of Canterbury.
