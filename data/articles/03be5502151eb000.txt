TITLE: Marblism Brings AI Agents to Mexico’s SME Market
OUTLET: Mexico Business News

British technology company Marblism has entered Mexico as part of its expansion into Latin America, offering AI agents that handle business functions such as email management, social media, sales prospecting and telephone support. The company aims to bring 20,000 businesses in the region onto its platform by 2027, targeting small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) seeking to automate recurring operations.
Marblism’s model assigns specific responsibilities to individual AI agents that users can instruct through conversations, without programming knowledge. The platform currently includes Eva, for email and calendar management; Sonny, for social media content; Stan, for identifying sales prospects; and Rachel, for answering telephone calls.
“Marblism is the simplest platform for generating revenue with artificial intelligence. If you know how to explain your business to a new employee, you already know how to use it,” said Jesús Malo, Marblism’s co-founder and chief marketing officer, according to Milenio.
The company’s approach positions AI beyond individual productivity tools, presenting agents as digital workers that can take responsibility for recurring activities and connect with existing business software.
Marblism, headquartered in London and led by Ulric Musset, is backed by startup accelerator Y Combinator. Its Latin American expansion is being coordinated with Frank Moreno, CEO, Grupo ēndor and the company’s official partner for Mexico and the region. The company held its first pilot event in Mexico in November 2026 at the Gran Museo del Mundo Maya in Mérida, Yucatán.
The launch comes as Mexican companies increase their use of AI, although adoption remains uneven. A study commissioned by Amazon Web Services (AWS) and conducted by Strand Partners found that AI adoption in Mexico increased from 38% to 48% of organizations in one year, equivalent to approximately 500,000 additional companies. However, only 13% had reached an advanced stage of adoption, while 63% remained focused on basic applications and incremental improvements, according to Mexico Business News.
For SMEs, the opportunity is linked to the ability to access functions that traditionally require dedicated personnel or external service providers. Automating lead follow-up, customer inquiries and administrative tasks could allow smaller teams to allocate more time to sales, customer relationships and business management.
Marblism says its platform integrates with applications including WhatsApp, HubSpot, QuickBooks, Zoho CRM and Notion. These connections allow agents to work with tools that businesses may already use for customer communication, accounting, sales management and internal documentation. The company reports more than 60,000 users worldwide, although the figure has not been independently verified.
The shift toward AI agents is also reflected in how Mexican companies are approaching automation. In an October contribution to Mexico Business News, Julio Velázquez, managing director of Google Cloud Mexico, described a transition from passive assistants toward systems that can coordinate workflows, interact with databases and perform tasks with greater autonomy. The article cited the Work:In Progress study, which found that 40% of companies in Mexico were accelerating AI initiatives across multiple operational areas, while 13% had fully scaled adoption.
However, deploying agents requires companies to address data access, oversight and integration with existing systems. A separate Mexico Business News report on AI agents in Mexico highlighted the importance of mapping business processes before granting agents greater autonomy. Santiago Lasso, senior solutions advisor at SAP LeanIX, noted that companies need to understand how data moves across their systems before deciding where to invest. Estafeta’s data and analytics director, Ana Cristina Flores, also emphasized that process maturity should precede the deployment of autonomous agents.
