TITLE: GTA 6 Standard Edition locks five shops, and the $20.00 Xbox upgrade opens them
OUTLET: Make Tech Easier

Official Xbox and PlayStation store pages for Grand Theft Auto VI list five shops that stay closed to Standard Edition owners, and the US Xbox Store currently sells a Grand Theft Auto VI: Ultimate Edition Upgrade for $20.00 that opens them. The Xbox Store listing groups the locations under the heading “Special Destinations Only Open for Business with the Ultimate Edition,” and the PlayStation US product page uses almost identical wording, “Only open for business in the Ultimate Edition,” for the same five names. The PlayStation Store carries a matching upgrade add-on page, but as of 24 November 2026 it shows no price at all and displays “Not available for purchase,” so the price confirmed here is the $20.00 US Xbox listing.
The five destinations that need the Ultimate Edition
Both official store pages name the same five businesses. Rideout Customs is described as a place to “transform vehicles into works of art with detailed interiors, exquisite rims, and donk stylings.” Sara’s Unisex Salon offers “signature salon styles, including facial hair for Jason and makeup and nails for Lucia.” Stock 305 is listed as “Stockyard’s premier destination for elevated streetwear,” with unique and exclusive looks for both protagonists. Electric Fang Tattoo advertises “over 50 signature tattoos for both Jason and Lucia” that are “all designed by the artist collective FAILE.” One-Eyed Willie’s is called “Lake Leonida’s storied mod shop, specializing in off-road modifications and hand-painted automotive artistry.”
What turned a bullet list into a talking point was a launch-day video from IGN that compared the two editions at the shop entrances. As summarized by The Neon Atlas, IGN’s test found Standard Edition players shut out at four of those doors, specifically One-Eyed Willie’s, Sara’s Unisex Salon, Stock 305, and Electric Fang Tattoo Parlor. Rideout Customs rounds out the official list of five, and there is no indication in that coverage that IGN checked it on camera. The detail that drew attention was not the lockout itself, which the store pages state plainly, but the fact that the interiors appear to be fully built and decorated behind those closed doors, according to the same reporting. By that account, the spaces exist in the game world behind the closed doors.
That distinction matters for anyone deciding which edition to buy. These are cosmetic and customization venues, covering vehicle work, hair and makeup, clothing, and tattoos, rather than story missions or map regions. A Standard Edition player can still walk up to the doors, but the official wording on both platforms is unambiguous about who gets to go inside.
What the upgrade costs and when it can be bought
Checked on 24 November 2026, the US Xbox Store lists the Grand Theft Auto VI: Ultimate Edition Upgrade at a flat $20.00. The add-on page also carries the standard requirement notice, stating that Grand Theft Auto VI is required to access the content and that the base game is sold separately, which rules out buying the upgrade as a standalone entry point.
The $20.00 figure is specific to the US Xbox Store listing, and this article does not apply it to other stores or regions.
Rockstar has addressed the upgrade path directly in its support documentation. The customer support article Grand Theft Auto VI Platforms, Editions, and Versions, last updated 18 November 2026, states that the Ultimate Edition Upgrade “will also be available for Grand Theft Auto VI: Standard Edition owners to purchase separately at any time.” The same article notes that a physical version of the Ultimate Edition is not available, and that Standard Edition owners can purchase the upgrade from the PlayStation or Microsoft stores after their Standard Edition code has been redeemed. In other words, there is no launch window on the offer, but the Standard Edition code has to be redeemed first.
The PlayStation listing exists, the price does not
The PlayStation Store US page for the Ultimate Edition Upgrade is live and correctly titled, but it does not function as a storefront yet. Rechecked on 24 November 2026, the page shows “Not available for purchase” with no base price and no discounted price attached to the listing. That is why this article does not quote a PS5 upgrade price. Rockstar’s support article says the upgrade can be bought from the PlayStation or Microsoft stores, and the PlayStation page is clearly built for that purpose, but the purchase state on Sony’s side is not active at the time of checking.
The mismatch is a small one in isolation, and it lands alongside other launch rough edges around how this game is being sold. Make Tech Easier has covered the scale of the day-one sales and the decision to ship the boxed edition without a disc, both pointing to a release built around digital entitlements more than physical goods. The shop lockout sits in the same bracket: on Xbox in the US, $20.00 is what the store asks for access to the five destinations.
Standard Edition owners who want the five destinations have a clear route on Xbox in the United States today and a listing without a price on PlayStation.
