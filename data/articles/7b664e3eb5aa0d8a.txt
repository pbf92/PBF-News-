TITLE: 'We need to pray for this region': 3 dozen Ethiopian Orthodox Christians killed in second attack in fortnight
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

A Christian persecution watchdog has urged for peace and investigations into deadly violence against Ethopian Orthodox Christians. It's after at least 34 people, the majority of whom were Ethiopian Orthodox Christians, were killed in a second tragic attack in two weeks.
Reports from residents, cited by Open Doors, fear that the death toll coud rise from a strike in Andato Kebele in Robe District, East Arsi Zone, on 5 November.
It was the second attack against Christians in a fortnight after 60 Orthodox Christians were killed in another attack in the same area - with Open Doors reporting that at least nearly 100 Orthodox Christians have been killed since October in targeted attacks.
East African persecution expert Fikuru Mehari, has urged greater awareness of the threats against Orthodox Christians in the central and south region of Oromia, as he sees attacks "intensifying".
"There have been recent attacks against Orthodox Christians in Ethiopia for six years, but over the last three, four years, it's intensified," he told Premier Christian News. "Local leadership of the Orthodox Church, are saying that they are targeted, their members are killed, and when they report the killings, they were mentioning about targets against: men and women, children, elderly - without any discrimination."
"They also report that the the church building itself has been attacked. Some of the church buildings are close to 190-195 years old. The government says that it's rebel groups whom are active in the area doing these attacks."
Ethopia is ranked as the 36th most dangerous country in the world for Christians in Open Doors' World Watch List. The group has called on the Ethiopian government to "take proactive measures to protect vulnerable citizens".
Road blockages are reportedly restricting emergency services from reaching the wounded and injured, and looting has begun in affected areas.
Members of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church have alleged that encouraging Muslim settlement into the majority Orthodox Christian zone has stirred tension.
"There is heated debate as to whether enough protection has been provided and this is a continued conversation across the country," Mehari said. "People are increasingly feeling unhappy and uneasy that how this situation is being handled and where it's heading. If these attacks are not addressed, it will be a motivation for revenge attacks in other parts of the country. It is very sad."
Christians in the region have called for an independent investigation into the attacks and have asked for prayer.
"Pray for wisdom for the government officials and for the leaders of Ethiopian Orthodox Church, because if they are not able to handle it well, there will be really grave consequences for future relationship between religious communities in the area," Mehari said.
