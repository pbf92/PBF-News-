TITLE: Manchester United, Liverpool and Spurs using same law firm to set up legal claim against City
OUTLET: The Guardian football

Manchester United, Liverpool and Tottenham are using the same law firm to prepare a legal claim against Manchester City for breaking the Premier League’s financial rules that could be worth hundreds of millions of pounds.
The clubs have engaged the services of the London-based media, entertainment and technology specialists Wiggin. Arsenal are using their retained lawyers, HFS Kramer, to prepare their own claim.
A source with knowledge of the case disclosed there had been a degree of cooperation between the clubs using Wiggin, because they have also engaged the same analysts to assess the size of the potential losses allegedly caused by City’s financial cheating, but that they would bring separate claims for damages.
“The cases are similar but different,” the source said. “The ultimate causation may be the same, but the damages will be different so they will be separate claims.”
Another source disclosed that Tottenham initially engaged Wiggin while Daniel Levy was the chair and were the first club to make legal moves against City after they were charged with financial fair play breaches in February 2023, with United and Liverpool following their lead.
Those three clubs and Arsenal lodged legal notices in November 2024 reserving their right to seek compensation against City to avoid their potential claims being ruled time-barred, one of the mechanisms through which City succeeded in 2020 in having a two-year ban from the Champions League imposed by Uefa for similar alleged offences lifted by the court of arbitration for sport.
Standard breach of contract claims have a six-year statute limitation from the moment they are made public, and with the allegations of City’s rule-breaking first published in the German news magazine Der Spiegel in November 2018, four of the other members of the big six acted to reserve their rights.
Chelsea did not instruct lawyers to begin a claim against City at that point, and are not believed to have done so since, despite the fact that extensions to the time limit can be granted in cases of fraud and concealment.
The four clubs who are preparing legal action have stepped up their activities since the Premier League announced in September that City had been found guilty of the vast majority of more than 100 charges against them, with an independent commission ruling that the club had artificially inflated its income by more than £900m through the use of “sham contracts” between 2009 and 2018.
Tottenham are likely to make the biggest claim against City because they missed out on Champions League qualification to them on four occasions during that period, with dropping into the Europa League costing them more than £120m.
United and Liverpool finished second in the Premier League to City during years when City have been found to have inflated their income, in 2012 and 2014 respectively, costing them prize money and commercial revenue and bonuses.
Arsenal missed out on Champions League qualification by finishing fifth in the Premier League in 2017, the start of a six-season run outside Europe’s elite competition that combined will have cost them hundreds of millions of pounds, as City ended up third.
Arsenal also sold five key players to City during the period of their breaches – Kolo Touré, Gaël Clichy, Emmanuel Adebayor, Bacary Sagna and Samir Nasri – and may claim additional losses on the grounds that their purchases were artificially funded.
Everton, Newcastle and Chelsea also missed out on Champions League places during the relevant period, and numerous clubs including Hull were relegated in part owing to their results against City.
Stoke lost the 2011 FA Cup final to City, costing them a Europa League place, and their then manager, Tony Pulis, said in October that the club should be awarded the trophy.
City have denied wrongdoing since being charged almost four years ago and have launched an appeal against the independent commission’s verdict, which will be heard by a new three-person panel next month. After launching their appeal in October, City issued a statement saying the commission’s judgment contained “multiple clear material errors of law, principle and fact and is unsafe” and that “the club is innocent of the accusations made by the Premier League and a comprehensive body of irrefutable evidence exists in support of all its positions, relating to this case”.
