TITLE: Meta Settles With Huckabee Over Fake CBD Endorsements 11/18/2026
OUTLET: MediaPost

Meta Platforms has resolved a lawsuit by
former Arkansas Governor Mike Huckabee over phony Facebook ads that made it appear that he endorsed CBD gummies, according to court documents.
Settlement terms were not
disclosed. The deal brings an end to a battle dating to July 2024, when Huckabee, currently U.S.ambassador to Israel, sued Meta over the ads, including one that linked to a fake Fox News site.
He claimed Meta violated Arkansas's misappropriation law, which gives state residents the right to control the commercial use of their names and images.
U.S.
District Judge Gregory Williams in Delaware initially threw out Huckabee's lawsuit, ruling last year that the allegations in his complaint, even if proven true, wouldn't support an inference that Meta
knew Huckabee's image was used without his authorization.

Huckabee appealed to the Third Circuit Court of Appeals, which revived his claim and returned the case to Williams for
further proceedings.
The Third Circuit pointed to several allegations in Huckabee's complaint that, if true, could have notified Meta that the ads were fake -- including that
Facebook previously hosted similar phony ads that used famous people's name and images.
After the case was sent back to Williams, Meta argued that it should be dismissed for a
different reason. 
Specifically, the platform contended that the allegations -- if proven true -- would not show Meta itself used Huckabee's name or likeness for its own commercial benefit.
"The only commercial benefit that Meta is alleged to have received was revenue it earned as a distributor of the third-party content, which is unrelated to the contents of the
challenged advertisements and the alleged appropriation of plaintiff’s name and likeness," the platform argued to Williams.
Williams rejected that argument in August,
writing that Huckabee's complaint "adequately alleges commercial use."
The judge specifically pointed to allegations that Meta "sells advertisements to support its operations,
accepts payments so that certain advertisements appear more prominently on the feeds of its users," and "approved and maintained the challenged advertisements."
Meta still
faces several other lawsuits over fake ads, including one filed last month by Rhode
Island Attorney General Peter Neronha.
