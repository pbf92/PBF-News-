TITLE: Site or app
OUTLET: PPC Land

Site or app is a reporting dimension in Amazon Ads unified reporting that names the specific property on which an ad was shown: a website domain, a mobile or television application, an Amazon-owned surface or, for sponsored ads, a retailer. It exists because Amazon campaigns now deliver far beyond Amazon.com: display and video budgets reach thousands of third-party publishers through exchanges, and Sponsored Products has served on outside sites and apps since October 2023. Without a field that records the property, an advertiser can see what a campaign cost but not where the money went. In Amazon's dimensions reference, Site or app sits in the Supply category, beside fields such as supply source, supply type, publisher name and deal ID.
What the field records
Amazon's unified report organises 156 attributes into 11 categories. PPC Land's reading of the November 11, 2026 version of the reference found that Site or app is marked for DVA+, Sponsored Products and Sponsored TV, and not for Sponsored Brands or Sponsored Display. That makes it unusual. Of the 35 supply attributes, the article's text says 32 apply to DVA+ alone, while its own summary table counts 34 for DVA+ and only two for Sponsored Products; Site or app is one of the few supply fields that crosses from programmatic buying into search advertising.
The same reference carries a mapping table of former names. Site or app is listed as "Site" in the old DSP (demand-side platform) reports and as "Retailer" in sponsored ads reports. Where a dimension does not apply to an ad product, Amazon's reference says its value appears as null, so Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display rows in a mixed report should return blanks.
Amazon has not published, in material readable outside its console, a list of the values the field returns or the format they take. Funnel, a data-integration vendor, lists "Site" among the dimensions of the legacy Amazon DSP Inventory report, next to Placement Name, Deal ID and Placement Size, without defining it. Adbrew, an Amazon reporting vendor, wrote on October 27, 2026 that the old Inventory report maps in unified reporting to "Supply source, Site or app, Deal, Placement name".
Where the value comes from
For DVA+ delivery on the open internet, the property name originates in the bid request. Under OpenRTB, the protocol maintained by IAB Tech Lab and used by most exchanges, a request describes where the ad will appear using either a site object or an app object (2.6 adds a third, dooh, for digital out-of-home screens). The site object carries a domain and page URL. The app object carries an app name, a store URL and a bundle ID, the store identifier such as com.example.news for Android or a numeric ID for iOS. The supply-side platform (SSP) that forwards the request fills these fields from what the publisher declares.
A worked example shows how the columns combine. A video ad shown inside a news publisher's iOS app arrives as an app request; Environment would read app, Device type would read mobile, Supply source would name the exchange and Site or app would identify the publisher's application. The same ad on the publisher's website in a desktop browser would change Environment to web and Site or app to the domain. On Amazon's own inventory the path is shorter, because no exchange is involved: placements on Amazon.com, IMDb, Fire TV, Prime Video or Twitch come from Amazon's ad servers. Amazon has not documented how those surfaces are labelled in the column, or whether apps appear by name or by bundle ID.
For Sponsored Products the route is different again. When Amazon extended the format to outside publishers, it said the ads would appear on "premium apps and websites" in the US, naming Pinterest, BuzzFeed, Hearst Newspapers, Raptive and Ziff Davis titles such as Lifehacker and Mashable. Those placements are bought through Amazon's own auction rather than the open exchange, so the property is known to Amazon directly.
Origin and evolution
The DSP half of the field is old. Site-level reporting was part of Amazon DSP's Inventory report for years, and declaring the property in the bid request dates to OpenRTB 2.0, published in 2012, which already separated site and app objects.
The sponsored ads half is recent. On October 25, 2023, at its unBoxed conference, Amazon announced that Sponsored Products would run on third-party sites and apps, with advertisers enrolled automatically. Colleen Aubrey, then senior vice president of Amazon Ads products and technology, said at the time that "Sponsored Products has always been about helping customers discover products they may love." Reporting for those placements went into the Sponsored Products placement report, where, according to Intentwise, an "Off Amazon" category appeared that "does not break down where specifically your ads are appearing."
A second strand began on January 9, 2025, when Amazon announced Amazon Retail Ad Service, technology built on Amazon Web Services that lets other retailers sell sponsored ads in their own online stores. The beta named iHerb, Oriental Trading Company and Weee!, with Tilly's launching. Amazon's page for the resulting product, Sponsored products across retailers, says its reports break down "by retailer, campaign, ad group, and keyword". That retailer breakdown is the most likely source of the "Retailer" field now folded into Site or app, although Amazon's mapping table does not say so explicitly.
Controls followed reporting gaps. In spring 2025 Amazon added campaign-level settings for ads served off Amazon, and on June 8, 2026 it added an off-Amazon ad serving column to Sponsored Products bulksheets for US advertisers. From August 10, 2026, Sponsored Products campaigns gained creator placements through the Amazon Influencer Program, with existing campaigns enrolled by default and deny lists available for sites and apps.
Unified reporting supplied the shared column. Amazon launched the system in beta in November 2025 and moved it to general availability on June 8, 2026. On September 29, 2026, Amazon DSP was renamed DVA and its new campaign type became DVA+, which is why the reference marks the field DVA+ rather than DSP.
Why it matters
For programmatic buyers, Site or app is the audit column. It is where low-quality inventory shows up: made-for-advertising sites, apps with improbable traffic, or properties an advertiser excluded but still paid for. Adbrew's guidance is to "add Site or app only when a supply source looks wrong, because the site list on open exchanges runs to thousands of rows." It is also the input for inclusion and exclusion lists.
For Sponsored Products advertisers, the field may close a gap that has existed since 2023. If Site or app returns the property for off-Amazon Sponsored Products rows, advertisers will be able to compare cost and sales by publisher or retailer rather than reading a single off-Amazon total.
The field matters for supply-path work too. Reseller routes to the same publisher are multiplying: DataBeat's analysis of the top 50,000 US publishers found that reseller ads.txt lines grew by a net 44,608 in a month against 10,334 for direct lines. Read with the Ad inventory seller fields, Site or app shows how many paths led to one property.
Limitations and disputes
The first limitation is documentation. Amazon has not published the value list, the naming convention for apps or how Amazon-owned surfaces appear.
Second, the value is only as accurate as the seller's declaration. Domain spoofing, where a low-quality site passes itself off as a premium one in the bid request, and misdeclared app bundles are long-standing fraud patterns. Ads.txt, introduced by IAB Tech Lab in 2017, and app-ads.txt, finalised in March 2019, let buyers check whether a seller is authorised for a property, but they confirm permission, not the identity of every impression. The proposed adagents.json format is one attempt to tighten this.
Third, coverage is uneven. The field is marked for Sponsored TV, yet PPC Land noted the reference also states "We do not support Streaming TV Smart mode/ Sponsored TV yet". It is not marked for Sponsored Display, even though Sponsored Display runs off Amazon too.
Fourth, volume constrains use. A site list running to thousands of rows becomes harder to pull as a daily breakdown now that Amazon limits daily unified reports to 120 days per request out of 24 months of history.
Commonly confused terms
Site or app vs publisher name. Publisher name identifies the owner of content rights for an app, site or video stream, and Publisher owner gives the parent company. One publisher can operate many sites and apps.
Site or app vs environment. Environment classifies the software container, web or app. Site or app names the specific property.
Site or app vs placement name. Placement name, a DVA+ field, identifies an ad slot or position within a property; Placement classification groups placements such as top of search or off Amazon.
Site or app vs supply source. Supply source names the exchange or Amazon-owned inventory pool the impression came through, not the property itself.
Recent developments
Amazon updated its unified reporting dimensions reference on November 11, 2026, publishing the former-name mapping that ties Site or app to "Site" and "Retailer". On November 17, 2026, it updated its data availability page, setting the 120-day daily pull limit. Legacy report creation stops on December 17, 2026, and unmigrated reports will be lost on January 31, 2027, so DSP-era Inventory reports and sponsored ads retailer reports must be rebuilt around the new column. From January 1, 2027, DVA display ads on the Amazon Store will count an impression only when on screen, and Amazon expects 63% fewer impressions on average, which will alter figures for Amazon-owned rows.
