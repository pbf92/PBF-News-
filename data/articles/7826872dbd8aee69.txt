TITLE: Archbishop of York to be consulted on penalty for former bishop over safeguarding misconduct
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

It’s been decided that the Archbishop of York should be consulted on the appropriate penalty for a former Bishop of Durham who admitted misconduct over safeguarding.
Rt Rev Paul Butler was one of seven clergy who faced disciplinary measures under the Clergy Discipline Measure (CDM) after being heavily criticised in the independent Makin Review into the late barrister John Smyth’s abuse. Bishop Paul was asked to “step back from ministry” in 2024 after the review found failings in the way disclosures of abuse were dealt with by senior figures in the Church of England.
At a Church hearing earlier this year, the former lead bishop for safeguarding admitted misconduct and the Vicar General of York said a penalty decision would be decided at a later stage.
It’s understood lawyers felt there was enough information to pass down a penalty without consulting Most Rev Stephen Cottrell, Archbishop of York but according to Church Times, in a ruling on 10 November, the court decided that involving him would be “extremely powerful” and was “important not only for the Respondent, the Church, and the public at large, but also for the victims of Mr Smyth and their families particularly.”
It’s understood a hearing to determine the penalty will take place on 14 December.
In the 1970s and 80s Smyth subjected around 130 boys and young men to extreme physical and sexual abuse under the guise of spiritual discipline. He’s believed to be the most prolific serial abuser to be associated with the Church of England.
Bishop Paul retired to the Diocese of Southwell and Nottingham in 2024.
