Anthropic opens beta for Claude safety monitoring with customer-held data
Enterprise Frontier Safeguards keeps automated misuse checks running without provider-held conversation logs. Customers supply the storage—and the people who review alerts.

Anthropic’s Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS) entered public beta on November 23, 2026, adding cross-request safety monitoring for eligible organizations using zero-data-retention setups. It stores encrypted prompts, responses, and model reasoning in customer-controlled cloud storage, then creates alerts for customers to investigate; Anthropic says it does not access EFS inputs or outputs. The design shifts review responsibility to customers, and its claimed ability to catch coordinated misuse has not been independently measured. Deployment also has material limits, including unsupported APIs and organization-wide compliance settings.
EFS is available through Claude Platform, Claude Platform on AWS, Amazon Bedrock, and Claude on Google Cloud; Microsoft Foundry support is planned.
Safety Cases omit prompts and responses but point customers to related transcripts; sealed records can remain in customer storage for up to 28 days.
Claude Platform EFS applies to every Messages API request in an enabled workspace; Batch API and Claude Managed Agents sessions are unsupported.
Eligible companies can now keep Claude’s safety-monitoring records in their own cloud accounts rather than Anthropic’s, while taking responsibility for reviewing flagged activity. Anthropic opened Enterprise Frontier Safeguards in public beta on November 23, 2026, pairing its zero-data-retention offering with automated checks designed to spot misuse across multiple requests.
The release follows a September announcement and private testing. Anthropic says it developed the system with more than 100 customers, including a quarter of the Fortune 100. Bridgewater, Salesforce and Stripe were among the companies evaluating it in private beta.
EFS is available through Claude Platform, Claude Platform on AWS, Amazon Bedrock and Claude on Google Cloud. Microsoft Foundry support is coming soon. Despite the public-beta label, access remains limited to eligible organizations; the Claude Platform setup guide restricts it to internal use and excludes organizations with HIPAA readiness enabled.
Anthropic’s rationale is that screening each request separately can miss a coordinated attack. Its older zero-data-retention arrangement uses real-time classifiers—automated checks that assess individual requests for potential harm. But an attacker can divide a larger operation into tasks that look benign on their own, the company says.
EFS adds automated monitoring that searches for patterns across requests using records stored on customer-controlled infrastructure. Anthropic says this can help detect intentional misuse, such as offensive cyber operations, as well as agents acting beyond a user’s intentions. Those are the company’s stated detection capabilities, not independently measured results.
When monitoring flags a risk, it creates a Safety Case available through cloud storage or an API, with support for integration into security-management systems. The case contains no prompts or responses. Instead, it identifies related transcripts that the customer’s team can read and investigate. Anthropic’s human review is always off under EFS.
The distinction is who holds the data. Anthropic says it deletes prompts and responses promptly after generating the response and writing them to the customer’s storage. Its launch announcement says neither Anthropic nor the cloud provider keeps a copy after delivery. The customer-controlled records remain available for automated monitoring.
Those records include Sealed Safety Data: prompts, responses and model reasoning encrypted in Anthropic’s format. Customers cannot read that sealed material, even in their own accounts. The customer-owned storage documentation specifies a retention period of up to 28 days, separate from the readable transcripts used for investigations.
Anthropic still keeps a record of safety correspondence, including cases, notices and customers’ responses. Authorized personnel can read that correspondence, but it contains no prompts or responses. Separately, Anthropic says operator personnel do not access EFS inputs and outputs, and those inputs and outputs are never used to train models or classifiers.
Customers must supply cloud storage; a customer-managed encryption key is optional. Claude Platform supports Amazon S3 or Google Cloud Storage, while the AWS route uses S3. Anthropic charges no EFS fee and says it changes neither model behavior, API pricing nor rate limits. Cloud storage, operations, key usage and applicable network transfers still incur charges.

Enabling access also has effects beyond the chosen workspace. Accepting the terms turns on the Compliance API for the organization and other eligible organizations linked through its Verification Portal profile. Access Transparency, which records covered Anthropic personnel access, becomes organization-wide once enabled and cannot be switched off.
The immediate deployment rule is to test before sending sensitive traffic. Anthropic’s setup guide warns that records may reach Anthropic-managed storage until the bucket selection takes effect. It instructs teams to use nonsensitive test requests, confirm a new object has arrived in their bucket, then wait another 10 minutes before sending real traffic.
