TITLE: Church urged to take action against racism towards Gypsy, Roma and Traveller communities
OUTLET: Premier Christian News

The Church of England has been urged to "visibly, loudly and practically" tackle prejudice against Gypsy, Roma and Traveller (GRT) communities.
The plea was made by Rt Rev Rosemarie Mallett, Bishop of Croydon and Acting Bishop of Southwark, at the Sanctuary Roads II Conference, hosted at Southwark Cathedral,
The conference is backed by funding from the Church of England’s Racial Justice Unit and organised by the Diocese of Salisbury and Gypsy, Roma, Traveller Friendly Churches. It's the second one held aimed at monitoring progress since the General Synod voted in 2019 to combat racism against GRT communities.
“As a lead bishop, I urge the church in each diocese and every parish and cathedral and every school, including our church schools, to commit itself afresh to combating racism against Gypsy, Roma and Traveller communities," the bishop said. “We are a church that has promised much, let us be known for what we do."
The conference heard about the shortage of land for GRT communities, with Rev Jonathan Herbert, Chaplain to GRT communities in the Salisbury diocese, sharing how they are working to build relationships, identify land and engage with local communities to create sites.
"Making land available for sites would show that the Church of England is serious about its commitment to justice for Gypsies and Travellers, for actions always speak louder than words," he said.
Other contributors included Sarah Mann, chief executive officer of Friends, Families and Travellers, (FFT) and Krish Kandiah, founder of the Sanctuary Foundation.
