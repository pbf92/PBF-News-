OUTLET: Manchester Evening News

'That's a load of rubbish' - How woman drugged and raped by husband batted away defence claims during dramatic 45-minute court testimony
The woman gave evidence in court for the first time during the trial
Four weeks into a trial centred around the horrendous ordeal she suffered at the hands of her husband, the 12 members of the jury finally saw the woman in court in person for the first time.
She sat in the witness box of court one at Minshull Street Crown Court for about 45 minutes. In a cramped, windowless rectangular room, perched just feet away from nine men who are accused of sexual offences against her, the woman was questioned by barristers acting on their behalf. She gave her account in full view of the defendants.
One lawyer insisted it was not his intention to upset her or cause distress. But during those three quarters of an hour, she did not display an ounce of any such emotion. Instead, she maintained that she had never met any of these men, let alone pretended to be asleep while having sex with them.

As far as she was concerned, the last time she had engaged in sex with her husband was about nine years ago. She had no idea her husband was secretly drugging and raping her, the court heard. And she was adamant that she had not been ‘pretending’ to be asleep as part of a ‘sleep fetish’.
“That’s a load of rubbish, is what I would say,” the wife said, as she batted away the suggestion. Of the emotions on show during her evidence in court, one was humour. Asked about her bedroom routine, joking with the interviewing officer in a pre-recorded video played to jurors, she said: "Depends on how good I'm being.”
And earlier in the recorded video, after telling police her sex life had been ‘vanilla’, she laughed: “I sound like a prude saying it like that.” Her day in court today (November 25) came more than a year after police turned up at her home on October 23 to inform her that her husband was under arrest. Weeks later, she still appeared to be in shock.
Asked if she knew anything about what had happened, the wife told police in her first police interview, which took place on November 7: "Well I had no knowledge of what you told me had happened and I still can't believe it's true and I think you have made a mistake."
She insisted that she’d never consented to anyone else other than her husband having sex with her, and she had never agreed for anyone to have sex with her while she was asleep or unconscious. Wearing a flowery white shirt, she remained composed as police asked her a series of direct questions as they took her account for about an hour. She had only ever taken drugs prescribed to her by her GP, jurors heard.
But she recalled feeling a ‘bit weird’ the morning after a night out in the summer of 2024, and getting up late which she said was unusual for her. She told how her and her husband had what she described as ‘a normal relationship’ and that they were a ‘loving couple’. “We did things together, went out with other people, we’ve always had quite a lot of friends and a good social circle and done things,” she said.
She said they had a ‘vanilla’ sex life. The wife said: “Just normal, vanilla, if you go to the Fifty Shades of Grey thing,” she said. Asked what her husband was like during the period they did not have sex, she said: “Is it my fault that he has done this then?”
The officer said: “Absolutely not, absolutely not, I don’t want you to ever think that.” “Ok,” she said. The officer said to the wife: “We need to understand what’s happening, what conversations you might have had or not had about it, just so we understand it.”
She held a glass of water and sat back on a brown couch in a nondescript police interview room as the questions continued. The wife laughed after being asked about her drinking habits, and recalled the morning after a funeral where she talked about a neighbour commenting ‘God, she is going to be bad in the morning’ .
The wife said: “I think I was a little bit rough actually.” In her second police interview, held on June 23 this year, she was shown images of the defendants on trial. She told the police that she did not recognise any of them.
The wife, wearing a polka dot top, sat on the edge of her seat on that same brown couch, in the same nondescript room, as she leant over to get a clearer look. She maintained she’d never met or had any sexual contact with any of them.
After jurors had firstly been shown those two video interviews, they then watched the woman make her way into court to give her live testimony. Given the first opportunity to question her, the prosecution only sought to confirm her name and other brief details, before giving way to allow defence barristers the opportunity to ask questions.
Only three of the nine defence barristers took that opportunity. Pierce Power, on behalf of the defendant Philip Wild, told her it was not his intention to 'upset you deliberately or at all', rather it was to give her an ‘opportunity to comment' upon his client's case.
She maintained that she stood by her account that she has never met Mr Wild before. The wife rejected claims that she had been awake and spoken with him when he attended. The wife said: “That’s not true.”

Rachel White, for the defendant Jordan Wallace, told the wife she was not suggesting she is ‘telling lies’. She said that Mr Wallace claims that the wife was sitting on her bed with a glass of wine and offered him a drink when he attended.
“That’s not true,” she replied. After suggesting she was taking part in ‘role play’, the wife replied: “No. I have never taken part in role play,” the wife said.
On behalf of Jonathan Kirk, Katy Thorne KC, said that the defendant claims to have spoken with her on the night he attended the home. “I have never met him or spoke to him ever,” the wife said.
She denied having a ‘sleep fetish’. “What would you say if I was to suggest that you were pretending to be asleep?” Ms Thorne asked. “That’s a load of rubbish, is what I would say,” the wife said.
Tomorrow, the jury was told they will hear testimony from the wife's husband. It promises to be another important day in the trial.
The husband - a man in his 60s from Stockport - has admitted multiple offences, including rape and administering substances with intent to stupefy his wife so other men could engage in sexual activity with her.
The Manchester Evening News and other media organisations are not naming the man in order to protect his victim's anonymity. The nine men on trial deny the charges they face. Seven of the nine defendants have been described as 'contact offenders', who jurors heard visited the couple's home expecting her to be 'wasted' and 'out of it'.
It is alleged the 'contact offenders' are taxi driver Philip Wild, 59; recruitment agency worker Mohammed Sabir, 28; building supplies employee Jordan Wallace, 31; industrial cleaning firm worker Alan Keelan, 42; retiree Robert Stewart, 70; sheet metal worker Richard Townsend, 38; and senior paramedic Jonathan Kirk, 43.
The Crown alleges 'non-contact' offenders, who allegedly 'hid behind a screen' as they conspired to administer substances to the wife, are kitchen firm sales rep David Graves, 60; and self-employed mechanic Daniel Rayner, 42. Four other men - Keith Fotheringham, Karl Lindsay, Sean Peers and Graham Brougham - pleaded guilty at earlier hearings to various offences linked to the case.
