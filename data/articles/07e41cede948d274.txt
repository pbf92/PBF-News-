TITLE: Gemini 4 Neon reportedly goes live in Google AI Studio after days of leaks
OUTLET: Crypto Briefing

A post on X says the newest Gemini 4 model is available in Studio, following code references, cloud billing listings and published pricing
A post on X says Gemini 4 Neon is now available in Google’s AI Studio. If that holds, Google has added a fourth model to its Gemini 4 lineup in roughly one season.
Google itself had not made an official announcement or confirmed API availability as of the latest reports on the model.
The breadcrumb trail
The name Gemini 4 Neon first appeared on November 16, 2026, inside code for the Gemini web app. It sat next to Argon and Carbon, two Gemini 4 models that had already shipped.
Two days later, on November 18, 2026, the model showed up in Google Cloud SKU listings. A SKU is a billing line item, the entry a cloud provider uses to charge customers for a specific product.
The same day, Logan Kilpatrick of Google posted a single word on X: “Gemini.” The timing lined up with the SKU disclosures.
What Neon costs
The listed pricing puts Gemini 4 Neon at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. The listing also showed lower rates for certain tiers.
Tokens are the small chunks of text an AI model reads and writes. Input tokens cover what you send the model. Output tokens cover what it sends back.

Under these rates, generating text costs five times as much as feeding text in.
A periodic table of models
Neon is not arriving alone. Google released three other Gemini 4 models, Argon, Carbon and Hydrogen, between September and mid-November 2026.
That is three launches in roughly two and a half months. Neon would make four.
The naming scheme borrows from chemistry. Hydrogen, Carbon, Argon and Neon are all elements, and Neon joins Argon in the noble gas family.
Neon has followed that pattern closely. Code reference first, then billing, then pricing, then a cryptic post, and now a claim of availability in Studio.
What this means for developers
For developers, AI Studio access is the practical milestone. It is where they can try a model, run test prompts and judge whether it is worth wiring into a product before committing real budget.
Until Google publishes official documentation, developers are working without a full spec sheet. Features, limits and even final pricing could shift between a quiet rollout and a formal launch.
There are a few things worth watching closely. First, whether Neon reaches the Gemini API, not just Studio. Second, whether the live pricing matches the $3 and $15 figures from the SKU listings. Third, which customers qualify for the lower tier rates, since that will shape real-world costs more than the headline numbers.
For now, the evidence points in one direction. The code mentioned it, the billing system listed it, the pricing was published, and a post on X says it is live in Studio. The only thing missing is Google saying so in an official announcement.
