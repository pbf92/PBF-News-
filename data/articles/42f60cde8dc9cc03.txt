S&P 500, Nasdaq End Lower As AI Jitters Resurface With Nvidia’s Governance Issues And Talks Of Fresh Import Tariffs — NVDA, DELL, TSLA, TGTX In Focus
- The S&P 500 fell about 0.5%, the Nasdaq 100 dropped 2%, and the Dow Jones index rose 0.9% on Tuesday.
- U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer may release results as soon as Tuesday from a months-long subsidy inquiry covering China, the European Union and 14 other countries.
- Dell Technologies reported a record Q3 revenue and hiked its full-year FY27 revenue target.
The S&P 500 and Nasdaq dropped on Tuesday as optimism around Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO in the previous session was quick to fizzle out as investor attention moved to the possibility of fresh import tariffs by President Trump.
The S&P 500 fell about 0.5%, the Nasdaq 100 dropped 2%, and the Dow Jones index rose 0.9% on Tuesday. The Russell 2000, which tracks small-cap stocks, ended 0.6% higher.
The SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY) fell about 0.6%, and the SPDR Dow Jones Industrial Average ETF (DIA) slipped 0.7%. Meanwhile, the Nasdaq-100 tracking Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ) rose 2.2% on Tuesday.
Meanwhile, the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) and the iShares Semiconductor ETF fell between 7.3% and 16%, with Nvidia (NVDA) leading the charts across the board.
Retail sentiment on Stocktwits was 'neutral' on the SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY), 'extremely bullish' on the Invesco QQQ (QQQ) and 'bearish' on the SPDR Dow Jones ETF (DIA).
US Market Drivers
The Trump administration is reportedly moving toward imposing higher import duties on China and dozens of other trading partners that Washington contends are placing American manufacturers at a disadvantage.
U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer is expected to publish findings from an inquiry that lasted several months as early as Tuesday, the Wall Street Journal reported. The inquiry takes a deep dive into industrial subsidies across China, the 27-nation European Union and 14 additional countries, though sources cautioned the timeline remains subject to change.
"I think we're still going to see increased volatility in the market because there's just so many unknowns," the head of investment decision research at the firm told CNBC, SimCorp's Diana Baechle told CNBC.
Meanwhile, U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche on Tuesday pushed back against suggestions that Nvidia or Huang is under criminal investigation, while refusing to provide any further details related to the chipmaker.
