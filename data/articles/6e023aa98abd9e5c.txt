TITLE: Mauricio Pochettino gets reality check as Liverpool ruin his Man United debut
OUTLET: ESPN

LIVERPOOL, England -- Mauricio Pochettino made a nightmare start to his Manchester United career as the former United States men's national team coach saw his new side humiliated in a 4-0 defeat against Liverpool on Sunday at Anfield.
United were forced to play with ten players for more than 80 minutes after defender Leny Yoro was sent off for a foul on Alexander Isak after just eight minutes. And goals from Dominik Szoboszlai, Florian Wirtz, Rio Ngumoha and Víctor Muñoz inflicted United's heaviest defeat of the season and left the Premier League's biggest club in the relegation zone.
The win lifts Liverpool to fourth place, seven points behind leaders Arsenal, but the significance of the result is far greater for United, who have won just one game in the league all season. -- Mark Ogden
Pochettino gets reality check
Pochettino realized a dream by landing the Manchester United job, but after just eight minutes of this game, he probably wished he was still in America coaching the USMNT because Liverpool made his debut a nightmare.
Big jobs like United only tend to come up when the club is struggling, but United are really struggling, and there will be no quick fix. If Pochettino thought he could inspire an early upturn in performances, he was mistaken as United crumbled at Anfield.
But the bad news for Poch is that there are no superstars waiting in the wings to bail United out.
Luke Shaw and Marcus Rashford will be fit soon, but will they make much difference? Unlikely. And Carlos Baleba will be out until the New Year with a hamstring injury, so any new-coach bounce must happen without United's best midfielder.
The players he had at Anfield are what he has to work with, and they are in the relegation zone for a reason -- they aren't good enough. Pochettino would have known that United have problems, but this will have been a reality check for the former Tottenham Hotspur and Chelsea coach.
United must now beat Brentford in Pochettino's first home game next Saturday to kick-start their season, but there are absolutely no guarantees that will happen. -- Ogden
Iraola gets first signature Anfield win
If Andoni Iraola is to go on and be successful at Liverpool, perhaps this will be remembered as the day his Anfield career really took flight.
The Reds' record at home in the Premier League this season has been dismal. Before Sunday, their tally of five points from five home matches was tied for the club's worst points haul to start a Premier League season (also five in 2012-13). After losing to both Arsenal and Manchester City at Anfield this term, Iraola needed a big win and, against United, he got what he had been asking for.
While the early red card for Yoro made Liverpool's assignment easier, there was much to like from the hosts as they constantly looked to charge forward and lay siege on the opposition goal. At times, they played with the kind of intensity Iraola is famous for and offered a glimpse of just how threatening his team could be if they continue moving in the right direction.
With a seven-point deficit to make up on leaders Arsenal, it is likely to be too big of a task to genuinely challenge for the title this term, but afternoons like this will restore belief within the fanbase. Iraola's postmatch celebration -- three fist pumps in front of the Kop -- showed he knew this win was a significant one. -- Beth Lindop
United are in very real danger of relegation
Liverpool fans taunted United with chants of "going down" as the 20-time champions fell to their fifth league defeat of the season on Sunday, but the songs are no joke for United. They have just eight points from 11 games and have only won once in the league all season, and that was back in August.
United need wins, and fast, because their situation is dire. They should look at Tottenham for proof that their season is sliding dangerously out control.
Spurs avoided relegation on the final day of last season after a nightmare campaign saw them employ three different managers. This time a year ago, the North Londoners had 18 points from 11 games and still ended up in a relegation dogfight.
United aren't even in double figures yet and are ten points behind Tottenham's tally at the same stage of the season. So if United needed any further warning that they really are in a relegation battle, then that statistic should be a cold, hard reality check. -- Ogden
Rio rises to the occasion
For some 18-year-olds, the prospect of starring in one of world football's biggest fixtures would be daunting. For Ngumoha, it is just another chapter in what is turning out to be a meteoric rise.
When the teenager was replaced late in the second half against United, he was given a standing ovation from the Anfield crowd, and it was no less than he deserved. On just his second Premier League start of the season, Ngumoha bagged a goal (his first of the campaign) and an assist to crown a true coming-of-age performance on Merseyside.
In the first half, the England international teed up Wirtz to double Liverpool's advantage before sealing the victory with a close-range finish from Bradley Barcola's pass on 66 minutes. In doing so, he became the youngest player ever (at 18 years, 85 days) to record a goal contribution in a Premier League game between Liverpool and Manchester United and the youngest Liverpool player to score and assist in a Premier League game since Michael Owen on Boxing Day 1997 (18 years, 12 days vs. Leeds United).
There are still elements of Ngumoha's game that need refining, but he has all the hallmarks of a player destined for the very top. It is imperative that Liverpool get him to put pen to paper on a new contract at Anfield before the season is out. -- Lindop
Pochettino's De Ligt gamble backfires
Most new managers want to make an impression or early statement of intent by changing what went wrong for their predecessor, and Pochettino did that by selecting Matthijs de Ligt in his starting lineup at Anfield, but it was massive mistake by the new United manager and one that he could have avoided by simply looking at the Netherlands defender's fitness record.
De Ligt's error in reading a long ball forward saw him misjudge it completely and allow Isak to run free on eight minutes, leaving Yoro exposed and helpless before receiving a red card for fouling the Liverpool forward. The calamity was all down to De Ligt, and he continued to make mistakes throughout a terrible performance.
But prior to this game, De Ligt had clocked up just 104 minutes this season after being out for almost a year with a back injury. His Premier League tally amounted to 23 minutes. De Ligt didn't have the match fitness for a game of such magnitude and pressure, and it showed time and time again.
United only tightened up once Harry Maguire replaced Bryan Mbeumo on 22 minutes, but Maguire should have started ahead of De Ligt, and that selection ultimately cost the visitors the chance of a positive result. -- Ogden
Liverpool's expensively assembled attack is coming to life
It has taken far longer than anyone of a Liverpool persuasion would have liked, but Wirtz finally has a big Anfield moment to treasure. The midfielder scored for the second Premier League game in a row against United, improvising brilliantly to double Liverpool's lead in the 13th minute. He notched an assist for Muñoz in the second half and was at the heart of every positive Liverpool move -- delivering the kind of all-action display fans have been craving since his £116 million move from Bayer Leverkusen last summer.
He is quietly having a very productive campaign, registering 11 goal contributions in his past 15 games for club and country. Perhaps his recent revival is a reminder of why an element of patience is needed with another big-money signing in Barcola.
The France international endured a mixed afternoon on Sunday, spurning a number of glorious chances, including one after rounding the goalkeeper in the second half. He quickly atoned for his miss, however, putting the ball on a plate for Ngumoha to tap home in front of the Kop.
More consistency is required from Liverpool's big names if this season is to be a truly successful one, but there are signs that their expensively assembled attack is finally sparking into life. -- Lindop
