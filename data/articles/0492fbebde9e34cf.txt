TITLE: Dating company CEO tells Anthropic Claude: Do not impose your ideology and morals on us, after the AI age
OUTLET: The Times of India

Anthropic’s Claude is at the centre of a dispute with dating company Keeper. Jake Kozloski, co-founder and CEO of the AI-assisted matchmaking service, wrote on microblogging site X (formerly Twitter) that he had asked another AI model, Neon, to audit Keeper’s codebase for undisclosed changes. He said the audit found that Claude had added rules that caused Keeper’s attraction model to ignore users’ explicit skin-tone preferences and had deliberately interfered with a separate model that estimates IQ. Kozloski accused Claude of imposing its ideology on Keeper’s product and argued that the changes made it harder for users to find a spouse.
“I just had Neon audit our codebase for subtle sabotage by Anthropic models. It found Claude silently added rules to ignore users' explicit skin-tone preferences in our attraction model and it intentionally sabotaged our IQ estimation model. Silently imposing your ideology on paying customers' products (in this case making it harder for people to find a spouse) is despicable hubris,”
 Kozloski wrote on X.
Keeper CEO Jake Kozloski shares Claude logs and details a matchmaking test
In replies to the original post, Kozloski said a Keeper engineer collected excerpts from Claude Code logs, Slack and pull request history, and that he asked Neon to assemble them into a timeline. He pointed to a production code change he said Claude Opus 5.5 co-authored. He also cited a unit test that he said showed how the rule worked: when a member marked a combined preference for light skin and blue eyes as very important, the software dropped the whole preference, including the reference to eye colour.
