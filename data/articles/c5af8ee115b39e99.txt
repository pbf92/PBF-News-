TITLE: Personalized Digital Nutrition Tools for Adolescents with Type 1 Diabetes: Systematic Review
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

Personalized digital nutrition interventions and automated insulin management tools can improve glycemic outcomes and lessen psychosocial distress among adolescents living with type 1 diabetes, according to a systematic review published in the Journal of Health, Population and Nutrition. Researchers synthesized findings to determine how modern digital health technologies support dietary self-management in young individuals navigating the distinctive daily demands of type 1 diabetes.
The review followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines and searched seven major academic databases from January 2012 to March 2026. The search covered PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, PsycINFO, and IEEE Xplore to gather literature examining personalized dietary interventions targeting individuals aged 10 to 25 years.
Eligible investigations required at least one reported glycemic, nutritional, behavioral, or psychosocial outcome. The final synthesis incorporated 35 studies published across 17 countries and encompassed more than 1,500 participants, capturing an international perspective on youth-focused nutritional technologies.
The included literature spanned diverse methodologies, comprising eight randomized controlled trials, ten pilot and pre-post interventional studies, 14 qualitative and mixed-methods evaluations, two survey studies, and one simulation study. Methodological quality was evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool for randomized trials, the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme checklist for qualitative research, and the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool for non-randomized and mixed-methods designs, with synthesis conducted via narrative and thematic approaches following SWiM guidelines.
Quantitative findings indicated that personalized macronutrient-aware bolus calculators accounting for dietary fat and protein enhanced postprandial glucose control and lowered estimation errors compared to standard carbohydrate counting alone. Additionally, dietary assessment technologies powered by artificial intelligence—incorporating photographic capture, 3D volumetric analysis, and voice-based logging—significantly improved carbohydrate estimation accuracy over traditional manual methods.
Within hybrid closed-loop automated insulin delivery systems, simplified meal announcement strategies proved non-inferior to rigorous carbohydrate counting regarding time in range. While these simplified approaches reduced cognitive burden for adolescents, investigators observed a concurrent increase in episodes of prolonged hyperglycemia.
Behavioral applications integrating gamification and real-time feedback successfully elevated self-monitoring frequency and user engagement. Qualitative evidence consistently documented reductions in treatment fatigue, diabetes-related stigma, family conflict, and broader psychosocial burden, while promoting personal empowerment, autonomy, and improved quality of life.
Investigators identified sustained engagement decay, technical usability hurdles, and cultural mismatches as notable barriers to continued intervention use. Conversely, sustained adoption was facilitated by dynamic individualization, social support components, low cognitive load, and seamless integration into youth digital environments, highlighting key considerations for future co-designed adolescent interventions.
Personalized macronutrient-aware bolus calculators improved postprandial glucose control by incorporating fat and protein. These calculators reduced estimation errors compared with standard carbohydrate counting. AI-driven dietary assessment tools, including photographic, 3D volumetric, and voice-based methods, improved carbohydrate estimation accuracy.
Simplified meal announcement strategies within hybrid closed-loop automated insulin delivery systems were evaluated. These strategies showed non-inferiority to precise carbohydrate counting for time in range. Simplified meal announcements maintained non-inferior time in range, though some increase in prolonged hyperglycemia was observed. These strategies reduced cognitive burden. An increase in prolonged hyperglycemia was observed with simplified meal announcements.
Digital interventions also demonstrated behavioral and psychosocial impacts. Gamified and real-time feedback applications increased self-monitoring frequency and engagement. Qualitative evidence showed reductions in psychosocial burden, diabetes-related stigma, treatment fatigue, and family conflict, alongside gains in empowerment, autonomy, and quality of life.
The review identified common barriers and facilitators to intervention use. Effective interventions share design principles including dynamic individualization, real-time feedback, low cognitive burden, and integration with the adolescent social and digital ecosystem. Barriers included engagement decay, usability issues, and cultural mismatches, while personalization, low cognitive load, and social support features facilitated sustained use. Future research should prioritize long-term engagement, cultural equity, and co-designed youth-centered implementation strategies.
