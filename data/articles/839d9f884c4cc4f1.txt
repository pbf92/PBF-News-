TITLE: 'GTA 6' broke a sales record no game or movie could touch for 13 years
OUTLET: Business Insider

For "Grand Theft Auto 6" to become the fastest-selling entertainment property — video game or movie — of all time, publisher Take-Two Interactive Software only had to beat itself.
Mission accomplished, and by a landslide.
Take-Two said on Friday that the latest installment in the crime-drama series generated $3.79 billion in worldwide sales in its first day.
The last game in the series, "GTA 5," rang up $1 billion in worldwide sales within three days of its 2013 release, making it the fastest-selling entertainment property at the time, per Guinness World Records. It beat Activision Blizzard's "Call of Duty: Black Ops II," which reached the milestone in 15 days in 2012, and Hollywood's then-fastest-grossing film, "The Avengers," which did so at the worldwide box office in 10 days.
Now, "GTA 6," made by Take-Two's Rockstar Games studio, owns the top spot. Its predecessor had held onto the video game record all this time, while the previous movie benchmark, the 2019 Marvel release "Avengers: Endgame," reached $1 billion at the worldwide box office in five days, according to Guinness World Records.
"It's a cultural event," said former longtime game-industry analyst Michael Pachter of the launch of "GTA 6" on November 19. "People are going to remember this day."
The higher price tag for "GTA 6" versus its predecessor likely helped. The new standard edition costs $80, while a $100 Ultimate Edition includes premium vehicles, weapons, apparel, and other in-game content tied to the story. When "GTA 5" debuted, the standard edition retailed for $60, and a special version for $80.
'GTA 6' frenzy
Early forecasts had pointed to a blockbuster launch for the new game, so the milestone isn't all that surprising.
Analytics firm Newzoo estimated in July that "GTA 6" was on pace for $3.3 billion to $5.2 billion in worldwide sales by the end of its launch week, while Benchmark analyst Mike Hickey noted in August that Take-Two's fiscal 2027 guidance embedded roughly 30 million unit sales. Take-Two's fiscal year ends March 31, 2027.
Hype surrounding the game's debut was exceptional. Workforce management tech company UKG said time-off requests for the day "GTA 6" debuted were about 20% higher than it has seen in comparable pre-Thanksgiving weeks.
Some employers leaned into the frenzy. The CEO of UK-based Raptor PR gave the agency's nine employees an extra day of annual leave for the release and bought each a copy of the game. Logitech, meanwhile, offered its more than 7,000 employees an extra paid day off to play any game they choose through a new "Logi Game Day" benefit.
In addition to "GTA 6," two other nostalgia-filled games debuted this month: Activision Blizzard's "World of Warcraft: Forever" and Nintendo's "The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time" remake.
Beyond the initial rush, "GTA 6" has more sales drivers ahead. A PC version expected next year could bring another wave of buyers, while price cuts and downloadable add-on content could also keep money flowing to Take-Two.
"I think people are still pricing the stock like it's a one-hit wonder," said Pachter, now managing director of strategic planning at financial-services firm Wedbush Securities. "It's not."
