TITLE: Scoping Review Identifies Diverse Coping Strategies Among Acute Care Nurses
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive scoping review examining international health literature has mapped the broad spectrum of coping mechanisms that registered nurses in acute and critical care environments employ to manage severe occupational stressors. Published in the Canadian Journal of Anesthesia, the systematic synthesis investigated how nursing professionals withstand the rigorous demands of intensive care units, emergency departments, and related acute settings. The research team highlighted that while nurses carry multifaceted clinical responsibilities that make them highly susceptible to heavy workplace strains, developing and maintaining constructive coping responses remains a continuous professional challenge. By systematically synthesizing evidence from published and unpublished documents, the investigators sought to clarify the specific behavioural, cognitive, emotional, and social techniques nurses adopt to manage intense clinical conditions. Their synthesis organized evidence across three overarching categories encompassing the explicit recognition of acute clinical stressors, the diverse coping strategies utilized by clinicians, and the organizational or environmental factors that either facilitate or impede coping effectiveness in day-to-day nursing practice.
To assemble an exhaustive evidence base, the investigators designed their scoping review in strict adherence to the Joanna Briggs Institute methodology and aligned their reporting with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for scoping reviews. The protocol for the inquiry was preregistered with the Joanna Briggs Institute collection, cataloged on the Open Science Framework, and previously published in an academic journal to maintain rigorous methodological transparency. The researchers formulated their eligibility parameters around the participant, concept, and context framework. Eligible participants were restricted to registered nurses possessing education equivalent to or exceeding an undergraduate degree in nursing, including advanced practice nurses and nurse practitioners. The examined concept centered on coping strategies deployed to address work-related stressors rather than personal or non-work difficulties. The clinical context encompassed acute and critical care settings globally, defining qualifying environments as emergency rooms, intensive care units, pediatric intensive care units, operating rooms, organ donation settings, and ambulance care services.
The review team executed comprehensive database searches on July 19, 2023, querying five major electronic repositories: MEDLINE, Embase, the Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library. The preliminary query was developed in MEDLINE using index terms, text elements, and keywords, after which the search architecture was tailored for each respective database. To identify unpublished grey literature, researchers also reviewed the first one hundred records retrieved through Google Scholar. Although searches were conducted without language limitations to ensure broad initial coverage, full-text inclusion was restricted to English, French, Spanish, and Portuguese, reflecting the linguistic competencies of the review team, while all publications dated prior to the year 2000 were systematically excluded. Initial electronic searches yielded 7,985 citations. After removing 184 duplicate records, 7,801 citations underwent primary screening of titles and abstracts. From this initial assessment, 567 references advanced to secondary full-text review, which ultimately yielded 168 relevant documents.
The 168 included documents spanned publication dates from 2003 through 2023, with a pronounced concentration of research emerging in recent years, as 77 studies representing 46 percent of the dataset were published between 2020 and 2023. Geographic distribution showed that the United States contributed the largest share with 56 reports accounting for 33 percent of the literature, followed by Iran with 18 reports or 11 percent, China with 12 reports or 7 percent, and Australia with 8 reports or 5 percent. The vast majority of documents were published in English, representing 163 studies or 97 percent, while Portuguese accounted for three studies and Spanish comprised two. Methodologically, 61 documents representing 36 percent were intervention-based studies, incorporating randomized controlled trials, nonrandomized quantitative designs, qualitative inquiries, descriptive quantitative surveys, and mixed methods. The remaining 107 observational studies relied predominantly on qualitative inquiries, quantitative descriptive surveys, and mixed methods. Investigated clinical settings most frequently featured intensive care units in 63 reports and emergency rooms in 43 reports.
Methodological quality across the included empirical literature was evaluated by two independent reviewers utilizing the Mixed-Method Appraisal Tool. This instrument assesses research quality across diverse methodologies, including randomized controlled trials, nonrandomized interventions, quantitative descriptive studies, qualitative investigations, and mixed methods research. Scores were calculated as percentages reflecting affirmative appraisal responses, ranging from 20 percent to 100 percent quality criteria met, with mixed methods publications evaluated across 15 criteria encompassing qualitative, quantitative, and integration perspectives. Following independent quality appraisals and consensus resolution, 66 included papers representing 39 percent achieved the maximum quality score. Although 102 studies representing 61 percent exhibited methodological inconsistencies on at least one appraisal item, the review team maintained an inclusive approach and retained all 168 identified documents in the final synthesis regardless of their individual appraisal score, determining that the relevant empirical findings warranted comprehensive integration.
Across the synthesized literature, 54 reports focused specifically on the identification and recognition of stressors encountered by registered nurses working in acute and critical care environments. The authors categorized these acute workplace strains into organizational, interpersonal, intrapersonal, and situational dimensions. In analyzing coping responses across 168 reports, the scoping review classified strategies into four distinct psychological and behavioural subcategories: problem-focused coping, emotion-focused coping, meaning-focused coping, and social-seeking coping. Sixty of these documents specifically detailed targeted interventions designed and implemented to assist acute care nurses in managing their immediate occupational stressors.
Problem-focused coping strategies, identified across 45 reports, encompass deliberate efforts aimed at addressing, modifying, or eliminating the underlying sources of workplace stress. Registered nurses frequently utilized planning, structured problem-solving, and active coping techniques to construct practical road maps for navigating clinical challenges. Clinicians also applied self-control, regulated their immediate physical work environments, established firm boundaries, and prioritized urgent patient tasks. In contrast, 78 reports described emotion-focused coping strategies intended to regulate the distressing feelings generated by high-stress clinical situations. These emotional responses included healthy mechanisms such as humour, deliberate distraction, and emotional venting, alongside avoidant techniques such as detachment, suppression, denial, and depersonalization. Several studies noted that nurses utilized cognitive distancing, compartmentalization, and withdrawal to endure critical trauma. Additionally, taking breaks, utilizing sick leave, stepping away through vacation, reducing work hours, or transferring units were documented, while several reports identified unhealthy coping strategies involving cigarette smoking, alcohol consumption, and medication use.
Meaning-focused coping strategies appeared across 120 reports, representing the most widely documented category within the scoping review. These strategies center on cognitive frameworks designed to cultivate personal purpose, coherence, and perspective amidst challenging workplace experiences. Registered nurses frequently utilized cognitive reframing, positive reappraisal, deliberate acceptance, and personal resilience to reinterpret acute clinical adversity. Self-care practices, formal educational training, and mindfulness activities such as guided meditation and controlled breathing exercises were prominent throughout the evidence base. Reflective practices, including personal journaling, self-talk, and recorded voice memos, allowed nurses to process demanding clinical events. Faith and spirituality constituted another recurring meaning-focused coping mechanism, with nurses drawing strength from religious convictions, personal prayer, spiritual connection, chaplain consultations, and spiritual reading. Outside clinical environments, nurses engaged in physical activities including sports, exercise, and yoga, as well as restorative hobbies such as listening to music, reading literature, traveling, and watching television.
Social-seeking coping strategies were documented in 86 reports, with general social support from family, personal friends, and loved ones representing the single most frequent technique, detailed across 55 publications. Peer support from clinical colleagues and cohesive unit teamwork provided essential emotional relief, reinforced by professional mentoring from senior nurses and administrative supervisors. Formal support avenues included professional counseling services and employee assistance programs. Clinicians also relied heavily on debriefing practices, distinguishing between informal discussions held immediately among colleagues following critical incidents and scheduled formal debriefings facilitated by nursing managers or hospital social workers.
Finally, 55 reports explored the external and structural influences governing the overall effectiveness of nurses’ coping mechanisms, identifying distinct facilitators and barriers within acute healthcare systems. Key facilitators enhancing coping capacity included accessible organizational resources, formal resilience training, and strong collegial cohesion. Conversely, severe workload pressures, understaffing, and absent supervisory support served as major barriers that impaired coping efficacy. The scoping review highlighted several methodological limitations, noting that the preponderance of English-language publications may introduce language bias, while reliance on published literature creates potential publication bias favoring statistically significant outcomes. Furthermore, the geographic concentration of studies in the United States, Iran, China, and Australia restricts global generalizability. The authors concluded that healthcare institutions must move beyond individual nurse adaptability by proactively reforming management practices, conducting early screenings for mental health strain, ensuring protected rest breaks, and implementing targeted organizational structures that sustainably mitigate clinical stress.
