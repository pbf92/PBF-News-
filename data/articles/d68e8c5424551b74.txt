Tesla Stock Tumbles: What's Going On?
Tesla Inc. TSLA shares are sliding Friday. A sweeping selloff in AI and growth stocks is weighing on the broader tech sector.
- Tesla stock is showing notable weakness. Why is TSLA stock dropping?
Tesla Stock Falls as AI And Growth Stocks Sell Off
A rout in tech-heavy stocks set the tone. The Nasdaq Composite fell 3.4%, CNBC said, which canceled its 2026 gains and left it 0.8% lower year to date. The S&P 500 slid 2%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 228 points, a 0.5% drop.
Both the S&P 500 and the Dow now sit more than 10% beneath their record closes, which qualifies as a correction.
Tesla may be especially exposed to that slide, since analysts value it heavily on future potential. HSBC, which upgraded the stock to Hold Friday, values Tesla across seven segments, including future-focused units such as Full Self Driving, Robotaxi, Dojo and Optimus, and says its cash flow model reflects the company’s long-term potential.
When investors pull back from AI and growth stocks, shares valued on that potential could come under pressure.
A fresh IPO adds to the strain. Anthropic PBC’s ANT shares have eased since their first-day pop, and some investors fear the debut is siphoning cash from other tech holdings. The company raised a record $81.3 billion on Nov. 19.
HSBC Upgrades Tesla to Hold
Against that backdrop, HSBC analyst Michael Tyndall lifted Tesla to Hold from Reduce and raised the price target to $359 from $157.
The target gives equal weight to two outcomes. In a takeover scenario, HSBC thinks Space Exploration Technologies Corp. SPCX would pay a premium, and adding 33% to Tesla’s 30-day average price yields $507 per share. In the other, Tesla trades on its fundamentals, with a fair value estimate of $212, up from $193.
Musk has called the requirement that 50% of Tesla shareholders approve a deal “quite a high hurdle,” and has said treating an agreement as certain would overstate it, since nothing assures shareholders would approve one.
HSBC also changed how it values the business. With acquisition headlines supporting today’s share price regardless of fundamentals, it dropped its blend of peer multiples and cash flow modeling in favor of a cash flow model alone.
HSBC reasons that, with a takeover in play, buyers would probably compare Tesla with peers less and weigh its long-term potential more.
