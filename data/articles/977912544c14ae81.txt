TITLE: Umbrella Meta-Analysis Clarifies Non-Drug Interventions for Adult Fatty Liver Disease
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive umbrella meta-analysis published in Frontiers in Nutrition has synthesized findings from thirty-four previously published meta-analyses to evaluate the efficacy and evidence certainty of non-pharmacological treatments in adults diagnosed with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Conducted by researchers Jinjin He, Li Ai, Guoai Liu, and Lihong Yang from Lincang People’s Hospital in Yunnan, China, the investigation systematically mapped twenty-three distinct non-pharmacological interventions across one hundred eighty-three intervention-outcome associations. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, which substantially overlaps with the newly established diagnostic nomenclature of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, currently represents the most prevalent chronic liver condition across the globe and places an escalating public health burden on modern healthcare systems. Pathologically defined by excess intrahepatic fat accumulation in the absence of substantial alcohol consumption, its clinical spectrum extends from simple steatosis to non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, progressive fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma. While the therapeutic agent resmetirom achieved accelerated regulatory approval from the United States Food and Drug Administration in 2024 for noncirrhotic steatohepatitis with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis stages F2 to F3, no approved pharmacological treatment spans the entirety of the clinical spectrum. Consequently, non-pharmacological lifestyle strategies remain the foundational standard of clinical management, making this systematic appraisal of evidence certainty across secondary syntheses critically timely.
The methodological framework of the umbrella review adhered strictly to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines, with prior prospective registration on the PROSPERO platform under the registration identifier CRD420261470715. Guided by synthesis principles established in the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions and the Joanna Briggs Institute Manual for Evidence Synthesis, the investigators implemented structured literature searches across PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from their respective database inceptions up through June 3, 2026. The initial identification retrieved two thousand two hundred sixty records, comprising three hundred thirty-six from PubMed, four hundred sixty-two from Web of Science, one thousand four hundred twenty-three from Embase, and thirty-nine from the Cochrane Library. Following the removal of six hundred twenty-one duplicate entries and the screening of one thousand five hundred sixty-two records during title review, seventy-seven full reports underwent detailed eligibility evaluation. Studies were restricted to adult populations diagnosed with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, while investigations exclusively applying metabolic dysfunction-associated criteria, animal studies, in vitro models, narrative overviews, and unsynthesized protocols were excluded. Ultimately, thirty-four systematic reviews featuring quantitative meta-analyses were selected for inclusion, and pooled effect estimates with corresponding ninety-five percent confidence intervals were extracted directly without recalculation from primary-study data.
To evaluate the structural overlap among primary studies included across the synthesis, the authors constructed an extensive citation matrix analyzing twenty-one of the meta-analyses. Across these twenty-one evaluations, the team identified two hundred eighteen unique primary studies and two hundred ninety-one primary-study occurrences, among which forty-seven primary trials appeared in more than a single meta-analysis. The overall corrected covered area was calculated at one point sixty-seven percent, which objectively represents a very low degree of overall study duplication within the broader literature base. However, pairwise corrected covered area calculations demonstrated that specific pairs of meta-analyses reached overlap values as high as forty-two point eleven percent, indicating substantial primary study duplication between particular topic-matched reviews. In parallel, the methodological quality of the included reviews was appraised using the eleven-item AMSTAR-2 instrument, yielding critical insights into the secondary literature. Among twenty-one reviews rigorously assessed via AMSTAR-2, four meta-analyses were categorized as possessing low overall confidence and seventeen were classified as critically low confidence, with zero reviews achieving a moderate or high methodological confidence rating. These scores highlight pervasive structural limitations in the design, conduct, and reporting of secondary evidence syntheses in this therapeutic domain.
Within the dietary modification category, multiple distinct nutritional protocols demonstrated statistically significant associations with surrogate markers of liver health and metabolic regulation. A meta-analysis by Del Bo and colleagues encompassing the Mediterranean diet identified a statistically significant reduction in circulating total cholesterol compared with control regimens, recording a mean difference of minus zero point forty-six with a ninety-five percent confidence interval of minus zero point fifty-five to minus zero point thirty-eight. Calorie restriction was evaluated in a meta-analysis by Haigh and colleagues, demonstrating statistically significant reductions across hepatic markers, including alanine aminotransferase with a mean difference of minus five point forty-four, the fatty liver index with a mean difference of minus seven point thirty-four, hepatic steatosis with a standardized mean difference of minus zero point seventy-two, and liver stiffness measurements with a standardized mean difference of minus zero point thirty-nine. Intermittent fasting protocols analyzed by Lange and colleagues demonstrated significant improvements across body weight, body mass index, triglycerides, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance, alanine aminotransferase, and aspartate aminotransferase, alongside reductions in hepatic steatosis with a mean difference of minus twenty-four point seventy-one and liver stiffness with a mean difference of minus zero point fifty-nine.
Additional dietary investigations analyzed the comparative and independent effects of dietary edible oils on anthropometric and biochemical parameters. A meta-analysis led by Gündüz and colleagues contrasted canola oil consumption against olive oil administration, detecting favorable shifts in liver transaminases and lipid profiles. Specifically, canola oil intake was associated with standardized mean difference reductions ranging between minus zero point forty-seven and minus zero point fifty-nine for alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, and circulating triglycerides. Under the statistical credibility grading framework, the association between canola oil and alanine aminotransferase was classified as Class IV weak evidence supported by moderate certainty under GRADE, whereas the associations with aspartate aminotransferase and triglycerides were categorized as low certainty. Complementing these comparative findings, Tsamol and colleagues documented that the dietary consumption of olive oil achieved a statistically significant reduction in adult body mass index compared with control groups, producing a mean difference of minus zero point fifty-seven with a ninety-five percent confidence interval ranging from minus one point zero eight to minus zero point zero six.
Exercise interventions constituted another heavily evaluated category, encompassing resistance training, high-intensity interval training, aerobic conditioning, and combined training formats. In a meta-analysis by Yu and colleagues examining resistance training, statistically significant favorable effects were identified across all assessed outcomes compared with controls, with reported mean differences ranging from zero point fourteen to zero point thirty-three and ninety-five percent confidence interval bounds between zero point zero one and zero point sixty. The same investigative team evaluated high-intensity interval training, observing statistically significant differences across all analyzed parameters with mean differences extending from zero point ninety-six to six point sixteen and confidence intervals spanning zero point twenty-one to eleven point forty-two. Aerobic exercise synthesized by Hejazi and colleagues yielded significant pooled reductions in alanine aminotransferase of minus zero point sixty-three and insulin levels of minus zero point fifty-five. Physical activity syntheses by Nam and colleagues revealed significant declines in intrahepatic fat content and alanine aminotransferase, with mean differences of minus two point zero three and minus four point seventeen, respectively. Combining aerobic and resistance training, Fu and colleagues reported significant reductions in body mass index, total body weight, alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, total cholesterol, triglycerides, visceral adipose tissue, intrahepatic fat content, blood glucose, and homeostatic insulin resistance.
Nutritional supplements and bioactive compounds were evaluated across several targeted meta-analyses, exhibiting selective effects on circulating biomarkers. Kim and colleagues established that omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid supplementation generated a statistically significant reduction in gamma-glutamyl transferase levels compared with controls, with a mean difference of minus five point thirty-eight and a ninety-five percent confidence interval of minus nine point sixteen to minus one point sixty-one. Supplementation with vitamin D, as synthesized by Guo and colleagues, produced significant improvements in fasting blood glucose with a standardized mean difference of minus zero point twenty-two, serum insulin with a standardized mean difference of minus zero point sixty-eight, and homeostatic model insulin resistance with a standardized mean difference of minus one point thirty-two. Combined curcumin and piperine supplementation assessed by Shahrebabak and colleagues demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in aspartate aminotransferase with a weighted mean difference of minus one point seventy-three. Furthermore, Nemati and colleagues reported that resveratrol supplementation yielded significant decreases in systolic blood pressure with a mean difference of minus one point eighty-two, alongside reductions in the circulating inflammatory cytokines interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha, with mean differences of minus one point thirty-seven and minus one point twenty-seven, respectively.
Investigations into whole-seed botanical extracts, digital health modalities, and complementary medicine therapies revealed additional surrogate improvements. Tiwari and colleagues analyzed Nigella sativa supplementation, identifying significant within-group standardized reductions in alanine aminotransferase of minus two point zero nine, aspartate aminotransferase of minus one point eighty-five, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol of minus zero point fifty-five, and triglycerides of minus zero point thirty-seven, alongside an increase in high-density lipoprotein cholesterol of zero point eighty-two. In digital therapeutics, Kwon and colleagues observed that eHealth interventions achieved significant standardized reductions in body weight, body mass index, alanine aminotransferase, and aspartate aminotransferase, while Kumar and colleagues found that mHealth tools significantly lowered body weight with a mean difference of minus one point eighty and aspartate aminotransferase with a mean difference of minus six point zero six.
