TITLE: The major A-list star set for billionaire status thanks to Anthropic IPO
OUTLET: Page Six

Ashton Kutcher could become one of the few actors to achieve billionaire status if AI giant Anthropic, the company behind AI assistant Claude, goes public today with a proposed $1.4 trillion IPO valuation.
The Hollywood actor, 48, would likely become a big winner from the business move thanks to his venture capital firm Sound Ventures’ $435 million investment in Anthropic – which is now worth about $7 billion, according to Newsweek.
It would be another major financial win for the “That ’70s Show” star, whose savvy investments in Airbnb, Uber, and Bitcoin have paid off big over the years.
While musicians such as Taylor Swift, Beyoncé, and Dr. Dre have become billionaires thanks to their music careers and business ventures, it is far less common for actors to achieve the same financial success.
Amid his flourishing acting career, he co-founded A-Grade Investments in 2010 with billionaire Ron Burkle and Guy Oseary, the longtime manager of stars such as Madonna and the Red Hot Chili Peppers.
The group built an impressive portfolio that included Spotify, Uber, and Airbnb, reportedly growing their investments from $30 million to about $250 million, per Newsweek.
Then in 2015, the “No Strings Attached” star and Oseary launched Sound Ventures as a successor to A-Grade Investments.
According to Bloomberg, the “Two and a Half Men” actor’s company owns 0.5 percent of Anthropic and could see a potential 1,500 percent increase when the AI giant goes public.

Kutcher wouldn’t instantly receive the $7 billion cash boost when Anthropic goes public because Sound Ventures manages money for outside investors, so the profits would be split among several parties.
As of 2026, other billionaires in the film industry include Tyler Perry, Jerry Seinfeld, and Arnold Schwarzenegger, according to Forbes.
Elsewhere, Kutcher stepped down from his partner role at Sound Ventures in July, but he remains a senior advisor.
According to the Wall Street Journal, “The Butterfly Effect” star’s choice to step down was partly due to different views on which startup stages to target for investments.
Sound Ventures reportedly wanted to back more established companies, while the actor prefers to focus on early-stage startups.
The former “Punk’d” host has since launched another venture capital fund in partnership with Morgan Beller – a co-leader at Meta’s Libra project.
Sound Ventures partners Oseary and Effie Epstein have signed on as advisers for the new firm.
