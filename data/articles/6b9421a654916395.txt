TITLE: Unofficial GTA 6 PC Ports Keep Advancing After Take-Two Clears Thousands of GitHub Repositories
OUTLET: TechEBlog -

Within days of GTA 6 reaching store shelves on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series machines, an unauthorized Xbox Series build began circulating through a torrent associated with the CyberLeek group. That dump, missing its main executable at first, quickly drew the attention of people skilled at translating console code into something that runs on Windows. Once the executable appeared, several recompilation efforts sprang up almost overnight, with developers using AI coding assistants to speed the conversion of shaders, assets, and system calls into forms a PC can understand.

One of the first and most notable of these initiatives was Leonida. On November 21, a 15-year-old coder known as NotRexed decided to get things started by launching the project on GitHub. He described it as a native PC version of the Xbox game, effectively a recompilation with players providing their own game data. That initiative immediately gained a large following, including thousands of forks, a giant Discord server, and the full shebang.

According to reports at the time, work had begun on shader code translation, with tens of thousands already translated, asset extraction was partially completed, and the team was even booting up into menus, albeit for a brief period. On November 24, the copyright enforcement team at Take Two was not having it. They went straight to the source, filing a DMCA notice with GitHub, and before you knew it, the entire business had been shut down. The original repository, 2900 forks, and connected Discord server were all erased completely. Three days later, the original project vanished.
This did not slow down other teams working on related projects. Victory and XCL, which are basically compatibility layers that transform Xbox instructions into native Windows code, remained active on GitHub. The individuals in charge say they obtain a decent 30 frames per second in the menus, a fair 50 seconds to load the globe in some testing, and a frame rate of 1 or 2 when the scene is really drawn on screen.
CGBJ, a private project built on the work done on Victory and XCL, appears to have made significant progress. Community trackers have measured it at around 25 frames per second during active gameplay and 30 frames per second during cutscenes, implying that approximately 80% of the tale is playable. The trouble is, independent verification is still quite restricted, and the graphics, well, let’s just say they’re a little rough around the edges, with missing lighting here and unfinished texturing there, and don’t even get me started on the instability they’ve been experiencing in public demos.
In the meantime, a simple mod menu has arrived, complete with a console reminiscent of the old Grand Theft Auto tools. One of the demos even showcased a simple launcher. Even with high-end technology, the frame rate dropped into the single digits. Some of the most ambitious designs require 8 GB of system memory and a graphics card with 16 GB of video RAM to provide a smooth experience. Who knows, that may change whenever the developers are able to do any meaningful optimization. Community trackers such as PortWatch VI are monitoring over 30 individual activities, the most of which are still a long way from being completed ports.

As for the official PC port, Rockstar has yet to announce a release date. Industry insiders suggest they may have a window in the first quarter of 2027, but this is far from certain. Meanwhile, these unofficial projects continue to progress, despite the continual threat of DMCA notices, with AI tools playing a significant role in speeding up the process.
