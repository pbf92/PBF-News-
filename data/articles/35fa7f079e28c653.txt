TITLE: Senacon demands answers from Google and investigates Meta for flaws in its ad libraries.
OUTLET: BNLData

Senacon demands answers from Google and investigates Meta for flaws in its ad libraries.
Brazil's National Consumer Secretariat (Senacon), part of the Ministry of Justice and Public Security, determined on Wednesday (November 25th) that Google must expand public information about advertisements displayed in Brazil. The company has ten days to comply with Brazilian rules on digital advertising transparency. Failure to comply could result in a daily fine of R$ 200,000, according to Metrópoles , which revealed the decisions.
On the same day, Senacon opened a preliminary investigation against Meta, owner of Facebook and Instagram, due to indications of non-compliance with the same rules. Both decisions are based on a survey by the National Secretariat for Digital Rights (Sedigi). According to the government, these flaws make it difficult to know who pays for the ads, how many people receive them, and which websites users are directed to.
The government cites the monitoring of illegal betting advertising as an example. The technical note that supports the measures states that boosted advertising can be the main gateway for consumers to access illegal operators. According to the experts, advertising is a tool for attracting users and sustaining the economic viability of the illegal fixed-odds betting market.
What is missing in libraries?
Google and Meta already maintain public pages for viewing ads. According to the Ministry of Justice, neither of them meets all the requirements. The charges levied on each company are different:
In the case of Google, the order also extends to YouTube. Senacon demands that data be searchable and filterable by all available fields, not just by the advertiser's name. The argument is simple: those who want to find out who is responsible for an advertisement often don't know who commissioned it.
This limitation is not new to the government. In a 2024 technical note, in a case against Facebook, Senacon itself already noted that Google's transparency report only allowed searches by advertiser, and that only Meta's library was searchable and navigable in Brazil ( Technical Note No. 25/2024 ).
Meta discloses the reach of ads in the European market, where the Digital Services Act (DSA) applies, but does not offer the same data for the Brazilian audience. According to the ruling, this information is only available for ads displayed in Europe, even though Brazilian law also requires it.
Decree and ordinance
The charges are part of the government's package to track scams in paid advertising. There are two regulations:
⇒ Decree 12.975/2026. Lula signed the decree on May 20th, updating the regulations of the Brazilian Internet Bill of Rights. It came into effect on July 20th. According to the text, platforms that sell advertisements must maintain information that allows the identification of those responsible for the campaigns.
⇒ Joint Ordinance Senacon/Sedigi/SPDigi No. 3/2026. Published on September 23, the ordinance detailed these obligations. It defined the data that ad libraries must display and mandated that some records be kept for at least one year after the end of the campaign, even when the ad is removed. The requirement applies to platforms with more than 1 million monthly active users in Brazil and has been in effect since October 23.
At the time, the Ministry of Justice defended the rules as necessary to track, among other frauds, advertisements that mimic government programs. A month before today's decisions, Meta, Google, and TikTok had still not explained how they would implement the ordinance or when the public repository would go live.
Illegal betting sites continued advertising after the Provisional Measure.
Gambling advertising has been the focus of scrutiny since Provisional Measure 1.394, issued on September 25. The measure gave ten days, until October 5, to remove advertisements already in circulation and prohibited new advertisements from the date of its publication.
⇒ Notifications. The Ministry of Justice notified eight platforms to remove betting advertisements within ten days: Google, Meta, YouTube, X, Telegram, TikTok, Kwai, and Discord. Failure to comply could result in administrative proceedings. In the same operation, 63 cases were opened at Senacon (National Consumer Secretariat).
⇒ Monitoring. A report from the Cyber Operations Laboratory (Ciberlab) of Senasp found 1,9 betting ads on Google. Meta documented seven, two of which were published after the Provisional Measure came into effect.
⇒ Traceability. The same report noted that some ads used page names different from the displayed brands and links with domains that did not match them. This is exactly the type of operation that an incomplete ad library helps to hide.
At the time, Meta and Google stated that they do not allow illegal ads and that they take steps to remove them. As of the publication of this report, the companies had not commented on Senacon's decisions.
Analysis: monitoring without tools
The decisions made by Senacon (National Consumer Secretariat) contain an implicit confession. Two months after banning betting, the government admits that it cannot clearly see who is advertising illegal bets, to how many people, and where it leads them. The technical note itself describes the advertising as an economic cog in the illegal market.
In the regulated market, operators had a CNPJ (Brazilian tax ID), authorization from SPA (Brazilian Advertising Self-Regulation Service), and advertising rules to comply with. With the end of that market, oversight now depends on advertising libraries that the government itself considers incomplete. Hunting down anonymous advertisers in a tool that only searches by advertiser name is like trying to bail water with a sieve.
The demand for betting did not disappear with Provisional Measure 1.394 and migrated to those without an address, CNPJ (Brazilian tax ID), or commitment to the bettor. More transparency on the platforms is welcome. But it does not replace the control that the country had over identified and authorized operators.
