TITLE: Google Maps puts grocery crowds at 2 p.m. the weekend before Thanksgiving
OUTLET: PPC Land

Google today published its annual Thanksgiving guide to traffic and crowds in Google Maps, naming around 2 p.m. on the Saturday and Sunday before the holiday as the peak for grocery store crowds and 11 a.m. to 3 p.m. as the heaviest stretch on the roads on Black Friday.
In Short
Google today put out a list of the busiest and quietest hours to drive and to shop in the US over Thanksgiving week, based on what it calls Google Maps trends. The hours matter to grocers, bakeries, cinemas and any business buying local or store-visit advertising, because they mark when shoppers are in their cars and where queues form. The post gives times but no volumes, no method and no years of data, so the windows describe a pattern rather than measure one.
A short post with a seasonal byline
The post is titled "Navigate holiday traffic and crowds with Google Maps trends" and sits on Google's Keyword blog under the Maps and Travel categories, marked as a three-minute read. Its byline belongs to Holly N. Jolly, "Chief Sleigh Logistics Officer", a seasonal pen name that also signed Google's Maps holiday posts in November 2024 and November 2025. This article attributes the post's contents to Google.
There are three parts. The first, headed "Beat the holiday traffic", lists the best and worst times to start a drive across Thanksgiving week. The second, "Dodge the crowds", covers three kinds of local business. The third, "Hit up top holiday spots", names five destinations. Each section comes with an illustrated card that repeats its contents in a slightly different format, and the card and text versions match.
What the post does not contain matters as much. There is not a single percentage in it, and no traffic volume, sample size, year range or city list. The body refers only to "Google Maps trends". The post closes by pointing readers to a separate Google post on new Ask Maps features for holiday travel and preparation.
Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, November 26, 2026. Matched against that date, the windows in the post run from Friday, November 20 to Sunday, November 29. The calendar dates in the tables below are PPC Land's mapping of the weekday names Google uses; the post itself gives no dates.
The road windows
Google's driving data starts a week before the holiday. According to the post, the Friday before Thanksgiving is a day to watch, with "traffic peaks between 3 p.m. and 5 p.m." The best options for long-distance travel come earlier in the week: an early departure on Sunday or Monday, or Tuesday before 1 p.m. and after 6 p.m., or Wednesday before 9 a.m. or after 6 p.m.
The worst windows sit on either side of those. On Tuesday, according to Google, the bad stretch runs from 3 p.m. to 5 p.m. On Wednesday it begins three hours earlier, at noon, and lasts until 3 p.m.
Thanksgiving Day itself is the quiet point of the week. "Roads are generally quiet on Thanksgiving Day, particularly after 12 p.m.," the post states. Black Friday is not. "Expect the heaviest traffic between 11 a.m. and 3 p.m.," according to Google, a four-hour block that covers the middle of the trading day for physical stores. For the trip home, the post gives Sunday before noon or after 3 p.m. as the times to start.
Two of those rows rest on PPC Land's reading of the post. The text says "Sunday or Monday" without saying which week, but the card places both days under its heading for the best times to start a drive to Thanksgiving, so the table treats them as November 22 and 23. The return-trip Sunday is read as November 29, the Sunday after the holiday. The post does not say whether the hours are local time, although its five destinations run from California to New York, across three time zones.
Store crowds: grocery, bakery, cinema
The second section is the one that concerns physical retail. It covers just three types of place: grocery stores, bakeries and movie theaters.
For grocery stores, according to Google, "Crowds peak the weekend before Thanksgiving at around 2 p.m. on Saturday and Sunday." The quieter time is before 4 p.m. from Monday through Wednesday of Thanksgiving week. For bakeries, the post puts the rush at 11 a.m. on the Saturday before Thanksgiving, with the quieter window before noon from Monday through Wednesday. Cinemas follow a different rhythm. "The busiest showtimes fall around 7 p.m. on the Saturdays before and after Thanksgiving," according to the post, while Black Friday before 5 p.m. is described as quieter.
Read together, the table points to one date. Saturday, November 21, is a listed peak for all three place types: bakeries at 11 a.m., grocery stores at around 2 p.m. and cinemas at around 7 p.m. That is five days before the holiday meal, not the day before it. Black Friday produces a contrast of its own. The hours Google gives as the heaviest on the roads, 11 a.m. to 3 p.m., overlap with the hours it describes as quieter for cinemas.
The scope is narrow. Malls, electronics stores and post offices, which featured in some of Google's earlier holiday posts, are absent this year. So is any guidance for the weeks between Thanksgiving and Christmas.
Five places drawing searches
The third section is a list. According to the post, "These are some of the highly-rated destinations that see the biggest spikes in interest across the country this time of year." Each name in the post links to its Google Maps place listing.
Two of the five are in New York City. The list is not ranked, and the post does not define "interest". It could mean searches, direction requests, saves or visits; Google does not say. Nor does it give the size of any spike or the baseline it was measured against.
How this year's windows compare with earlier posts
Google has published a Maps holiday trends post in November for several years, and the last three show how much the advertised windows move. The 2023 edition, signed by Madison Gouveia as Santa's Helper and published on November 15, 2023, said it drew on the previous year's Thanksgiving traffic patterns across more than 20 major US cities, plus Popular Times information. The 2024 edition, published November 14, 2024, cited traffic predictions and popular times data. The 2025 edition, published November 6, 2025, referred to Google Maps trends and to busyness at popular malls in each state. It was also the only one of the four to put a number on congestion: traffic 14% above usual between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving.
Sources: Google's posts of November 15, 2023, November 14, 2024, November 6, 2025 and today. "Not in post text" means the post's written body gives no time for that item; some earlier posts carried further figures inside images.
Some shifts stand out. The Black Friday peak that Google gave as 11 a.m. to 2 p.m. in 2024 now runs to 3 p.m. The Friday before Thanksgiving, busiest between 1 p.m. and 2 p.m. in the 2024 post, now peaks between 3 p.m. and 5 p.m. The Saturday bakery peak, given as 10-11 a.m. in the 2023 post, is 11 a.m. in this one. Whether any of that reflects changed behaviour is impossible to tell from the posts. Google has described a different basis almost every year and, this year, none at all.
How Google says its busyness figures are built
Today's post does not name its data source. The two earlier posts that did, in 2023 and 2024, cited popular times data, the hour-by-hour busyness charts shown on place listings in Google Maps. Google described how that feature works in a post titled "Behind the scenes: popular times and live busyness information", written by Matt D'Zmura, a software engineer on Google Maps, and published on October 15, 2020. According to that description, the process has five parts.
- Google analyses aggregated and anonymised Location History data from people who have chosen to turn that setting on in their Google Account.
- From that data it estimates how busy a place typically is for each hour of the week.
- The busiest hour serves as the benchmark, and every other hour is shown relative to it, with text labels describing how busy that hour usually is compared with the peak.
- Live busyness uses the same data in real time to flag when a place is busier or quieter than its usual pattern; Google's example was a promotion that doubled a shop's usual Tuesday customers.
- Differential privacy is applied, and when there is not enough information for an accurate, anonymous estimate, Google does not publish one, which is why some places show no busyness data.
The 2020 post also said that, during the pandemic, Google began favouring data from the previous four to six weeks. It gave no standard window for normal periods. Whether that description still matches how Maps works in 2026, Google has not said.
The relative scale in step three may explain the format of today's post. Popular times express busyness against a place's own peak, not as a count of people. If the 2026 figures come from the same system, a list of hours rather than volumes is what that kind of data produces.
Why the hours matter to retailers and local advertisers
Why should a consumer guide with no numbers interest advertisers? Because the hours describe when people are physically moving between home, roads and shops, and a growing share of local advertising is sold against exactly that movement.
The money involved in the season is large. Adobe forecast US online holiday spending of $275.1 billion for November 1 to December 31, 2026, up 6.7%, with Black Friday at $12.9 billion and Cyber Monday at $15.1 billion. Those are e-commerce figures. Google's Maps post covers the other channel: trips to physical stores in the days that lead into the Cyber Week stretch. For grocers, those trips compete with delivery offers such as Amazon's $25 Thanksgiving meal, which comes with free same-day delivery on orders of $25 or more in more than 2,300 US towns, while supplies last, through November 26.
Ad inventory tied to maps and local results has grown quickly. Places Scout data showed ads in the local pack on 21.99% of tracked mobile keywords in January 2026, up from under 3% in early November 2025, an increase of 733% in three months. In November 2025, Google added Waze inventory to Performance Max for store goals campaigns in the US, showing ads as "Promoted Places in Navigation" pins. In September 2026, Waze ads reached 41 more countries, including all 27 EU member states, for 42 in total. Apple has moved into the same space: Apple Maps ads began serving in the US and Canada on August 21, 2026.
Store-visit campaigns are judged on footfall attribution, which links ad exposure to later visits to a location. Today's post does not mention advertising at all, and Google does not say whether the signals behind its busyness trends play any role on the advertising side.
The scheduling tools that advertisers use also work in days and hours. In late October, Google Ads asset scheduling was spotted with three day ranges, All days, Mondays - Fridays and Saturdays - Sundays, plus multiple schedules per asset; Google has not formally announced the change. The grocery peak in today's post falls on a Saturday and a Sunday, and the quieter grocery window on weekdays, which maps directly onto those ranges. That is a description of fit, not evidence that any schedule changes results; Google has published no data linking its busyness trends to ad performance.
Google is also pulling more of the local decision into AI products built on the same place data. Google's Business Profile listings now feed AI, Maps and Search, with Ask Maps, the Gemini-powered conversational search in Maps, drawing on data from 300 million places. A business's Google Business Profile is part of the place data those answers draw on. In August, Ask Maps added food ordering through Square and Toast, and on November 12 a sponsored pack of local retailers was spotted inside AI Mode answers.
