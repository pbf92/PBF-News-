TITLE: Manchester United set to miss out on defender in January due to previous issues
OUTLET: Yahoo Sports

Man United Face Costly Consequences of Nathan Collins Transfer Mistake
Manchester United's predicament in the Premier League has lent an uncomfortable urgency to their January transfer plans. Mauricio Pochettino's side sit 19th following Sunday's humiliating 4-0 defeat to Liverpool, possessing the worst defensive record across Europe's five major leagues.
Reinforcements are desperately required. According to The Mirror, Brentford captain Nathan Collins features prominently among United's defensive targets, alongside Calvin Bassey, Jarrad Branthwaite and Malick Thiaw.
There is something particularly galling about United's interest in Collins. Five years ago, the club had an opportunity to pursue the Republic of Ireland international for a relatively modest £15 million.
Nathan Collins Represents Missed Opportunity
Darren Fletcher reportedly championed Collins during his time at Stoke City, having witnessed the defender's development at close quarters towards the end of his playing career.
Photo: IMAGO
United failed to capitalise. Collins subsequently joined Burnley, moved to Wolves for £20.5 million and eventually arrived at Brentford in a club-record £23 million deal.
Brentford Captain Commands Premium Valuation
At 25, Collins has accumulated 154 Premier League appearances, including 124 appearances for Brentford across all competitions. He captains both club and country, with his contract running until June 2029.
Brentford manager Keith Andrews highlighted his development in September.
"I think we've seen it with Nathan now," the Brentford manager said in September. "He's growing all the time, he's developing, his leadership skills for someone who's so young, he keeps making strides. He leads by example on a daily basis and he's so eager to develop his individual game, and I think that's in a really good place."
Those leadership qualities would appear particularly valuable at Old Trafford, where confidence and defensive cohesion have deteriorated alarmingly.
January Transfer Budget Complicates United's Search
United reportedly have less than £50 million available for January recruitment, presenting Pochettino with a considerable challenge.
Brentford, currently sixth following their 4-0 victory over Everton, have little incentive to negotiate the departure of their captain. Similar complications surround Bassey, Branthwaite and Thiaw.
United's recruitment history has frequently involved expensive attempts to address problems that might have been anticipated years earlier. Collins provides another compelling example.
Identifying emerging talent, trusting internal recommendations and acting decisively remain fundamental responsibilities for any successful recruitment department. United's failure to pursue Collins in 2021 could now prove particularly expensive.
Our View – EPL Index Analysis
As a Manchester United supporter, it is difficult to know quite how to feel about this report. Nathan Collins has developed into an accomplished Premier League defender, and his leadership qualities would certainly appeal given the current state of affairs at Old Trafford.
However, spending potentially our entire January budget on one central defender feels like an enormous gamble. Sunday's defeat at Anfield exposed deficiencies throughout the team, from midfield protection to defensive organisation and individual decision-making.
The Darren Fletcher revelation is particularly frustrating. How many times have we heard similar stories about players United could have signed before their valuations became prohibitive? There appears to be a recurring problem with translating sound scouting recommendations into decisive recruitment.
Collins would presumably improve our defensive options, although there must also be questions about whether United represent an attractive proposition for him right now. He captains a thriving Brentford side and enjoys considerable stability.
Pochettino needs reinforcements, and our league position makes January recruitment absolutely essential. My concern is that desperation could once again dictate our spending.
There needs to be evidence of a coherent recruitment strategy. Another expensive signing, made under considerable pressure, could leave United facing familiar problems further down the line.
