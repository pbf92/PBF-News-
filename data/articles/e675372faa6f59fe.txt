TITLE: Soros Fund Management's 596% Bet On Meta Platforms (NASDAQ: META) Is Already Underwater
OUTLET: Foreign Policy Journal

A quarterly filing is, by definition, old news, and for Soros Fund Management, the gap between record date and reality is costing the firm on paper.
The family office run by George Soros expanded its position in Meta Platforms, Inc. (NASDAQ: META) by 596% in the three months ending September 30, one of the largest proportional additions across its entire portfolio.
Since that quarter closed, META shares have shed roughly 15%, meaning the disclosed stake is worth considerably less than the $109,868,396 recorded on the last day of September.
That stake represented 1.29% of Soros Fund Management’s total disclosed holdings, placing META seventh among its common stock positions by value at quarter-end.
The broader portfolio, filed in mid-November, lists 263 separate positions with a combined value of approximately $8.51 billion, though its construction is far from conventional.
Two of the three largest disclosed positions are put options on energy index funds rather than outright equity holdings, and a further $1.17 billion sits in convertible notes.
Common stock accounts for just under two-thirds of the total, and even within that slice, the holdings split into two camps that share almost no industry overlap: AI-linked technology companies and regulated electric utilities.
The logic connecting them is the same infrastructure demand: the chips powering large language models require electricity, and utilities collect regulated returns regardless of whether any specific model succeeds commercially.
Soros, who was born in Budapest in 1930 and built his name and his fortune through the Quantum Fund, converted the firm into a family office after returning outside capital, freeing it from the reporting pressures that constrain conventional hedge funds.
Institutional interest in META is not confined to Soros: 301 hedge funds held the stock at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up from 254 just three months prior, according to data from Insider Monkey.
The bullish case for META rests on how its advertising business actually generates profit, with advertisers bidding against each other in a real-time auction for access to a specific user’s attention.
Because serving an additional advertisement costs almost nothing at the margin, improvements to targeting flow almost entirely to the bottom line, producing an operating margin of 37.78%.
Artificial intelligence accelerates that dynamic by improving the ranking and relevance of each impression without requiring the platform to acquire a single new user.
Revenue grew 26.10% year-on-year during the reported quarter, while earnings climbed 264%, though the latter figure reflects a favorable comparison period as much as underlying momentum.
At 17.94 times expected earnings, META trades at a discount to the broader market multiple, which is an unusual position for a platform generating returns at this scale.
The counterargument centers on capital allocation: the company is committing vast sums to data centers and model training, with those costs landing on the income statement well before any associated revenue materializes.
Unresolved regulatory pressure across multiple jurisdictions over how the company collects and uses targeting data adds a layer of legal and operational uncertainty that the valuation does not fully price in.
The filing itself carries a structural caveat that applies with unusual force this quarter: technology holdings across the Soros portfolio have largely declined since September 30, while utilities and non-technology positions have broadly gained.
For a firm that can hold positions indefinitely without answering to outside investors, a 15% drawdown on a newly enlarged stake is an unrealized loss rather than a forced decision, but it illustrates the risks of reading quarterly disclosures as forward-looking endorsements.
