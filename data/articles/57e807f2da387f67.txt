TITLE: El Elyon / God Most High
OUTLET: Christianity Today

Something in us wants to ascend. It isn’t wrong to want to be exalted. We were made to rule (Gen. 1:26–28). But sin begins when creatures reach for a height that belongs only to God.
That is why the name El Elyon matters. It means “God Most High.” It is a name of supremacy. God is not merely high, he is the highest—the Creator of all. He is not one power among many, one ruler among other rulers, or one being competing for the throne. He is above all.
Some lesser beings desire a height that belongs only to the Most High. Isaiah 14 gives voice to this ancient pride: “‘I will ascend to the heavens; I will raise my throne above the stars of God … I will make myself like the Most High’” (vv. 13–14). This is the heart of rebellion: seeking to take God’s place.
When Satan tempted Jesus, he showed Jesus “all the kingdoms of the world” and said, “‘All this I will give you … if you will bow down and worship me’” (Matt. 4:8–9). In exchange, he wanted the Son of God to bow before him. But that act would have elevated Satan to be the most high.
Jesus refused. “‘Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only’” (Matt. 4:10). The Son would inherit the nations but not by worshiping a false most high. He would reclaim them by becoming low.
This is the wonder of Christmas: The Most High became the lowest.
The one exalted above all gods was laid in a manger. He was rich, but he became poor. The Lord of glory wrapped a towel around his waist and washed filth from his disciples’ feet (John 13). The eternal Son was despised and rejected, pierced for our transgressions, and crushed for our iniquities (Isa. 53:3, 5). “He humbled himself by becoming obedient to death—even death on a cross” (Phil. 2:8).
He did this so that we might become like him again. “Now we are children of God,” John writes, “and what we will be has not yet been made known. But we know that when Christ appears, we shall be like him” (1 John 3:2).
Children resemble their families. They learn the family likeness. They grow into the image they were made to bear. And Jesus shows us what that likeness looks like. He stooped. He descended. He did not grasp onto status. He stepped into a world of pain and willingly took it upon himself. He became poor so we could become rich. He became low so we could be lifted up. He became like us so that we might become like him again.
This Advent season, here is what I remember: I do not have to spend my life climbing. I do not have to grasp for control, defend my little kingdom, or prove my worth by positioning myself above someone else. El Elyon is God Most High. That throne is eternally occupied.
