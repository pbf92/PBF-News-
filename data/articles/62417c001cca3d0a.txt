SpaceX Stocks Climb 2.74% as FCC Clears One Million AI Satellites
SpaceX SPCX shares climbed 2.74% to $130.94 at 10:35 a.m. ET on Friday, November 27, extending a rebound after the Federal Communications Commission authorized up to one million Starmind AI data-center satellites. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr announced the approval on November 25.
Starmind is SpaceX's bet that AI compute can move into orbit, with the first launch targeted for the third quarter of 2027 on Starship. The shares still trade below the $135 IPO price from June and far from the first-week peak of $225.64, according to Finexus, which also reported Elon Musk's pledge to give one Class A share a year to each American child with a Trump Account. Anthropic's prospectus puts its potential monthly AI bill with SpaceX at about $3.1 billion, 24/7 Wall St. reported on Friday.
The GuruFocus chart shows a GF Score of 21/100, with very weak profitability the main drag, which is typical for a company spending ahead of revenue. That makes the $1.78 trillion valuation a bet on new lines of business rather than current earnings. If Anthropic's bill reached $3.1 billion a month, it would equal about $37 billion a year from one customer, a concrete anchor for the orbital compute story. The next test is Starship's launch cadence through 2027, since every Starmind satellite depends on it.
