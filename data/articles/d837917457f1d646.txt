TITLE: Sporting 2 Man Utd 2: Lammens horror show costs Pochettino first win in rollercoaster clash after TWO howlers
OUTLET: The Sun

SENNE LAMMENS was likened to Peter Schmeichel on his Manchester United debut but is now resembling Andre Onana.
With United in dire need of stability under Mauricio Pochettino, they have an unstable and error-prone goalkeeper who is starting to cost them.
This was another chaotic night in the Champions League for United where there were three penalties, goalkeeping howlers, two disallowed goals and a bizarre recreation of Stan Collymore bamboozling Tim Flowers at Ewood Park.
That was not even the one Lammens conceded. But the dolly he dropped that gifted Luis Suarez an 80th-minute equaliser will fuel doubts about his long-term credentials at United.
A first win for Mauricio Pochettino and first Champions League away victory in five years eluded United. Howard Webb’s equivalent at Uefa may now be in line for a rollocking.
The dubious decision to deny Amad a first-half equaliser will only fuel United’s agenda with officials domestic and overseas after their midweek attack on ProRefs chief Webb.

So too will the late call to chalk off goalkeeper Kaique Pareira’s own goal. Harry Maguire challenged rumbustiously but it was debatable as to whether it was a clear foul.
Bruno Fernandes and Cristiano Ronaldo graced the pre-match banner that Sporting supporters unfurled. Fernandes almost emulated his compatriot.
United’s previous tie at Sporting in 2007 was settled by the returning Ronaldo. Fernandes, the only member of the current squad deserving of mention in the same breath as Ronaldo, also put them ahead.
This was a captain’s performance on his return to the Jose Alvalade Stadium. The 32-year-old received a commemorative frame on his homecoming from Sporting president Federico Varandas. He was not in as generous a mood after kick-off.
Even under a new manager, United remain the club where anything that can go wrong will still go wrong. Goalkeeper Senne Lammens conceded a penalty 71 seconds after saving one in the first half as he faced two spotkicks 155 seconds apart.
The tune has already changed among the matchgoers, though. Some of the 2,650 travelling supporters shouted, “Bring on United”, a bullish chant that has not been heard all season.
Lisbon lionised United in the past, and their followers aired tributes to Ronaldo and George Best on the 21st anniversary of his death. By the end of the night, it was the seasonal “Five Cantonas”.
United scrapped their pre-match huddle in the final weeks of Michael Carrick’s reign but reintroduced it in Lisbon. Emboldened by the FA overturning Leny Yoro’s red card, this was a chance to wipe the slate clean in Pochettino’s second match on the touchline.
Yoro was again entrusted at centre back but this time was partnered by the more able Harry Maguire while Lisandro Martinez continued at left back. United managed to get through the first 30 minutes without conceding for the first time in four games.
In the absence of Matheus Cunha, Joshua Zirkze made his first United start in 200 days and his second of the calendar year.
United had an uncharacteristically steadier start until Fernandes’ miscued header was slashed into the crowd by Maximiliano Araujo in the 12th minute.
Zirkzee quickly learnt his lesson to stay onside and cushion Yoro’s visionary pass for a first attempt at goal on 16 minutes.
Pochettino demanded on the eve of the match that United be “compact and narrow” without the ball. Youri Tielemans and Harry Maguire took issue with Noussair Mazraoui and Bryan Mbeumo, moved to the left wing, failing to get up to speed to neuter Sporting attacks.
With Martinez increasingly isolated, he lost his nerve and kicked the tricky Geny Catamo inside the area. Catamo’s tumble was delayed but there was contact.
Martinez was bailed out by Lammens, who gathered Luis Suarez’s pitiful penalty low to his left in the 30th minute. A minute later, United fans were lamenting Lammens.
The Belgian is distinctly uncomfortable when called upon to leave his goal line and his delayed rush ensured he took all of Araujo and none of the ball.
Suarez stepped up again and stroked the ball accurately into the opposite corner.
With chaos back on the agenda, United unwittingly added to it when Amad’s goal was chalked off for a foul by Tielemans on Gonçalo Inácio.
Tielemans undeniably played the ball and followed through innocently, yet was penalised and remarkably booked by 2022 World Cup final referee Szymon Marciniak.
Three seconds after Tielemans’ tackle, Marciniak blew his whistle just as Amad went to head the ball into the goal his initial effort had rebounded off the crossbar.
That ensured that the VAR could not intervene and potentially allow the goal to stand. Tielemans continued to protest to Marciniak as they approached the tunnel at the interval, his body language becoming increasingly animated as he risked a second yellow.
Marciniak was no doubt relieved that Eduardo Quaresma clumsily upended the marauding Martinez inside two minutes into the second half. Fernandes nervelessly converted.
The United skipper was reluctant to say whether he would celebrate scoring. His second goal was so surreal it did not warrant one.
Fernandes received the ball outside the penalty area, turned and his left-footed pot-shot bounced off a divot eight yards out and flew over goalkeeper Kaique Pareira. The ball bounced as wickedly as a leg-spinner finding a crack at a batsman’s crease.
United were seeing out the game professionally until Lammens’ late clanger. They responded almost immediately, only for Marciniak to be summoned to the pitchside monitor by the VAR and deny them another goal.
His speculative shot from outside the box changed direction after going over the damaged bit of turf and bamboozling Sporting stopper Rui Silva.
That looked to be like the bit of good luck the Red Devils had been searching for in this torrid season.
But Lammens’ calamitous attempt to cut out a looping cross in the 80th minute gave Suarez the chance to double his tally for the night, which he gleefully gobbled up.
