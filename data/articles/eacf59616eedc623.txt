TITLE: Three-Dimensional Organoid Models Advance Intracranial Tumour Research and Personalised Therapy Testing
OUTLET: Bioengineer.org

A comprehensive narrative review evaluating the preclinical landscape of intracranial tumour research demonstrates that three-dimensional brain tumour organoids offer substantial advantages over conventional two-dimensional monolayers and animal models by faithfully preserving tumour-specific genomic alterations, cellular diversity, and spatial architecture. Intracranial tumours represent a highly heterogeneous group of neoplasms originating within the cranial cavity, including gliomas, embryonal tumours such as medulloblastoma, meningiomas, craniopharyngiomas, and pituitary neuroendocrine tumours. These malignancies constitute a disproportionate burden of cancer-related morbidity and mortality relative to their incidence, driven by frequent recurrence and limited clinical responses. Traditional two-dimensional cell cultures frequently undergo genetic drift and loss of cellular heterogeneity during extended passaging, whereas xenografts and genetically engineered mouse models face interspecies biological discordance and constrained mutational diversity. Brain tumour organoids, derived from patient resections or genetically engineered from pluripotent stem cells, bridge these longstanding translational gaps by providing accessible in vitro platforms that maintain patient-specific molecular landscapes and therapeutic response profiles.
The methodological framework of the review synthesised evidence across 102 eligible studies selected from an initial pool of 2,297 records retrieved from PubMed, Scopus, Embase, and the Cochrane Library. Screened according to the Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Reviews, the literature established that patient-derived organoids retain both the genotype and phenotype of parental neoplasms across distinct tumour types. Investigators derive these models by mechanically or enzymatically dissociating surgical biopsies or resections into single-cell suspensions or tissue fragments, which are subsequently embedded within extracellular matrix hydrogel domes and cultivated in specialized media. Complementing patient-derived tissues, researchers employ CRISPR-Cas9 genome editing in non-malignant induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cerebral organoids to introduce precise oncogenic mutations, such as alterations in TP53, PTEN, and EGFR. These engineered systems, including neoplastic cerebral organoids and engineered glioblastoma organoids, directly illuminate how targeted mutations initiate tumorigenesis, drive sustained cell proliferation, and induce tissue invasion within structured human neural microenvironments.
In glioma research, patient-derived glioblastoma organoids maintain hallmark genomic features with high fidelity, demonstrating strong concordance with matched surgical specimens across whole-exome sequencing and copy-number profiling. Documented alterations preserved in organoids include EGFR amplification, EGFRvIII rearrangements, PTEN loss, TP53 mutations, homozygous CDKN2A/B deletions, and concurrent chromosome 7 gain and chromosome 10 loss. Furthermore, organoids derived from lower-grade gliomas reliably retain IDH1 and IDH2 mutations, ATRX loss, 1p/19q codeletions, and the associated CpG island methylator phenotype, all of which are rapidly lost in monolayer cultures. Even when generated from small stereotactic biopsy specimens, organoids preserve critical alterations in TP53, ATRX, and PDGFRA.
Brain tumour organoids have substantially advanced the understanding of glioblastoma invasion patterns and adaptive therapeutic resistance. Longitudinal exposure to chemotherapeutic or radiotherapeutic pressure reveals an enrichment of therapy-resistant mesenchymal phenotypes governed by STAT3 and NF-κB signalling. Specific functional investigations have uncovered novel molecular mechanisms of resistance, such as centromere protein U accelerating the ubiquitination and degradation of RPS3 to suppress apoptosis, and dynamic structural remodelling of long non-coding RNA LINC01956 modulating DNA repair genes like RAD51 and BRCA1 in MGMT-methylated tumours. Furthermore, organoids enable the identification of actionable epigenetic vulnerabilities, including dependency on the histone methyltransferase complex subunit WDR5, which sustains cancer stem cell self-renewal.
Translational drug-screening applications in glioblastoma organoids demonstrate notable predictive accuracy, with multiple studies reporting over 80 percent concordance between in vitro responses to temozolomide and clinical patient outcomes. Beyond standard alkylating agents, organoid platforms facilitate the testing of targeted small-molecule inhibitors directed against EGFR, CDK4/6, and PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathways, alongside experimental biological approaches including oncolytic Zika virus replication. Multi-omic molecular profiling integrating epigenomics, transcriptomics, and proteomics has identified convergence on MYC-, E2F-, and mTOR-driven regulatory circuits, guiding synergistic combination therapies. For example, combining albumin-bound paclitaxel with temozolomide downregulates homologous recombination repair factors while triggering ferroptosis, effectively overcoming chemoresistance. Similarly, inhibiting the hexosamine biosynthesis pathway with FR054 sensitizes resistant glioma organoids to alkylating agents, illustrating how metabolic and molecular vulnerabilities can be systematically probed within physiologically relevant three-dimensional contexts.
In meningioma research, patient-derived organoids established from surgical resections maintain key histopathological and molecular characteristics, including expression of the progesterone receptor and somatostatin receptor-2. Pharmacological studies in meningioma organoids revealed that the transcription factor FOXM1 actively drives tumour cell proliferation, which can be suppressed by combining the FOXM1 inhibitor thiostrepton with radiotherapy or through RAF inhibition using AZ628. Single-cell RNA sequencing identified an aggressive SULT1E1-positive tumour-initiating subpopulation that is selectively eliminated via SIRT1 activation using SRT1720. In a prominent pharmacological screen spanning 60 meningioma organoids, the histone deacetylase inhibitor panobinostat exhibited broad antitumour activity across approximately 70 percent of samples through HDAC1/2 inhibition, while identifying HDAC8 upregulation as an adaptive resistance mechanism. These preclinical findings establish meningioma organoids as effective platforms for interrogating novel targeted compounds and oncolytic viral therapies.
Organoids derived from pediatric medulloblastomas have similarly provided critical insights into subtype-specific drivers and therapeutic opportunities. Explant and induced pluripotent stem cell-derived models faithfully capture the distinct morphology and transcriptomic signatures of aggressive group 3 medulloblastomas, demonstrating how oncogenic drivers such as c-MYC and OTX2 induce tumour proliferation while SMARCA4 functions as a proliferative suppressor. Organoid-based functional testing revealed that MYC upregulation confers heightened vulnerability to dual CDK12 and CDK13 inhibition, as well as sensitivity to the dual HDAC/PI3K inhibitor CUDC-907. In Sonic Hedgehog medulloblastoma organoid models, researchers identified OLIG2-positive stem-like cells as major drivers of recurrence, highlighting the therapeutic potential of the OLIG2 inhibitor CT-179. In parallel, organoid platforms confirmed metabolic dependencies on mitochondrial NADH shuttles under hypoxic microenvironments and revealed sensitivity to small-molecule compounds targeting EPLIN in MYCN-amplified tumours.
For rare and structurally complex sellar region tumours, organoids offer unprecedented preclinical modelling capabilities. Adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma organoid biobanks derived from surgical resections preserve diagnostic tissue histoarchitecture, nuclear β-catenin localization, and somatic mutation profiles, while highlighting substantial differences in therapeutic efficacy between two-dimensional monolayers and three-dimensional systems. For example, B7-H3-targeted CAR-T cells demonstrated potent cytotoxicity in monolayer cultures but exhibited reduced efficacy within three-dimensional organoids, reflecting the structural barriers present in native tissue. Similarly, pituitary neuroendocrine tumour organoids achieve derivation success rates of approximately 87 percent across diverse functional subtypes, preserving hormone production and subtype-specific markers such as SOX2, S100β, and KRT8/18. Cushing’s disease organoids carrying USP8 or USP48 mutations successfully secrete adrenocorticotropic hormone and exhibit differential responses to glucocorticoid modulators, such as relacorilant-induced apoptosis versus mifepristone-induced hormonal hypersecretion.
Advanced bioengineering strategies continue to resolve traditional organoid constraints, expanding their experimental utility. Three-dimensional bioprinting utilizing cell-laden bioinks deposits defined geometries and extracellular matrix hydrogels, establishing standardized, high-throughput architectures with controlled nutrient and oxygen gradients. Co-culture systems and multi-regional assembloids recreate intricate cell-cell interactions, including neural-tumour crosstalk and microglial integration. Furthermore, vascularisation efforts incorporating endothelial progenitor cells form functional vascular networks within cerebral organoids, reducing central core necrosis and establishing perivascular niches characterised by VEGFA, ANGPT2, and PDGFB signalling. Organoid-based immunotherapy assays have evaluated dual-targeting CD44/CD133 IL7Rα-armoured CAR-T cells and multi-antigen cocktail CAR-γδ T cells, tracking real-time immune infiltration, cytotoxicity, and antigen escape dynamics while assessing potential off-target neurotoxicity prior to clinical translation.
Despite significant technical advances, the review emphasizes critical limitations that currently restrict the broad clinical implementation of brain tumour organoids. Most published studies rely on small patient cohorts conducted within specialized academic centres, lacking standardized derivation protocols, culture media, and extracellular matrices. The turnaround time required to establish, expand, and pharmacologically test organoids frequently exceeds the narrow therapeutic window required for rapidly progressing malignancies like glioblastoma. Furthermore, many current platforms lack fully integrated functional vasculature, blood-brain barrier components, and systemic adaptive immune circulation, limiting their ability to model complete immune cell trafficking and physiological drug penetration. The authors conclude that addressing these challenges through multi-institutional organoid biobanks, automated microfluidic culture systems, air-liquid interface techniques, and prospective clinical validation trials will be essential to establish organoids as robust precision oncology tools capable of guiding individualized patient care.
